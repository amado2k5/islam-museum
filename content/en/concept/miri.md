## Temperament
Hot and dry to the third degree. Ibn Masawayh said: the fish kind is less hot and dry than the barley kind; the author adds that he does not believe him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Actions and properties
It cleanses thick humours, softens and dries, and has astringency and a clearing of phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.573}.

## Cosmetic uses
It sweetens the breath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Good for putrid ulcers; the kind made from fish and salted meats, it is said, stops the spreading of the malignant ulcer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.

## Joints
Helpful for pain of the hip and for sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.

## Eyes
It is applied as kohl at the start of smallpox and keeps the pustules from the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It helps moistness of the stomach and clears moistures from the viscera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.

## Excretory organs
It helps colic and enters its remedies and the enemas that cleanse ulcers of abrasion in particular {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.

## Poisons and bites
It is said to help the bite of a rabid dog {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.574}.
