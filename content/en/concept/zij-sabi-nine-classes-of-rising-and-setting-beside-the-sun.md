## The nine classes
The fixed stars and the wandering planets stand in nine relations to the sun, according to their place on the horizon and the meridian. The first is the morning rising, when the star is with the sun on the eastern horizon, in two forms. The second is the meridian of the rising, when the star is on the meridian as the sun rises. The third is the morning setting, when the sun is on the rising part and the star near the western horizon. The fourth is the southern rising, when the sun is on the meridian and the star in the east. The fifth is the southern meridian, when sun and star are both on the meridian. The sixth is the southern setting, when the sun is on the meridian and the star in the west. The seventh is the evening rising, when the sun is in the west and the star on the eastern horizon. The eighth is the western meridian, when the sun is on the western horizon and the star on the meridian. The ninth is the evening setting, when star and sun are together on the western horizon. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.183-184}

## Why there are so many forms
The text explains that stars rise and set equally on either side of the meridian line only on the equator of the day, and that bodies off the ecliptic do not rise and set evenly; this is why the forms differ. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.182-183}
