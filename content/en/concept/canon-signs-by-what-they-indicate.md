## Summary
Ibn Sina sorts the symptoms that point to diseases by what they indicate: the disease itself, its place, or its cause, with examples from the pulse and from fullness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Pointing to the disease itself
Some point to the disease itself, as a change in the speed of the pulse in fever points to the fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Pointing to the place
Some point to the place of the disease. A serrated pulse, when the pain is in the region of the chest, shows the swelling to be in the membrane and the diaphragm; a wavy pulse in the same setting shows the swelling to be in the substance of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Pointing to the cause
Some point to the cause, such as the signs of fullness: by their differing states each kind points to a kind of fullness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.
