## Summary
In the lexicon, umra is what a man is given for the length of his life. In the usage of the jurists it is the owner's giving of a thing to a person for the lifetime of the grantee {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11818}.

## How it differs from related grants
It differs from loan of use, the gift of a palm's fruit, the gift of a ewe's milk and the ruqba {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11819}.

## Its validity and nature
It is permitted in general on the report of Jabir; it is a gift and needs offer and taking possession. The Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that it transfers ownership at once, while the Malikis hold it transfers only use {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11819} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11820}.

## The Shafi'i forms
If the owner says 'I give you this for your life, and when you die it goes to your heirs', it is a gift and ownership passes to the heirs; if he says 'I give it to you for your life', the newer view makes it a gift {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11820}.

## Where to look further
Gifts are treated under «hiba» and loan of use under «ariya» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11820}.
