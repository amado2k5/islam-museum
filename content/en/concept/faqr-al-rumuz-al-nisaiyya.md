# The scarcity of female symbols across history

## The episode and the question
The 'Al-Wasatiyya' episode debated the scarcity of female symbols across history — not to weaken woman but to ask those who broke the barrier how they broke it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:03:30}. The question of causes offered three options: male domination, the woman's psychological and physical constitution, or social pressure — in East and West alike {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:04:30}.

## The vote and the figures
Fifty percent voted that female symbols exist today but more faintly than in the past {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:08:30}. From the episode's figures: women in political work in that era numbered about 3% — and the umma, over a billion, needs some ten million female leaders {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:16:30}.

## The symbols in history
Panelists cited prominent female symbols: Khadija, the mothers of the believers, Fatima bint Abd al-Malik and Zubayda — noting woman stood beside man as a symbol of giving {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:16:30}. They stressed the transition will not be a leap from zero to a hundred {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:22:00}.

## A Western study
One panelist drew on the Western book 'The Promotable Woman' — 'purely Western studies, a hundred percent; no Muslim wrote it about the West' — to study the phenomenon {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep16 00:31:30}.
