## Summary
Hadm in the lexical sense is tearing down a building, destruction, or collapse; the technical sense does not depart from this. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16458}

## Real and figurative demolition
Demolition is of two kinds: real, of a structure, and figurative, of something else such as a contract, meaning annulment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16459}

## Cases where demolition is required
A church built by People of the Book in a city the Muslims founded must be demolished; so must a structure within a river's buffer, and a projecting structure over a public road that harms passers-by, which the judge orders removed. A leaning wall may be demolished without liability on the demolisher. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16459} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16460}

## Effects of real demolition
Demolishing a mosque wall obliges its restoration; a buyer who demolishes part of the house confirms the sale; one who demolishes a house decorated with figural images owes its value. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16460}

## Figurative demolition
An example is triple divorce: a woman divorced three times returns to her husband after another marriage under a new marriage contract, the earlier divorce being demolished. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16460}
