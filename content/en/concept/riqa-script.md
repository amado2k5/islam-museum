## Summary
Riqa' is one of the [[concept/al-aqlam-al-sitta|six pens]], the small partner of [[concept/tawqi-script|tawqi']]. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 25} Maryam Ekhtiar notes that, like tawqi', it joins letters that are not linked in other scripts, and that it was preferred for chancery documents, colophons, book covers and carpet pages. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26}

## Example
The Met holds two carpet pages from a prayer book written by [[person/ahmad-nayrizi-calligrapher|Ahmad Nayrizi]] and illuminated, it is thought, by Muhammad Hadi, probably at Isfahan, 1132 AH/1719–20 CE. Each page has a central medallion with two pendants bearing Arabic prayers in riqa', in gold on crimson, amid gold vines and scrolls on pistachio green. They are among four pages in the manuscript that show the decorative use of riqa' within illumination, including the unorthodox joining of alif and lam, or of ra with another ra. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 41} See [[practice/manuscript-illumination-tazhib|illumination]].
