## Overview
The third class of drops from the chain in [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]]'s [[work/nuzhat-al-nazar|*Nuzhat al-Nazar*]]: if two or more consecutive narrators are missing, the report is obscured (*mu'dal*); otherwise, if two non-consecutive narrators are missing, in two places for example, it is interrupted (*munqati'*), and so too if a single narrator is missing, or more than two, provided they are not consecutive. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 79}

## Obscured and suspended
Between the obscured and the [[concept/al-hadith-al-muallaq|suspended]] report there is a partial overlap: since the obscured is defined as missing two or more, it coincides with some forms of the suspended; since the suspended is restricted to a compiler's omission at the start of the chain, it parts from it. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 76} The editor explains: a report missing two together at the beginning of its chain is both suspended and obscured; the suspended alone covers one missing at the beginning or the whole chain dropped; the obscured alone covers two consecutive narrators missing from the middle of the chain. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 76}

## An example
The editor's example of the obscured report is Malik's report from Mu'adh ibn Jabal: "The last counsel the Messenger of God gave me, as I put my foot in the stirrup, was: make your character good for people, Mu'adh ibn Jabal." Between Malik and Mu'adh there are two or more links. He quotes Ibn 'Abd al-Barr that its meaning is sound and attested with full chains. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 79}

## Its place among the kinds of drop
Ibn Hajar arranges the visible drops in four kinds: the [[concept/al-hadith-al-muallaq|suspended]] at the start of the chain, the [[concept/al-hadith-al-mursal|mursal]] at its end, and the obscured and the interrupted elsewhere. The editor traces the rejection of all of them to one root: the narrator who dropped out is unknown, and caution requires allowing that he may be weak. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 76} See also [[concept/inqita|inqita']].

## In Ibn al-Salah's Muqaddima
### Overview
For [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] mu'dal is a name for a special kind of [[concept/munqati|munqati']]: every mu'dal is munqati' but not every munqati' is mu'dal, and some call it [[concept/irsal-fiqh|mursal]]; it means a chain from which two or more narrators have dropped out. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 59}

### The word
Hadith scholars say *a'dalahu fa-huwa mu'dal*, with *a* on the *d*; Ibn al-Salah finds the term's lexical source unclear, says he searched and found the expression *amr 'adil*, "a hard, locked matter", and pays no heed to *mu'dil* with *i*, even though it has the same meaning. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 59}

### Examples
An example is a Follower of the Successors saying "the Messenger of God said", or anyone later citing the Prophet, Abu Bakr or Umar without naming the intermediaries. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 59} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 60} Abu Nasr al-Sijzi said that a narrator's "it reached me", as in [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]]'s "it reached me from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] that the Messenger of God said: the slave is owed his food and clothing", is what hadith scholars call mu'dal. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 60} Ibn al-Salah adds that when jurists and other authors write "the Messenger of God said such-and-such", all of it is of the mu'dal kind, and that [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]] once called it mursal, following those who call every unconnected chain mursal. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 60}

### Mu'dal by stopping
[[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim]] counted as a kind of mu'dal a Follower's report from a Successor that stops at the Successor while others transmit it connected to the Prophet; his example is [[person/amir-al-shabi|al-Sha'bi]]'s saying, in [[person/al-amash-sulayman-ibn-mihran|al-A'mash]]'s version, "a man will be told on the Day of Resurrection: you did such-and-such…", which al-Sha'bi has connected from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]] from the Prophet. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 60} Ibn al-Salah approved, since a break of one link combined with stopping amounts to a break of two — the Companion and the Messenger of God — and so better deserves the name. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 61}

### Appendices
Ibn al-Salah attaches to this type discussions of [[concept/muanan|chains linked by "from"]], of [[concept/al-hadith-al-muallaq|suspended chains]], and of hadiths some trustworthy narrators give as mursal and others as connected; these have their own cards. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 61} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 67} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 71}
