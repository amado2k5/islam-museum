## Summary
"We call the people of our qibla Muslims and believers, as long as they acknowledge what the Prophet brought and believe him in all he said and reported" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 20}.

## No takfir for sin
"We do not declare anyone of the people of the qibla an unbeliever for a sin, as long as he does not deem it lawful" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 21}. He does not say: no sin harms whoever commits it alongside faith {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 21}. A servant leaves faith only by denying what brought him into it {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 21}.

## Hope and fear
He hopes for the good-doers among the believers that God will pardon them and admit them to Paradise by His mercy — "but we do not feel secure for them, nor do we testify Paradise for them" — and asks forgiveness for their sinners, fears for them yet does not drive them to despair, for "security and despair remove one from the religion of Islam" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 21}.

## Judging individuals
"We do not assign any of them to Paradise or the Fire, nor testify against them unbelief, polytheism or hypocrisy, unless something of that appears from them; we leave their secrets to God" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 23} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 24}. He says he holds prayer valid behind every righteous and unrighteous man of the people of the qibla, and over whoever of them dies {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 23}. He does not hold the sword against anyone of the umma except one upon whom the sword is due {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 24}.

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama says: "We do not affirm for anyone of the people of the qibla Paradise or Fire, except one the Messenger affirmed it for; but we hope for the good-doer and fear for the sinner. We do not declare anyone of the people of the qibla an unbeliever for a sin, nor expel him from Islam for a deed" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 39}. He relates Anas's hadith that part of faith's foundation is "refraining from whoever says: there is no god but God" — not declared an unbeliever for a sin, nor expelled from Islam for a deed {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 39}.

## In Ibn Taymiyya's al-'Aqida al-Wasitiyya
After his definition of faith, Ibn Taymiyya says: "They do not strip the sinful believer of faith entirely, nor do they hold him in the Fire forever" — the sinner falls under the unqualified name of faith in some texts and not in others — "and we say: he is a believer deficient in faith, or a believer in his faith, a sinner by his major sin; he is not given the unqualified name, nor stripped of the name unqualifiedly for his major sin" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 23}.

## In al-Ash'ari's Maqalat al-Islamiyyin
In "the account of the sum of the saying of the people of hadith and sunna" in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin, Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari presents what the people of hadith and sunna hold, ending: "And in all we have mentioned of their saying we speak, and to it we go" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 350}. He relates there that they "do not declare anyone of the people of the qibla an unbeliever for a sin he commits — such as adultery, theft and the like of the major sins — they are believers by the faith they hold, even if they commit the major sins" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 347}, and "they do not testify the Fire for anyone of the people of major sins, nor judge Paradise for anyone of the monotheists; their affair is with God — He may punish them or forgive them" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 347}.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the positions of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya, Ibn Qudama's Lum'at al-I'tiqad, Ibn Taymiyya's al-Wasitiyya, and what al-Ash'ari related in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin of the people of hadith and sunna and declared his own — each attributed to its book. The sayings of the Maturidis and the remaining Sunni and non-Sunni schools from their own books have not yet been added; the entry needs specialist review before promotion.
