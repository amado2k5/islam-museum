## Summary
Ibn Sina notes that a swelling changes the pulse according to its organ: the nervous, the vascular and the moist soft organs each have their rulings, and some organs give a named pulse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.

## Nervous, vascular and moist organs
Nervous organs add hardness and the saw-like quality to the pulse; vascular organs add greatness and sharper unevenness, above all where arteries predominate, as in the spleen and lung, and this greatness lasts only as long as power does. Moist, soft organs make it wave-like, as the brain and the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.

## Named pulses by organ
Where a swelling changes the pulse through an intermediary, a swelling of the lung makes the pulse choking, of the liver wilting, of the kidney mat-like, and a swelling of an organ of keen feeling, such as the mouth of the stomach and the diaphragm, makes it convulse with a fainting spasm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.
