## The motor acts
The failure and weakness of the motor acts indicate a thin, plentiful, superfluous moisture in their instruments, and in whatever organ they are they indicate an affliction in the brain, the most particular to it being what is in the whole body, like apoplexy, or in one side, like palsy and slack facial palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.14} Failure and weakness may also come from the heat or dryness of the brain or the dryness of some nerve growing from it, but that comes after many diseases, little by little and over days {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.14}

Their change, if sudden, indicates moisture, and if little by little, dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15} An example is the changed movements of the epileptic in epilepsy, which is a general convulsion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15}

He says that if a person is brisk in movements the temperament of his brain is originally hot or dry, and if he leans to laziness and slackness it is cold or moist {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15} If he is ill and his movements tend to restlessness he is hot, and if they tend to stillness without a severe collapse of strength he leans to cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15}

## Sleep and waking
He says sleep always follows a moist, slackening ill-temperament, or a cold one that congeals the movement of the sensing powers, or a strong dissolving of the soul-spirit through excess of movement, or the powers' rushing inward to digest matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15} So when sleep does not run its natural course and does not follow fatigue and movement, its cause is moisture or congealing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15}

Not every moisture brings sleep, since old men, despite the moisture of their temperaments, stay awake long; Galen sees the cause in the quality of their borax-like moistures, which keep them awake by harming the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15} Dryness, however, in every case keeps one awake {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.15}
