## Summary
Is the Friday sermon valid in a language other than Arabic? The four Sunni schools differed on whether Arabic is a condition: for the Hanafis, in Abu Hanifa's view, it is recommended; for the Malikis and Hanbalis it is a condition for one who is able; and for the Shafi'is a condition with a qualification. The page gives each school's view under its holders as the fatwas of Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya and the IslamQA site report them, and does not judge between them. It rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the books and fatwas it points to were not opened.

## The question and its background
The sermon is one of the elements of the Friday prayer for most jurists, and it is given to a congregation that may include people who do not know Arabic. The questions that arise are whether the preacher may speak in the congregation's tongue, whether some Arabic must at least be present in the sermon, and what the ruling is for one who gives it in another language while able in Arabic. Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya's Fatwa 8127 gathers the schools' statements on a Friday prayer with a single sermon in other than Arabic in a country where Arabic is not spoken {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-8127 on Fatwa 8127}, and its Fatwa 2711 treats translating the sermon for non-speakers of Arabic {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-2711 on Fatwa 2711}.

## The Hanafis
Ibn Abidin in Radd al-Muhtar (Dar al-Fikr ed., vol. 2, p. 147) and Ibn Maza in al-Muhit al-Burhani (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya ed., vol. 2, p. 74) report that for Abu Hanifa Arabic in the sermon is recommended, not a condition, so a sermon in another language is valid even if the preacher knows Arabic. Abu Yusuf and Muhammad stipulate Arabic unless the preacher is incapable of it {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-8127 on Radd al-Muhtar 2/147 and al-Muhit al-Burhani 2/74}.

## The Malikis
Al-Nafrawi in al-Fawakih al-Dawani, vol. 1, p. 306, states that Arabic is a condition of the sermon whenever the preacher is able to give it, even if the listeners do not understand Arabic, as IslamQA 112041 cites it {cite:islamqa-112041 on al-Nafrawi, al-Fawakih al-Dawani 1/306}.

## The Shafi'is
Their relied-upon view, as in al-Nawawi's al-Majmu' (Dar al-Fikr ed., vol. 4, pp. 521-522) and al-Mahalli's Sharh Minhaj al-Talibin (vol. 1, p. 322), is that Arabic is a condition of the sermon if any Arabic-speaker is present. If none of those present knows Arabic, it suffices to preach in their language, except for the Qur'anic verse, which stays in Arabic. A minority wajh of al-Mutawalli holds Arabic to be recommended, not a condition {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-8127 on al-Majmu' 4/521-522 and al-Mahalli 1/322}. Al-Bayjuri in his Hashiya on the commentary of Ibn Qasim (Mustafa al-Halabi ed., p. 228), as Fatwa 2711 quotes him, speaks to the ruling on translating the sermon {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-2711 on al-Bayjuri p.228}.

## The Hanbalis
Al-Buhuti in Kashshaf al-Qina' (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya ed., vol. 2, pp. 35-36) states that the sermon is not valid in another language if the preacher is able to give it in Arabic, and is valid in another language if he is incapable of it {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-8127 on al-Buhuti, Kashshaf al-Qina' 2/35-36}.

## How the views differ
The schools agree that one unable in Arabic is a special case and part company over the one who is able: for Abu Hanifa Arabic is not required of him; for his two companions, the Malikis and the Hanbalis it is; and for the Shafi'is it depends on whether anyone present knows Arabic, with the verse remaining in Arabic. This meets the question of learning in [[concept/wujub-taallum-al-arabiyya-lil-ibada|The duty to learn Arabic for worship]], translation in prayer in [[concept/qiraat-al-quran-bil-tarjama-fi-al-salat|Reciting the Qur'an in translation in prayer]], and the sermon more generally in [[concept/khutba-fiqh|The sermon]].
