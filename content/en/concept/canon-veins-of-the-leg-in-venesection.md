## Summary
Ibn Sina names the veins of the leg that are opened, the sciatic vein, the saphena, the vein of the hollow of the knee and the vein behind the Achilles tendon, with the place of each and its uses in sciatica, gout, piles and bringing on the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## The sciatic vein
Of the veins of the leg there is the sciatic vein: it is opened on the outer side at the ankle, either below it or above it between the hip and the ankle, and is wrapped in a bandage or a strong band; it is better to bathe before it, and best to open it lengthwise. If it is hidden, a branch between the little and the ring digit is opened. The benefit of opening the sciatic vein in sciatic pain is great, and likewise in gout, varicose veins and elephantiasis. A second bleeding at the sciatic vein is hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## The saphena
The saphena is also one of these: it is on the inner side of the ankle and plainer than the sciatic vein; it is opened to evacuate blood from the organs below the liver and to turn blood from the upper parts to the lower, and so it brings on the menses and opens the mouths of the piles. The best way to open the saphena is obliquely, tending across {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## Reasoning and experience
Reasoning requires that the sciatic vein and the saphena be alike in benefit, but experience favours by a good deal the effect of opening the sciatic vein in sciatic pain, and that was owing to its being in line with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.

## The knee hollow and behind the tendon
There is also the vein of the hollow of the knee, which goes the way of the saphena but is stronger than the saphena in bringing on the menses and in pains of the anus and piles. There is also the vessel behind the Achilles tendon, which is like a branch of the saphena and goes its way. The opening of the veins of the leg in general is useful in diseases that come of matters inclined toward the head and in black-bile diseases, and weakens the strength more than opening the veins of the arm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.297}.
