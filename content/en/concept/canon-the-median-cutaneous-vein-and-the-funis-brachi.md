## Summary
Ibn Sina warns of the danger to the nerve beneath the median cutaneous vein and says what is done if it is struck, and says that the funis brachi is best opened obliquely unless it rolls from both sides {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## The danger of the nerve
In the median cutaneous vein there is danger to the nerve under it, and it may lie between two nerves, so effort must be made to open it lengthwise. Sometimes a thin nerve stretched like a tendon lies above it; one must learn of this and take care that the stroke does not strike it, for a chronic numbness would follow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## If the nerve is struck
In one whose vessel is thicker this branch is plainer and the error in it more injurious. If the mistake happens and that nerve is struck, do not let the bleeding wound close; put on it what prevents its closing, and treat it with the treatment for wounds of the nerve, of which he has spoken in the fourth book. Beware of bringing near it a cooling agent such as the juice of nightshade or sandalwood; rather soften its surroundings and the whole body with warmed oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## The funis brachi
The funis brachi too is best opened obliquely, unless it rolls from both sides, when it is opened lengthwise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.
