## Overview
Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn holds that man loves to have his honor kept from defamation, and that the law makes honor as inviolable as blood and flesh: publishing another's concealed faults is unlawful {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 84} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 85}. The exception is what warding off harm requires — disclosing a man's fault to one set on dealing or intermarrying with him, as sincere advice {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 85} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 86} — and the man who proclaims his own sin and tears his own veil is not backbitten by mention {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 85}. He links a person's will to guard his honor with the community's freedom.
