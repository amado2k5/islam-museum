## Summary
From each of the two parts of the descending hollow vein Ibn Sina counts ten branches to the buttocks, sacrum, womb, bladder, pudenda and thigh, then follows what remains through knee, shank and foot to the saphenous vein, and closes the anatomy of the organs of similar parts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.92-93}.

## The ten
From each of the two there branch, as the printed text has it before reaching the liver, which probably means the thigh, ten layers: the first aims at the buttocks; the second, fine and hair-like, at the lower parts of the abdominal membrane; the third spreads in the muscles on the sacrum; the fourth in the muscles of the anus and outside of the sacrum; the fifth goes to the neck of the womb in women and to the bladder, and what goes to the bladder divides in two, one in the bladder and one aimed at its neck, which in men is very large because of the penis and small in women, while from the vessels coming to the womb from its sides rise vessels to the breast, so that womb and breast are made alike {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.92}. The sixth goes to the muscles on the pubic bone; the seventh rises to the muscle running straight along the belly and joins the ends of vessels descending in the chest to the soft flank; the eighth reaches the pudenda in men and women; the ninth the muscles of the inner thigh; and the tenth starts from the side of the ureter, goes outward to the flanks and joins the ends of descending vessels, especially those descending from the breasts, and a great part of it goes to the muscles of the testicles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.92-93}.

## In the thigh and shank
What remains of the tenth reaches the thigh and branches: one in the muscles on the front of the thigh, another deep in the lower and inner thigh, and many spread deep in the thigh. What remains still divides, just below the knee joint, into three: the outer runs along the small shin bone to the ankle; the middle runs descending in the bend of the knee, leaves branches in the muscles of the back of the shank and splits in two, one vanishing in the shank and the other running between the two shin bones to the front of the foot and mixing with a branch of the outer; and the third, the inner, inclines to the veined place of the shank, then runs to the ankle and the convex end of the great shin bone and descends to the inner front, and this is the saphenous {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.93}. These three become four: two outer going to the foot by the small shin bone, and two inner, one rising over the foot and spreading in the upper part toward the little toe, the other mixing with the outer branch of the inner part, and the two spreading in the lower parts. Ibn Sina says this is the number of the veins, that the anatomy of the organs of similar parts is complete, and that the anatomy of the instrumental organs will be given in the book on their states and treatments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.93}.
