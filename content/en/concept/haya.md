## Overview
*Haya'* (modesty) is a character trait that keeps its possessor from committing shameful acts. Ibn Rajab explains the hadith of Abu Mas'ud al-Badri in al-Bukhari: "Among what people have obtained from the words of the earlier prophethood is: if you are not ashamed, do as you wish." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.464-465}

## "The words of the earlier prophethood"
Ibn Rajab explains that the phrase means this saying is transmitted from the earlier prophets. It was passed around and inherited by people generation after generation until it reached the beginning of this community. He notes that in some reports people obtained from the words of the earlier prophethood only this. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.464}

## Two meanings of "do as you wish"
Ibn Rajab sets out two meanings. The command is not a real command, but a reproach and a prohibition. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.465}

The first is a threat and a warning, as in a verse that Ibn Rajab cites (its text is not reproduced here), and as in the hadith "whoever sells wine, let him sell pigs." It is the choice of a group, among them Abu al-Abbas Thaʿlab. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.465}

The second is a command in its wording but a statement in meaning: whoever has no modesty does as he wishes, because what restrains from shameful acts is modesty. He illustrates it with the hadith "whoever lies about me deliberately, let him take his seat in the Fire." It is the choice of Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam, Ibn Qutayba, and Muhammad ibn Nasr al-Marwazi. Ibn Rajab reports that Abu Dawud relayed from Imam Ahmad something indicating the same. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.465}

Modesty belongs to the chapter on [[concept/adab|good manners]]. The text of the verse mentioned is not given here. It needs a further source.
