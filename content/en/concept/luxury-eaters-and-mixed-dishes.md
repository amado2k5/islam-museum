## Summary
Ibn Sina says one who eats much meat in luxury should look to bloodletting, and if the temperament leans cold, to the digestive compounds called jawarishnat and itrifalat; the worst thing is to gather different foods at once and to prolong the meal; the most suitable food is the most pleasant when its substance is sound and the principal organs are healthy, and a harm of very pleasant food is eating too much of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.

## The luxurious eater
One who eats much meat in luxury should keep watch on bloodletting; if he tends toward a cold temperament he should take the jawarishnat and itrifalat and what cleans the stomach, the bowels and the nearby channels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.

## Mixed foods and long meals
The worst thing is to gather different foods together and prolong the time of eating, so that the later food arrives when the first has begun to digest and the parts of the food do not digest alike {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.

## The most pleasant food
The most suitable food is the most pleasant, because the stomach and the retentive power embrace it strongly, provided its substance is good and all the principal organs are in accord and sound. A harm of very pleasant food is that one can take too much of it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.
