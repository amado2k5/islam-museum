## Summary
Ibn Sina notes that white urine may accompany a hot bilious temperament and red urine a cold phlegmatic one, shows how to tell the whiteness of cold from the whiteness of hidden bile, and gives causes of red urine in cold diseases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.187-188}.

## Telling the whiteness apart
Know that urine may be white while the temperament is hot and bilious, and red while it is cold and phlegmatic, for if bile turns aside from the urinary path and does not mix with the urine, the urine stays white. If its colour is bright, its sediment abundant and thick and its consistency thick, the whiteness comes from the cold of phlegm; if the colour is not bright and the sediment neither abundant nor well separated, it comes from hidden bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.187}.

## Why urine is red in cold diseases
The reason urine is red in cold diseases is one of several: severe pain that dissolves bile, as happens in cold colic; or a strong presence of phlegm in the passage between the gall bladder and the bowels, so that bile does not flow into the bowels as usual but is driven to go with the urine; or a weak liver whose power falls short of separating water from blood, as in cold dropsy, when the urine resembles the washings of fresh meat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.187-188}.
