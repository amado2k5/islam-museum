## The proverb and its meaning
The proverb 'The promises of Urqub' is one of the Arabic proverbs recorded in the old proverb books, as a research summary supplied to the museum sets out in a choice of ten. Its meaning, in the summary, is empty, broken promises. It belongs to the matter of the great Arabic proverb collections treated in the page [[concept/kutub-al-amthal-al-arabiyya|The Arabic proverb books]] {cite:noonpost-16943}.

## The story as the books tell it
Urqub promised his brother the yield of a date palm of his: first he told him to wait until it blossomed, then until the fruit formed unripe, then until it ripened, each time telling him to wait for the next stage, and in the end he harvested the dates secretly at night. {cite:noonpost-16943}

## The old sources
The summary places the proverb in Majma al-Amthal as number 4070 and ascribes the telling to the Noonpost article. Volume, page and serial numbers stand as in the summary, and the museum has not checked them {cite:noonpost-16943}. Majma al-Amthal is the work of Abu al-Fadl al-Maydani, who died in 518 AH, 1124 CE, and holds according to the summary over 6,000 proverbs; the summary tabulates it with Abu Hilal al-Askari's Jamharat al-Amthal and al-Zamakhshari's al-Mustaqsa {cite:arabictypinglab-arabic-proverbs}.

## When the proverb is used
The proverb is used for what fits its meaning, that is empty, broken promises, and the hearer calls its tale to mind even when it is not told. The proverb books agree in keeping a proverb together with its story, because the story explains why it was said, and the sense it is applied to may widen with use; but the summary gives no other instances of its use, so the page gives none.

## A note on the nature of the story
The story here is as the books tell it. It is one of the widely repeated traditional reports, and the museum cannot establish that it happened; it is a narrative told to explain the proverb, not confirmed history. The museum has not opened any of the books and pages named.
