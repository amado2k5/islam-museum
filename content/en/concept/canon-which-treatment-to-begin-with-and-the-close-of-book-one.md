## Summary
Ibn Sina ends chapter 31 with advice on the order of treatments when illnesses come together, lists three features by which to begin, then says he has finished the general principles and will go on to his book on simple drugs, so closing Book I {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## The first feature
When illnesses come together, we must begin with what is marked by one of three features. The first is that the second cannot be healed without its healing, as when a swelling and an ulcer come together: we treat the swelling first until the bad temperament that goes with it is gone, since the ulcer cannot heal alongside it, and then we treat the ulcer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## The second feature
The second is that one is the cause of the other, as when a blockage and a fever occur: we treat the blockage first and then the fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## The third feature
The third is that one is the more urgent, as when a continuous fever, synochus, and palsy come together: we treat synochus by cooling and venesection and take no notice of the palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## Disease and symptom
When a disease and a symptom come together, we begin with the disease unless the symptom overcomes it, and then we aim at the symptom and take no notice of the disease, as we give narcotics in colic of severe pain when it grows hard, though it harms the colic itself. So too we may postpone a needed venesection for weakness of the stomach, an earlier diarrhoea or present nausea. Sometimes we bleed without completing the cutting off of the whole cause, as in the illness of convulsion we do not aim to expel all the humour but leave some of it for the convulsive movement to dissolve, lest it dissolve the innate moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## The close of the book
Ibn Sina says this much of his discourse on the general principles of the art of medicine is enough, and that he will go on to compose his book on simple drugs. At the end of the page the printed text declares Book I of the books of the Canon, the Generalities, complete. Book II, on simple drugs, follows, divided into two parts: the natural laws of drugs, and knowledge of the powers of particular drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.311-314}.
