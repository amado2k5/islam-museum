## Summary
Ibn Sina counts travel in severe cold a great danger: many a well-wrapped traveller has been killed by cold with spasm, tetanus, rigidity and apoplexy; he advises closing the pores, guarding nose and mouth from sudden cold air, warming not at once but by degrees, keeping from the fire, eating dishes rich in garlic, walnut, mustard and asafoetida, and not travelling empty {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.262-263}.

## Danger and protection
Travel in severe cold is very dangerous even with equipment and provision, so how much more without them; many a wrapped-up traveller has been killed by cold and by the snowstorm with spasm, tetanus, rigidity and apoplexy; the first care is that they close the pores, keep sudden cold air from entering nose and mouth, and protect the extremities {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.262-263}.

## Warming by degrees
When the traveller halts in the cold he does not warm himself at once but little by little, and does not hasten to the fire, for it is better not to approach it, and if he must he does so by degrees; if he halts in the cold hungry and takes something hot, a strange fever-like heat comes over him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.263}.

## Foods and asafoetida
Travellers have foods that ease the cold for them, those rich in garlic, walnut, mustard and asafoetida, sometimes with whey, and ghee is good for them, especially with unmixed wine drunk on top; the traveller does not set out empty in the cold but fills himself with food, drinks wine instead of water, then waits until it settles in his belly and warms before riding; asafoetida warms what has congealed, a full dose being a dirham of it in a ratl of wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.263}.
