## Summary
Mabarik is the plural of mabrak, the place where camels kneel; some jurists treat mabarik and ma'atin (watering and resting places) alike {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13819}. Marabid are the pens where sheep rest and marad (camel pens) are wider than mabarik {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13819-13820}. Most jurists dislike prayer in the resting places of camels, even when they are clean; Ahmad has two narrations, one that prayer there is invalid, and one like the majority, valid unless the place is impure {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13820}. The Hanafis and Shafi'is give the reason as the camels' skittishness, which may break the prayer or distract from humility in it; the Malikis say the rule is devotional and not based on a reason; one Hanbali view is that it is because such places may be impure {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13820-13821}.
