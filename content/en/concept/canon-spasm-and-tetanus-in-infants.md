## Summary
Ibn Sina mentions the spasm that befalls infants, above all at teething, mostly from spoiled digestion with weakness of the nerve and treated with oils, then tetanus, and the case where it comes from dryness after fevers and diarrhoea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.212-213}.

## Spasm at teething
Spasm may befall them especially at teething, and most of it comes from the spoiling of digestion that befalls them together with great weakness of the nerve, above all in one whose body is thick and moist. It is treated with iris oil, oil of the lily, henna oil or oil of the wallflower {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.212-213}.

## Tetanus and dryness
Tetanus may also occur, and is treated with water in which squirting cucumber was boiled, or with violet oil. If the spasm is judged to come from dryness, because it follows fevers and violent diarrhoea and arrives little by little, the joints are made to sweat with violet oil alone or with a little refined wax, and oil and violet oil and the like are poured plentifully over the brain, and the same if a dry tetanus befalls them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.213}.
