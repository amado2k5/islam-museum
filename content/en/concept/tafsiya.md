## Nature
The gum of wild rue, also said with the letter tha; it is of no use except fresh, and when time has passed over it it weakens and is not of use, since the superfluous moistures in it have dissolved; the editor's footnote says it is thafsiya {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.690}

## Temperament
Very hot and burning, strong in heating and drying, with a strange superfluous moisture because of which it does not sting at once {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.690}

## Actions and properties
It cleanses, purges, ripens and bursts; because of its superfluous moisture it does not burn at once but after a while, and it draws strongly from the depth of the body, though after a time; it has no equal in changing the temperament toward heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.690}

## Cosmetics
It grows hair and benefits alopecia greatly, and rarely is an equal found for it in that; the book says it has mentioned its use in its own chapter; it also benefits blood-bruise marks, scars, freckles and baras {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.690}

## Joints
It is rubbed on slackness, on gout and on cold joints, and used as an enema for sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits the spitting of pus and difficult breathing and pain of the two sides, especially the old pains, applied, as a poultice and by evacuation through it, and helps to bring up superfluities applied and in electuaries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Excretory organs
In its root, bark and tear there is loosening of the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Fevers
The book gives amounts for its bark, juice and tear, which are left out here, and says that taking much of it harms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}

## Substitutes
The book names tragacanth and hurf as its substitute, the amount of the substitute left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.691}
