Wat', lexically, is standing over a thing; it is also used for intercourse, the insertion of a male member into a female's sexual organ until the two are as one. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17482}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17483}

Related words: nikah, whose root is joining and gathering, is used in the strict sense for the marriage contract and in a figurative sense for intercourse, or the reverse, as the lexicographers and jurists differ; liwat is intercourse with males, which falls under the wider wat'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17483}

First, wat' as intercourse. It is either lawful or forbidden. The lawful is intercourse with a wife or a concubine, which may be forbidden at times, such as during menstruation or post-natal bleeding, before a zihar is expiated, in ihram, while fasting or in i'tikaf. The forbidden is either of a kind that could be lawful in another case, such as intercourse with a woman who may be married to one, which carries the penalty of zina, or of a kind that can never be lawful, such as sodomy, anal intercourse with a wife or a non-wife, and bestiality. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17483}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17484}

The causes that make intercourse lawful are the marriage contract and ownership of the right hand. Ownership of a female slave is lawful without a contract, because ownership of the person carries the use. The Qur'an alludes to this in the sura of al-Mu'minun (cited by sura only) without a verse being quoted here. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17484}

The ruling: the majority of jurists hold that intercourse with a wife or concubine is in principle permitted, being a pleasure that the natural disposition calls for, with no obedience or disobedience in itself. It becomes recommended with a good intention, such as keeping oneself chaste from the unlawful, may become obligatory when it is the only means of chastity for oneself or one's household, and may be forbidden or disliked according to circumstances. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17484}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17485}

Ibn al-Qayyim lists the purposes of intercourse: preserving the progeny, releasing the water that harms the body if it is retained, and the fulfilment of desire and pleasure; its benefits include lowering the gaze, restraining the self and securing chastity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17485}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17486}

The reward for lawful intercourse: the report of Abu Dharr that 'in the intimacy of one of you there is charity' is cited; the majority hold that a man is rewarded for intercourse with his wife when accompanied by a good intention, such as keeping himself or his wife from the unlawful, fulfilling her right in proper companionship, or seeking a righteous child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17486} Where the man intends nothing but the fulfilment of his desire, the jurists differ: Ibn Qutayba holds he is rewarded in every case, while Ibn Hajar al-Haytami and others say there is no reward where no such intention exists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17486}

Intercourse and its rewards: a Muslim is rewarded for what he spends on his household if he seeks the reward of God in it; if this is so for obligatory spending, it is more so for lawful intercourse, as the book states. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17487}

The courtesies and recommended acts of intercourse: it is recommended to begin with the basmala; to turn away from the qibla out of respect for it; to begin with play, embrace and kissing, so that the woman's desire rises; to take care to reach the satisfaction of both together; to cover oneself with a cloth; to lower the voice and speak little. It is disliked for a man to have intercourse where he can be seen or his sounds heard, and forbidden when the private parts are exposed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17487}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17488} It is recommended for one who wishes to repeat intercourse to wash his private part and perform wudu', and ghusl is better; the book cites the report that the Prophet went round his wives with a single ghusl. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17488}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17489}

Speaking about intercourse and disclosing its secrets: the jurists differ. The Hanafis, a Hanbali view which al-Mardawi prefers, and al-Nawawi hold that boasting of intercourse and disclosing what passes between spouses is forbidden, on the report of Abu Sa'id about the worst of people in rank before God and the saying that spreading (such talk) is forbidden. Ibn al-Qayyim, al-Haytami and others count it among the major sins. The school view of the Hanbalis is that the spouses' speaking of what passes between them is disliked, even to a co-wife. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17489}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17490}

Impediments to lawful intercourse: there are nine. The jurists agree on six of them, namely menstruation, post-natal bleeding, i'tikaf, fasting, ihram and zihar before its expiation; they differ on three, namely istihada, failure to wash after the end of menstruation, and residence in the abode of war. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17490}

First, menstruation: the jurists agree that intercourse with a menstruating woman in the vagina is forbidden, on the verse of sura al-Baqara (cited by sura only) and the saying that one may do everything except intercourse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17491} Second, post-natal bleeding: the jurists agree that intercourse in the vagina is forbidden, and that its rules, including washing and expiation, are those of menstruation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17491}

Third, istihada: the jurists differ on whether intercourse with an irregularly bleeding woman is permitted. The majority of Hanafis, Shafi'is and Malikis, and one Hanbali report, permit it, a view reported by Ibn al-Mundhir from Ibn 'Abbas, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, al-Hasan, Ata and others, citing that she is pure of menstruation and the case of Hamna bint Jahsh, whose husband Talha had intercourse with her while she bled. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17491}
