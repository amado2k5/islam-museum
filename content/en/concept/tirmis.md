## Nature
Dioscorides claimed that of tirmis some is garden and some wild; the wild is smaller than the garden and is like it and suits all that the garden suits; both are a flattened grain, bitter in taste and hollowed in the middle, and it is the Egyptian broad bean {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Choice
The wild of it is stronger in all that is described of its actions, but it is smaller {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Temperament
Hot in the first degree, dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Actions and properties
The tirmis that has bitterness cleanses and dissolves without stinging in it. Galen said: tirmis whose bitterness has been removed is coarse and no sweetness remains in it, and on the whole it is bad and hard to digest, generating rawness in the veins if it is not digested well; the sweetened one nourishes much when its cooking is made firm and it is digested, is not bad in humour and has some drying and viscosity; and on the whole it is nearer to a drug than to a food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Cosmetics
It thins hair, cleanses freckles, bahaq, scars, dark discolouration and pustules, and cleanses the face, especially when cooked in rain water until it falls apart; and using its decoction benefits baras {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Swellings and pustules
It benefits pustules of the face, ulcers, hot swellings, scrofula and hardness with vinegar, or with vinegar and honey, and its decoction poured on gangrene prevents its corruption {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Wounds and ulcers
It benefits scab, so that with the root of black madhariyun it can remove the scab of livestock; it benefits the eating ulcer, hasaf and bad and malignant ulcers, its flour with barley flour soothes the pains of wounds, and it benefits al-nar al-farisi {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Joints
A poultice of tirmis is made on sciatica and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Organs of the head
Its flour benefits moist ulcers of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It opens blockages of the liver and the spleen, especially when cooked with vinegar and honey, and especially with honey, rue and pepper; the one without bitterness calms nausea and opens the appetite, but the one whose bitterness has been taken out is heavy in passing through {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Excretory organs
It expels worms and the tapeworm as a decoction, as a smear on the navel, as a linctus with honey or drunk with mixed vinegar; it benefits pains of sciatica, promotes urine, and has in it a binding of the belly, though some have said that the sweetened one is neither loosening nor binding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it brings on the menses and brings out the foetuses with rue and pepper, drunk and as a pessary, and that it may be used with myrrh and honey for that {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.689}
