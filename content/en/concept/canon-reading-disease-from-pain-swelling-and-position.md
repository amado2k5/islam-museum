## Summary
Ibn Sina says pain points by its place and its kind, swelling by its substance, place and shape, and position by places and by sympathies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.

## Signs from pain
Pain points either by its place, so that pain on the right means the liver and on the left the spleen, or by its kind to its cause, as set out in the lesson on causes: heavy pain points to a swelling in an organ without feeling or whose feeling is gone, stretching pain to abundant matter, and irritating pain to sharp matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.

## Signs from swelling
Swelling points in three ways: by its substance, redness pointing to bile and hardness to black bile; by its place, one on the right pointing to the liver and one on the left to the region of the spleen; and by its shape, a crescent on the right pointing to the liver itself and an elongated one to the muscle above it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.

## Signs from position
Position points either by places, which is plain, or by sympathies, as a pain in the finger from an earlier cause is read as a defect in the sixth pair of nerves of the neck {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.
