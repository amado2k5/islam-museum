## Summary
Ibn Sina describes how to do the massage of recovery: begin with oil and with force, then ease toward moderation without stopping at its roughest, with many hands; the person tenses the rubbed organs, a swaddling band is passed over them, the breath is held, he walks and lies back; and he notes that firm massage and pressing at bedtime dries the body and keeps moisture from flowing to the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## The method
In the massage of recovery one begins first with oil and with force, then leans toward moderation and does not stop at the harshest point; best if many hands work on it. After the rubbing the person should tense the rubbed organs to shake the residues out of them: a swaddling band is taken and passed over all the regions of the organs while they are tense, and the breath is held as far as possible, especially with the abdominal muscles relaxed and the chest muscles tensed if that is easy, then at the end the abdominal muscles are tensed a little too so that the viscera also get some recovery; between these steps he walks and lies back {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## Skilled athletes
Skilled people of the exercising class hold the breath between their bouts, and sometimes bring this recovery into the middle of the exercise, stopping and taking it up again if they want to lengthen it. Someone seeking recovery who complains of nothing and does not mean to resume has no need of much rubbing; if he finds fatigue he anoints gently with oil, and if he finds dryness he lengthens the massage until the organs reach moderation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## Pressing at bedtime
Firm massage and pressing at bedtime may help, for it dries the body and keeps moisture from flowing to the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.
