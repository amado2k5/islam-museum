## Summary
Sabil is the road; ibn al-sabil is the traveller whose road has been cut off. The widest technical definition is one cut off from his property, whether he is outside his country, in it, or passing through it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1463}

## General ruling
The jurists agree that a wayfarer who wants to return home and has nothing to live on is given from zakat, booty and fay' according to his need, and nothing beyond that is lawful to him. On borrowing, the Hanafis regard it as preferable where possible; the Malikis make it obligatory if he is not poor in his own land; the Hanbalis and the Shafi'is, by the accepted view, neither oblige it nor regard it as preferable. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1463} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1464}

## Where it is discussed
The jurists treat this in the heads of zakat disbursement, in fay', and in the division of booty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1464}
