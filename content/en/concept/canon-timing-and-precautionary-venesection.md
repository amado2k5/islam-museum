## Summary
Ibn Sina explains that bleeding may be done as a precaution after a blow or a fall or when a swelling is feared to burst before it ripens, that bleeding in the early stage of a disease already fallen into thins the superfluities and runs them through the body so it is put off until ripening shows, and that no one is bled on the day the disease stirs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.

## Precaution
Those struck by a blow or a fall may be bled as a precaution so that no swelling arises in them, and one who has a swelling and is feared to have it burst before ripening is bled even if he has no need of it and there is no plethora {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.

## Before and after the disease falls
While these diseases are only feared and not yet fallen into, the allowance of bleeding in them is wider; once they have fallen, bleeding is left in their early stages, for it thins the superfluities, runs them through the body and mixes them with the sound blood, and may drain nothing of what is needed and call for ruinous repetitions. When ripening appears and the disease has gone beyond its beginning and its end, then, if bleeding is due and nothing prevents it, the patient is bled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.

## The day of movement and the crises
No one is bled or evacuated on the day the disease stirs, for it is a day of rest, of sleep, and of the illness rising. When a disease has crises during its course for some time it is not permitted to draw much blood at all; if the disease can be quieted that is done, and if not he is bled and a little blood is drawn, leaving in the body a reserve of blood for further bleedings should they arise and to keep strength to meet the crises {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.

## Winter after a long interval
If someone in winter, long since last bled, complains of a breaking in the limbs, he is bled and a reserve of blood is left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.
