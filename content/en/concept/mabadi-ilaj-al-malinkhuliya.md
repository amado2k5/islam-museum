## Acting early
Its treatment must be taken up before it settles, for it is easy at the beginning and hard once settled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.107}

## The principles
In every case the patient must be gladdened and cheered, and care for moistening him is to exceed care for warming him as far as possible; sleep is among the most fitting of their treatments, and what harm the means of bringing it on may leave is made good by what remedies it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.107}

## When it comes from an excess of cold and dryness
If melancholia comes from a bad temperament of excess cold and dryness, attention is to be given to warming the heart and to the gladdening remedies, and the head is treated as already said, as was set out in the chapter on ru'una; the strong form comes after another hot illness, and its treatment is easy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.107}

## What is left out
In the same place the book gives practical particulars (the surroundings, foods, what is avoided and what is taken, means of bringing sleep and named drugs), and they are not reproduced here; the fuller rule of treatment is in [[concept/ilaj-al-malinkhuliya-al-madawiyya]] {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.107}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are left out here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.107}
