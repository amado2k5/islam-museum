## Summary
Khul\' is lexically drawing off or stripping; for the Hanafis it is taking money from the wife in return for the marriage bond, by the word khul\'; for the majority it is separation for a consideration due by the wife, by the word talaq or khul\'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7758}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7759} It is near to reconciliation (sulh, ending a quarrel), to divorce (lifting the marriage tie), to ransom (fidya, the sum paid to be free), to mubara\'a (another name for it, in which the wife drops her claims on the husband), and to annulment (faskh, dissolving the contract). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7759}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7760} Is khul\' divorce or annulment? The Hanafis in the chosen view, the Malikis, the Shafiis in the new view and the Hanbalis in one report hold it divorce; the Shafii in the old view and the Hanbalis in the best-known report hold it annulment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7760}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7761} Those who call it divorce say it is a final single divorce, and if the husband intends more, more falls, by the Malikis, Shafiis, Hanbalis and Zufar; the Hanafis count three if he intends three. Those who call it annulment cite the verse on two divorces then the khul\', then the divorce after it, and the report of the wife of Thabit ibn Qays who was told to wait one menstrual period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7761}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7762} Results of the two views: one who has khul\'ed twice and marries her again is free to do so on the annulment view; the Hanafis and Malikis treat khul\' as an exchange on the wife\'s side and an oath on the husband\'s, the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis as an exchange on both sides. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7763} Ruling: khul\' is lawful in general; the Hanbalis divide it into three: lawful when the wife hates the marriage and fears she cannot keep its duties, when the husband is encouraged to accept; disliked without cause while the marriage is sound; forbidden when the wife is harmed to make her pay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7764}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7765} The Hanbalis divide khul\' into three: lawful when the wife hates the marriage and fears she cannot keep its duties, and the husband is encouraged to accept; disliked without cause while the marriage is sound, with two views on its validity; forbidden when the husband wrongfully withholds so she pays to be free, in which case the divorce is revocable and he takes no consideration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7766} A khul\' by other words than divorce: if it is a divorce, its rule applies; if not, the marriage stands. Beating the wife rightly for rebellion or neglect of duty does not forbid khul\'; withholding to make her pay for adultery is lawful; beating her wrongly, without seeking money, still permits khul\'; using khul\' to void an oath of divorce is invalid for the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7766} Ibn al-Mundhir requires that both parties be in conflict, and al-Tabari answers that the fear is attributed to both since the wife\'s dislike implies his; the Prophet did not question Thabit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7766}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767} The Malikis return the consideration if the wife paid to escape his harm, even if she dropped the witness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767} Taking a consideration: the Malikis and Shafiis allow the husband to take an equal, lesser or greater sum by agreement, from the wife or another; the Hanbalis hold that more than the dowry is disliked, forbidden if he withholds her to make her pay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767} Hanafis: if the rebellion is the husband\'s he should take nothing; if the wife\'s, he may; al-Qaduri says if it is hers he may not take more than he gave, though a court would enforce it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768} Khul\' without a judge: most jurists allow it, on the report of a case judged by Umar; al-Hasan al-Basri holds it requires the ruler. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768} Time: the Shafiis and Hanbalis allow it in menstruation and in the purity in which he had intercourse, since the harm of a long waiting period is less than that of ill treatment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768} Pillars: five by non-Hanafis (the one who offers, the one who accepts the obligation, the thing for which, the consideration and the wording); two by the Hanafis (offer and acceptance). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7769} The one who offers must have power to divorce; the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis allow a husband under restraint for debt, folly or servitude to khul\'; the Hanbalis allow a discerning boy in one view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7769} The one who accepts must be fully competent; khul\' for a child on her dowry is a divorce without consideration by the Hanafis and a Shafii view; a wife under restraint for folly, minority or madness cannot khul\' by Hanbali law, while one under restraint for debt may do so on a sum owed, not on a specific asset. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7770} During fatal illness: a wife in fatal illness may khul\' by agreement; the Hanafis count what the husband takes against the third; the Shafiis treat excess over the dower as a bequest; the Hanbalis allow the husband the lesser of the sum or his share of inheritance; the Malikis hold the sum until death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7770}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7771}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7771}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7772} A husband in fatal illness may khul\' with effect for the agreed sum; there is no inheritance between them by most jurists, while the Malikis have the divorced wife inherit from him if he dies of that illness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7772}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7773} A guardian: the Malikis allow a guardian to khul\' for a minor boy or a madman if it serves; the Hanafis, Shafiis and Hanbalis forbid a father to khul\' a minor son\'s wife, while one Hanbali view lets the father act like the judge; a father may not khul\' his young daughter on her property by the Hanafis, Shafiis and most Hanbalis, while the Malikis allow a compelling guardian to do so. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7772}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7773}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7773} A guardian and a minor girl: one Hanbali view lets a father khul\' from her property if he sees benefit, as to free a mad woman from one who wastes her money. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7774} Khul\' by an unauthorized agent: the Hanafis allow it if he makes the consideration his own; the Malikis allow it; the Shafiis, reasoning that khul\' is divorce, and most Hanbalis allow it; Abu Thawr forbids it, reasoning that khul\' is annulment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7774}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7775} Appointing an agent in khul\': lawful for either spouse; the Hanafis hold one person cannot be agent for both sides except in Muhammad\'s view, the Shafiis have one agent take one side, the Hanbalis and Muhammad (and one Shafii view) allow one for both. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7775}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7776} The waiting period of the woman who has khul\'ed: most jurists say it is three menstrual periods like a divorcee; Uthman, Ibn Umar and Ibn Abbas said one menstrual period, on the report of Thabit ibn Qays\' wife. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7776}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7777} The thing exchanged, the marriage itself: the wife must be in the husband\'s bond in fact; a wife already separated by a final divorce cannot be khul\'ed, while a revocable divorcee may be khul\'ed by the Malikis, the preferred Shafii view and the Hanbalis except al-Kharaqi; the Hanafis count her divorce as taking effect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7777}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7778} The consideration: it must be fit as a dowry; it may be specific or described goods, a debt, or a benefit such as nursing the child; the woman cannot be made to leave the house where she was divorced, since the lodging during the waiting period is God\'s right. An invalid consideration by ambiguity or hazard: the Hanafis allow it; the Malikis allow the unknown and the hazardous; the preferred Hanbali view allows the unknown and the future; the Shafiis forbid it and award the dowry of the like. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7778}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7779}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7779}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7780} Wording: an offer and an acceptance; the Shafiis require that they match and that no long foreign speech intervene; if the husband begins with a conditional phrase acceptance need not be spoken; a negative condition is immediate. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7780}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7781} Conditional khul\': if the wife begins it, it cannot be conditional by the Hanafis and Shafiis, since it is an exchange; if the husband begins it, it may be conditional by the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafiis, since it is an oath; the Hanbalis forbid conditioning it, on analogy with sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7781} Option in khul\': the Hanafis allow the wife to stipulate it but not the husband; Abu Yusuf and Muhammad allow neither. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7781} Wording of khul\': seven words in the Hanafi view (khala\'tuka, bayanatuka, bara\'tuka, faraqtuka, "divorce yourself for a thousand", "I sell you to yourself", "I buy yourself"), and four in the Maliki view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7781}
