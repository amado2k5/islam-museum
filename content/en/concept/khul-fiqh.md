## Summary
Khul\' is lexically drawing off or stripping; for the Hanafis it is taking money from the wife in return for the marriage bond, by the word khul\'; for the majority it is separation for a consideration due by the wife, by the word talaq or khul\'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7758}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7759} It is near to reconciliation (sulh, ending a quarrel), to divorce (lifting the marriage tie), to ransom (fidya, the sum paid to be free), to mubara\'a (another name for it, in which the wife drops her claims on the husband), and to annulment (faskh, dissolving the contract). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7759}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7760} Is khul\' divorce or annulment? The Hanafis in the chosen view, the Malikis, the Shafiis in the new view and the Hanbalis in one report hold it divorce; the Shafii in the old view and the Hanbalis in the best-known report hold it annulment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7760}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7761} Those who call it divorce say it is a final single divorce, and if the husband intends more, more falls, by the Malikis, Shafiis, Hanbalis and Zufar; the Hanafis count three if he intends three. Those who call it annulment cite the verse on two divorces then the khul\', then the divorce after it, and the report of the wife of Thabit ibn Qays who was told to wait one menstrual period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7761}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7762} Results of the two views: one who has khul\'ed twice and marries her again is free to do so on the annulment view; the Hanafis and Malikis treat khul\' as an exchange on the wife\'s side and an oath on the husband\'s, the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis as an exchange on both sides. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7763} Ruling: khul\' is lawful in general; the Hanbalis divide it into three: lawful when the wife hates the marriage and fears she cannot keep its duties, when the husband is encouraged to accept; disliked without cause while the marriage is sound; forbidden when the wife is harmed to make her pay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7764}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7765} The Hanbalis divide khul\' into three: lawful when the wife hates the marriage and fears she cannot keep its duties, and the husband is encouraged to accept; disliked without cause while the marriage is sound, with two views on its validity; forbidden when the husband wrongfully withholds so she pays to be free, in which case the divorce is revocable and he takes no consideration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7766} A khul\' by other words than divorce: if it is a divorce, its rule applies; if not, the marriage stands. Beating the wife rightly for rebellion or neglect of duty does not forbid khul\'; withholding to make her pay for adultery is lawful; beating her wrongly, without seeking money, still permits khul\'; using khul\' to void an oath of divorce is invalid for the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7766} Ibn al-Mundhir requires that both parties be in conflict, and al-Tabari answers that the fear is attributed to both since the wife\'s dislike implies his; the Prophet did not question Thabit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7766}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767} The Malikis return the consideration if the wife paid to escape his harm, even if she dropped the witness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767} Taking a consideration: the Malikis and Shafiis allow the husband to take an equal, lesser or greater sum by agreement, from the wife or another; the Hanbalis hold that more than the dowry is disliked, forbidden if he withholds her to make her pay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767} Hanafis: if the rebellion is the husband\'s he should take nothing; if the wife\'s, he may; al-Qaduri says if it is hers he may not take more than he gave, though a court would enforce it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7767}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768} Khul\' without a judge: most jurists allow it, on the report of a case judged by Umar; al-Hasan al-Basri holds it requires the ruler. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768} Time: the Shafiis and Hanbalis allow it in menstruation and in the purity in which he had intercourse, since the harm of a long waiting period is less than that of ill treatment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768} Pillars: five by non-Hanafis (the one who offers, the one who accepts the obligation, the thing for which, the consideration and the wording); two by the Hanafis (offer and acceptance). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7768}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7769} The one who offers must have power to divorce; the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis allow a husband under restraint for debt, folly or servitude to khul\'; the Hanbalis allow a discerning boy in one view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7769} The one who accepts must be fully competent; khul\' for a child on her dowry is a divorce without consideration by the Hanafis and a Shafii view; a wife under restraint for folly, minority or madness cannot khul\' by Hanbali law, while one under restraint for debt may do so on a sum owed, not on a specific asset. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7770} During fatal illness: a wife in fatal illness may khul\' by agreement; the Hanafis count what the husband takes against the third; the Shafiis treat excess over the dower as a bequest; the Hanbalis allow the husband the lesser of the sum or his share of inheritance; the Malikis hold the sum until death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7770}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7771}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7771}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7772} A husband in fatal illness may khul\' with effect for the agreed sum; there is no inheritance between them by most jurists, while the Malikis have the divorced wife inherit from him if he dies of that illness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7772}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7773} A guardian: the Malikis allow a guardian to khul\' for a minor boy or a madman if it serves; the Hanafis, Shafiis and Hanbalis forbid a father to khul\' a minor son\'s wife, while one Hanbali view lets the father act like the judge; a father may not khul\' his young daughter on her property by the Hanafis, Shafiis and most Hanbalis, while the Malikis allow a compelling guardian to do so. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7772}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7773}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7773}
