## Summary
Ibn Sina explains when the physician withholds food and when he reduces it, that reduction may be in quantity, in quality or in both together, and that the two differ: some food is large in amount yet low in nourishment, such as pulses and fruit, and some is small in amount yet highly nourishing, such as eggs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.267}.

## When to withhold and when to reduce
Food is withheld when the physician wants nature occupied with ripening the humours; it is reduced when he also aims to keep up the strength through what nourishes. In what he takes away he keeps the matter in view, so that nature is not busied with digesting much food, and he always attends to the weightier of the two, the strength if it is very weak, the disease if it is very strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.267}.

## Quantity and quality
He may need to reduce the quality and increase the quantity when appetite is strong and raw humours lie in the vessels: the appetite is calmed by filling the stomach with food of little nourishment so that what is in the vessels ripens first. He may need the reverse when he wants to strengthen the power and the nature in charge of the stomach is too weak to digest much {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.267}.
