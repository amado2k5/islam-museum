## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the second group of the anatomy of simple organs, the muscles, in thirty chapters, with a general discourse on nerve, muscle, tendon and ligament {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.60}.

## How the limbs are moved
Voluntary movement comes to the limbs by a power flowing to them from the brain through the nerve. Because the nerve is delicate and does not join well to the hard bones, the Creator grew from the bones something like nerve, called the sinew and ligament, and joined and interlaced it with the nerve as one thing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.60}. Since the substance joined of nerve and ligament is fine and could not move the limbs, he gave it thickness by loosening its substance into fibres, filling the gaps with flesh, wrapping it in a membrane, and setting in its middle a pillar of nerve like an axis. This organ, made of nerve, sinew, their fibres, the filling flesh and the covering membrane, is the muscle. When it contracts it draws the tendon made of ligament and so draws the limb; when it relaxes the tendon slackens and the limb moves away {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.60-61}.
