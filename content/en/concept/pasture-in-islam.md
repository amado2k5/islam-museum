## Summary
Pasture (kala') is vegetation that grows on its own, whether green or dry, and it may be on public land, private land, or state land {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}. It is freely accessible (mubah) and cannot be owned except in a manner the author specifies for private land {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}.

## Public Land
Pasture on public land is shared, and no one may monopolize it to the exclusion of others—neither through a private reserve nor by subterfuge, such as withholding surplus water to monopolize the pasture for one's own livestock, or selling surplus water as a roundabout way of charging for the freely accessible pasture, since "what is freely accessible has no price" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}.

## State Reserves
Pasture on state land may function as a public reserve (hima), where cavalry horses for jihad and charity livestock graze until distributed, and where a poor person owning a few camels or sheep may also graze {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}. Details on the public reserve appear elsewhere in the book {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224} (see [[concept/hima|Hima]]).

## Private Land
If a landowner cultivates vegetation through their own effort, it is indisputably their property; if it grows on its own, does the landowner own it by virtue of owning the land? {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}. There are three legal positions on this: one holds that the owner does not own it and it remains freely accessible, and must grant access to anyone who requests it without charge, though some jurists exempted residents of borderland outposts (ahl al-thughur) so that if they relocated, the frontiers would not be left exposed, and thus granted them ownership of it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 222}; a third view grants ownership if the land is fenced, because enclosure is akin to taking possession {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 222}.

## Ownerless Land
Pasture on ownerless (mubah) land belongs to no one, but whoever gathers and takes possession of it owns it—which accords with what jurists have established regarding acquisition by possession {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}.
