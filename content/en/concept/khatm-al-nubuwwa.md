## Summary
Al-Tahawi says: Muhammad is "His chosen servant, His elect prophet, and His pleasing messenger" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 12}, and he is "the seal of the prophets, the imam of the pious, the master of the messengers, and the beloved of the Lord of the worlds" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 12}.

## The ruling on claiming prophethood after him
"Every claim to prophethood after him is delusion and desire" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 12}. He is, for al-Tahawi, "the one sent to all the jinn and all mankind" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 12}.

## Belief in the prophets and the books
Al-Tahawi believes in the angels, the prophets and the books sent down upon the messengers, and testifies they are upon clear truth {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 20}, "we make no distinction between any of His messengers" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 22}. Of the awliya he says none of them is preferred over a prophet, and a single prophet is better than all the awliya {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 30} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 31}.

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama says: "Muhammad is God's messenger, the seal of the prophets and the master of the messengers; a servant's faith is not valid until he believes in his message and testifies to his prophethood" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 35}; his umma is the best of nations and his companions the best of the prophets' companions {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 36}.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the position of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya and that of Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad alone — each attributed to its book. The sayings of the Ash'aris, Maturidis and the remaining Sunni and non-Sunni schools from their own books have not yet been added; the entry needs specialist review before promotion.
