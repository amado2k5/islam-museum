## Summary
Al-Battani teaches how to find a place's latitude by observing one of the never-setting stars: take its greatest altitude as it crosses the midheaven line above the pole, then wait until it crosses the same line below the pole and take its lowest altitude; adding half the difference of the two altitudes to the smaller, or taking half their sum, gives the altitude of the north pole, which is the place's latitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.26}.

## The two observations
He says: observe a star in the circle centred on the northern pole of the equator, drawn at the pole's altitude from the horizon; take its highest altitude as it passes the midheaven line above the pole, between the zenith and the pole; wait until it is on that line below the pole, between the pole and the horizon, where it is lowest, and take its altitude then. Subtract the smaller from the larger and add half the excess to the smaller altitude, and the result is the altitude of the north pole. Or add the two altitudes and take half the sum: that is the pole's altitude and the place's latitude in the north. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.26}.
