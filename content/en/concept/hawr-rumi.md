## Nature
Hawr rumi, which the book also calls al-turus. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.487}
The book says the gum of the Rumi tree is kahraba (amber), which it treats in its own entry. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}

## Temperament
The book first gives it as hot in the second degree and dry in the first; its flower heats more, and its gum heats strongly. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.487}
Elsewhere it is given as moderate, inclining to dryness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}

## Actions
It is fine in action, its seed is finer, and it is not very hot. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}

## Uses by organ
Organs of the head: its fruit with vinegar helps epilepsy. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.487}
Organs of the head: its leaf juice is dropped into the ear, which soothes the pain. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Organs of the eye: its fruit with honey is used as kohl, which the book says strengthens the eye. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Joints: its fruit helps sciatica, and its leaf with vinegar is a poultice for gout; the measured quantity is left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Excretory organs: its fruit helps difficult urination; the quantity is left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Labelled listing, set aside for the founder's ruling: the book says its fruit with vinegar after purification, and likewise its leaf, prevents conception. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
