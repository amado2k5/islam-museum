## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that God is free of likeness to His creatures in essence, attributes and acts, and this includes freedom from offspring. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.547}

## The argument from need
A son, Sha'rawi says, is taken to keep a kind alive, for help or for security of life. God is living from eternity and needs none of these, and the world did not change with the alleged son, so God gains no perfection from it. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.547} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.548}

## A creature or a god
A son would be either a creature, and so not a god, or a god, and then there would be several gods. Since God owns all in the heavens and the earth, a son of the same kind cannot be His, for the maker does not produce something of his own kind. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.547} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.548}
