## Nature and selection
Temperament: hot and moist in the first degree, with the moisture higher in degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}

## Actions
Actions: it ripens, dissolves and softens; it dissolves best in moderate bodies, less in hard ones, and easily in soft ones. Its smoke dries and gently astringes, and it calms the pains of humours flowing into the organs. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: smeared on the body it nourishes and fattens. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}
Wounds and ulcers: it helps nerve wounds, fills ulcers and cleans them. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}
Head organs: mixed into remedies for wounds of the membranes of the brain, for swellings at the roots of the ears, the lobes and the mouth, for swelling of the gum and for thrush; smeared on the gums of children, it eases the coming of teeth. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}
Excretory organs: it helps dry cold cough, especially with almonds and sugar, and likewise pleurisy and pneumonia; it eases expectoration and ripens; with almond oil its ripening is greater; alone its cleansing is less than its ripening, and with sugar it is the opposite; it prevents spitting of blood, and helps the vomiting of pus when licked with honey. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}
Excretory organs: it is a mild laxative, and much of it purges; it is given as an enema for hot swellings in remedies for abscesses at the neck of the bladder. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}
Poisons: it counters poisons, and when applied it helps viper bites. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.462}
