## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat contrasts "natural activity," which is based on production, with "unnatural activity," which in his view results from a flaw in the behavior of ordinary individuals or of those who steer and manage production {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 83}. He considers the existence of an unproductive class in society to be expected, because a group stands by the wayside watching farmers, livestock breeders, factory producers, and sea workers, and will inevitably try to extort their efforts {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 83}.

## Intermediation and Extortion
This group extorts the efforts of producers through methods that appear honorable on the surface, such as management, marketing, and intermediation between producer and consumer, and those methods then shift from their ostensible service aims to extortionate ones—or, when not honorable, through fraud and theft {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 83}. In his view, the greatest danger in this is that in the long run it turns productive activity into service activity that cannot ward off hunger, and paves the way for conflict between the forces of production and the forces of exploitation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 83}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 84}.

## External Exploitation
The author discusses the exploitation of land and raw materials in colonized countries, arguing that the companies that seized raw materials paid the landowners nothing but poverty, hunger, and disease, and turned human beings from producers into consumers {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 84}. He adds that competition with local production is unequal, because the exploiters possess capital, numbers, materials, and partners {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 84}.

## Oil and Monopolies
The author considers the exploitation of oil by foreign companies a distortion that transformed Arab and Islamic societies into consumer service societies whose goal is making money rather than producing {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 85}. He describes Arab and Islamic lands as having become the focus of capitalist monopolies, with the price of raw materials going to financial centers in capitalist states or into the pockets of employees on fixed salaries {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 85}. This is the author's assessment and opinion, and the book does not present data in these pages to support it.

## Exploiting Needs
The author moves from production to needs: consumption is unavoidable even in the absence of natural production, so importation occurs, which enlarges the unnatural class and increases exploitation until exploitation binds the state in a manner akin to colonialism {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 85}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}. He states that what seems minor in individual estimation leads to ruin for the individual, the community, and the state {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}.

## Its Place in the Book
The author reads this activity in contrast to the natural activity that Islam prepares, and links it to inflation and hoarding in the following chapters {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}.
