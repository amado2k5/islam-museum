## Overview
Ibn Rajab defines hypocrisy (*nifaq*), in the forty-eighth hadith of his additions to the Forty, by its practical signs: "Four things, whoever has them is a hypocrite, and whoever has one of them has a trait of hypocrisy until he leaves it." These are lying in speech, breaking a promise, treachery in disputes, and betrayal of a covenant. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.901}

## Full hypocrisy and a single trait of it
The commentary explains that the gathering of the four traits is full hypocrisy, while the presence of one of them is a trait of hypocrisy that does not by itself amount to unbelief. In the version of Abu Hurayra: "even if he fasts and prays and claims that he is a Muslim." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.901}

## Disputed interpretations
Ibn Rajab mentions an interpretation by someone inclined to the doctrine of postponement (*irja'*), who took the hadith to refer only to the hypocrites of the Prophet's time, through a report from Muhammad al-Muharram from Ata' from Jabir. Ibn Rajab rejects it, saying that this report is false, and that al-Muharram is known for lying. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.901}

Within its chapter, it is part of [[concept/iman|faith]] in its relation to deeds.
