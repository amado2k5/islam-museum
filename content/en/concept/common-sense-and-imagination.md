## Summary
Among the five powers of inner perception in Ibn Sina is the one called common sense and imagination, which physicians count as one power and the philosophers who verify it as two, and whose seat is the front ventricle of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.100}.

## What it is
The inwardly perceiving power, the animal one, is like a genus of five powers. One is the power called common sense and imagination, which for physicians is one power and for the verifying philosophers two. Common sense is that to which all the sensibles are carried, which is affected by their forms and in which they gather; imagination is that which keeps them after the gathering and holds them once they are absent from sense, and the receiving power of the two is not the retaining one. The truth of this is for the philosopher; either way, their seat and the origin of their acts is the front ventricle of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.100}.
