## Summary
When readers multiplied and spread through the lands, precision weakened and falsehood began to blur into truth, so the scholars of the readings drew the line between the well-known and the irregular by principles they laid down and pillars they spelled out. Ibn al-Jazari set this criterion out at the opening of al-Nashr, and al-Suyuti carries it into the Itqan. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 19} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 491}

## The three pillars
Every reading that agrees with Arabic in at least one way, agrees with one of the Uthmanic codices even potentially, and has a sound chain is the sound reading that may not be rejected or denied; it belongs to the seven ahruf on which the Qur'an was revealed, whether it comes from the seven imams, the ten, or other accepted imams. Whenever one of the three pillars fails, it is called weak, irregular or invalid, even if it comes from the seven. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 19} Ibn al-Jazari says this is the view of the verifiers early and late and names al-Dani, Makki ibn Abi Talib, al-Mahdawi and Abu Shama. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 492}

## What each pillar means
**Arabic, in at least one way.** Any grammatical way, eloquent or more eloquent, agreed or disputed within tolerable limits, provided the reading was widespread and the imams received it by a sound chain. Many readings were denied by some or many grammarians and their denial was not counted, such as a lightening sukun on some letters and a genitive on a conjoined noun in [[passage/quran-4-1|al-Nisa' 4:1]]. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 493} Al-Suyuti quotes al-Dani that the imams of the readers do not follow what is commonest in the language or most analogical in Arabic but what is best established in the report and soundest in transmission; recitation is a followed practice, and he reports from [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]] the saying 'the reading is a followed sunna'. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 494}

**The codex, even potentially.** Agreement means being present in some codices and not others, such as [[person/abd-allah-ibn-amir|Ibn Amir's]] reading at [[passage/quran-2-116|al-Baqara 2:116]] without the conjunction, which the Syrian codex lacks, and [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir's]] reading at [[passage/quran-9-100|al-Tawba 9:100]] with the added preposition, which the Meccan codex has. A reading in none of the codices is irregular because it breaks the agreed script. Potential agreement means the script allows it: the first word of [[passage/quran-1-4|al-Fatiha 4]] was written without an alif in all codices, so the reading without the alif agrees in fact and the reading with it agrees by assumption. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 495} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 496}

**A sound chain.** The reading is related by an upright, precise narrator from his like to its source, and is well known among the masters of the field. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 22} Whether that suffices or mass transmission is required is a separate matter: [[question/is-tawatur-a-condition-of-a-valid-reading|Must a reading be mass-transmitted?]]

## How far agreement with the script goes
A difference in a letter that is assimilated, substituted, kept or dropped does not count as departing from the script if the reading is established and widely known, as with sad and sin, or the extra ya's; such differences are close and return to one meaning. Adding, dropping, advancing or delaying a word, even a particle of meaning, is judged as a word and may not depart from the script; for Ibn al-Jazari this is the dividing line in following or breaking the script. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 22} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 497}

## What follows
A reading's soundness is therefore not measured by whether it is attributed to the seven: what is attributed to each reader, of the seven and others, divides into the agreed and the irregular, though the heart settles more on what is reported from the seven because of their fame. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 492} See [[concept/al-qiraat-al-shadhdha|irregular readings]] and [[concept/al-qiraat-al-ashr|the ten readings]].

## The relation of the ten readings to the seven ahruf
The research summary states that the ten qira'at are not identical to the seven ahruf but later systematised transmissions that fall within the ahruf surviving after the final review and the Uthmanic standardisation; that their differences are all compatible with the Uthmanic rasm and with Arabic, the very conditions this page sets out; and that Ibn al-Jazari's al-Nashr is the decisive reference for their chains and general rules {cite:research-summary-qiraat-dialects-quran-2026 on seven ahruf and dialects}. Examples of these differences in pronunciation, lengthening, assimilation, softening of the hamza, imala and a limited number of word variants are set out in [[concept/al-qiraat-al-ashr|The ten readings]], and their link to the tribal dialects in [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|The seven ahruf]].
