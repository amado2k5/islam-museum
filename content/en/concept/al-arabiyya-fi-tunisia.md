## Summary
This page sets out the place of [[language/arabic|Arabic]] in [[place/tunisia|Tunisia]] as it appears in the Languages section of the Encyclopaedia Britannica article on Tunisia and in the Tunisia page of The World Factbook. It ascribes each statement to its page and estimates no number of speakers itself.

## Official status
The encyclopedia says Arabic is the official language, that most natives speak a dialect of Tunisian Arabic, and that Modern Standard Arabic is taught in schools {cite:britannica-tunisia-languages}. The Factbook says: Arabic (official, one of the languages of commerce), French (commerce), Tamazight {cite:cia-factbook-tunisia}. In its article on the Semitic languages the encyclopedia lists Tunisia among the places where Arabic is official {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. The sources agree that Arabic is official, and only the Factbook calls it one of the languages of commerce.

## The standard and the spoken
On the standard and the spoken the encyclopedia says no more than that Modern Standard Arabic is taught in schools and that a Tunisian dialect is what most people speak; it gives no features of the dialect and no relation to the neighbours' dialects, and the museum adds none. For the duality in general see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Fusha and the dialects]]. French, the encyclopedia says, was introduced during the protectorate of 1881 to 1956 and came into wider use only after independence because of the spread of education; it continues to play an important role in the press, education and government, and English and Italian serve as lingua francas to a lesser extent {cite:britannica-tunisia-languages}. The Factbook adds in a note that despite having no official status French plays a major role in the country and is spoken by about two thirds of the population {cite:cia-factbook-tunisia}. That share does not appear in the encyclopedia.

## Other languages in the country
The encyclopedia says a tiny fraction of the population, most of them in the south, still speak one of the Berber languages {cite:britannica-tunisia-languages}. The Factbook lists Tamazight among the languages without calling it official or giving a share, and gives the ethnic groups as Arab 98 percent, European 1 percent, and Jewish and other 1 percent {cite:cia-factbook-tunisia}. Neither page names other languages. Compare [[concept/lughat-al-qabail|the languages of the tribes]].

## From the history of the language
The encyclopedia says the cultural Arabization of the country was largely completed by the end of the 12th century {cite:britannica-tunisia-languages}. For the region generally it says that in the 7th and 8th centuries the Arabs imposed on the native peoples of the Maghrib the religion of Islam and Arabic, the language of the Qur'an {cite:britannica-maghrib}. These are the encyclopedia's statements, and the museum adds no account of the stages. For Tunisia among the Arab states see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab states and the League]].
