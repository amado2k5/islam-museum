## Summary
In the jurists' usage tasmiya means saying "in the name of God", giving a proper name to a child, fixing the consideration in a contract, or identifying by name as against leaving a thing vague. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5098} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5112} The father has the better right to name a child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5099}

## General ruling
The schools differ on when a child is named: the Malikis say on the seventh day after the aqiqa, the Shafi'is recommend that day, and the Hanbalis report two views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5099} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5100} Naming after prophets is allowed according to most jurists. Abdullah and Abd al-Rahman are recommended. Ill-omened and ugly names are disliked; names belonging to God alone, and names built on servitude to anything other than God, are forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5102} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5104} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5106} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5107}

## Where it is discussed
Its rules appear in the sections on slaughter and the aqiqa, and on marriage; the use of the word for fixing a consideration is treated under hire and dower. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5098} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5112}

## Good names in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lesson
The Sheikh says the Prophet changed the names of many Companions whose names had bad meanings, and that a father should choose a good name for his son. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:04:30}
He recounts the story of a man called Hazn who refused to change the name his father had given him, and says that the grief never left his house afterwards (needs_source: the name and details are not clear in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:05:30}
He says a person will be called on the Day of Resurrection by his name and his father's name, so the choice should be made well. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:06:00}

## In al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar
Just before the Book of Names al-Nawawi gives a chapter on the call to prayer in a newborn's ear. From the Sunan of Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, from Abu Rafi', he reports that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] gave the call in the ear of al-Husayn ibn Ali when Fatima bore him; he quotes al-Tirmidhi's grading of it as good and sound, and reports from a group of his colleagues that the call is given in the right ear and the iqama in the left {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 286}. A chapter follows on supplication at the tahnik of a child: from Sunan Abi Dawud, from Aisha, by a chain he calls sound, that infants were brought to him and he would pray for them and rub their palates with a date; and from the two Sahihs, from Asma bint Abi Bakr and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, the prayer for blessing on the newborn {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 286}. He then opens the Book of Names with the chapter on naming the newborn, saying the practice is to name on the seventh day or on the day of birth {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 286}. For the seventh day he cites Amr ibn Shu'ayb's report in al-Tirmidhi's book and Samura ibn Jundub's in the Sunan, that every boy is held in pledge for his aqiqa, slaughtered for him on his seventh day, when his head is shaved and he is named; al-Tirmidhi graded it good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 287}. For the first day he cites Anas's report in Sahih Muslim on the naming of Ibrahim {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 287}. He recommends naming a miscarried child {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 287} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 288}. In the chapter on choosing a good name he gives, from Sunan Abi Dawud, from Abu al-Darda, by a chain he calls strong, the instruction to make names good; and in the chapter on the names dearest to God, from Sahih Muslim, from Ibn Umar, that they are Abd Allah and Abd al-Rahman {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 288}.
