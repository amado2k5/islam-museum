## Summary
In the jurists' usage tasmiya means saying "in the name of God", giving a proper name to a child, fixing the consideration in a contract, or identifying by name as against leaving a thing vague. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5098} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5112} The father has the better right to name a child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5099}

## General ruling
The schools differ on when a child is named: the Malikis say on the seventh day after the aqiqa, the Shafi'is recommend that day, and the Hanbalis report two views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5099} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5100} Naming after prophets is allowed according to most jurists. Abdullah and Abd al-Rahman are recommended. Ill-omened and ugly names are disliked; names belonging to God alone, and names built on servitude to anything other than God, are forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5102} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5104} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5106} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5107}

## Where it is discussed
Its rules appear in the sections on slaughter and the aqiqa, and on marriage; the use of the word for fixing a consideration is treated under hire and dower. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5098} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5112}
