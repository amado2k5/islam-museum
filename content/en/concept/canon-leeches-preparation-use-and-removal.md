## Summary
Ibn Sina explains how leeches are prepared before use, how they are encouraged to attach and made to fall off once full, what is done to stop the blood after they fall, and the skin diseases for which they help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## Preparation
Leeches should be caught a day before use and made to vomit by turning them over until what is in their bellies comes out, if possible. A little blood, from a lamb or another animal, is then poured for them to feed on before they are set. They are taken and cleaned of their sliminess and dirt with something like a sponge, and the place where they will be set is washed with borax and reddened by rubbing. The leeches are then put into sweet water, cleaned and set {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## Making them fall
When they are full and are to be removed, a little salt, ash, borax, the ash of burnt linen rags, burnt sponge or burnt wool is sprinkled on them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## After they fall
The right course after they fall is to draw on the place with a cupping glass, so that some of its blood is taken and the harm of their bite and sting leaves with it. If the blood does not stop, burnt gall-nut, lime, ash, finely powdered pottery or other blood-stopping drugs are sprinkled on it, and these must be ready at hand with the one who applies the leeches {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.

## Their uses
The use of leeches is good in skin diseases such as favus, ringworm, sunburn freckles and spots, and the like {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.302}.
