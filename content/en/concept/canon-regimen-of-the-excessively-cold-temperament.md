## Summary
In the second chapter Ibn Sina says the colder-than-balanced fall into three kinds; one who is balanced in the passive qualities aims to rouse his heat with warm foods moderate in moisture and dryness, warming oils, the great electuaries, evacuations specific to the moistures, sweating baths and suitable exercise; one with dryness besides has exactly the regimen of the old {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.

## The three kinds
These are of three kinds. The one balanced in the passive qualities aims to rouse his heat with warm foods moderate in moisture and dryness, warming oils, the great electuaries, evacuations specific to the moistures, sweating baths and suitable exercise, for even if moderate in moisture for a time, they are liable to moistures arising in them because of the cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.

## With dryness
As for those who have dryness besides, their regimen is exactly that of the old. In this passage of the printing some words are garbled before the reference to the regimen of the old; only the evident sense was taken, and the other two kinds are left as the text has them, mixed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.
