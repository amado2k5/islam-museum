## Nature
A plant whose root resembles su'd and is very bitter, and it is at times called the many-jointed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Actions and properties
Its astringency is greater than that of badhawurd, especially in its bark and root, and so it is stronger than it in everything {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Organs of the head
Its decoction is beneficial when gargled for toothache, and it and its root benefit swelling of the uvula {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.677}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits the stomach and the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.677}

## Excretory organs
The decoction of its root is used as a suppository and as a sitting bath for swellings of the anus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.677}

## Fevers
Beneficial for old fevers, especially for boys {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.677}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that the decoction of its root holds back the bleeding of women {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.677}
