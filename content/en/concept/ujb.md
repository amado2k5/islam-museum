## Overview
Self-admiration ('ujb) is a person's esteem for the deeds, knowledge or opinion he has, and his seeing the excellence as belonging to himself. Al-Sallabi presents it in the context of 'Ali's words on the faults that spoil deeds {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 262}.

## Ali's saying
Ali ibn Abi Talib said: "Self-admiration is the affliction of minds". The book establishes that it is among the faults that spoil deeds and destroy servants, a disease that is incompatible with sincerity and opposed to it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 262}.

## Its causes and treatment
Al-Sallabi sets out the views of scholars. Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak said that self-admiration is that you see in yourself something that others do not have, and Ibn Taymiyya distinguished between showing off and self-admiration in the words "You alone we worship" and "You alone we ask for help" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 263}. He reports that al-Ghazali counted self-admiration as a root of arrogance and self-deception, and that al-Qarafi saw in its prohibition a lapse of manners towards God {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 263}. The book gives two causes: ignorance of God's right and of esteeming Him as He deserves, and heedlessness of the reality of the self and its faults {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 263}. It cites al-Shafi'i, al-Nawawi, Ibn al-Qayyim and Ibn al-Jawzi on its treatment through remembering God's favour and one's shortcomings and through self-examination, including Ibn Hazm's words on searching the self for its faults {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 264}.

## Ibn al-Qayyim's wisdom
The book relates from Ibn al-Qayyim that when God wills good for His servant, He makes him forget the sight of his acts of obedience and places his sin before his eyes, which is mercy for him; whereas a good deed for which the servant boasts may be the cause of his ruin {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 265}.

## Reading the book
Al-Sallabi presents this material as an educational and doctrinal warning, and relies on the sayings of scholars without adding a new ruling {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 265}. (See [[concept/riya]].)

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh recounts the story of a teacher in Basra who had five hundred students attend his lesson, and who feared that self-admiration would enter him (needs_source: the details of the account) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:31:30}. The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that obedience may turn into admiration of one's deeds, that deeds carry their own danger, and that a servant's hope in God's pardon for his sins may exceed his hope in his deeds {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 00:51:30}.

He says that rejoicing in good deeds is right, since God granted one the means to them, while one guards against pride (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 01:03:30}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy explains Ibn Ata Allah's maxim that a sin that leaves humility and brokenness is better than an act of obedience that leaves pride and arrogance, and says that for a servant to sleep through the night and wake remorseful is better than to stand in prayer all night and wake pleased with himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep08 00:04:30} He says the servant should rejoice in obedience not because it came from him but because God made him fit for it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep08 00:05:00}
