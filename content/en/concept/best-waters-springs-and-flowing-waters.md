## Summary
Ibn Sina says waters differ not in the substance of wateriness but by what mixes with them and by the qualities that prevail in them. The best are spring waters, not every spring but the springs of free earth, whose soil is not dominated by any foreign quality {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.135}.

## Running water and its bed
Not every free spring either, but one that flows, and not every flowing one but one open to the sun and the winds. Standing water may become bad by being uncovered. Waters whose course is clay are better than those running over stones, because clay purifies water and takes the foreign admixtures from it, provided the clay is free and not mud or salt marsh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.135}.

## Direction
The best runs eastward, especially toward the summer sunrise, above all when far from its source, then what runs northward; what runs westward or southward is bad, especially when the south wind blows. Water that descends from high places is better {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.135}.
