## Temperament
The red is the hottest. Ibn Masawayh and Archigenes said it is cold and dry; in the author's view its substance is dry with a superfluous moisture and it leans to heat, the red being hotter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Actions and properties
It is quicker than the mung bean to digest and to pass, and no less nourishing. Some say it is less windy, which is open to doubt; the sounder view is that it is windier than the mung bean, though the broad bean is windier still. Its humour is moist and phlegmy, and it shows bad dreams {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Chest and breathing
Good for the chest and the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.544}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It breeds a thick humour; mustard prevents its harm, as do vinegar with salt, pepper and thyme, and a strong wine drunk after it. Preserved in vinegar it has little moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.544}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on the menses, especially the red, and especially with spikenard oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.544}.
