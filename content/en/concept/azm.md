## Summary
In the lexicon, azm is the resolve of the heart to do a thing; it also means patience and perseverance. As a technical term it is an inclination to a thing with a settled intention to do it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11595}.

## Hamm and azm
Hamm is the first stage of azm, and azm is the end point of hamm {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11596}.

## Reward and punishment for resolve
A person is not punished for evil thoughts that he neither acts on nor speaks; one who resolves on an evil and does not do it incurs no penalty, while a resolve on a good act is recorded as a good deed {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11596} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11597}. Many scholars hold that a firm resolve to commit a sin is itself punished {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11597}.

## Resolve in repentance
A firm resolve never to return to the sin is a condition for valid repentance {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11598}.

## Where to look further
Questions of resolve for a wide-time obligation or abstaining from the forbidden are treated in the legal theory appendix {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11597} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11598}. Intention is treated under «niyya» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11596}.
