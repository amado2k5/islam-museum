## Summary
Hayawan (animal) in the Quran is the life that does not end in death; technically it is a growing, sensing body that moves by will, distinguished from dabba (a creature that crawls), bahima (a beast without discernment) and ni'am (grazing livestock). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7502} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7503} Eating is lawful for animals in general, except pigs by consensus; the majority forbid beasts with fangs and birds with talons, while a Maliki view allows every animal except humans and pigs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7503} Lawful animals must be slaughtered properly, fish and locusts need no slaughter; zakat is due on camels, cattle and sheep only; and the owner must feed and water his beasts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7503} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7504}  Most jurists hold a rider liable for what the beast tramples with its hoof or foot but not for what it kicks with its tail or leg; a beast injured is compensated, and Umar fixed a quarter of its value for an injured eye; other rulings on animals are placed under sale, salam, pre-emption, hire, found property and hunting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7505}
