## Overview
Qira'at is the science of transmitting the recitation of the Qur'an through the readings transmitted from the Prophet and his Companions; al-Suyuti counts it among the types of Qur'anic sciences in the Itqan {cite:itqan-suyuti}.

## In the sources
The Itqan gives the readings their own type among the Qur'anic sciences, naming the readings of the Prophet, the transmitters and the memorizers under the isnad section {cite:itqan-suyuti}.

## Definitions
Ahmad Khalid Babakr gathers definitions of the readings: for [[person/badr-al-din-al-zarkashi|al-Zarkashi]] they are the variation of the words of revelation in the writing of letters or their manner, such as lightening and doubling; for [[person/ibn-al-jazari|Ibn al-Jazari]], the knowledge of how the words of the Qur'an are delivered and how they vary, each attributed to its transmitter; for al-Zurqani, a way of pronouncing the Qur'an taken by one of the imams of the readers, differing from others. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.88}

## A followed practice
The thesis holds that reading is a mass-transmitted practice (sunna) handed on by the Companions from the Prophet, then by the Successors, generation after generation with connected chains, and that scholars of the readings studied its chains and criticised its transmitters as the hadith scholars did. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.89} It argues that the Companions' differences in delivery all went back to hearing the Prophet or his approval, not to opinion or a preference for a linguistic form — as when [[person/ubayy-ibn-kab|Ubayy ibn Ka'b]] heard two men read Surat al-Nahl differently from him and the Prophet approved each. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.89} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.90} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.91} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.92} It cites Ibn al-Jazari that the imams of the readings act not on what is most widespread in the language or most regular in grammar but on what is best established in transmission, and [[person/ibn-mujahid|Ibn Mujahid]] that the reading followed in Medina, Mecca, Kufa, Basra and Syria is what people received from their predecessors, upheld in each city by a man who learned from the Successors — together with the saying, reported from Umar, Zayd ibn Thabit, Urwa, Ibn al-Munkadir, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and al-Sha'bi, that "reading is a sunna". {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.92} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.93}

## In al-Tabari
In his lost book on the readings, as Makki quotes it, [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]] held that no one reading a sound reading in agreement with the script of the codex is to be faulted; from Jami' al-Bayan the thesis concludes that his judgements rest on the script, the agreement of most readers and the most eloquent Arabic; see [[question/did-al-tabari-reject-established-readings|Did al-Tabari reject established readings?]]. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1364} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1381}

## The pillars of a valid reading in Ibn al-Jazari
In [[work/al-nashr-fi-al-qiraat-al-ashr|al-Nashr]], [[person/ibn-al-jazari|Ibn al-Jazari]] sets a criterion for an accepted reading: every reading that agrees with Arabic in at least one way, agrees with one of the Uthmanic copies even potentially, and has a sound chain is valid and may not be rejected, whether from the seven, the ten or others; if any pillar fails it is weak, anomalous or void, even if from the seven. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p.19} He holds that what matters is meeting these conditions, not the reader to whom a reading is ascribed, since what is ascribed to each of the seven and others divides into the agreed and the anomalous. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p.20} See: [[question/is-tawatur-a-condition-of-a-valid-reading|Must a valid reading be mass-transmitted?]] Among the best-known books on the seven readings is Abu Amr al-Dani's [[work/al-taysir-fi-al-qiraat-al-sab|al-Taysir]]. {cite:taysir-pretzl-istanbul-1930 p.10}
