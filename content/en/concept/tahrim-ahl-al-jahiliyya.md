## Summary
This record gathers what the people of the Jahiliyya forbade and permitted without God's leave, as the verses and reports relate it. The Qur'an names the bahira, the sa'iba, the wasila and the hami and says God appointed none of them [[passage/quran-5-103|al-Ma'ida 103]] {cite:tanzil-quran}; it describes a share of crops and cattle they assigned to God and a share to their partners [[passage/quran-6-136|al-An'am 136]] {cite:tanzil-quran}, and cattle and crops they put off limits to whomever they chose [[passage/quran-6-138|al-An'am 138]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. Ibn Abbas reports that the people of the Jahiliyya ate some things and left others out of disgust, that God then sent His Prophet and sent down His book and made plain what is lawful and unlawful, and that what He was silent about is pardoned [[passage/abudawud-api-3800|Sunan Abi Dawud 3800]] {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3800}. The Qur'an links the postponing of the sacred month to making lawful what God forbade [[passage/quran-9-37|al-Tawba 37]] {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an names four kinds of cattle, the bahira, the sa'iba, the wasila and the hami, and says God appointed none of them and that those who disbelieve invent a lie against Him, most of them not using reason [[passage/quran-5-103|al-Ma'ida 103]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. In al-An'am it says they assigned a portion of crops and cattle to God and a portion to their partners, that what was for their partners does not reach God while what was for God reaches their partners, and it counts this judgement an evil one [[passage/quran-6-136|al-An'am 136]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. It relates their saying that these cattle and crops are off limits and none may eat of them save whom they please, by their claim, that some cattle have their backs forbidden and over some they do not mention God's name, and it calls this an invention against Him [[passage/quran-6-138|al-An'am 138]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. It relates their saying that what is in the bellies of such cattle is reserved for their males and forbidden to their wives, but if it is born dead they all share in it, and says He will repay them for their description [[passage/quran-6-139|al-An'am 139]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. It says that those who killed their children foolishly and without knowledge, and forbade what God had provided, inventing a lie against Him, are losers and have gone astray [[passage/quran-6-140|al-An'am 140]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. The next two verses ask about the eight pairs of sheep, goats, camels and oxen: did God forbid the two males, or the two females, or what the wombs of the females hold, and were they witnesses when God charged them with this [[passage/quran-6-143|al-An'am 143]] {cite:tanzil-quran} [[passage/quran-6-144|al-An'am 144]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. It records the idolaters saying that had God willed, neither they nor their fathers would have ascribed partners or forbidden anything, says those before them said the same, asks them for knowledge to bring forward, and says they follow only conjecture [[passage/quran-6-148|al-An'am 148]] {cite:tanzil-quran}; the same saying appears in al-Nahl, with the remark that the messengers are charged only with plain conveyance [[passage/quran-16-35|al-Nahl 35]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. It asks them to bring witnesses who testify that God forbade this [[passage/quran-6-150|al-An'am 150]] {cite:tanzil-quran}, and asks about the provision God sent down, of which they made some unlawful and some lawful: did God give them leave, or do they invent a lie about Him [[passage/quran-10-59|Yunus 59]] {cite:tanzil-quran}. As for the postponing of the sacred month, it calls it an increase in disbelief: they allow it one year and forbid it another, to match the number of months God made sacred, and so allow what God forbade [[passage/quran-9-37|al-Tawba 37]] {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the Sunnah
A report in Muslim has the Prophet say that his Lord commanded him to teach them what they did not know, and that God says every property He gave a servant is lawful, that He created all His servants upright, and that the devils came to them and turned them from their religion, made unlawful to them what He had made lawful for them, and commanded them to associate others with Him [[passage/muslim-2865|Sahih Muslim 2865]] {cite:sahih-muslim 2865}. In another report in Muslim the Prophet says that their blood, their property and their honour are unlawful to one another like the sanctity of that day in that month in that land, and tells the one present to convey it to the one absent [[passage/muslim-api-4384|Sahih Muslim 4384]] {cite:sahih-muslim 4384}. Abu Hurayra reports that the Prophet said he saw Amr ibn Amir ibn Luhayy al-Khuza'i dragging his entrails in the Fire, and that he was the first to set the sa'ibas loose [[passage/bukhari-api-3521|Sahih al-Bukhari 3521]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3521}.

## In the reports of the Companions, the Successors and the scholars
In Sahih al-Bukhari, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab says the bahira is the one whose milk is withheld for the tawaghit so that no one milks it, and the sa'iba is the one they let loose for their gods so that nothing is loaded on it [[passage/bukhari-api-3521|Sahih al-Bukhari 3521]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3521}. Abu Dawud relates from Ibn Abbas that the people of the Jahiliyya ate some things and left others out of disgust, that God sent His Prophet, sent down His book, made lawful what is lawful and forbade what is unlawful, and then said: «what He made lawful is lawful, what He forbade is unlawful, and what He was silent about is pardoned»; he also recited a verse of the Qur'an on the matter [[passage/abudawud-api-3800|Sunan Abi Dawud 3800]] {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 3800}.
