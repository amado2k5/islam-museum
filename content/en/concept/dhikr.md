## Overview
Ibn Rajab discusses *dhikr* (remembrance of God) in the fiftieth hadith, which closes his additions to the Forty, as the comprehensive principle to hold on to when the laws have multiplied. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.927}

## In Ibn Rajab's commentary
The text of the hadith is: "Let your tongue remain moist with the remembrance of God." In the hadith of Mu'adh: "that you die while your tongue is moist with the remembrance of God." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.927}

Ibn Rajab says that many of the virtues of remembrance were mentioned earlier, scattered through the book, and that here he mentions the excellence of persisting in it and doing it often. He cites the verses that command much remembrance and that praise the men and women who remember, with forgiveness and a great reward. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.927}

He holds that remembrance is the easiest and most honourable of deeds, and the nearest to God, since it needs no place, no ablution, and no turning toward the qibla. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.927}
