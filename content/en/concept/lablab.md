## Temperament
Balanced toward some heat and a soft dryness; al-Khuzi held it to be cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Actions and properties
It dissolves and opens; the kind known as the beggars' rope has an astringent earthiness, a softening wateriness and a fiery pungency, and drying removes the wateriness; it has a cleansing power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Cosmetic uses
The milk of the large lablab removes hair and kills lice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Wounds and ulcers
The fresh leaf of the beggars' rope is suitable for large abscesses and heals them boiled in wine; as a poultice it helps burns from fire, especially with cerate, so that it has no equal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Its juice is dropped on cotton into the aching ear, especially with rose oil and especially when the swelling is hot. It helps chronic headache, and its juice helps matter that has long been flowing into the ear and old ulcers in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546}.

## Chest and breathing
Good for the chest and lung, and it clears asthma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It opens obstructions of the liver, and its leaf with vinegar is good for the spleen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546}.

## Excretory organs
Its water loosens the burnt yellow bile, and uncooked it is stronger. One kind of lablab is bad and loosens blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546}.
