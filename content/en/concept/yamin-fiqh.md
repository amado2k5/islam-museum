Yamin in lexicon is the side and the limb, the opposite of yasar, the left; al-Zamakhshari cites "I took his yamin and his yumna," and the right hand is called al-yumna. Yamin also names the oath, so called because men pressed their right hands when swearing alliance, and the one sworn by may itself be called yamin. The word is feminine in every sense, and its plurals are ay mun, ayman, ayamin and ayamin; the jurists' use does not depart from the lexical meaning. What is treated here is the right hand and the right side; the oath was detailed under the headword ayman. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18005}

The related term is yasar, which lexically means the side, the left hand, ease and wealth; the jurists use it in the same sense, and the link is that yamin is the opposite of yasar as limb and side. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18006}

For the right as a limb, it is recommended to put the right before the left in whatever honours, such as ablution and washing, and the left before the right in whatever involves harm or lowliness, such as blowing the nose and cleaning after the privy. The basis is Aisha's report that the Prophet's right hand was for his purity and food and his left for the privy and what was hurtful, and Hafsa's report that he used his right for eating, drinking and dressing and his left for all else. Al-Mawwaq set the rule: where both limbs are used, the right goes first in the weightier act and the left in the lesser, unless that is hard, as in mounting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18006}

One leaving a place for relieving oneself should put his right foot forward, and one entering it his left; the same holds for every unclean place, a bath-house, a washing place or a rubbish heap, left foot in and right foot out. Cleaning with the right hand is disliked unless an excuse prevents the left. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18006} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18007}

In ablution one begins with the right hand before the left and the right foot before the left; rinsing the mouth and sniffing water are done with the right and blowing the nose with the left; and in tayammum the right hand precedes the left by wiping the left over the right and then the right over the left. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18007}

Most jurists, the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis and the Malikis in one narration, hold it sunna to place the right hand over the left in prayer; the Malikis on the school view hold it better to let the arms hang at one's sides. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18007}

Most jurists, the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, hold that putting the right foot first is sunna when entering any mosque; the Malikis regard it as recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18007} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18008}

Eating with the right hand is sunna on Umar ibn Abi Salama's report that his hand kept roaming the dish until the Prophet told him to name God, eat with his right and eat from what was nearest; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis state that eating and drinking with the left without need is disliked. At sleep it is sunna to place the right hand under the right cheek, on al-Bara ibn Azib's report. The jurists also recommend beginning the trimming of the nails with the right hand then the left, and the right foot then the left. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18008}

In fixed punishments and retaliation, the jurists agree that the right hand is cut for a first theft, and that retaliation below the soul requires matching limbs, so the right is not cut for the left nor the left for the right, nor is the right eye taken for the left. Most jurists hold it sunna to call the adhan in a newborn's right ear and the iqama in the left, while Malik disliked calling the adhan in the newborn boy's ear at all. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18008} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18009}

For the right as a side, it is sunna to begin the tooth-stick on the right side of the mouth, for Aisha reported that the Prophet loved tayammun in putting on his sandals, combing his hair, his purification and all his affairs, and the bather begins pouring water on his right side on her report that the Prophet began washing from major impurity with the right side of his head then the left. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18009}

Most jurists hold that the caller to prayer turns his face right and left at the two words of invitation while his feet stay in place, to the right at "come to prayer" and to the left at "come to success"; the Hanafis add that when the minaret is wide it is good to turn in it, putting his head out of the right opening for the first call and the left for the second unless turning the head already gave notice, and the Malikis on the relied-upon view let him go around the minaret and call as he may. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18009} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18010}

It is sunna to begin washing the dead with their right sides, on Umm Atiyya's report that the Prophet told the women washing his daughter to begin with her right sides. The jurists hold it a duty of the tawaf that the walker keeps the House on his left. Tayammun in shaving the head means presenting the right side first, though they differ on whether the right of the shaved or of the shaver counts. And it is sunna to pass the vessel to the right and on to the right after the first drinker when others are sitting with him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18010}
