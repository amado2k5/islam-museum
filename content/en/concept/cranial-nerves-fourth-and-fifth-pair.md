## Summary
The fourth goes to the palate; the fifth to hearing, the cheek, the broad muscle and the muscles of the temples {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.79}.

## The fourth pair
It begins behind the third and nearer the base of the brain, mixes with the third, parts from it and reaches the palate, giving it feeling. It is a small pair but harder than the third, since the palate and its lining are harder than the lining of the tongue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.79}.

## The fifth pair
Each of its members splits in two like a double, and arises from the sides of the brain. The first part goes to the membrane lining the ear passage and spreads through all of it; it arises from the back part of the brain and gives the sense of hearing. The second, smaller, leaves by a hole in the stony bone, called the blind or the one-eyed one for the great twisting and winding of its course, which lengthens the distance and so hardens the nerve away from its origin; emerging, it mixes with the nerve of the third pair, and most of both goes to the cheek and the broad muscle, the rest to the temple muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.79}.

## The wisdom of it
Taste was put in the fourth pair and hearing in the fifth because the organ of hearing had to be open, its way to the air not blocked, while the organ of taste had to be guarded; so hearing had to be harder and its origin nearer the back of the brain. The muscles of the eye got a single nerve, while the temple muscles have many, since the eye's hole needed to be wide for the thick nerve of sight and the bone could not bear many holes, whereas the temple nerves leave through a hard stony bone that can bear many {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.79-80}.
