## Summary
Haya (modesty) is lexically a change and recoil that comes upon a person from fear of what would bring blame; in the law it is a quality that moves a person away from ugly acts and words and prevents negligence toward one with a right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7426} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7427} The article separates it from khajal (shame showing in the face), from badhaa (foul speech), and from waqaha (brazenness). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7426} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7427} Among the sayings: modesty is part of faith, and modesty adorns whatever it is in. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7427} The article goes on to the rulings on modesty and is not complete on these pages. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7427}  The sayings on modesty make it a branch of faith, a thing that brings only good, a form of duty toward God who must be guarded in head and belly and remembering death; modesty that keeps one from learning religion is not legal modesty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7428} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7429} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7430} Modesty before wrongdoers and not commanding right is weakness, not modesty; a sum taken from another only out of modesty is like usurped property and must be returned. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7430} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7431}
