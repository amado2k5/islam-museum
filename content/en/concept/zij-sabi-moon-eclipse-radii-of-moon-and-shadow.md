## Correcting the diameters
The calculation begins by correcting the Moon's diameter from its known size, adding the minutes taken from the third table, the table of minutes set against the Moon's corrected anomaly. The shadow's radius is corrected in the same way, adding table minutes to a fixed number of minutes that the text gives. The corrected half-radii are then combined and halved to yield the half-sum of the two radii, the measure against which the Moon's latitude is compared. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.150-151}

## The numerical base
The text gives a fixed number of minutes to which the Moon's correction is added and another fixed number for the shadow. In this passage the numerals are disturbed in the OCR, so the structure is carried over and the figures are left for the Latin text to settle. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.150-151}
