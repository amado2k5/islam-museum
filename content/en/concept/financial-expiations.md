## Summary
Expiation, as defined by the author, is what becomes obligatory upon a Muslim—whether freeing a slave, fasting, or feeding the poor—upon committing an offense that requires it, the most well-known being breaking an oath, hunting while in ihram, shaving the head during Hajj, breaking the fast in Ramadan due to inability or intentionally, and zihar {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 329}.

## Feeding and the Number of Poor People
One method of expiation is feeding a number of poor people, varying by offense from one to sixty {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 329}. The author mentions the types for expiation of an oath, hunting, the ransom for shaving the head, the ransom for one unable to fast, and zihar; in some expiations a Muslim does not have a free choice among the types, as they follow a strict sequence {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 329} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 330}. He relies on the Qur'anic verses on expiation in surahs al-Ma'idah, al-Baqarah, and al-Mujadilah, which are cited here by number without quoting their text {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 329}. See [[passage/quran-5-89|al-Ma'idah 89]] and [[passage/quran-2-184|al-Baqarah 184]].

## Financial Expiations for Intentionally Breaking the Fast
The author notes that intentionally breaking the fast through sexual intercourse during the day in Ramadan is expiated by fasting two consecutive months, or, if unable, by feeding sixty poor people, and that the Hanafis, Malikis, al-Thawri, and others applied the same ruling to one who intentionally breaks the fast by eating or drinking without excuse {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 330}. The book describes it as an authentic hadith, though no grading is made here {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 330}.

## Financial Summary
The author concludes that among the types of expiation are financial expiations: charity, feeding or clothing poor people, and freeing slaves {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 330}.
