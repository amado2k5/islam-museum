## Nature
Some warn that drinking it is dangerous, because it clings to the folds of the stomach, to the throat and to the gullet. The editor's footnote, quoting Ibn al-Baytar, calls it a shining stone that dissolves into small sheets when pounded and was used for bathhouse lamps in place of glass. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Temperament
Cold in the first degree and dry in the second. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Properties
The calcined form is stronger and finer. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Actions
It is astringent and holds back blood. It is used in depilatory paste, as Paul and others claimed, so that it dries more; fire does not burn it except by contrivance. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Organs of the chest
It helps swellings of the breasts, the testicles, behind the ears and other soft flesh, at the start of the swelling. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.501}

## Organs of the breath
It holds back spitting of blood with the water of lamb's tongue. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.501}

## Excretory organs
It holds back blood from the womb and the anus, and it helps dysentery, when the washed stone is used as a drink and as a poultice. The womb listing is the book's, labelled. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.501}
