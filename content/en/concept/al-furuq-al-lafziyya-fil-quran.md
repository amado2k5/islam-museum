## Summary
The Sheikh says the Qur'an does not wear out however often it is read, and that its companion discovers a new meaning in each reading; he likens companionship with the Qur'an to that of a friend who shares his secrets only after long acquaintance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:02:30} He holds that the Qur'an added to Arabic what the Arabs had never dreamt of. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:01:30}

## Matar and ghayth
He holds that the Qur'an distinguished matar from ghayth, though people take them as the same: matar appears in it only for evil, ghayth only for good. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:04:30}

## Ajdath
He says the Arabs knew 'jadath' as the first strike of a horse's hoof on the ground at the start of a race, and that the Qur'an called graves ajdath, as if people come out of them racing like horses to the place of gathering; he links this to the Arabs' denial of resurrection and the Qurayshi who brought a decayed bone, crumbled it and asked who would revive it (needs_source: the Arabs' sense of 'jadath' as the Sheikh tells it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:09:00}

## 'Ilm and ma'rifa
He holds that the Qur'an distinguished 'ilm from ma'rifa: 'ilm covers universals and particulars and is an attribute of God, while ma'rifa covers particulars, is preceded by experience and effort and is marked by deficiency; so one does not say God 'knows' in the sense of ma'rifa but in the sense of 'ilm. He digresses that ministries of education were once called 'ministries of knowledge (ma'arif)'. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep27 00:07:30}

## Jasad and jism
He holds that jasad in the Qur'an is a frame without a soul, like al-Samiri's calf, 'a body that lowed', while jism is a body with life, soul and movement, as in the description of the hypocrites whose bodies please the onlooker. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:09:00}

## Mutawaffa, not mutawaffi
He points out a common error: people call the deceased 'mutawaffi' (the one who takes the soul), but the one who takes souls is God, and the deceased is 'mutawaffa'; he calls for tasting the language and making it loved by the young. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:10:00}

## His view on the neglect of Arabic
He criticises parents who boast that their children master English, French and German while they cannot form a correct Arabic sentence, and holds that the cause is that they have not read the Qur'an; this is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep26 00:04:00} See also: [[concept/al-tahaddi-bil-quran|the Qur'an's challenge]].
