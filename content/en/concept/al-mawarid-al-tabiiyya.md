## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat holds that the Earth abounds with the economic resources on which the basic structure of production rests, and that human beings cannot build a prosperous life without them {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 49}.

## Colonialism and Raw Materials
The author states that the colonial West sought raw materials from around the world and transported them to its own countries—gold, iron, tin, zinc, cotton, coffee, oil, and coal—built its industry with them, and then sold essential and non-essential goods back to developing nations for small sums representing the nations' share of their own raw materials {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 49}. He argues that developing nations fell for this, replacing production with the import of goods {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 49}.

## Agricultural, Industrial, and Livestock Resources
The author enumerates the resources in the Arab and Islamic countries: the rivers of Egypt, Iraq, and Syria; fertile soil needing care and reclamation; cotton cultivation in Egypt; and date palms in Iraq and Saudi Arabia {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 50}. He notes that industrial resources such as iron, tin, gold, zinc, and oil are abundant {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 50}, and that livestock resources are sufficient to produce the meat on which vast sums are spent to import from abroad {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 51}.

## The Hadith of Partnership in Water, Pasture, and Fire
The author cites the hadith "People are partners in three things: water, pasture, and fire," considering that it establishes people's right to basic resources, without mentioning its source or grade {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 67}. See [[concept/al-hajat-al-asasiyya|Basic Needs]].

## From Zaydan Qa'dat's Book: pp. 83–164 of the PDF (Natural Resources in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat places the Earth's bounties and treasures—water, rivers, seas, and the minerals, oil, and gas wealth within them—under public ownership, so that all people may benefit from them and they may be distributed justly without oppression or aggression {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 154}.
