## Summary
Mal, in lexicon, is anything a person owns. The Hanafis define it as what human nature inclines to and can be kept for need; the Malikis, as what can be owned and the owner may exclusively control; the Hanbalis, as what it is lawful to benefit from in every case, or lawful to keep without need {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13800-13801}. The jurists differ on whether usufructs are property: the Hanafis hold they are not in themselves property, except when they are the object of a contract of exchange such as hire; most Shafi'is, Malikis and Hanbalis hold they are property in themselves {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13801-13802}. On debt as property, the Hanafis treat it as property in a figurative sense; one Shafi'i view says it is property in fact, another that it is a right to claim which becomes property in the end {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13802-13803}. The jurists divide property in many ways: valued and unvalued (Hanafi), fungible and non-fungible, goods subject to another's right, movables and immovables, cash and goods, property lost or hoped for, growing and held, and apparent and hidden property for zakat {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13803-13810}. Its rulings include the prohibition on taking or consuming another's property, the rule that a minor's property is not handed over until he is proved of sound judgement, the duty to earn enough for oneself and dependants, and the duty to rid oneself of mixed unlawful property {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13809-13810}. The six kinds of ribawi goods are fixed by a hadith; other goods are not ribawi {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13810-13811}.

## Property, legal value and ownership in Radd al-Muhtar

At the start of the Book of Sales in [[work/radd-al-muhtar|Radd al-Muhtar]], [[person/ibn-abidin|Ibn Abidin]] explains that mal means what human nature inclines to and can be stored for a time of need; that property status is established when all or some people treat a thing as wealth, and legal value (taqawwum) by that together with the law's permitting its use. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10} What is permitted but not treated as wealth, such as a grain of wheat, is not mal; what is treated as wealth but not permitted for use, such as wine, has no legal value; and where both are missing neither is established, as with blood, as he reports from al-Bahr citing al-Kashf al-Kabir. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10} So mal is wider than what has legal value: wine is mal without legal value, which is why a sale with wine as the price is defective, while a sale with wine as the goods is not concluded at all, since the price is not the aim but a means, the aim being the use of goods. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10}

He reports from al-Talwih that a usufruct is owned but is not mal, since ownership is what can be disposed of exclusively and mal what can be stored for use at need, and that legal value implies property status for Abu Hanifa and ownership for al-Shafi'i. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10} He reports al-Hawi al-Qudsi's definition of mal as whatever other than a human being was created for human benefit and can be secured and disposed of by choice, and discusses it: use of property is judged for each thing by what suits it, and no property may be destroyed without use, such as killing an animal without due cause. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.10}
