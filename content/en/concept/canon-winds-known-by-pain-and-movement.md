## Summary
In the ninth chapter Ibn Sina names four ways of knowing winds in the body: pains, movements, sounds and touch. This record covers pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.

## Tensive pains
Winds are known from the pains they cause in sensitive organs, following the separation of continuity they bring about. Tensive pains point to wind, above all when they come with lightness, and if the pain moves from place to place the sign is complete {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.

## Bone and glandular flesh
Where continuity is separated in organs that do not feel, such as bone and glandular flesh, pain does not show it; the winds of bones may even break and crush the bones without any pain beyond what neighbouring sensitive parts report {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.
