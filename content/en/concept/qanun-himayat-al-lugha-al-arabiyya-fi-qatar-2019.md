## Summary
This page presents Qatar's Law No. 7 of 2019 on the protection of the Arabic language as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it. The museum opened neither the pages cited nor the text of the law, so what is here is ascribed to the summary, and speaks of the law in its general lines only.
## Body and year
The year is 2019, and the body, by the summary, is the Emir of Qatar, Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani, and Qatari legislation {cite:axl-cefan-ulaval-language-legislation}. The Emir has a record at [[person/tamim-bin-hamad-al-thani|Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani]], and the state at [[place/qatar|Qatar]].
## Provisions
The law requires all governmental and non-governmental entities to protect and support Arabic in their activities; mandates Arabic in ministry and government meetings, decisions, regulations, documents, contracts, correspondence, publications and advertising, and in education, as the language of instruction in public institutions, with private schools teaching it as a core subject. It requires Arabic names for many companies and Arabic labelling on Qatari products, with limited exceptions, and provides fines of up to 50,000 Qatari riyals for violations {cite:al-arab-qatar-home} {cite:al-jazeera-home}.
## Where it is found
The summary says the text and summaries are available through Qatari media, such as Al Arab and Al Jazeera, and through the Universite Laval language legislation archive, and that it was published in the Official Gazette {cite:al-arab-qatar-home} {cite:al-jazeera-home} {cite:axl-cefan-ulaval-language-legislation}. These are media sources and the summary names no particular page, so they are a description and not an official text.
## Differences not to be forgotten
This law differs from [[concept/qanun-al-taarib-al-jazairi|the Algerian law]] in making Arabic the language of instruction in public institutions and letting private schools teach it as a core subject, and in fixing a money fine, whereas the summary gives the Algerian law phased deadlines without mentioning fines. This comparison comes from reading the summary, not the two texts.
## Place in the record of Arabic
It is read with [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-qatar|Arabic in Qatar]] and [[language/arabic|Arabic]], and with [[concept/mashru-qanun-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-fi-al-imarat|the draft Arabic language law in the Emirates]], which has not been enacted in the summary. The authority for the texts is their Arabic original and the Official Gazette.

## How to read this page
The summary does not say which companies are exempt from the requirement of an Arabic name or which goods from the label, and says only limited exceptions. It does not say who imposes the fine or how it is challenged. Nothing should therefore be understood from this page beyond what the summary said, and whoever needs practical application should go to the published text. The summary names Qatari media as sources for the text and its summaries, and these are descriptive sources, not an official reference.
## Arabic required in education and commerce
In the summary the Qatari law is distinctive in touching two fields outside administration, education and trade: a company's name and a product's label are what people see daily, and with them the law leaves official documents for public façades. It reaches beyond governmental bodies to non-governmental ones, so the obligation covers the private sector too. All of this is in the summary's wording, which fixes only the upper limit of the fine.
