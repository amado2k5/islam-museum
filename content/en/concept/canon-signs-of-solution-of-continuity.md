## Summary
In the eleventh chapter Ibn Sina shows how a separation of continuity is recognised in outer and inner organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.167-168}.

## Outer and inner
In outer organs sensation finds it; in inner ones a piercing, pricking or eroding pain points to it, above all without fever. It is often followed by a flow of humour, such as spitting or pouring of blood into the chest cavity, and the passing of matter and pus if it comes after the signs of swellings and their ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.

## After swellings
What follows swellings may or may not be a bursting from ripening. If it is from ripening, the fever calms with the bursting and the pus is emptied and the heaviness eases; if not, the pain sharpens and grows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.

## Other signs
It is also indicated by organs displaced from their places, by an organ moving out of position like a hernia, and by the holding back of evacuations from their channels, as when a person's bowel is torn and his stool is held {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.
