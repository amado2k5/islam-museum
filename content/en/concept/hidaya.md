## Summary
Sha'rawi distinguishes two senses of guidance: showing the way, which God extends to all people, and enabling, which is for those who believe {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2524}.

## What Sha'rawi says
He uses the image of a traffic policeman who shows a traveller the road to Alexandria without forcing him to walk it; if the traveller then accepts the road in good faith, the policeman goes further and helps him along it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2524}. On this basis he holds that God guided all people by showing, and enables those who believe in Him {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2524}.

## The caller and misguidance
He reads the two verses, one denying that the Prophet guides whom he loves and one affirming that he guides to a straight path, as meaning that the Prophet shows the truth but does not enable it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2525}. He explains that God's misguidance of the hypocrites follows from what they earned, so the path to their guidance is closed, not guidance alone {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-2501-3000 p.2525}. This is his view at this point in the commentary; the theologians' views on the meaning of guidance are not yet set out from a named source.
