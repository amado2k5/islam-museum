## Summary
Sha'rawi distinguishes two kinds of guidance: the guidance of indication, by which God has shown the way to all creation, and the guidance of help and ease, given to those who believe and respond. On this basis he reads the verses that ask how God could guide people who disbelieved after believing ([[passage/quran-3-86|Al Imran 86]], [[passage/quran-3-87|Al Imran 87]]) {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1597}.

## Guidance of indication
Sha'rawi compares indication to road signs, which show the way to the goal but do not take a traveller by the hand. God has guided all people in this sense, believer and disbeliever alike, by showing them the law {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1597}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1598}.

## Guidance of help
Whoever accepts the law and believes in it, Sha'rawi says, is given further guidance through help and ease, and God gives him the sweetness of obedience so that he turns to it with energy. He illustrates this with a traveller asking a policeman the way to Alexandria. If the traveller thanks him, the policeman gives more direction and help; if he denies him, the policeman ignores him {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1598}.

## The question in the verse
Sha'rawi holds that the question "how could God guide" denies the guidance of help and not the guidance of indication, since help requires an act on the part of the one helped, and the disbeliever has done nothing to earn it. He ends by saying God does not guide the wrongdoers, meaning those who associate partners with God, which he calls the original wrong {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1599}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1601}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1602}.
