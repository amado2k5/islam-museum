## Summary
After the Uthmanic codices were sent to the cities, the people of each city read what was in their codex, having received it from the Companions who received it from the Prophet; then the Successors took the Companions' place; then some devoted themselves to recitation, taking and mastery until they became imams to whom people travelled and from whom they took. This is what Ibn al-Jazari describes at the start of al-Nashr, and al-Sabt summarizes. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 18} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 326}

## The Companions who taught recitation
Ibn al-Jazari relays from Abu Ubayd that among those from whom some variant of reading was carried are, from the Muhajirun, Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, Sa'd, Ibn Mas'ud, Hudhayfa, Salim, Abu Hurayra, Ibn Umar, Ibn Abbas, Amr ibn al-As and his son Abdallah, Mu'awiya, Ibn al-Zubayr, Abdallah ibn al-Sa'ib, A'isha, Hafsa and Umm Salama, and from the Ansar Ubayy, Mu'adh, Abu al-Darda', Zayd, Abu Zayd, Majma' ibn Jariya and Anas. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 16}

## The Successors in each city
**Medina:** Ibn al-Musayyib, Urwa, Salim, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, Sulayman and Ata', sons of Yasar, Mu'adh the reciter, Abd al-Rahman ibn Hurmuz al-A'raj, Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, Muslim ibn Jundub and Zayd ibn Aslam. **Mecca:** Ubayd ibn Umayr, Ata', Tawus, Mujahid, Ikrima and Ibn Abi Mulayka. **Kufa:** Alqama, al-Aswad, Masruq, Ubayda, Amr ibn Shurahbil, al-Harith ibn Qays, al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym, Amr ibn Maymun, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami, Zirr ibn Hubaysh, Ubayd ibn Nudayla, Abu Zur'a ibn Amr ibn Jarir, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i and al-Sha'bi. **Basra:** Amir ibn Abd Qays, Abu al-Aliya, Abu Raja', Nasr ibn Asim, Yahya ibn Ya'mar, Mu'adh, Jabir ibn Zayd, al-Hasan, Ibn Sirin and Qatada. **Syria:** al-Mughira ibn Abi Shihab al-Makhzumi, Uthman's pupil in recitation, and Khulayd ibn Sa'd, pupil of Abu al-Darda'. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 18}

## The imams of recitation
From these some devoted themselves to recitation and took such care of its mastery that the people of their city agreed to accept their reading, and the reading was attributed to them: in Medina Abu Jafar, then Shayba, then Nafi'; in Mecca Ibn Kathir, Humayd ibn Qays al-A'raj and Ibn Muhaysin; in Kufa Yahya ibn Wuthab, Asim and al-A'mash, then Hamza, then al-Kisa'i; in Basra Abdallah ibn Abi Ishaq, Isa ibn Umar and Abu Amr ibn al-Ala', then Asim al-Jahdari, then Yaqub al-Hadrami; in Syria Abdallah ibn Amir, Atiyya ibn Qays al-Kilabi and Ismail ibn Abdallah ibn al-Muhajir, then Yahya ibn al-Harith al-Dhimari, then Shurayh ibn Yazid al-Hadrami. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 18} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 19}

## The map around the year 200
Al-Suyuti quotes Makki that around the year two hundred the people of Basra followed the reading of Abu Amr and Yaqub, those of Kufa Hamza and Asim, Syria Ibn Amir, Mecca Ibn Kathir and Medina Nafi', and they continued so. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 526} That describes that age; I found no text in my sources that sets the spread of the readings today by country, so I do not speak of it; see [[concept/al-qiraat-al-ashr|the ten readings]] and [[concept/tasbi-al-qiraat-ibn-mujahid|Ibn Mujahid's limiting to seven]].

## A note on names
The names of these Successors are taken from Ibn al-Jazari's list as I read it in the scanned text; the machine reading may have flaws in some names, so I attribute to no one more than the appearance of his name in the list; see the person records for the biographies of the imams.
