## Summary
Ibn Sina closes chapter 30 with what calms windy pains: pouring hot water and its danger, fomentation and its kinds, cupping glasses with fire, then walking, fats and pleasant song {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.310-311}.

## Hot water
Some pains, though very severe, are sometimes easy to treat, such as windy pains, which pouring hot water on them may calm and suffice for. But there is one danger in this: the cause may be a swelling mistaken for wind, and if hot water is used on it, especially at its beginning, harm results. Hot water may also harm the windy case if it is too weak to dissolve the wind and adds to the spreading of its volume {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.310-311}.

## Fomentation
Fomentation is also among the treatments of winds, and the best is with what is light, such as millet, except for an organ that cannot bear it, like the eye, which is fomented with cloths. Some fomentations are with warmed oil. A strong fomentation is to cook bitter vetch flour in vinegar, dry it and make a compress of it; a lesser one is to cook bran the same way. Salt has a stinging vapour, and millet is better and weaker than it. Water in a bladder may also be used as a compress, which is mild and soft {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## Cupping glasses with fire
Cupping glasses with fire are of this kind, strong at calming windy pain, and if repeated they abolish the pain altogether, though what happens with the relaxants may happen with them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.

## Walking and song
Among the relievers of pain are a gentle walk of long duration for the relaxing in it, and likewise the known light fats and the oils we named, and pleasant song, especially if sleep is brought on by it; being busy with what gladdens is a strong reliever of pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.311}.
