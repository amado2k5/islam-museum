## Nature
This is the fruit of the terebinth tree, according to the editor's note; the best comes from the island the book names. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}

## Temperament
Its heat is not small; while moist its drying is slight, and when ripe it is in the third degree; its gum is hot with a slight dryness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}

## Actions
It warms, softens and cleans, with some binding; its gum dissolves more than mastic, being more bitter, draws from the depths of the body, and often stands in for mastic. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it clears the face and freckles; a gum the book calls al-anbat helps cracks of the face. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Swellings and pustules: its gum ripens hard swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Wounds and ulcers: it clears scabies and the itch the book calls qawabi; its gum enters ointments to clean wounds and dry the discharge; it heals surface ulcers, and helps the itch of ulcers, ulcerated scabies and phlegmatic scabies. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Joints: its oil goes into oils and ointments for fatigue, and it helps paralysis and facial palsy. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Organs of the head: its gum with honey and good oil is good for moisture of the ear. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Organs of the eye: its smoke goes into kohl to preserve the lashes and treat the wearing away of the eyelids. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Organs of the chest: it helps pains of the side as a poultice and rub; its gum is good for lung ulcers and chronic cough as an electuary, alone or with sweetness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.493}
Organs of digestion: it helps the spleen, especially the oil of the terebinth, but it takes away appetite; it also cleans the chest. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Poisons: its gum and fruit are drunk in wine for the bite of the tarantula. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Labelled listing, set aside for the founder's ruling: excretory organs: the book says it stirs and promotes flow, and that its gum also promotes flow and softens the body. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
