## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Mali: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. In Mali the summary presents Hassaniya as a recognised national language, and the 2022 census counts are given as they appear. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Mali", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The summary's dates here are the 2023 constitution of Mali and the 2022 census (RGPH5), from which the report on the cultural characteristics of the population comes. It does not date the arrival of Hassaniya in northern Mali or of religious Arabic schooling, and it describes the situation after 2022. For the historical frame see [[concept/taarib-al-maghrib|The Arabisation of the Maghrib]], which treats the waves that carried Arabic far from the Mediterranean coast.

## Legal status
Article 31 of the 2023 Constitution of Mali, according to the summary, makes the national languages the official languages and French the working language {cite:constitution-mali-2023-sgg}. Hassaniya Arabic, called Maure or Hasaniya, is one of the 13 recognised national languages of Mali and so has official status; Classical or Modern Standard Arabic is not among the 13 national and official languages {cite:cefan-mali} {cite:cefan-mali-constitution-2023}. This is the summary's account, ascribed to two pages of Université Laval; the museum did not read the constitution or the list of thirteen languages itself.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names Hassaniya, spoken in northern and north-western Mali, and Arabic, which the census reports as a separate category, alongside Classical and Modern Standard Arabic in religious and educational settings {cite:cefan-mali} {cite:instat-mali-rgph5-2022-cultural-characteristics}. In other words the census separates Maure/Hasaniya from Arabe, and the summary does not explain what the second category contains. For the link of Hassaniya with Mauritania see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-mauritania|Arabic in Mauritania]] and [[concept/lahjat-al-maghrib|The dialect of the Maghrib]].

## Official figures
From Mali's 2022 census (RGPH5) of INSTAT, among the resident ordinary-household population aged 3 and over, 19,141,586 in all, the summary gives these figures {cite:instat-mali-rgph5-2022-cultural-characteristics}. Mother tongue: Maure/Hasaniya 176,197 (0.92 percent) and Arabe 81,208 (0.42 percent). Main spoken language: Maure/Hasaniya 158,958 (0.83 percent) and Arabe 100,654 (0.52 percent). These are the census figures as the summary relayed them; the museum neither adds the two categories nor adjusts them.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: Constitution of Mali, 2023 (Journal officiel) {cite:constitution-mali-2023-sgg} and INSTAT Mali, RGPH5 2022: cultural characteristics of the population {cite:instat-mali-rgph5-2022-cultural-characteristics}; Reference, academic and press pages: CEFAN / Université Laval page on Mali {cite:cefan-mali} and CEFAN page on the Mali Constitution 2023 {cite:cefan-mali-constitution-2023}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
