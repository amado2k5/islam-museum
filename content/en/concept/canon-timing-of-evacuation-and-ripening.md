## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that the time of evacuation turns on ripening: in chronic diseases only ripening is awaited and attenuants are given before evacuating; in acute ones waiting is also wiser, especially if the matter is quiet, but if it is moving, quick evacuation is better, and thick matter is not stirred until it has been thinned {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.274-275}.

## Chronic and acute diseases
Galen is firm that in chronic diseases only ripening is awaited, and before evacuation, after ripening, attenuants such as hyssop water are given. In acute diseases waiting for ripening is also wiser, above all when the matter is quiet; when it is moving, hurrying to evacuate it is better, since the harm of its movement exceeds the harm of evacuating before ripeness, above all if the humours are thin and lie in the cavities of the vessels without having entered the organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.274}.

## Confined and thick humour
If a humour is confined within a single organ it is not moved at all until it ripens and gains a moderate consistency. If the strength cannot be trusted to last until ripening, evacuation is done after care in judging its time and thickness; if it is thick and heavy it may not be stirred until it has been thinned. Its thickness is inferred from earlier indigestion, a stretched pain under the ribs, or swellings in the viscera, and among the most necessary things to watch is the state of the passages, that they not be blocked; after all this he may purge before ripeness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.274-275}.
