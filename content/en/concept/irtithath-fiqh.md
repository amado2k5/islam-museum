## Meaning
In the lexicon irtithath is for a wounded man to be carried from the battle, weak and overcome by his wounds; one says he was 'carried out' (ruththa) meaning carried from the battle with a little life left. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2090} The jurists add conditions: the mutarrath is one carried from the battle whose life has settled, by speaking, eating, drinking, sleeping, buying or selling, or by surviving for a customary time, and then dying. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2090}
## General ruling
The mutarrath is washed and prayed over, since he is not a martyr in the rulings of this world and the rulings of martyrs do not apply to him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2090} He is a martyr in reward and gains the reward of martyrs; this is agreed for one who dies after the battle with unbelievers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2090} The case of a rebel carried away, or of one of the just side in a battle between Muslims, is disputed as to washing and prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2090}
## Where it is discussed
The jurists treat the rulings on the mutarrath in the chapter on funerals and in the chapter on rebels. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2090}
