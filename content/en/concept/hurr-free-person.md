## Summary
The free man is the opposite of the slave; in the jurists' usage he is one whose person is free of any trace of bondage or ownership, and freedom is either firmly established or judged by appearance, as with a found child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6984} A partly freed person, half free and half owned, is treated under the rules of partial manumission. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6984} The principle is freedom: bondage is accidental, and the law is addressed chiefly to free persons, slaves sharing most of its rules. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6985} A maxim holds that a free person cannot be taken as property, so he is not sold or bought, and that the free person's clothing is not within a usurper's liability. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6985}
