## Summary
Ibn Sina explains how the degree of drying is set by the temperament of the organ, of the ulcer and of the body and by the strength of the drying drugs, then treats internal ulcers and what hinders an ulcer's healing, and ends with what to watch in the colour of the pus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.307-308}.

## The organ and the ulcer
Among the conditions is weighing the original temperament of the organ and the temperament of the ulcer. If the organ in its temperament is very moist and the ulcer is not very moist, a slight drying in the first degree suffices, since the disease has not gone much beyond the organ's nature. If the organ is dry and the ulcer very moist, a drug that dries in the second and third degree is needed to return it to its temperament, and the case should be balanced when both are moderate {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.

## The body and the drying drugs
Likewise the temperament of the whole body is weighed: if the body is very dry, an organ that exceeds in moisture is moderate relative to the body and should be dried with a moderate drug. If both go to excess, then if the excess is of moisture the drying is stronger, and if of dryness weaker. The strength of the drugs is weighed too: the flesh-growing driers, though no strong drying is sought of them, hold back the matter pouring to the organ, while the sealing driers must be more cleansing and washing of the ichor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.

## Where the ulcer lies
All drugs that dry without stinging are useful for growing flesh. Every ulcer in a place that is not fleshy is slow to answer in healing, and so is a round one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.

## Internal ulcers
In internal ulcers, penetrating drugs such as honey are mixed with the drying and astringent drugs used in them, together with drugs specific to the place, such as diuretics in treating ulcers of the urinary organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.

## What hinders healing
An ulcer's healing is hindered by the bad temperament of the organ, which must be set right; by the badness of the temperament of the blood heading to it, to be met by what generates good chyme; and by the abundance of blood that flows to it, to be met by evacuation, lightening the food and exercise if possible. Corruption of the bone that eats away and makes the ichor flow has no remedy but setting that bone right and scraping it, or taking it out and cutting it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.

## Food and colour
Ulcers need food to strengthen them and less food to cut off the matter of pus, so the physician must manage this. When ulcers are at the beginning and the increase, the patient should not enter the bath or be wetted with hot water. The colour of the pus and of the lip of the wound is always to be watched, and when the pus is abundant without much extra food, that is from ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.
