## Nature and selection
Anzarut is the gum of a thorny tree in the land of Fars, and it has bitterness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.350}. The good kind is the one that leans to yellow and resembles frankincense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.350}.

## Temperament and actions
Some said it is hot in the second degree and dry in the first; Ibn Jurayj said that it occurs in Fars and in al-Lurdajan and is very hot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.350}. It glues without stinging, and for that reason it cicatrizes and joins and is used in ointments, and it has ripening and resolving power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.350}.

## Uses by organ
It soothes all swellings as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.350}. It eats dead flesh, heals fresh wounds and mends sprains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.350}. It is said that a remedy made from its powder, for an aching ear, heals it in days, and the way it is made is not reproduced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}. It helps ophthalmia and ocular sordes in particular, and catarrhs of the eye, especially the kind prepared with ass's milk (the preparation is not reproduced), and it brings out the mote from the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}. It purges the raw and the thick phlegm, especially from the hip and the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}.
