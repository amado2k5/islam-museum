## Summary
Ibn Sina lays down a general rule: anything that changes from its habit, in appetite, stool, urine, desire for intercourse, sleep, sweat, dryness of the body, keenness of mind, taste, or habit of nocturnal emission, becoming less or more or altered in quality, warns of disease; and a very bad habit is not dropped at once but by degrees because habit is like nature {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.260}.

## The general measure
Anything that changes from its habit in appetite, stool, urine, desire for intercourse, sleep, sweat, dryness of the body, keenness of mind, taste or the habit of nocturnal emission, becoming less or more or changed in quality, foretells a disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.260}.

## Unnatural habits
So too with habits that are not natural, such as bleeding from piles, menses, vomiting, nosebleed, or the habit of craving something whether corrupt or not; for habit is like nature, and so even a very bad one is given up gradually {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.260}.
