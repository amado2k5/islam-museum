## Temperament
Hot in the first degree and dry in the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Actions and properties
Subtle and bitter, with some earthiness and a subtilising; Galen is quoted that its flower is stronger than wormwood; and it has talqih, as the book puts it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Cosmetics
Its burnt form benefits alopecia, especially with castor oil, radish oil or olive oil; it helps a slow-growing beard when cooked in some heating oils to open it; and it binds the gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Swellings and pustules
It dissolves phlegmatic swellings, and cooked with quince it benefits swellings hard to dissolve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Wounds and ulcers
It does not suit fresh wounds but stings them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Joints
Its decoction benefits sprain of the muscles and chronic, stubborn sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Organs of the head
Cooked in oil, it warms the head and removes its coldness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Respiratory organs
Its decoction benefits orthopnoea, and the best is the decoction of its blossoms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Cooked in oil, it warms the stomach and removes its coldness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Excretory organs
It breaks the stone of the bladder and the kidney {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Fevers
It benefits rigors when mixed with oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it brings on the menses and expels the foetus, and that its oil, warmed, benefits contraction of the womb and difficulty of urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}

## Poisons and bites
Given in wine, it benefits poisons, and spread as a bedding it drives off vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.655}
