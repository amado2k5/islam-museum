## Nature
It is the seed of the qinnab tree, of which we have spoken, so the two chapters are to be read together; of it there is a well-known garden kind and a wild kind; Hunayn said that the wild is a tree that comes up in the wastelands, its leaf mostly white and its fruit like pepper, its seed resembling the summana, a seed from which oil is pressed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Actions and properties
It dissolves winds and dries strongly, and its humour is little and poor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Swellings and pustules
The wild qinnab, when its roots are cooked and laid as a poultice on hot swellings in the hard places, soothes the hot and dissolves the hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Organs of the head
By its heat it causes headache; its juice is dropped for earache from blockage and for moisture of the ear, and so is its oil; and its leaf removes scurf from the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Ocular organs
It darkens the sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It harms the stomachs, as is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Excretory organs
The milk of the wild shahdanj loosens gently, and its juice releases phlegm and yellow bile, going the way of safflower {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it dries up semen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.671}
