## Nature and selection
The skin of an animal; the best is the skin of sucklings, for its moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.

## Temperament and actions
Properties: its food is scant and sticky, close in its qualities to the trotters and the shavings of goat skin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}. Laid on a flow of blood, it cuts and stops it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: burnt snake skin as a plaster for fox-mange {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Swellings and pustules: it is said that the skin of the water-horse, laid on a pustule, dissolves it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Wounds and ulcers: the ash of mules and the like is put on burns and hot ulcers without swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}, and it is a remedy for chafing of the sole, the thighs and piles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}. Fresh sheep or goat skin laid on a blow at once stops the harm, and it is good for malignant ulcers, scabies and eating ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Food: the inner membrane of birds' gizzards and crops, especially of cocks, dried, crushed and drunk in a broth, helps stomach pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
Poisons: it is said that the skin of a goat, which is hot, drawn over a viper's bite draws out the poison {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.426}.
