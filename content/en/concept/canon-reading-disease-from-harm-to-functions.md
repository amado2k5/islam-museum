## Summary
Ibn Sina says that when an organ's function does not run its natural course, the faculty has been struck, and harm to a faculty follows disease in the organ; the harm to functions takes three forms: diminished, altered and abolished {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.156-157}.

## The basis
When an organ's function does not run in its natural course, the faculty has been afflicted, and affliction of the faculty follows disease in the organ that holds it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.156}.

## Diminished, altered, abolished
A function may be diminished, as sight weakens and sees a thing less or only from nearer, or the stomach digests harder, slower and in smaller amount; altered, as sight sees what is not there or sees a thing otherwise than it is, or the stomach spoils food and digests it badly; or abolished, as the eye does not see and the stomach does not digest at all {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.156-157}.
