## Overview
Injustice (*zulm*), for many scholars, is placing a thing in other than its proper place. Others defined it as acting on another's property without his permission. Ibn Rajab presents it in his commentary on the twenty-fourth sacred hadith. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.513-515}

## The two definitions
Ibn Rajab relates the second definition from Iyas ibn Mu'awiya, and on this definition injustice is impossible for God, because everything He does is an action within His own dominion. Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali answered Imran ibn Husayn in a similar way when he asked him about predestination. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.513-515}

## "I have forbidden injustice to Myself"
Ibn Rajab explains the phrase as God having withheld injustice from His servants. On the first definition, the hadith shows that God is able to do injustice but does not do it, out of grace and generosity. He notes that His creating the acts of the servants, among which is injustice, does not require that He be described as unjust, since He is described only by His own acts. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.513-515}

He cites a *mawquf* report from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, and a similar one from Ibn Mas'ud and Zayd ibn Thabit, that if God punished His creatures He would not be unjust to them. Ibn Rajab notes that its chain is open to question. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.513-515}

## The two kinds of injustice among servants
The first is injustice to oneself, the greatest of which is polytheism, on which he cites [[passage/quran-31-13|Luqman 13]], and then sins, great and small. The second is one servant's injustice to another, which is what is meant by his words "and I have made it forbidden among you, so do not wrong one another." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.513-515}

He cites in support the sermon of the Farewell Pilgrimage on the prohibition of blood, property, and honour, the hadith of Ibn Umar "injustice is darkness on the Day of Resurrection," and the hadith of Abu Musa on God's respite for the wrongdoer. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.513-515}

## In Ibn Khaldun
Ibn Khaldun makes it a herald of the ruin of settlement. See [[concept/zulm-mukharrib-al-umran|injustice heralds the ruin of settlement]]. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p.276-278}

## Injustice in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episodes 3 and 4)
The Sheikh says injustice is transgression against others' rights, like a flood that leaves its banks (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:21:00} He says that injustice and disobedience to parents bear fruit in this world before the next, that no tyrant in history goes unpunished, and recalls the Meccan persecutors of Bilal and Ammar (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:22:30}
He says that whoever takes a span of land unjustly is punished, that judges decide by arguments, and that what a person takes of his brother's right without cause is a piece of the Fire (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:45:00}
He holds that the tyrant's fire returns to him, since he planted fire in people's hearts and is consumed by it (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:46:30} He says each person's account is like the goods he takes from the supermarket, for which each is held to account (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:47:00}
