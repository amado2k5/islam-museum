## Summary
Nuqud, the plural of naqd, means in fiqh gold and silver, coined or not, the minted coin, or anything used as a medium of exchange. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16002}

## Money in fiqh
Dealing in money is lawful by the Qur'an and the Sunna; the natural coins are the dinar and the dirham, the conventional ones are fulus, debased dirhams and paper money. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16003} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16005}
Fixed rulings: the zakat threshold, the least dower, the cut-off for theft, blood money and the poll tax. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16006} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16007}
Minting belongs to the ruler; clipping and decorating with coins are subject to rules. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16008} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16011}
In contracts the local currency is meant when none is named; the schools differ on whether coins are specified by the contract, and on coins in partnership and salam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16013} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16019}
Loans, pledges, loan of coins, hire, endowment and bills of exchange: the rulings are set out in the schools' views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16021} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16024}
Change in value: scarcity, cheapness, slump and disappearance affect debts; the ruler should keep prices stable, and Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim and al-Maqrizi held that stable money is the basis of justice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16025} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16027}
