## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that God is near and answers the one who calls on Him, and that the answer is conditioned on responding to His way and believing in Him. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.780} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.782}

## Why not every prayer is answered
Sha'rawi says a person may ask for what he thinks good and it is evil, so God withholds the answer for his benefit; the answer follows the wisdom of the one who answers, not the measure of the caller. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.784} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.785}

## Conditions of the answer
Sha'rawi says lawful provision is a condition of prayer. He recounts the report of a man whose food, drink and dress are unlawful and whose prayer is not answered, and the prayer taught to Aisha on the Night of Power, asking for pardon. The footnotes name Tirmidhi, Ahmad, Hakim and Muslim, which were not checked here. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.786} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.787}

## Remembrance in place of asking
Sha'rawi holds that one whom remembrance of God keeps from asking is given the best of what is given to those who ask, and that the believer's share in prayer is worship and humility. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.787}

## Limits of this batch
The originals of the hadiths on supplication were not checked; the jurists' rules of supplication, the academic view and other views are not entered. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.787}
