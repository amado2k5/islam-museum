## Nature and temperament
Nature: Dioscorides says hasak has two kinds. One has leaves like those of the stupid purslane but thinner, round trailing stems on the ground, and a hard stiff thorn by the leaves; it grows in ruins. The other grows in damp places and by rivers, with raised stems, broader leaves than its thorn, and a hard fruit. The wild kind is more earthy and the cultivated more watery. The people on the bank of the river Satarmus feed their animals on it when it is fresh, and make bread from its fruit because it is sweet and nourishing. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Temperament: cold and dry according to Dioscorides; others say it is hot in the first degree at the start and dry in it, and it resembles the hasak of our own country. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}

## Actions
It prevents matter from settling, because it binds, and it ripens and softens. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}

## Uses by organ
Swellings: prevents hot swellings and the settling of matter, and is good for swellings of the throat. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Wounds: helps putrid ulcers, and granulates with honey. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Organs of the head: good for putrid ulcers of the gum. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Organs of the eye: its juice is used in kohl. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Organs of breathing: helps swellings around the muscles of the throat. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Excretory organs: increases sexual power, breaks up stone in the kidney and bladder, and its juice does the same; it helps difficult urination and colic. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
Poisons: a measured amount of the fruit of the wild kind helps the bite of the viper, and its decoction sprinkled on a place kills fleas there; the amount is left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.478}
