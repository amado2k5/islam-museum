## Definition
Imsak in the lexical sense is grasping with the hand; one may also say "I refrained from the matter", meaning I held back. Jurists use it in several places in both senses. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3393}

## Kinds
- Holding game: the trained hunting dog holds the prey for its master and does not eat it. The majority forbid the game if the dog ate from it, citing the verse on what trained hounds hold for you and the hadith of Adi ibn Hatim, "if it eats, do not eat, for I fear it held it for itself"; Malik and a view of Ahmad do not make holding a condition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3393}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3394}
- Abstention in fasting: holding back from eating, drinking and intercourse under the conditions is the fast; one who broke the fast thinking it was a day of Sha'ban, and learns it was Ramadan, must abstain for the rest of the day. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3394}
- Those whose excuse ends during Ramadan, such as a child who reaches puberty, a sick person who recovers or a traveller who returns: the Malikis, the preferred Shafi'i view and one Hanbali view do not require abstention, while the Hanafis, the second Shafi'i view and another Hanbali view do. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3394}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3395}
- Holding in a killing: whoever holds a man while another kills him is not retaliated against in the Hanafi and Shafi'i views, but Malik and a view of Ahmad retaliate against him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3395}
- Holding in divorce: in the majority view, holding is a form of taking back a revocable divorcee, so saying "I held you" is a return. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3395}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3396}
