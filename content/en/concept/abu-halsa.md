## Nature and selection
Some say that abu halsa is donkey lettuce, also called shanjar or shanqar. It is a hairy, rough, black plant with many leaves clinging to the stem; its root is the thickness of a finger and very red, and it stains the hand in summer. There is a small-leaved red kind. The book lists four kinds: abu halsa, abu sawiris, abu jalsus and aksufanin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. The first two are the strongest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. The editor's note says it appears under donkey lettuce in al-Mu'tamad.

## Temperament and actions
Galen said that some kinds are hot and dry and others are the opposite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. The one called abu halsa thins with astringency, so it is a bitter astringent; the astringency is more evident in the rest, and the other two are sharper and hotter. The root is stronger than the leaf {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: applied with vinegar, it helps vitiligo and the condition in which the skin peels; its leaf is weaker than its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Swellings and pustules: the root with fine kishk flour prevents redness, and the root of abu jalsus does likewise; it dissolves scrofulous swellings when applied with fat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Wounds and ulcers: applied with wax on all ulcers, and for burns in particular {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Food organs: the root astringes the stomach, and its decoction in water of qaratun helps jaundice and spleen pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Excretory organs: its decoction in qaratun water helps kidney pain and stone. The book states that a woman who takes the root miscarries; this is the book's listing, not a recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. The leaf, fried in wine, binds the belly, but abu halsa dissolves bitter humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. The root of the yellow-leaved kind, with zufa and mustard, kills and expels worms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Fevers: a decoction of the root in qaratun water helps chronic fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Poisons: a decoction of the fruit of the yellow-leaved red kind, chewed and spat on the head, kills lice; the other two kinds help a viper's bite when drunk, or applied, or spread {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}.
