## Summary
Derivatives, according to the author, are futures or futures contracts on the stock exchange; they are called derivatives because their value is derived from something else, and most of them are used for price speculation {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 353}.

## Description by Some Westerners
The author quotes some Westerners as saying that derivatives are weapons of mass destruction or ticking time bombs, that they are instruments of gambling and betting, and that they separate risk from the underlying asset, turning risk into a commodity through which markets fluctuate and bubbles and crashes occur {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 353}. This is a description quoted from others, and this entry does not adopt it as a judgment.

## Subordinate and Independent Risk
The author states that when risk is subordinate to capital or labor, it is permissible in Islam and increases the return, whereas when it is independent of capital or labor, it may not yield a return, for in Islam something may be permissible as a subordinate element yet impermissible on its own {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 353}. For this reason, riba in loans and gambling on the stock exchange are prohibited, because riba is trading in independent time, and gambling is trading in independent risk {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 353}.
