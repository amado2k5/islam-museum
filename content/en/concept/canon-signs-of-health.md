## Summary
Ibn Sina divides the signs of health into those pointing to an even temperament and those pointing to a sound composition, the latter being essential, accidental and complete, and then shows how the principal organs are read from their functions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Signs of balance and of composition
Some signs of health point to an even temperament, which he promises to treat in its place, and some to a sound composition. These are of three kinds: essential, when form, position, size and number are as they should be; accidental, such as sensation and beauty; and complete, drawn from the completeness of the functions and their continuing in perfection {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Reading organs from functions
Any organ whose function is complete is sound, Ibn Sina says. The brain is read from the voluntary acts, the acts of sense and of estimation; the heart from pulse and breath; the liver from stool and urine, since weakness of the liver brings stool and urine like the washings of fresh meat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.
