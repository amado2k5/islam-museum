## Summary
A wasila is a woman who joins her hair to another's; a mustawsila is one for whom this is done at her request. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16600}

## Joining human hair
Most jurists hold that joining human hair is forbidden, whether a woman's or a man's, on the reports from Asma bint Abi Bakr, Ibn Umar and Mu'awiya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16601} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16602}
A Hanafi view and a Hanbali view dislike it, and one Hanbali view permits it with the husband's leave. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16602}

## Animal hair and other material
The jurists differ on joining animal hair: the Hanafis and one Hanbali view allow it, the Malikis and the Hanbali school forbid it, and the Shafi'is distinguish clean from unclean hair and a married woman from an unmarried one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16602} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16603}
The Hanafis and Hanbalis allow joining with material other than hair, such as cloth; the Malikis forbid it; the permission is reported from Ibn Abbas, Umm Salama and Aisha. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16602} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16603}
