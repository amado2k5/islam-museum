## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Nigeria: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. According to the summary, neither Standard Arabic nor Shuwa Arabic has constitutional official or national status in Nigeria. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Nigeria", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The summary's sequence is short: the 1999 constitution, the 2022 National Language Policy, which included optional Arabic instruction, and its cancellation in November 2025 in favour of English as the sole medium of instruction. The Nigeria Arabic Language Village is mentioned as existing, with no founding date. The summary does not tell the history of Arabic's arrival in Borno, and the museum does not invent it.

## Legal status
According to the summary, neither Standard Arabic nor Shuwa Arabic has constitutional official or national status in Nigeria; under the 1999 Constitution (Sections 55 and 97) English is the primary official language, with Hausa, Igbo and Yoruba alongside it in the National Assembly {cite:nigerian-constitution-section-55-languages} {cite:nerdc-national-language-policy}. The Federal Government operates the Nigeria Arabic Language Village at Ngala, Borno State {cite:nalv-nigeria-arabic-language-village}. The summary adds that the 2022 National Language Policy, which included optional Arabic instruction, was cancelled in November 2025 in favour of English as the sole medium of instruction {cite:tvcnews-fg-cancels-mother-tongue-policy}. This is the summary's statement about its pages; the museum read none of them.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names Shuwa Arabic, that is Nigerian or Chadian Arabic, in north-eastern Nigeria and especially Borno State, and Classical and Modern Standard Arabic in Islamic education and religious contexts {cite:wikipedia-chadian-arabic} {cite:dubawa-muric-fact-check-arabic-inscription}. The Shuwa kind thus runs across the borders with Chad, Cameroon and Niger, as the pages on those countries also show. For comparison see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-chad|Arabic in Chad]] and [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-niger|Arabic in Niger]].

## Official figures
The summary found no official Nigerian census and no UN or UNESCO count of speakers, and none of the pages it names for this section carries a number. On that basis the museum gives no count and no share for speakers of Arabic in Nigeria, and draws no figure from the fact-checking page or from any other page named here.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: Nigerian Constitution, Chapter V, Part I, Section 55: Languages {cite:nigerian-constitution-section-55-languages}, National Language Policy {cite:nerdc-national-language-policy} and Nigeria Arabic Language Village (NALV) {cite:nalv-nigeria-arabic-language-village}; Reference, academic and press pages: FG cancels policy on use of mother tongue, adopts English for child education {cite:tvcnews-fg-cancels-mother-tongue-policy} and Israel's currency has Arabic inscription, Nigeria is not a secular state: other claims by MURIC fact-checked {cite:dubawa-muric-fact-check-arabic-inscription}; Wikipedia pages: Chadian Arabic {cite:wikipedia-chadian-arabic}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
