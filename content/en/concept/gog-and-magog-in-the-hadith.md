## Summary
The hadith on Gog and Magog fall into three groups: the opening of a gap in the rampart in the Prophet's own time, their number set against the people of the Fire, and their coming out at the end of time after the Dajjal and the descent of Jesus. Ibn Kathir stopped at some of them, accepting the chain and rejecting the wording; al-Nawawi stopped at rare words and at ways of reconciling versions. This page carries what was read in the books, gives each judgement to the one who made it, and applies none of it to our own day. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:94-99, pp. 712-716}

## Zaynab bint Jahsh's hadith: a gap opened in the rampart
Al-Bukhari, number 3346, and Muslim, number 2880, relate from Zaynab bint Jahsh that the Prophet came in to her alarmed, or woke from sleep with his face red, saying that there is no god but God, that woe is coming to the Arabs from an evil that has drawn near, and that today a gap like this has been opened in the rampart of Gog and Magog, and he made a ring with his fingers. Zaynab asked whether they would perish while there were righteous people among them, and he said yes, when corruption has multiplied. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3346} {cite:sharh-nawawi-minhaj-risala on Muslim 2880} Al-Nawawi notes that the chain brings together four women Companions, two wives of the Prophet and two stepdaughters, narrating from one another, and that he knows no other hadith with that feature. {cite:sharh-nawawi-minhaj-risala on Muslim 2880} Ibn Kathir agrees that the chain is rare and says al-Bukhari and Muslim both included it. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:96, p. 715}

In Abu Hurayra's hadith, al-Bukhari 3347 and Muslim 2881, the gap is "like this" and Wuhayb made a ring of ninety, while in Zaynab's hadith Sufyan made a ring of ten and Yunus joined two fingers. Al-Nawawi relays Qadi Iyad's view that Abu Hurayra's hadith may be the earlier one and the gap widened afterwards, or that the gesture is meant as an approximation rather than a measurement. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3347} {cite:sharh-nawawi-minhaj-risala on Muslim 2881} Al-Nawawi explains khabath as, for most, immorality and wrongdoing, with some saying adultery specifically; he says the apparent sense is sin in general, and that the hadith means that when khabath multiplies general destruction can fall even where righteous people are present. {cite:sharh-nawawi-minhaj-risala on Muslim 2881}

## Abu Sa'id's hadith: those sent to the Fire, and their number
Al-Bukhari 3348 relates from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that God says to Adam to bring out the contingent of the Fire; Adam asks what it is, and is told that of every thousand it is nine hundred and ninety-nine, at which the young turn grey and every pregnant woman delivers. This weighed on the Companions, who asked which of them would be the one, and he said to rejoice, for from them there would be one man and from Gog and Magog a thousand. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3348} In Muslim's version he then hoped they would be a quarter of the people of Paradise, then a third, then a half. {cite:sahih-muslim} The editors of the edition of Ibn Kathir read give its numbers as al-Bukhari 6530 and Muslim 222. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:92, p. 712}

## al-Nawwas's hadith: their coming out at the end of time
Muslim 2937 is al-Nawwas ibn Sam'an's long hadith on the Dajjal, which ends with God revealing to Jesus that He has brought out servants whom no one has the power to fight, so he should shelter His servants at al-Tur. Gog and Magog are sent and come from every height; the first of them pass the Lake of Tiberias and drink what is in it, and the last say there was once water here. Jesus and his companions are besieged until the head of an ox is worth more to one of them than a hundred dinars. They turn to God and He sends the naghaf worm into their necks, so that they die as one soul; then God sends birds that carry them away and rain that washes the earth. {cite:sharh-nawawi-minhaj-risala on Muslim 2937} Al-Nawawi glosses "no power" as having neither ability nor strength, "shelter" as drawing them in and guarding them, and "they hasten" as walking quickly; the naghaf is a worm found in the noses of camels and sheep, and farsa means the slain. {cite:sharh-nawawi-minhaj-risala on Muslim 2937} Ibn Kathir gives the same hadith at Surat al-Anbiya 96. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 21:96, p. 172 of the Anbiya section}

In Abu Sa'id's report in Ahmad, which Ibn Kathir quotes, they are opened and come out from every height, drink the waters of the earth until a river is left dry, and the Muslims shelter in their strongholds; then they shoot their arrows at the sky and the arrows come back stained with blood as a trial, and God sends a worm in their necks so that they are found dead. The editors of the edition note that it is hasan, with references to Ahmad, Ibn Majah, al-Hakim and Ibn Hibban. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 21:96, p. 171 of the Anbiya section}

## How the exegetes read the verses
At Surat al-Anbiya 96 Ibn Kathir says Gog and Magog are of Adam's line and of Noah's descendants through Japheth, and that they come out as the verse describes, from every high place, hastening to corruption. He reports that Ibn Abbas saw boys playing, leaping on one another, and said this is how Gog and Magog will come out, and that their coming is before the Day of Resurrection and after the Dajjal. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 21:96, pp. 170-171 of the Anbiya section} Al-Qurtubi relates a statement from Ka'b al-Ahbar that they were created from Adam's wet dream and then comments that this is open to question because prophets do not have wet dreams; Ibn Kathir says al-Nawawi reported it in his commentary on Muslim from some people, and calls it very strange, with no support in reason or transmission. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari vol. 11 p. 56} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:92, p. 713}

:::perspectives
**Sunni: Ibn Kathir on Abu Hurayra's hadith of their digging at the rampart.** He quotes the hadith that they dig the rampart every day until they can almost see the sun's rays, and are told to go back and dig again tomorrow, until God wishes to send them out, when they say "if God wills" and dig through. He calls its chain good and strong but says its being raised to the Prophet is objectionable, since the verse implies they could not pierce the barrier; he judges that Abu Hurayra probably received it from Ka'b al-Ahbar and some narrators assumed it was raised. He notes al-Tirmidhi's remark that it is gharib, known only through this route. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:97, p. 715}

**The editors' footnote in the same edition.** They note that al-Hakim transmitted it as meeting the conditions of the two Sheikhs with al-Dhahabi agreeing, that al-Albani graded it sound in al-Silsila al-Sahiha, number 1735, and answered the author's objection, and that some contemporary researchers weakened it in a long study. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:97, p. 715}

**Sunni: al-Qurtubi relays the hadith without comment on its wording.** He gives Ibn Majah's report from Abu Hurayra of the daily digging, the saying "if God wills", and the naghaf. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari vol. 11 p. 57}
:::

## What the sources do not specify
The hadith do not name the place of the rampart, the number of Gog and Magog, or the date of their coming out beyond its being among the signs of the Hour. The reports on their size and appearance, including those al-Qurtubi gives from Ibn Mas'ud and Ali, are set down in the books without a grading by al-Qurtubi, and Ibn Kathir says the reports Ibn Abi Hatim relates on the subject are strange and their chains unsound. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari vol. 11 pp. 56-58} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-harvest-0012994 on 18:94, p. 713} The museum does not link any of these reports to any people or to any event of today.
