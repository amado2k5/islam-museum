## Summary
The forbidden (haram), which the scholars of legal theory also call the prohibited (mahzur) and the made-unlawful (muharram), is the last of the five degrees: the act whose avoidance the Lawgiver required decisively. Under this section the browser gathers every text in the museum's sources that names prohibition in its wording: "it has been made unlawful for you", "God has made unlawful", "it is not lawful", "the unlawful things" and "the sacred limits of God". It reads these texts with the very same lexicon as the Halal and Haram Browser, so the two browsers do not differ.

## Among the scholars of legal theory
Al-Ghazali defines prohibition (hazr) as that for which the address came requiring its avoidance, joined to a warning of punishment for doing it, and he contrasts it with dislike, in which there is no warning of punishment {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.297}. He points out that the word "I dislike" may be uttered while prohibition is meant, as occurs in the speech of al-Shafi'i {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.303}. The place of the forbidden among the degrees is in the entry on [[concept/al-ahkam-al-taklifiyya|the taklifi rulings]], and what concerns the lawful and the unlawful in foods, earnings and marriages is in the entry on [[concept/halal-haram|the lawful and the unlawful]].

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an table places under this section the verses that name prohibition:
- The verses "it has been made unlawful for you" and "He has only made unlawful for you": [[passage/quran-2-173|al-Baqara 173]], [[passage/quran-4-23|al-Nisa' 23]], [[passage/quran-5-3|al-Ma'ida 3]], [[passage/quran-5-96|al-Ma'ida 96]] and [[passage/quran-16-115|al-Nahl 115]].
- What God has made unlawful: the commandments in [[passage/quran-6-151|al-An'am 151]], the indecencies, sin and wrongful aggression in [[passage/quran-7-33|al-A'raf 33]], usury in [[passage/quran-2-275|al-Baqara 275]], and the soul that God has made sacred in [[passage/quran-17-33|al-Isra' 33]] and [[passage/quran-25-68|al-Furqan 68]].
- "It is not lawful for you": [[passage/quran-2-229|al-Baqara 229]], [[passage/quran-4-19|al-Nisa' 19]] and [[passage/quran-33-52|al-Ahzab 52]].
- The prohibition of making unlawful what God has not made unlawful: [[passage/quran-5-87|al-Ma'ida 87]], [[passage/quran-7-32|al-A'raf 32]], [[passage/quran-10-59|Yunus 59]], [[passage/quran-16-116|al-Nahl 116]] and [[passage/quran-66-1|al-Tahrim 1]].

The text of each verse is in its own entry, from the Tanzil text, and is not written here.

## In the hadith
- "God has made certain things obligatory, so do not neglect them; He has set limits, so do not transgress them; He has made certain things unlawful, so do not violate them" [[passage/arbaun-30|Forty Hadith of al-Nawawi 30]].
- The hadith of al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, that God has made unlawful disobedience to mothers, the burying alive of daughters, and withholding and demanding, and has disliked "gossip, excessive questioning and the wasting of wealth" [[passage/bukhari-api-2408|al-Bukhari 2408]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Bukhari 2408}. This text brings together in one passage the word for prohibition and the word for dislike, and for that reason it appears in both the section on the forbidden acts and the section on [[concept/makruh|the disliked acts]].
- The saying of Ibn 'Abbas: the people of the Jahiliyya used to eat some things and leave others out of disgust, and God sent His Prophet and sent down His Book, and made lawful what He made lawful and made unlawful what He made unlawful, "so what He made lawful is lawful, and what He made unlawful is unlawful, and what He was silent about is pardoned" [[passage/abudawud-api-3800|Abu Dawud 3800]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Abu Dawud 3800}.

## The bare prohibition
Many hadiths of prohibition do not name the degree of what is forbidden, and the jurists differ over taking a prohibition as forbidding or as disliking. The browser does not classify the bare prohibition; so a hadith "he forbade such and such" does not enter this section unless the word for prohibition occurs in it. An example of a prohibition whose narrator stated that it was not decisive is the saying of Umm 'Atiyya, "We were forbidden to follow funerals, but it was not made binding upon us" [[passage/bukhari-api-1278|al-Bukhari 1278]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Bukhari 1278}, and it is in the section on the disliked acts.

## What falls outside this section
Not included here are the Sanctuary in the sense of Mecca and Medina, nor the Sacred Mosque and the sacred month, nor the pilgrim in a state of consecration, nor "Haram" as a proper name, nor "God has made the Fire unlawful for him". As for the major sins, they have the Hudud and Kaba'ir Browser, and [[concept/minor-sins-saghair|minor sins]] have their own section in this browser.
