## Summary
In the third chapter Ibn Sina limits the diseases of composition to four genera, those of constitution, quantity, number and position, and divides those of constitution into shape, channels, vessels and cavities, and the surfaces of organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.105}.

## Diseases of constitution
Diseases of constitution are four genera. The first are diseases of shape, where the shape changes from its natural course and the change causes harm in the act, like the straight made crooked, the crooked made straight, the round made square or the square made round; to this belong a very large head when it does harm, excessive roundness of the stomach, and the absence of the hole in the pupil. The second are diseases of channels, in three kinds: they widen, as in spread of the eye, vascular keratitis and varices; narrow, as with a narrowed eye hole or narrowed breath passages and gullet; or are blocked, as with a blocked pupil or the vessels of the liver. The third are diseases of vessels and cavities, which may grow and widen, like the widening of the scrotal sac; shrink and narrow, like a narrowed stomach or the narrowing of the brain's ventricles in epilepsy; be blocked and filled, like the blocking of the brain's ventricles in apoplexy; or be drained and empty, like the heart's cavities emptied of blood in fatal joy or fatal pleasure. The fourth are diseases of the surfaces of organs, either made smooth where they should be rough, like the stomach and gut, or rough where they should be smooth, like the windpipe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.105}.

## Quantity and number
Diseases of quantity are of two kinds: of the genus of excess, like elephantiasis and enlargement of the penis, and as befell a man called Nicomachus whose organs all swelled until he could not move; and of the genus of deficiency, like wasting of the tongue and pupil and like withering. Diseases of number are either of excess, natural like an extra tooth or finger or unnatural like a tumour, or of deficiency, whether in nature, as for one born without a finger, or not in nature, as for one whose finger has been cut off {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.105}.

## Diseases of position
Position for Galen involves both place and association. Diseases of position are four: the dislocation of an organ from its joint; its displacement from its place without dislocation, as in hernia of the intestines; its movement in place not along the natural or voluntary course, as in tremor; or its being fixed in place so that it does not move from it, as happens when the joints turn stony {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.105}.
