## Summary
Ibn Sina says that a vessel may be opened to stop a flow of blood from the nose, the womb or the anus by turning it to the opposite side, that more bleedings are better than a larger quantity, and that a bleeding that was not needed stirs bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.292-293}.

## Bleeding toward the opposite side
A vessel may be opened to stop the flow of blood from the nose, the womb or the anus toward the opposite side. This is a strong, useful treatment, and the incision must be very narrow. The bleedings are many, not in one day unless necessity compels, but day after day, and each time as little as possible {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.292-293}.

## More in number, not in amount
In sum, multiplying the number of bleedings is better suited than multiplying their amount, and a bleeding for which there was no need stirs bile and is followed by dryness of the tongue and the like {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.293}.
