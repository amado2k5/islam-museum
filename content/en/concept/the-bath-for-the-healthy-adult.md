## Summary
In chapter 5, on bathing and the baths, Ibn Sina says the person whose regimen he describes here has a clean body and needs no dissolving bath, only a gentle heat and moderate moistening; he should not stay long, should leave when dissolving begins, and the exerciser should not hurry to the bath until fully rested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.

## The clean body's need of the bath
The person whose regimen is being described has no need of the dissolving bath because his body is clean; he needs the bath only to gain a gentle heat and a moderate moistening, so these people should not stay long in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.

## How to enter
If they use the tub they use it only until the skin reddens and swells, and leave when dissolving starts. They should cool the air by pouring sweet water around them, wash quickly and go out, and the exerciser should not hurry to the bath until he has fully rested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.

## Going by degrees
The conditions and rules of baths have been set out elsewhere. What should be said here is that all bathers should pass by degrees into the rooms of the bath and stay in the hot room only as long as they are not distressed, so they gain by the dissolving of residues and the readying of the body for food, while guarding against weakness and against a strong cause of putrid fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.226}.
