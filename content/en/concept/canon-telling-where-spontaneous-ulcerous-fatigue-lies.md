## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the sixteenth chapter, on spontaneous fatigue, by saying that for the ulcerous kind one must learn whether the humour that causes it is inside the vessels or outside, from the stench of the urine, the foods of the past days, his habit in generating superfluities and the clarity or turbidity of his drink; the outside kind is treated with restorative exercise, the other with rest, hunger, oiling and bathing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.247}.

## Finding the seat
For the ulcerous kind his state must be learned: whether the humour that causes it is inside the vessels, from the stench of the urine, the foods of past days, his habit of generating many superfluities in his vessels or few, with quick clearing, and whether what he drank was clear or turbid. If these signs point that way it is in the vessels, and otherwise it stands outside. Some words of this passage are garbled in the printed text and are not taken {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.247}.

## Treatment by seat
If the fatigue comes from superfluities outside and the inside of the vessels is clean, restorative exercise suffices, with the regimen given in the chapter on the ulcerous kind that comes from exercise. If it is the other kind, do not expose him to exercise, but give him rest, sleep and hunger, rub him each evening with oil and bathe him in moderate water if he can bear a bath, and feed him a little of what improves his chyme, of the kind of broths without much stickiness or much nourishment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.247}.
