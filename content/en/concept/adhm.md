## Summary
In the lexicon, adhm is the bone that has flesh on it; its plurals are 'idham and 'izam {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11666}.

## Purity of bones
Most jurists hold a human bone pure, living or dead, and the bone of a lawfully slaughtered edible animal pure; a fish bone remains pure after death {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11666}. The bone of carrion or of a non-edible animal is impure according to the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis; the Hanafis hold it pure {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11666} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11667}.

## Cleansing with a bone
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis forbid it; the Hanafis dislike it as forbidden but accept that it suffices; the Malikis distinguish the pure bone from the impure one {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11667} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11668}.

## Retaliation for a bone
Most jurists allow retaliation for a bone only at a joint {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11668}.

## Where to look further
Slaughter with a bone is treated under «slaughter», and ivory under «ivory» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11668}.
