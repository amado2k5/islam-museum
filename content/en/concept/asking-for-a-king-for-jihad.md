## Summary
Sha'rawi recounts the notables of Banu Isra'il who asked their prophet to send them a king to fight under his command {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1041}.

## The place of the prophet and the king
He holds that prophethood oversees how matters are carried out but does not run them. The king does the work. So they asked for a king to take the blame for management, leaving prophethood as a reference for truth {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1042}.

## The reversal
He reports that the prophet warned them that if fighting were prescribed they might fail, and they answered that they had been driven from their homes and children. When fighting was prescribed, only a few stayed. He calls their turning away a wrong to themselves and to their cause {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1043} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1045}.
