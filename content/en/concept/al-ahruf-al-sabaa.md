## Overview
Al-Bukhari transmits the Prophet's saying that the Qur'an was revealed in seven *ahruf* (modes), so that whatever is easy of it may be recited. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2419}

## The report
It was transmitted by Umar, when he heard Hisham ibn Hakim reciting Surat al-Furqan differently from his own recitation. He led him to the Prophet, who had each of them recite, and said to each that it had been revealed in that form. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2419}

## The meaning of the ahruf
Scholars differ widely on what is meant by the seven ahruf. Some counted them as seven aspects: command and prohibition, promise and warning, disputation, narratives, and parables. Others took them to be differences in vowelling, omission, anticipation and postponement, and substitution. Others linked them to the readings of the Arab tribes. The question is among the most delicate in the sciences of the Qur'an. The attribution of each view to its author would need a further source.

The hadith is linked to [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]]. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2419}


## Al-Tabari's view
In the introduction to [[work/jami-al-bayan|Jami' al-Bayan]], [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]] wrote a section titled "On the language among the languages of the Arabs in which the Qur'an was revealed", gathering the hadith of the seven ahruf in its chains, chief among them the report of Umar ibn al-Khattab and Hisham ibn Hakim ibn Hizam disputing over a recitation. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.76} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.77} His view is that the seven ahruf are seven of the Arabs' dialects within a single harf and a single word — different words with the same meaning, as one says halumma, aqbil, ta'al, ilayya ("come") — for the Arabs' dialects are more than seven. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.77} He rested this on the hadith of Abu Bakra through Hammad ibn Salama from Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an, in which Gabriel had the Prophet recite on one harf and then more, up to seven, each healing and sufficient so long as a verse of punishment is not closed with mercy or a verse of mercy with punishment; Ahmad Khalid Babakr notes that Ali ibn Zayd is in every chain of it and that Ibn Ma'in and Ibn Hibban judged him weak. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.77} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.78} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.79} Al-Tabari argues that making them dialects scattered through the Qur'an, as al-Zuhri held, does not explain why Companions differed over the same place. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.82} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.83}

## Kinds of views
The thesis notes that al-Suyuti lists thirty-five views and al-Zurqani forty, without naming who held them, and sorts them into two kinds: one reads them as meanings — command and prohibition, lawful and unlawful, clear and ambiguous, parables — or as names of the Lord, or as letters of the alphabet; the other is linguistic, in ten forms, among them that they are seven dialects of named tribes, and that they are the dialects of the tribes for one meaning, like halumma and ta'al, close to the view of [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Sufyan ibn Uyayna]] and al-Tabari. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.80} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.81} The thesis rejects the first kind because the hadith settled a dispute over pronunciation and delivery among Companions, not over rulings and meanings, and it rejects Ibn Sa'dan the grammarian's view that the hadith is among the ambiguous whose meaning is unknown, since no Companion is reported to have found it obscure. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.83} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.84} It cites Ahmad's report from [[person/ubayy-ibn-kab|Ubayy ibn Ka'b]] on the progression from one harf to seven, with Mahmud Shakir's judgement that its chain is sound. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.85} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.86}

## The thesis's preference
Ahmad Khalid Babakr prefers the view of Abu al-Fadl al-Razi: the variation falls within seven aspects — nouns in singular, dual and plural; the conjugation of verbs; grammatical case; omission and addition; order; substitution; and vocal delivery such as lengthening, inclination (imala), heavy and light articulation. He holds that the survival of such variation in the mass-transmitted readings refutes al-Tabari's view that the codex was written on only one of the seven ahruf. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.87}

## In Ibn Atiyya
In the introduction to [[work/tafsir-ibn-atiyya|al-Muharrar al-Wajiz]], [[person/ibn-atiyah-al-andalusi|Ibn Atiyya]] devotes a chapter to the hadith, noting that people differed sharply over it. He judges weak the view that they are what may be said in up to seven ways, like ta'al and aqbil, and the view that they are the Book's meanings — command, prohibition, promise, threat — since these are not called ahruf and the latitude never extended to making the unlawful lawful or the reverse. {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.21} He reports Qadi Abu Bakr Ibn al-Tayyib ([[person/al-baqillani|al-Baqillani]]) on the seven ways readings differ and his rejection of the view that they are seven dialects, then answers him that Quraysh, the Ansar and Hudhayl could use different expressions for one meaning. {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.21} {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.22} {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.23} He prefers that the Qur'an contains the expressions of seven tribes of Mudar and came down in the language of the whole group: Quraysh, then the Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr, then Kinana, Hudhayl, Thaqif, Khuza'a, Asad and Dabba, then Tamim and Qays; their speech stayed pure because they lived in the middle of the peninsula — the Hijaz, Najd and Tihama — unlike Yemen and the lands bordering Iraq and Syria. {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.23} {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.24} The latitude, he says, was given to the Prophet in what Gabriel recited with him, not to each Companion to change a word of his own accord. {cite:muharrar-wajiz-dar-ibn-hazm p.24}

## Al-Tabari in his own text (volume 1 of the Shakir edition)

Al-Tabari rejects the view that the seven ahruf are seven different words scattered through the Qur'an. For him each ahruf is a different wording of the same word with one meaning {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 57}. He holds that reciting in the other six ahruf was a permission, not an obligation: had it been obligatory, knowledge of each one would have had to reach the community by a secure transmission {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 64}. The imam who gathered the community on one written text is Uthman, as the volume's index names him {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 620}. Al-Tabari says that the community accepted his decision, that the other ahruf were no longer known, and that Muslims read today only the one ahruf chosen for them {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 64}.
