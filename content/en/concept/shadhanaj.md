## Nature
It is sometimes found in the mine and is sometimes dug upon the stone of shadhanaj from the mines of Egypt, and it may be adulterated; the book describes a test by rubbing it on a whetstone and looking at its colour, with a time of burying in hot ashes that is left out as part of the method of testing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.680}

## Choice
The best of this kind is that which crumbles quickly, of even hardness, with no dirt mixed in it and without lines and different colours; the adulterated is told from the rest by the absence of bubbles in it and by how the stone breaks along straight lines, and by colour, for the stone that is not shadhanaj, when rubbed, is less red in colour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.680}

## Temperament
The unwashed is hot in the first degree, dry to the third; the washed is cold to the second, dry to the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.680}

## Actions and properties
It has strong astringency, which shows when it is rubbed in water until it dissolves in it and thickens it; its power is preventive, with some heating, subtilising and a thorough drying; some said it has the power of marqashitha but is drier and less hot, without subtilising and cleansing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.680}

## Ulcers
It is used as a dusting on proud flesh and shrinks it greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.680}

## Ocular organs
It cleanses the ulcers of the eye and heals them when used with egg white, and alone for roughness of the eyelids; if there are hot swellings it is used first thinned with water and then thickened by degrees, or dusted like powder on proud flesh; alone it may benefit the scars of eye ulcers, it benefits ophthalmia with milk, and it helps with a protrusion in some of the tunics; the physicians were right to mix it into eye salves, and it is said that using it alone is better for roughness of the eyelids, and that all this has been tested and tried and found beneficial {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.680-681}

## Excretory organs
It is given to drink with the sharab for difficulty of urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it is given to drink for the continual flow of the menses, and that shadhanaj is good for the ejaculation of semen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.681}
