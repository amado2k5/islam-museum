## The starting points
Ibn Sina says the starting points from which we come to know the state of the brain are the sensory acts and the governing acts, meaning remembering, thinking, forming, the power of estimation and intuition, and the motor acts, which are the acts of the power that moves the organs by means of the muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10}

Then the kind of what is expelled from it of superfluities in consistency, colour and taste, and its amount, little or much, or its complete retention {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10}

Then the agreement or disagreement of airs and foods with it and their harm to it, the largeness and smallness of the head, the goodness or badness of its shape, its heaviness and lightness, the feel of the head and its colour and the colour of its veins {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10}

The sores and swellings in its skin, the colour of the eye and its veins, its soundness and illness and its feel, the state of sleep and waking, and the hair in its amount and quality {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10} The state of the neck in its thickness and thinness and the frequency of swellings and scrofula in it, and likewise the state of the uvula, the tonsils and the teeth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10}

And the state of the powers and acts in the nervous organs that share with the brain, of which he names the stomach and the bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10}

## Inference about sharing, and about what is to come
Inference about sharing goes two ways: one from the state of the organ that shares with the brain, what befalls it befalling the brain; the other from the state of the organ with which the brain suffers by sharing, which organ it is, what is wrong with it and how it reaches the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.11}

These inferences can be drawn about what is present of acts and states, and about what will come and is not yet present {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.11} For example, from long grief and loneliness one infers melancholia, or qutrub arriving soon {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.11} From anger without a reason one infers epilepsy or melancholia, hot, and mania {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.11} From laughter without a cause one infers folly or rashness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.11}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
In listing the nervous organs that share with the brain, Ibn Sina names the womb together with the stomach and the bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.10}
