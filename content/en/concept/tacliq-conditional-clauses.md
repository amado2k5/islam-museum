## Definition
Ta'liq is, in language, hanging one thing on another. In law it links the occurrence of one clause to the occurrence of another, and is called an oath by metaphor, since it contains a condition and its consequence and a sense of cause. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5463} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5464}

## Distinctions
Idafa in the jurists' usage is attributing a ruling to a person or quality, or specifying it by time. Ta'liq differs from it in being an oath meant to move a person to act or refrain, and in having an uncertain condition, while idafa has no uncertainty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5463} A legal condition is one whose absence entails absence of the ruling; a condition the parties agree upon is what contracting parties stipulate. Ta'liq puts the particle on the act itself, while shart makes the act certain and adds another condition. Ta'liq and oath resemble each other in moving the self toward or away from an act. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5463} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5464}

## Forms
Ta'liq may be made by any particle, or without one where the context shows the link. An example with a particle: if you enter the house, you are divorced; without one: the profit that returns to me this year is given to the poor. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5464}

## The particles
The particles listed are in, idha, mata, mata ma, mahma, man, ayy and kullama, and others add law, kayfa, haythu, ayna and anna. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5465}

## In
In is the basic conditional particle, since its only sense is condition and linking; it is used for what may or may not occur. Because it is a pure condition, a man who makes the divorce of his wife depend on his not divorcing her is not divorced until one of them dies; if the wife dies first, she is divorced in one of two accounts. For the Hanbalis in is delayed, while mata, man, kullama and ayy act at once. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5465} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5466}

## Idha
Idha is sudden-occurrence when it takes a nominal clause and has the sense of now; otherwise it is a time adverb for the future with the sense of condition. For Abu Hanifa, when it is a condition, it loses its time sense and becomes like in; Abu Yusuf and Muhammad keep the time sense. The effect appears when a man says: idha lam uttalliqik, you are divorced; if he meant time it takes effect at once, if condition not until one dies, and without intention not until one dies on Abu Hanifa's view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5466} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5467}

## Mata
Mata is a noun for time, then given the sense of condition. It differs from idha in that idha is used for what must occur, while mata is used for what may or may not. It is used as a condition while keeping its time sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5467} When the act is linked to a negation with mata, and a span of time passes in which the act could have happened without it happening, the divorce falls. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5467}

## Mata ma, man, ma, mahma
Mata ma follows mata in rule and is stronger, since ma makes it a pure condition. Man and ma are nouns for rational and non-rational beings and take general form by their wording, and they replace in when generality is meant, as in: whoever comes, I honour him; whatever you do, I do. The ma that is verbal (masdariyya) in fiqh gives attribution, not linking: if he says you are divorced for as long as I do not divorce you and stays silent, the divorce falls by agreement. Mahma is for what is not rational and then takes the sense of condition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5468} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5469}

## Ayy
Ayy follows what it is attached to: for persons it acts like man, for things like ma, for time like mata, and for place like ayna. If it is used to negate an act, the divorce falls after a span in which the act was possible. Tabyin al-haqa'iq holds that ayy does not generalise by the generality of the attribute, so that whoever I marry is divorced applies to one woman only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5469}

## Kull and kullama
Kull is used for totality by context and sometimes for multitude, and it is always attached to a noun; kullama carries the generality of what it is attached to. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5469}
