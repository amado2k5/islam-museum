## Summary
Ibn Sina lists what excessively hot air does to the body, and which patients it may help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.125-126}.

## Its effects
If the air grows very hot it loosens the joints and dissolves the moistures, so thirst increases and the spirit is dissolved, which drops the powers and hinders digestion by dissolving the innate heat inside, which is nature's instrument. It yellows the colour by dissolving the reddish sanguine humours and giving bile the upper hand over the other humours, heats the heart with a heat that is not innate, and makes the humours flow and putrefy and incline toward the cavities and the weak organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.125}.

## His verdict
He says it is not good for bodies in a sound state, yet it may benefit those with dropsy, the paralysed, and those with cold tetanus, cold catarrh, moist spasm and moist facial palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.125-126}.
