## Summary
Al-Battani teaches how to find the chord of any degree from the tables: the number is looked up in the row of numbers differing by half a degree and the degrees, minutes and seconds facing it are taken; when the degree carries minutes, the entry facing the nearest whole or half degree is taken and corrected by the share of the remaining minutes out of thirty, multiplied into the difference between the two successive chords. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.19-20}.

## Direct reading
Whoever wants the chord of a particular degree seeks his number in the row of numbers differing by half a degree in the table of half-chords and takes the degrees, minutes and seconds facing it, which is the chord of those degrees. If the number appears in the table no correction is needed. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.19}.

## The extra minutes
If the degrees carry minutes, more than thirty or fewer, take the entry beside the nearest whole or half degree and keep it; subtract the number in the row from yours; multiply the minutes that remain by the difference between the kept chord and the one next to it; divide that by thirty minutes, the step of the numbers in the row; and add the result to the kept chord if it is the smaller, or subtract it if it is the larger. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.20}.

## The proportion method
If you prefer, work out what share of thirty minutes the leftover minutes are, a half, a third, less or more, take the same share of the difference between the chords, and apply it by addition or subtraction; the result is the chord of the arc you wanted. This is the linear interpolation astronomers used in tables, and al-Battani describes it as proportion. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.20}.
