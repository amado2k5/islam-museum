## Overview
A historical rule that al-Suyuti cites at the end of *Tarikh al-khulafa'*, from Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As: whatever has been since the world began, at the head of every hundred years there has been a matter. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.796-799}

## What al-Suyuti counts
- The first century: the tribulation of al-Hajjaj.
- The second: the tribulation of al-Ma'mun and his wars with his brother, then the testing of people over the createdness of the Qur'an, which he counts among the greatest tribulations of this community.
- The third: the rising of the Qarmatian and the tribulation of al-Muqtadir and the dispersal of unity, and from it began the Ubaydid state.
- The fourth: the tribulation of al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah.
- The fifth: the Franks' seizure of Syria and Jerusalem.
- The sixth: a famine unheard of, and the beginning of the affair of the Tatars.
- The seventh: the great tribulation of the Tatars.
- The eighth: the tribulation of Timur Lang.

{cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.796-799}

## The ninth century
Al-Suyuti asked God to take him before the tribulation of the ninth century. His student Jarmard al-Nasiri al-Hanafi said that it was the tribulation of Selim Shah ibn Uthman, his wars, and his entry into Egypt, and that God answered the prayer of his teacher. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.796-799}

This is the classification of al-Suyuti and his student, not a ruling by the museum.
