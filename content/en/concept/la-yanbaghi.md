## Summary
Imam Ahmad's companions differed over whether his phrase "la yanbaghi" (it is not fitting) should be carried to prohibition or to disapproval {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 127}. Ibn al-Qayyim noted that many later scholars removed prohibition from what the imams had called disapproved, then carried "disapproval" to mere indecorousness or to disapproval of leaving the better {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 128}.

## The positions
- Ibn Muflih in al-Furu': the companions carry these expressions to prohibition {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 127}.
- Ibn Hamdan in al-Ri'aya: "la yanbaghi" is carried to disapproval; "I dislike" and "it does not please me" to recommendation — Ibn Taymiyya chose this {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 127}.
- The book transmits from al-Ri'ayatayn, al-Hawi al-Kabir and Adab al-Mustafti that the better course is to look to the contextual clues in each expression: if they indicate obligation, recommendation, prohibition, disapproval or permissibility, the saying is carried to it; al-Mardawi approved this view {cite:madkhal-ibn-badran-turki p. 128}.
