## Summary
In the sixth chapter Ibn Sina makes the exercise of the old depend on the state of their bodies, the illnesses that beset them and their habit of exercise: moderate exercise for balanced bodies, exercise of the lower half for a head troubled by vertigo or epilepsy, upper exercise for a fault in the leg, and upper exercise alone for a fault in the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.254}.

## According to condition
The exercise of the old differs according to the condition of their bodies, the illnesses that beset them and their habit of exercise. If their bodies are in the utmost balance, they use moderate exercises. A few words here are garbled in the printing and are not taken {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.254}.

## Head, leg, middle and chest
If one organ is not at its best, the exercise is made to follow the other organs. Those whose head is troubled by vertigo or epilepsy, by matter flowing to the neck, or by vapours often rising to the head, lean to exercise by walking, running and riding, and every exercise of the lower half. If the fault is toward the leg, upper exercises are used, such as throwing stones. If it lies in the middle region, in the spleen, liver, stomach or intestines, both the end exercises suit them unless something prevents. If it is in the chest, only upper exercise suits them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.254}.

## Weak and diseased organs
The old are not to bring weak organs gradually into exercise to strengthen them by it, which differs from the other ages and from the worn-out old, for whom most of what suits the old suits, and who strengthen weak organs by bringing them gradually into the fitting kind of exercise. As for diseased organs, they may sometimes exercise them and sometimes are not permitted, when the organ is hot or dry or holds matter feared to turn to putrefaction without ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.254}.
