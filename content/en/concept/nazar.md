## Summary
Nazar, in the lexicon, is the sense of sight or considering a thing with the eye; it can also mean keeping or contemplating, and ru'ya (seeing) is narrower. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15762} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15763}
Its rulings vary with the person looked at, the one who looks, the purpose and whether desire is involved.

## A man looking at a foreign woman
For a young foreign woman, looking at her private parts is forbidden by agreement. On the face and hands the jurists differ in four views: permitted when there is no desire and no fear of temptation (Hanafis and Malikis); all of the body forbidden (the sounder Shafi'i view and the Hanbali school); the rest forbidden with the face and hands disliked; and the face, hands and feet permitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15763} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15764} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15765} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15766} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15767}
For an old woman who is not desired, the majority of Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis permit the face and hands; the settled Shafi'i view forbids them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15768} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15769}

## A man looking at a girl, mahrams and others
Looking at a girl with desire is forbidden at any age; without desire, all of her body except the private parts is allowed by the jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15769}
With mahrams, looking with desire is forbidden, looking between navel and knee is forbidden in all cases, and places of adornment may be seen without desire. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15770}
Men without need (eunuchs, castrated men, effeminate men and the impotent) are debated: the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis differ on whether they count as mahrams. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15770} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15771} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15772}
An adolescent is treated as an adult by most jurists; a child who cannot distinguish nakedness has no veil before the women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15773} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15774}

## Detached parts, mirrors, the dead, men and beardless youths
A part detached from a woman: most jurists forbid looking at what was forbidden before separation; the Malikis permit it for a part severed in life. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15774} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15775}
Looking at a woman in a mirror or water is not forbidden, according to Ibn Abidin and al-Ramli; some Shafi'is forbid it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15775}
A dead woman is treated as when alive, except in necessity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15776}
A man may not look at another man's private parts without a reason; looking with desire at a beardless youth is forbidden, and in the Hanafi and Shafi'i view it is a greater sin than looking at a woman with desire. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15776} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15777}

## Women looking at men, women and the hermaphrodite
A woman looking at a foreign man is forbidden with desire; without desire, four views are reported, including permission above the navel and below the knee and a view that she may look as a man does. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15777} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15778} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15779} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15780}
A woman may look at another woman, without desire, outside the part between navel and knee according to most jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15781} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15782}
On a non-Muslim woman looking at a Muslim woman, the sounder Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi'i view treats her like a foreign man; others treat her like a Muslim woman. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15782} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15783} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15784}
The hermaphrodite is treated with caution: as a man among women and as a woman among men. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15787}

## Spouses and self
Spouses may look at each other's whole body, with views on the private parts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15785} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15786}
Looking at one's own private parts without need is disliked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15787}

## Permitted looking for need
A sudden glance is forgiven; the sin lies in continuing it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15787} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15788}
Looking at a woman to propose is recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15788}
Treatment is allowed under conditions: a real need, looking only as far as needed, no seclusion, and no same-sex practitioner available. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15788} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15789} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15790} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15791}
Judging, testimony, dealing and teaching allow looking to the extent of the need, with views on the witness and the teacher. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15791} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15792} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15793} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15794}
