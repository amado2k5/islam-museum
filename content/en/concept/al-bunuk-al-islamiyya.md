# Islamic banks and conventional banks — the difference, the doubts and the performance

## Summary
The [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|Al-Wasatiyya]] programme on Al-Resalah channel gave two episodes, the thirty-fourth and thirty-fifth, to Islamic banks: the first to the difference between an Islamic and a conventional bank and the basics of riba and the contracts, the second to the Islamic banks' efficiency, the doubts about them and their role in society. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:01:00} Introducing them, [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] said money is the sinew of life and Islam does not fight wealth, and that Islamic banks came to fill a gap: he recalls that when he was growing up there was no Islamic bank in the world, that the first was [[listing/dib|Dubai Islamic Bank]] in 1975 and then [[listing/kfh|Kuwait Finance House]], thirty or forty years against four hundred years of interest banking. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:01:30} He chose to call the non-Islamic bank "conventional" rather than "usurious" in the episode. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:02:30} He said neither a call to faith nor a community can stand without an economy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:03:30}

## First episode: what is riba?
The presenter asked the young audience whether they could define riba: only 6% said precisely, 38% in general terms, and 56% could not. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:09:30} He asked viewers by text message whether the differences between Islamic and conventional banks are large and fundamental or small and formal; the result was not read out. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:05:30}

- A woman student defined riba as any increase on borrowed money, and a young man as a fixed interest rate. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:10:30}
- **Rajab Abu Mulayh**, sharia adviser to the Islam Online website, said riba has two kinds: riba al-fadl in the six items the Sunna specified, and riba al-nasi'a. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:11:30} The presenter simplified: riba al-fadl is exchanging a kilo of one item for two kilos of the same, the lawful way being to sell the better quality for cash and buy with the proceeds; riba al-nasi'a, the episode's subject, is an increase fixed in advance on the capital in return for delay. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:13:30}
- **Hamid al-Attar**, member of the International Union of Muslim Scholars and sharia researcher at Islam Online, added that riba al-nasi'a is also called the riba of debts, and that the increase need not be of a known percentage nor stipulated at the start: whoever lends interest-free and then tells a debtor who cannot pay "I will give you another year with an increase" has fallen into riba. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:15:00}
- **Salah Sultan**, sharia adviser to Bahrain's Supreme Council for Islamic Affairs and professor at Dar al-Ulum, Cairo University, put the rule: every loan that brings a stipulated benefit is riba; a gift on repayment without condition or agreement is not. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:16:30} He distinguished a loan, money one may use and must return in kind, from a trust that may not be used, and the presenter noted that a "deposit" in banking language is not a deposit in fiqh. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:18:00}
- **Muhammad al-Banna**, professor of sharia with studies in Islamic economics and in replies to fatwas permitting bank interest, was asked about the claim that only riba "doubled and multiplied" is forbidden; he said this understanding is wrong, since a small increase, when the debtor cannot pay, breeds increase upon increase. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:19:30} Salah Sultan later said the decisive verse is the command to give up what remains of riba, which some jurists consider abrogating. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:32:00}
- **Muhammad al-Azizi**, professor of comparative fiqh from Jordan, author of about seventeen books, eight of them on economics and comparative fiqh, divided riba into three: the riba of loans, the riba of deferred sale, and riba al-fadl, and held that every increase on account of time is riba, whether through a loan or a sale. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:22:30}
- **Abd al-Hamid al-Ghazali**, professor of Islamic economics at Cairo University, asked about worldly punishment, said riba wipes out the blessing of wealth and attacks the economy's immune system, and that in a state applying the sharia its punishment is a discretionary penalty (ta'zir) set by the ruler or judge, since it has no fixed penalty. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:25:30}

## First episode: the contracts
The presenter illustrated a conventional bank: it takes deposits and pays them 7%, lends at 12%, and the difference is its profit; Islamic banks do not lend this way but use contracts, chief among them murabaha, salam, tawarruq, istisna', mudaraba and musharaka. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:26:30} Asked about these contracts, only 3% of the young audience said they knew them precisely, 18% knew some, and 79% knew nothing about them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:31:30}

- **Murabaha**: al-Ghazali explained that the client asks for a good, the bank buys and takes possession of it and bears the consequences of ownership, so that if it perishes it perishes at the bank's risk, then sells it to the client at an agreed profit. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:34:30} The presenter summed up the two differences as he understands them: the Islamic bank sells something it owns, while the conventional bank lends you money to buy something it does not own; and in the Islamic bank the price is fixed after the contract and does not rise with delay, while in the conventional bank it rises the longer the term. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:37:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:38:00}
- **Mudaraba**: al-Banna explained it as an owner of capital giving his money to a skilled worker who has none, who works with it, the profit shared in an agreed proportion; the presenter said the basis of Islamic banks' work is mudaraba, musharaka and murabaha, so much so that some say they should be called investment companies. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:40:30}
- **Salam**: the buyer pays the price in advance and receives the good later, on condition that the good is described closely enough to prevent dispute; it is the reverse of instalment sale. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:42:00}
- **'Ina and tawarruq**: the presenter explained 'ina as someone in need buying a good from a moneyed person on deferred payment and at once selling it back to him for a lower cash price, so the good stays with its owner and the deal becomes a loan with an increase; he said it is a forbidden sale whose prohibition the Sunna states. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:45:30} He explained tawarruq as the same cycle run among three parties instead of two, said tawarruq is the only disputed contract among those mentioned, used by some banks and not others, and that no Islamic bank gives loans at interest. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:49:00}

## First episode: are they Islamic?
After this explanation the presenter asked whether Islamic banks have no doubtful element, a few, or many: the audience split into thirds, 33%, 33% and 34%, and he said no audience of his had split evenly before. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:49:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:01:30}

- A woman student saw a large doubt because the banks concentrated on murabaha, which encourages consumption, rather than mudaraba, which builds production, and the presenter endorsed her point. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:57:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:58:30} A young man said clients can cheat in salam and tawarruq; the presenter answered with the rule that the forbidden does not make the lawful forbidden, citing the story of the charity given to Barira and gifted by her to the Prophet. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:56:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:57:00}
- One of the women guests held that the doubts are few and do not take the banks out of being Islamic, that the fiqh academies rejected organised tawarruq as resembling 'ina even though individuals permitted it, and that some contracts may be carried out only in form because some staff do not understand their basis. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:58:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:59:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 00:59:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:00:00} The presenter remarked that an employee's error is not the bank's doubt, and that banks thirty or forty years old are bound to make mistakes. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:00:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:00:30}
- Al-Azizi said that in his view Islamic banks are interest banks differing only in form, since every increase on account of time is riba, that the first planners did not mention murabaha, and gave the example of a client who found the Islamic bank dearer than the conventional housing bank, with a mortgage besides. The presenter put to him the example of the marriage contract: the outcome is the same but the contract separates lawful from unlawful; al-Azizi answered that murabaha to the purchase orderer has no basis in fiqh. The presenter summed up that he supports the Islamic bank as an idea, not the existing banks. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:05:30}
- Al-Banna held that Islamic banks now deal in goods rather than money, and need encouragement and purging of disputed practices such as organised tawarruq; sharia supervisory boards have a large role, and he trusts their members' knowledge and faith. The guests then debated whether such boards should be appointed or elected. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:08:00}
- Salah Sultan held that "the cart came before the horse", since the banks began before their staff were trained; that the Islamic Research and Training Institute had drawn up a full model of the Islamic bank twenty-five years earlier; and that the experiment is a serious attempt in a world economy run on interest, but it resorted to easy murabaha instead of mudaraba and musharaka. He said its doubts are very few, that he trusts its sharia councils, and that he is not a member of any bank's supervisory board. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:10:00}
- Al-Ghazali closed by saying these banks arose in a sea of riba and some of its dust settled on them, but they came to stay; necessity pushed them into murabaha until in many of them it exceeded 90% of their financing, and then banks, especially in the UAE, began returning to long-term investment until mudaraba and musharaka exceeded 90% in them; these are his figures. He said a depositor in an Islamic bank can be at ease, and any errors are borne by those who run it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:14:30} He cited the decisions of the three fiqh academies forbidding bank interest: Egypt's Islamic Research Academy at its second session in 1965, the Fiqh Academy of the Organisation of the Islamic Conference at its ninth session in 1985, and the Muslim World League's academy in Makka in 1406 AH (1985). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:17:30}

The presenter closed the first episode by saying a fiqh academy has about a hundred and twenty members, scholars, economists and merchants, that the guests differed over Islamic banks but agreed that conventional banks deal in riba, that Islamic banks need correction, development and purging, and that the differences between them and conventional banks are fundamental, touching the lawful and the unlawful. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep34 01:19:30}

## Second episode: size and growth as the presenter gave them
The presenter said the Islamic economy is the fastest-growing in the world, that Islamic banks and financial institutions number more than three hundred in more than sixty countries and manage more than a trillion dollars, correcting after the break that a trillion is a thousand billion, not a thousand million; he said these were the latest figures he had read about two years earlier. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:07:30} He said their growth reaches 34% a year, and that global banks such as Citigroup, Chase Manhattan and a Hong Kong bank opened branches or institutions run on the Islamic system; these are his figures. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:08:00}

## Second episode: where would you buy the car?
The presenter asked the young audience: to buy a car in instalments, would you buy by murabaha from an Islamic bank, from a conventional bank, or feel no difference? Sixty-five percent chose the Islamic bank, 3% the conventional, and 32% said no difference; he noted that those present love their religion and the figures do not represent society as a whole. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:10:00} He asked viewers by text message which banks give better banking service; the result was not read out. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:04:30}

- A woman student said the Islamic bank, even with a few doubts, is better than the outright forbidden, and a young man said he would choose it even if it profited more from him, to avoid the forbidden, adding that any wrong in it is on the scholars' heads. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:11:00} Another saw no difference, since both take an increase, and a third was not convinced that the bank owning the car changes the ruling. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:12:30} The presenter restated the two differences, ownership and a price fixed after the contract, and one guest noted that some Islamic banks do not keep to the second. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:14:00}

## Second episode: the doubts again
- **Muhammad Baraka**, managing editor of the newspaper al-Mal, said the difference between the two contracts is clear from the sharia point of view, not merely a matter of name. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:15:30}
- Al-Azizi repeated that there is no difference but the name, since the calculation is the same, cited Jamal al-Din Atiyya, and named one merit he sees in the interest bank: early repayment reduces the interest, whereas the Islamic bank holds the client to the full price. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:18:00}
- The presenter replied that he had read thirty works of tafsir that take the sale which the people of the Jahiliyya likened to riba to be the deferred sale, and that God permitted sale despite the resemblance. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:19:30} Al-Azizi answered that Abu Zahra and most scholars take the verse as restricted, since many sales are forbidden. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:20:30}
- The presenter set out his argument from the pillars of sale: seller, buyer, good and price. In negotiation the seller may set ten for cash and twelve for a year; once they sign, the price is settled and does not change. In the conventional bank they agree on a price that changes with the term, so the pillar of price is never settled. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:25:00} Al-Azizi insisted that an increase built into the price is like an open one, and reported al-Albani as saying murabaha contains two stratagems. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:27:00}
- **Husayn Shahata**, professor of Islamic economics at al-Azhar University and adviser to many banks, said the confusion is between a loan contract and a sale contract: a loan at interest is forbidden by consensus of the jurists, and that is what the conventional bank does; the Islamic bank buys and takes ownership of the good and resells it at an increase called profit. He cited the verse on debts, said the cash price and the deferred price are both prices, and that the method of calculating profit is an arithmetic technique that is neither lawful nor unlawful in itself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:32:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:32:30}
- Salah Sultan held that the Islamic bank uses money to trade in goods, whereas the conventional bank trades in money itself, and that a sale whose price changes is forbidden not as riba but as gharar, uncertainty in one of its pillars. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:34:30}

## Second episode: can riba be abolished?
The presenter asked the young audience whether riba could be abolished and an Islamic economy set up in their own country: 12% said immediately, 31% gradually, and 57% said it is impossible while the world economy runs on interest. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:40:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:41:00} He reminded them that Iran, Sudan and Pakistan abolished riba by their own definition, and that in 1975 there was only one Islamic bank in the world, while assets have since reached a trillion dollars, and urged them to keep hope. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:44:00}

- Hamid al-Attar said it is quite possible, but not at once, because states need time to settle their debts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:44:30} Rajab Abu Mulayh said permissibility is the rule and riba the exception, cited al-Ghazali's experience in converting Pakistan's economy, and called for an immediate decision carried out gradually. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:45:30}
- Muhammad Baraka was cautious, starting from reality: the single Gulf currency, he said, had its date moved from 2010 to 2015, and Muslim states are in debt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:46:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:47:00}
- Al-Azizi held the change possible on condition that those running the banks are not the owners of the capital and that sharia auditors are elected and hold real authority; he told of "Dr al-Najjar's" savings bank in Egypt between 1963 and 1967, which he said opened nine branches and made large profits before it was closed; this is his account. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:49:00}
- Salah Sultan said Bahrain has hundreds of Islamic institutions, and that the world system watches the Islamic economy closely, which he took as a sign that it is a healthy phenomenon. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:49:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:50:30}
- Al-Ghazali held that the Islamic economy is an integrated system of which the prohibition of riba and the payment of zakat are two pillars, that Britain changed its law to allow Islamic banks, that America has thirty-six Islamic financial institutions, and that Japan gave zero-interest loans between 1999 and 2001 to lift its economy out of recession; these are his figures. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:52:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:53:00}

## Second episode: social role and efficiency
The presenter said he was troubled that no one had spoken of development, solving poverty and encouraging the young to start projects, and asked the audience to compare Islamic with conventional banks in improving society: 24% said they are better, 27% the same, and 49% weaker. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 00:56:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:01:30}

- Muhammad Baraka said about 80% of services to small and medium enterprises in Egypt came through conventional banks, especially via the Social Fund for Development, and that there is no difference today in service standards between the two; the presenter counted this an achievement for Islamic banks given their short life. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:04:00}
- Al-Azizi said Islamic banks were planned to rest on musharaka and mudaraba and arose to help the poor and the young, but they now finance the rich, their service is ordinary like the rest, and he called for an academy to qualify their staff. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:07:30}

## The presenter's conclusion
Al-Suwaidan said half of the Gulf economies are expected to be run through Islamic banks and financial institutions within ten years, a forecast he gave as his own, that Islamic banks began unsteadily and have entered a phase of correction, and asked that criticism of them be taken as the criticism of those who love them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:21:00} He said their services have lately become equal to conventional banks' or sometimes better, and listed four things by which whoever focuses on them overcomes competitors in material terms: training, technology, service before, during and after the sale, and innovation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:22:00} He held that Islamic banks have crossed the bridge and succeeded, and that the question now is how to guarantee that they are Islamic and pure, that they achieve, and that wealth does not circulate only among the rich but serves the development of society and the young; he called this the middle way. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep35 01:23:00}
