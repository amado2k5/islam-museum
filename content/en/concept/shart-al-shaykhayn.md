## Summary
"The condition of the two Shaykhs" (*shart al-shaykhayn*) means what [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] and [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] held themselves to in choosing the hadiths of their Sahihs. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 21} In the introduction to [[work/irshad-al-sari|Irshad al-Sari]], summarising the introduction to Fath al-Bari, [[person/al-qastallani-shihab-al-din|al-Qastallani]] relays Ibn Tahir al-Maqdisi's point that neither is reported to have said "my condition is to include such-and-such"; their conditions are known only from studying their books. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 21}

## The dispute over defining it
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Hakim al-Naysaburi in al-Madkhal ila al-Iklil**: the first degree of sound hadith, chosen by al-Bukhari and Muslim, is a report from a well-known Companion who has two trustworthy transmitters, then transmitted by a precise, well-known hafiz of the following generations with transmitters of his own, down to a teacher of al-Bukhari and Muslim who is a precise hafiz known for uprightness. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22}
- **Sunni: Ibn Tahir al-Maqdisi (Ibn al-Qaysarani)**: their condition is a report whose transmitters are agreed to be trustworthy back to a well-known Companion, without disagreement among reliable authorities, with a connected chain; two transmitters from the Companion is good, but if he has only one and the route to him is sound, they include it. He holds al-Hakim's rule contradicted in both books: al-Bukhari included Mirdas al-Aslami, whose only transmitter is Qays b. Abi Hazim, and 'Amr b. Taghlib, transmitted only by al-Hasan al-Basri; Muslim included al-Musayyab b. Hazn on the death of Abu Talib, transmitted only by his son Sa'id, and al-Agharr al-Muzani, transmitted only by Abu Burda. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 21-22}
- **Sunni: Abu Bakr al-Hazimi**: al-Hakim's claim is "the view of one who did not dig deep into the hidden corners of the Sahih"; had he surveyed the book properly he would have found much of it contradicting him. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22}
:::

## Which Sahih ranks higher
Al-Qastallani notes that the community agreed in accepting the two Sahihs and differed over which is stronger, the majority explicitly preferring al-Bukhari's. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22}
- He reads Abu 'Ali al-Naysaburi's saying "there is nothing under the sky sounder than Muslim's book" as denying that any book is sounder than Muslim's, not as asserting that it is sounder than al-Bukhari's. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22}
- He takes the preference of some Maghribis for Muslim's Sahih to concern its fine presentation and arrangement, not its soundness. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22}
- He gives reasons for preferring al-Bukhari: in connection, because al-Bukhari requires proof that a narrator met his source at least once while Muslim is content with contemporaneity; in uprightness and precision, because more of Muslim's narrators were criticised; and in freedom from anomaly and defect, because fewer of al-Bukhari's hadiths were criticised than Muslim's. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22} See [[concept/ittisal-al-isnad|connection of the chain]].

## Narrators they used despite criticism
Al-Qastallani relays that a Sahih author's inclusion of a narrator implies his uprightness and precision in that author's view — all the more so since the community agreed to call the two books "the Sahihs" — at least for the main reports, while narrators of corroborating and suspended reports vary in standing; and that criticism is accepted only when its damaging ground is explained. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 22-23} Abu al-Hasan al-Maqdisi used to say of a narrator used in the Sahih, "he has crossed the bridge" — what was said about him is no longer considered. {cite:irshad-sari-amiriyya-1323 p. 23}

## In al-Nawawi, quoting Ibn al-Salah
[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] quotes [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] that Muslim's condition in his Sahih is a chain connected by reliable men from reliable men from beginning to end, free of anomaly and defect — which is the definition of sound hadith. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 15} He explains the formula "sound on Muslim's condition but not al-Bukhari's" as meaning that some narrators met the conditions for Muslim but not for al-Bukhari — such as Abu al-Zubayr al-Makki, Suhayl b. Abi Salih, al-'Ala' b. 'Abd al-Rahman and Hammad b. Salama — and conversely for men al-Bukhari relied on but Muslim did not, such as 'Ikrima, client of Ibn 'Abbas. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 15} Al-Nawawi counts among the reasons for preferring al-Bukhari's book that Muslim treats an "'an" chain as connected on mere contemporaneity, whereas al-Bukhari does not until the two are proven to have met. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 14}
