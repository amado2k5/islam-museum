## At the beginning
The sign of the beginning of melancholia is a bad opinion, fear without a cause, quick anger, love of solitude, a twitching, giddiness and a humming, especially in the hypochondrium {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.106}

## When it has settled
When it has settled there is alarm, ill opinion, grief, loneliness, distress and raving speech, and kinds of fear of what is not or may be, most of his fear being of what is not ordinarily feared, and these kinds are without limit; some fear the sky falling on them, some the earth swallowing them, some the jinn, some the sultan, some thieves, and some guard against a beast coming in on them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.106}

## Imaginings
Past events may have an effect here, and they may imagine before their eyes things that are not there, and at times imagine themselves to have become kings, beasts of prey, devils, birds or manufactured instruments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.106}

## Laughter and weeping
Some of them laugh, especially the one whose melancholia is sanguine, for he imagines what pleases and delights him; some weep, especially the one whose melancholia is purely black-bilious; some love death and some hate it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.106}

## Desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Among the signs of settled melancholia Ibn Sina lists a shabaq (desire) 'for the abundance of wind' (as printed, not interpreted) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.106}
