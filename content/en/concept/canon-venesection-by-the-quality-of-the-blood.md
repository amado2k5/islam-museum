## Summary
Ibn Sina warns against bleeding on a fullness that comes from raw humours, allows it for one in whom black bile predominates if he has not been evacuated by diarrhoea, and explains how to bleed one whose good blood is little and whose body holds much bad humour, by taking a little blood, feeding him, and bleeding again {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.290-291}.

## Fullness from raw humours
It must be known that not every time the signs of fullness already mentioned appear is bleeding due; the fullness may come from raw humours, and then bleeding is very harmful, for if you bleed while it has not ripened the patient is feared to perish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.290-291}.

## One in whom black bile predominates
As for one in whom black bile predominates, there is no harm in bleeding him if he has not been evacuated by diarrhoea, after heeding the state of the complexion on the condition he will state and weighing the distension, for the spread of distension through the body alone gives a surmise that bleeding is due {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.

## Little good blood and much bad
As for one whose good blood is little and whose body holds many bad humours, bleeding takes the good from him and leaves the bad. If his blood is bad and little, or is inclined toward an organ whose harm from that inclination is great, and there is no escape from bleeding, a little of his blood should be taken, then he is fed with praiseworthy food, then bled again, and so over several days, so that the bad blood goes out and the good is left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.

## Bad humours bilious or thick
If the bad humours in him are bilious, a way is found to evacuate them first by gentle diarrhoea or by vomiting, or to calm them, and effort is made to quiet and rest the patient. If they are thick, the ancients used to have such people bathe and walk about their business, and sometimes gave them, before the bleeding and after it before the second bleeding, a thinning oxymel boiled with hyssop and hasha {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.291}.
