## Summary
The Sheikh says the soul sometimes slackens in obedience and grows sluggish, and that some people remain in that state until they are used to it; he attributes it to faith increasing with obedience and decreasing with sins, and mentions the hadith that hearts have times of eagerness and times of turning away: in eagerness, voluntary acts; in turning away, the obligations. He holds that the believer should not leave this state long. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:02:30}

## Signs of slackening
- Laziness in obedience: missing the dawn prayer for days until the sun is about to rise while one is still in the tashahhud, and finding a sick visit burdensome. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:03:00}
- Hardness of heart: seeing slain Muslims or famine victims with their bones showing on the screen and being unmoved, then eating and sleeping soundly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:04:00}
- Wasted time: a day passing with no Qur'an memorised, no gathering of knowledge, no sick visit and nothing achieved, so that one feels blessing has left one's time. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:04:30}
- Disorder in work and loss of success, discovering at day's end that nothing was done. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:05:00}
- Hearing the Qur'an and hadith as one reads a newspaper, while listening closely to stock prices and football matches. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:05:30}
- A tongue quick to disparage people's good deeds, belittling a rich man's charity or an uncle's care of his orphaned nephews. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:06:30}
- Many idle wishes, which he likens to sailing the sea of the bankrupt, and taking death lightly as though the angel of death visited only the sick or the old. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:07:00}
- Laxity toward sins and belittling them; he quotes the scholars: no sin is small with persistence, and none is great with seeking forgiveness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep23 00:07:30}
