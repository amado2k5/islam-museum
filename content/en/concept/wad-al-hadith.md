## Overview
Hadith forgery arose after the Arbitration and the killing of Imam 'Ali, when the parties split and each sought to back its claim from Qur'an and Sunna; the Qur'an's many memorizers made tampering with it impossible, so they turned to the Sunna and added to it {cite:manhaj-tawthiq-al-sunna-azhar p. 37}. Its causes: the political factions, the zanadiqa enemies of Islam, the Shu'ubiyya, storytellers and preachers, currying favor with rulers, and the madhhab and kalam disputes {cite:manhaj-tawthiq-al-sunna-azhar p. 39}. The muhaddithun set the signs of fabrication in isnad and matn — feeble wording, corrupt meaning, contradiction of certain texts — and Ibn al-Qayyim held that spotting a forgery without consulting the isnad belongs only to one steeped in the sound Sunna.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says the probity ('adala) of a narrator is the second condition hadith scholars set for accepting his report; it concerns religion and decency (muru'a), and it is impaired by unbelief, open sin, lying, being suspected of lying, and innovation. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:02:30} He says lying occurred in the Prophet's lifetime from a man of Banu al-Najjar who used to write down revelation for him, then apostatised and became a Christian, and when he died the earth cast him out three times; he says the hadith is in the two Sahihs from [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]]. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:04:30} He states that all the Companions are of established probity, and that hadith scholars never ascribe an error to a Companion but to those below him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:06:00}

He relates from the introduction to Sahih Muslim that Bushayr ibn Ka'b kept narrating from the Prophet while [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] paid him no attention; Ibn Abbas then said that once, when they heard anyone narrate from the Prophet, they listened intently, but when people took to every road, rough and smooth, they accepted only from those they knew. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:07:00} He says the preferred view is that lying appeared in the community in the year 40 or 41, after the killing of Uthman and the division of the community. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:08:00}

He divides the forgers into kinds: the zanadiqa, who show Islam and seek to disfigure the Sharia; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:09:00} then the people of desires and innovations, whom he divides into political, creedal and juristic; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:11:00} then the storytellers and preachers. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:24:30} As a political example he gives a beggar whom Ibn Hibban relates in the [[work/al-majruhin-min-al-muhaddithin|introduction to «al-Majruhin»]] of [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]]: when given nothing he said that [[person/yazid-ibn-harun|Yazid ibn Harun]] told him from "Dhi'b ibn Abi Dhi'b" a hadith about a beggar crying "God is greatest" over those who turn him away; when Yazid heard of it he laughed and said he had never heard it, and there is no narrator named Dhi'b, only [[person/ibn-abi-dhib|Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Dhi'b]], a peer of Malik. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:13:00} Another is the story of [[event/ghiyath-fabricated-hadith|Ghiyath ibn Ibrahim]] with al-Mahdi. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:14:00}

As creedal examples he says one forger made up a hadith that faith neither increases nor decreases, an ignorant Sunni made up one saying the opposite, and some turned the scholars' definition of faith into a hadith. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:19:30} As a juristic example he gives a hadith made up by a partisan of the Hanafis against Muhammad ibn Idris [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]; al-Heweny says it is worthless to the scholars, that the great imams of the schools and their true followers were too noble to lie, and that the liars were ignorant men claiming affiliation to the schools. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:23:00} He says preaching was once among the noblest of crafts, practised only by scholars; when it declined, people unfit for it entered it and made up hadiths of encouragement and warning, and he tells the story of [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Yahya ibn Ma'in]] and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] with the preacher of Kufa. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep08 00:25:30}
