## Overview
The transmigration of souls (*tanasukh al-arwah*) is a doctrine that al-Masudi relates from a group of the ancient philosophers of the Greeks and the Indians, who did not affirm a revealed word or a sent prophet. Among them he mentions Plato and those who followed their way. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.546}

## What he transmits
They claim that the soul is a substance that is not a body, living, knowing, distinguished by its own essence, governing composite bodies. It feels pleasure and pain, and it dies. Its death, in their view, is its passage from one body to another, and the person who has decayed is annulled. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.546} {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.547}

This is a presentation of al-Masudi's account of the adherents of the doctrine, and not a statement of the museum.
