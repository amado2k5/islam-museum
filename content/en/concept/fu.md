## Nature
A plant with a leaf like the large-leaved celery and a smooth, soft stem, thickest near the top, purple and jointed, with a flower like the narcissus but larger and, in its whiteness, purplish; its root divides into branches and has an aromatic quality, and its power resembles spikenard in many things, so some call it wild nard; from below the root crooked branches spread like those of idhkhir and black hellebore, tangled with one another and of a reddish colour, and it grows in the lands called Nitus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.624}

## Actions and properties
The power of its root is warming {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.625}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits pain in the side {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.625}

## Excretory organs
It promotes urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.625}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists it as promoting the menses when drunk dried or as a decoction, more than Indian and Roman spikenard, and as like manjusha in that {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.625}
