## Summary
Ibn Sina describes grappling as of several kinds: two men lock hands about each other's waists and each tries to slip free while held, with chest pushing, neck holds and leg twists, then the quick exercises, among them two companions swapping places and the exercise of the two pegs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.

## Grappling
Grappling has several kinds: each of two men clasps his hand about the other's waist and holds on, each trying to free himself while gripped; or one twists with his hands about the other, right hand to the other's right and left to his left, face to face, then lifts and turns him over; also pushing with the chests, each holding the other's neck and pulling it down, twisting the legs, the cross-leg hold and parting the other's legs with his own, and the like of the postures wrestlers use {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.

## The exercise of the two pegs
Among the quick exercises are two companions swapping places at speed, and a run of backward leaps with forward leaps between them, in order or not. Another is the exercise of the two pegs: a person stands in one spot and drives two pegs into the ground at his sides, a fathom apart, then turns to them moving the right-hand peg to the left hole and the left-hand peg to the right hole, aiming to do it as quickly as he can {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.
