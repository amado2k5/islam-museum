## Nature
Dioscorides said: some people call it qarawtiya, which is al-qirad (the tick), and they named it so because its seed resembles the tick; it is a small tree about the size of a small fig tree, with leaves like those of the dilb but larger, smoother and darker, its stalk and branches hollow like a reed, and its fruit in rough clusters; when the fruit is husked the seed appears in the shape of a tick, and from it is pressed the oil called aqanqus, which is the oil of khirwa', and this is not fit for food but is fit for the lamp and for the mixtures of some salves and some drugs; and if a number of its seeds are pounded, crushed and drunk they loosen phlegm, the number of seeds not being given; the book gives no identification beyond this description {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.718}

## Actions and properties
Al-Dimashqi said that khirwa' is dissolving and softening and its oil subtilises, subtler than plain olive oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.718}

## Cosmetics
Pounded and applied as a poultice it removes warts and freckles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.718}

## Swellings and pustules
Its leaf, pounded with barley flour, calms phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.718}

## Ulcers
Its oil is suitable for scab and moist ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.718}

## Organs of nourishment
The book reports that its seed provokes vomiting because it relaxes the stomach greatly and causes nausea; the count is not given {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.718}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Applied alone as a poultice, and with vinegar, it calms swellings of the breast; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.719}

## Excretory organs
Its seed, taken, loosens phlegm and bile and brings worms out of the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.719}
