## Nature
Dioscorides: some people call it aqakiya and some aqaqiya; it is the sap of a tree that grows in Egypt and elsewhere, a thorn that is among the larger trees, whose branches and limbs are not upright, with a white flower and a white fruit like lupin, in pods from which the sap is made, dried in the shade {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.660}

## Choice
When the fruit is ripe the sap is black, and when unripe its colour tends to that of the ruby, so the book says to choose the one with something of the ruby colour, which sweetens the smell when added to the rest of the acacia; some gather its leaf with its fruit and extract the sap of both {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.660}

## Actions and properties
Gum arabic also comes from this thorn; the good gum is the one resembling worms, in colour like clear glass with no wood in it; second after the good is the white; the one resembling dirty resin is poor; and its power is gluey, curbing the sharpness of hot drugs when mixed with them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.660-661}

## Another kind
Likewise of the acacia tree there is another kind growing in Cappadocia, like the one in Egypt but much smaller and fresher, full of thorns, with a leaf like the leaf of rue, bearing seed in autumn in double pods, the seed smaller than the lentil; it is also astringent, its power is weaker than that of the Egyptian, and it is not fit for the drugs that go into the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.661}

## The book's note
The book brings it here and explains what it is because some people call it qart, and says it heard from a trustworthy man of Kerman that they call acacia the sap of qaraz, and that its actions were completed in the chapter of the letter alif {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.661}
