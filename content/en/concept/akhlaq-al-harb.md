## Summary
The ethics of war, as [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] presents them in the fifteenth episode of the programme [[work/what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world|What Did Muslims Bring to the World]], are the limits he says Islam set on war so that it stays at the least harm possible: that war is not sought for its own sake, that non-combatants are not killed, that covenants and safe-conduct are kept, that there is no wanton destruction, and that captives are treated well. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:05:30} This entry presents his view and his figures, attributed to him.

## Morals as part of civilization
He says morals are an inseparable part of Islamic civilization, citing a hadith that makes the purpose of the mission the perfecting of noble character, and one that those nearest to the Prophet in the gathering on the Day of Resurrection are those best in character. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:03:30} Good character, he says, is not tied to a place: it is required in the mosque and the home and on the battlefield, and the world did not know the phrase "the ethics of war" until Islam. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:04:30} He holds that Muslims do not fight out of a wish to fight but under necessity, war being for them the last stage, "and the last remedy is cautery". {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:05:00}

## The count of those killed in the campaigns (the speaker's view and figures)
He says he himself counted those killed in the Prophet's lifetime to gauge how bloody his wars were, and that the Prophet fought in twenty-five or twenty-seven campaigns, as the historians differ, and thirty-eight expeditions, at least sixty-three engagements in all. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:08:00} He says the Muslims killed in them numbered two hundred and sixty-two, that the total killed, Muslims and others, was about one thousand two hundred and eighty-four over ten years, and that this was about one and a half per cent of the armies that fought. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:10:00} He sets this against the Second World War, saying its fighters numbered fifteen million six hundred thousand and the dead more than three times that number, and mentions Hiroshima, Nagasaki and the bombing of German cities. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:12:00} (needs_source: figures from his own count without detail of his sources; the number of non-Muslims killed is inconsistent in the machine transcript and is not written.)

## Non-combatants
He says one of the most important limits of the Sharia is that civilians are not killed, citing the Prophet's forbidding the killing of the old man, the woman and the small child, and the command in [[passage/quran-2-190|Surat al-Baqara]] to fight those who fight you. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:15:30} He relates that Umar ibn al-Khattab charged the armies setting out for Persia to fear God regarding the peasants, and that the Prophet was angered on seeing a woman killed in one of the battles, saying she had not been fighting. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:16:00} See also: [[concept/prohibition-killing-women-children|the prohibition of killing women, children and labourers]].

## Covenant and safe-conduct
He says that "war is deception" concerns military planning and indirect speech, not the breaking of a covenant, that the Prophet charged his armies not to act treacherously, and mentions the disowning of whoever grants a man safety and then kills him. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:18:30} See: [[concept/aman|aman]].

## No wanton destruction
He cites Abu Bakr's charge to the armies setting out for Syria and Persia not to cut down a fruit tree, hamstring a palm or pull down a church, and not to kill a beast except for food, and says the Muslims pulled down in their battles only fortresses and what military need required. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:22:30}

## Captives
He closes the episode with the treatment of captives, citing the prisoners of Badr and the story of Thumama ibn Uthal. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep15 00:25:30} See: [[event/badr-prisoners|the prisoners of Badr]], [[event/capture-and-islam-of-thumama-ibn-uthal|the capture and Islam of Thumama ibn Uthal]].
