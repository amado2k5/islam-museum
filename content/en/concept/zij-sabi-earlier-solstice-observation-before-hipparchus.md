## Summary
Al-Battani, again quoting Ptolemy, mentions an old observation of the summer solstice that came before Hipparchus's, by an observer whose name is written in disturbed forms in the manuscript; the solstice was found to precede the whole quarter by one day and a fraction, a ratio to the stated span, as the text puts it, of two whole days to three hundred years, which agreed with the one day in three hundred years he had relied on {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.66-67}.

## What is kept and what is not
The observer's name, the year, and the names of the king and months are disturbed in the printed text, so only the structure is given here: an old summer observation, a gap between the solstice and the whole quarter of about a day and a fraction, and the fit of this to a ratio of two days to three hundred years. Al-Battani notes that solstice observations are less exact than equinox ones, as in [[concept/zij-sabi-equinox-observations-trusted-over-solstice|the preference for equinox observations]], and that the agreement is a confirmation rather than a proof {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.67}.
