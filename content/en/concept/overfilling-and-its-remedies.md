## Summary
Ibn Sina says one who has overeaten, or whose stomach has been shaken by movement or disturbed by a drink, should hasten to vomit; failing that, he sips hot water little by little and sleeps, or nature is helped with a gentle loosening; if not, he sleeps long and goes without food for a day, then bathes, applies warm compresses and lightens his diet; and if heaviness stays the vessels are full of residue and are treated by what clears the vessels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.232-233}.

## Vomiting and hot water
If someone overeats in filling himself, or the stomach's contents are shaken by movement or disturbed by a draught, he should hasten to vomit; if that is missed or impossible, he drinks hot water little by little, which sends the fullness downward and brings drowsiness, and he sleeps {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.232}.

## Helping nature
If that is not enough, he weighs matters: if nature can be trusted to expel, well and good; otherwise it is helped with something that loosens gently, for the hot-tempered itrifal and the loosening khalanjabin, for the cold-tempered kammuni and the tamri described in the dispensatory. Ibn Sina adds that being filled with drink is better than being filled with food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.232}.

## When the vessels are full
If none of this works, he sleeps long and abstains from food for a day; if he lightens he bathes, applies warm compresses and lightens his food. If he stays heavy, stretched and sluggish, his vessels are full of residue, for excess food, though it may digest in the stomach, seldom digests in the vessels but stays raw there, stretching and sometimes splitting them and bringing sluggishness, stretching and yawning, and it is treated with what clears the vessels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.232-233}.
