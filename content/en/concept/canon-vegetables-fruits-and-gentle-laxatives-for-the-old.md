## Summary
Ibn Sina lists the vegetables and fruits the old may take, such as beet, celery and a little leek with fermented sauce and oil before the meal, garlic for those used to it, preserved ginger, figs and plums, and what softens the bowels: bindweed, polypody root, cabbage water and safflower kernel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.252}.

## Vegetables and fruits
The vegetables and fruits the old take are beet, celery and a little leek, flavoured with fermented sauce and oil, especially before the meal to help soften the bowels. If they are used to garlic, they benefit from it at times. Preserved ginger is among the suitable remedies, as are most warm preserves, in an amount that warms and digests, not one that dries the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.252}.

## What softens the bowels
Among the fruits they use to soften the bowels are figs and plums in summer and dried figs cooked in honey water in winter, all before the meal; also bindweed cooked in salted water and flavoured with fermented sauce and oil, and polypody root in chicken broth, beet broth or cabbage broth. If the bowels move every other day, no laxative is needed; if they move one day and are held two, bindweed, cabbage water and safflower kernel with barley water suffice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.252}.
