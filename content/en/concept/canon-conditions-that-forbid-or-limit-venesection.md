## Summary
Ibn Sina gathers the states in which bleeding is guarded against: the pregnant and the menstruating woman and colic, a very cold temperament and very cold countries, severe pain, after a bath and after coitus, youth and old age, bodies very lean or very fat, and long illness, unless the physique gives confidence {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.290-292}.

## Colic, the pregnant and the menstruating
Bleeding and colic seldom meet. The pregnant and the menstruating woman are not bled except for great necessity, such as the need to stop strong spitting of blood if her strength allows; and the better and more binding course is that the pregnant woman is not bled at all, since the foetus dies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.290}.

## Temperament, pain, the bath and coitus
Bleeding must be guarded against in a very cold temperament and in very cold countries, in severe pain, after a dissolving bath, and just after coitus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## Age
Also under the age of fourteen as far as possible, and in old age as far as possible, unless you trust the physique, the firmness of the muscles, the width and fullness of the veins and the redness of the colours; such among the old and the young we venture to bleed. The young are brought along little by little with a small bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.

## Bodies
Bleeding must be guarded against, as far as possible, in bodies very lean or very fat, porous ones, the white flabby and the yellow that lack blood, and it is avoided in bodies on which illnesses have lasted long, unless the corruption of their blood calls for it; then bleed and watch the blood: if it is black and thick, let it out, and if you see it white and thin, stop at once, for there is great danger in that {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.292}.
