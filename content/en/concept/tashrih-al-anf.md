## The channels, the sieve and the mamillary pair
The anatomy of the nose comprises its bones, its cartilage and the muscles moving its two ends — matters already dealt with. Its two channels reach the sieve set under the two nipple-like bodies; the brain's membrane there is pierced opposite a hole of the sieve so the pneuma passes and performs its work, and each channel runs through to the pharynx. The instrument by which smelling happens is the two mamillary processes at the front of the brain, drawing from the two front ventricles; the wastes are sifted through those holes too, and by their path the air bearing the odour reaches the brain and the two processes rising from it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.234}
## The brain's breathing, the passages to the eyes, and how smell occurs
The brain itself breathes to preserve its innate heat: it swells and heaves like a pulse, and may swell when shouting or when air and spirit are choked upward. At the far end of the nose are two channels to the eyes, which is why the taste of kohl is felt descending to the tongue. How smelling occurs was treated in the book of faculties; whether the odour is in the air by the air's being affected, by transmission, or by a resolving vapour, is the philosopher's question. Let the physician accept that smell may at root come from a change wrought in the air by way of transmission, aided by vapour rising off the odorous thing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.234}
