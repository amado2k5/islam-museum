## Summary
Ibn Sina names three states of a child: hiccough, treated with coconut and sugar; violent vomiting, treated with half a daniq of clove and poulticing the stomach with mild vomit-stoppers; and a weak stomach, treated by smearing it with liquorice water and giving quince water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.218}.

## Hiccough and vomiting
A child may have hiccough, and then should be given coconut with sugar. A violent vomiting may befall it, and a drink of half a daniq of clove may help, as may poulticing the stomach with some of the mild vomit-stoppers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.218}.

## Weak stomach
A child may have a weak stomach, and then its stomach should be smeared with maysus in rose water or myrtle water, and quince water given with some clove and sukk, or a qirat of sukk in a little maiba {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.218}.
