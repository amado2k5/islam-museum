## Nature and selection
Dioscorides said that it is a kind of mi'a (storax), and according to some it is the gum of the olive, and its smoke takes the place of the smoke of frankincense in everything {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}. The editor's footnote says that istirak is al-mi'a and al-hawz, a kind of plant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}. The best is what is sharper in smell; Dioscorides said that the best is the yellow-red, fat, resin-like, with whitish parts in its body, sweet-smelling and lasting long, and when rubbed a moisture comes out of it like honey; the black, coarse kind like bran is bad; a gum like gum arabic, clear in colour and smelling like myrrh, may be taken from it, and this gum is seldom found; and some people melt fat and wax and knead them in with the istirak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}.

## Temperament and actions
It is hot in the third degree and dry in the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}. It warms, ripens and softens greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}.

## Uses by organ
It is mixed into the drugs for fatigue of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}. It induces torpor, heaviness and headache, and helps the cold in the head and catarrhs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}. It helps cough, hoarseness of the voice and its cutting off {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}. And if some of the gum of the terebinth (butm) is swallowed, it loosens the bowel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that its oil is useful for hardness of the womb, promotes the menses and opens the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.355}; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation.
