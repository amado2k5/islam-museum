## Summary
Al-Battani calls the converse operation the taqwis of the risings, turning rising times into the degrees of equality, which are the degrees of the signs: in the table of the right sphere or of the clime one looks for the known number of times or the nearest smaller one, takes the degrees of the signs standing opposite, and then works out the remainder by multiplying by sixty or six hundred minutes, according to the step, and dividing by the difference of the risings between the two entries. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.45}.

## From times to degrees
He says: if you want to know the degrees of the signs from the risings, which is called the taqwis of the risings and their conversion to the degrees of equality, which are the degrees of the signs, look for a number equal to the rising times you have in the table of the right sphere's risings or the clime's, whichever you wish; wherever you find an equal one, or the nearest below it, take what stands opposite among the degrees of the signs drawn in the common-number row. That is the degree you want of the sign in which you found the number of times. The wording itself says that the degrees of equality are the degrees of the signs. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.45}.

## The remainder and the difference of the risings
He goes on: then subtract the times you hold, and look at what remains. If the step of the number is one degree, multiply it by sixty minutes; if the step is ten degrees, multiply it by six hundred minutes; divide the result by the difference of the risings between that entry and the next, and add the degrees and minutes that come out of the division to the degrees you found; the total is the amount that rises of that sign or comes to midheaven, whichever you worked with. Or look at how much the remainder is of the difference of the risings, take that part of the step of the number and add it to the degrees you have. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.45}.
