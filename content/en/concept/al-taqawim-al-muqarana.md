## Summary
In the first chapter, al-Farghani compares the calendars of four peoples: the Syrians, the Romans (Byzantines), the Copts, and the Persians, explaining for each its months, days, and method of intercalating the year {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 127}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 129}.

## The Syrians and the Romans
The Syrian months are: Tishrin al-Awwal, Tishrin al-Akhir, Kanun al-Awwal, Kanun al-Thani, Shubat, Adhar, Nisan, Ayyar, Haziran, Tammuz, Ab, and Aylul {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 129}. Their year has three hundred and sixty-five and a quarter days, with one day added every four years so that a leap year has three hundred and sixty-six days {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 129}. The Romans are like them in the number of days, and their first month is January, which is Kanun al-Thani, then February, which is Shubat, and so on {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 129}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 135}.

## The Copts
The Coptic months are: Tut, Babah, Hatur, Kiyahk, Tubah, Amshir, Baramhat, Baramudah, Bashans, Ba'unah, Abib, and Misra, followed by five extra days called al-lawadiq {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 127}. Each month has thirty days, and the year has three hundred and sixty-five days {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 127}. Al-Farghani notes that the Copts of his time added a quarter of a day to the days of the year, so their months came to align with those of the Romans and Syrians {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 127}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 137}.

## The Persians
The Persian months are twelve months of thirty days each, with five days inserted between Aban and Adhar that are not counted among the months, making the year three hundred and sixty-five days {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 128}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 137}. The names of their months and days are in the same chapter, and their details are in the entry "Persian Months."
