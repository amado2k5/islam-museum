## Summary
These codices are the material fruit of what [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] did: he took the leaves [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd]] had gathered under Abu Bakr, kept by [[person/hafsa|Hafsa]], and had codices copied from them and sent to the garrison cities, one kept in Medina, and one held back for Uthman himself, called the Imam. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 17} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 388} For the event see [[event/uthman-standardizes-mushaf|Uthman's standardization of the mushaf]].

## The number
Al-Suyuti says the number of codices Uthman sent to the regions is disputed; the best-known number is five; Ibn Abi Dawud relays from Hamza al-Zayyat that he sent four, and from Abu Hatim al-Sijistani that he wrote seven and sent to Mecca, Syria, Yemen, Bahrain, Basra and Kufa, keeping one in Medina. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 393} Al-Sabt lists the views: six, namely the Meccan, the Syrian, the Basran, the Kufan, the general Medinan and the private Medinan kept for himself, which is the Imam; five; four; seven; eight, five agreed and three disputed, the Meccan, the Bahrain and the Yemen codices; and it is said Uthman sent a codex to Egypt. He concludes that what is understood is that he had enough copied to meet the community's need and unite its word, and that nothing sound is reported about the number. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 325} Al-Suyuti quotes Makki that the codices sent to the cities were five and that it is said he sent seven, the five plus one to Yemen and one to Bahrain, but nothing was heard of those two. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 527}

## How they were copied
Ibn Abi Dawud relays with a sound chain from Kathir ibn Aflah that when Uthman wanted the codices written, he gathered twelve men of Quraysh and the Ansar, who sent to Umar's house for the rab'a, the box holding the parts of the codex; Uthman checked on them, and when they disputed something they put it back; Muhammad ibn Sirin said he thought they postponed it to look for the one with the most recent contact with the final review, and write it on his word. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 390} Another report says that when they differed over a verse they sent for the man the Prophet had had recite it, though he was three nights from Medina, and left a space for the verse. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 389} Al-Sabt records that the committee reported is of four, Zayd ibn Thabit, Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr, Sa'id ibn al-As and Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith ibn Hisham, the last three Qurashi, and that some reports say twelve. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 236}

## Where the codices differed
The differences among the Uthmanic codices were few, and Ibn al-Jazari takes them to be included in what 'even potentially' covers: [[person/abd-allah-ibn-amir|Ibn Amir's]] reading at [[passage/quran-2-116|al-Baqara 2:116]] without the conjunction is present in the Syrian codex, as is his reading at [[passage/quran-3-184|Al Imran 3:184]] with an added preposition in two words; [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir's]] reading at [[passage/quran-9-100|al-Tawba 9:100]] with an added preposition is present in the Meccan codex. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 495} Al-Sabt notes that a verb in [[passage/quran-5-54|al-Ma'ida 5:54]] was written with two dals in the Medinan and Syrian codices and with one assimilated dal in the others. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 327} Many hold this difference to be what remains of the seven ahruf that the script can bear; see [[concept/al-arda-al-akhira|the final review]].

## The readers who were sent
Al-Sabt relays, without finding a chain, that Uthman ordered Zayd to teach with the Medinan codex and sent Abdallah ibn al-Sa'ib with the Meccan, al-Mughira ibn Abi Shihab with the Syrian, Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami with the Kufan and Amir ibn Abd Qays with the Basran; the Successors then took from the Companions and each region read what was in its codex. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 326} Ibn al-Jazari says the community, protected from error, agreed on what these codices contained and left aside what departed from them in addition, omission and substitution of one word for another that had been allowed as a concession, because its being Qur'an was not established to them widely. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 17}

## Where are they now?
Al-Sabt says we have no decisive evidence that the Uthmanic codices exist now, let alone where, and that Ibn al-Jazari saw the codex of the Syrians in his day and one in Egypt; he doubts the attribution to Uthman of the ancient codices in the treasuries of Egypt because they have ornament, engraving and markers between suras and for tens, whereas the Uthmanic codices lacked all this and dots and vowels. He mentions a codex in the al-Husayni mosque attributed to Uthman in old Kufic whose spelling agrees with the Medinan or Syrian, and another said to have been written by Ali that agrees with the rest. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 326} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 327} For the move of the Uthmanic codex from Tiberias to Damascus see [[event/uthmanic-mushaf-tiberias-damascus-493|the transfer of the Uthmanic codex]]. As for Abu Bakr's leaves, al-Sabt says Marwan asked Hafsa for them and she refused, then Abdallah ibn Umar sent them to him after her death, and Ibn Abi Dawud's reports say he burned, washed or tore them, said to be from fear that someone might doubt them. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 324} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 325}
