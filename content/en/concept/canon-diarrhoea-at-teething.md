## Summary
Ibn Sina mentions loosening of the bowels in infants, above all at teething, gives two explanations, then fomenting and poulticing the belly, a drink of kid's rennet, and a food in place of milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.211-212}.

## Explanations
Among what befalls children is looseness of the belly, especially at teething. Some claimed it comes because the child sucks a salty, pus-like surplus from its gum along with the milk; it may instead be that nature, busy forming an organ, cannot digest well, and that pain comes, which prevents digestion in weak bodies. A little of it should not be made a concern {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.211}.

## Treatment
If excess is feared, it is met by fomenting the belly with rose seed, celery seed, anise or cumin, or by poulticing the belly with cumin and rose moistened in vinegar, or with millet cooked in a little vinegar. If that does not help, a daniq of kid's rennet is given in cold water, and then care is taken that the milk does not curdle in the stomach, by feeding that day what stands in for milk, such as a soft egg-yolk dish, the crumb of bread cooked in water, or sawiq cooked in water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.211-212}.
