## Summary
Ibn Sina gives the spine (sulb, the column of vertebrae) four uses, then describes the vertebra, its processes, holes and joints, and closes with a summary on the form of the whole spine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.47-48} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.52}.

## The four uses
First, to be a passage for the spinal cord. Were all the nerves to grow from the brain, the head would need to be far larger and would weigh on the body, and the nerves would be exposed to harm and breaking; so a part of the brain, the cord, was sent down the body like a channel from a spring, and the spine made a secure way for it. Second, the spine is a shield for the noble organs lying before it, which is why it has thorns and spines. Third, it is the frame of all the bones of the body, like the timber first laid in making a ship, to which the rest of the wood is tied. Fourth, to give the human frame independence and the ability to move in every direction, which is why it was made of ordered vertebrae, not one bone nor a great number of bones, and why the joints between vertebrae are neither loose, which would weaken the frame, nor fixed, which would prevent bending {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.47}.

## The vertebra and its processes
A vertebra is a bone with a hole in its middle through which the cord passes. It may have four processes on right and left, those above pointing up and those below pointing down; sometimes there are six or eight. They let the vertebrae join by joints, with hollows in some and condylar heads in others. Other processes serve protection, resistance and the weaving of ligaments: those pointing backward are called thorns and spines, those to right and left are called wings. The wings next to the ribs have hollows in which the rib heads are fixed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.47-48}.

## Holes for nerves
Besides the middle hole the vertebrae have other holes for the nerves leaving and the vessels entering, some wholly within one vertebra, some shared by two. They were placed at the sides because at the back the nerves would lack protection and meet blows, and at the front they would fall where the body leans with its weight and its movements, which would weaken them and press the nerves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.48}.

## The whole spine
In closing he says the spine as a whole is like one thing given the best figure, the round, which is furthest from the harm of blows; so the tips of the upper spines are hooked downward and the lower ones upward, and they meet at the middle, the tenth vertebra, which is the middle of the spines in length, not in number {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.52}. Because the vertebrae are firmly bound on one side, he says, they are like a single bone made for stillness, and because they are loose on another side, like many bones made for movement {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.48}.
