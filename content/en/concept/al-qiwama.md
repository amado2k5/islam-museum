## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi reads "men are in charge of women" as a duty of standing for the family's affairs and bearing its burden, not as domination, and he grounds the preference in men's labour and maintenance. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2192}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2193}

## Burden, not domination
He says the word refers to one who stands for affairs with great effort, and that the man is charged with earning a living and with what serves those in his care: wife, daughters, sisters and mother. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2192}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2193}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2194}

## Disobedience and the order of response
He sets out the order in the verse: admonition at a moment of harmony, then separation in bed within the home without public exposure, then light striking that leaves no mark. He reports that some scholars said the striking should be with a tooth-stick, presenting it without adopting it. He holds that obedience is judged by outward conduct. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2199}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2200}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2201}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2202}

## The two arbiters
If discord is feared, an arbiter from each family is sent, with the aim of reconciliation, and he holds that success is God's alone. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2203}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2204}
