## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi reads "men are in charge of women" as a duty of standing for the family's affairs and bearing its burden, not as domination, and he grounds the preference in men's labour and maintenance. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2192}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2193}

## Burden, not domination
He says the word refers to one who stands for affairs with great effort, and that the man is charged with earning a living and with what serves those in his care: wife, daughters, sisters and mother. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2192}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2193}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2194}

## Disobedience and the order of response
He sets out the order in the verse: admonition at a moment of harmony, then separation in bed within the home without public exposure, then light striking that leaves no mark. He reports that some scholars said the striking should be with a tooth-stick, presenting it without adopting it. He holds that obedience is judged by outward conduct. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2199}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2200}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2201}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2202}

## The two arbiters
If discord is feared, an arbiter from each family is sent, with the aim of reconciliation, and he holds that success is God's alone. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2203}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2204}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh explains guardianship (qiwama) by spending, and says that men are favoured over women in some matters and women over men in others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:44:00}. He holds, as his own opinion, that guardianship must be exercised with ethics and faith and not for display, and that bearing the burdens of the household is a mark of true manliness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:45:30}.

He says that guardianship is a respect for a woman's humanity, and that misunderstanding it and putting it in the wrong place is injustice {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:48:00}; he holds that a husband who understands guardianship in a balanced way does not belittle his wife's humanity {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:49:30}. He notes that a husband may err when he assumes his wife is like himself {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:37:00}.

He says, as his own view, that a woman bears pregnancy and nursing and stays at home, and he cites what the Qur'an says about the male not being like the female (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:46:30}.

## In the programme "Al-Wasatiyya": qiwama, domination or responsibility
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gave an episode of the programme [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|Al-Wasatiyya]] to men's qiwama over women: is it domination or responsibility? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:00:30} Opening it, he said the verse of qiwama ([[passage/quran-4-34|al-Nisa' 34]]) is held in full respect by a Muslim, that the episode did not aim to evade or reject it but to understand it in order to apply it, and he set its questions: is qiwama guardianship over a woman or a removal of her freedom, does it cover everything, what are its conditions, and does it lapse if the woman is more capable, or if she is the earner and the man unemployed? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:02:00} With him were men and women specialists in the sharia, guests from the human and psychological sciences, and an audience of young people which he said was mostly female that day. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:05:00}

### First poll and the linguistic meaning
He asked the audience whether most men understand the sharia meaning of qiwama correctly; only 14% said yes and 86% no. He said the result was influenced by the mostly female audience but still pointed to a problem in how both sides understand it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:05:30} A guest specialist in Arabic explained that qiwama comes from the verb qama, and that here it means care, running the family's affairs and providing for it; the presenter summed it up as justice, care, maintenance and managing the household. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:06:30}

### Dr Hayel Abd al-Hafiz: an administrative role within the family
Dr Hayel Abd al-Hafiz, a doctor of sharia, said qiwama is an authority to manage the family's affairs, care for it and meet its needs: an administrative role, not one of coercion, control, superiority or removal of the woman's freedom, which the husband carries out together with his wife through mutual understanding and consultation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:08:30} He said no institution works with two opposing heads, so a decision is needed in the end after consultation, and the sharia gave the man the final decision in specific matters, not in all matters. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:09:30} He held that qiwama concerns family affairs only, and that what lies outside the family is not covered, even though the verse says "men" and not "husbands". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:10:00}

He explained the "degree" men have as a degree of responsibility and obligation, above all maintenance and care, not a degree of preference in this world or the next, and said the verses begin by stating that women have rights equal to their duties in fairness. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:11:00} He explained the "preference" as the physical and created characteristics each side was given for its role: women are preferred in some respects and men in others; he was speaking of the general rule, not of individuals, and pointed out that jihad was not made an individual duty on women. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:13:30} He said maintenance is not a cause whose absence cancels qiwama: if the man does not provide, the verse is not being applied, but qiwama is not abolished, or conflict would return to the heart of the family. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:15:30}

### Second poll: forbidding a wife to work
The presenter described a childless couple in which the husband is rich enough to support the household and the wife wants to work to gain experience and contribute to civilisation, not for money, and asked whether he may forbid her in the name of qiwama. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:16:30} Sixty-seven percent said he may not, all of them women according to the presenter, and 33% said he may. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:22:00} Dr Hayel voted that he may not forbid her. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:19:30}

### Thirteen cases
The presenter put thirteen questions to Dr Hayel to fix the limits of qiwama, and his answers were these: {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:19:30}
- The husband does not interfere in his wife's religion or sect, does not bind her to his legal school, and does not impose a legal opinion on her in a matter of ijtihad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:20:30}
- He does not stop her joining an Islamic group or a study circle as long as it is within the permissible, nor from completing her studies, since seeking knowledge is a duty. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:21:30}
- He does not interfere in her vote, since it is a testimony and a choice. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:21:30}
- He does not forbid her a lawful job, nor force her to work even if the family needs it, since work is not required of her. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:22:30}
- He may forbid her to travel without a mahram if he holds it impermissible, even if she is convinced it is allowed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:23:00}
- He may stop a friendship he sees as harmful to the family, but may not cut her off from her close relatives. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:23:30}
- In choosing the children's school and in the method of raising them, consultation is the rule; if they cannot agree, the decision is the man's. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:25:30}
- On dress: he does not bind her in disputed matters such as the face veil, a colour or a fashion, but may bind her to the limits of 'awra on which the jurists agree. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:26:30}

The presenter asked the audience how far they agreed with these answers: 40% agreed with all of them, 55% with most, and 5% disagreed with most. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:29:30}

### Replies and objections
One participant held that in a disagreement the decision should go to the sounder view, not to the man as such, then granted that if the disagreement remains, the man decides. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:30:30} Another, among those who disagreed, held that in a disagreement the wife should obey of her own accord, and if she does not, the path is the steps for nushuz set out in the verse, not qiwama as such. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:32:00} A young man asked why she would want to work if her husband provides enough. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:33:00}

A woman professor among the guests agreed with most of what Dr Hayel said, noting that it differs from the fatwas of earlier scholars in many points, that the family in Islam rests on affection, mercy and consultation, and that the Qur'anic phrase on consultation refers to the family more than to politics. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:34:30} She disagreed with him on compelling the wife to wear the hijab, because Islam gave woman full legal capacity and every soul is pledged for what it earns: he may give his opinion but not force her. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:35:30} A researcher from Yemen studying for a doctorate said she supports most of what he said and sees it as a new direction in Islamic discourse, but that it is international organisations, not Islamic discourse, that go into the reality of the Arab family and offer programmes; a man may ask his wife to consider the social dimension in her dress, but binding her religiously against her conviction is not his right. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:38:00} She added that women ran their households when men went out to jihad, that masculine and feminine traits exist in both sexes, and that the man who neither provides nor is competent is a question fiqh must take up, since many families today are headed by women. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:40:00}

A scholar among the guests, whom Dr Hayel called Dr Jum'a, said these were theoretical answers that please women, not convictions and practice, and asked Dr Hayel whether he would let his wife join a party or group other than his; Dr Hayel answered, "Honestly, no." {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:43:00} The guest said frankly that men are domineering, and that he began his married life domineering and, after about seventeen years, ended up "semi-domineering". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:44:00} Dr Hayel granted that much of what happens in society contradicts what he had said, and that fatwas have at times been shaped by circumstances and custom, and said the task is to change reality to fit the religion, not to change the religion. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:45:30}

### Third poll: do the details of qiwama change?
The presenter asked whether the details of qiwama change with time or from one society to another, for example between an Arab family at home and one in America. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:46:30} Seventy-three percent said yes and 27% that it is a fixed concept. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:48:30} The presenter saw in this a radical change in the community that many scholars do not sense; he said women make up more than 70% of the government sector in Kuwait, that in most Arab families the man can no longer support the household alone, and recalled that [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] changed some of his views when he moved from Iraq to Egypt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:49:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:49:30} The percentage is his own figure.

A guest from a religious family said her generation carried into marriage a culture of obeying and respecting the husband, that she dropped from it the domination her mother had lived under, and that her daughters added dialogue and the claiming of rights. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:50:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:51:00} The presenter told a report about [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]]: the people of Mecca used to prevail over their wives, but in Medina they found the Ansar's wives prevailing over their husbands, and the Emigrants' wives learned from them; a man came to complain about his wife to Umar, heard Umar's wife raising her voice at him and turned back, and Umar told him that his wife serves him and raises his children though none of that is required of her, so he bears with her. This is the presenter's telling in the episode. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:53:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:53:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:54:00}

Mrs Amal Abidin said she believes in qiwama, but that in Muslim societies there is much injustice and domination of women by men. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:56:00} A married young man, Hani Ashur, said that whoever uses qiwama as a sword over a woman's head, as some use divorce, pleases neither God nor His Messenger. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 00:58:30}

### Fourth poll: sharing or struggle
The presenter said he has been married for more than thirty years and has never once needed to use "the sword of qiwama", and that in his view whoever uses it is the weak one who does not understand the religion or cannot reach agreement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:00:00} He then asked whether, among the young generation today, there is a sharing of roles or a struggle. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:00:30} Only 47% said families show understanding and shared roles, and 53% said what they see is moodiness and domination; he said he had expected a larger share, because talk on television is fine while practice at home sometimes contradicts it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:03:00}

### The presenter's conclusion
The presenter said this is a difficult concept with fine details, shaped by custom and tradition, which shaped even the fatwas and commentaries of the early jurists; its details must therefore be reconsidered to fit today's reality, since today's woman has been educated, competes with men for office and has reached the highest levels of religious scholarship, and he called on scholars to do so. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:15:30} He proposed to the young a formula: each spouse has left a home to form a new family; qiwama has nothing to do with matters outside the family, so if a woman sits in parliament her husband cannot dictate her vote; inside the family there is a sphere belonging to the man and one belonging to the woman in which the other does not interfere, so he does not change her religion or her thought or dispose of her money; and there is a shared sphere, such as raising the children and relations with other families, where understanding and consultation are the rule, and whoever resorts to authority and decree is the weak one who failed to persuade. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:17:30} He compared it to companies: when votes are tied, the chair's side prevails, and likewise the man's side prevails in minor shared matters when no agreement is reached; he closed with the teaching that the best of people are the best to their families, not those who dominate them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep32 01:18:30}
