## Summary
Ibn Sina holds that a good regimen, with exercise, rubbing and the bath, often spares a person vomiting and purging; if need forces venesection or a strong drug, venesection comes first, but purging comes first when the humours are sticky and cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.276}.

## The order of the means
Ibn Sina says a person of good regimen needs something lighter than vomiting and purging, and exercise, rubbing and the bath may be enough for him. When need requires venesection or an evacuation with strong drugs such as hellebore, venesection should come first; he gives this as one of the counsels of Hippocrates in the book on epidemics and calls it right, and the same holds when phlegmatic humours are mixed with blood. But when the humours are sticky and cold, venesection may make them thicker and stickier, so purging should come first. In general, if the humours are equal venesection goes first, and if they are unequal the surplus is evacuated first until they are even, and then blood is let {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.276}.

## When the order is reversed
Ibn Sina states that whoever took the drug before a venesection that ought to have come first should put the venesection off, and that someone who was bled not long before is better suited to taking the drug. Taking a drug where venesection was due has often led to fever and disturbance, and if the sedatives do not calm it, that shows the venesection should have been done first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.276-277}.
