## Summary
Al-Battani teaches that the chord of the arc remaining to complete the semicircle is found by squaring the known arc's chord, subtracting it from the square of the diameter and taking the root of what remains; that the chord of a quarter of the circle is the root of twice the square of the radius; and that the chords of a tenth and a fifth come out by rules he gives with the squares of the radius and of its half and the root. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.17-18}.

## The complementary arc and the third of the circle
If the chord of a sixth is multiplied by itself and taken from the whole diameter multiplied by itself, the root of the remainder is the chord of a third of the circle. Likewise for any arc of known chord: square its chord, subtract that from the square of the diameter, take the root of the rest, and you have the chord of the arc remaining to complete the semicircle. This is the Pythagorean relation of the right triangle in a semicircle, which al-Battani uses without naming it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.17}.

## The chord of a quarter
He gives the chord of a quarter of the circle as the root of twice the radius multiplied by itself. With the radius sixty the chord is the root of twice 3,600, that is the root of 7,200. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.17}.

## The chords of a tenth and a fifth
For the chord of a tenth he gives this rule: multiply the radius by itself, add to it a quarter of the diameter multiplied by itself, take the root of the sum, subtract a quarter of the circle's diameter, and what is left is the chord of a tenth. Then square the chord of a tenth, add the radius squared, take the root, and that is the chord of a fifth. These two formulas carry in the text the chords of the decagon and pentagon known from Ptolemy. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.17-18}.
