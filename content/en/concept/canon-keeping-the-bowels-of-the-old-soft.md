## Summary
Ibn Sina names what softens the bowels of the old: terebinth gum and a remedy of safflower kernel with dried figs; he recommends the oily enema, especially sweet olive oil, since it softens the viscera as well as evacuating, avoids hot enemas which dry the intestines, and prefers moderate purging to venesection in the middle-aged and the old {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.252-253}.

## Terebinth gum and the safflower remedy
One or two nuts' measure of terebinth gum, three at most, softens their bowels by a property of its own and cleanses the viscera without harm. A remedy compounded of safflower kernel with ten times its weight of dried figs also helps them, the dose being about a nut {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.252-253}.

## The enema
An enema of oil helps them, since besides evacuating it softens the viscera, above all sweet olive oil. Hot enemas are avoided for them, for they dry the intestines; the moist, oily enema is among the most useful things for them when the bowels are held for days. For them there are special softening remedies which the author promises to describe in the pharmacopoeia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.253}.

## Purging instead of venesection
Evacuation of the middle-aged and the old should be done without venesection where possible, for moderate purging suits them better {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.253}.
