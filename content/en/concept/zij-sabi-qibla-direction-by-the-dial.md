## The direction of the qibla in a town
To find the direction of the qibla a line is drawn from the centre of the circle on the known figure. The latitudes of the town and of Makkah, and the longitudes of Makkah and Madinah from the city tables, are compared; the difference in longitude decides on which side of the town Makkah lies, east or west, and north or south of it. The point where the longitude and latitude lines meet on the rim is joined to the centre, and that line is the qibla azimuth. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.210-211}

## The exact computation of the azimuth
For an exact figure the chord of the difference in longitude and the chord of the difference in latitude are taken, each is squared, the two are added, and the root of the sum is the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle. The chord of the latitude difference is then multiplied by the radius and divided by the diameter of the hundred, which gives an arc, and this arc is the azimuth of Makkah from that town. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.211}
