## Summary
The science of ma'ani is one of three sciences gathered under [[concept/ilm-al-balagha|the science of rhetoric]], and its subject, as al-Qazwini and al-Sakkaki define it, is the states by which speech matches the demand of the situation. This page sets out the two definitions, its eight chapters, Ibn Khaldun's examples, al-Sakkaki's examples from the Qur'an with what the exegetes say at those verses, and the link of the science to the nazm of [[person/abd-al-qahir-al-jurjani|Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani]]. Each statement is ascribed to its author, and verses are pointed to by their pages without writing out their text.

## al-Qazwini's definition
Al-Khatib al-Qazwini defines ma'ani as the science by which are known the states of the wording through which it matches the demand of the situation. Khafaji explains that the states of the wording are the things that befall it, such as putting first or last, stating and omitting, and that the definition leaves out what is needed to convey the bare meaning, like inflection, and what comes after the matching, the embellishments {cite:idah-khafaji-dar-al-jil 1 p.52}. The aim of the science is to guard against error in conveying the intended meaning {cite:idah-khafaji-dar-al-jil 1 p.50}.

## al-Sakkaki's definition
[[person/al-sakkaki|al-Sakkaki]] says in Miftah al-Ulum that ma'ani is the following up of the properties of speech structures in conveying meaning and what goes with it of beautifying and the like, so as to guard by knowing them against error in fitting speech to what the situation demands. By structures he means the structures of the eloquent, and he illustrates a property of a structure by what comes first to the mind of a sound-natured hearer on hearing a sentence such as that Zayd is departing, namely that it was meant to remove doubt or answer a denial {cite:miftah-al-ulum-zarzur-1987 p.161}. Al-Qazwini objected to this definition, saying that following-up is not a science and that the definition goes in a circle {cite:idah-khafaji-dar-al-jil 1 p.54}.

## The eight chapters
For al-Qazwini the aim of ma'ani falls into eight chapters: the states of the report's predication, of the subject, of the predicate, of the complements of the verb, restriction, the performative, separation and joining, and brevity, prolixity and the even measure. The reason for the limit is that speech is either report or performative {cite:idah-khafaji-dar-al-jil 1 p.55}. Al-Sakkaki likewise says that two things count in the first place in Arab speech, report and request, and that all else comes from taking the speech away from its base {cite:miftah-al-ulum-zarzur-1987 p.164}.

## Ibn Khaldun's examples
Ibn Khaldun explains this part by examples rather than abstract rules. Saying 'Zayd came to me' differs from 'came to me Zayd', since what comes first is the more important to the speaker: whoever puts the verb first shows concern for the coming, and whoever puts the noun first shows concern for the person. 'Zayd is standing', 'Zayd is indeed standing' and 'Zayd is surely standing' differ in what they convey though they agree in inflection: the first is for the empty-minded, the second for the hesitant, the third for the denier. He adds that making 'man' indefinite may be meant to magnify him, that a sentence is a report or a performative, that joining two sentences may be required or forbidden, and that the situation may call for brevity or for fullness {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.760}.

## Report in the place of request
Al-Sakkaki lists the fine reasons for putting a report where a request would be expected, among them hopeful expectation of the thing's happening in a prayer, metonymy, and moving the hearer to the thing named in the gentlest way. He says that no verse of the Qur'an that comes in this style fails to turn on one of these points, and he gives two: what was taken from the Children of Israel in [[passage/quran-2-83|al-Baqara 83]], and what follows a question about a trade that saves from a painful punishment in [[passage/quran-61-10|al-Saff 10]] and [[passage/quran-61-11|11]] {cite:miftah-al-ulum-zarzur-1987 p.325} {cite:miftah-al-ulum-zarzur-1987 p.326}. In al-Baghawi's commentary on al-Baqara the sense is 'that you not worship', and when 'that' was dropped the verb became raised {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 2:83}. Al-Qurtubi gives other analyses, among them Sibawayh's that it depends on an oath, and notes that the reading of Ubayy and Ibn Mas'ud came in the form of a prohibition {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 2:83}. On the al-Saff verse he relays from al-Mubarrad and al-Zajjaj that the raised verb there has the sense of a command, which is why the verb after it is jussive, as an answer to it {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 61:11}. This page sets out these analyses and chooses none.

## The manner of the wise, and the question about the crescents
Al-Sakkaki calls 'the manner of the wise' meeting the addressee with something other than he expects, or the questioner with something other than he asks, and gives as an example the people's question about the crescents in [[passage/quran-2-189|al-Baqara 189]]: they asked why the crescent appears thin, grows until full, then wanes, and they were answered with what benefits them in it {cite:miftah-al-ulum-zarzur-1987 p.327}. In al-Baghawi the question was about the state of the crescent, and the answer is that they are appointed times for the people by which the times of hajj and umra, of fasting and ending it, the terms of debts and the waiting period of women are known {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 2:189}. Ibn Kathir relays from Ibn Abbas and others that they are times by which people know when their religious obligations fall due, the waiting period of their women and the time of their hajj {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 2:189}.

## The link to nazm
Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani says in Dala'il al-I'jaz that nazm is only placing one's speech as the science of grammar requires, and that the arranger seeks nothing but to look into the forms of each chapter and their differences, such as the forms of the report in 'Zayd is departing', 'Zayd departs' and 'departs Zayd' {cite:dalail-al-ijaz-shakir-1992 1 p.81}. Khafaji says in the introduction to his commentary that the Dala'il holds many studies that are the roots of ma'ani {cite:idah-khafaji-dar-al-jil 1 p.7}. The theory has its page, [[concept/nazariyyat-al-ijaz-wal-nazm|the theory of i'jaz and nazm]], and grammar has [[concept/ilm-al-nahw|the science of nahw]].

## What this page does not reach
The eight chapters were not read in al-Idah itself: what was read is the definition of the science, the list of its chapters and the index of the third volume. Not read: the commentaries of al-Sa'd al-Taftazani and others. Of the Miftah only the pages named were read, not its poetic examples. The exegetes' statements quoted here concern the inflection and the meaning, and none of them says that he had ma'ani in mind; tying the verse to this science is al-Sakkaki's alone.
