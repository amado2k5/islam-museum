## Summary
Rimad (ashes) in the lexicon is the fine dust of burnt coal, what remains after burning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8983} There is no dispute that the ash of a pure thing burned is pure; the Prophet used the ashes of a mat to staunch a wound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8983} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8984} The jurists differ on the ash of an impure thing: Abu Hanifa, some Malikis and one Hanbali view hold it pure because fire changes the substance, while the Shafi'is and most Malikis hold it impure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8984} Most jurists forbid tayammum with ashes because they are not of the earth, while the Hanafis allow it when made from stone. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8984} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8985} The ash of a pure thing is property that may be sold; the ash of an impure thing is not a valuable property for those who hold it impure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8985} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8986}
