## Overview
The science of the terminology of hadith comprises the rules for knowing the states of the chain and the text: the sound, the good, and the weak; the *mawquf*, the *marfu'*, and the *maqtu'*; the connected, the disconnected, the *mu'dal*, and the *mursal*. {cite:sahih-muslim}

It arose in the service of [[concept/al-hadith-al-nabawi|the Prophetic hadith]], and its relation to [[concept/isnad|the chain of transmission]] is close. Its origin lies in the observations of the early hadith scholars, and afterwards books were composed on it. {cite:sahih-muslim}

Among the oldest foundational texts that survive is Muslim's introduction to his *Sahih*. The summary of the school in books such as the introduction of Ibn al-Salah needs a further source.

## In «Asdaf al-Lu'lu'» by Abu Ishaq al-Heweny
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] relates that when he read al-Albani's «Silsilat al-Ahadith al-Da'ifa» he did not understand why one hadith is called munkar, another batil and a third mawdu', and the terminology books did not help him; so he thought of gathering every hadith al-Albani called munkar, every one he called batil and every one he called mawdu', then reading them together to find what each group shared, and this took him a full year at least. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:21:30} He says books of rules such as Ibn al-Salah's «Muqaddima» give few examples. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:16:30} His conclusion was that a fabricated (mawdu') report usually has a liar or a man accused of lying in its chain, while a false (batil) one may come from an unknown or weak-memoried narrator, and that the two terms overlap. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:18:00} He illustrates it with the story of Thabit, who came in while Sharik was dictating a chain, when Sharik said something about a man who prays much at night; Thabit took it for the rest of the hadith, so al-Heweny says it may be called munkar or batil, but not fabricated in the technical sense, because Thabit erred and did not lie. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:21:30}

He mentions that he taught terminology to children in order to understand it himself, and that he did not understand the definition of a weak hadith as one that "the setter fails to raise" until he thought of the bonesetter in his village. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep13 00:25:30} He says hadith science is the understanding of hidden defects (ilal), and that real takhrij is not listing sources, since twenty routes may share hidden defects. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep23 00:13:30} He sees hadith science as a "servant science" that creed, fiqh, language, history and the attribution of books all need, while it needs no other. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep22 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep22 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep22 00:14:30} His example is that books of fiqh almost all dislike ablution with sun-heated water on the strength of a fabricated hadith. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep22 00:15:30}

He says the early critics judged by context rather than fixed rules, so they might fault a hadith on a probability, and a reliable narrator's hadith may be munkar while a weak narrator's may be sound through corroboration. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep10 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep09 00:06:00} He disagrees with those who make Ibn Hajar's «Taqrib al-Tahdhib» the judge, building verdicts on narrators on its single word. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep09 00:04:00} He says he taught terminology to ordinary people in mosques and on al-Nas channel, and that this is one reason he has not completed a book on it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep17 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny asdaf-al-loaloa/ep17 00:20:00}
