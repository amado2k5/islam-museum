## Summary
In the lexicon, ushr is one tenth. This entry treats only the tithe on trade: the levy on goods of non-Muslims that they carry for trade from one town of the Muslim lands to another {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11608}.

## Basis
The tithe is taken from non-Muslim merchants when they enter the territory of Islam with goods. The jurists rely on a prophetic report, on the consensus of the early community and on the reasoning that traders need protection, while Muslim merchants owe nothing beyond zakat {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11609} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11610} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11612}.

## Who pays
Treaty merchants and protected non-Muslims are liable. The schools differ on further conditions: the Hanafis require adulthood and sanity, the Hanbalis do not require adulthood, and Abu Ya'la requires the payer to be male {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11613} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11614}. Only goods held for trade are liable, and the Hanafis and Hanbalis require a threshold; some require the goods to be free of debt {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11614} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11615} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11616}.

## Rate
The Hanafis and Hanbalis take half a tenth from a protected non-Muslim; the Malikis take a full tenth, except half a tenth on grain carried to Mecca and Medina; the Shafi'is leave the rate to the imam's terms {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11616} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11617}.

## Collection
The imam or governor is entitled to collect it, through a collector who must be a Muslim, free, knowledgeable and able to protect traders. The collector must not repeat the levy within a year (the report of a Christian trader is cited) and some jurists disliked farming the levy out {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11619} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11620} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11621} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11622}.

## Lapse and purposes
The tithe lapses on conversion, and the imam may waive it for goods the Muslims need. It is spent on the purposes of fay' {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11622} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11623}.

## Where to look further
Fay' is treated under «fay'», and zakat under «zakat» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11609}.

## For Scholars
Ushr in the language is one part in ten. This entry is confined to the ushr of trade — what is levied on the goods of the protected people kept for trade when they carry them from town to town within the lands of Islam. The ushr is taken on non-Muslims' trade when they bring it into the abode of Islam, the jurists arguing it by sunna, consensus and reason; nothing is taken on Muslims' trade but the zakat. It is taken from the safe-conducted and the dhimmis, and the schools differ on the conditions of obligation: the Hanafis require maturity and reason, the Hanbalis do not; Abu Ya'la required maleness, the rest did not. And the ushr falls only on wealth kept for trade — the Hanafis and Hanbalis require the threshold, and some require being clear of debt.
