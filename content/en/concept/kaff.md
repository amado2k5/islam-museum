## Summary
Kaff in the lexicon is the palm with its fingers, plural kufuf or akuff. It is named so because it keeps harm from the body {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13401}. A finger is one of the ends of the palm {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13401}.

## Washing the palms in ablution and on waking
The jurists agree on washing the palms up to the wrists at the start of ablution. They differ on washing on rising from sleep: the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is, and one Hanbali narration, call it a sunna; Ahmad in another narration and others call it obligatory, on the hadith "when one of you wakes from sleep, let him not dip his hand in the vessel until he has washed it three times" {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13401} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13402}.

## Washing the hands with the arms and wiping in tayammum
Washing the palms with the arms up to the elbows is a pillar of ablution, by the verse and the reports describing the Prophet's ablution {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13402} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13403}. Wiping the palms with dust is a pillar of tayammum, by the verse and the report of Ammar {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13403}.

## Washing before and after meals
Most jurists recommend washing the palms before and after eating, even for one already in ablution. A Hanbali narration dislikes it before and after, and al-Qadi preferred this view {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13404}.

## Retaliation for cutting the palm
The jurists agree that retaliation applies to cutting the palm when its conditions hold, and the hand is cut at the wrist joint. The injured party may not cut the fingers of the offender instead of the palm; the Shafi'is would discipline anyone who does so {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13404}.

## Blood-money for the palm
All agree that half the blood-money is owed for cutting the hand at the wrist joint, whether the act was intentional with retaliation waived, mistaken, or semi-intentional, on the report of Mu'adh that there is blood-money for the two hands, and the letter to Amr ibn Hazm that a hand carries fifty camels {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13404} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13405}.

## Cutting the thief's and highwayman's hand
Most jurists hold that a thief's hand is cut at the wrist joint, following the Prophet's cutting of a thief's hand at the joint and the practice of Abu Bakr, Umar and Ali. Some early scholars held that it is cut at the elbow, the base of the fingers or the shoulder. The highwayman's hand is cut at the wrist according to most jurists {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13405}.
