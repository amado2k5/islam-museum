## Summary
Ibn Sina closes the chapter on winds with the east and west winds and ties the character of each to the hour of the day at which it blows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.126}.

## The east wind
If the east wind comes at the end of the night and the start of the day, it comes from air that the sun has tempered and made fine and whose moisture is slight, so it is drier and finer; if it comes at the end of the day and the start of the night the case is the reverse. The east wind is, overall, better than the west {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.126}.

## The west wind
If the west wind comes at the end of the night and the start of the day, from air on which the sun has not worked, it is denser and thicker; if it comes at the end of the day and the start of the night the case is the reverse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.126}.
