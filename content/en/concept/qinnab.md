## Nature
The editor's note says that qinnab is shahdanaj, and refers for this to the book al-Mu'tamad fi al-adwiya al-mufrada of al-Malik al-Muzaffar Yusuf ibn Umar ibn Ali ibn Rasul {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.653}

## Actions and properties
Its seed drives out winds and dries; it is hard to digest, of bad humour and strongly heating; the roasted seed is less harmful, and sugared sakanjabin repels its harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.653}

## Swellings and pustules
A decoction of the roots of the wild kind is a poultice for hot swellings and erysipelas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.654}

## Organs of the head
Its juice and oil benefit earache; the head is washed with the juice of its leaf; and its seed causes headache by its strong heating and its vapour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.654}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its seed is hard to digest and bad for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.654}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that its seed, if taken in much, cuts the semen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.654}
