## Summary
An epicycle is a small sphere carried on an eccentric sphere, in which the heavenly body revolves while its centre moves on the circumference of the carrying sphere. With it al-Farghani explains the planets' retrogradation, advancement and delay {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 65}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 67}.

## The radii in chapter sixteen
Al-Farghani gives the dimensions on a scale where the eccentric sphere's radius is sixty parts. The distances of the eccentric spheres' centres from the earth's centre are thus (OCR reading): for Saturn three and a quarter and a sixth parts, for Jupiter two and a half and a quarter, for Mars six, for Venus one and a quarter, for Mercury three, and for the moon twelve and a half {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 65}.

He then gives the epicycle radii on the same scale: for Saturn six and a half parts, for Jupiter eleven and a half, for Mars thirty-nine and a sixth, for Venus forty-three and a sixth, for Mercury twenty-two and a half, and for the moon six and a third {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 65}.

## Motion
Al-Farghani shows that a body moving on the epicycle advances on the carrying sphere from the apogee side and lags from the perigee side, so retrogradation occurs when the body's motion on the epicycle equals the epicycle-centre's motion in the opposite direction {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 67}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 68}.

## The radii repeated in chapter sixteen
In chapter sixteen al-Farghani repeats the epicycle radii on the same scale, adding that the moon's epicycle radius is six and a third parts and Mercury's twenty-two and a half {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 197}. The chapter opens by fixing the measures by which the spheres' and epicycles' centres are set {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 196}. The fractional readings on page 197 are unconfirmed, so only what recurs on pages 65 and 197 is relied on.

## Perspectives
- **The author (al-Farghani):** he counts the epicycle a principle in explaining the five planets' retrogradation and the moon's motion, and all motions in longitude are built on it.
