## Summary
Ibn Sina separates consistency from clarity by noting that a thick urine may be clear and a thin one turbid, and explains turbidity by a mixture of foreign-coloured parts not separately perceived {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.184}.

## The difference and the cause of turbidity
The difference between this genus and that of consistency is that a urine may be thick and clear at once, like the white of an egg, melted fish fat and oil, and may be thin and turbid, like muddy water, which is far thinner than egg white. The cause of turbidity is a mixture of parts of a foreign colour, dark or tinted otherwise, not perceptibly distinct, which block the sight and are not felt by themselves; turbidity differs from sediment because the senses can distinguish sediment, whereas turbidity does not separate from the colour, which is spread through the substance of the moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.184}.
