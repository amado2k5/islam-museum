## Overview
Anger is the agitation of the soul at an offence or a violation. In *Jami al-ulum wal-hikam*, Ibn Rajab presents the hadith of the man who asked the Prophet for advice, and was told: "Do not become angry." It belongs to [[concept/adab|good manners]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

## The hadith
A man came to the Prophet and said, "Advise me," and he said, "Do not become angry." The man repeated the question several times, and the answer was always the same. In another version he said, "Teach me something and do not make it too much for me," and in another, "Show me a deed that will enter me into Paradise." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

## Ibn Rajab's commentary
Ibn Rajab draws from the repetition of the answer that anger is the sum of evil and that guarding against it is the sum of good. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

He relates from Abu Amr ibn al-Salah, from Abu Muhammad ibn Abi Zayd, that "do not become angry" is one of four hadiths from which the branches of good manners spring. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

Among the definitions of good character according to Ahmad is "that you do not become angry and do not become harsh," and according to Ishaq ibn Rahawayh, "openness of countenance and not becoming angry." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.430}
