## Overview
Anger is the agitation of the soul at an offence or a violation. In *Jami al-ulum wal-hikam*, Ibn Rajab presents the hadith of the man who asked the Prophet for advice, and was told: "Do not become angry." It belongs to [[concept/adab|good manners]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

## The hadith
A man came to the Prophet and said, "Advise me," and he said, "Do not become angry." The man repeated the question several times, and the answer was always the same. In another version he said, "Teach me something and do not make it too much for me," and in another, "Show me a deed that will enter me into Paradise." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

## Ibn Rajab's commentary
Ibn Rajab draws from the repetition of the answer that anger is the sum of evil and that guarding against it is the sum of good. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

He relates from Abu Amr ibn al-Salah, from Abu Muhammad ibn Abi Zayd, that "do not become angry" is one of four hadiths from which the branches of good manners spring. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.343-344}

Among the definitions of good character according to Ahmad is "that you do not become angry and do not become harsh," and according to Ishaq ibn Rahawayh, "openness of countenance and not becoming angry." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.430}

## Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's account
He says a person must master himself when desiring and when afraid, and fear only the One who created him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep02 00:22:00}
He says anger comes from Satan, and Satan was created from fire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep02 00:23:00}
He recounts the man who asked the Prophet for counsel and was told, "do not become angry", and urges imitating the forbearance of the Prophet, the Companions and the scholars. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep02 00:23:30}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Sharh Ketab Al Fawaed lectures (episode 4)

The Sheikh says that anger comes from Satan and Satan from fire, and that the fire of anger is put out only by gentleness; the transcript here reads "al-na'im" (needs_source: the word is unclear in the transcript and is likely "gentleness", to be verified) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep04 01:00:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Sharh Ketab Al Fawaed lectures (episode 9)

The Sheikh says that the fire of revenge comes from the fire of anger, so anger is its source {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep09 00:07:30}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series Nafahat Ramadaniyya (the faults of the tongue)
Among the remedies for the faults of the tongue, the Sheikh advises the angry person, before bad words escape him, to change his posture, sitting if he is standing and standing if he is sitting, then to make ablution, because anger is from Satan, Satan is from fire, and fire is put out only by water; he suggests that the angry person look in a mirror to see a stranger, and pray God to protect him from the heat of his anger. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:10:00}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
Episode 10 of [[work/fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah|Fi rihab al-hadith al-qudsi]] explains the hadith of the prophet stung by an ant who had the ant colony burned, and whom God reproached for destroying a community that glorifies Him. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep10 00:00:00} [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] says that the ant that stung him was guilty, but the others had done nothing, and that he punished them because he did not know which one had stung him. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep10 00:15:00} He holds that the lesson is that punishment should not exceed the act, and that better still God opens the door of pardon. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep10 00:16:00} He says the reproach came in a form that preserves the standing of prophethood. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep10 00:17:00}

He sets out three degrees for one who is wronged: restraining anger (kazm al-ghayz), in which the anger stays in the heart but is not acted on; pardon ('afw), removing it from the heart; and doing good (ihsan) to the wrongdoer, which God loves. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep10 00:17:30} He illustrates this with a father who comes home to find that his older son has hit the younger: his heart is with the one who was struck, and he embraces him. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep10 00:18:00}
