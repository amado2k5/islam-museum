## Summary
When was the Qur'an first translated, and by whom? This page gathers three reports given among the first translations: the rendering of al-Fatiha into Persian by Salman al-Farisi in the seventh century CE, as Hanafi books report; Robert of Ketton's Latin translation of 1143, the first into a Western European language; and Alexander Ross's English version of 1649. It rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the sites and pages it points to were not opened, and the date of each translation is the summary's.

## Salman al-Farisi and al-Fatiha in Persian
Early Hanafi books state that Salman al-Farisi translated Surat al-Fatiha into Persian for Persians who had entered Islam, to recite until they learned Arabic. The summary gives this from al-Sarakhsi's al-Mabsut (Dar al-Ma'rifa ed., vol. 1, p. 37) and al-Ayni's al-Binaya Sharh al-Hidaya (Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya ed., vol. 12, p. 237), as Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya cites them in its Fatwa 7281 and as the al-Bayan site has it {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-7281 on Fatwa 7281, citing al-Mabsut 1/37 and al-Binaya 12/237} {cite:albayan-uk-text-609 on id=609}. The report bears on the ruling on reciting a translation in prayer, in [[concept/qiraat-al-quran-bil-tarjama-fi-al-salat|Reciting the Qur'an in translation in prayer]]. Its subject is [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]].

## Robert of Ketton's Latin translation, 1143
This translation was completed in 1143 under the patronage of Peter the Venerable, Abbot of Cluny, and titled Lex Mahumet pseudoprophete; it is held to be the first translation of the Qur'an into a Western European language, and it was first printed in Basel in 1543 by Theodor Bibliander {cite:wikipedia-quran-translations on Quran translations} {cite:lexilogos-coran on Coran} {cite:salafcenter-2901 on article 2901}. Its translator is [[person/robert-of-ketton|Robert of Ketton]].

## Alexander Ross's English translation, 1649
Titled The Alcoran of Mahomet, it was the first complete English version, but it was not translated from Arabic: it was made from the French translation by André Du Ryer of 1647 {cite:archive-org-first-latin-french-english-translations-quran on the first Latin, French and English translations} {cite:wikipedia-quran-translations on Quran translations}. Du Ryer's French itself, according to the summary, was the first French translation made directly from Arabic {cite:lexilogos-coran on Coran} {cite:wikipedia-list-of-translations-of-the-quran on French}.

## After the first: George Sale
After these came the translation of [[person/george-sale|George Sale]] in 1734, which the summary describes as the first English translation made directly from Arabic, its author drawing on the Latin edition Marracci produced in 1698 {cite:wikipedia-list-of-translations-of-the-quran on English} {cite:lexilogos-coran on Coran}. The other translations by language are in [[concept/tarajim-al-quran-hasab-al-lugha|Qur'an translations by language]], and the naming of a translation as tafsir in [[concept/limadha-tusamma-al-tarjama-tafsiran|Why a translation is called an interpretation]].

## What joins the three reports and what separates them
The three reports concern translating the Qur'an but serve different purposes: Salman's report sits in Hanafi legal books in the context of what is allowed in prayer; Robert's concerns a translation made by Christians for Latin Europe under an abbot's patronage; Ross's concerns the languages of modern Europe and the chain of renderings from Latin to French to English. The sources quoted differ too: Hanafi books and Dar al-Ifta fatwas for the first, encyclopaedic sites and study sites for the others.

## What this page does not reach
The Dar al-Ifta page, the al-Bayan site, Wikipedia, Lexilogos, the Salaf Center and the Internet Archive were not opened, and al-Mabsut and al-Binaya were not read; the reports and dates come from a research summary supplied to the museum, and the founder approved using it on 21 November 2026 without re-verification. Wikipedia is used here as a source for entity facts only, not for prose. The soundness of the Salman report was not examined, and the dates 1143, 1543 and 1649 were not checked against another source.
