## Summary
This page presents the Arabisation law of Algeria as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it: Law No. 91-05 of 16 January 1991 and Ordinance No. 96-30 of 21 December 1996, which revised it. Everything here is ascribed to the summary. The museum opened neither the pages cited nor the text of the law, and this page does not replace the official texts.
## Year and body
The year is 1991 for the law and 1996 for the revision. The body, by the summary, is the Algerian Parliament and the Presidency, the texts being promulgated under Presidents Chadli Bendjedid and Liamine Zeroual {cite:axl-cefan-ulaval-language-legislation}. This makes the law the product of two different political phases, the first at the generalisation of the language in administration and education, the second at the readjustment of the law by presidential ordinance.
## Main provisions
By the summary the law establishes rules for the generalisation, promotion and protection of Arabic as a core element of national identity and public order. It requires the exclusive use of Arabic in public administrations, institutions, enterprises, associations, official documents and education, with phased deadlines, and in meetings and related activities, with limited exceptions for foreign relations. It thus joins generalisation of use with protection of the language, and links language with public order.
## Where the text can be found
The summary says the full text is available through Algeria-Watch and the language legislation archive of Universite Laval (axl.cefan.ulaval.ca), and in the Journal Officiel de la Republique Algerienne (joradp.dz) {cite:algeria-watch-home} {cite:axl-cefan-ulaval-language-legislation} {cite:joradp-journal-officiel-algeria}. It gives no page for each site, so the site is named and not the page.
## What was not found
The summary says it identified no major 2019 revision of this specific law, and that constitutional updates around Tamazight occurred separately {cite:axl-cefan-ulaval-language-legislation}. What concerns Tamazight is therefore not ascribed to this law here. The summary says nothing of how the law has been applied or opposed, and the museum says nothing of it either.

## How to read this page
The summary does not say how many articles the law has, which institutions were exempted in particular, how the phased deadlines in education were set, or what penalties exist, if any. Anyone who needs detail should go to the full text at the places named. The museum places this page with the other laws in this branch of the Arabic Language Browser so that the reader can see how Arab states have each treated the language as an element of identity, and the differences of style, body and penalty between them.
## The reach of the obligation
The phrase exclusive use in the summary shows the law is not a guideline but an obligation, and that its field is wide, covering administration, economic enterprises, associations, education and meetings. Phased deadlines in education go with it. But the summary does not say whether the obligation was applied in full, so the museum does not judge the matter. It records the text and what the summary said of it.
