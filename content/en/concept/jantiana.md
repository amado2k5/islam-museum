## Nature and selection
Its leaves resemble walnut leaves and lamb's-tongue leaves; its colour is red; its stalk is hollow and smooth and up to two cubits high; its root is long, like the root of zarawand, and it grows in mountains and in shade and dew {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}. The book says it is named gentian after the king Gentius, who first knew it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}. Its juice is made by soaking for days in water, then boiling, clarifying and thickening until it becomes like honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}. The best is the Roman kind, redder and harder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}. The editor's footnote gives it as a plant that is the medicine of the snake and the wolf's palm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.421}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot in the third degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}. Actions: it opens obstructions and has some astringency; its root is strongly opening, thinning and polishing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: its root clears vitiligo, especially its juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.
Wounds and ulcers: heals wounds and eating ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.
Joints: drunk with wine for twisting of a sinew; helps a person who has fallen from a height {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.
Eyes: made into a salve for eye inflammation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.
Respiratory: its juice is good for pleurisy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.
Food: opens blockages of the liver and spleen, and drunk in wine it helps pain and cold and swelling of the liver and spleen; helps a stomach upset by cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}.
Excretory organs: moves urine and menses; the book says its root, carried as a suppository, expels and removes the foetus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.420}. These are the book's listings, labelled.
Poisons: the strongest remedy for a scorpion sting; drunk with wine, it helps the sting of all vermin, the bite of a mad dog and the bite of all beasts of prey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.421}.
Substitutes: asarun and the rind of the caper root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.421}.
