## Summary
Ibn Sina shows winter as the best season for digestion and one whose diseases are mostly phlegmatic, and says who is harmed and who gains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.119-120}.

## Digestion
Winter is best for digestion because cold confines the innate heat, so it strengthens and does not dissipate; people also eat little fruit, keep to light food, move less and shelter by the fire. It is the season that most holds matter in, and the one with greatest need of dilutants and attenuants {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.119}.

## Diseases
Most winter diseases are phlegmatic. Phlegm is plentiful, so that most vomit is phlegm and tumours lean to white. Nasal catarrh is frequent, beginning with the autumn air and followed by pleurisy, pneumonia, hoarseness and sore throat, then pain in the side and back, afflictions of the nerves, chronic headache, even apoplexy and epilepsy, all from the holding in and abundance of phlegmatic matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.119-120}.

## Ages and urine
The old and those like them are hurt by winter, while the middle-aged benefit. Sediment in the urine is greater in winter than in summer, and so is its quantity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.
