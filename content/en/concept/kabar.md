## Nature and selection
It is a fruit and has a root; it has another fruit like a cucumber, distinct from the kabar, which is sharp and hot and, put into grape juice, keeps it from boiling, like mustard. Its root is bitter and sharp. One kind, the Qulzumi, blisters the mouth until it swells and inflames the gums. The most useful part is the bark of its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527}.

## Temperament
The kind in hot countries is hotter; all of it is hot and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527}.

## Actions and properties
It dissolves, opens and cleanses; its root cuts, thins, purifies and opens, and its bark has bitterness, sharpness and astringency. The fruit gives little nourishment, especially when salted, and the fresh is more nourishing than the dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527}.

## Swellings and pustules
Its root dissolves scrofulous swellings and hard lumps, mixed with something that breaks its strength; its leaf has been tried for this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527}.

## Wounds and ulcers
The bark of its root, laid on malignant and dirty wounds, helps them greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527}.

## Joints
The bark of its root helps sciatica and pains of the hip, and an enema of its juice helps greatly. It helps paralysis and numbness and braces the limbs because of its astringency, and so helps the tearing that befalls the heads and middles of a muscle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Chewed, the bark of its root draws moisture from the head and quiets cold pain there. Its juice is dropped into the ear for worms. The bark of the root is bitten on with the tooth for toothache and helps, especially when fresh, as does the leaf; a rinse of vinegar in which it was boiled does the same {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.527} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.528}.

## Chest and breathing
The salted kind helps those with asthma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.528}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is the most useful thing for the spleen and its hardness, drunk or applied as a poultice with barley flour and the like, especially the bark of its root; it often empties a thick black-bile matter from the spleen, and health follows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.528}.

## Excretory organs
It loosens the bowels of a raw thick humour, kills snakelike worms and worms in the intestine, and helps piles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.528}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on the menses and increases sexual desire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.528}.

## Poisons and bites
The book calls it a good antidote {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.528}.
