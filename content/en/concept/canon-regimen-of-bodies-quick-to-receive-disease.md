## Summary
In the third chapter Ibn Sina describes bodies quick to receive disease, which are prone either from fullness, so the amount of the humours is made balanced, or from raw humours, so their quality is made balanced; foods are chosen that nourish moderately between little and much, meals are spread out and the person is not loaded to full satiety at once, and the food is advanced or delayed according to the bath, sweating and the pouring of bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.

## Balancing the humours
They are prone to this either from fullness, so the amount of their humours is balanced, or from raw humours, so their quality is balanced. Foods are chosen for them that nourish moderately between little and much. The amount is balanced by balancing the quantity of food and increasing exercise and rubbing before bathing if they are used to them, or lighter ones if not, spreading the meals and not loading them with full satiety at once {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.

## Timing the meal
If the body sweats easily and is used to it, it sweats at times; if delaying the food does not make bile pour into the stomach, the food is put off until after the bath, otherwise it comes first. The moderate time, if nothing prevents, is after the fourth hour of the equinoctial day. If signs of obstruction in the liver are felt, he is treated with the opening remedies mentioned that suit his temperament; if he finds harm in his head, he mends it by walking; if his food spoils in the stomach and passes down by itself, that is a gain, otherwise it is sent down with cumin and the fig paste kneaded with safflower {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.
