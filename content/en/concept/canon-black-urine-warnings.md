## Summary
Ibn Sina says black urine that is not a crisis is a bad sign, especially in acute diseases and when scanty and thick, and deadly at the start of fevers, and he reports the saying of Rufus and comments on it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.186-187}.

## In acute diseases
If it is not of that kind, black urine is a bad sign, especially in acute diseases and above all when scanty, for its scantiness shows that burning has consumed the moisture; the thicker it is the worse, the thinner the less bad. Black or deep red urine may also be passed because a drink of that colour was taken, on which nature has done no work, so that it leaves as it was {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.186}.

## Rufus and the comment
Rufus said that black urine is welcome in kidney ailments and in disorders stirred by thick humours, and is a deadly sign in acute diseases. Ibn Sina replies that black urine may also be bad in kidney and bladder ailments where burning is severe. In the old and in women it occurs only from great corruption, after exertion it shows spasm, and at the start of fevers it is deadly, as it is at their end if no lightness accompanies it and it is no sign of crisis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.186-187}.
