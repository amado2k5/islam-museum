## Summary
Ruqya in the lexicon is the charm recited over a sufferer from fever or fits, and its plural is ruqa. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8942} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8943} The majority allow healing by recitation under three conditions: that it use the words of God or His names and attributes; that it be in Arabic or in words whose meaning is known; and that the one who recites believe that it works only by God's leave, following the saying that there is no harm in charms when they contain no shirk. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8943} Some scholars disliked it as harming trust in God, and some allowed it only for the evil eye and a sting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8943} The majority allow a fee for ruqya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8944}

## Bedtime protection, and Surat al-Baqara at home
The speaker describes the protective recitations he teaches children at bedtime: the opening chapter, seeking refuge from Satan, and the two refuge chapters with breath on the palms. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:09:30} He says that reciting Surat al-Baqara at home drives Satan away, and he criticises those who question people about their children. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:10:30}

## Taqwa and refuge in God
The speaker says that refuge in God places a barrier between a person and Satan, and that taqwa opens a way out and gives provision from sources one does not expect. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:13:00} He cites a verse that one should not look to people for the solution. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:13:00} He says the Qur'an leaves nothing out, that the opening chapter is fit for seeking healing, and he calls on a person in distress to ask God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:25:30}

## The basis of ruqya in the speaker's account

## In Ahmed Abdussalam's programme 'Osalli Alayh'
In the fifteenth episode of his programme [[work/ossali-alayh|'Osalli Alayh']], [[person/ahmed-abdussalam|Ahmed Abdussalam]] explains a prophetic healing prayer for the sick: one moistens the index finger with saliva, sets it on the earth, wipes the painful place with it and says words the Prophet taught, naming God, the soil of the earth and the saliva, and that healing is by God's leave. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep15 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep15 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep15 00:07:00} He says it does not replace the doctor and the medicine, which are lawful means, but puts it before them; he holds that the healer is God and not the doctor, giving the example of two patients with one illness, given one medicine by one doctor, of whom one was cured and the other not. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep15 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep15 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep15 00:08:00} This is the speaker's view as recorded, attributed to him.
