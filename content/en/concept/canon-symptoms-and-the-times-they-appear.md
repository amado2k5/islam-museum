## Summary
Ibn Sina sorts symptoms by their timing against the disease: those fixed to it, beginning and ending with it, those with no known time that sometimes follow it and sometimes not, and those that come at the end, the signs of crisis, of ripening and of ruin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.154-155}.

## Fixed symptoms
Some begin and cease with the disease: acute fever, pricking pain, shortness of breath, cough, and the serrated pulse with pleurisy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.154-155}.

## Symptoms with no set time
Some have no known time and sometimes follow the disease and sometimes not, as headache with fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.

## Symptoms that come last
Some come at the end: the signs of crisis, the signs of ripening and the signs of ruin, mostly in acute diseases. The edition's footnote glosses crisis as the change acute diseases bring about, which the patient feels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.
