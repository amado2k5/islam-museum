## Nature and selection
It is the mi'a, whose liquid is called honey of lubna and storax; it is the gum-tear of a tree like the quince, and the author repeats what he said in the chapter on storax. It is also said to be the oil of another, Roman tree. The best kind is the mi'a that flows of itself, honeyed and gummy, sweet-smelling and yellowish, neither black nor bare; a flowing kind like myrrh is also found, and it may be adulterated with oils and honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Temperament
Hot in the first degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Actions and properties
It has a ripening, very softening, warming and dissolving power; its smoke is like that of frankincense, it numbs by nature, and the oil made of it in Syria softens strongly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Swellings and pustules
It helps hard lumps in the flesh and is smeared with oils on moist and dry pustules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It is smeared on moist scabies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Joints
It strengthens the limbs and helps stiffness of the joints, drunk and smeared {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Fresh or dry, as a fumigation it holds back a catarrh, and it is the best for a cold; it has a drowsy power, especially its oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Chest and breathing
It helps chronic cough, phlegm and sore throat and clears a hoarse voice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It aids digestion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Excretory organs
It softens the bowels and brings on urine. Drunk, the dry or the liquid kind with the gum of almond is said to loosen sticky phlegm without harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on the menses well, drunk or as a pessary, and softens hardness of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.

## Substitutes
Its substitute is castoreum, and as much oil of jasmine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.538}.
