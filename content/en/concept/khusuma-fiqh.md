## Summary
Khusuma is lexically dispute, argument and victory by proof; the jurists use it for bringing a claim before the judge. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7650} It differs from enmity, a purpose lodged in the heart to harm or avenge; disputing is speech, enmity is an act of the heart. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7650}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7651} The Hanafis define a claim as an accepted statement before the judge seeking a right against another or defending one's own, so for them claim and dispute coincide; the Shafiis define it as news of a right against another before a ruler. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7651} A dispute is of two kinds: one in which a party stands alone, as one whose admission would bind him is a disputant when he denies; and one needing another party, as in deposit, loan, hire, pledge and usurpation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7651} Rule: a claimant is a disputant if his admission would bind; a respondent whose admission cannot bind (a claim for debt against a minor's guardian) is not a disputant by denying. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7651}
