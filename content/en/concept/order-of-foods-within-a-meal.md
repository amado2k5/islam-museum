## Summary
Ibn Sina says foods have an order of use that the guardian of health should heed: a thin, quickly digested food must not follow a stronger, harder one, which it would outrun and spoil; after a slippery food a firm one follows to carry it; fish is not eaten after tiring exercise; and some with slack stomachs benefit from something astringent before the meal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.

## Thin on strong
One should beware of taking what is thin and quickly digested on top of a stronger, harder food, for it digests first and floats on it with no way through, so it rots and spoils and spoils what mixes with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.

## Slippery foods and fish
A slippery food of this kind should not be taken alone; a strong, firm food follows it so that it slides along with it into the bowels. Fish and its like are not taken after tiring exercise, lest it spoil and spoil the humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.

## Astringents and the stomach
For some people it is allowed to take something astringent before eating, namely one with a slack stomach that hurries its food downward. One must always weigh the condition and temperament of the stomach: some spoil light, quickly digested food in the stomach while strong, slow food digests well, the fiery-stomached; others are the reverse, and each is managed according to his case {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.230}.
