## Summary
Al-Battani reports from Ptolemy that Hipparchus (the name as the manuscript reads it) first worked with a year of three hundred and sixty-five days and a quarter, then found it to be somewhat less, and said it is three hundred and sixty-five days and in truth less than a quarter day, because he found the solstice arriving before the whole quarter; this put him in doubt about the Sun's motion, so that he imagined for it an eccentric sphere {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}.

## What the text says
The text says no more of him at this point. It presents him as the first of the ancients to correct the year, and makes his doubt about the Sun's motion the root of the idea that al-Battani sets out in [[concept/zij-sabi-eccentric-model-for-the-suns-equation|the eccentric sphere of the Sun]]. The man's name is written in disturbed forms in the manuscript, and his identity with the Greek astronomer Hipparchus is this work's reading, not the book's statement {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}.
