## Nature
Its strongest part is its seed, then its peel, then its leaf, then its flesh; its oil is in power like castor oil, except that it is hotter, and the wild kind shares all its qualities but is stronger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.633}

## Choice
Its strongest part is its seed, and the boiled is the most nourishing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.633}

## Temperament
Its root is hot in the first degree and moist, and its seed is hot in the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.633}

## Actions and properties
It engenders winds but its seed dissolves them, and it has a strong attenuating power, especially its seed; the wild kind inflames; the boiled is more nourishing because it leaves its medicinal quality, and its nourishment is phlegmatic and also small, and it has a substance quick to rot; its spring leaf, boiled and eaten with oil and muri, nourishes more than the root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.633}

## Cosmetics
Mixed with rye flour it grows hair in alopecia, and applied with honey as a poultice it removes the marks that come under the eye together with darkness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.633}; its seed benefits freckles on the limbs, the other strange colours, the marks of blows and the spots, and with kundus in vinegar as a lotion it removes black vitiligo, especially in the bath; and it multiplies lice on the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Swellings and pustules
With rye flour it clears milky pustules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Wounds and ulcers
Applied as a poultice with honey it removes malignant ulcers and milky ulcers, and its seed with vinegar entirely removes a gangrenous ulcer, and likewise on ringworm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Joints
Its seed repels the throbbing in the joints and is very good for pain of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Organs of the head
It is harmful to the head, the teeth and the palate, and its juice and oil are of great benefit against wind in the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Ocular organs
It is harmful to the eye, though it cleanses it when its water is dropped in and removes the marks under the corners. Ibn Masawayh said that its leaf sharpens the sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Organs of the chest
The cooked is good for old, chronic cough and for the thick chyme formed in the chest; it benefits the choking that comes from the deadly mushroom, and cooked with oxymel and gargled it benefits quinsy, though it also harms the throat; and it increases milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is bad for the stomach and causes belching; after a meal it softens the bowels and passes the food, and before a meal it floats the food and does not let it settle, and so eases vomiting, especially its peel with oxymel; as a poultice it suits the side and the spleen, and its seed with vinegar makes one vomit greatly and dissolves swelling of the spleen. Ibn Masawayh said that eaten after a meal it digests, especially its leaf, and the water of its leaf opens the obstructions of the liver and removes jaundice. Some said its leaf digests, its body nauseates, and its seed dissolves wind in the belly, eases the passing of food, stirs the appetite and ends pain of the liver, and its water is good for dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits the bite of the viper, and with wine the bite of the horned viper too; its seed benefits poisons and vermin; if a crushed piece of it is put on a scorpion it dies, and its water was tried in this and found stronger, and if a scorpion stings one who has eaten radish it does not harm him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}
