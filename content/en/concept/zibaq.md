## Nature and selection
Nature: some mercury is drawn from its ore, and some from its stones by fire, as gold and silver are drawn. When it is pure, with no earth or stone mixed in, it is close in colour to cinnabar. Galen and others thought it was made like sublimate, because it is drawn by fire; the book argues that this does not follow, since cinnabar is made from sulphur, and mercury can be drawn from it as from mineral cinnabar, which is the substance of mercury. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
Temperament: cold and moist in the second degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}

## Actions
Actions: its sublimate is astringent. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: the killed form is a remedy for lice and for the complaint the book calls sibyan, with rose oil. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
Wounds and ulcers: the killed form is used for scabies with rose oil, and with remedies for bad scabies and ulcers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
Joints: its vapour causes palsy and tremor. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
Head organs: its smoke takes away hearing; the mouth fumigated with it is also affected. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
Eye organs: its smoke takes away sight. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
Poisons: sublimated mercury is deadly through its strong cutting; its strong remedy is drinking milk and vomiting. Galen is recorded as having no experience of it. Some say the killed form kills by its weight, which the book calls an account without substance. It kills mice, and vermin and snakes flee its smoke. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.458}
