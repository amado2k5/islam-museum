## Summary
Ibn Sina says some things alter by contact and not by ingestion, such as the onion, which when applied outside as a poultice blisters but does not blister from within. Others are the opposite, such as ceruse, which if drunk alters greatly yet if smeared does nothing. Some act both ways {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.139-140}.

## Six reasons for the onion
He counts six reasons: the digestive power hastens to break it and change its temperament; it is mostly taken mixed with other things; it mingles in the vessels of food with moistures that overwhelm it; outside it keeps to one place while inside it moves on; outside it clings firmly while inside it merely touches without clinging; and once inside, the natural power takes charge of it, so its surplus is expelled and the good part turns to blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.140}.

## Ceruse
For ceruse the cause of the difference is that its parts are coarse, so it does not pass into the pores from outside, and if it did it would not reach the principal organs, whereas swallowed the case is reversed; and its poisonous nature is stirred only by the excessive action of the innate heat, which does not occur by contact outside {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.140}.
