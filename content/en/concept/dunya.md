## Summary
In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons the world is compared to a large store where a person fills his cart and pays the bill at the end {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:44:00}. ## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)
The Sheikh compares the world to a large store, a supermarket, where a person keeps filling his cart and at the end must pay the bill {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:44:00}.

The Sheikh holds that money can be one of the colours of happiness and a pillar of life, but that happiness itself cannot be bought with money; he prays that God make us happy through obedience to Him and not wretched through disobedience {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:48:30}.

He says that every stage of life has its own dignity for a Muslim. He faults the poet who wished that youth might return one day, and urges a person not to hope that time will turn back, since every age has its own merits {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:52:30}.

He adds that one should not demand things against their nature, like someone looking for a spark of fire inside water, and he applies this to marriage: if a wife is not good at showing affection (or the reverse), that is her nature and she is accepted as she is {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:53:00}.

He tells a humorous story of a wife who went with her husband to a tower after sunset in a romantic moment and expected him to say something kind about the moon and the lovely evening; instead he asked her jokingly what she would think if he buried her in the sand right now, and she wondered at such romance (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:53:30}.
