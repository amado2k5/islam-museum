## Summary
In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons the world is compared to a large store where a person fills his cart and pays the bill at the end {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:44:00}. The Sheikh holds that money may be a pillar of happiness but that happiness itself cannot be bought with it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:48:30}. He urges accepting every stage of life with its merits and accepting people's natures as they are {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:53:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)
The Sheikh compares the world to a large store, a supermarket, where a person keeps filling his cart and at the end must pay the bill {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:44:00}.

The Sheikh holds that money can be one of the colours of happiness and a pillar of life, but that happiness itself cannot be bought with money; he prays that God make us happy through obedience to Him and not wretched through disobedience {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:48:30}.

He says that every stage of life has its own dignity for a Muslim. He faults the poet who wished that youth might return one day, and urges a person not to hope that time will turn back, since every age has its own merits {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:52:30}.

He adds that one should not demand things against their nature, like someone looking for a spark of fire inside water, and he applies this to marriage: if a wife is not good at showing affection (or the reverse), that is her nature and she is accepted as she is {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:53:00}.

He tells a humorous story of a wife who went with her husband to a tower after sunset in a romantic moment and expected him to say something kind about the moon and the lovely evening; instead he asked her jokingly what she would think if he buried her in the sand right now, and she wondered at such romance (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:53:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Sharh Ketab al-Fawaed lectures (episode 5)

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh cites a saying in which the Prophet says that he does not fear poverty for his community, but fears that the world will be opened to them so that they compete for it and forget one another {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:06:00}. He says that whoever wakes up safe in his home, healthy in his body, with his daily food, has as it were been given the world in its entirety {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:50:30}.

The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that worldly fortune is divided as a test and the fortune of the Hereafter as a reward, and that success must be met with gratitude {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:45:00}. He describes the world, for those who seek it, as a carcass, while the righteous see it for what it is {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:45:30}.

The Sheikh holds, as his own view, that when the Companions honoured the Hereafter the world served them rather than ruling them, and that a person should use the world rather than serve it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:49:00}. He quotes the saying: "O world, serve whoever serves you" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 00:51:00}.
