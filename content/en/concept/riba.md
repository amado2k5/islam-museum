## Overview
Zaydan mentions the forms of riba in the chapter "Fitnat al-Mal" as a means a person resorts to in order to accumulate money, and he classifies it among economic crimes because it involves exploiting the needs and financial circumstances of others {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 140}.

## Ruling in His Methodology
The author treats engaging in riba as a crime for which the perpetrator is punished in this world and the hereafter, citing the Quranic verses that command leaving behind whatever remains of riba and establish that repentance means taking only the principal without wrongdoing {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 140}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 141}. Among the verses he cites are [[passage/quran-2-275|Al-Baqarah 275]] and [[passage/quran-2-279|Al-Baqarah 279]] {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 140}.

## His Proposed Alternative
He contrasts riba with charitable spending intended for the sake of God, citing a verse in Surah Al-Rum [[passage/quran-30-39|Al-Rum 39]] {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 141}. This is the author's own interpretation, and the book does not provide detailed juristic rulings there regarding a specific sale, loan, or type of transaction.

## From the Book Usul al-Iqtisad al-Islami
The author considers riba one of the prohibited means of acquiring ownership; he does not examine the rulings of its sub-branches in this section, referring instead to other places in his book under the chapter on exchange and distribution {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 53}. In his critique of the capitalist system, he portrays riba as a tool for entrenching the weakness of the weak and the poverty of the poor {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 46} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 47}.

## From the Book Usul al-Iqtisad al-Islami: Part Two (pp. 101–200 of the PDF)
The author holds that a loan with guaranteed interest is not permissible, while he permits qirad (profit-sharing partnership) for a share of potential profit in which any loss falls on the capital provider {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}.

The author points out that loans and suretyship are gratuitous contracts in which compensation is not permissible, and that the sharia-compliant solution for bank letters of guarantee is not charging commissions {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 147}.

The author maintains that economists have demonstrated that interest and its fluctuations have a negligible effect on saving and investment, and that attracting savings is achieved through qirad rather than interest-bearing loans {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 193}.


## Riba, Profit, and Deferred Sale
The author distinguishes between riba and profit: riba is an excess in the exchange of two identical or nearly identical items, such as gold for gold or gold for silver, whereas profit is an excess in the exchange of two different items, such as gold for wheat. For this reason, he considers the phrase "usurious profit" a major misnomer {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 261}.

Exchanging currency for currency in unequal amounts is permissible if done hand-to-hand, and deferment is not permissible in it; therefore, realizing a profit in spot currency exchange is permissible {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 261}.

In a deferred sale, it is permissible to increase the price because of the deferment period, and he gives an example: the cash price is 100 and the price deferred for one year is 110; if the debt falls due and the debtor is unable to pay, no further amount may be added, and it takes on the ruling of a loan {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 262}.

The return on monetary capital, if not lent at interest, is a share of profit in a mudarabah partnership; in the author's view, this differs from capitalism, which permitted every return, and from communism, which prohibited every return {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 262}.

The author argues that interest on production and consumption rests on fallacies, replying that Islam recognizes the productivity of capital and grants it a share of profit, and that mutual consent does not make the forbidden permissible {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 265}.

## Riba in the Financial Crisis (pp. 350–355)
The author states that all religions prohibit riba, but scholars of the capitalist system legitimized it and formulated theories to justify it; yet those theories proved only that capital has a return, not that the return must be interest {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 350} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}. He holds that Islam permits the return to be a share of profit, and does not permit a fixed lump sum or a percentage of the capital {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}. A loan in Islam is for charitable purposes and carries no interest {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}.

He permits deferred sales at a deferred price higher than the spot price, recognizing a share of the price for time, though it is not permissible to charge the debtor late-payment interest after the price falls due {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}. Islam does not advocate a zero interest rate: consumption loans may be interest-free, while production financing is based on a percentage of profits {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 351}. The author urges abandoning riba because it is forbidden and its consequences are grave {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 352}.

The author distinguishes between exchanging currency for currency—in which neither deferment nor inequality within the same genus is permissible—and exchanging currency for a commodity, in which an increase for deferment is permissible as long as the relationship remains bilateral {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}. See [[concept/currency-exchange-sarf|Currency Exchange]] and [[concept/bay-al-dayn|Sale of Debts]].

## Classification of Riba in the Paper by Qaddi and Bouzid
The researchers Qaddi Abd al-Majid and Bouzid Issam divide riba into riba al-buyu' (riba in sales) and riba al-qard (riba in loans), considering sales to be exchange contracts while loans are not {cite:qeddi-bouzid-islamic-finance-concept p.10}. They then divide riba al-buyu' into riba al-fadl (riba of excess) and riba al-nasi'ah (riba of delay) {cite:qeddi-bouzid-islamic-finance-concept p.12}. This is the classification of the two researchers and is attributed to them; it was extracted from a garbled text that needs verification against the printed edition.
