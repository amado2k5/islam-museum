## Summary
A category among the disposals of fay' and zakat in Islamic law, legislated to win hearts over, fix them upon Islam and turn harm away from the Muslim community; its broad prophetic application stood out at [[place/al-jirana|al-Ji'rana]] when the Prophet singled out Quraysh's chiefs and Najd's tribes with generous gifts {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/135-138}.

## Its origin in the Sira
After Hunayn the Prophet divided Hawazin's spoils at al-Ji'rana, giving Mecca's nobles and the chiefs of Najd's tribes — Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, Uyayna ibn Hisn, al-Aqra' ibn Habis and Suhayl ibn Amr — a hundred camels each, to comfort their hearts and settle their recent Islam {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/135-136}.

When the Ansar took the matter back to the Prophet, he explained his political and educative wisdom: "A trifle of this world by which I have reconciled people to Islam, while I entrust you to your Islam" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/142}.

## For young visitors
A wise principle in dealing with people: the Prophet gave the new tribal chiefs much wealth and many camels to win their hearts, gain their love, and fix them on good and peace.
