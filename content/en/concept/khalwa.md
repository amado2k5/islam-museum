## Summary
For many later [[concept/tasawwuf|Sufis]], the khalwa is the wayfarer's withdrawal to a place for set remembrance and the gathering of the heart, sometimes fixed at forty days. In volume ten of [[work/majmu-al-fatawa|Majmu' al-Fatawa]], [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] discusses what he calls "innovated retreats", as part of his treatment of kinds of worship that arose among later generations, and says that they resemble the lawful i'tikaf. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 393} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 404}

## Lawful i'tikaf and the retreat
Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes seclusion in mosques, as the Prophet and his Companions practised it, which is lawful worship, from the newly invented retreats. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 393} He rejects the argument some draw from the Prophet's devotions in the cave of Hira before revelation: what he did before prophethood is followed only if he made it law afterwards; once God made him a prophet he never again climbed to the cave of Hira, nor did his rightly guided successors, though he lived in Mecca for more than ten years before the Hijra and entered it for the Fulfilled Umra, the year of the conquest and the Farewell Pilgrimage. It is said, he adds, that Abd al-Muttalib introduced the custom of going there in the Jahiliyya. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 393}

## The forty days
Ibn Taymiyya reports that one group makes the retreat forty days and holds it in great esteem, citing God's appointment with Moses of thirty nights completed with ten, and the report that the Messiah fasted forty days; they say that address and descent follow it, as revelation followed the cave of Hira. He regards this as clinging to an abrogated law, just as the Sabbath was prescribed for Moses and Muslims do not keep it. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 394}

## Abu Hamid's method as Ibn Taymiyya presents it
Ibn Taymiyya says that some people of retreats keep to lawful kinds of worship – prayer, fasting, recitation and remembrance – but that most go beyond them to unlawful kinds, among them the method of [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]]: the one in retreat is told not to go beyond the obligatory acts, with no Qur'an reading or study of hadith, and to devote himself to remembrance, which is divided into that of the common folk, "There is no god but God", that of the elect, "God, God", and that of the elect of the elect, "He, He". {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 396} Ibn Taymiyya holds remembrance by the single name to be an innovation in the law and a mistake in language, since a bare noun is not speech, and cites the hadiths on the best words and remembrance: "Glory be to God, praise be to God, there is no god but God, God is greatest." {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 396} He reports that al-Ghazali praises this method at length in the [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya']] and says he heard address as Moses heard it, though it was not directed at him; Ibn Taymiyya sees this as a residue of philosophy in him, since philosophers like [[person/ibn-sina|Ibn Sina]] claim that knowledge in hearts emanates from the "active intellect". {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 398}

## The grounds of his rejection
Ibn Taymiyya lists grounds for rejecting the method, among them: that the "active intellect" has no reality; that what is cast into hearts comes through angels if it is true and through devils if it is false; that revelation came to the prophets through angels or by direct speech, not by mere emanation; that one who has emptied his heart of every thought cannot know that what then comes to it is true; and that a heart empty of the remembrance of God is taken over by the devil. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 398} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 399} This, he says, is where many wayfarers confused merciful states with satanic ones and took what befell them for the miracles of God's friends. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 400} The emptying that the Messenger brought, for him, is to empty the heart of what God does not love and fill it with what He loves – worship, love, fear and trust. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 401} He criticises al-Ghazali's comparison of purifying the heart with the Byzantines polishing their wall until the Chinese painters' work appeared in it, and holds that al-Ghazali places knowledge as inscribed in the "celestial soul", which he calls the Preserved Tablet, following Ibn Sina. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 402}

## Hunger, wakefulness and silence
Ibn Taymiyya reports that some people of retreats have set litanies, set food and known "descents", described at length by [[person/ibn-arabi-muhyi-al-din|Ibn Arabi]] and those who followed his path, and that they prescribe hunger, wakefulness and silence in retreat without limits set by the law. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 403} [[person/abu-talib-al-makki|Abu Talib al-Makki]], he says, mentions some of this but holds more firmly to the Book and the Sunna than they do, though he cites weak and even fabricated hadiths, such as the "Musabba'at" related from al-Khidr, and prayers for particular days and nights. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 403} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 404}

## The lawful retreat
Ibn Taymiyya states that lawful retreat and withdrawal is what is commanded as obligatory or recommended: the obligatory is withdrawing from and avoiding forbidden things; the recommended is withdrawing from people in needless permitted things and what is of no benefit, as Tawus said, "How good a hermitage for a man is his house". One who withdraws to some place to pursue knowledge or work while keeping to Friday and congregational prayer is, for him, in the right, and he cites the hadith of the man withdrawn in a mountain pass who prays and gives zakat. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 404} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 405} This connects with the question of [[question/is-seclusion-better-than-company-for-the-seeker|whether seclusion or company is better for the seeker]]. He criticises people of retreats who seek places without call to prayer or congregation – caves, graves and sites of relics – and holds that what befalls them there are satanic states they take for miracles. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 406}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Hanbali): Ibn Taymiyya in Majmu' al-Fatawa.** Newly invented retreats fixed by time or special litanies are an innovation; what is lawful is i'tikaf in mosques and withdrawal from evil and needless permitted things while keeping Friday and congregational prayer. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 404} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 405}

**Sufi: Abu Hamid al-Ghazali and his followers, as Ibn Taymiyya presents them.** Retreat confined to the obligatory acts and remembrance, with the heart emptied, is a path by which the wayfarer reaches unveiling; it is praised in the Ihya'. {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 396} {cite:majmu-fatawa-qasim-v10 p. 398}
:::
