## Summary
In fiqh a maḥram is someone whom a person is permanently forbidden to marry through kinship of blood, suckling or marriage alliance; the Kuwaiti encyclopedia gives the term its own entry detailing the three grounds of maḥramiyya {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13969}. Blood maḥrams are fixed by revealed text — mothers, daughters, sisters, aunts and the children of siblings — and suckling forbids what kinship forbids in the view of the majority, though they differ on the details of the analogy {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13981}.

## Effects of the maḥram relation
Maḥramiyya reaches beyond the marriage prohibition: maḥrams come first in washing and burying the dead in the view of the majority of jurists {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13974}. It appears in the law of looking and the ʿawra, where a maḥram may look "without desire" at what lies beyond the zone between navel and knee {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13972}. And it runs into transactions, where the rules distinguish maḥrams in sale, maintenance and guardianship {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13976}.

## For young visitors
A maḥram is a close relative who can never marry you — like a mother, a sister or an aunt — and the law books explain the reasons through blood, nursing and marriage ties.
