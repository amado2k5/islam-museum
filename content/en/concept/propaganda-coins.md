## Summary
The *Islamic Coins* booklet published in Doha defines propaganda (literally "media") coins as coins put into circulation either officially by the sultan or ruler for the occasion they were struck for, or unofficially by the elite or the public, secretly or openly, according to their political stance toward the state. {cite:nccah-islamic-coins-brochure p. 28} Their form was not fixed: they could carry a text, an image, a mark or a symbol expressing a favourable or hostile idea. {cite:nccah-islamic-coins-brochure p. 28}

## Examples in the booklet
- A Ghurid coin struck in Afghanistan, at Nimruz or Herat, for Sultan Ghiyath al-Din's victories over the Khwarazmians in 597 AH, meant to let people taste the victory by recording it on the chief emblem of kingship, with the motto "God is most high". {cite:nccah-islamic-coins-brochure p. 28}
- A Seljuk dinar that the booklet calls a publicity issue, struck for some occasion and inscribed with the Throne Verse. {cite:nccah-islamic-coins-brochure p. 24}
