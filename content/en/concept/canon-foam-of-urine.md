## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter five on the signs taken from the foam of urine: its origin in wind within the liquid, and what its colour, size, amount and staying power show. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.192}.

## How foam arises
Foam arises in a liquid from wind driven into the water; the force of the urinary jet and the wind passing with the urine help it, above all when wind prevails in the water, as in the urine of people with distension from much flatulence. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.192}.

## What it shows
Foam shows through its colour, since blackness and yellowness point to jaundice; through large size, which shows viscosity; through abundance, which shows viscosity and much wind; and through slow vanishing, which shows lingering viscosity and wind in kidney ailments and a long illness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.192}.
