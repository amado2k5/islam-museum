## Nature and selection
Pieces of varied shape in the colour of agaric, with a dust that inclines to astringency and bitterness; sweet in smell and pricking the tongue; it is the root of a plant and only the root is used, and it is plentiful in the lands of Macedonia. The editor's note calls it the lion's spikenard and wild dill {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the third degree, with a foreign, unripe and trifling moisture in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Actions and properties
Fine, cleansing and opening, like spikenard in power but hotter and more astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Joints
Drunk and applied it helps pains of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Too much of it causes headache, because of a surplus of raw moisture in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It helps the cold liver and the wind in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Excretory organs
Useful for difficulty of urine drunk or as a poultice, likewise for bladder pains and the retention of residues in it; it helps cramps, rumbling and bloating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on the menses and helps pain of the womb, even sitting in its water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.
