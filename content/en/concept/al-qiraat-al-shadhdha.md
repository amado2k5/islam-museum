## Summary
In the books of the readings, "irregular" does not mean for the verifiers what lies outside the seven but what has lost one of the three pillars. Applying the word to whatever is not in al-Shatibiyya and al-Taysir or not from the seven is, for Ibn al-Jazari, the idiom of one who does not know what he says. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 19} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 26} See [[concept/shurut-al-qiraa-al-sahiha|the conditions of an accepted reading]].

## Al-Bulqini's division
Al-Suyuti quotes al-Bulqini that readings divide into mass-transmitted, solitary and irregular: the mass-transmitted are the seven famous readings; the solitary are the readings of the three that complete the ten, with Companions' readings joined to them; the irregular are those of the Successors such as al-A'mash, Yahya ibn Wuthab and Ibn Jubayr. Al-Suyuti remarks that this needs examination and puts Ibn al-Jazari's criterion in its place. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 491}

## Makki's three kinds of what is reported
Ibn al-Jazari relays from Makki ibn Abi Talib that what is reported of the Qur'an falls in three kinds. **What is read today** has three marks: transmission by trustworthy narrators from the Prophet, a way that is sound in Arabic, and agreement with the script of the codex; it is held certain and one who denies it is a disbeliever. **What is soundly reported by solitary chains and sound in Arabic but against the script** is accepted but not read, for two reasons: it was not taken from consensus but from a single report, and it contradicts what has been agreed. **What a non-trustworthy narrator reported, or a trustworthy one reported with no footing in Arabic,** is not accepted even if it agrees with the script. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 23} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 24} For the second kind Ibn al-Jazari gives the reading of [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]] and [[person/abu-al-darda|Abu al-Darda']] at [[passage/quran-92-3|al-Layl 92:3]], which drops a relative particle and is reported in Sahih al-Bukhari, number 3742, as al-Sabt's reference notes. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 24} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 175}

## Reading irregular forms in prayer
Scholars differed on reading in prayer what is sound from the Companions and Successors: some allowed it because the Companions and Successors read so, which is one of two views among the followers of al-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa and one of two reports from Malik and Ahmad. Most scholars forbid it, on the grounds that it was not mass-transmitted, or was abrogated by the final review, or by the Companions' consensus on the Uthmanic codex, or was not transmitted in a way that establishes Qur'an. Others took a middle way: one who reads it in the Fatiha when able to read another has not prayed validly, while one who reads it where reading is not obligatory has not invalidated his prayer. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 24} Ibn al-Jazari also records Ibn Daqiq al-Id's difficulty: if irregular readings were transmitted by solitary reports from the Prophet, then it is known by necessity that he recited some of them, so that reading is mass-transmitted even if not identified, and how can it be called irregular? {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 25}

## What was rejected outright
Among the rejected Ibn al-Jazari lists what a non-trustworthy narrator reported, like most of what is in the books of irregular readings, including readings attributed to Abu Hanifa that al-Khuza'i compiled and al-Hudhali relayed; he reports that al-Daraqutni and others judged that book fabricated. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 26} He also lists what agrees with Arabic and the script but was never transmitted: Ibn Miqsam of Baghdad had allowed reading anything sound in Arabic that agrees with the codex, and a council was held in Baghdad, with jurists and readers present, which agreed to forbid it; he repented and withdrew. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 27} For this reason reading by unbounded analogy was ruled out, on the saying of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] and Zayd ibn Thabit among the Companions and Urwa ibn al-Zubayr and others among the Successors: 'recitation is a sunna that the later takes from the earlier'. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 27}
