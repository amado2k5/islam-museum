Yawm in lexicon is a stretch of time beginning at sunrise and ending at sunset; its plural is ayyam, and the feminine plural is the more common. The technical sense is the span from the rising of the second dawn to sunset. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18011}

Among related terms, nahr (daylight) runs from sunrise to sunset, so the yawm is longer than the nahr; layl (night) runs from sunset to the true dawn or to sunrise, and stands opposite the day. Hin is a period of time, short or long: the Hanafis, with al-Awza'i and Abu 'Ubayd, take an unqualified hin to be six months, since the shortest absolute hin in God's speech is six months, and they carry men's unqualified speech on that; Malik held that an oath not to do something "for a hin, a time or an age" binds for a year; the Shafi'is let hin fall on the long and the short alike, so "you are divorced for a hin" takes effect in a moment. Waqt is a stretch of time appointed for a matter, and is more general than yawm. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18011} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18012}

Of the rules attaching to the day is the vow of a day's retreat (i'tikaf): if the day is named the jurists differ on when the retreatant enters; if it is unspecified the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold he must enter before dawn and leave after sunset, that being the sense of an unqualified day, while the Malikis add a night, the night of the vowed day not the one after it, so he enters before sunset or at it; Ibn al-Hajib says one who enters before sunset is credited with his day and one who enters after dawn is not, with two views on what falls between. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18013}

On splitting the hours of a vowed retreat day: if the day was named, like Thursday, splitting is not allowed by agreement; if unspecified, the Hanbalis and the Shafi'is on the sound view forbid splitting because the word day implies consecutiveness, while the counter-view among the Shafi'is allows it, counting the hours of a day as the days of a month. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis state that one who vows a day's retreat during the daylight, then enters the mosque and stays until the same hour the next day, has fulfilled it, the night in between doing no harm since staying overnight keeps the sequence; Abu Ishaq of the Shafi'is disagreed, and the case does not arise for Hanafis and Malikis since fasting is a condition of a valid retreat for them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18013} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18014}

On vowing the retreat of the day a person arrives: the vow is valid since it is possible; if the man comes during the day the remainder is due and no making-up of what passed, since it passed before the condition of obligation — the view of the Shafi'is and Hanbalis where fasting is not required; where it is required, among the Hanafis, Malikis and some Shafi'is and Hanbalis, a full day is due; if he comes at night nothing is owed. If an excuse such as imprisonment or illness held the vower back, he makes it up and pays expiation for missing its time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18014}

The jurists agree in general that one who misses a vowed retreat on a named day owes a make-up; the Shafi'is hold that making it up at night suffices, since the unqualified vow can be fulfilled as bound while the named day cannot; a vow of half a day's retreat binds nothing, for fasting is a condition of its validity with them and half a day cannot be fasted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18014}

On nights following days: the Hanafis state that a vow of a number of days' retreat binds the nights with the days, since the plural of days draws in the nights that face them and conversely; every night follows the day after it, so night enters a vow of days only when a number is named. They except the rites of hajj, where the nights follow the preceding days in ruling, not in reality: standing at Arafa on the night of the sacrifice before dawn suffices, and slaughter and stoning are valid on the night of the sacrifice and the two nights after, since the acts done by day may be done in the night that follows, out of ease for the people. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18015} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18016}

On suspension to a day: most jurists hold that "you are divorced on Friday" or "on the first day of a named month" takes effect at the dawn of that day, and "on the day Zayd arrives" at the dawn of his day of arrival even if he comes at its last moment. The Malikis ask the intent: if he meant to hang it on the arrival itself, the divorce falls with the arrival even at night; if on the time, it falls at once. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18015} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18016}
