## External causes, weakness of the brain and strength of its sense
As for headache from causes that come from outside, such as a blow or a fall, meeting hot or cold things or drying poisonous winds, winds strongly scented, pleasant or foul, or a wind pent up in the nose and the ear, it is inferred from their presence; if they were overlooked one goes back to their traces and infers from them in the way that will be shown {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.50} The one that comes from weakness of the brain is shown by its stirring at the least cause and by dullness of the senses and a fault in the acts of the brain; the one that comes from strength of the sense of the brain is shown by quickness to be affected, again by the least cause that the brain can sense, of sounds, smells and the like, but the sense is keen, the passages are clear and the acts of the brain are not afflicted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.50-51}

## Matters, winds and vapours
Headache from all the material causes shares in the heaviness found and the moisture of the nostril. If the matter is sharp, the heaviness comes with redness and heat, especially with the thicker matters, and pulsation may accompany it. As for moisture of the nostril, it may become heavy if the matters are thick, and dryness of the nostrils in such a headache is no proof that there is no matter if heaviness accompanies it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} The bilious one is marked by stinging, strong burning and pricking, which are stronger in it than in others, with dryness of the nostrils, thirst, sleeplessness and a yellow colour, and with less heaviness in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} The cold one may be shown by the urine, by the length of time and by the colour; if the fullness is from indigestion, loss of appetite and sluggishness show it; and the moist matters, cold or hot, may be shown by the coma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} The phlegmatic and the black-bile ones do not hurt much; with dry matters the heaviness is less and sleeplessness is greater; the cold ones are free of inflammation and bring much corrupt thought and a dull colour; and each humour may be inferred from the colour of the face and the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} This may sometimes differ in a few cases, and the reason is either a thrusting of the inflamed humour into the depth or its being pent up there, or a drawing of hot matters, other than the painful cold matters, to the region of the eyes and the face because of the pain; for pain, when it settles in an organ, draws matter to it and to what adjoins it, and what is most drawn to the organ in such a case is blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} As for the one from winds, heaviness is less with it and distension is greater, sometimes with pricking and sometimes as if eating away; humming and ringing in the ears may show the windy and the vaporous one; the jugular veins may swell much with it; and there may be much movement, that is, movement of the pain from place to place {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} When the vapour is much, the pulsing of the arteries grows strong, corrupt imaginings appear, and giddiness and vertigo accompany it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51}

## The plain temperaments
The signs of the one from plain temperaments are the feeling of those temperaments without heaviness and with dryness of the nostrils, for dryness of the nostrils is a fitting sign for this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} In the hot one the sick person himself feels heat and inflammation, and so does one who touches his head; there is redness of the eye, and he benefits from coolers and cold. In the cold one it is the opposite: their faces do not show the thinness of wasting nor redness of colour, and the pain is not excessive even if long-lasting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51} The dry one is shown by earlier evacuations, exercises, much sleeplessness or griefs, and it is its nature to increase with the repetition of any of these {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51}

## Intercourse (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
In listing what points to the dry headache, Ibn Sina names much intercourse among the things that came before it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.51}
