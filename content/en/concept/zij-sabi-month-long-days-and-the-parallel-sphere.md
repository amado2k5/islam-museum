## Summary
Beyond the line at 66 degrees the length of the day is known from the declination table: the day lasts a month, two, three, more or less, with a night to match, up to the line where the pole stands ninety parts high. There the whole year is one day, half day and half night; the north pole is at the zenith, the equator lies on the horizon, and the rotation of the heavens is millstone-like. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.27-28}.

## Between the wheel and the millstone
He explains that the arcs of the ecliptic the Sun crosses on either side of the solstice point are always visible, or always hidden, on these lines, and that the remainder left when the declination of those arcs is taken from ninety is the line's distance from the equator, the pole's altitude there. On the ninety-degree line the whole northern half of the zodiac, from the first of Aries to the end of Virgo, is above the earth always and the southern half always hidden, so the year is one day of about six months of daylight and six of night. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.28}.

## Between the equator and this line
Between the equator line and this line the circuits vary between the wheel-like and the millstone-like, according to how far the place lies from the equator and how near it is to each limit. So the text knows three kinds of position for the heavens: the wheel at the equator, the millstone at the ninety-degree line, and the graded positions between. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.28}.
