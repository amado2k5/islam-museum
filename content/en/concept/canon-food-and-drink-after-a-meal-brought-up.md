## Summary
Ibn Sina advises one who has vomited after a meal eaten for the purpose to hold back from food until hunger is strong, to quench thirst with apple drink rather than julep or oxymel, to eat young chicken, and, if he has brought up something sour, to put off his meal until midday {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.285-286}.

## After the meal
One who vomits after a meal eaten for vomiting should hold off eating until hunger sharpens and quench his thirst with something like apple drink, not julep or oxymel, which satisfy; his fitting food is young chicken and three cups after it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}.

## One who brought up something sour
If one brings up something sour, being unused to it, and his pulse shows a slight fever, let him put his meal off till midday and drink hot rose water before it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.285-286}. One who brings up black bile should lay on his stomach a sponge soaked in hot, warmed vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.286}.

## Varying the food
It is best that the food for vomiting be varied. After excessive vomiting small birds and young pigeons are of benefit, provided the bones of their limbs are not eaten, being heavy and slow in the stomach, and then he enters the bath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.286}.
