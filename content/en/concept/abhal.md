## Nature and selection
Abhal is the juniper tree (al-ar'ar), in two kinds, small and large, brought from the land of Rum; it resembles the hawthorn (zu'rur) except that it is blacker, with a sharp smell {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}. Its tree is of two kinds: one has leaves like the leaves of the cypress, with many thorns, spreading without height; the other has leaves like the tamarisk, with a taste like the cypress, and is drier and less hot; it is said to take the place of cinnamon in an amount the book gives and which is not reproduced here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}.

## Temperament and actions
Some said: hot and dry in the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}. It is strongly resolving, dries with a sting, has a hidden astringency, and enters the warming oils and the perfumed oils, mostly the oil of 'asir (grape juice) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}.

## Uses by organ
Its powder helps the eating ulcer (akila) and putrid ulcers, with honey, and holds back the creeping ulcer (al-sa'iya) and blackened ulcers; it has been applied as a poultice, but it does not cicatrize because of its sting, its great heat and its dryness, rather it dries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}. It is said that an ear drop made from the nuts of abhal helps deafness greatly, and the way it is made is not reproduced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that when it is drunk it "makes blood pass in the urine and makes the foetus fall", and that it does the same when used as a pessary or as a fumigation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation.
