## Principles of cure
This illness is treated by warming the brain and moistening it if with dryness, or by dissolving what is in it, and evacuations if from matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.98} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.99} With that, attention must turn to rousing the heart; he says there is no need to lengthen the speech on this, for the way of such regimen was learnt in the earlier rules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.99}

## General regimen
His dwelling must be a bright house; in sum, wakefulness and sleeplessness, the lightening of the food, the inclination to a drier temperament and to the lightening, tempering and warming of the blood, so that it is not in violent boiling and vaporising but hot, fine and not surging, is what kindles the mind and clears it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.99} Nothing is worse for the mind than fullness from moist foods {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.99} Dryness harms the mind, not through deficiency but through excess in the speed of movement, or through a great lack of spirit and its dissolving with the least movement {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.99}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are left out here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.98-99}
