## Nature and selection
Anisun is the seed of Roman fennel; it is less pungent than the Nabatean fennel and has sweetness, and it is better than the Nabatean {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}.

## Temperament and actions
Galen says it is hot in the second degree and dry in the third, and the printed text adds: "and both said (it is) in the third" {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}. It opens with a slight astringency, soothes pains, induces sweat and resolves winds, especially when roasted, and it has a sharpness by which it approaches the burning drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}.

## Uses by organ
It helps puffiness of the face and swelling of the limbs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.342}. For the head, if it is used as a fumigation and its vapour is inhaled it soothes headache and giddiness, and if it is ground, mixed with rose oil and dropped into the ear it heals what befalls the inside of the ear from a blow or a knock, and their pains as well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}. It helps chronic pannus of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}. It cuts the thirst that comes from the borax-like humours, and helps obstructions of the liver and spleen that come from humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}. It promotes urine, and may bind the bowel, its promoting of urine helping it in this, and it opens obstructions of the kidneys and bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}. It wards off the harm of poisons and vermin, and fennel corrects it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it promotes milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}; that it promotes the white menses and cleans the womb of the flow of white humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}; that it stirs desire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}; and that it opens obstructions of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.343}; reports attributed to the book, without recommendation.
