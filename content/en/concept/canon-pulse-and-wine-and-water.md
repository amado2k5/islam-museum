## Summary
Ibn Sina gives wine a ruling of its own for the pulse, then speaks of water, and separates hot wine from cold and both from its strengthening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.178-179}.

## Hot and cold wine
Wine has a feature of its own: although much of it brings unevenness, through the thinness, fineness, delicacy and lightness of its substance it calls for a smaller amount than its like among foods. When wine is actually cold it brings what cold things bring, smallness, wide spacing and slowness, and quickly, for its rapid penetration. When wine passes into the body hot it is not far from the innate heat and quickly disperses; if it passes cold it harms more than other cold things, since it hurries to penetrate before it is warmed through, and the harm is great, especially in bodies prone to it. One garbled line here is not cited {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.178}.

## Strengthening, and water
Considered from the side of its strengthening, wine has other rulings, being in itself a strengthener of the healthy and a reviver of power. The discussion here concerns its power to turn quickly into spirit, which always strengthens, more when heating or cooling in a body helps it, less when it opposes it. Galen is cited that pomegranate juice always strengthens the hot-natured and honey water the cold-natured. Water strengthens by carrying food through and acts like wine, but since it cools and does not heat it does not reach wine's measure in raising need {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.179}.
