## Overview
The most beautiful names of God (asma' Allah al-husna) are the names of perfection and majesty by which God named Himself; the famous hadith reports: "God has ninety-nine names — a hundred less one — whoever enumerates them enters Paradise" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.

## In the scholars' books
Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya devoted a section of Jala' al-Afham to the ninety-nine names, transmitting and commenting on them {cite:jala-afham-ibn-qayyim}; later writers expanded the subject into chapters on creed, remembrance and supplication, making it among the most-repeated topics in Islamic writing.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 3)

The Sheikh says that God is the Guide before there exists anyone who needs guidance, that is, from eternity, and that He is the First, the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:04:00}. He adds that God is the Provider before there exists anyone who needs provision {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:04:00}.

The Sheikh says these are among the most beautiful names of God, and when they settle in the servant's heart he gains full certainty in the sincerity of his reliance on God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:04:30}. He explains that God's guidance exists before the existence of those who need it, and that His provision exists before the servants are created {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:04:30}.

The Sheikh stresses again that God is the Provider before He creates those who need provision {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:05:00}. He then turns to the softening of hearts, likening it to a gradual process of making something supple, prays that God soften the hearts of those present, and links this to opening the Qur'an and reading it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:05:00}.

## In Ahmad Farid's lessons 'The Boat'
In [[work/the-boat-ahmad-farid|'The Boat']] [[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] names knowing God as the first thing by which the tree of faith grows: the more a servant knows God, the more he loves Him, fears Him and relies on Him, as the Prophet said he was the most knowing of God and the most fearful of Him. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep09 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep09 00:01:00} We should come to know God by His Most Beautiful Names, he says, and 'husna' is the plural of 'ahsan', the best, not of 'hasan', good, because God chose for Himself the best names, which contain the best attributes; souls are made to love perfection, so the more one knows the Exalted Lord the more one loves Him. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep09 00:01:30} He gives [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]]'s reading of the verse that jinn and humans were created to worship as 'to know', that is, to know their Lord by His names, attributes, lordship and divinity. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep09 00:02:00}

## In Ahmed Abdussalam's programme 'Osalli Alayh'
In the seventeenth episode of his programme [[work/ossali-alayh|'Osalli Alayh']], [[person/ahmed-abdussalam|Ahmed Abdussalam]] dwells on the names of God in the praise with which the Prophet opened his night prayer. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:03:00} He reads 'the Light' as an invitation to ask God to bring one out of the darkness of poverty into the light of sufficiency, and out of the darkness of defeat and disgrace into the light of honour and covering. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:02:30} 'The Sustainer' he reads as God's lasting care for His servants' affairs, with the example of a student who sleeps trusting his mother to wake him for an examination, and sleep overtakes them both. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:04:30} He distinguishes 'the Owner' from 'the King': an owner may own while another commands, as a father owns a building that his son runs, whereas God is both Owner and King. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:07:00} He adds that God is forbearing: He gives respite but does not neglect. {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-ahmed-abdussalam ossali-alayh/ep17 00:06:00}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: forbidden and permitted names
Al-Tabari explains the order of "Allah", then "al-Rahman", then "al-Rahim" in the basmala: names God forbade creatures to bear come first, because they are His alone, such as Allah, al-Rahman and al-Khaliq. Names He permitted creatures to bear among themselves follow, such as al-Rahim, al-Sami', al-Basir and al-Karim. Since no one can be a god in any sense besides Him, the name Allah is put first. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 133} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 134}
He reports al-Hasan al-Basri's view that al-Rahman is among the names God forbade His servants to bear, and supports it with [[passage/quran-17-110|al-Isra' 110]], where God is called upon as Allah or as al-Rahman. The editor calls this argument one of the best that has been said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 133} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 134}
