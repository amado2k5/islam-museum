## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the chapter on vomiting by naming those least fit to be made to vomit by the physician: by nature the narrow-chested and those prone to spitting blood, and the thin-necked; by habit those who find vomiting hard; he adds that the weak of stomach and the very fat suit purging, and the lean suit vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}.

## By nature
Those least deserving of an emetic by their nature are everyone narrow in the chest and poor in breath and prone to spitting blood, and all thin-necked people and those liable to swellings in the throat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}. The weak of stomach and the very fat suit purging, while the lean are better suited to vomiting because of their bilious nature {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}.

## By habit
Anyone who finds vomiting hard or is not used to it, if made to vomit with strong emetics, soon has the vessels of the breathing organs split and falls into consumption. Where the case is unclear he is tried on light emetics, and if these come easily he may then be given strong ones such as hellebore {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}.
