## Definition
The ghunna is a resonant sound that issues from the nasal passage; Ibn al-Jazari closes his count of the makharij with "and the ghunna — its makhraj is the khayshum", making it the seventeenth makhraj on its own {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## Where it occurs
The ghunna is intrinsic to the nun and the mim, and appears at its fullest in the doubled form of each — "sound the ghunna in a doubled mim then a doubled nun, and call each letter of ghunna that comes forth", says al-Jamzuri {cite:ar-wikisource}. It is also obligatory in ikhfa and iqlab, in the idgham of «ينمو» among the rules of the still nun, and in hiding the mim at the ba on the reading Ibn al-Jazari attributes to "the people of performance" {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## Its measure
The people of performance measure the ghunna at about two counts; anything longer is not called ghunna but a sound in the nose — the distinction the tajwid manuals keep among the qualities of performance {cite:matn-shatibiyya-ghawthani-2010}.
