## Summary
In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons, mercy is the principle that governs how a Muslim treats people and animals: it calms the soul and keeps hope alive. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:04:00}

## God's mercy
He says God has made mercy binding on Himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:04:30} He distinguishes God's mercy in this world, which covers believer and unbeliever, from His mercy in the Hereafter, which is shown to those who are merciful to His servants. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:06:00} He also says a Muslim recites the basmala many times a day; the number he gives is not clear in the recording (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:05:00}

## The mercy of the mission
He cites the verse that calls the mission a mercy to the worlds and sees God's mercy as shown in sending the Prophet. [[passage/quran-21-107|the verse that calls the mission a mercy to the worlds]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:06:30} He says believers show mercy to one another, like one body. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:07:00}

## Mercy to the poor: the pomegranate
He tells how Fatima craved a pomegranate; Ali bought one, and on his way back met a beggar. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:08:00} He says Islam gives the needy their right to help, not the honour of poverty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:08:30} He says the beggar asked for the pomegranate and was given it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:11:30}

## Mercy to animals
He says mercy extends to animals and urges kindness in slaughter: the slaughterer should sharpen his knife and ease the animal. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:14:00}

## Mercy at home and in the neighbourhood
He says mercy includes kin, and that a neighbour must not be harmed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:18:30} He tells how Hind, the wife of Abu Sufyan, asked the Prophet whether she might take from her stingy husband's money without his knowledge, and was told to take what is reasonable. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:20:30}

## His conclusion
He says God does not show mercy to those who do not show mercy to one another. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:21:00} He says a heart that holds mercy does not find the way of hypocrisy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:50:00}

## Mercy between spouses and in upbringing
Between spouses, the Sheikh distinguishes affection from mercy and places affection in the days of engagement, when each adorns themselves for the other. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:06:00}
He recounts that a man told the Prophet he had ten children (needs_source: the number is spoken once in the transcript) and had never kissed one of them, and the Prophet told him that whoever shows no mercy is shown none. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:10:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh urges a person to put himself in another's place when he sees someone weeping bitterly, for example someone dismissed from his job, and says our problem is that none of us puts himself in anyone else's place {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:28:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh describes the Prophet as compassionate towards the believers, and distinguishes compassion (ra'fa) from mercy (rahma): in his account, ra'fa is fear for the believers' hardship, and rahma is prayer for them (needs_source: the distinction) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 00:38:30}. He describes the believers who spend in ease and in hardship, restrain their anger and pardon people, and he notes that God loves the good-doers {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:21:00}.

The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that the sinner relies on God's mercy, and that his hope in God's pardon can exceed his hope in his deeds {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 00:51:30}. He says that Suhayl rejected the word "the Merciful" at the opening of the document, and compares this with the disputes of Abraham (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:37:00}.

## In Abdul Hamid Kishk's sermons
[[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Abdul Hamid Kishk]] makes mercy to the poor, the wayfarer, the widow and the afflicted the last of [[concept/shurut-qabul-al-salah|the conditions for the acceptance of prayer]], and calls it the humane side of the hadith. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep05 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep05 00:15:30} He says that if he spoke of mercy forever the talk would not end, because Muslims have grown hard-hearted. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep06 00:17:30} He describes the Prophet as a mercy to people, merciful to widows, to the afflicted and to all people. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep06 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep06 00:19:00} He makes the one who strives for the widow and the poor the image of the social side of the Sharia. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:16:30}
