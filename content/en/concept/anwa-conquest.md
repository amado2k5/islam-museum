## Summary
In the lexicon, 'anwa is overpowering and seizure by force; as a term among the jurists it is what is taken from land in war by force and conquest, as against land taken by treaty {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11902}. The jurists agree that movables taken in war are part of the spoils; they differ on land taken by force. The Hanafis hold that the imam chooses between dividing it and leaving its people on it for a land tax (kharaj), so that the army does not own it. The Malikis hold, in the preferred view, that it becomes an endowment for the Muslims. The Shafi'is hold that a fifth goes to the purposes named in the Qur'anic verse on spoils and the rest is divided among the fighters, unless they give it up for the public interest {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11902} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11903}. The details are treated under «ghanima» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11903}.
