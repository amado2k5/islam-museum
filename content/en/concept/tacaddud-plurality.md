## Overview
Ta'addud means plurality, and it arises in many matters, some lawful and some forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5393}

## Muezzins and the congregation
More than one muezzin for one mosque is lawful, and for the Shafiis and Hanbalis recommended, not beyond four. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5393}
If the quarter's imam has prayed and another congregation arrives, the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafiis dislike forming a second congregation in it, except a road-mosque without an imam or a muezzin; they cite the report of Abu Bakra's son about the Prophet praying with his household after returning. The Hanbalis do not dislike it, citing the general virtue of the congregation and the hadith of Abu Sa'id. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5394}

## More than one Friday prayer
The majority forbid more than one Friday prayer in one town except by necessity, such as a crowded mosque; the Hanafis allow it generally. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5394}

## Expiations, fidyas and sales with several things
The expiation of the fast is not repeated by repeated intercourse in one day, but is repeated if the breaking recurs after the first expiation; when days were broken before any expiation the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis require several, the Hanafis one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5394} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5395}
Fidyas multiply with several forbidden acts in pilgrimage, and with several game killed, by agreement. A sale multiplies with several sellers or buyers, with a split price, and with different things sold. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5395}

## Pledges, pre-emption and bequests
If two houses are pledged for one debt, neither is released until the whole debt is paid, since the whole of each is pledged for all of it. In pre-emption, when several have the right, the Shafiis divide by shares and the Hanafis by heads. In bequests, duties owed to God come before voluntary ones. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5395} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5396}

## Marriage up to four and the guardians
Marriage to four wives is lawful by the Qur'an, with justice between them. When a woman's guardians are equally near, the eldest and best is preferred, and if they dispute, lots decide. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5396}

## Divorce, retaliation and discipline
A free husband holds three pronouncements over a free wife, after which she is irrevocably separated. When several kill one, all are killed in retaliation by agreement; when one kills several, he is killed too. For abuse with several insults, some Hanafis punish for each word. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5396}

## Judges and imams
The ruler may appoint two or more judges in one town; the majority forbid two or more imams at once, however far apart the regions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5396}
