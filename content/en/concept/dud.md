## Nature and selection
Kermes, the dyers' worm. Its strength is like that of asfidhaj but finer and deeper; it is taken from many things, even oak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: the fresh worm is cooling, and it has some dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}. Actions: it dries without sting; Galen is cited as saying it is moderately astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds: powdered with wine, or vinegar with honey, for wounds of the nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}. A many-legged red worm, when a dose is drunk, is said to cure spasm and tetanus; this is a listing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}.
Head: the many-legged worm found under jars, crushed with pomegranate peel and rose oil, and dropped into the ear, calms its pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}.
Chest: the red worm found under water jars, rubbed on the palate with honey, helps angina and asthma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.435}.
Food: the many-legged worm helps jaundice when drunk with wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.436}.
Excretory organs: the many-legged worm under bubbles and jars, drunk with wine, is good for difficult urination {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.436}.
Poisons: green worm of the herb, crushed with oil, is wiped on the bites of pests {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.436}.
