## Summary
Muqasa is the set-off of a debt that a person can claim from his debtor against a debt that person owes the debtor in return; it is one way debts are discharged. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14891}

## Related terms
Muqasa is one debt cancelling another of equal kind under conditions; hawala moves a debt from one debtor to another; ibra' is release without return, while muqasa is release for a consideration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14891} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14892}

## Ruling
Muqasa is lawful. The transmitted evidence is the report from Ibn 'Umar about exchanging camels at al-Baqi', in which the Prophet says there is no harm in taking the price on the day's rate so long as the parties have not parted with something owed between them; the rational argument is that a debt cannot be taken in kind, so it is taken by its equivalent. The source notes that the report is weak. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14892}

## Voluntary and compelled
Muqasa is voluntary when the two debtors agree, and compelled when two debts meet under conditions. The majority require for compelled set-off that the two debts match in kind, quality, due date and strength; voluntary set-off needs none of this. The Malikis rarely hold set-off to be compelled. It is always for the lesser of the two debts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14893} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14894}

## Conditions by school
The Malikis distinguish cases by whether the kinds match, whether the debts arise from sale or loan, and whether they are due, and set out when set-off is permitted and when forbidden. The Shafi'is require debts fixed in the liability, not specific goods; that they be in money; that both be settled; that they match in kind, type, due date and term; and that the matter not concern retaliation or a fixed penalty. The Hanbalis allow set-off between two cash debts of one kind that are due or share a term, and forbid it between cash of different kinds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14894} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14895} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14896} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14897} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14898}

## Applications
In zakat, a debt owed by a poor man does not count as zakat until he has been paid and returns it, in the sounder Shafi'i view. The Hanafis allow a husband to set his claim against a wife's maintenance only by consent; the Hanbalis allow it if she is solvent. In usurpation and deposits the Hanafis hold that the item must be in the hand of its holder before set-off applies. Salam, guarantee, waqf and bequest have their own rulings, given in their places. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14898} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14899} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14900} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14901} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14902}
