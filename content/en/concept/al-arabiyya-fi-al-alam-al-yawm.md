## Summary
This page gathers what the United Nations page and the UNESCO page say of World [[language/arabic|Arabic]] Language Day and of the General Assembly decision of 1973, and it sets the speaker figures of three sources side by side without picking one, because the sources themselves differ.

## The United Nations
The United Nations page says World Arabic Language Day is celebrated every year on 18 December since 2012, and that the date coincides with the day in 1973 on which the General Assembly adopted Arabic as the sixth official language of the Organization. Its background says the Department of Public Information decided, on the eve of International Mother Language Day, to celebrate each of the six official languages, and that 18 December was designated for Arabic to mark resolution 3190, marked XXVIII on the page, which introduced Arabic as an official and working language of the United Nations. It states the aim of the day as raising awareness of the history, cultures and evolution of Arabic {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day}. The text of the resolution itself was not read; the number and the date come from the page.

## UNESCO
The UNESCO page is headed World Arabic Language Day, 18 December 2026, and gives the theme Arabic Language and Cultural Diversity: Celebrating Dialogue and Mutual Understanding. It says Arabic is a pillar of the cultural diversity of humanity, one of the most widely spoken languages in the world, used daily by more than 400 million people, and that the day is celebrated every year on 18 December since 2012, the date on which the General Assembly adopted Arabic as the sixth official language in 1973 {cite:unesco-world-arabic-language-day}.

## The speaker figures as given
The figures differ between sources, and even within a single page. The United Nations page says in its short description that Arabic is used daily by more than 400 million people, and says in its background paragraph that more than 500 million people speak it daily {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day}. The UNESCO page says more than 400 million daily {cite:unesco-world-arabic-language-day}. The Encyclopaedia Britannica says in its article on the Semitic languages that Standard Arabic is spoken as a first language by more than 200 million people and that a further 250 million in the region speak it as a second language, and that Arabic was the most important Semitic language by number of speakers in the early 21st century {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. The sources use different wordings: daily, first language, second language. The museum confirms none of these figures and does not prefer one.

## Where the speakers live
The United Nations page says speakers live throughout the Arab region and in many neighbouring areas, including Türkiye, Chad, Mali, Senegal and Eritrea {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day}. For the states where Arabic is official according to the encyclopedia see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab states and the League of Arab States]]; for what the page says of Arabic's influence on other languages see [[concept/athar-al-arabiyya-fi-al-lughat|what Arabic gave other languages]]; and for its place in the family see [[concept/al-lughat-al-samiyya|Arabic among the Semitic languages]].

## What this page does not reach
The text of General Assembly resolution 3190 in the United Nations Digital Library was not read, so the number and the date are credited to the United Nations page. That page shows no publication date, although its text refers to the 2026 observance. UNESCO's decisions establishing the day were not read here, nor national statistics, nor the method by which any of the figures was counted, so the differences between daily use and first language remain unknown. The page therefore relays the figures and does not estimate the number of Arabic speakers.
