## Summary
Chapter 13 begins with the risings of the signs in each country. At the equator the signs pass through midheaven and the horizon by one and the same amount of equatorial times, but in places that lean north of it the risings differ: a sign rises by more than its rising in the right sphere or by less, its opposite rises by less by the amount of that excess, and each sign's setting equals the rising of its opposite. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.43}.

## The right sphere and the rest
He says: the risings of the signs at the equator we have already given; they pass there through midheaven and the horizon by one amount of the equator's times, and so they do through midheaven in every country. But elsewhere, in places leaning from it to the north in all countries, their risings differ on the horizons. The risings in the right sphere are those given earlier, in chapter 5, and their table begins at Capricorn. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.43}.

## Excess, shortfall and each sign's opposite
He explains: when a place has a latitude, that is, when it leans from the equator, the risings of the signs differ for it, exceeding their risings at midheaven, which are their risings in the right sphere, or falling short of them. Every sign that rises in a country by more than its rising in the right sphere has an opposite that rises there by less than its rising in the right sphere, by the amount of that excess, and the setting of each sign in each country equals the rising of its opposite in it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.43}.
