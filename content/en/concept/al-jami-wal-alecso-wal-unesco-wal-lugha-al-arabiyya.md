## Summary
This page sets out the regional and international framework of the Arabic language as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it: the Charter of the League of Arab States of 1945, the related instruments, the role of UNESCO, and that of ALECSO, the Arab League Educational, Cultural and Scientific Organization. The museum did not open the pages cited, so everything here is ascribed to the summary.
## No binding Arab charter of the language
The summary says no single formal Arab Charter of the Arabic Language matching a binding League-wide treaty was identified. The 1945 Charter of the League of Arab States is in Arabic and supports cultural cooperation, and related instruments promote joint planning, protection, development and use of Arabic as a core element of Arab identity, culture, education and knowledge production, among them the 2021 UAE Declaration on the Arabic Language and ongoing ALECSO frameworks such as the Language Policy System in Arab Countries {cite:refworld-league-of-arab-states-charter} {cite:alecso-org}.
## UNESCO
UNESCO promotes Arabic as one of the six official UN languages, since 1973, and supports multilingualism and cultural heritage, including Arabic calligraphy on the Intangible Cultural Heritage list, and programmes such as the Prince Sultan Bin Abdulaziz Al Saud Programme for the Arabic Language, launched in 2016 with a second phase, to enhance Arabic's global presence, digital content, education and intercultural dialogue {cite:unesco-arabic-language-page}. Calligraphy has its record at [[concept/islamic-calligraphy|Islamic calligraphy]].
## ALECSO
The organisation, by the summary, promotes, develops and coordinates Arabic language policies, education, Arabisation, terminology standardisation, digital tools and cultural initiatives across the 22 member states, for example the Common Framework of Reference for Arabic, the Language Policy System, unified terminology platforms and activities for World Arabic Language Day {cite:alecso-org}. The organisation has a record at [[group/alecso|ALECSO]] and the occasion at [[concept/yawm-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-al-alami|World Arabic Language Day]].
## Where the texts are
The summary names the League Charter texts on Refworld and United Nations sources, the ALECSO site, coverage of the UAE Declaration through official UAE channels, and UNESCO's page on the Arabic language {cite:refworld-league-of-arab-states-charter} {cite:alecso-org} {cite:unesco-arabic-language-page}.
## The summary's closing note
The summary observes that these instruments generally treat Arabic as a core marker of national and Arab identity while navigating multilingual realities, such as Amazigh recognition in Algeria and Morocco and English and French influences, and that official texts are best consulted in the original Arabic or the national gazettes and the Universite Laval database for precision. National texts stand against this in [[concept/qanun-al-taarib-al-jazairi|Algeria]] and [[concept/qanun-himayat-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-fi-qatar-2019|Qatar]].
## What the summary does not say
The summary gives neither the text of the 2021 UAE Declaration nor its articles, nor details of the Language Policy System, so the museum adds nothing about them. For the older history of the institutions see [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|the Union of Arab Language Academies]].

## Between a binding instrument and a guiding framework
The summary separates a binding instrument not found from guiding frameworks that exist: the Charter, the declarations and ALECSO's systems organise cooperation and planning and do not impose penalties as the national laws of Algeria and Qatar do. This contrast is understood from the summary, and the museum does not ascribe it to any particular text, saying no more than that regional and international instruments run in a field other than that of national laws.
