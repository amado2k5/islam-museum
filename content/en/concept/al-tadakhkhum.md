## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat discusses inflation after his treatment of monopoly and the exploitation of needs, considering it one of the maladies of the economy caused by deviating from the natural line of production {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}.

## The Mechanism as He Describes It
The author argues that ever-increasing money must one day lead to inflation and stagnation: when people rely on jobs, salaries, and pensions, the productive process diminishes, and consequently foodstuffs and basic goods in the market decrease, causing their prices to rise sharply {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}. This then requires issuing paper currency to meet prices and imports, and replacing hoarded money with new money—which is what he calls financial or monetary inflation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}. Prior to inflation came rising prices and scarcity of goods, all of which are maladies caused by deviating from the natural line of production {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}.

## The Role of Hoarding
Hence, in the author's approach, the Qur'an encourages work and traveling through the land, and warns against wealth and hoarding it, because hoarding halts investment and production and reduces the turnover of working capital {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 86}. See [[concept/al-iktinaz-al-mal|Hoarding Wealth]].

## In Modern Economics
The author notes elsewhere that rising prices are one of the harmful effects of hoarding, and that they trouble the capitalist systems of his time {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 133}. This is a description by the author, not a critical analysis supported by data in the book.
