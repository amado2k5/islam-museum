## Summary
Ibn Sina says some signs show on the outside of the organs, taken either from the special sensibles such as colour and touch, or from the common sensibles such as the make, position and movement of the organs, and they may point to inner states {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.

## The special sensibles
The special sensibles are states of colour and of touch, in hardness and softness, heat and cold. He gives reading the stool as black, white or yellow, which is a matter of sight; the gurgling of the bowels for wind and poor digestion, a matter of hearing; and smells and tastes in the mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.

## The common sensibles
The common sensibles are those taken from the make of the organs, their position, movement and rest, and the amounts and numbers of what is passed. Examples are a twitching lip pointing to vomiting, short fingers to a small liver, and a curved nail to consumption; wasting is seen by the eye but belongs to the common sensibles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.

## Pointing to the inner
An outward sensible may point to something inner, as a flushed cheek points to pneumonia and a curved nail to an ulcer of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.
