## Summary
Ibn Sina says cold water of moderate quantity is the water most suited to the healthy, though it may harm the nerves and those with tumours of the viscera; it rouses the appetite and braces the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.137-138}.

## Hot and warm water
Hot water spoils digestion and puts out the food, does not quench thirst at once, may lead to dropsy and wasting, and shrivels the body. Warm water, if only tepid, brings on nausea; if hotter, sipped fasting it often washes the stomach and loosens the bowels, but taking much of it is bad and weakens the stomach's power. Very hot water may sometimes dissolve colic and break wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.138}.

## Salty and turbid water
Salty water makes thin and dries and loosens, first through its cleansing, then in the end constipates through its drying; it spoils the blood and so breeds itch and scab. Turbid water breeds stones and obstructions, so something diuretic should be taken after it, though one with loose bowels often gains from it and from other coarse, heavy waters, because they are held in his belly and pass down slowly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.138}.
