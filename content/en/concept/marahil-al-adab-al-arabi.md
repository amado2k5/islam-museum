## Summary
The research summary supplied to the museum arranges Arabic literature in eight periods, from the Jahili, about 500 to 622 CE, to the modern, from the early twentieth century to today, and for each names an author and a work that represents it and a reference from Encyclopaedia Britannica and other sources. The periods overlap in time: the Andalusi, 711 to 1492, runs across the Abbasid and the Mamluk. The museum claims no clean division and gives the table as it came {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:britannica-islamic-arts-the-modern-period}.

## The Jahili period
From about 500 CE to 622, represented by [[person/imru-al-qays-ibn-hujr|Imru al-Qays]], who died about 500 or 540, and his Mu'allaqa, the first of the seven Mu'allaqat, which are treated in the page [[concept/al-muallaqat|The Mu'allaqat]] {cite:britannica-imru-al-qays} {cite:britannica-al-muallaqat}.

## The early Islamic period
From 622 to 661. It is represented by [[person/kab-ibn-zuhayr|Ka'b ibn Zuhayr]] of the seventh century and his poem Banat Su'ad, which the summary calls the Qasidat al-Burda, alongside the praise poems of [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan ibn Thabit]] on the Prophet {cite:britannica-early-islamic-literature} {cite:wikipedia-kab-ibn-zuhayr-en}.

## The Umayyad period
From 661 to 750. It is represented by [[person/jarir-ibn-atiya|Jarir]] and [[person/farazdaq|al-Farazdaq]] of the early eighth century with the naqa'id, the flytings, and by [[person/abd-al-hamid-ibn-yahya|Abd al-Hamid al-Katib]] of the eighth century with his secretarial epistles {cite:britannica-arabic-literature-genres-and-themes} {cite:britannica-arabic-literature-belles-lettres}.

## The Abbasid period
From 750 to 1258. It is represented by [[person/al-jahiz|al-Jahiz]], who died about 868 or 869, with the [[work/kitab-al-hayawan|Kitab al-Hayawan]] and the Kitab al-Bukhala, by [[person/abu-nuwas|Abu Nuwas]], who died about 813 or 815, with his Diwan, and by [[person/al-hariri|al-Hariri]], who died in 1122, with the Maqamat {cite:britannica-al-jahiz} {cite:britannica-kitab-al-hayawan} {cite:britannica-islamic-arts-the-abbasids}.

## The Andalusi period
From 711 to 1492. It is represented by [[person/ibn-hazm|Ibn Hazm]], 994 to 1064, with [[work/tawq-al-hamama|The Ring of the Dove]], and by [[person/ibn-tufayl|Ibn Tufayl]], who died in 1185, with [[work/hayy-ibn-yaqzan|Hayy ibn Yaqzan]] {cite:britannica-ring-of-the-dove} {cite:wikipedia-arabic-literature-en}.

## The Mamluk and Ottoman periods
The Mamluk, 1250 to 1517: [[person/al-busiri|al-Busiri]], about 1212 to 1295, with the [[work/qasidat-al-burda|Burda]]; [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]], 1332 to 1406, with the [[work/al-muqaddima|Muqaddima]]; and Ibn Daniyal of the thirteenth century with Tayf al-Khayal. The Ottoman, 1517 to about 1800: [[person/abd-al-ghani-al-nabulsi|Abd al-Ghani al-Nabulsi]], 1641 to 1731, with al-Hadra al-Unsiyya fi al-Rihla al-Qudsiyya and Ta'tir al-Anam {cite:britannica-al-busiri} {cite:britannica-the-muqaddimah} {cite:britannica-arabic-literature-the-short-story} {cite:wikipedia-abd-al-ghani-al-nabulsi-en}.

## The Nahda and the modern period
The Nahda, from about 1800 to the early twentieth century, often dated 1798 to 1939: [[person/rifaa-al-tahtawi|Rifa'a Rafi al-Tahtawi]], 1801 to 1873, with Takhlis al-Ibriz fi Talkhis Bariz of 1834, and [[person/ahmed-shawqi|Ahmad Shawqi]], 1868 to 1932, with al-Shawqiyyat. The modern, from the early twentieth century to today: [[person/taha-hussein|Taha Hussein]], 1889 to 1973, with al-Ayyam, 1929 to 1967, and [[person/naguib-mahfouz|Naguib Mahfouz]], 1911 to 2006, with the [[work/al-thulathiyya|Cairo Trilogy]], 1956 to 1957. The Nahda and the reform of the language have a page, [[concept/islah-al-lugha-fi-al-nahda|Language reform in the Nahda]], and Mahfouz has [[concept/najib-mahfuz-wa-nobel-1988|Naguib Mahfouz and the 1988 Nobel Prize]] {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:cambridge-companion-modern-arabic-literature-when} {cite:wikipedia-nahda-en} {cite:britannica-taha-hussein} {cite:britannica-naguib-mahfouz} {cite:britannica-islamic-arts-the-modern-period}.

## A reading of the table
The first three periods lean to poetry: the Mu'allaqa of Imru al-Qays, then Banat Su'ad and the praise poems of Hassan, then the flytings of Jarir and al-Farazdaq, with in the Umayyad period the secretarial epistles of Abd al-Hamid al-Katib beside them. In the Abbasid period the art widens to literary prose, with al-Jahiz's Hayawan and Bukhala, to the poetic diwan in Abu Nuwas, and to the maqamat in al-Hariri. In al-Andalus come two prose works, Ibn Hazm's Ring of the Dove and Ibn Tufayl's Hayy ibn Yaqzan. In the Mamluk period come the Burda and the Muqaddima, and in the Ottoman the works of al-Nabulusi. Then the Nahda with al-Tahtawi and Shawqi, and the modern with two writers of the twentieth century. This reading is the page's arrangement of the summary's titles, not the summary's own judgement {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:britannica-islamic-arts-the-modern-period}.

## The titles as the summary glosses them
The summary renders al-Naqa'id as Flytings, Kitab al-Hayawan as Book of Animals, Kitab al-Bukhala as Book of Misers, Tawq al-Hamama as The Ring of the Dove, Qasidat al-Burda as The Poem of the Scarf, al-Ayyam as The Days and al-Thulathiyya as The Cairo Trilogy. These are English renderings the summary gives after each title; they are given here for clarity only and do not replace the titles of the works {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:britannica-islamic-arts-the-modern-period}.

## Basis
The page rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the museum did not open the pages it cites, and the founder approved relying on it as it stands on 21 November 2026. Every name, date and title here is attributed to the summary, the dates are approximate as given, and the museum has read none of the works. The page adds no literary judgement and no ranking of the periods.
