## Nature and selection
A fruit shaped like a date stone, with a kernel like the walnut's, sweet and harmless, and a porous shell whose holes contain a sticky, strong-smelling juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Some people nibble it without harm, especially with walnut {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.

## Temperament and actions
Dry at the end of the fourth degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Its juice causes ulcers and swellings and burns the blood and humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it cuts off warts, removes white patches, takes off tattoos, and cures the phlegmatic alopecia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Swellings and pustules: it stirs up hot swellings within the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Joints: it helps cold and relaxed nerves and paralysis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Head: it helps impaired male potency when its electuary known as andaqarya is taken, but it stirs up obsessive thoughts and melancholy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Excretory organs: it is smoked on piles to dry them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Poisons: the book counts it among the poisons and says it kills. Its antidote is buttermilk and walnut oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.

## Substitutes
In all ailments the substitutes named are hazelnut, balsam oil, and white naphtha. The proportions are not given here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.389}.
