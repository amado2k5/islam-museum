## Summary
'Awar is the loss of sight in one eye; the jurists use the word in its plain sense {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11908}. It is distinguished from 'asha, night blindness, from 'amash, weak sight with a watering eye, from hawal, squint, and from 'ama, blindness of both eyes {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11909}. A one-eyed animal is not valid for sacrifice where the eye is gone, though the Hanafis allow it where the eye is only white and still sees {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11909} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11910}. Defect of one eye gives no option to annul the marriage unless one spouse stipulated soundness {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11910}. A one-eyed slave may be freed in expiation, by the majority {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11911}. For injury to a sound-eyed person by a one-eyed man, the rulings differ: the Hanafis and Shafi'is require a full blood-money, the Hanbalis require half, and the Malikis give the victim a choice {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11911} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11912}. The Hanafis and Shafi'is allow retaliation on the sound eye of a person who blinds a one-eyed man's only sound eye, while the Malikis give the choice between retaliation and blood-money {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11912}.
