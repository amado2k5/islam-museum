## Summary
In the sixth chapter Ibn Sina divides fullness into fullness in respect of the vessels and fullness in respect of the faculty, and gives the danger of each and the remedy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.

## In respect of the vessels
Here the humours and spirits grow in quantity, though not in quality, until they fill and stretch the vessels. The person is in danger when he moves, since the fullness may split the vessels and flow to the throat passages, producing choking, seizure and apoplexy; the treatment is to hasten to venesection {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.

## In respect of the faculty
Here the harm comes from the poor quality of the humours and not from their quantity alone; they overcome the faculty and do not yield to digestion and concoction, and the person is in danger of putrefactive diseases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.164}.
