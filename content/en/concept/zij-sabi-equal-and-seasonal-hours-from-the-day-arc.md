## Summary
Al-Battani teaches that the equal hours of the day and night come from dividing the day arc or the night arc by fifteen, and that the seasonal hours, which are always twelve for the day and as many for the night, come from dividing the arc by twelve; the time of one arc's hour taken from thirty leaves the time of the other's hour. He gives a third way by a sixth of the excess of the difference of the day, and a way by the table. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.46-47}.

## Equal hours
He says: if you want to know the number of the equal hours of the day and of the night, divide the day arc or the night arc by fifteen; the result is the hours of whichever you computed it for. Once you know the hours of one of them, subtract them from twenty-four and the remainder is the hours of the other. An equal hour is thus fifteen parts of the equator's sphere, and a day with its night is twenty-four hours. See [[concept/equal-hours|equal hours]] for how zij makers count the day from midday. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.46}.

## Seasonal hours, and the hour's time by table
He says: if you want the times of the seasonal hours of day and night, which are always twelve hours for the day and as many for the night, and are called the crooked hours, divide the arc of whichever you like, day or night, by twelve; the result is the times of its hours. Subtract the hour-times of whichever you computed for from thirty and the hour-times of the other remain, because thirty is the time of two equal hours, so what an hour of night or day loses the other gains. Another way: take a sixth of the excess of the difference of the day; if the Sun is in the northern half add the sixth to fifteen, if in the southern subtract it from fifteen. By the table: enter the Sun's degree in the risings table of the clime in the common-number row and take what stands opposite among the hour times in the table of that sign; for the night take the Sun's opposite degree. The name 'crooked hours' is read from a damaged word in the scan. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.46-47}.
