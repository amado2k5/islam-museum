## Summary
Hajj, in the sacred sense, is the purposeful journey to the Sacred House and to Arafat in the months of hajj to perform set acts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836} It is an individual obligation on every adult who is able, once in a lifetime, established by the Qur'an, the Sunna and consensus. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6837} On whether it must be performed at once or may be delayed, Abu Hanifa (in the sounder of his two reports), Abu Yusuf, Malik (in the preferred view) and Ahmad hold that it is immediate, while al-Shafi'i and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan hold that it may be delayed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6837} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6838} The texts praise the merit of the hajj at length, including the report that a hajj accepted has no reward but paradise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6838} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6839} Five conditions of obligation are agreed: Islam, reason, puberty, freedom and capacity. The hajj of a madman is not valid; a child's hajj counts as a voluntary one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6840} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} General capacity has four elements: provision and a mount, bodily health, safety of the road, and the possibility of travel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} The majority require provision and a mount, while the Malikis accept the ability to walk with provision for a person in good health. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6842} The majority also require that the provision and mount be owned beyond the basic needs, such as maintenance of dependants, housing and household goods. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6842} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6843} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6844}


## Conditions of obligation and capacity
Capacity is the condition of obligation. The general elements are provision and a mount, bodily health, safety of the road, and the possibility of travel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} The majority require provision and a mount; the Malikis accept the ability to walk with provision for a person in good health. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6842} The provision and mount must be owned beyond basic needs such as the upkeep of dependants, housing and household goods. A large house surplus to need must be sold, according to the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, but not according to the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6843} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6844} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6845} A woman must also have a husband or a mahram, or a trusted company of women in the view of some schools, and a woman in the waiting period after divorce or widowhood may not set out. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6848} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6849} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6850} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6851} Bodily health is a condition of obligation for Abu Hanifa and Malik, and a condition only of performing the hajj in person for the Shafi'is, Hanbalis and the two companions of Abu Hanifa; the disabled person sends a deputy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6846} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6847} Safety of the road is a condition of obligation for the Malikis and Shafi'is, and of performing it in person in the sounder Hanafi view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6847} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6848} Possibility of travel is a condition of obligation for the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6848} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6849}

## Conditions of validity, times, places and the forms of hajj
Eight conditions make hajj count for the obligation, including remaining a Muslim until death, reason, freedom, puberty, and performing it oneself where able. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6854} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6855} The hajj has a fixed time, the months of shawwal, dhu al-qa'da and the first ten days of dhu al-hijja, and a fixed place for its rites: Arafat for standing, the House for circumambulation, and the space between Safa and Marwa for the running. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6853} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6854} The three forms, ifrad, qiran and tamattu', are all lawful by consensus, and qiran and tamattu' require an offering. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6856} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6857} For preference, the Malikis and Shafi'is favour ifrad, the Hanafis qiran, and the Hanbalis tamattu'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6857} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6858}

## Pillars and rites
The pillars are four for the majority: the state of ihram, standing at Arafat, the visit circumambulation, and the running; the Hanafis count two, standing and the visit circumambulation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6862} Standing at Arafat is a pillar whose loss voids the hajj, beginning at the decline of the sun on the day of Arafat and lasting to dawn of the feast; the Malikis hold that the night is its time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6862} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6863} The visit circumambulation may be done from dawn of the feast by the Hanafis and Malikis, and from midnight of that night by the Shafi'is and Hanbalis; a delayed visit circumambulation is not dropped. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6864} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6865} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6866} The running is seven rounds between Safa and Marwa and is a pillar for the three Imams, but a duty for the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6866} Staying at Muzdalifa is a duty for the majority and a sunna for the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6859} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6860} On the feast day the pilgrim casts seven stones at the Aqaba pillar, sacrifices, shaves or trims, performs the visit circumambulation, and on the days of tashriq casts the three pillars in order before leaving Mina. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6860} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6861} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6868} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6869} Duties are made good by a sacrifice, and a duty left out for a lawful excuse carries no sin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6866} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6867}


## Hair-cutting, the nights at Mina, and the farewell circumambulation
Shaving or trimming the hair is a duty for the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis, and a pillar in the best-known Shafi'i view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6870} Spending the nights of the days of tashriq at Mina is a duty for the majority, whose omission requires a sacrifice; the Hanafis hold it a sunna. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6870} The farewell circumambulation is a duty for the Hanafis, Hanbalis and, in the sounder view, the Shafi'is, and a sunna for the Malikis; it is not required of the lesser pilgrim for the Hanafis, nor of the people of Mecca in the Hanafi and Hanbali view, while the Malikis and Shafi'is require it of a Meccan who leaves on a journey that shortens prayer. A menstruating or post-partum woman does not perform it, and owes nothing for leaving it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6870} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6871} The majority require purity from minor and major impurity and the sequence after the visit circumambulation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6871} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6872}

## Dependent duties and the order of the day of sacrifice
Dependent duties are performed within a pillar or a duty of the hajj. The majority set conditions for validity of the circumambulation: purity, covered 'awra, starting at the Black Stone, and keeping the House on the right; the Hanafis add walking for the able and the two rak'as. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6872} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6873} The order on the day of sacrifice is: the Aqaba pillar, then the sacrifice for the qarin and mutamatti', then shaving, then the visit circumambulation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6873} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6874} Whether the order is obligatory is disputed: the Hanafis, Malikis and a view of Ahmad require it, while al-Shafi'i and the two companions of Abu Hanifa treat it as a sunna; in either case the rite is valid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6874} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6875}

## Sunan
The sunan are desired and rewarded, and omitting them carries no sacrifice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6875} They include the arrival circumambulation, a sunna for the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis and a duty for the Malikis when the pilgrim entered the state of ihram outside the sacred zone. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6875} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6876} It falls away for people of Mecca and those in the same position. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6876}
