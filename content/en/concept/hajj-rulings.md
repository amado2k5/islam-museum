## Summary
Hajj, in the sacred sense, is the purposeful journey to the Sacred House and to Arafat in the months of hajj to perform set acts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836} It is an individual obligation on every adult who is able, once in a lifetime, established by the Qur'an, the Sunna and consensus. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6837} On whether it must be performed at once or may be delayed, Abu Hanifa (in the sounder of his two reports), Abu Yusuf, Malik (in the preferred view) and Ahmad hold that it is immediate, while al-Shafi'i and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan hold that it may be delayed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6837} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6838} The texts praise the merit of the hajj at length, including the report that a hajj accepted has no reward but paradise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6838} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6839} Five conditions of obligation are agreed: Islam, reason, puberty, freedom and capacity. The hajj of a madman is not valid; a child's hajj counts as a voluntary one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6840} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} General capacity has four elements: provision and a mount, bodily health, safety of the road, and the possibility of travel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} The majority require provision and a mount, while the Malikis accept the ability to walk with provision for a person in good health. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6841} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6842} The majority also require that the provision and mount be owned beyond the basic needs, such as maintenance of dependants, housing and household goods. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6842} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6843} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6844}


## Arrival circumambulation, sermons, the days of Mina, and the road to Arafat and Muzdalifa
The arrival circumambulation falls away for people of Mecca and those in the same position; the Malikis require it of one who entered ihram outside the sacred zone, unless he was a youth close to puberty (muraahiq), and owe a sacrifice for omitting it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6877} Its time runs from entering Mecca until standing at Arafat for the majority; it is performed like the visit circumambulation, without the exposed shoulder and the quick step unless running is brought forward. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6878} The sermons: one at Mecca on the seventh of dhu al-hijja to teach the rites, held by the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Malikis; two on the day of Arafat before the prayer; a third at Mina on the eleventh for the Hanafis and Malikis, or on the day of sacrifice for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis add a fourth on the second day of tashriq. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6878} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6879} It is recommended to leave for Mina on the day of tarwiya after sunrise and to pray there the five prayers, a sunna for all the Imams. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6879} Going out to Arafat in the morning after sunrise is a sunna for the majority and recommended for the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6880} Spending the night at Muzdalifa on the night of the feast and remaining there until full daylight is a sunna for the Hanafis and Shafi'is, recommended for the Malikis and desirable for the Hanbalis; the duty there is standing, not the night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6880} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6881}



## Other sunan and prohibitions
Among the sunan is the sacrifice of a voluntary offering; the Prophet slaughtered a hundred camels as a voluntary offering on his pilgrimage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6881} Ghusl is desirable on entering Mecca for one who came from outside, and at Muzdalifa after midnight for the Hanafis and Shafi'is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6881} It is also desirable to bring the visit circumambulation forward to the day of sacrifice, and to repeat prayers, supplication and remembrance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6881} Halting at the Muhassab valley on the way back from Mina is recommended for the majority and a sunna for the Hanafis, but not required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6882} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6883} Prohibitions are of three kinds: disliked acts such as leaving a sunna; forbidden acts such as leaving a duty, which is sinful and carries a redemption; and invalidating acts, which concern the state of ihram in general. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6883}

## The menstruating and post-partum woman
A woman has special rules. If she enters ihram and then menstruates before circumambulating, she waits until she has stood at Arafat and done the other rites, then performs the circumambulation and the running after she is clean; the farewell circumambulation is not dropped. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6883} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6884} If she menstruates during the umra before standing at Arafat, the Hanafis hold that she enters the hajj and abandons the umra; others say she becomes a qarin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6884} If she menstruates after standing and the visit circumambulation and leaves Mecca before she is clean, the farewell circumambulation falls away. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6884}

## Hajj on behalf of another
Hajj on behalf of another is lawful according to the majority (Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis), on the basis of the reports of the Khath'ami and Juhayni women. Malik holds that the hajj is not transferable, and that a guardian should rather give alms or make a supplication for the dead. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6884} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6885} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6886} The Shafi'is and Hanbalis require the hajj of a dead person to be done from his estate even without a bequest; the Hanafis and Malikis require a bequest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6887} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6888} The deputy must intend the hajj for the principal, perform it in person, begin at the principal's station, and if he acts against the instruction, the hajj does not count for the principal in Abu Hanifa's view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6888} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6889} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6890} Taking a fee for hajj is lawful for the Shafi'is and Malikis and void for Abu Hanifa, Ishaq and most Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6890} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6891}



## Etiquette of the hajj
The pilgrim should learn the rules of the hajj and how it is performed, carry a clear manual of the rites, seek God's guidance for the year, repent, repay what he owes, and write his will. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6894} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6895} It is recommended to please one's parents: if a parent forbids the obligatory hajj the prohibition is disregarded; for a voluntary hajj the Shafi'is hold in the sounder view that the parent may release him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6895} The pilgrim should take care that his money is lawful. A hajj with unlawful money is valid outwardly, but is not an accepted hajj in the majority view; one report from Ahmad holds that it does not suffice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6895} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6896} On the road he should pray, remember God and keep good company; a sign that the hajj was accepted is that he is better after his return than before. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6896} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6897} The article ends at the next headword, hajr (interdiction), which starts on the same page. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6897}
