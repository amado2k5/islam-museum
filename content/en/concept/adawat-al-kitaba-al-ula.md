## Summary
When Zayd ibn Thabit said he sought the Qur'an out 'from palm stems, thin stones and the chests of men', he named writing materials that were in use in Medina, which the philologists and hadith scholars explained in their commentaries on the wording of hadith. Al-Suyuti gathers the words that recur in the reports of Abu Bakr's collection and explains them. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 379} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 385}

## The words and their meanings
**'Usub:** plural of 'asib, the palm stem; they used to strip the fronds and write on the broad end. **Likhaf:** with a kasra on the lam and a light khaa' ending in fa', plural of likhfa, thin stones; al-Khattabi said flat slabs of stone. **Riqa':** plural of ruq'a, which may be of leather, parchment or paper. **Aktaf:** plural of katif, the shoulder-bone of a camel or sheep; once dry they wrote on it. **Aqtab:** plural of qatab, the wood placed on a camel's back for riding. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 385} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 386} Al-Suyuti notes that reports add pieces of hide and ribs, and that in Ibn Wahb's Muwatta from Salim ibn Abdallah ibn Umar, Abu Bakr 'gathered the Qur'an in papers'. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 386}

## Suhuf and mushaf
Khalid al-Sabt distinguishes suhuf from mushaf: suhuf is plural of sahifa, a piece of paper or hide written on, while mushaf has the form of a passive participle of 'ashafahu', to gather the suhuf in it, so the covers, the two sides or skins that hold its leaves together, are felt in the word. As a technical term suhuf are the loose leaves in which the Qur'an was gathered under Abu Bakr, with the verses of each sura in order, each sura on its own; the mushaf is the leaves in which the Qur'an is gathered with the order of both its verses and suras as the community agreed in Uthman's time. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 323} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 324}

## What became of what was written
Abu Bakr gathered what was written on these materials in leaves that stayed with him until he died, then with Umar, then with Hafsa. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 379} Al-Suyuti quotes Ibn Hajar preferring that what was in hide and palm stems was before the collection, then was collected into leaves under Abu Bakr, as the many sound reports show. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 387} Reports say Abu Bakr was the first to gather the Qur'an in leaves, and that the very word 'mushaf' was chosen when they agreed on a name: they suggested 'sifr', disliked it because it is a name the Jews use, and Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, said he had seen the like in Abyssinia called 'mushaf', so they agreed on it; al-Suyuti calls the chain broken and takes it as meaning he was one of the collectors by Abu Bakr's order. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 382}

## A note
This sets out what the commentaries on the wording say; I do not claim that the leaves Abu Bakr collected were all of one material, since the reports vary, as you have seen, and I leave aside the details of the material of the leaves themselves (papers, leaves, hide), which are reports by meaning.
