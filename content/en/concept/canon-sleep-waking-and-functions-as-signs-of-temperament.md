## Summary
The seventh genus is the state of sleep and waking, the eighth the signs of functions; the function-sign is surest for a balanced temperament, while its pointing to heat, cold, dryness or moisture is only conjecture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.162}.

## Sleep and waking
Balance of sleep and waking shows balance of temperament, above all in the brain; excess of sleep comes from moisture and cold, excess of waking from dryness and heat, especially in the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.162}.

## Signs from functions
Functions that keep to their natural course, complete and whole, show a balanced temperament. If they turn into excessive movements they show heat, and likewise if they are quick, such as quick growth, quick growth of hair and quick teething; if they are dull, weak and slow they show a cold temperament. Weakness may also come from a hot temperament, but then with a departure from the natural course {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.162}.

## Sleep is not an absolute need
Ibn Sina notes that sleep is not absolutely needed for life but arises because the spirit is freed from its occupations by weariness or by the need to turn to digesting food; it is needed only from a kind of incapacity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.162}.

## Strong functions that show heat
Among strong functions showing heat are strength and loudness of voice, fast and continuous speech, anger and quick movements and glances, though these may come from a cause special to the organ of the act and not a general one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.162}.
