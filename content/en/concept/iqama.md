## Definition
In the law, iqama is the call that tells those present and ready for prayer to stand, in set words. The word also means staying in one place, the opposite of travel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3144}

## Wording and repetition
Its words follow the adhan, with qad qamati al-salat added after hayya ala al-falah. The Hanafis differ from the other three schools by repeating most phrases. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3146}

## Ruling
The Hanbalis hold it a communal obligation; the Malikis, the preferred Shafi'i view and the more correct Hanafi view hold it a confirmed sunna. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3144} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3146}

## Conditions and manner
It is recited standing, not before the prayer time, and quickly; singing it is disliked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3147} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3149} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3151}

## When it is called
It is called for the five daily prayers, Friday, missed prayers and the solo worshipper, but not for funeral, witr, voluntary or festival prayers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3152} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3153}

## Interval with the adhan
A pause, by prayer, sitting or a short time, is recommended between adhan and iqama, except at maghrib when the iqama is hastened. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3154}

## Payment
The jurists agree that no one may be hired for the adhan and iqama when a qualified volunteer is available; they differ otherwise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3155}
