## Summary
Khushu\' is lexically stillness and humility; one who is humble in prayer or supplication turns his heart wholly to it, from the phrase the earth is still when it settles. Khushu\' is more often in the limbs, and dara\' (submission) more often in the heart; takhshu\' is forced humility, and Qatada called it fear and lowered gaze in prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7640} Khudu\' is lowliness, found in the body; ikhbat is steady obedience and stillness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7641} Most jurists hold that khushu\' in prayer is a sunna, not a pillar, since someone thinking of worldly matters still has a valid prayer while he keeps its acts. The worshipper should be humble in heart and limbs: attend to nothing but the prayer, not fidget with his body or clothes, and recall that he stands before the King who knows the secret and the most hidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7641}
