## Summary
Khushu\' is lexically stillness and humility; one who is humble in prayer or supplication turns his heart wholly to it, from the phrase the earth is still when it settles. Khushu\' is more often in the limbs, and dara\' (submission) more often in the heart; takhshu\' is forced humility, and Qatada called it fear and lowered gaze in prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7640} Khudu\' is lowliness, found in the body; ikhbat is steady obedience and stillness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7641} Most jurists hold that khushu\' in prayer is a sunna, not a pillar, since someone thinking of worldly matters still has a valid prayer while he keeps its acts. The worshipper should be humble in heart and limbs: attend to nothing but the prayer, not fidget with his body or clothes, and recall that he stands before the King who knows the secret and the most hidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7641} The basis for seeking humility in prayer is the verse "the believers prosper, those who are humble in their prayer"; Ali explained it as a softened heart and restrained limbs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7642} The worshipper should ponder the recitation and empty his heart of distraction and not follow the flow of his thoughts, since presence of heart is being free from anything but what one is engaged in. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7642} Most jurists hold that prayer is valid without humility, since it is not voided by the heart's act, though it is disliked and earns no reward; some schools made it a pillar that is excused, a pillar whose lack voids the prayer, or a condition for part of it, which some fix at the opening takbir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7642}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7643} Blameworthy humility is the feigned kind before people, with bowed head and weeping, a deception of Satan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7643}
