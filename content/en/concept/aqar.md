## Summary
In the lexicon, aqar is anything with a fixed base and a settled place, such as land, a house, an estate or palms; as a term it is what cannot be moved from one place to another {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11693}.

## Immovables and movables
Movables are what can be moved from place to place; the Malikis require that they can be moved without losing their form {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11694}.

## Effects of the distinction
Pre-emption applies to real estate and, by the majority, not to movables sold on their own; easements attach to real estate; and the rules for selling a minor's or a bankrupt's property differ by kind {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11694} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11695}.

## Prayer on usurped land
The majority hold that prayer on usurped land is valid though sinful; the preferred Hanbali view holds it invalid {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11696} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11697}.

## Zakat
No zakat is due on needs such as a home. By the majority there is none on rented property; some Hanbalis, including Ibn Aqil, hold that it is due {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11698} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11699}.

## Sale, mortgage and endowment
An owner may sell real estate in full or a shared part, subject to conditions; everything that may be sold may be mortgaged; and endowment of real estate is valid by agreement {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11698} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11703} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11704}.

## Where to look further
Pre-emption is treated under «shufa», endowment under «waqf», and usurpation under «ghasb» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11703}.
