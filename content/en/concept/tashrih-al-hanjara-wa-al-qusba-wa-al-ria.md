## The anatomy of the larynx, the windpipe and the lung
A nervous membrane covers the lung, and the lung itself by its softness is the heart's bedding and its guard. The chest is divided into two cavities, parted by a membrane arising alongside the middle of the breastbone — in truth two membranes — joined behind to the vertebra and above to the meeting of the two collarbones; the purpose of the two is that the chest have two bellies, so that if harm befalls one the other completes the acts and ends of breathing, and among their benefits is the binding of the oesophagus, the lung and the chest's organs one to another. The diaphragm is in truth one of the muscles, of three layers: the middle one is the true sinew by which its act is done, the upper is as a foundation for the chest-membranes lining it, and the lower likewise for the membranes of the peritoneum; and in the diaphragm are two holes — the larger the passage of the oesophagus and the great artery, the smaller admitting the vein called the azygos, strongly attached and welded to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.303}
