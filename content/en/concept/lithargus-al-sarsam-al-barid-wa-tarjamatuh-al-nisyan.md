## Nature
Lithargus is said of the phlegmatic swelling inside the skull, the phlegmatic sarsam, and most of it is in the channels of the substance of the brain, not in the membranes, the ventricles and the body of the brain, because phlegm seldom gathers and passes into the membranes, for their hardness, nor into the substance of the brain, for its viscosity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84} He notes that dhat al-janb too is mostly bilious and seldom phlegmatic, from the little passage of phlegm into a hard, nervous, membranous substance, though the lesser case of either is possible, so that this swelling may fall in the substance of the brain and in its membranes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84}

## The name
This illness is named for its sign, for the rendering of lithargus is forgetfulness, and forgetfulness goes with this illness; from its name many physicians went wrong about it, not knowing that what is meant is the disease from a cold swelling, and thinking this illness is forgetfulness itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84} Some physicians call lithargus every cold swelling in the brain, whether black-bilious or phlegmatic, though most of the ancients keep the name for the phlegmatic, and you may name both by it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84}

## Causes
The matter of this illness is near to the matter of dizziness (sudar), but firmer set; it is bred by whatever breeds a phlegmatic humour that has vaporising in it, and so is often bred by eating onions, and by much indigestion, much drinking and much eating of fruit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84}
