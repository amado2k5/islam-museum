## Definition
Infirad is lexically the reflexive of tafarrud, to stand alone, and the jurists use it in that sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3515}

## Its applications in fiqh
- One guardian acting alone in a marriage: where equal guardians dispute who shall act, the Shafi'is and Hanbalis draw lots, the Malikis leave it to the judge to choose the best judgement, and the Hanafis allow each to marry her. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3515}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3516}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3517}
- One guardian acting alone over a minor's property: the Malikis hold that an uncle or senior brother who acts for the child's benefit is valid, since custom treats him as the father. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3517}
- One of two agents acting alone: allowed when the principal gives each the power; the Hanafis allow it in matters needing no joint judgement, such as litigation, and not in sale, purchase or marriage without both. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3518}
- One of two partners acting alone: in co-ownership it needs consent or a rotation of use; in a partnership of trade on general terms it is allowed by agreement of the jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3519}
- One of two executors or supervisors acting alone: the majority hold that where a testator names two together, neither may act alone unless the testator allowed it; Abu Yusuf allows it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3519}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3520}
- A wife's separate dwelling: she has a right to a dwelling with its own door and facilities, by the majority; the Malikis do not require one where she agreed to live with her husband's relatives or a co-wife. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3520}
