## Nature and selection
A tree like the ḥilāf tree; the people of Syria call it dardar, those of Iraq the bug tree; swollen cones like pomegranates hold a moisture that turns into bugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.439}. The editor's footnote gives the name and the bug tree; that is the editor's identification {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.439}.

## Actions
Astringent and polishing; the bark is astringent and the root is near it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.439}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: the moisture of its cones polishes the face; its bark with vinegar, when still moist, polishes the sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.439}.
Wounds: its bark wrapped as a bandage on blows and wounds heals them; its leaf, bark and flower also suit wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.439}.
Joints: a decoction of its root and leaf is applied to broken bones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.440}.
Excretory organs: its thick bark, drunk as a decoction or in cold water, expels phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.440}.
