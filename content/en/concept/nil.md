## Nature and selection
Some is garden and some is wild, and its action is the action of the garden kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Temperament
Hot in the first degree, dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Actions and properties
Astringent, stopping bleeding; the garden kind dries strongly without sting, the wild has sharpness and dries more and draws matters from the depth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Cosmetic uses
It cleanses freckles and vitiligo and helps alopecia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Swellings and pustules
It shrinks the flaccid swelling and helps bad wounds in the hard organs; in general it helps every swelling at its onset and the creeping ulcer and erysipelas, being used on them with barley flour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.575}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It heals hot wounds in hard bodies through the strength of its drying, and this is the fruit of the garden kind. The wild has sharpness and is good for putrid ulcers, wondrous in its action on them; the garden kind is better in treating ulcers for its lesser sharpness, and helps old ulcers with honey, powdered on fire burns and wounds of the nerve, and draws out thorns especially with rye flour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Chest and breathing
Helpful for the severe cough of children that makes them vomit, and so is its juice, and for ulcers of the lung; it helps the melancholic pleurisy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It helps the spleen, especially the wild kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.
