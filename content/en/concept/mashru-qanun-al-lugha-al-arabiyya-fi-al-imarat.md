## Summary
This page presents the draft Arabic language law of the Emirates as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it. It is a draft and not a law: the summary says no final enacted text was located as of late 2026. The museum did not open the pages cited, so what is here is ascribed to the summary, and no provision of it is in force.
## Year and body
The year, by the summary, is a draft Arabic Language Law under development as of 2026, targeted for introduction around 2027. The body is the UAE Ministry of Culture, with discussion in the Federal National Council, as part of a broader National Identity Strategy {cite:khaleej-times-home} {cite:the-national-home}.
## What the draft aims at
According to the summary the draft aims to protect and promote Arabic across government transactions, economy and business, education and research, culture and media, translation and Arabisation, technology, customer service and related areas. Among its proposals are requirements for Arabic-speaking staff in certain private-sector customer-facing roles, stronger Arabic teaching standards, and Arabic use and translation in public advertising and events.
## What was said in the press
The summary says the discussions were reported through Ministry of Culture presentations to the Federal National Council and in coverage by official-aligned outlets such as Khaleej Times and The National, and that earlier related efforts included recommendations for a federal protection law {cite:khaleej-times-home} {cite:the-national-home}. These are media sources, so the draft's content is as the press carried it, not as issued in a text.
## What was not found
No final enacted text was located as of late 2026. This entry must therefore not be read as a presentation of a law in force, and no fines or obligations are to be ascribed to the Emirates that have not been established. If the law is issued later it will receive a record with its official text.
## Place in the record of Arabic
It stands against [[concept/qanun-himayat-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-fi-qatar-2019|Qatar's law]], in force since 2019, and is read with [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-united-arab-emirates|Arabic in the Emirates]], [[place/united-arab-emirates|the United Arab Emirates]] and [[language/arabic|Arabic]].

## How to read this page
This page records what was announced about a draft at the time the summary was prepared, a time that may change. The summary gives no article number, no wording of any obligation, no timetable for application and no executing body. All the proposals above are headlines as they came in media coverage, and may change in the final version or be dropped. The record will therefore be updated if the law is issued, and stays here as news of a draft. The summary notes that earlier efforts included recommendations for a federal protection law, without detailing them.
## A draft between strategy and legislation
The summary made the draft part of the National Identity Strategy, that is the product of a wider policy before it is a legislative text. That is not enough to foresee its wording, since many draft laws change in discussion, and the summary tells of the stages of discussion only that it was presented to the Federal National Council. This entry therefore stays open to what comes, and is read as a record of the matter's state when the summary was prepared.
