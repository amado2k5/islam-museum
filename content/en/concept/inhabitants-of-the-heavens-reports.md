## Overview

This page gathers prophetic reports and sayings about the angels who fill the sky, about its building and distances, about the Kursi and the Throne, about the Bayt al-Ma'mur, and about the ascent through the seven heavens. The reports differ in rank, so each is given with the grade of those who judged it. The gradings attributed to a named scholar here are those an open hadith database shows beside each hadith; the scholars' own books were not consulted, so they should be rechecked before being relied on.

## The groaning of the sky

Al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah relate from Abu Dharr that the Prophet said he sees what they do not and hears what they do not, that the sky groans and has a right to groan, for there is no space of four fingers in it but an angel is placing his forehead in prostration to God, and that if they knew what he knows they would laugh little and weep much {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 2312} {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 4190}. Al-Tirmidhi calls it hasan gharib and says it is also related by another route from Abu Dharr as his own saying {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 2312}. The database credits the grade hasan for the Tirmidhi version to Ahmad Shakir, al-Albani, Bashar Awwad Ma'ruf and Zubair Ali Za'i, and for the Ibn Majah version to al-Albani, Muhammad Fu'ad Abd al-Baqi and Zubair Ali Za'i, with Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut grading it hasan li-ghayrihi {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0}.

## What lies between the heavens

Abu Dawud relates from al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib that the Prophet asked the company about the distance between heaven and earth and answered that it is seventy-one, seventy-two or seventy-three years, and the heaven above it likewise, until he counted seven heavens; above the seventh is a sea whose depth equals the distance between one heaven and another, then eight mountain goats whose hoofs and haunches are likewise apart, on whose backs is the Throne, and God is above that {cite:sunan-abi-dawud 4723}. The database credits the grade daif to al-Albani, Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut and Zubair Ali Za'i {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0}.

Al-Tirmidhi relates from Abu Hurayra, by way of al-Hasan, a report saying the distance between earth and heaven is a journey of five hundred years, and likewise between each heaven and the next, that above the seventh is the Throne at the same distance, and that beneath the earth is another earth five hundred years below, to seven earths {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 3298}. Al-Tirmidhi comments that it is gharib by this route, that it is related from Ayyub, Yunus ibn Ubayd and Ali ibn Zayd that al-Hasan did not hear from Abu Hurayra, and that some scholars explained its saying that one lowered by a rope to the lowest earth would descend upon God as meaning descending upon God's knowledge, power and authority, which are everywhere while He is on the Throne as He described in His Book {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 3298}. The database credits daif to Ahmad Shakir, al-Albani and Zubair Ali Za'i, and hasan to Bashar Awwad Ma'ruf {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0}. The two reports give different distances: seventy-one to seventy-three years in Abu Dawud and five hundred years in al-Tirmidhi.

## The Kursi and the Throne

At the verse of the Throne Ibn Kathir collects several reports: from al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas that the seven heavens and seven earths, if spread and joined, would be in the Kursi's breadth only as a ring in a wilderness; from Ibn Zayd from his father that the seven heavens in the Kursi are like seven dirhams thrown into a shield; from Ibn Jarir, by Ibn Zayd, from Abu Dharr from the Prophet that the Kursi in the Throne is like an iron ring thrown between the backs of a desert; and from Ibn Mardawayh, by Abu Idris al-Khawlani, from Abu Dharr that the seven heavens and seven earths beside the Kursi are like a ring cast in a desert and the Throne's excellence over the Kursi is like the desert's over the ring {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 2:255}. Ibn Kathir gives no explicit grade for Abu Dharr's report there, though he judges another report in the chapter not sound, and the grade of Abu Dharr's report in Ibn Hibban, al-Bayhaqi or elsewhere was not verified here, so it is left ungraded. The Kursi has its own page, [[concept/kursi-allah|The Kursi]].

## The Bayt al-Ma'mur

Al-Bukhari relates from Malik ibn Sa'sa'a that the Prophet was shown the Bayt al-Ma'mur in the seventh heaven, asked Gabriel, and was told that seventy thousand angels pray in it each day and when they leave they do not return {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3207}. Muslim relates from Anas that he saw Ibrahim with his back against the Bayt al-Ma'mur, into which seventy thousand angels enter daily and do not return {cite:sahih-muslim 162}. The house has its own page, [[concept/bayt-al-mamur|The Bayt al-Ma'mur]].

## The ascent through the heavens

In Muslim's hadith from Anas the Prophet rode the Buraq to Bayt al-Maqdis and prayed two rak'as, then was taken up to the heaven. In the first he found Adam; in the second Isa and Yahya; in the third Yusuf; in the fourth Idris; in the fifth Harun; in the sixth Musa; in the seventh Ibrahim; then he was raised to the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary, and fifty prayers were enjoined {cite:sahih-muslim 162}. In al-Bukhari's report from Malik ibn Sa'sa'a, after the seventh heaven came the Lote Tree, from whose root four rivers flow, two apparent, the Nile and the Euphrates, and two hidden in Paradise {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3207}. See [[event/isra-and-miraj|The Night Journey and Ascension]], [[concept/sidrat-al-muntaha|The Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary]] and [[concept/al-samawat-al-sab|The seven heavens]].

## What the sources do not say

These reports do not identify the heavens' inhabitants beyond the angels and do not describe any other life in them. Some, like the distance reports, differ in figures and in grade, so they are not made a basis for an astronomical estimate. Each report stands at its own grade, and nothing is asked of one that belongs to another.
