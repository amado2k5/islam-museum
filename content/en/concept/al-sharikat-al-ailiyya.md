## Summary
In his Practical Administration lesson Tareq al-Suwaidan gives figures about family businesses: he says 95 per cent of companies in the Gulf region are family-owned (the latest statistic he cites), and that 68 per cent of family businesses worldwide collapse after the founder dies and his children take over (needs_source: both figures as the speaker gave them, unattributed) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:53:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:53:30}.

## The causes of failure in his account
He names two main causes: first, that the founder did not train his children enough to run the business, so they inherit an enterprise they do not understand {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:54:00}.
Second — this is his own claim, which we mark as contested — women's intervention in the money side: after the division of the inheritance a daughter holds a share and wants a say while — in his general characterisation, to which he admits exceptions — she has less financial experience than men, so she treats the company as a bank to draw from rather than a firm, and the cash flow is hit until the business collapses (needs_source) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:56:00}.
He adds that of the remaining 32 per cent, those that succeed do so because the father trained his children, or the women did not intervene, or they intervened while trained: a trained woman can run the business competently, and his rule applies to both sexes alike — whoever does not understand the business does not enter it, but either learns it or is content with his or her share {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:55:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:57:30}.
He adds that the cause of most companies' collapse is not the lack of assets but the lack of cash {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:56:30}.

## A third cause: clinging to the founder's vision
In the fourth episode al-Suwaidan adds a third cause of family businesses' failure in the second generation — besides those already named — the heirs clinging to the very vision and method their father set, without review or criticism {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:14:00}.

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders": why family firms fail after the founder
In his test on change in organisations in [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]], al-Suwaidan includes a statement for the trainees to judge. It says that heirs who keep their father's vision without criticising it run a high risk of failure, and he marks it true. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:23:00} He gives figures: family firms are ninety-two per cent of the world's companies. He says sixty-eight per cent of them collapse in the second generation and only four per cent remain in the third. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:23:30} He names three causes. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:24:00} The first is clinging to the founder's vision; he says he once offered a founder to triple his results within three years. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:25:00} The second is that the founder's relationships are not handed down. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:26:30} The third he first names as "the intervention of women", then at once says the fault is not the women's. Founders train their sons but not their daughters, and do not pass their relationships to them. Since the daughters will own a share, which is their right, the remedy is to train them, not to keep them out. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:28:00} These are his statements and his reading.
