## Summary
Ibn Sina divides the muscles of the back into those that bend it backward and those that bow it forward, and gives the abdomen eight muscles with shared uses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.72} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.73}.

## The back
Some back muscles bend the spine backward and some bow it forward, and the other movements branch from these. The backward ones are those properly called the back muscles: two, each made of twenty-three muscles, one for each vertebra receiving an oblique fibre except the first. Stretched in measure they set the spine upright; overstretched they bend it back. The bowing muscles are two pairs: an upper pair, among those that move the head and neck, running beside the gullet and joined below to five of the upper chest vertebrae in some people and to four in most; and a pair under it, called the two backs, starting from the tenth and eleventh chest vertebrae and descending to bow the spine downward {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.72}.

## The abdomen
The abdomen has eight muscles with shared uses: helping to squeeze what is in the viscera, stool, urine and the child in the womb; supporting the diaphragm and helping it when it contracts on breath; and warming the stomach and intestines by covering them. They are a straight pair descending from the xiphoid cartilage with fibres running lengthwise to the pubis, wholly fleshy; two muscles crossing these transversely at right angles, above the membrane stretched over the whole belly and below the straight pair; and two oblique pairs at the two sides, each of two muscles crossed like a cross, from the soft end of the rib to the pubis and from the flank to the xiphoid {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.72-73}.
