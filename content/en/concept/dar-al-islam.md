## Summary

Dar al-Islam in the terminology of jurists is every geographic territory where the rulings of Islam are openly established and its public laws prevail. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8047} It is conceptually related to other classical juristic categories such as Dar al-Harb (the abode of war), Dar al-Ahd or al-Sulh (the abode of treaty), and Dar al-Baghy (rebel territory). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8047}

If non-Muslim forces seize any territory of Dar al-Islam, armed defense becomes an individual obligation (fard 'ayn) on every capable resident of that area—men and women, young and old—and if they are unable to repel the enemy, the obligation expands sequentially to neighboring regions until encompassing all Muslims, who all bear collective guilt if they allow any part of it to be taken. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8047}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8048} Inhabitants of Dar al-Islam must publicly establish manifest Islamic rites, including Friday prayers, congregational prayers, Eid services, and the call to prayer (adhan); if the population of a town refuses to perform these rites publicly, the authorities are commanded to fight them even if they pray privately. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8048}

## Legal Classifications and Areas of Inquiry

First, transformation into Dar al-Kufr: jurists held three distinct doctrines regarding whether Dar al-Islam can ever become an abode of disbelief; Shafi'is hold that it never reverts to Dar al-Kufr under any circumstance, even if entirely conquered, its Muslim inhabitants displaced, and non-Muslim laws enforced, drawing evidence from the prophetic maxim that Islam prevails and is not prevailed over. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8048} Malikis, Hanbalis, and Abu Hanifa's two foremost disciples (Abu Yusuf and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan) argue that it transforms into Dar al-Kufr immediately upon the dominant public enforcement of non-Muslim laws, just as it originally became Dar al-Islam through the public establishment of Islamic statutes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8048} Abu Hanifa ruled that it transforms into Dar al-Kufr only if three conditions occur simultaneously: public enforcement of the laws of disbelief, direct territorial contiguity with enemy lands, and the total absence of any Muslim or dhimmi living securely under the original Islamic covenant; he explained that the division of lands rests fundamentally on safety and fear, such that if general security belongs to Muslims it remains Dar al-Islam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8048}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8049}

Second, rules regarding entry and safe-conduct: a harbi may only enter Dar al-Islam with official permission or safe-conduct (aman) justified by public interest such as delivering a message or essential trade provisions; he may not stay in the Hijaz for more than three days, and non-Muslims are permanently barred from entering the sacred sanctuary (Haram) of Mecca based on Surah al-Tawbah: 28. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8049} Whatever property and family he brings under safe-conduct is protected; if he departs for enemy territory, his property left behind remains shielded and may be recovered, and if he dies his heirs inherit it, with the ruler having the right to levy a tithe ('ushr) on commercial goods. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8049}

Third, settlement in the Arabian Peninsula: jurists agree on prohibiting non-Muslims from establishing permanent settlement in the Arabian Peninsula, citing prophetic commands; Shafi'is and Hanbalis define the prohibited zone specifically as the Hijaz because early Caliphs permitted non-Muslims to remain in Yemen, Tayma, and Najran, whereas other scholars extend the prohibition across the entire peninsula from Aden to the rural borders of Iraq. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8050}

Fourth, shrines, foundlings, and land reclamation: building new non-Muslim temples or churches within Dar al-Islam is forbidden; any abandoned child (laqit) found within its borders is legally adjudged to be Muslim by virtue of the territory; and non-Muslims are barred from claiming ownership through reclaiming wasteland or extracting underground minerals. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8050}
