## Summary
Ibn Sina separates the red vetch-like sediment from the bran-like when it is not red, names their sources in the liver, kidney or bladder, and gives itching, stench and heat as ways of telling them apart. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.194}.

## The vetch-like
Among the scraped sediments is a kind less wide and thicker in body; if red it is called vetch-like. It may be burnt pieces of the liver or burnt blood in it, or it may come from the kidney; that from the liver is more closely joined and fleshy, the other more crumbly, and if it leans strongly to yellow it is surely from the kidney, since the liver's leans to dark. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.194}.

## The bran-like
The bran-like may come from scab in the bladder or from the melting of organs. If there is itching at the root of the penis and stench, above all when it follows pus-like urine and the other signs show matured urine, it is from the bladder; if it comes with burning, weakened strength, sound urinary organs and a dull colour, it is from the melting of a humour. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.194}.
