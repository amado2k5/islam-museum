## Summary
The disliked (makruh) is, in the lexicon, the opposite of the beloved and what nature and the law reject. In usul it is what is sought to be left, without blame for doing it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14933} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14934}

## Related terms
It is opposed to the obligatory and the recommended, and differs from the forbidden in that the doer is not blamed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14934}

## Senses of the word
Usul scholars give the word four senses: the forbidden, leaving the better, what is disliked as reprehensible, and what is doubtful in its prohibition; al-Ghazali, al-Amidi and al-Zarkashi hold this view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14934}

## The Hanafi division
The Hanafis divide it into disliked as reprehensible (tanzih), which is closer to permitted and carries no punishment, and disliked as near prohibition (tahrim), which is closer to forbidden and carries lesser penalties such as loss of intercession. Muhammad ibn al-Hasan holds that a near-prohibition founded on a probable text is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14935}

## Its ruling
Usul scholars differ on the ruling on the disliked, on whether the doer is bound to leave it, and on whether a general command covers it; the detail is in the usul appendix. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14935}
