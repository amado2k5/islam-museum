## Summary
In the lexicon, ittika' means leaning on something. It also means inclining to one side while sitting. Jurists use the word only in these two senses. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1488}

## Difference from istinad
Istinad, in the lexicon, means leaning with the back alone. It is therefore narrower than leaning in the first sense, and it is distinct from the second sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1488}

## Leaning in prayer
Leaning in prayer, in either sense, is permitted for those with a valid excuse, by agreement of the jurists. For others, leaning is disliked in an obligatory prayer and permitted in a voluntary one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1488}

## Leaning on a grave, or on an object with an image
Leaning on a grave is treated like sitting on it. The majority hold it disliked; the Malikis permit it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1488} Leaning on something bearing the image of a living creature, such as a cushion, is discussed under marriage, in the section on the wedding feast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1489}

## Where the topic is treated
The jurists treat the rules in their own places: in prayer, under the disliked acts; in funerals, under burial; in purity, under the manners of relieving oneself; in food, under eating; in mosques, under what is allowed for necessity or for reviving dead land; and in marriage, under the wedding feast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1488} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1489}
