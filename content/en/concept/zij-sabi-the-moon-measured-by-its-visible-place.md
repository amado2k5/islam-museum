## Summary
When the measurement is made with the Moon, the observer needs the parallax of its view so that its visible place in longitude and latitude is right; with it he finds its visible distance from the equator, the visible part that culminates with it and the half day arc of its visible position, and then takes the altitude of the Moon and begins by finding its altitude at midheaven. Al-Battani repeats the point in the chapters on altitude and azimuth: its visible altitude is known from its visible distance, and its azimuth from its visible distance from the equator. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.56-58}.

## What lies beyond this chapter
This points to the whole chapter resting on the star's true or apparent place, and to the Moon alone having what is known as parallax because it is near the earth, a subject of a later chapter devoted to it. The text ends the chapter on azimuth by sending the reader in the same way to any star whose azimuth is wanted. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.58}.
