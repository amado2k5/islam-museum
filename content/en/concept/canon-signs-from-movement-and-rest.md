## Summary
Ibn Sina says that reading movements and rests needs fuller treatment, and lists signs of rest such as apoplexy, epilepsy, fainting and palsy, and signs of movement such as chill, shivering, hiccough, sneezing and cough, which he sorts by whether nature or will drives them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.155-156}.

## Signs of rest and of movement
From rest are taken apoplexy, epilepsy, fainting and palsy. From movement come chill, shivering, hiccough, sneezing, yawning, stretching, cough, twitching, spasm and tremor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.

## Natural and voluntary
Some movements come from the original nature, like hiccough; some from an incidental nature, like spasm and tremor; some are purely voluntary, like restlessness and tossing; some are mixed, like cough and urination. In some the will comes first, as in cough; in others nature comes first, as in urine and stool when the will does not hasten to them. Some are noticed by sense, like chill, and some are not felt, like twitching {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.155}.

## How movements differ
These movements differ in themselves, cough being stronger than twitching; in the number of movers, sneezing having more than cough, since cough moves the organs of the chest and sneezing those of chest and head together; in degree of danger, dry hiccough being graver than cough though cough is stronger; in what nature uses to help itself; in their origins in the organs; in the acting powers, twitching being natural in origin and cough from the soul; and in matter, cough coming from spitting and twitching from wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.155-156}.
