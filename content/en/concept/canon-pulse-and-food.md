## Summary
In the eleventh chapter Ibn Sina shows that what is taken in changes the pulse by its quality and its quantity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.178}.

## Quality and quantity
What is taken in changes the pulse by its quality and quantity. By quality, it inclines to heating or cooling and the pulse changes accordingly. By quantity, if moderate, the pulse grows in size, speed and frequency for the increase of power and heat, and the effect lasts for a time {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.178}.

## Much and little
If the quantity is very great, the pulse becomes uneven without order for the weight of the food on the power, and every weight brings unevenness. Archigenes claimed that its speed is then greater than its frequency, and the change lasts since the cause stays. If the quantity is less than that, the unevenness is regular; if small, the pulse is less uneven, large and quick and the change does not last, since the matter is small and is soon digested. If the power slackens and weakens from too much or too little, the two pulses become alike in smallness and wide spacing in the end, and if nature is strong enough to digest and transform, the pulse returns to moderate {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.178}.
