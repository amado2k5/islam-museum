## The purpose of the head
Ibn Sina reports from Galen that the purpose of creating the head is not the brain, nor hearing, smell, taste or touch, since these organs and powers are found in an animal that has no head; the purpose is the good condition of the eye in the work for which it was made {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.5}

He says the eye needs a lookout from which it surveys all the organs on every side, because the eye stands to the body about as a scout stands to an army, and the best place for scouts is a commanding one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.5}

A head is not needed for every eye without exception; it is needed by the animal whose eyes need shelter and need nerves coming to them for the various movements of the globe and the lids {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.5}

## The head's own parts
Ibn Sina lists the head's own parts in this order: the hair, then the skin, then the flesh, then the membrane, then the skull, then the hard membrane, then the thin membrane the print calls mashimi, then the brain with its substance and its ventricles and what is in it, then the two membranes beneath it, then the net, then the bone that is the base of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.5}

He says he will treat the matter in full in the chapter on the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.5}
