## Summary
When the great artery reaches the end of the vertebrae it divides, with the vein that accompanies it, like the Greek letter lambda, and each part rides the sacrum toward the thigh, leaving before the thigh a vessel to the bladder and navel that shows in fetuses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.86-87}.

## The division
He says that when the great artery reaches the end of the vertebrae it divides with the vein that accompanies it in the shape of the Greek lambda, one part going right and one left, each riding the sacrum toward the thighs. Before reaching the thigh each leaves a vessel going to the bladder and the navel; they meet at the navel and show plainly in fetuses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.86}. In those who have reached full growth their ends dry and their roots remain, and branches from them spread in the muscles lying on the sacrum; the one reaching the bladder divides in it and its tips reach the penis, and the rest goes to the uterus in women, as a small pair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.87}.

## In the legs
The two descending to the legs divide in the thighs into two great branches, outer and inner. The outer inclines toward the inner and leaves branches in the muscles lying there, then descends, and from it a large branch turns forward between the first and second digits, while the rest goes deep; in the largest parts of the leg it runs stretched beneath the venous branches {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.87}.
