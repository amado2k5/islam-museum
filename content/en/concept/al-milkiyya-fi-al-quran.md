## Source of Ownership
Zaydan Qa'dat states that sovereignty belongs to God first and last, that what is on Earth is His handiwork, and that God grants the Earth as an inheritance to whomever He wills of His servants {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 149}. He sees this as a religious truth with an economic consequence, chief among them that the Earth belongs to no one, but is given to be used and cultivated {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 149}, and that all people are equal before God in the right of usufruct {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 149}.

## Work as the Basis of Ownership
The author concludes that ownership is granted for the sake of use and work, and that God is the one who grants it, so every person capable of working has the right to own a piece of land to use, by virtue of being a human being and a citizen {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 150}. Whoever seizes vast tracts of land without using or cultivating them is accepted by neither the law of religion nor the law of the world {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 150}. He states that work is a legitimate basis for ownership, as is good citizenship, and that exploitation and evading work are unacceptable to the Qur'anic method {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 150}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 151}.

## Limits of Private Ownership
The author holds that the ownership of wealth is bounded by principles and obligations toward the homeland, the citizen, and religion: spending in the way of God without excess, refraining from hoarding or monopoly, and investing hoarded wealth so that it becomes active {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 152}. A believer accepts of possessions what he needs for his life, which in his view are food, a means of transport, shelter, medical treatment, and knowledge {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 152}. Private ownership also includes the tools and working materials in the owner's hands, which may not be taken from him {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 153}.
