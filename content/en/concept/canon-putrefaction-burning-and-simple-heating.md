## Summary
In the chapter on warming agents Ibn Sina distinguishes putrefaction, burning and simple heating: putrefaction yields a foreign heat and its action is plain warming; burning separates the moist from the dry substance by raising one and settling the other; simple heating leaves the moistures in their natures but hotter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.143}.

## Putrefaction
Putrefaction's property is to produce a foreign heat and nothing else, and its action is plain warming, which is not burning. Warming often happens without putrefaction, and can occur before it, since putrefaction often arises when an external heat remains after the warming cause has gone and kindles in the moist matter, changing its moisture away from suitability for the temperament of the substance in which it sits. When the moist heat changes a thing from one specific temperament to another, that is not putrefying but digesting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.143}.

## Burning and simple heating
Burning is when the moist substance is separated from the dry, by lifting the one and settling the other. Simple heating is when all the moistures remain in their specific natures but grow hotter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.143}.
