## Definition
In the books of adab a nadira (plural nawadir) is a short funny or striking report told of a wit, a fool, a miser or a party-crasher. It is not the jurists' 'rare case', which has its own record: [[concept/nadira|nadira]].

## The anecdote books
Authors gathered anecdotes in chapters of their books or in whole books. The editor of Nawadir Juha al-Kubra records that Ibn al-Nadim's Fihrist lists a book called Nawadir Juha among the books on the heedless, and that al-Jahiz was the first Arab author to mention Juha {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 5}. In volume 4 of Nihayat al-Arab, al-Nuwayri gives a chapter to the anecdotes of wits, fools, judges, grammarians and singers, among them the reports of Ash'ab {cite:nihayat-al-arab-nuwayri-vol4-scan p. 27-29}. Books of this kind in the museum include Ibn Qutayba's [[work/uyun-al-akhbar|'Uyun al-Akhbar]], al-Abshihi's [[work/al-mustatraf|al-Mustatraf]], and Ibn al-Jawzi's [[work/akhbar-al-humaqa|Akhbar al-Hamqa wa-l-Mughaffalin]], [[work/akhbar-al-zuraf|Akhbar al-Ziraf wa-l-Mutamajinin]] and [[work/kitab-al-adhkiya|Kitab al-Adhkiya']].

## A cumulative literature
The editor of Juha's anecdotes holds that this literature is cumulative, the work of no single person or age, and that people composed many anecdotes in Juha's name {cite:juha-nawadir-kubra-djuwaydi p. 4-6}. The museum therefore presents an anecdote as a told report, not as an established historical event.
