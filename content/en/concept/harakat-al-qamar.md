## Summary
The motion of the moon in the zodiac, as explained by al-Farghani, is compounded of five circular motions succeeding one another in a single sphere {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 182}. Al-Farghani placed this in the thirteenth chapter of the book {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 183}.

## The Five Motions
The first motion is the motion of the body itself on the epicycle; the second is the motion of the center of the epicycle on the eccentric; the third is the motion of the center of the eccentric in a small circle around the center of the zodiac; the fourth is the motion of the inclined sphere carrying the head and tail of the Dragon; and the fifth is the motion of the entire sphere, which equals the motion of the fixed stars {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 182}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 183}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 185}.

## Example of One Day
Al-Farghani presents an example for one day: the body of the moon moves westward on the epicycle by about thirteen degrees and four minutes; the center of the epicycle moves eastward on the eccentric by about fourteen degrees and twenty-three minutes; the apogee regresses slightly westward by about nine minutes; and the outermost sphere advances by a tiny amount in the cycle {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 183}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 184}. ## Cycles
Al-Farghani notes that the moon traverses the epicycle in about twenty-seven days and thirteen hours, and that its two nodes traverse the zodiac in about eighteen years and seven months {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 197}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 198}.
