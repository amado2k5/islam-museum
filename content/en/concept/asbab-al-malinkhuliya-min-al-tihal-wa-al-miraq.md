## From the spleen
What has its cause outside the brain, by sharing with something else from which a humour or a dark vapour rises to the brain, is either that thing being of a black-bile temperament, or the spleen when black bile is held in it and it cannot cleanse it, or when it is unable to draw black bile from the blood, whether a swelling has arisen in it or not and another affliction, or because of the intense heat of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.104}

## From the hypochondrium
Or that thing is al-miraq (the hypochondrium), when the residues of food and the vapour of the intestines accumulate in it, its humours burn and turn to a black-bile kind, whether they cause a swelling or not, and a dark vapour rises from them to the head; this is called the hypochondriac puff (nafkha miraqiyya), flatulent melancholia and hypochondriac melancholia, and it often comes from a swelling at the gates of the liver, which burns the blood of the hypochondrium {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.104}

## Views on the cause
Ibn Sina reports that Galen made this the cause of hypochondriac melancholia, that Rufus made its cause the intense heat of the liver and the intestine, that others make it the obstruction in the vessels known as the mesentery together with a swelling, and others the obstructions in it even without a swelling; those of the last view argued that these people's food does not pass into the vessels and so spoils, and those who spoke of a swelling argued from the long holding of the food in them, mostly raw as it was, so that this swelling is not hot, since there is no fever, thirst or bilious vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.104}

## Starting in the brain with a cause outside
The cause of its arising may be outside the brain while its starting point is in the brain, as when there is a hot swelling in the stomach whose vapour burns the moistures of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.104}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
In this place Ibn Sina names the womb, or the other organs that share with the head, as a possible starting point of the arising {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.104}
