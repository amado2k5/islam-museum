## Summary
Maryam Ekhtiar treats maghribi as a regional script, developed and used mainly in Spain and North Africa. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 27} Qur'ans from the Islamic West, she notes, diverged early from developments farther east and were nearly always written on parchment in this script exclusive to the region. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 51}

## Features
Its origins are not confirmed, but some scholars hold it to be the only script derived from [[concept/kufic-script|kufic]]. Unlike kufic it was written freehand and did not follow set proportions; it was limited to manuscripts and rarely appeared on buildings or objects. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 51} Parchment remained in use for Qur'ans in North Africa and Spain into the fourteenth century, long after it was abandoned elsewhere. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23} {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 51}

## Example
A bifolium from the Andalusian [[object/pink-quran-andalusia|"Pink Qur'an"]] of the thirteenth century, in bold maghribi, with verse numbers in [[concept/abjad-numerals|abjad]] letters. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 51}
