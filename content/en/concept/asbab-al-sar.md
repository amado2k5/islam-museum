## The obstruction and the humour
Epilepsy is either from a contraction of the brain to expel something harmful — a bad-substance vapour or an incompleteness of humour that makes an obstruction in a brain ventricle or at the roots from which the nerves spring; that may come from the humour through a wave-like motion that occurs in it, or from a boiling of excessive heat; and where the obstruction occurs, the power of sense and motion does not pass its natural passage {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.118}

## The thick wind and the contraction of the brain
As for a thick wind held in the passages of the pneuma, that is as the great philosopher Aristotle holds it, counting it one of the causes of epilepsy; and if there is an obstructing humour, the brain nonetheless contracts to expel the harmful, like the hiccup and retching that come upon the stomach, and like the twitching that comes upon organs for what they are expelling; when the brain contracts its motions are disturbed and the contraction of the nerve in the face and elsewhere follows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.119}

## Natures of the matter
This matter is either prevailing and abundant blood, or phlegm, or black bile, or yellow bile — that is very rare, and next in rarity unmixed blood; most of what brings on epilepsy prevails from phlegm, and Hippocrates said that most sheep that are epileptic, when their brains are dissected, are found to contain a bad fetid moisture; every cerebral cause of epilepsy rests on weak digestion in it, so it is either in the substance and marrow of the brain — which is worse — or in its membranes, which is milder; strong melancholic epilepsy is the worst, though the phlegmatic is more frequent, because the melancholic is the most obstructing of the pneuma passages {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.120}
