## Summary
In the lexicon, udw is any bone surrounded by flesh, of a person or an animal; its root sense is cutting and separating, and it names a distinct part of the body {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11653}.

## Washing with a missing or extra limb
Where part of a limb is cut off, washing applies to what remains. If the cut is above the elbow or ankle, washing of the cut part falls away, and the Malikis do not wash the stump of an elbow {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11654}. An extra limb is washed when it grows in the place of washing; the jurists differ when it grows elsewhere {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11654} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11655}.

## A separated limb
A limb separated from a living person is buried without washing or prayer. For a dead person's separated part, the Hanafis wash and pray over it where it is more than half the body, while the Shafi'is and the main Hanbali view pray over the part of a Muslim whose death is known {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11655} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11656}.

## A limb of an animal
A limb cut from a living animal is carrion, except fish and locusts {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11656}.

## Injury to a limb
A deliberate injury to a person's limb entails retaliation where the match can be made; otherwise blood-money or an assessed award is due {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11656}.

## Where to look further
Retaliation is treated under «qisas», blood-money under «diyat», and food under «at'ima» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11656}.
