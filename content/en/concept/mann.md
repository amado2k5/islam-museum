## Summary
Mann in the lexicon has several senses: a favour, the counting of services, a measure, a cut, and the food from heaven. Jurists take it in two ways: its legal measure, and reminding others of a favour one has done. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15100}

## Its measure
The Hanafis hold that a mudd is two rutls, a rutl is half a mann, and a mann is six hundred dirhams; the mudd and the mann are each a quarter of a sa'. Al-Rafi'i fixed the five wasqs of the basic subsistence in mann, and the Damascene rutl was found equal to the great mann. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15101}

## Reminding of a favour
When God confers, the reminder is to make the creature grateful; when a servant does so, counting favours and reproaching them is a major sin, and it voids the reward of charity. The Qur'an and the Sunna confirm it, and the Sunna says the one who reminds will not be spoken to by God on the Day of Rising. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15101} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15102} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15103}

## A kind word
A kind word and a prayer for the one who asks may be better than a gift followed by harm, since a good word is charity. Jurists differ on whether the reward is lost in whole or only in part; the first is the sounder view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15102} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15103}

## Refusing a gift from fear of obligation
Most jurists hold that a person may refuse a gift even when it is needed for a duty, so he may perform the dry ablution if refused water. The Shafi'is hold that a wife need not accept maintenance from one who gives it, because of the obligation it brings. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15104}

## Freeing captives
All agree that the imam may free adult captives of war as a favour if he sees good in it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15104}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series Nafahat Ramadaniyya (the faults of the tongue)
The Sheikh calls reminding people of one's gift or kindness a major fault of the tongue, and says that the true Bestower (al-Mannan) is God, so that it is God who confers favour, not the servant. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:00:30}
He relates that Abu Bakr, Umar and al-Abbas sat at the Prophet's door talking about kindness: one said its best is hastening it, another concealing it, the third making little of it, and the Prophet joined the three (needs_source: the report is not referenced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:01:30} He concludes that the doer of kindness conceals it and does not expose the recipient before witnesses, criticises those who publish in the media what they gave to poor families, and recalls the one among the seven shaded by God who hid his charity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:02:00} The doer then hastens it when asked, and makes little of it, telling the one who thanks him that it was a small thing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:03:30}
He holds that whoever does a kindness and then grieves because the recipient did not return it or treated him badly did not do it for God's sake. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:05:00} He cites the prohibition of voiding charity by reminders and hurt, and the hadith of the three at whom God will not look on the Day of Resurrection, one of whom is the one who reminds of his gifts. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:06:30} He quotes [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Ibn Sirin]], who heard a man listing his favours to another and told him: there is no good in a kindness once it is counted. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:07:00}
He holds that the worst degree of mann is a son reminding his father and mother of what he spends on them, since he and his wealth belong to his father, and since parents are not given zakat because they are in their son's care. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:11:00} He forbids a husband to remind his wife of the life he raised her to, and a wife to remind her husband of her service. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:11:30} He says Abu Bakr was never known to remind [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal]] or the others he freed, and that the wealthy Companions hung food at the back of the mosque so that the poor took it without the rich knowing who took or the poor knowing who gave. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep01 00:13:00}
