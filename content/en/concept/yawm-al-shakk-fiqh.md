Yawm al-shakk is a compound of day and doubt. Shakk in lexicon is misgiving, the opposite of certainty, plural shukuk; in the jurists' usage it is the equal standing of the two sides of perception, denial and affirmation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18027}

As a compound it is a juristic term for the thirtieth of Sha'ban, or whatever follows its twenty-ninth, when the sighting of Ramadan's crescent is not established by a legally valid establishment — otherwise it is the first of Ramadan, on the hadith "fast at its sighting." It is called the day of doubt because it may be the first of Ramadan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18027}

The schools differ on its definition. The Hanafis say it is the day doubted to be of Ramadan or of Sha'ban, that is when people talk of a sighting that is not established. The Malikis say it is the thirtieth of Sha'ban when the sky is overcast on its night and the sighting is not established; Abu al-Hasan defines it as the morning after an overcast thirtieth night without an established sighting. The Shafi'is say it is the thirtieth when people talk of the crescent being seen on a clear night while no one testifies to it, or boys, slaves or sinners testify and are presumed truthful or upright without being relied on. The Hanbalis say it is the thirtieth when the sky has no hindrance on the thirtieth night and people do not watch for the crescent — or, on the judge's and most companions' wording, when one whose testimony is rejected testifies to it, and the judge adds also when the sky has a hindrance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18027} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18028}

On the ruling of fasting it: the Hanafis say the day of doubt is fasted only as a supererogatory act; fasting it for another obligation is disliked and counts for that obligation if Ramadan is not afterward established, and if it is established the fast is valid for Ramadan on the soundest view for a resident, while a traveller's fast is valid for whatever he intended. Fasting it as a voluntary act is allowed and even recommended for the select who can form a firm intention, but is disliked for others who waver — unless it coincides with a customary fast, as when a regular Monday fast falls on the day of doubt. It is best for a Muslim to refrain until near noon in case the month is confirmed, then intend Ramadan if confirmed; the select intend a voluntary fast if it is not, and the common people intend a voluntary fast only if it matches a prior habit, otherwise they break fast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18028}

The Malikis state it is not to be fasted as a precaution for Ramadan; on the face of the Mudawwana doing so is disliked, and Ibn Abd al-Salam says forbidden, on Ammar ibn Yasir's report "whoever fasts the day of doubt has disobeyed Abu al-Qasim." If one fasts it as a precaution and it is then established as Ramadan, it does not count for him since the intention was not firm; he must still abstain the rest of the day in honour of the month and make it up after Ramadan. Likewise, one who refrains until near noon then intends Ramadan when it is confirmed does not have it count, and must make it up, since he did not settle the intention before dawn. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18028} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18029}

The Shafi'is state fasting the day of doubt is not lawful without a reason, and on the sounder view a fast of it is invalid; one may fast it for a make-up or a vow, or when it coincides with a habitual voluntary fast. Al-Isnawi says the known transmitted view held by most is dislike, not prohibition, while al-Shirbini says the relied-upon view is the text's, that is prohibition. The Hanbalis state it is disliked to fast it intending Ramadan as a precaution; al-Khiraqi says that after twenty-nine days of Sha'ban the crescent is sought — if the sky is clear that day is not fasted, and if cloud or haze bars the sight, fasting it is obligatory and counts if it proves to be of Ramadan. Ibn Qudama records differing narrations from Ahmad: one matching al-Khiraqi's, chosen by most of the companions' elders; another that the people follow the imam, fasting if he fasts and breaking if he breaks; and a third that the day is neither obligatory to fast nor does it count for Ramadan if fasted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18029}
