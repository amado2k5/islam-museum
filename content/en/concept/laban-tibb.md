## Nature and selection
Milk is composed of three substances, watery, cheesy and fatty. Fat is plentiful in cow's milk; the milk of the she-camel has less fat and cheese and is very thin; donkey's milk too is thin and low in fat; goat's milk is balanced; ewe's milk is thick and fatty; cow's milk is fattier and thicker; mare's milk is thin and watery like the camel's {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546}. The best milk for a human is woman's milk, and the best of milks is that drunk from the udder or as drawn; the best is very white, even in consistency, stays on the fingernail and does not run off, from an animal that grazes good plants, with no foreign taste of sourness, bitterness, pungency or odd smell, and it should be used as drawn before it turns {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.546} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.547}.

## Temperament
The watery part is hot and the buttery part balanced, though inclining to heat; sour milk is cold and dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.547}.

## Actions and properties
Its watery part is thinning and washing, with no sting; milk balances the chymes, strengthens the body and binds, and drunk with honey it cleans inner ulcers of thick humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.547}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is of good chyme and especially nourishing to the brain, most of all woman's milk, and near to digestion because it is made from blood in its most digested state. It can harm the phlegmatic because it leans to cold, and helps those of hot dry temperament if their stomach holds no yellow bile to turn it. One who has drunk milk should rest after it, not sleep on it and take no other food until it has gone down. It helps the elderly by moistening and removing the itch peculiar to them, and they are helped to digest it with honey. Colostrum is slow to digest, thick in humour and slow to descend, and honey corrects it. Every milk breeds obstructions, especially in the liver, except camel's milk, which has little cheese and a cleansing watery part. It helps against matters that pour onto the inner organs and hurt them by their sharpness and sting, washing them and moderating their quality; it harms those who suffer a flow of blood. Milk is not good for the viscera, and goat's milk harms them most because its animal mostly grazes astringent plants. In substance milk turns quickly, especially toward heat, and nothing harms the body more than bad milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.547}.

## Nature and selection
Spring milk is watery compared with summer milk, and the best is in midsummer. Cow's milk is rich in butter, ewe's in cheese and butter; there is little cheese in the milk of camels, then of mares, then of donkeys, so it seldom curdles in the stomach. Camel milk has some saltiness because its animal loves salty plants; it is the best of milks, though it is said to be very slow in the stomach. Milk varies with the colour of the animal, its age and its condition {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548}.

## Cosmetic uses
Drinking much milk breeds lice, some claim; but applied it clears ugly marks on the skin, and drunk it improves the colour, though it often brings on white patches (wadah), except camel's milk. Given with sugar it greatly improves the complexion, especially of women. Whey fattens people of hot dry temperament if it loosens them, and applied it removes freckles and marks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548}.

## Swellings and pustules
People with bad swellings, boils, itch and scab often recover by drinking milk, if their temperament holds nothing that spoils it and turns it to yellow bile. Milk is harmful to those with inner swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Milk suits inner ulcers by washing, cleansing and soothing them, and those with ulcers benefit if the temperament holds nothing to spoil it. Whey with myrobalan is for scab {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548}.

## Joints
Milks are bad for the nerves and for people with nerve diseases, especially the cold phlegmatic kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Goat's milk helps catarrhal descents, stops them and sweetens their sharpness, and helps ulcers of the throat. Milk is a remedy for dry forgetfulness, gloom and evil thoughts, but it harms the teeth, eating, pitting and crumbling them, especially when the tooth is cold in temperament, and it loosens the gums, so one rinses after it with honey, wine or oxymel; donkey's milk, it is said, firms the teeth and gums when used as a rinse. It does not suit those with headache, vertigo or ringing in the ears, above all sleeping on it, and in general it harms those with weak heads {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548}.

## Eyes
Milk brings dimness and film on the sight, but milked into the eye it helps ophthalmia, the harm of hot matters pouring into the eye, and roughness; so too mixed with egg white and raw rose oil and laid on the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.548} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.549}.

## Chest and breathing
The milk of the she-donkey and of the goat is good for cough, consumption and spitting of blood; ewe's milk is more useful in spitting of blood. Milk is among remedies for ulcers of the lung and tonsils, but is harmful to those with moist palpitation. Camel milk helps asthma and bites. Milk suits the chest better than the head and the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.549}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Milk breeds obstructions in the bladder, and whey helps jaundice. Goat's and camel's milk are useful, donkey's milk helps dropsy, and all of these help hardness of the spleen. Camel milk with castor oil is for inner hardnesses. Milk causes bloating and pain in the stomach, especially colostrum, and both stir hiccup and smoky belching and harm those with a diseased spleen or liver, except camel milk, which helps many swellings of the spleen and liver and softens the liver. Camel milk helps dropsy greatly, especially drunk with the urine of Arab she-camels, and it stirs the appetite and thirst. Sour milk is very slow to digest and raw in humour, but a naturally hot stomach digests it and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.549}.

## Excretory organs
Whey loosens burnt yellow bile, and with epithymum it loosens burnt black bile. Milk produces stone. Milk boiled down until its watery part goes binds the bowels and stops bloody flux. Cow's buttermilk is good for bilious diarrhoea, and goat's milk is useful for ulcers of the bladder. Any thick milk stirs colic and breeds stone, especially colostrum. Milk often loosens the bowels, above all that of mares, camels and donkeys, then cow's, then goat's; boiled or heated milk binds the bowels. Milk helps excoriation, and boiled sour milk stops bilious and bloody diarrhoea. Camel milk helps piles, and milk laid on swellings and ulcers of the anus and the groin soothes the pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.549}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says camel milk brings on the menses, that milk is given as an enema for ulcers of the womb, that it makes up the harm of intercourse and strengthens the capacity for it, and that milk stirs desire, even the sour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.549}.

## Fevers
Goat's and donkey's milk are good for hectic fever, and sour milk has often driven off hectic fevers if its fat is well removed and it can be digested; but fresh thick milk is often thrown into fevers, and a feverish person should not go near it at all {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.549} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.550}.

## Poisons and bites
Milk is useful after drinking deadly drugs, and after drinking sea hare, hemlock and henbane, and especially after cantharides, the drug called fafsiya, hellebore, wolfsbane, leopard's bane and all corrosive putrefying drugs; it is a remedy for one given henbane, restoring his reason {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.550}.
