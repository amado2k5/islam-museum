## Its causes
Loss of sight may come from the causes of weak sight pushed to the extreme — to be looked for there — but he speaks of it afresh, leaving what comes by sharing of the brain and the rest, for that is understood from there. Loss of sight either occurs while the visible parts of the eye are sound in substance, or after a consuming, dissolving lesion or what runs their course; his discourse concerns the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.215}
## Its detailed causes with the surface sound
If the eye's parts are outwardly sound in substance but stricken by a lesion from another side not visible to the crowd and the layman, then either the pupil is in its healthy state or not. If healthy, either there is a watery occlusion, or the occlusion is not there but in the hollow nerve — something standing in its tube, a closure befalling it from dryness, relaxation, a swelling in it, a swelling in its muscles pressing of itself or following a pressure befalling the forebrain as explained before, or a rupture befalling it; or the icy humour has shifted from facing the aperture or its temperament corrupted so it can no longer serve as the instrument of sight. Most often that is from a moisture greatly overwhelming it or a dryness overwhelming it so it gathers into itself and shrinks — this cause is called alqum; there is no remedy if widening reaches the utmost or narrowing reaches closure {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.215} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.216}
## Its signs
The sign of the water, the widening, the narrowing and the rest is what was said in their chapters; the cause in what belongs to the hollow nerve is easily encompassed in sum by the sign named in the water's chapter, but its detail is hard and can scarce be compassed in knowledge. With throbbing and redness, surmise a hot swelling in the nerve; with heaviness and little heat, a cold swelling; severe heaviness in a very moist eye means a moist matter, in a dry eye a melancholic one. If a blow or fall on the head first bulges the eye then sinking and loss of the eye follow, surmise the nerve has ruptured {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.216}
