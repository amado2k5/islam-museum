## Summary
Chapter 20 opens with finding the degree of the ecliptic that rises with a star in a given place and the one that sets with it. One takes the half day arc of the star and of the degree that culminates with it; their difference is half the difference of the two days, and this is subtracted from the rising times of the given clime if the star's half arc is the larger, and added if it is the smaller. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.53-54}.

## From the times to the degree
The times left after the addition or subtraction are the rising times of the degree with which the star rises in that place; the degree of the signs opposite those times is then taken in the table of the clime, and that is the part of the sign in which the number of times falls, the part with which the star rises. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.53}.
