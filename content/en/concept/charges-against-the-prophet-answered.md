## Summary
Sha'rawi sets out the Quran's argument that the Prophet had no known gift for poetry or oratory before his mission and was unlettered. He then takes the charges reported against him (sorcery, madness, poetry, soothsaying) and answers each. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.20} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.21}

## Answers to the charges
Sha'rawi holds that a bewitched man cannot resist a sorcerer while claiming to be under his spell; that madness does not fit the noble character the Quran attests; that a poetic gift does not appear suddenly at forty; and that a soothsayer forgets and contradicts himself, while the Prophet recited what was revealed without changing a letter. He cites [[passage/quran-68-2|Q. 68:2]], [[passage/quran-69-41|Q. 69:41]], and [[passage/quran-10-15|Q. 10:15]], on the request to change the Quran. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.21} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.23} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.24}

## Author's view
These are Sha'rawi's arguments and their order is attributed to him. The historical reports and modern studies of these charges are not yet presented.
