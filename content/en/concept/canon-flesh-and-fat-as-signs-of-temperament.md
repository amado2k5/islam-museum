## Summary
Ibn Sina says much red flesh points to moisture and heat, a little with no great fat to dryness and heat, that fat and suet point to cold, and that their scarcity points to heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.159}.

## Red flesh
Much red flesh shows moisture and heat, with firmness; a little with no great fat shows dryness and heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.159}.

## Fat and suet
Fat and suet always show cold, with flabbiness. If with them go narrow vessels, little blood and weakness under hunger, the temperament is inborn and natural; otherwise it is acquired. Scarcity of fat shows heat, since its matter is the richness of blood and its agent is cold, which is why it is thin on the liver and thick on the intestines, and thicker on the heart than on the liver because of the matter, not the temperament {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.159}.

## The fleshy body and the most brittle bodies
A fleshy body without much fat is hot and moist; with a little fat it shows excess of moisture, and with much it shows excess of cold and moisture, a cold moist body. The most brittle, that is the weakest, bodies are the cold dry, then the hot dry, then the dry that is moderate in heat and cold, then the hot that is moderate in moisture and dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.159}.
