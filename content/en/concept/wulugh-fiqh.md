Wulugh (lapping) lexically means a beast of prey drinking with its tongue: a dog laps (waligha) when it drinks what is in a vessel with the tips of its tongue, or puts its tongue in and moves it. The hadith "if a dog laps in the vessel of one of you, let him wash it seven times" uses the word. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17942}

Related terms: su'r is the remnant left in a vessel by the drinker, and it applies to what is left of a drink whether lapped or not. Shurb (drinking) is wider than lapping: every lapping is drinking, not the reverse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17942} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17943}

Impurity of the vessel: the majority hold that a dog's lapping makes the vessel impure; the Malikis and some Hanafis hold that it does not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17943}

Number of washings: the Hanafis and the Hanbalis (in the school's view) do not distinguish a dog from other beasts of prey; the vessel is washed three times for the Hanafis and seven for the school's Hanbalis. One Hanbali narration requires three, and a third requires repeated pouring without a fixed count. The Malikis say washing is recommended, not required, seven times without earth. The Shafi'is hold that the lapping of beasts other than dogs and swine leaves the vessel pure, with no washing required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17943} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17944}

Repeated lapping: the majority (Hanafis, Malikis, the sounder Shafi'i view and Hanbalis) hold that repeated lapping, by one dog or several, does not require repeated washing, since causes of the same kind merge, as breaking wudu' or causes of hadd or retaliation do. A Maliki view allows repetition; a second Shafi'i view requires seven for each lapping, and a third requires seven for one dog and seven for each dog. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17943} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17944}

Testimony of a trustworthy person: the Shafi'is hold that if a trustworthy person tells a man that a dog lapped one of two vessels, and he knows it lapped in one without knowing which, the report must be accepted, the named vessel judged impure and the other pure. If two trustworthy persons report lapping at two times, both reports are acted on. If they conflict as to the same moment, al-Saydalani and al-Baghawi hold that one must use ijtihad, while the Iraqis and most Khurasanis treat it as a conflict of two testimonies, with views that both fall, that they are used by lot, by division, or that they are suspended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17944} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17945}

The Hanbalis accept the report of a dog's lapping whether the reporter sees or is blind. If one says it lapped in this vessel and another says it did not, the affirmative report is preferred, unless the affirmer is blind and cannot be certain of the act, in which case the seer's word prevails. The Hanafis hold that a Muslim must accept a just Muslim's report that water was made impure by a dog's lapping, while the report of a sinner or an unknown person must be weighed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17945}
