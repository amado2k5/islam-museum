## Definition
Imhal in the lexical sense is granting respite and postponing a demand; the jurists use it in the same sense, and it is the opposite of hastening. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}

## Related terms
- I'dhar: the judge asks one who is liable whether he has anything that cancels the liability. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}
- Tanjim: postponing a payment to two or more dates. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}
- Tallum: waiting and patience, as when the judge gives a husband a time before dissolving the marriage for poverty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}
- Tarabbus: waiting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}

## Ruling
- A man whose poverty is proven must be given time until he is able to pay, and may not be jailed, following al-Baqara (2:280). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}
- The impotent husband is given a year by most jurists, as Umar did, in case the obstacle passes with the season. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}
- The one who has sworn off intercourse has four months, following al-Baqara (2:226). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3418}
- A claimant who asks for time to bring his proof is granted it by most jurists; some fixed three days, and others left it to the judge. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3419}
- Respite is refused where immediacy is required: the husband who divorced one of two wives and must name her, the buyer who wants to return a defective sale, and the pre-emptor. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3419}

## Related places
Suretyship, maintenance, dower, and judging and claims. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3419}
