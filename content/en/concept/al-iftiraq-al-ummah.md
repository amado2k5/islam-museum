## The report in Abu Dawud's Sunan and al-Khattabi's commentary
[[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] opens the Book of the Sunna of the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan]] with the chapter "explaining the Sunna", in which he gives [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]'s report: the Jews split into seventy-one or seventy-two sects, and so did the Christians, "and my community will split into seventy-three sects". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 7} Then the report of [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]]: "seventy-two in the Fire and one in Paradise, and that is the Community (al-jama'a)", to which some transmitters add: "and there will come out of my community peoples in whom those whims run as rabies runs through its victim, leaving no vein or joint untouched." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 7-8}

The annotator, quoting Muhammad Muhyi al-Din 'Abd al-Hamid, notes that al-Tirmidhi gave Abu Hurayra's report and called it *hasan sahih*, and gave from 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr a version with "all of them in the Fire but one… what I and my Companions are upon", calling it *hasan gharib mufassar*; its chain includes 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Ziyad al-Ifriqi. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 7}

From the words "my community will split" [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|al-Khattabi]] infers that none of these sects is outside the religion, since the Prophet counted them all as his community, and that one who errs in interpretation does not leave the faith. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 8} He explains *kalab* as the disease a man catches from the bite of a rabid dog, which makes him refuse water until he dies of thirst; the persistence of whims in their followers is likened to it. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 8}

## In Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi
[[person/al-baghdadi-abd-al-qahir-ibn-tahir|Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi]] (d. 429 AH) devotes the first chapter of [[work/al-farq-bayna-al-firaq|al-Farq bayn al-Firaq]] to the hadith on the community's division, giving it with his chains from Abu Hurayra, Abd Allah ibn Amr and Anas, and noting that it has many chains and was related by a number of Companions, among them Anas, Abu Hurayra, Abu al-Darda', Jabir, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, Abd Allah ibn Amr, Abu Umama and Wathila ibn al-Asqa'. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.23} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.24} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.25} As his editor presents it, he holds that the groups meant are doctrinal - on divine unity, justice, prophecy and the return - not the legal schools. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.9} His second chapter treats how the community divided into seventy-three groups, opening with the disagreement over who is included under "the community of Islam": al-Ka'bi's view that it is everyone who affirms Muhammad's prophethood and that all he brought is true, the view of those who made it everyone who prays towards the Ka'ba, and the Karramiyya's view that it is everyone who utters the two testimonies. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.28} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.29} He identifies the saved group as Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a and counts it in eight classes. {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.271} {cite:farq-bayn-al-firaq-khisht-ibn-sina p.272}
