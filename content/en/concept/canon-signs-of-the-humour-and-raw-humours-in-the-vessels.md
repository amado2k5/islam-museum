## Summary
Ibn Sina judges the kind of humour from the urine, the sweat and the state of sleep and wakefulness, counts the refusal of sleep despite good regimen a bad sign, and then describes the treatment when raw humours predominate in the vessels: rest, food, what cuts, such as honey oxymel, and a small amount of pepper or cumin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.248}.

## The signs
You judge the kind of humour from the urine or the sweat and from the state of sleep and wakefulness; if sleep is refused despite your good regimen, that is a bad sign {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.248}.

## Raw humours in the vessels
If you judge that good blood is scarce in the vessels and the raw humours predominate, give him rest, feed him, and give him what thins, though not what heats much, and give what cuts, such as honey oxymel. If the thinning remedies need more strength, put a little pepper in the food or in the barley water he drinks. If you are driven to the cumin or pepper preparations because the humours are unripe, give about a small spoonful before food, after it and at sleep; pennyroyal does not suit them, for it goes beyond the limit in heating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.248}.
