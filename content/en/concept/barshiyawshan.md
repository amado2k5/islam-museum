## Nature and selection
A fine herb that grows in pools, on banks and in rivers, and inside wells looks like fresh coriander, but its stems are red tending to black, with no stalk, flower or bloom, and its power goes quickly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}. The editor's footnote says the name is Persian and has several names.

## Temperament and actions
Galen said it is moderate; the book adds that it sometimes leans toward heat and dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

## Uses by organ
Actions: dissolves, thins and opens; it is astringent and stops flowing; mixed with the feed of cocks and quail it strengthens them for fighting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Adornment: its ash with vinegar and oil treats mange and snake disease; with myrtle oil and wine it lengthens hair and stops it falling out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Swellings and pustules: helps cold abscesses and disperses scrofulous swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Wounds and ulcers: helps fistulas and malignant wet ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Head: its ash-water helps lichen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Eyes: helps the book's listed eye condition gharb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Breath and chest: cleanses the lung very much and helps cough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Excretory organs: with wine it helps flowing of humours and helps jaundice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}. It moves urine and breaks the stone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}. The book also lists that it brings on the menses, expels the afterbirth, cleanses the woman after childbirth and stops bleeding; most say it binds the bowel, and Ibn Masawayh says it loosens it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Poisons: with wine it helps the bites of snakes, mad dogs and other vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.

Substitutes: in asthma, a substitute is violet with licorice juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.403}.
