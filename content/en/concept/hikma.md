## Summary
Sha'rawi defines wisdom as placing a thing in its beneficial place, glossing the verse in al-Baqara 269 that whoever is given wisdom has been given much good {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1164}.

## What God commands is wisdom
He says that all God commands of spending on weak descendants and of the good of the Hereafter is wisdom, because it secures life in this world and happiness in the next {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1164}.

## Faith before wisdom
He holds that the believer takes the command from God before knowing its wisdom, and that the reasons of the commands appear after the command is carried out, as the believer finds the effect of fasting in himself {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1220}.

The Sheikh tells an anecdote about the Commander of the Faithful in Baghdad, who grew angry with his governor of Egypt, named Musa ibn Isa (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:38:30}. He says the caliph ordered that the governor be killed when he reached Baghdad {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:39:00}. He relates that one of the caliph's close companions at court wanted to warn the governor without the advice becoming a scandal if the letter fell into someone else's hands, so he wrote a letter that opens with the basmala and breaks off at the word "inna" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:39:00}. He says the letter was left unfinished on purpose, as a hint that those around the governor were plotting against him (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep03 00:39:30}..
