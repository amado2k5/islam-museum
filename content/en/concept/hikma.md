## Summary
Sha'rawi defines wisdom as placing a thing in its beneficial place, glossing the verse in al-Baqara 269 that whoever is given wisdom has been given much good {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1164}.

## What God commands is wisdom
He says that all God commands of spending on weak descendants and of the good of the Hereafter is wisdom, because it secures life in this world and happiness in the next {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1164}.

## Faith before wisdom
He holds that the believer takes the command from God before knowing its wisdom, and that the reasons of the commands appear after the command is carried out, as the believer finds the effect of fasting in himself {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1220}.

## From the series Al Naba Al Safi: wisdom is the believer's lost property
The Sheikh gives a saying, based on a hadith, that wisdom is the believer's lost property, and says that early scholars sought it in the Qur'an, in the Sunna, and from teachers who opened hearts. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:00:30}
He returns to the same saying that wisdom is the believer's lost property, and then offers a new piece of wisdom from the early righteous. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:00:30}
He reports a saying attributed to a wise man: would that people were created for knowledge, that they worked by what they knew and were sincere in it (needs_source: the saying is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:20:00}

## The wise and the wise son
The Sheikh gives an example: a father restricts the spending of a foolish son and entrusts a wise son with his affairs. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:35:00}
He explains that the wise son trusts his father's promise and expects a return. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:19:30}
He says that a person who fears God is wise, and that God makes time serve him so that it works for him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep07 00:05:30}

## Wisdom in gratitude and patience
The Sheikh holds that blessings must be credited to God's favour and not to one's own strength. He cites a verse (sura not named) that few of God's servants are grateful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:12:30}
He gives the family of David as an example of thankfulness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:12:30}
He cites a hadith about patience, then a line of verse attributed to an Arab sage, which says to turn away from one's worries and entrust one's matter to God (needs_source: the attribution of the line was not checked in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:31:00}
He quotes an Arab sage who says to turn away from one's worries and put one's affairs in God's hands, and that relief comes through prayer, sincerity, seeking forgiveness and charity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:22:00}

## Sayings of the sages, and justice
He quotes the sages that if you want a friend with no fault you must live alone, since everyone has faults, so people should be judged with balance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:27:30}
He says that the religion requires justice even toward others. He cites ease in worship, such as praying seated, dry ablution when water is unavailable, and relief for the sick, the pregnant, the nursing mother and the traveller. He names wisdom and uprightness among the traits. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep05 00:48:00}
