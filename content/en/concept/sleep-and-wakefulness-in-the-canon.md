## Summary
In the thirteenth chapter, on the causes that lie in sleep and wakefulness, Ibn Sina says that sleep closely resembles rest and wakefulness closely resembles motion, but that each has properties to be weighed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.

## Sleep
Sleep strengthens all the natural powers by shutting in the innate heat, and slackens the psychic powers by moistening the passages of the psychic spirit and clouding the spirit's substance, and it checks what is dissolved. It removes kinds of fatigue and holds excessive evacuations. It brings more sweat than waking, since its sweating is by mastering the matter, not by fine continuous dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.

## What it does to matter
If sleep meets matter ready for digestion or concoction, it turns it to the nature of blood and warms it, the heat spreads through the body, and the body warms with an innate heat. If it meets hot, bilious humours and is long, it heats the body with a foreign heat. If it meets emptiness, the body cools through what is dissolved {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.

## Wakefulness and excess
Wakefulness does the opposite of all this, but if excessive it spoils the temperament of the brain toward a kind of dryness and weakens it, so that the mind is confused, and it burns the humours and brings acute diseases. Excessive sleep brings dullness of the psychic powers, heaviness of the brain and cold diseases, because it prevents dissolving. Sleeplessness increases appetite and hungers by dissolving matter and lessens digestion, and dissolving between sleeplessness and sleep is bad in every way {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.130-131}.

## Covering
As a rule in sleep the heat goes inward and the cold shows outward, so sleepers need covering for all their limbs that a waking person does not need. He promises more rulings on sleep in the coming books {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.131}.
