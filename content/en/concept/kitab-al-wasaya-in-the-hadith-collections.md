## Summary
The 'Book of Wills' (Kitab al-Wasaya) is a distinct chapter of the six canonical collections and of Malik's Muwatta, in which these works gather the reports and rulings on bequest: that a man may make a bequest of his property, the limit of a third, the validity of the bequest of the weak and the sick, bequests to heirs, and related matters. The museum's graph records the wills chapter of each of these seven books; the numbering shown here is that of the open hadith database the records were taken from, and differs from the numbering of printed editions. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 chapter ranges as listed in the passage records}

## The chapter in each book
The Book of Wills in [[passage/bukhari-kitab-55|Sahih al-Bukhari]] holds reports 2738 to 2781 in the database numbering, forty-four reports. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Bukhari book 55, reports 2738-2781} In [[passage/muslim-kitab-25|Sahih Muslim]] it holds 4204 to 4234, thirty-one reports. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Muslim book 25, reports 4204-4234} In [[passage/tirmidhi-kitab-30|Jami' al-Tirmidhi]], 2116 to 2124, nine reports. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Tirmidhi book 30, reports 2116-2124} In [[passage/nasai-kitab-30|Sunan al-Nasa'i]], 3611 to 3671, sixty-one reports. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Nasa'i book 30, reports 3611-3671} In [[passage/abudawud-kitab-18|Sunan Abi Dawud]], 2862 to 2884, twenty-three reports. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Abu Dawud book 18, reports 2862-2884} In [[passage/ibnmajah-kitab-22|Sunan Ibn Majah]], 2695 to 2718, twenty-four reports. {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Ibn Majah book 22, reports 2695-2718}
In the [[passage/muwatta-kitab-23-wasiyya|Muwatta of Malik]], in Yahya ibn Yahya's recension edited by Bashshar Awwad Ma'ruf, the Book of Bequests is the twenty-third book, with twenty-six texts, 2214 to 2239, in ten babs, on pages 309 to 321 of the PDF of volume 2. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 vol. 2 pdf pp. 309-321} The chapter's description in the graph says it opens with the command to make a bequest and with the bequests of the weak and the young, then the limit of a third, then the bequest of a pregnant woman and of a man at battle, then bequests to an heir, then gifts (nuhl) to children. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 vol. 2 pdf pp. 309-321}

## What the chapter gathers: examples
Among reports in al-Bukhari's chapter is no. 2742, which Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas relates: the Prophet came to visit him in Mecca while he disliked dying in the land he had emigrated from, and Sa'd asked whether he might bequeath all his property, and the Prophet said no; half, and he said no; a third; the text of the record in the graph stops at this third question. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2742} [[passage/bukhari-api-2742|Bukhari 2742]]. In the same chapter is 2758, the report of Abu Talha, who came to the Prophet about a verse on spending from what one loves (I do not give its wording) and said that his dearest property was Bayruha', a garden the Prophet used to enter, shade himself in and drink from, which he offered to God and His Messenger. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2758} [[passage/bukhari-api-2758|Bukhari 2758]]. It also holds 2766, which begins with the command to avoid 'the seven destructive things' and goes on to list them, among them consuming an orphan's property. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2766} [[passage/bukhari-api-2766|Bukhari 2766]].
The Muwatta has Malik's own statements alongside reports, among them in no. 2218 that the agreed matter with them is that the weak-minded, the foolish and the afflicted who recover at times may make valid bequests if they have enough of their minds to know what they bequeath, while one overcome in mind has no bequest. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 report 2218} [[passage/muwatta-23-2218|Muwatta 2218]]. In 2221 Malik says of one who names amounts in his third and whose heirs say he exceeded it that the heirs choose between giving the legatees their bequests and taking all the dead man's property, or dividing off a third of the estate for the legatees. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 report 2221} [[passage/muwatta-23-2221|Muwatta 2221]]. In 2228 he says of one who bequeathed and said he had given some heirs something they had not received, when the other heirs refused to allow it, that it returns to the heirs as inheritance, because the dead man did not intend any of it to fall in his third, and the legatees share in none of it. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 report 2228} [[passage/muwatta-23-2228|Muwatta 2228]].

## A caution
The numbers here follow the division used by the database; a printed edition may divide and number the reports differently. The museum does not display the text of each report in these records, only links them to the chapter. This record gives no legal ruling; it describes where the topic stands in the books.
