## Summary
In chapter nine Ibn Sina describes urine by age: infants' milky and whitish, boys' thicker, young men's fiery and moderate, middle-aged men's whitish and thin, and the old thinnest and whitest. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.197}.

## From infants to the old
Infants' urine leans to milky because of their food and the moisture of their temperament, and more to white. Boys' urine is thicker and denser than young men's and has more bubbles. Young men's leans to fiery with moderate consistency. Middle-aged men's leans to white and thin, though it may be thick according to waste that they often purge. The old have still thinner and whiter urine, and thickness comes to them rarely. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.197}.

## Thick urine in the old
If their urine is very thick, they are liable to develop a stone. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.197}.
