## Nature
Stems and crumbled leaves about as thick as basil and greener; the Greeks call its herb ground oak, because it has small bitter leaves like oak. Its root tends to purple. The book says it should be gathered when it has set seed. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Temperament and actions
Galen says it is hot and dry in the third degree, and its heating is stronger than its drying. It is opening, cutting and thinning, and it warms. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Wounds, eye and chest
With honey it cleans chronic ulcers. The book says that pills are made from it, dried, and used for sores of the eye. It helps chronic cough. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Joints and digestion
The fresh herb, or its decoction when drunk, helps a torn muscle; its drink helps spasm, and the older the better. It reduces a thickened spleen and helps black jaundice. Its drink is very helpful for poor digestion and helps at the start of dropsy. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling): the book says it brings on urine and the menses, and expels the foetus. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.519}

## Poisons and substitutes
A poultice for the bites of venomous creatures. The book names two substitutes, the roots of al-ghafat or the askulukandrion, and their weights are left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.519}
