## Summary
In a second way al-Battani takes the rising times of the Sun's degree in the clime and in the right sphere, subtracts ninety from the first, then takes the gap between the risings and subtracts it from ninety or adds it to ninety according to which of the clime's risings is the greater, and the result is half the day arc. He states that this gap is the Sun's degree's share of the difference of the day, which he adds to ninety if the Sun is in the northern signs and subtracts from ninety if it is in the southern. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.46}.

## The second way
He says: if you want the day arc in another way, take the rising times opposite the Sun's degree in the clime and the rising times opposite the Sun's degree also in the right sphere, whatever they are, and subtract ninety from them; having done so, take the difference between them and the risings that came out for you from the climes. Then look: if the clime's rising times are the greater, subtract that difference from ninety; if they are the lesser, add that difference to ninety; ninety, after the addition or subtraction, is half the day arc, and by doubling it you have the whole day arc. The wording of subtracting ninety at the start of the sentence is disordered in the scan. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.46}.

## The Sun's share of the difference of the day
He adds: know that this gap between the risings is the share of the Sun's degree in the difference, that is, the difference of the day. When you know it, look at the Sun's degree: if it is in the northern signs, add that to ninety; if in the southern, subtract it from ninety; what results is half the day arc, that is, the part of the equator that passes from the Sun's rising to its reaching midheaven at midday, and double that is the whole day arc. The sense is the same in both ways. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.46}.
