## Summary
Al-Battani says the earth was divided into three parts that gather the climes, districts and all other inhabited lands: the first from the Green Sea northward to Bontos and the lake of Maetis, which they call Europe; the second from the sea of Egypt south to the Abyssinian sea, called Libya; and the third everything left of the inhabited world as far as China, called Great Asia. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.31-32}.

## Europe and Libya
The first part is bounded west and north by the western sea Oceanus, south by the sea of Egypt and the Romans, and east by the river Tanais and the lake of Maetis, so it is like a peninsula, and they named it Europe. The second runs from the south of the sea of Egypt to the Abyssinian sea, bounded by the Green Sea on the west, the sea of Egypt and the Romans on the north, al-Arish on the east and the Abyssinian sea on the south, and is called Libya. The names and limits are read from the scan and need a later check against the complete printed text. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.31}.

## Great Asia
The third part is all that is left of the settled earth to its far end, bounded on the west by Tanais, the river, the gulf, al-Arish and Ayla, on the south by the sea of Yemen and India, and on the east by the farthest settlement of China on the eastern side and China itself, and it is called Great Asia. He then says these three parts gather the climes, districts and all other inhabited lands, while what is unknown whether settled or ruined is eleven parts out of twelve. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.31-32}.
