## Summary
Ibn Sina ascribes tearing pain to matter that dissolves out of the muscle and its membrane, stretching the membrane and separating its continuity; breaking pain to matter or wind between bone and the membrane that covers it, or to cold that grips that membrane hard; and loose pain to matter stretching the flesh of the muscle but not its tendon {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.149}.

## Tearing
The cause of tearing pain is the matter of what dissolves from the muscle and its membrane, so that it stretches the membrane and separates its continuity, indeed the muscle's {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.149}.

## Breaking
The cause of breaking pain is matter or wind lying between the bone and the membrane that covers it, or cold that contracts that membrane forcefully {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.149}.

## Loose
The cause of loose pain is matter that stretches the flesh of the muscle without its tendon. It is called loose because flesh is softer than nerve, tendon and membrane {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.149}.
