## Summary
Ibn Sina distinguishes the massage of preparation before exercise, begun gently and made firmer as the person is about to rise to exercise, from the massage of recovery after it, also called the calming massage, whose aim is to dissolve residues held in the muscles that exercise did not expel, so as to refresh and avoid fatigue; it should be light and moderate, best with oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## The massage of preparation
One kind of massage is the massage of preparation, before exercise; it begins gently and is made firmer when the person is about to get up to exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## The massage of recovery
Another is the massage of recovery, after exercise, also called the calming massage; its aim is to dissolve the residues held in the muscles that exercise did not expel, so that the person is refreshed and fatigue does not come. It should be light and moderate, best done with oil, and not forced into hardness, stiffness or roughness, which would make the organs hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## Erring toward hardness or toward softness
Ibn Sina says that an error in massage leaning to hardness is safer than one leaning to softness, because strong dissolving is easier to put right than a body made ready by soft massage to take corruption; yet hard, rough massage taken to excess holds children back from growing, while its harm in adults is less {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.
