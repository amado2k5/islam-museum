## Summary
Thuluth is one of the [[concept/al-aqlam-al-sitta|six pens]] canonised by [[person/ibn-muqla|Ibn Muqla]], forming a large–small pair with [[concept/naskh-script|naskh]]. Maryam Ekhtiar notes that with [[concept/muhaqqaq-script|muhaqqaq]] it suited large Qur'ans, and that it was frequently used to decorate portable objects and architectural surfaces. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 25}

## Examples
A calligraphic folio by Zayn al-Abidin ibn Muhammad al-Shirazi, Shiraz, 888 AH/1483 CE, combines naskh, thuluth and tawqi' (Ashmolean Museum). {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26} In an album by [[person/shaykh-hamdullah|Shaykh Hamdullah]], Istanbul, about 1500, a large thuluth (Turkish sülüs) above sits over a minute naskh, framed by marbled (ebru) borders, for a balanced composition. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 42} The author describes Hamdullah as a follower of [[person/yaqut-al-mustasimi|Yaqut al-Musta'simi]] and royal scribe in the imperial atelier of [[person/bayazid-ii|Bayezid II]]. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 42}
