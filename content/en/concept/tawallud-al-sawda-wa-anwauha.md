## Where it arises
Black bile grows and arises at times through the organ that makes the food, the liver, when it burns the blood or is too weak to expel the black residue (the rarer way), and at times through the organ that is the emptying place of black bile, the spleen, when it is too weak for two things: drawing the heaviness of the blood from the liver, and expelling the residue of what is drawn to it into its own outlet; it may also arise in another organ, through its intense burning of its food, or its inability to expel the residue of its food so that the fine part dissolves and the thick turns turbid into black bile, or through its intense cooling and drying of what reaches it; foods that generate black bile may also be a cause {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.105}

## The view of some physicians about the jinn
Some physicians held that melancholia may come from the jinn; Ibn Sina says that, for learning medicine, it does not matter to him whether this happens or not, for if it happens it happens by turning the temperament to black bile, so its near cause is black bile, whether the cause of that black bile is a jinn or something else; among the strong causes of melancholia is excess of grief or fear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.105}

## Kinds of black bile
The black bile that makes melancholia is either natural black bile, or phlegm when it turns to black bile by condensing or the slightest burning, though this is rare and uncommon, or blood when it turns by cooking or condensing without intense burning; as for the bilious humour, when its burning reaches the extreme it makes mania and does not stop at melancholia; each kind of black bile, when it falls in the brain in the place described, makes melancholia, but some of it makes mania with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.105}
