## Summary
If the validity of prayer depends on an Arabic recitation, must a Muslim who cannot recite it learn it? This is the question of the duty to learn Arabic for worship. The page gives each of the four Sunni schools' views under its holders, from their books and the fatwa houses, without a verdict from the museum. It rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the books and fatwas it points to were not opened.

## The question and its background
The question builds on the rulings before it: reciting a translation in prayer, in [[concept/qiraat-al-quran-bil-tarjama-fi-al-salat|Reciting the Qur'an in translation in prayer]], and the Friday sermon in other than Arabic, in [[concept/khutbat-al-jumua-bi-ghayr-al-arabiyya|The Friday sermon in a language other than Arabic]]. For one for whom the schools allow a non-Arabic text because of incapacity, does he stay unable for good, or must he learn? The sources deal with this by distinguishing the one whose incapacity is temporary from the one able to learn, and individual prayer from the communal Friday sermon.

## The Hanafis
For Abu Yusuf and Muhammad, and on the relied-upon Hanafi fatwa after Abu Hanifa's retraction, non-Arabic recitation or takbir is only an interim concession for incapacity, and one able to learn the required Arabic recitation must learn it, as in al-Kasani's Bada'i al-Sana'i, pp. 113 and 131 {cite:ablibrary-badai-sanai-kasani-p113 on al-Kasani p.113} {cite:ablibrary-badai-sanai-kasani-p131 on al-Kasani p.131}. IslamWeb Fatwa 111542 reports this from Ibn Abidin's Radd al-Muhtar, vol. 1, p. 484 {cite:islamweb-fatwa-111542 on Radd al-Muhtar 1/484}.

## The Malikis
The IIUCS Journal article quotes al-Dasuqi's Hashiya on the Sharh al-Kabir: every legally responsible Muslim is obligated to exert utmost effort to learn al-Fatiha in Arabic for prayer {cite:banglajol-iiucs-arabic-prayer-article-2681 on al-Dasuqi}. What binds one who cannot, following an Arabic-reciting imam and then dhikr, is set out in [[concept/qiraat-al-quran-bil-tarjama-fi-al-salat|Reciting the Qur'an in translation in prayer]].

## The Shafi'is
IslamWeb Fatwa 111542 states that Imam al-Shafi'i holds that every Muslim must learn of the Arabic language to the extent of his ability, in order to perform the obligatory recitation and the adhkar of prayer {cite:islamweb-fatwa-111542 on the Shafi'i position}. For the Friday sermon, al-Bayjuri in his Hashiya on Ibn Qasim's commentary, p. 228, as Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya's Fatwa 2711 quotes him, says that of a congregation that does not know Arabic at least one must learn it as a communal duty if able, and if none does all sin and their Friday prayer is invalid {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-2711 on al-Bayjuri p.228}.

## The Hanbalis
Ibn Qudama in al-Mughni, vol. 1, p. 352, states that whoever cannot recite al-Fatiha in Arabic is obligated to learn it, and if he does not learn it while able, his prayer is invalid; the IIUCS article supports this {cite:ketabonline-mughni-ibn-qudama-v1-p352 p.352} {cite:banglajol-iiucs-arabic-prayer-article-2681 on the Hanbali position}.

## What the schools share and where they differ
These sources hold that the duty is proportioned to ability: whoever is unable is excused from learning, temporarily or entirely according to the school, and whoever is able must learn. The wording differs: the Hanafis speak of an interim concession, the Malikis of utmost effort, the Shafi'is of learning according to capacity and a communal duty for the sermon, and the Hanbalis of the invalidity of the prayer of one who is negligent. The sources quoted here do not speak of a duty to learn all of Arabic, only what the prayer or the sermon requires.
