## Summary
Ibn Sina devotes the fifth chapter to protecting the extremities from the harm of cold: the traveller first rubs them until warm and then coats them with a hot oil among the scented oils, does not make the boot so tight that the limb cannot move, since movement is one of the things that repel cold, covers the limb with paper and hair or fur, and watches for loss of sensation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.264}.

## Rubbing and oils
The traveller must first rub them until they warm, then coat them with a hot oil among the scented oils such as iris oil and ben oil, or olive oil if these are not at hand, especially with pepper, pellitory, euphorbium and asafoetida or castoreum added; among protective dressings is one of galbanum and garlic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.264}.

## Boot, movement and sensation
The boot and the hand-covering must not be such that the limb cannot move in it, for movement of the limb is one of the causes that drive cold off it, a constricted limb being struck hard by cold, and covering with paper and hair or fur protects it better; and when a foot or hand no longer feels the cold without the cold having eased, know that sensation is on its way to being lost and that cold has worked in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.264}.
