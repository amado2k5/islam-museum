## Summary
When rights or qualifications are equal in every respect, it is preferable to resort to drawing lots (qur'ah) rather than arbitrary selection, whether in allocating rights or choosing jobs and tasks {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 342}. This applies to certain forms of primary and tertiary distribution, and to secondary distribution with respect to job selection {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 342}.

## Its Legitimacy
The author cites the Prophet's saying, "If people knew what is in the call to prayer and the first row, and could find no way other than drawing lots for it, they would draw lots" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 342}, where istiham means drawing lots. He quotes al-Izz: drawing lots was prescribed when rights are equal in order to prevent rancor and grudges, and to foster acceptance of what destiny brings {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 342}.

## Its Limit
In the footnote, the author quotes al-Izz: there is no place for drawing lots when two pieces of evidence conflict, nor when two reports conflict, because it confers no reliability upon either report or either testimony {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 342}. Drawing lots applies to equal rights, not to weighing testimony or reports.
