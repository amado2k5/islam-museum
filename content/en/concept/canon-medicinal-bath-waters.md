## Summary
Ibn Sina lists what bathing in natron, sulphur, sea, ash, salt, copper, iron, vitriol and desert waters does, which kinds help and which harm, and advises gradual and gentle entry into the bath {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.141-142}.

## Natron and salt waters
If the waters of a bath are natron, sulphurous, marine, ashy or salty, by nature or by art, as when raisin of the mountain, bay berries or sulphur are boiled in them, they dissolve, make fine, remove flabby swelling and oedema, stop matters pouring into ulcers and help those with guinea worm. The copper, iron and salt waters help diseases of cold and moisture, pains of the joints, gout, slackness, asthma and diseases of the kidneys, strengthen the knitting of fractures and help boils and ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.141-142}.

## Copper, iron and boraceous waters
The copper waters help the mouth, the uvula, the slack eye and moisture of the ear; the iron waters the stomach and the spleen; the salty boraceous waters help heads that take in matter and a chest in that state, as well as the moist stomach, dropsy and flatulence {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.142}.

## Vitriol, sulphur and desert waters
Bathing in vitriolic waters helps spitting of blood, bleeding of the anus, menses, turning of the stomach, miscarriage without cause, agitation and excess sweat. Sulphurous waters cleanse the nerves, calm pains of stretching and cramp, clean the outer body of pustules, bad chronic ulcers, freckles, leukoderma and vitiligo, dissolve superfluities that pour to the joints, spleen and liver, and help hardness of the womb, but they slacken the stomach and remove appetite. Desert waters fill the head, so the bather must not dip his head in them; they warm over a long span, especially the womb, bladder and colon, but are bad for women {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.142}.

## Gentleness on entering
Anyone who wishes to bathe should do so quietly, calmly, gently and by degrees, never suddenly. Ibn Sina sends the reader to the section on preserving health for more on the bath, and says the same of using cold water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.142}.
