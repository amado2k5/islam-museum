## The verse and its setting

The phrase belongs to a verse of al-Muddaththir about the number of the keepers of the Fire, nineteen, whom God made only angels, whose number is a trial for those who disbelieve, and which ends by saying that none knows the hosts of your Lord but He: [[passage/quran-74-31|Al-Muddaththir 74:31]]. Al-Tabari records, in the setting, that Abu Jahl said to Quraysh: can ten of you not overcome one of them? and that the verse answers that the keepers of the Fire are angels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 74:31}

## How the commentators explain the hosts

**Al-Qurtubi** explains the clause as: none knows the number of your Lord's angels whom He created to punish the people of the Fire but God, an answer to Abu Jahl, who said Muhammad has only nineteen soldiers. He then gives: from Ibn Abbas, that the Prophet was dividing the spoils of Hunayn when Jibril came and sat with him, and an angel came and said "your Lord commands you thus and so", and the Prophet feared it might be a devil and asked Jibril, "Do you know him?" He said: he is an angel, and I do not know every angel of your Lord. From al-Awza'i, that Musa asked his Lord who is in heaven, and He said: My angels; how many, He said twelve tribes; how many in each tribe, He said: the number of the dust. Al-Qurtubi says al-Tha'labi mentioned the two, that is, he takes them from him, and the museum read no chain for them. He then gives al-Tirmidhi's hadith: the heaven groans and has the right to, there is not four fingers' room in it but an angel has put his forehead down in prostration to God. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 74:31}

**Ibn Kathir** says the meaning is that none knows their number and multitude but God, so that no one should imagine they are only nineteen, as a group of the astray and ignorant said, with the Greek philosophers and the followers of the two religions who followed them, who wanted to apply the verse to the ten intellects and nine souls whose existence they invented and could not prove; he says they understood the beginning of the verse and disbelieved its end. He then cites the hadith of the Night Journey in the two Sahihs on the Frequented House in the seventh heaven, which seventy thousand angels enter every day and never return to. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 74:31}

## The hadith of Abu Dharr: the heaven groans

Ahmad reports, and al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah report, from Abu Dharr that the Prophet said he sees what they do not see and hears what they do not hear; the heaven groans and has the right to; there is no space of four fingers in it but an angel is placing his forehead in prostration to God; and if they knew what he knows they would laugh little and weep much and go out to the open ground crying to God. Abu Dharr says he wishes he were a tree to be cut down. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 2312} {cite:sunan-ibn-majah 4190}

On its grading:

- Al-Tirmidhi said "hasan gharib", and that it is also reported from Abu Dharr as his own statement (mawquf), as Ibn Kathir quotes him. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 74:31}
- In the hadith-api copy of sunnah.com's data, al-Tirmidhi's hadith is graded hasan by Ahmad Muhammad Shakir, al-Albani, Bashar 'Awwad Ma'ruf and Zubayr 'Ali Za'i; Ibn Majah's is graded hasan by al-Albani, Muhammad Fu'ad 'Abd al-Baqi and Zubayr 'Ali Za'i, and hasan li-ghayrihi by Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut. {cite:sunnah-com 2312, 4190}

These are gradings given by those named in the database, not the museum's own.

## The wider theme: what people do not know of God's creation

The verse sits with others that point to creatures beyond people's knowledge, some covered in [[concept/life-beyond-earth-in-the-sources|Life beyond the earth in the sources]]. On Ya-Sin 36:36 al-Qurtubi says that what people do not know may be known to the angels, or to no creature. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 36:36} On An-Nahl 16:8 readings are given of the creation people do not know, among them creatures on the earth who do not know God is disobeyed. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 16:8} The commentators read here do not explicitly link the hosts of the Lord with those creatures; what the passages share is that human knowledge of God's creation is limited.

:::perspectives
**Sunni: the hosts are the angels, whose number only God knows.** Al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir, both citing the hadith of the heaven's groaning, Ibn Kathir also the hadith of the Frequented House. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 74:31} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 74:31}

**Sunni: a reply to limiting the angels to nineteen or to intellects and souls.** Ibn Kathir: whoever applies the verse to the ten intellects and nine souls has understood its beginning and disbelieved its end. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 74:31}

**Later gradings of Abu Dharr's hadith.** Hasan according to those named at sunnah.com; hasan gharib according to al-Tirmidhi. {cite:sunnah-com 2312, 4190}
:::

## What the sources do not say

- The commentators read here take the hosts to be angels in the setting of the keepers of the Fire; the museum found no text confining the hosts to angels alone or ruling others out.
- No kinds or numbers of the hosts are given in what was read, beyond the hadiths and reports quoted.
- The reports al-Qurtubi gives from al-Awza'i and from Ibn Abbas at Hunayn came with no chain in the source the museum read.
