## Summary
Ibn Sina closes the general signs by noting that a separation of continuity may be hidden and need the organ-by-organ discussion, and that swellings and separations are hardest in the nervous organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.

## When it hides
It may be hidden and not found by the general signs, and its explanation then needs discussion organ by organ; this happens when the organ has no sensation, holds no moisture that could flow, has no room to shift from its place, or rests on no organ whose dislocation would show it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.

## Nervous organs and joints
The hardest swellings and separations of continuity are those in the strongly sensitive nervous organs, which may even be fatal, and fainting and convulsion always follow them, fainting from the severity of pain and convulsion from the nervous nature of the organ. Those at the joints are slow to take treatment because of the joint's constant movement and the space near it ready to receive flowing matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.
