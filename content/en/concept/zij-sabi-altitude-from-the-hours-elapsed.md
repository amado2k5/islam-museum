## Summary
Chapter 17 is the converse of chapter 16: al-Battani finds the altitude from the hours that have passed since sunrise. He turns the hours into an arc by multiplying by fifteen if they are equal or by the day's hour times if they are seasonal, then takes the Sun's distance from midheaven and uses the returned chord and the chord of the midday altitude, so that the arc of the altitude comes out, from the eastern side before midday and from the western after. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.50}.

## From the hours to the Sun's distance from midheaven
He says: if you want to know the altitude from the hours of the day that have passed, take the hours from sunrise to the chosen hour; if they are equal hours multiply them by fifteen, and if seasonal, by the hour times of that day. Look at what is gathered either way: if it is less than half the day arc, subtract it from half the day arc; if it is more, subtract half the day arc from it; what results is the Sun's distance from midheaven. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.50}.

## The returned chord and the arc of the altitude
Then he says: find its returned chord and subtract it from the returned chord of half the day arc; multiply what remains by the chord of the Sun's altitude at midday of that day; divide the product by the returned chord of half the day arc; take the arc of the result as I laid down in the conversion of chords; the arc is the amount of the altitude above the horizon at that chosen hour. If it is before midday the altitude is on the eastern side, if after, on the western. This inverts the working of chapter 16. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.50}.
