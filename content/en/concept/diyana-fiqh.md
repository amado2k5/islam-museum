## Summary
As a term, diyana means accepting a swearer's or divorcer's stated intention, when the words are clear and the claimed meaning is a remote possibility. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8306}
A judge does not accept it against the apparent meaning of the words; the matter is left between the person and God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8306}

## Example
A man tells his wife 'you are divorced' and then says he meant a divorce from a physical bond. The court does not accept that, but he is accepted in diyana. The same holds if his tongue slipped to the word of divorce while he meant something else. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8306}

## How the schools fix what is accepted in diyana
The Hanafis allow a mufti to advise that no divorce has taken place, while a judge does not accept it and rules that the divorce took effect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8307}
The Shafi'is set four grades of claimed intention: a claim that removes what was stated outright is not accepted; a claim that restricts an absolute divorce is not accepted in court, with a dispute over diyana; a claim that restricts a general term is disputed; and words open to divorce take effect by intention. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8307}
For oaths, the vow of abstinence, zihar and similar formulas, a claim contrary to the apparent meaning is not accepted in court, but is accepted in diyana if the words bear it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8308}
