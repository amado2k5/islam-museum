## Summary
What a research summary supplied to the museum says about the Arabisation of the dīwāns, that is the change of the language of government registers to Arabic under the Umayyads: Iraq in 697 CE, Syria in 700, Egypt in 705-706 and Khurasan in 741-742, the languages Arabic replaced, and what happened afterwards to Aramaic, Coptic and Persian. The page records that the caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan ordered the change alongside a reform of the coinage. The museum did not open the pages named, and the founder approved using the summary on 21 November 2026.

## What this page is
The dīwān in this page is the government register and its accounting, not the poetic collection. The page follows what the summary says about the language of these registers before and after the reform and about the persons it names, and links the summary's account with the person and event records the museum already holds, without claiming that those records say what the summary says. It distinguishes the names and dates supported by Wikipedia from a scholarly chapter of 2024 that the summary names as support for this subject and that the museum did not open.

## Before the reform: the languages of administration
The summary says that in the first decades after the conquests the Umayyad state kept using the existing bureaucracies and languages: Greek in Syria, Greek and Coptic in Egypt, and Middle Persian in Iraq and the former Sasanian lands, while Aramaic and Syriac stayed the main vernacular of Syria and Iraq {cite:wikipedia-divan} {cite:wikipedia-arabization}. Arabic at this stage is the language of the rulers, not of the register. This is the summary's description of two pages the museum did not read.

## Abd al-Malik's reform, about 697 to 706
The summary says that the caliph Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, who ruled from 685 to 705, ordered the standardisation and Arabisation of government registers and bookkeeping, at the same time as the currency reform that introduced Islamic dinars and dirhams between 693 and 699 {cite:wikipedia-umayyad-caliphate} {cite:wikipedia-divan}. The museum has a record for the caliph: [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]]. This stage, as the summary describes it, is a central reform carried out region by region by officials it names.

## The four regional steps
Iraq, 697: Arabic replaced Persian in the Iraqi dīwān, carried out by Salih ibn Abd al-Rahman under the governor al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf {cite:wikipedia-divan} {cite:wikipedia-umayyad-caliphate}; the museum has records of both, [[person/salih-ibn-abd-al-rahman|Salih ibn Abd al-Rahman]] and [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]], and of the event, [[event/arabization-of-iraq-diwan|Arabisation of the Iraqi dīwān]]. Syria, 700: Arabic replaced Greek in the Syrian dīwān, carried out by Sulayman ibn Sa'd al-Khushani, with a [[person/sulayman-ibn-sad-al-khushani|record]] and [[event/arabization-of-syrian-diwan|the event]]. Egypt, 705-706: under the caliph al-Walid I Arabic replaced Greek and Coptic in the Egyptian dīwān through the governor Abdallah ibn Abd al-Malik {cite:wikipedia-al-walid-i}; see [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-abd-al-malik|Abdallah ibn Abd al-Malik]]. Khurasan, 741-742: the Khurasan dīwān passed from Persian to Arabic under Ishaq ibn Tulayq al-Nahshali {cite:wikipedia-divan} {cite:wikipedia-umayyad-caliphate}; the museum has no record for this man. The museum's existing records were not checked against the summary and no agreement with it is claimed.

## After the Arabisation: a long replacement
The summary says that once Arabic became the sole language of state administration, non-Muslim and newly converted communities shifted to Arabic gradually: Syrian Christians had largely adopted it between the 9th and 11th centuries, and Coptic gave way to Arabic as Egypt's majority vernacular by the Mamluk era {cite:wikipedia-arabization}. In Iran, it adds, Arabic became the language of scholarship and administration for centuries, but Persian re-emerged as a literary and court language and was not replaced as a spoken tongue {cite:wikipedia-divan} {cite:wikipedia-arabization}. For comparison see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-egypt|Arabic in Egypt]], [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-iraq|Arabic in Iraq]] and [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-syria|Arabic in Syria]]; for the places [[place/iraq|Iraq]], [[place/syria|the Levant]], [[place/misr|Egypt]] and [[place/khurasan|Khurasan]].

## How the sources differ
The Wikipedia pages (Divan, Umayyad Caliphate, al-Walid I, and Arabization) support names, dates and places. The non-Wikipedia support the summary names is a chapter by Marie Legendre of 2024, The Translation of the Dīwān and the Making of the Marwanid Language Reform: Secretarial Agency, Economic Incentives, and Regional Dynamics in the Umayyad State, pages 89 to 165 of Navigating Language in the Early Islamic World, shown at the University of Edinburgh repository and in a Brepols copy {cite:legendre-translation-of-the-diwan-2024-edinburgh} {cite:legendre-translation-of-the-diwan-2024-brepols}. The museum has two source records for the two addresses and did not open the chapter, so it does not say what the author argues; it says only that the summary names it as support for the four dated steps.

## What this page did not reach
The museum opened none of the pages the summary names, so everything above is ascribed to the summary as it arrived. The museum does not know from the summary what the registers were in detail before the reform, whether the Arabisation was completed in one year or stretched out, or how long non-Arab scribes stayed in service. Historians differ on this and the summary did not reach their differences. The four dates are as the summary gives them: 697, 700, 705 to 706 and 741 to 742, and the museum adds none.
