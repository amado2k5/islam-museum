## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter three, on the diseases of children and their treatment, by saying that the first aim in treating them is the regimen of the wet nurse: she is treated according to her condition, and another woman nurses the child on a day when she is purged or vomits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.211}.

## The nurse first
The foremost aim in treating children is the regimen of the nurse. If she is judged full of blood she is bled or cupped; if full of a humour it is drained from her; and if her bowels need holding or loosening, a vapour needs keeping from the head, the organs of breathing need mending or a bad temperament needs changing, she is treated with the suitable things taken by mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.211}.

## The day of her purge or vomit
If she is treated with a purge, or one comes on of itself excessively, or if she is treated with a vomit, or a strong vomit comes of itself, it is better that another woman nurse the child that day. Ibn Sina then turns to particular diseases of children {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.211}. After the pustules and thrush he says again that the best of all this is to put right the nurse's food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.217}.
