## Summary
Parallax is the difference between a body's position as seen from the earth's surface and its true position as seen from its centre; it affects the moon and the planets near the earth. Al-Farghani calls it ikhtilaf al-manzar in chapter twenty-seven {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 29}.

## The moon
The moon's greatest displacement in longitude is about one degree and four minutes, gathered for it at its farthest and nearest orbits according to its position {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 27}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 26}. The displacement grows at the horizon; he gives it at the horizon as one degree forty-four minutes for the moon's nearest orbit and fifty-four minutes for the farthest (doubtful OCR reading) {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 28}.

## The sun and the planets
For the sun al-Farghani estimates the displacement at three minutes at its greatest, reckoned by measurement as an approximation. No significant parallax is felt for the superior planets above the sun, while Venus and Mercury are felt to a small degree {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 28}.

## Direction
A body is displaced in longitude toward the direction of its sphere, and in latitude north or south according to its position from the zenith. If the body is on the eastern side of the circle through the pole of the zodiac it appears advanced of its place; if on the western side, delayed {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 27}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 26}.

## Its effect on eclipses
Al-Farghani builds on parallax his explanation of why an eclipse differs between climes: the visible conjunction is not the true conjunction, so the eclipse differs from place to place {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 23}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 22}.
