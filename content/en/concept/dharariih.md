## Nature
An animal resembling the fasafis except that it is red, and the kind found in wheat and generated in it is one of them; the method of storing it and of killing it is not given {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Choice
The strongest in action is the many-coloured, with yellow transverse lines on its wings, resembling the banat wirdan in size; the one of a single colour, not varied, is weak in action {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Temperament
Some said it is excessive in heat, and others said it is hot and dry in the second degree, and the first is sounder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Actions and properties
Hot, acrid, putrefying and burning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Cosmetics
Applied, it removes warts; a cerate (qayruti) is made from it and applied to whiteness of the nails, which benefits; it quickly cuts nails that are due to be removed when they are covered with it as a poultice; with vinegar as an application it removes bahaq and baras; and applied with mustard it makes hair grow; the preparations are not given {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Swellings and pustules
It is applied to cancerous swellings and dissolves them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Ulcers
It is applied to scab and qawabi {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Ocular organs
It is said to remove pterygium greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Excretory organs
A little of it is strongly urine-promoting, so that it benefits dropsy, and a little of it also helps the urine-promoting drugs without harm; the book records a saying that it ulcerates the bladder and Galen's explanation that it turns the sharp matter toward the bladder together with a property in it; the rest of the wording at this place is unclear and is not given {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It promotes menses; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}

## Poisons
Some claim that the wings and legs of dharariih are an antidote to it if drunk afterward; and it is said that one who drank of it swelled in his body, his urine became blood, and it then killed him that day; a report attributed to the book, without quantities or any method {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.720}
