## Overview
Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn holds that people were created equal in fitra and differed only in social life: Islam set obligations on a basis of parity — no privilege of noble over commoner or king over subject, and piety alone alters neither limits nor rights {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 58} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 59}. Brotherhood in religion forbids distinction in rights; Abu Bakr and 'Umar addressed the ruled on the principle that the strong is weak until right is taken from him and the weak strong until he is given justice {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 60} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 61}. The judge does not rule for himself; 'Umar litigated against a commoner; retaliation demands equality.
