## Summary
In the fifth chapter Ibn Sina divides the psychic faculty into perceptive and motive, and the perceptive into outer, the senses, and inner, and says the outer senses are five for some and eight for others {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.99-100}.

## The two faculties
The psychic faculty comprises two powers that are like its genus: one perceptive, one motive, and the perceptive is a power perceiving outwardly and a power perceiving inwardly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.99-100}.

## Five senses or eight
The outwardly perceiving is the power of sense, which is like a genus for five powers according to some and for eight according to others. Taken as five they are the powers of sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch. Taken as eight, the reason is that most of the verifiers hold touch to be many powers, indeed four, assigning each of the four kinds of touchable things its own power, though they share the sensing organ, as taste and touch share the tongue and sight and touch the eye; the verification of this is for the philosopher {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.100}.
