## Definition
Ihsan (ihsan in this sense: marital status as a condition of the penalties) means in the lexicon the original sense of restraint, and among its other meanings chastity, marriage and freedom. Its technical sense varies with its two kinds: ihsan for stoning (for unlawful sex) and ihsan for slander. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1871}
## Its legal character
The chief condition of ihsan for stoning for adultery is marriage, which is subject to the five legal qualifications; the chief condition of ihsan for slander is chastity, which is required by law. The Qur'an and the hadith contain many references, such as the verse in al-Nur (33) enjoining chastity on those who cannot marry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1871}
## Kinds
There are two. Ihsan for stoning is a set of conditions which, if all are present in the adulterer, makes his punishment stoning; ihsan is the state that the joining of these conditions creates, there are eight, and each is a cause of stoning. Ihsan for slander is the joining of qualities in the one slandered that make the slanderer liable to flogging; the qualities differ with the form of the slander: an accusation of adultery, or a denial of lineage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1872}
## The wisdom of the requirement
Ihsan for stoning requires that the adulterer be free, sane, adult, Muslim, and have married a woman in a valid marriage and had intercourse with her while both were in the state of ihsan. Sanity and puberty are conditions of liability to punishment, since there is no address without them; the other conditions are required so that the wrong is complete through the completeness of the blessing, since ingratitude for blessing is graver when blessings are many; these are great blessings, so stoning is tied to them. The Shari'a did not link stoning to rank or knowledge, because it did not so provide, and setting a measure by opinion is impossible. Chastity is required in ihsan for slander because one who is not chaste is not harmed by being accused of adultery, since it would be to establish what is already established; if he suffers another shame, that is truth, and the penalty for slander is for lying, not truth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1872}
## Conditions for stoning
The jurists agree on some conditions and differ on others. Puberty and sanity are conditions of the basic liability and must be present in the ihsan and in the one without ihsan at the time of the offence. If intercourse was done by a child or a madman, and he came to puberty or sanity afterwards, the earlier act does not make him ihsan; he is then flogged as one without ihsan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1872} Some Shafi'i jurists, a weaker view in the school, say the one who had intercourse becomes ihsan by intercourse before puberty or during madness, on the ground that the intercourse was lawful. The majority argue that stoning is the penalty of the one who has been married, and if the intercourse before puberty counted, a child or madman would be stoned, which no one holds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1873}
The Malikis, the sounder Shafi'i view and a Hanbali view say it suffices that the conditions be present in one of the spouses, whatever the other's state; the Malikis, however, count a wife as ihsan only if her husband was adult. The Hanafis, the opposite of the sounder Shafi'i view, require puberty and sanity in both parties at intercourse for either to be ihsan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1873}
Third, intercourse in a valid marriage. Ihsan requires intercourse in a valid marriage, in the front passage, since the state of being a previously married woman is attained by intercourse there; contract without intercourse does not make ihsan, nor does seclusion, nor intercourse short of the passage, nor intercourse by the back. The intercourse counted is penetration in the front passage that requires a bath, whether or not there was emission. Intercourse outside marriage, such as fornication or intercourse by mistake, makes no one ihsan by agreement. The marriage must be valid; if it was void, the majority say intercourse in it does not make ihsan, since it is intercourse in what is not owned, like intercourse by mistake. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1873} Intercourse forbidden by the law, such as during menses or ihram, does not make ihsan even in a valid marriage. The Malikis add that the valid marriage must be binding, so that if a spouse has a defect or deception that gives an option of annulment, ihsan is not established. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1873} Abu Thawr says intercourse in a void marriage makes ihsan, as reported from al-Layth and al-Awza'i, since void and valid marriages are alike in most rulings, like the dower, the forbidding of stepdaughters and mothers, and the attribution of the child; so also for ihsan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1873}
Branches of the front-passage condition: intercourse by a eunuch who cannot have intercourse, or by an impotent or castrated man, does not make the woman ihsan; but if she bears a child whose lineage is established from the husband, a eunuch or impotent man does make her ihsan, since establishing lineage is a ruling that intercourse occurred. The castrated man, in most scholars' view, does not make his wife ihsan for want of the organ; Zufar disagreed, since establishing lineage from a castrated man makes the wife ihsan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1873} Intercourse with a woman with a closed passage does not make her ihsan, since intercourse is impossible with it closed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1874}
Fourth, freedom. A slave is not ihsan even if he is a mukatab, partly freed, or a mother of a child by her master, since he is half a free person, and stoning has no half; to impose it wholly would contradict the text and agreed consensus. The Qur'an sets half the penalty for slave women against that of free married women, in the chapter of al-Nisa (25). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1874} Abu Thawr disagreed and held slaves are ihsan and are stoned if they commit adultery; al-Awza'i is reported to hold a slave married to a free woman is ihsan, but not if his wife is a slave. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1874} The jurists hold that a slave who is freed together with his slave wife, and then has intercourse with her after freedom, becomes ihsan, whether or not they knew of the freedom; and if a free man marries a slave woman, or a free woman marries a slave, there is no ihsan unless one has intercourse after freedom. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1874}
