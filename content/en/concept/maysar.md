## Overview
Maysar: the Jahiliyya's gambling by casting arrows over the shares of a slaughtered camel; the Qur'an forbade it with wine, counting it a great sin.

## The story
Among their games was gambling with the azlam over the slaughtered camel in dividing its shares: they cast the arrows — whoever drew a share took it, and whoever drew none paid the camel's price {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## In the Qur'an
The Prophet was asked about wine and maysar, and God sent down: "Say: in them is great sin and benefits for people; their sin is greater than their benefit" ([[passage/quran-2-219|al-Baqara 219]]), then explicitly forbade them in Surat al-Ma'ida among Satan's works {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
Maysar is a game of gambling with arrows over a camel's meat; God forbade it because it brings enmity and loss to people.
