## Summary
The codices the Companions wrote by Uthman's order were bare of dots and vowel marks, so reliance was on memory and not on the script alone; Ibn al-Jazari says they were stripped 'so that they could carry what is soundly transmitted and established in recitation from the Prophet'. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 17} When tongues changed with non-Arabs entering Islam, foreignness began to affect the language of the Qur'an, and the need arose for marks that show the vowels; this is the first 'dotting', dotting for vowels. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330}

## Abu al-Aswad's story
Khalid al-Sabt relates, with 'it is said', that [[person/abu-al-aswad-al-duali|Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali]] heard a reader recite [[passage/quran-9-3|al-Tawba 9:3]] with a genitive ending on the final word, the one meaning 'his messenger', which reverses the sense; the glaring error alarmed him and he said: God's face is too exalted to disown His messenger. He went to [[person/ziyad-ibn-abi-sufyan|Ziyad]], governor of Basra, and said: I have granted what you asked. Ziyad had asked him to make marks by which people would know God's Book, and he had been slow to answer until this alarmed him. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330} See [[event/abu-al-aswad-pointing-the-mushaf|Abu al-Aswad's pointing of the codex]].

## What he laid down
He made the sign of fatha a dot above the letter, of kasra a dot below it, of damma a dot between the letter's parts and of sukun two dots. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330} Al-Suyuti says vowelling in the first age was by dots: the fatha a dot at the head of the letter, the damma at its end and the kasra under its head, which is the way al-Dani went. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249} Al-Sabt relates that people followed his path and then added and invented: they made a sign like a bow for the doubled letter and a hook above, below or inside for the connecting alif, according to the vowel before it. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331}

## Who was first?
Al-Suyuti says people differ about dotting and vowelling the codex: it is said the first was Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali by order of Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, or al-Hasan al-Basri and Yahya ibn Ya'mar, or Nasr ibn Asim al-Laythi. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} Al-Sabt distinguishes: the nearest account is that Abu al-Aswad's dotting was dotting for vowels, that is vowelling, with dotting of letters coming after; he says this is a matter of probable judgement and nothing in hand makes any of these views certain, and notes that many of the reports of the dotting of codices go back to Ziyad ibn Abihi's governorship of Basra (44-53 AH), and some to the time of Ubaydallah ibn Ziyad (d. 67 AH). {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 329} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331} See also [[question/who-first-pointed-the-mushaf|Who first pointed and vowelled the codex?]]

## What replaced it
When dotting of letters came in under Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, the caliph saw that keeping the vowel signs as dots would collide with the dots of letters, and replaced them with the vowel signs known today. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331} See [[concept/naqt-al-ijam|dotting of letters]] and [[concept/shakl-al-khalil|al-Khalil's vowel signs]].
