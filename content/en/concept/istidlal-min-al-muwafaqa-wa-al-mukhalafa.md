## What agrees and what disagrees
What agrees and disagrees is considered either in a state in which the person does not deny his health, where what agrees is what resembles his temperament, so his temperament is known from it, and what disagrees is the opposite of his temperament; or in a state of departure from health and change of temperament, where the judgement is by the opposite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.16-17}

He says health is not on one temperament in all bodies, and the health of one body may come from a temperament that would bring disease to another body if it had it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17} One must consider what opposes it at both ends, since both excesses oppose and harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17}

The brain with a hot ill-temperament benefits from cool breezes, cold applications and cold smells, pleasant or foul, and from ease and rest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17} The one with a cold ill-temperament benefits from the opposite: hot air, hot smells, the pleasant and also the foul ones that dissolve and warm, exercises and movements {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17} The one with a moist ill-temperament benefits from what is evacuated and expelled from it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17}

Not all agreements and disagreements are of qualities; some are taken from postures and movements, as the sufferer of the complaint known as the egg prefers lying on his back to all other positions in which he lies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.18}

## Quickness of reaction
One who heats quickly indicates a heat of temperament, and likewise one who cools quickly; one who dries quickly may do so from little moisture or from the heat of his temperament {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17} The difference between them is that the first has with him the other signs of dryness of the brain, such as sleeplessness, while the second suffers dryness at times, on violent movement or severe heat, and has at other times no sign of dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17}

One who moistens quickly may do so from the heat of his substance or from its cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.17-18} If from heat, the signs of heat are there, and the moistening follows an excessive heat that fell on the brain and drew moistures to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17} If from cold of temperament, the moisture does not arise at once but over days {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.17}

Ibn Sina notes that quickness of reaction is not to be judged by weakness of the natural powers, above all in moistening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.18}
