## Summary
Ibn Sina notes that the traveller is cut off from habits he kept at home and meets weariness and pain, so he must look to food and fatigue: his food is of good substance and moderate quantity, he does not ride full but puts off the meal until the halt, does not set out full of blood or other matter, and trains gradually in exercise, wakefulness and hunger before they befall him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.261}.

## Food and fatigue
What the traveller most needs to attend to is food and fatigue: he improves his food and makes it of good substance and moderate, not much, so that digestion is good and residues do not gather in his vessels, and manages his fatigue as set out in the chapter on fatigue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.261}.

## Before the journey and in the saddle
He does not ride full lest his food spoil and he need to drink water, which adds to sloshing and vomiting; he puts off the meal until the halt, and if he must he takes a small amount by way of distraction; he does not travel full of blood or other matter but clears his body first; and if he is surfeited he fasts, sleeps and disperses the indigestion before setting out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.261}.
