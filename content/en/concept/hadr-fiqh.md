## Summary
Hadr means waste or nullity. A hadr soul is one for which there is neither retaliation, diya nor expiation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16451}

## Who may be killed with impunity
A human life is protected in principle. The apostate's blood is wasted by agreement: the imam or his deputy executes him, and anyone else who kills him without authority owes neither retaliation nor diya, though he is disciplined. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16452}
The blood of a heretic whose innovation is takfiri is wasted, and so is that of a belligerent non-Muslim. Non-combatants such as women, children, the old and monks may not be killed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16453}
Most jurists hold the blood of a married adulterer wasted; the Hanafis require that the killing follow a judgment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16453} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16454}

## A change in the wounded person's status
If someone was wounded and his status changed between protected and wasted before he died, most jurists hold the earlier wound unliable; Ibn al-Qasim of the Malikis holds a diya due. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16454} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16455}
If a Muslim shot a hadr person who converted before the arrow struck, the majority hold no retaliation is due; the Shafi'is hold, in the sounder view, a lighter diya paid by the kin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16455} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16456}

## Execution, water and self-killing
For the Shafi'is the retaliation is claimed by the Muslim next of kin; for the Hanbalis it is the imam who executes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16457}
A hadr person need not be given water he needs, and the Shafi'is and Hanbalis permit a compelled person to kill him to save his own life when no other food is found. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16457} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16458}
One whose blood was wasted by a crime who kills himself gets no expiation from that act. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16458}
