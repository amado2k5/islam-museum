## Summary
"Power and influence" is a two-part lecture that [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gives in [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]]. He says he had given it only to the young people of this academy and to no one else. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:02:00} When he studied management, he says, influence was not taught. It was thought negative, intangible and broadly unethical, until management scholars found that organisations could not be understood without it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:03:00} He presents the subject so that his trainees will recognise these tactics and not fall victim to them. Ethics, he says, must govern any use of them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:25:30} The laws and strategies below are his teaching.

## Authority, responsibility and influence
He separates three terms. Authority is "the right given to the official to act and be obeyed". Responsibility is "the commitment to achieve the goals". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:04:00} Influence differs from both because it belongs to the person, not the post. When an official moves to another post, his authority stays behind for whoever fills it, but his influence goes with him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:05:00} He defines influence as the ability to bring about a change that is not supposed to happen, without being punished for it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:06:00} It can come from family, knowledge or money. A young man or woman with none of these can still acquire it, and he holds that it must be learned and can be trained. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:09:00} Some influence is good and some bad, he says, and most of what is seen is bad, but not all of it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:09:00}

## Five general laws
1. Understand your organisation: its plan, where it stands and where it is going, and its centres of influence. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:12:30}
2. Be flexible: give up some things for bigger ones. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:14:00}
3. Seek "win-win". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:14:00}
4. Keep to ethics. He says it is a great illusion that life runs only on cheating and lies, and that a person bound by ethics can achieve a great deal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:15:00}
5. Think before you act and before you speak, and learn silence. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:15:30}

He weighs the effects of influence tactics on both sides. They can breed power struggles, lies and backbiting. Understanding them eases negotiation, wins the respect of shrewd counterparts, blocks destructive behaviour and sharpens decisions. He says everything finally depends on intention. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:24:00} On mutual respect between the shrewd he tells how [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]], hearing of a cunning Byzantine commander called al-Artabun, sent [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] against him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:22:00}

## Strategies
He sets the trainees five test cases, such as public criticism in meetings, a blocked promotion and rumours, and then gives long-term strategies. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:38:30} They are: a positive spirit without complaint; learning negotiation and persuasion; patience; deep social relationships with superiors outside work, which leave a person "immune"; a wide network inside and outside the organisation; and long-term goals that keep one from reacting to small things. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:40:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:41:30} Among his tactical advice: be visible; market yourself and your ideas; gather information, because "information is power"; and never truly lose your temper, because "whoever loses his nerve loses his control". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep19 00:46:00}

In the second part he says professionals "play billiards" in relationships, reaching a decision-maker through whoever he listens to. He also says real influence does not always match rank on the organisation chart. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:04:30} He advises making one's abilities known to those who decide rather than waiting for one's work to speak. He illustrates this with a story about [[person/abd-al-rahman-al-dakhil|Abd al-Rahman al-Dakhil]] and a young soldier who congratulated him without ceremony after a victory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:06:30}

## Situations to refuse, and mistakes
He lists situations to refuse: backing a superior one knows to be wrong; taking the blame for others; even listening to backbiting and talebearing; acting against one's ethics, because "the end does not justify the means"; revealing a secret; letting others take one's ideas; and promoting a project one believes will fail. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:16:00} On keeping secrets he tells how [[person/aisha|Aisha]] kept the Prophet's preparation for the march on Mecca from her own father. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:14:00} After a mistake the best course, he says, is to admit it and do something positive. "Sorry is a golden word." The mistake should be corrected quickly, anyone harmed compensated, and the matter not reopened. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:22:30}

## Malicious tactics
He then describes malicious tactics, he says, so that they are neither fallen into nor practised. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:25:30} Against blackmail he advises disclosing one's own secret rather than conceding anything. Against a deliberately hostile climate he says to leave. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:37:00} His general rules of resistance include allies, analysis before action, admitting one's weaknesses up front, and multiplying alternatives, because "the more alternatives, the better the decision". He adds that no end justifies lying or betrayal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:41:30} On close partnerships he gives his own rule: he takes a partner only after ten years of relationship. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:51:00} He closes by saying this knowledge is absent among Islamic activists in particular, and that he hopes for a climate that is clean but open, in which no one is deceived. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep20 00:54:00}
