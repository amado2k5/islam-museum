## Nature
Its seed resembles the seed of celery, near the wild kind in power but weaker {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Temperament
The wild is stronger in heat and dryness and nearer the third degree, while the heat of the garden kind is in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Actions and properties
It opens obstructions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Ocular organs
It sharpens sight, especially its gum, and benefits the beginning of the water in the eye and at its descent; Hippocrates claimed that vermin graze on the fresh seed of razyanaj to strengthen their sight, and that vipers and snakes rub their eyes against it when they come out of their shelter after the winter, to give light to the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Organs of the chest
Its fresh form increases milk, especially the garden kind with tarunjabin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Given with cold water it benefits nausea and burning of the stomach; its digestion is slow and its nourishment very poor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Excretory organs
The wild especially breaks the stone, and in the wild and the river kinds there is benefit for the kidney and bladder; it benefits dribbling of urine, especially the wild kind; and when its root is eaten with its seed it binds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it promotes urine and menses and that it cleanses the woman after childbirth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Fevers
It benefits chronic fevers, given with cold water, and so benefits the nausea in fevers and the burning of the stomach in them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}

## Poisons and bites
Its decoction with the drink (sharab) benefits the bites of vermin, and its root is pounded and smeared on the bite of the rabid dog and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.664}
