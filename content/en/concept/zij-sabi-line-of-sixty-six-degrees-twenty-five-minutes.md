## Summary
Al-Battani says that on lines farther from the equator than the obliquity the Sun never reaches the zenith, and the variation of declination and of the length of day grows until the line at 66 degrees 25 minutes, the amount that the whole obliquity falls short of ninety. On that one line the day reaches a full twenty-four hours at the summer solstice and the night as much at the winter one, and the shadows of upright gnomons turn to every side of the horizon. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.27}.

## A distance of 66 degrees 25 minutes
He explains the line as what is left of ninety once the whole obliquity is taken away: the Sun at the head of Cancer on that line does not set for a whole day and night, and at the head of Capricorn does not rise for a whole day and night. This agrees with the greatest declination of 23 degrees 35 minutes he fixed in chapter 4, since the two add up to ninety. See [[concept/zij-sabi-battani-obliquity-observation-at-raqqa|his observation of the obliquity at Raqqa]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.27}.

## The first line where the shadow goes round the horizon
This line, he says, is the first on which upright shadows lean to every side of the horizon, and beyond it to the north the noon is unbounded. On this line alone the zodiac becomes the horizon itself when the vernal equinox point rises from it: the head of Cancer rises from the north point and the head of Aries stands on the eastern horizon, so when the Sun passes the summer solstice point it does not set for a day and night but circles the horizon at varying distances until it returns to the north point, and that day has no night at all. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.27}.
