## Summary
The purely serving natural faculties are four and serve the nutritive: the attractive, retentive, digestive and expulsive, each with its work upon food and its tool in the fibres of the organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.96}.

## Attractive and retentive
The attractive was made to draw what is useful, and does so by the fibre of the organ it is in, running lengthwise. The retentive was made to hold what is useful while the altering faculty works on it, and does so by oblique fibre, which the transverse may sometimes help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.96}.

## Digestive
The digestive turns what the attractive drew and the retentive held into a consistency ready for the altering faculty to act on and a temperament fit to become food in actuality; this, its work on what is useful, is called digestion. On the residues it either turns them, if possible, to that state, or eases their way to be pushed out of the organ that holds them by the expulsive, thinning their consistency if thickness is what blocks them, thickening it if thinness is, or cutting it if stickiness is; this act is called ripening, and digestion and ripening are sometimes used as synonyms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.96}.

## Expulsive
The expulsive pushes out the residue of food that is unfit to nourish, or exceeds the sufficient amount, or is no longer needed, or has been drained after being used for its intended purpose, like urine. It pushes these residues out by ways and outlets prepared for them; where none are prepared it pushes them from the nobler organ to the baser and from the harder to the softer. When the direction of the push is the direction the residue inclines to, it does not turn it away from that direction if it can avoid it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.96}.
