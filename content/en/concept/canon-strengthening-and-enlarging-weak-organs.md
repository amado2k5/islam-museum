## Summary
In the eleventh chapter Ibn Sina shows that weak and small organs are strengthened and enlarged by nourishment in those still growing, and by moderate rubbing, the organ's own exercise, a coating of pitch and holding the breath in the old, takes the thin-shinned as his example, and warns of swelling, varicose veins and elephantiasis once widening of the vessels appears {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.242}.

## The method
Weak and small organs gain strength and size: in one still in the age of growth by nourishment, and in the old by moderate rubbing and the constant exercise that belongs to the organ, followed by a coating of pitch, with holding of the breath counted in this practice, above all when the organ lies near the chest and lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.242}.

## The shin and the chest
For one who is thin in the shins, Ibn Sina has him hold the breath a little, rub moderately and coat the leg with pitch; on the second day the rubbing stays as it was and the exercise is increased, and on the third likewise, unless a sign of widening vessels and a pouring in of matter appears, when swelling and the disorder of fullness proper to the organ are feared, as varicose veins and elephantiasis are here. If any of this shows, exercise and rubbing are reduced, indeed stopped; he lies down, the limb is raised and rubbed in reverse, from its edge toward its root. For an organ near the breathing organs, such as the chest, what lies below is bound with a bandage of middling tightness, then the exercises of the arms, strong holding of the breath, shouting, a loud voice and gentle rubbing are used. The full detail is promised for the book on adornment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.242}.
