## Nature
A herb with a seed like the seed of the myrtle or near it, but dustier than it, its kernel resembling peeled lentil in colour and taste, with a slight sweetness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Temperament
Hot in the first degree, moist and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Wounds and ulcers
It heals wounds, and stops the spread of the malignant ulcer when poulticed with it together with vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Swellings and pustules
It dissolves phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Cosmetics
Its decoction blackens the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that the decoction of its branches promotes urine and menses and brings out the foetus, and calms the itching that arises in the private parts when washed with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}
