## A general discourse on breathing
Some kinds of breath have names of their own — the cut breath, the doubled breath, the upright breath, the strangling breath, and the forced breath with pauses such as comes in the stroke. The harms befalling the breath's tools and so entering a harm into the breath are either in the breath's organs — the larynx, the lung, the windpipe, the rough vessels, the arteries, the diaphragm and the chest muscles, for the harm may be in the chest itself when it is narrow and small; or in their sources, the brain and the cord, the diaphragm's origin, for it sprouts mostly from the fourth pair of the cord's nerves with a branch of the fifth and sixth joining; or in what shares by neighbourhood, as the stomach, the liver, the womb ⁦(the womb named among the neighbours on the book's wording — a labelled line set before the founder)⁩, the bowels and the rest of the entrails. Those harms are either a weakening bad temperament — hot, cold, moist or dry, simple or with matter of a retained humour or one poured in — or an instrumental disease: palsy, convulsion, a loosened join of cracking, rotting, ulceration or corrosion, or a cold, hot or hard tumour, or a pain. The breath is a strong indicator, running the pulse's course, once the wont is regarded in it as the ordinary natural is regarded in the pulse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.307}
