## From the brain itself
As for confusion of the mind and raving among these, that which is by reason of the brain itself is either black bile, or hot inflamed blood, or yellow bile, or red bile, or simple heat, or hot vapour, and the burden in such cases is light; or dryness from sleeplessness or thought having come before, or other things that dry, so that the brain lacks the matter of the innate spirit by whose like the way of reason can be kept {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.97}

## From another organ
That which is by reason of another organ or the body, that organ being such as the stomach or its mouth or the hypochondria, or the whole body as in fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.97} All of that is either from a simple quality conveyed to it, as it rises from the finger of the foot and from the hand when they swell, and from organs of spoiled temperament that are swollen, or from a hot vapour from bile or from phlegm that has putrefied and sharpened {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.97} The safest disturbance of the intellect is that with laughter and that with stillness, and the worst is that with agitation, weariness and boldness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.97}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina names the womb in the list of organs from which confusion of the mind may come, with the stomach and its mouth, the hypochondria and the whole body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.97}
