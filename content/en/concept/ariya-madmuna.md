## Summary
A juristic and legal concept in Islamic law concerning the guarantee of borrowed property; the Prophet established it before the [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn expedition]] when he borrowed weapons from [[person/safwan-ibn-umayya|Safwan ibn Umayya]] {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/84}.

## The historical origin
When the Prophet resolved to set out for Hunayn, word reached him that Safwan ibn Umayya had weapons and mail; he sent asking for them: "Abu Umayya, lend us these weapons of yours to meet our enemy tomorrow." Safwan said: "By force, Muhammad?" He said: "Rather a guaranteed loan until we return it to you." So he lent him a hundred coats of mail with their weapons {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/84}.

This incident became a settled foundation for Muslim jurists in distinguishing seizure from loan, and in the obligation to guarantee what is borrowed if it is lost or damaged {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/84}.

## For young visitors
The Prophet asked a man in Mecca for weapons and armour as a loan, and assured him he was not taking them by force but would return them safe or compensate him — teaching people honesty and keeping others' rights.
