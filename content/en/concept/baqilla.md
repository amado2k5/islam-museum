## Nature and selection
There is the known kind, and Egyptian, Nabataean and Indian kinds. The Nabataean is the most astringent, the Egyptian is moister and less nourishing, and the fresh bean has more surplus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}. The footnote names a reference work only; its claims are not used.

## Temperament and actions
Close to moderate, leaning more to cold and dryness; it has a surplus moisture, especially when fresh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

## Uses by organ
Selection: the best is fat, white and not worm-eaten; the worst is the fresh. It is improved by long soaking, good cooking, and eaten with pepper, salt, galbanum, thyme and similar, with fats. The Indian kind is used only in emetic and purgative compounds at a set weight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Actions: it cleanses a little and bloats very much, unless well cooked. Roasted, it bloats little but is slower to digest; boiled in its husk, it bloats a lot. The Nabataean is the most astringent; the Egyptian is the most binding and cleansing, produces soft flesh and thick humours; Hippocrates praised its nourishment and the health kept on it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Properties: the book says that a hen fed on it sees confused dreams, and that it causes itching, especially fresh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Adornment: clears white patches on the face, especially with its husk, and freckles, and improves the complexion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Swellings and pustules: a poultice with wine on a swollen testicle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Wounds and ulcers: helps ulcers of the muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Joints: helps cramp of the muscles; its decoction with pig fat is applied to gout {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.412}.

Head: causes headache, harmful to all who suffer headache; the green part inside the Egyptian kind, crushed and mixed with rose oil and dropped in the ear, helps earache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.412}.

Eyes: with honey and fenugreek, a poultice for an eye's redness and blinking; with frankincense, dry rose and egg white, a poultice for protrusion of the eyeball {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.412}.

Breath and chest: good for the chest, for spitting of blood and for cough; mixed with honey and fenugreek flour it helps swellings of the throat and tonsils, and a poultice of it is good for breast swelling and curdling of milk in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.412}.

Digestion: hard to digest, not slow to pass; it causes blockage; the bean boiled in vinegar in its husk stops vomiting, and the Indian kind provokes vomiting strongly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.412}.

Excretory organs: boiled in vinegar and water it helps chronic diarrhoea and abrasion, especially the Nabataean; a poultice is good for swelling of the testicles, especially boiled with wine; the Indian kind, in the smallest measure, loosens and purges {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.412}.
