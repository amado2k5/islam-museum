In the lexicon wisaya means to commission someone to act after one's death; one can say he bequeathed something to him, or made him his executor. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17216} Technically it is the commission to act after death. It differs from the bequest, which is a gift attached to the time after death made by way of a voluntary act; and from agency, which operates in life, while wisaya is after death; authority is the broader term. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17216}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17217} Its ruling for the testator: it is obligatory when it is for returning wrongful takings and unknown debts, or for young children when their loss is feared; it is recommended for paying a known debt, carrying out bequests and caring for children not at risk of loss. The appointee may accept if he is able and trusts himself. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17217} There are two kinds of executor: the judge's executor, appointed by the judge to look after a minor's money, and the testator's executor, chosen by the father, grandfather or one holding their right. The Hanafis list differences between them; for example, the testator's executor may, on Abu Hanifa's view, sell to himself where there is clear benefit, which the judge's executor may not do. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17218} The judge appoints an executor in several cases named by the Hanafis, including a debt owed to or by the deceased, carrying out a will, a minor child, and a minor or absent heir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17218}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17219} If an executor cannot carry out his duties, the Hanafis, Hanbalis and Shafi'is let the judge add another to help him where the executor was appointed by the father or grandfather; if he proves dishonest or dissolute, he is removed in the view of the four schools, and the judge appoints another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17219}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17220} An executor is either a volunteer, which is a pure trust, or paid: by contract, a binding hire that can be paid from the estate, or without contract, a reward whose amount is set by the work done. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17220} Wisaya has four pillars: the executor, the testator, the thing entrusted and the formula. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17221} The executor must be sane, a Muslim where the ward is Muslim, and able to act; the disputed cases are a minor, a woman, a blind man, a dishonest person, a slave and an unbeliever. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17221}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17224} Under the executor's rank for a minor's guardianship, the Hanafis put the father, then his executor, then the grandfather, and so on; the Malikis and Hanbalis put the father, his executor, then the judge; the Shafi'is do not allow a father to appoint an executor while the grandfather is present. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17230}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17231} The testator must be sane and of age, free according to the majority, and in the view of the Malikis and Hanbalis of sound judgement; the Shafi'is require him to be just, and he must hold authority over the ward. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17231}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17234} The thing entrusted must be a known act the testator may do, such as paying debts or looking after a child. The formula requires offer and acceptance; a dumb person's clear sign suffices. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17234}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17235} Acceptance or refusal may be made in the testator's lifetime according to the detailed views, and the Shafi'is in the preferred view only after death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17236} Making the appointment conditional or time-limited is permitted by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17237} A full account of the executor's duties follows in the next part of this headword.
