## The reference to the general rules
Ibn Sina says this chapter and the one before it are like the conclusion from the principles he gave for inference about the state of the head, and that these signs should be remembered so that they need not be repeated in each chapter on the diseases of the regions of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.24-25} If he repeats them in a chapter it is to help in knowing how to go back to these general rules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25}

## The signs
The sign of a hot ill-temperament without matter: inflammation with no heaviness, sleeplessness and restlessness in movements, confusion in imaginings, quickness to anger, redness of the eye, benefit from coolers, and warmers having been used or applied before {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25}

The sign of a cold ill-temperament without matter: cold felt with no heaviness, laziness, torpor, whiteness of the colour of face and eye, a lessening of imaginings, a leaning to cowardice, benefit from warmers and harm from coolers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25}

The sign of a dry ill-temperament without matter: lightness, earlier evacuations, dryness of the nostrils and prevailing sleeplessness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25}

The sign of a moist ill-temperament without matter: laziness and torpor with little heaviness and little flow of what flows, or a moderate one, excessive forgetfulness and prevailing sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25}

In the compound temperaments that are without matter the signs of the two temperaments mix; prevalence of heat with dryness is inferred from sleeplessness and mixing of reason, prevalence of cold with it from a state like the disease known as congealing, to which it may lead {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25} Prevalence of moisture with heat is inferred from a prevailing sleep that is not deeply comatose, and prevalence of cold with moisture from comatose sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.25}
