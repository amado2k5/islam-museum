## Nature and selection
A well-known vegetable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}. The fresh is the safer; the old is poor, and its taste and nature are like those of the fried {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}.

## Temperament and actions
In the view of Ibn Masarjawayh it is cold, but the correct view is that its dominant power is heat and dryness in the second degree, because of its bitterness and pungency {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}. It produces black bile and obstruction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it spoils the colour, darkens the skin, and yellows the colour; the small aubergine is all husk, and it brings on freckles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}. Swellings and pustules: it produces cancers and hardness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}. Head: it produces headache and obstruction and causes mouth ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}. Food organs: it produces obstruction of the liver and spleen, except when cooked in vinegar, which sometimes opens the obstruction of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}. Excretory organs: it produces piles, but the powder of its dried calyxes, dried in shade, is a useful ointment for piles. The aubergine has no relation to loosening or binding by itself, but cooked in oil it loosens, and cooked in vinegar it binds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.399}.
