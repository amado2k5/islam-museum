## Summary
Wajh in the lexical sense comes from confronting; in a person it runs from the hairline down to the chin, and across between the earlobes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16619}

## Ablution and tayammum
Washing the face is required in ablution, and wiping it is a pillar of tayammum by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16619} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16620}

## The woman's face and gazing
Most jurists hold that a free adult woman's face is not an intimate part; some Hanbalis hold it is. Looking at a woman's face with desire, or where temptation is feared, is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16619} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16620}
Jurists rebuke a young woman who uncovers her face before unrelated men out of fear of temptation; some Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that the men are bound to lower their gaze. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16620} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16621}

## Striking and branding the face
Striking or branding the face is forbidden, in man or beast, by the Prophet's prohibition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16621}

## Covering a pilgrim's face and wiping the face in supplication
The Hanafis and Malikis forbid a pilgrim in ihram to cover his face; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16621} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16622}
The Hanafis and, in the accepted view, the Shafi'is allow wiping the face with the hands after supplication, and the Shafi'is recommend it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16622}
