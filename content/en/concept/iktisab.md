## Definition
Iktisab means seeking livelihood and gaining property in general; the jurists add that it is gaining property by lawful means. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3234} Kasb differs in that iktisab requires effort, while kasb can be had without effort, as through inheritance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3234}

## Ruling
Iktisab is obligatory on a capable person in need, since through it a person meets what is due in upkeep: himself, his wife, his young children, poor parents, and jihad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3235}
Ibn Muflih divides its ruling by the earner's situation: it is a sunna for one who has enough, permitted for more wealth and status, and obligatory for someone without subsistence or with a debt, a vow or an expiation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3235} Al-Mawardi held that seeking earnings only as far as one's needs is the best of the seekers' conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3235}

## Who is not required to earn
- A woman is not required to earn for herself or others; her upkeep, if she is poor, is due from others. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3235}
- A child not fit to earn is not required; fitness includes the physical and mental ability to tell lawful from unlawful, and the Muwatta' carries a report from Uthman on this. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3235}

## Ways of earning
Iktisab requires work, and the work must be lawful; earning by serving wine to drinkers is not allowed. Lowly trades are disliked, with conditions set out under ihtiraf. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3235}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3236}
