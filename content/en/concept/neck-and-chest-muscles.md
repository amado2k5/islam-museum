## Summary
The neck alone has two pairs of muscles, and the chest muscles are expanding, contracting and both, which Ibn Sina counts at eighty-eight in all {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.67-68}.

## The neck
Two pairs move the neck alone, one on the right and one on the left. When one contracts alone the neck is drawn to its side obliquely; when two on one side contract together it leans straight to that side; and when all four act the neck stands upright without leaning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.67}.

## Expanding and contracting
Some muscles of the chest only expand it, such as the diaphragm between the organs of breathing and of nutrition, a pair from the collarbone to the head of the shoulder joined to the first rib, and other pairs joined to the neck, shoulder, back ribs and sternum. Of those that contract it, some contract by themselves, such as a pair stretched under the roots of the upper ribs that tightens and gathers, and a pair at their ends beside the sternum between the xiphoid and the collarbone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.67-68}.

## Between the ribs
The muscles between the ribs both contract and expand, but close study requires that the contracting ones differ from the expanding. Between any two ribs there are in truth four muscles, though they are thought one, for their oblique fibre has four arrangements: some lines within, some covers, and what lies toward the cartilage end of the rib differs from what lies toward the other end. Those placed above expand, those below contract, and the chest muscles total eighty-eight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.68}.
