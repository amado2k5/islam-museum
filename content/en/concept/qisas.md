## Summary
In Sha'rawi's commentary, retaliation (qisas) is the right of the victim's heir, who may pardon or take the blood-money. The hadd penalties are God's right, and no one may waive them. He holds that retaliation preserves two lives, since a killer who knows he will be retaliated against refrains {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2538}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2543}.

## What Sha'rawi says
He links the verse of al-Baqara on retaliation with the verse of al-Nisa on killing, and holds that the law prescribes retaliation to protect people, not to embolden killing. Someone who calls retaliation harsh is answered that a killer who knows he will be killed refrains {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2538}. He separates retaliation from the hadd: retaliation is the heir's right, who may pardon or take the blood-money, while the hadd is God's right and no one can renounce it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2543}.

## Accidental killing
Sha'rawi does not apply retaliation to accidental killing. There the law requires a freed believing slave and blood-money, because the killer did not intend the act {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2543}. This is his view at this point in the commentary; the conditions of retaliation and the rules of the hadd penalties are not yet set out from a named source.
