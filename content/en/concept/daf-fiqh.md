## Summary
In the lexicon daf' means pushing away, delaying, pleading on behalf of another, and returning. As a term it means turning a thing away before it arrives. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8213}
Jurists use it to mean giving or paying, returning, warding off harm, and rebutting a claim to void it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8213}

## Related terms
In the lexicon daf' is close to dar' (repelling) and to radd (returning or refusing), and it stands against raf' (removing a thing after it has arrived). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8214}

## Where the rule appears
In zakat, daf' means giving or paying out, and the detail is in the zakat entry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8214}
In a deposit it means returning the deposit to the depositor or his agent on demand; a depositary who delays until it is lost is liable. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8214}
In repelling an aggressor (sial) it means stopping the attacker from reaching his aim; a defender may repel an attack on life, limb, benefit, honour or property, starting with the least harmful means, and if only killing will do he may kill without retaliation, diya or expiation. A defender who dies is counted a martyr, on the hadith about one killed defending his property. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8215}
In a claim it means rebutting the plaintiff's statement and voiding his claim, for example by the defendant saying the thing is a deposit, pledge or loan, and proving it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8215}

## Repelling is stronger than removing
This is a legal maxim cited by al-Zarkashi in al-Manthur. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8215}
Among its applications, water already used that reaches two qullas does not become pure in one view. A journey before starting the fast permits breaking it, while a journey begun in the middle of a day of Ramadan does not permit it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8216}
The term appears in many fiqh chapters, such as prayer, pilgrimage, salam, hawala, pledge, guarantee, surety, partnership, agency, loan, endowment, bequest, maintenance, crimes, penalties, jihad and jizya, each treated under its own entry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8216}
