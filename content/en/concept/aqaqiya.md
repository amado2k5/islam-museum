## Nature and selection
Aqaqiya is the juice of the qaraz tree, dried; it has a sting that goes with washing, because it is compounded of an earthy astringent substance and a fine substance from which its sting comes, and the sting is removed by washing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. The book reports Dioscorides as saying that the acacia is a thorny tree that grows in Egypt and elsewhere, its thorns and its branches not upright, with a white flower and a white fruit like the lupin in pods {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. The way its juice is extracted is not reproduced here. The best is the sweet-smelling, green leaning to black, heavy and hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}.

## Temperament and actions
The washed kind is cold and drying in the second degree, and the unwashed is cold in the first, with dryness near the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}. It is astringent and stops the flow of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}.

## Uses by organ
It darkens the hair, improves the complexion and helps cracks that come from cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}. It helps in all that was said for al-as, and in whitlow, and with egg white on burns by fire and hot swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}. It prevents flaccidity of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}. It helps sores of the mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}. It strengthens and refines the sight, and only the kind called al-madhri (as printed) suits the eye; it soothes ophthalmia and the redness that befalls it, and enters the drugs for pterygium {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}. It binds the bowel drunk, as an enema and as a poultice, helps abrasion and bloody diarrhoea, and returns the protrusion of the anus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it cuts the flow from the womb, returns the protrusion of the womb and helps the slackness of the anus and the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.347}; reports attributed to the book, without recommendation.
