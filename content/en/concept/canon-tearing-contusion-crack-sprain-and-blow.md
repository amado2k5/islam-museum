## Summary
Ibn Sina treats tearing, a break in continuity deep beneath the skin, and then contusion, crack, sprain, fall and blow, and shows what suits each {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.308-309}.

## Tearing
Tearing is a break in continuity deep beneath the skin, so it is plain that its drugs must be stronger than those for open wounds, and since blood pours into it in quantity it needs a dissolver. The dissolver must not dry too much, lest it dissolve the fine part and turn the coarse part to stone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.

## After the dissolving
When the need for the dissolver is met, a joining drying drug should be used, lest a dirt that hardens become lodged between the joined parts and then putrefy on the slightest cause or be torn away, so that the break returns. If the tear is deeper, the place is scarified so that the drug sinks further {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.

## Contusion and crack
For a slight tear, venesection and contusion may suffice for treating it. A crack, if large, is treated with drying drugs, and if small, like the prick of a needle, its matter is left to nature itself, unless it is poisoned, twisted, very severely dislocated, or has reached a nerve, so that swelling and throbbing are feared from it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.308}.

## Sprain, fall and blow
For a sprain a thin, painless binding suffices, with the sprain drugs laid on it. For a fall and a blow, venesection from the opposite side is needed, with lightening of food, abstaining from meat and the like, and use of the plasters and drinks written for this in the particular books {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.308-309}.
