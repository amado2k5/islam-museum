## Overview
Ibn al-Nadim, in the *Fihrist*, records what reached him about the codices (*masahif*) of the Companions. In the arrangement of the codex of Ubayy ibn Ka'b, as transmitted by al-Fadl ibn Shadhan, there are one hundred and sixteen surahs, among them the two surahs of *al-Khal'* and *al-Hafd*. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim}

In the codex of Ali ibn Abi Talib, as transmitted by Abd Khayr, he collected the Qur'an after the death of the Prophet. Ibn al-Nadim mentions that in his time he saw a codex in the hand of Ali, from which some leaves had fallen, in the possession of Abu Ya'la Hamza al-Husayni. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim}

He also transmits the words of Ata' ibn Yasar on the number of surahs, verses, and words. These are reports that Ibn al-Nadim transmits, and their verification needs a further source. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim}

## Private codices and what Uthman did with them
A number of Companions had codices of their own, in which they sometimes wrote what is not Qur'an, such as the explanation of a meaning, a note on what abrogates and is abrogated, or a supplication; al-Sabt names the codices of Ibn Mas'ud, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, A'isha, Ali and Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, and says that after Uthman's unification they came to be washed or burned. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 239} A note in the edition of the Itqan says that the supplication of the Qunut that Ubayy wrote in his codex, if that is sound, does not mean he held it to be Qur'an, being among what some Companions added to their private codices of explanation or supplication before Uthman ordered burned whatever was not the Imam codex. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 1465} From the codices of the Mothers of the Believers: Muwatta 367 and 368, [[passage/muwatta-1-367|A'isha's codex]] and [[passage/muwatta-1-368|Hafsa's codex]].
