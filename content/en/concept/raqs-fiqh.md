## Summary
Raqs (dancing) in the lexicon is quick movement and agitation; technically it is swaying, bowing and rising in measured motions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8855} Those who allow it cite the hadith of the Abyssinians who played with their spears in the Prophet's mosque; the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis dislike it as baseness, folly and a loss of manly dignity, and Ibn Taymiyya forbids making it a worship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8855} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8856} It is forbidden when accompanied by something unlawful, such as drinking or exposing the private parts, and the Shafi'is allow it only where it is fitting and free of affectation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8856} The testimony of a dancer is rejected for loss of manly dignity; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis count habitual and frequent dancing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8856} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8857} Hiring someone to dance follows its ruling: if the dance is forbidden the hire is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8857}
