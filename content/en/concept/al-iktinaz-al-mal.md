## Overview
Hoarding money, according to Zaydan Qa'dat, is withholding funds from circulation and productive investment, and it is one of the causes of the economic problem {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}.

## Its Form
The author holds that hoarding funds and failing to put them into production and commodity development deprives the state of the capacity for useful, productive investment, forcing it to import from abroad {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}.

## Its Relation to Monopoly
The author links hoarding with monopoly, as both rely on taking people's necessities and storing them until their prices rise, disrupting the economy so that goods become scarce in one country while abundant in another, and similarly in the case of individuals {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}. See [[concept/ihtikar|Monopoly]].

## The Surplus Example
The author mentions that European countries dumped surplus grain and other goods into the sea in order to keep commodity prices high, viewing this as an example of materialistic goals that ignore human needs {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}. The author does not name these countries, so this example is cited only as the author's own illustration.

## Its Place in the Book
Hoarding falls under the sixth cause of the economic problem, and is contrasted in his work with the sound employment of money in production {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 58}. See [[concept/al-nudra-al-iqtisadiyya|Economic Scarcity]].

## From Zaydan Qa'dat's Book: pp. 83–164 of the PDF (Hoarding in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat argues that the accumulation of wealth in the pockets of specific individuals creates an arrogant class that breeds fear and domination, citing the verse in [[passage/quran-59-7|al-Hashr 7]] to show that just distribution deprives the rich of the opportunity to hoard {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 131}. He views the warning in [[passage/quran-9-34|al-Tawbah 34]] against hoarding gold and silver as evidence of its danger to the individual and society, and notes that hoarding is a habit of miserliness that freezes society's economic activity and is tied to monopoly by the same individualistic impulse {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 132}.

## From the Book Usul al-Iqtisad al-Islami: Part Two (pp. 101–200 of the PDF)
The author presents Ibn al-'Arabi's seven opinions on the meaning of hoarding (kanz), noting that the dispute centers on whether wealth on which zakat has been paid counts as hoarding: one group says no, and another says yes if it is not set aside for spending {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 194} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 195}.

The author considers hoarding to have two degrees: hoarding by accumulation and hoarding by withholding, noting that hoarding is withholding money from spending, and that the punishment for hoarded wealth on which zakat has not been paid is far greater {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 196} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 197}.
