# Democracy and the Sharia — compatibility and conflict

## The episode and the vote
The third 'Al-Wasatiyya' episode debated 'democracy: justice or despotism?' and its relation to Islam {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:01:00}. The audience voted on its conflict with the sharia: 16% agreed, 42% disagreed, 42% partially agreed — a marked shift on which Ishaq al-Farhan commented: twenty-five years ago the prevailing view was 'democracy is unbelief'; today most youth take it as a mechanism for collecting majority opinion {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:10:00}.

## The dissection — procedural and philosophical
Azzam al-Tamimi split liberal democracy into two arms: the procedural — electing the ruler, holding him accountable, rule of law, basic rights — on which the panel found no problem, and the philosophical legislative arm where the real dispute sits; and reported from his doctoral research that no recognised Western philosopher makes secularism a prerequisite of democracy — 'whoever insists on that is either a Westerner who does not want Islamists to succeed through it, or a Muslim who misunderstands it' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:28:00}. The host summarised: the problem narrowed to 'parliament's right to legislate' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:29:00}.

## Adopting versus engaging — sovereignty
Munir al-Bayati drew the distinction precisely: democracy becomes 'tyranny of the majority' if the nation is granted unrestricted legislation, colliding with 'the judgement is God's alone' — but adopting it is one thing and engaging with it another; when the benefit to Islam and Muslims outweighs, entering becomes obligatory, not merely permissible {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:15:00}. Abd al-Latif Arabiyat agreed with a clarification: the confusion mixes the foreign term with the Islamic meaning — shura as the people's authority under God's sovereignty — and testified that his own parliament voted on banning alcohol and it failed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:18:30}.

## The Islamist record — success or failure
The audience voted 60% failure, 40% success {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:32:00}. Al-Farhan objected: Islamist electoral success made the West recalculate — 'they call for democracy until it brings Islamists to power, then back the tyrants' — citing Hamas in Palestine, Justice and Development, and the Turkish experience {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:33:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:34:30}. Ali al-Atoum judged the record 'partially successful' but rulers will never permit Islamists to reach power — recalling Algeria — and the worthier path is seeking an authentic alternative {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:36:30}. Mahdi al-Dajani said participation is a step toward imitating the Western model, not toward establishing God's rule {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:37:30}. Arabiyat replied: democracy's mechanisms bring us nearer to Islam — pluralism, rotation of power, rights — and gradualism is necessary since building person, family and society cannot be decreed overnight {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep03 00:40:00}.
