## Summary
Mai', in lexicon, is a liquid or a melting thing, and its technical sense does not depart from this {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13792}. Among its rulings: purification by liquids. Most jurists hold that only absolute water lifts the minor ritual impurity and removes physical impurity; others allow any pure liquid that cleans {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13793}. On contamination, the Malikis, Shafi'is and, in the stronger narration, the Hanbalis hold that any liquid other than absolute water becomes impure on touching an impurity; the Hanafis and one narration of Ahmad hold that a liquid is impure only by what makes water impure; Ibn Abidin says the stronger view is that all liquids are like water {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13793-13794}. Most jurists hold that a contaminated liquid cannot be cleansed, unlike oils and ghee which some say can be washed; mercury is excepted by some Shafi'is and Hanbalis {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13795-13796}. The Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis forbid using the fat of a carrion for anything, including lamps; the Shafi'is allow lighting with contaminated oil outside the mosque, with reluctance {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13796-13797}.
