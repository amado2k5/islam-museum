## Summary
Fiscal imposition (tawzif) in the author's view is levying additional charges on the wealth of the rich sufficient to meet the needs of the army, the poor, and others; his order of expenditure is: the needs of the poor first from the zakat treasury, then from the public-interest treasury, and then through fiscal imposition {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 322}.

## First to Address It
The author notes that the first to discuss fiscal imposition was Imam Al-Juwayni, who stated decisively as his chosen view that the ruler obligates the wealthy to spend their surplus wealth until sufficiency and self-reliance are achieved {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 322}, and said that funds must be levied as the ruler deems sufficient to cover provisions {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 322}.

## Standard of Need
Al-Juwayni defined the basic needs that must be met for the poor, including staple food—among which is meat—medicine, fruit, clothing befitting dignity, and housing, while not including marriage {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 322} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 323}. Need in his view is not limited to bare survival, but extends to the level of sufficiency {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 323}.

## Al-Ghazali and the Condition of an Empty Treasury
Al-Ghazali describes an obeyed ruler who needs a large army to guard the frontiers when the public treasury is empty of funds; in that case, the ruler may levy on the wealthy whatever he deems immediately sufficient until funds appear in the treasury {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 323}. He recommends that attention then be paid to distributing the levy across crops and fruits so that singling out certain people does not provoke resentment {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 323}.

## Ibn Hazm
Ibn Hazm states that the wealthy of every town are obligated to support their poor, and the ruler compels them to do so if zakat and fay' revenues do not suffice, providing them with the food they eat, winter and summer clothing, and a dwelling that shelters them {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 323}. The author observes that this principle is general across the legal schools, and that Ibn Hazm elaborated the evidence for it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 324}.
