## Summary
In chapters 6 and 7 Ibn Sina says that obstruction arises when something foreign lodges in a channel, foreign in kind, amount or quality, or when the passage is joined shut or squeezed, and that channels widen through weakness of the retentive power, strong movement of the expulsive power or relaxing drugs, and narrow through the opposites {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.145}.

## Causes of obstruction
Obstruction arises when something foreign falls into the channel: foreign in kind, like a stone; in amount, like much dregs; or in quality, being thick, viscous or congealed, like a clot. Some of it is fixed in its place and some loose and wandering. It may also arise from the passage knitting shut when an ulcer heals, from growth of an extra thing such as a wart-like flesh, from closing of the channel by a pressing tumour or by the contraction of severe cold, from great strength of the retentive power, or from a binding tied too tight. Winter brings many obstructions through the heaping of superfluities and the contraction of cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.145}.

## Causes of widening
Channels widen through weakness of the retentive power or strong movement of the expulsive power; the holding of the breath belongs here; or through opening drugs or relaxing drugs, hot and moist. Channels narrow from the opposites of these {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.145}.
