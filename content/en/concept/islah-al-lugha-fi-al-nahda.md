## Summary
A research summary supplied to the museum says that print culture, journalism and translation forced nineteenth-century writers to adapt classical Arabic from ornate courtly prose into a direct, standardised medium able to express modern science, politics and mass communication. This page sets out four of the figures of that reform as the summary names them: Ahmad Faris al-Shidyaq, Nasif al-Yaziji, his son Ibrahim, and Butrus al-Bustani {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:cambridge-arabic-thought-beyond-the-liberal-age-intro}.

## What drove the reform
The drive in the summary is three things: printing, which spread the text; journalism, which asked for a language near the reader; and translation, which brought into Arabic sciences and ideas that had no words. The summary adds that classical Arabic was ornate in courtly prose and so needed a direct, standard language. The chapter 'Fusha' in the Cambridge Companion to Modern Arabic Literature bears on this {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:cambridge-arabic-thought-beyond-the-liberal-age-intro} {cite:cambridge-companion-modern-arabic-literature-fusha}.

## Ahmad Faris al-Shidyaq
[[person/ahmad-faris-al-shidyaq|Ahmad Faris al-Shidyaq]], 1804 or 1805 to 1887. He satirised the pedantry of the traditionalists and experimented with modern Arabic prose in al-Saq ala al-Saq in 1855, and helped shape modern journalistic style and linguistic debate through his influential newspaper al-Jawa'ib {cite:encyclopedia-com-western-thought-middle-east} {cite:cambridge-arabic-thought-beyond-the-liberal-age-intro}.

## Nasif al-Yaziji
[[person/nasif-al-yaziji|Nasif al-Yaziji]], 1800 to 1871. He revived classical Arabic prose in his collection of maqamat, Majma al-Bahrayn, in 1856, and co-founded in 1847 the first literary society in Beirut, as the summary says from Wikipedia and from its entry on the maqama, which are used here only for facts about persons and titles {cite:wikipedia-nasif-al-yaziji-en} {cite:wikipedia-maqama-en}.

## Ibrahim al-Yaziji
[[person/ibrahim-al-yaziji|Ibrahim al-Yaziji]], 1847 to 1906, son of Nasif. He policed and refined journalistic usage in Lughat al-Jara'id, coined modern scientific terms, edited journals including al-Diya, and in 1886 designed a simplified Arabic printing font that reduced the variants of the letters, fitting modern presses and early Arabic typewriters {cite:cambridge-companion-modern-arabic-literature-fusha} {cite:cambridge-ijmes-arabic-typewriter-multiple-identities}.

## Butrus al-Bustani
[[person/butrus-al-bustani|Butrus al-Bustani]], 1819 to 1883. He compiled the modern Arabic dictionary [[work/muhit-al-muhit|Muhit al-Muhit]] in 1870, launched the first modern Arabic encyclopedia, [[work/dairat-al-maarif-bustani|Da'irat al-Ma'arif]], in 1876, and founded the secular National School in 1863 and the periodical al-Jinan in 1870, to make Arabic a vehicle for modern science and non-sectarian education {cite:routledge-rem-al-bustani-butrus} {cite:wikipedia-butrus-al-bustani-en}.

## What links them
The four work in near places of renewal: the newspaper for al-Shidyaq and Ibrahim al-Yaziji, the maqama for Nasif, the dictionary, encyclopedia and school for al-Bustani, and the printed letter for Ibrahim. This is the page's grouping of the names the summary gave, not a division taken from it. For its place in literature see [[concept/marahil-al-adab-al-arabi|The eight periods of Arabic literature]], for the novel that came at the end of the age [[concept/bidayat-al-riwaya-al-arabiyya-zaynab|Zaynab]], and for the classical language and the dialects [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Fusha and the dialects]] {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:cambridge-arabic-thought-beyond-the-liberal-age-intro}.

## Timeline
1847: founding of the first literary society in Beirut, with Nasif al-Yaziji. 1855: al-Shidyaq's al-Saq ala al-Saq. 1856: Nasif's Majma al-Bahrayn. 1863: al-Bustani's National School. 1870: Muhit al-Muhit and the periodical al-Jinan. 1876: Da'irat al-Ma'arif. 1886: Ibrahim al-Yaziji's simplified font. These years are all from the summary, which does not detail what passed between them. The timeline is the page's ordering, not the summary's thesis {cite:wikipedia-nasif-al-yaziji-en} {cite:cambridge-ijmes-arabic-typewriter-multiple-identities} {cite:wikipedia-butrus-al-bustani-en}.

## What the page does not say
The summary does not give the stand of the conservative scholars of the language toward these men, nor what later happened to their terms or to Ibrahim al-Yaziji's font, nor examples from their texts. The page therefore describes no results of the reform and keeps to the work and year ascribed to each. The subject is linked to the pages on terminology and Arabisation, which show what passed to the language academies, among them [[concept/taarib-al-ulum-wal-majami|Arabisation of the sciences and the academies]] {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance} {cite:cambridge-arabic-thought-beyond-the-liberal-age-intro}.
