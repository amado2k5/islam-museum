## Summary
Where does the Qur'an's inimitability lie? The mainstream answer is in its composition, eloquence and style, so it is inimitable in itself. Al-Nazzam's answer is that the Arabs were linguistically able to match it but God turned them away from opposition, so the miracle lies in the turning. This page gives the two views as a research summary supplied to the museum reports them, assigns each to its holders, does not judge between them, and did not open the books named in it.

## The mainstream view
The majority hold, according to the summary, that the Qur'an's composition, eloquence, rhetoric and unique style are inimitable in themselves, and that the challenge, as in [[passage/quran-2-23|al-Baqara 23]] and [[passage/quran-17-88|al-Isra 88]], remains unmet because no one can produce the like of the Qur'an {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section c}. The matter connects to [[concept/al-tahaddi-bil-quran|The challenge of the Qur'an]] and [[concept/ijaz-al-quran|The inimitability of the Qur'an]].

## Al-Nazzam's sarfa
[[person/al-nazzam|Al-Nazzam]], who died about 230 AH, 845 CE, held that the Arabs possessed the linguistic capacity to match the purity and eloquence of the Qur'an, but God turned them away from it, either by removing motivation and knowledge or by direct prevention. For him the miracle lies in the divine act of turning away, not in the superiority of the text itself {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section c}. Al-Nazzam did not, however, set aside the Qur'an as a proof of prophethood; he counted it a proof by other features, among them knowledge of the unseen.

## The reach of the theory and the majority's rejection
The doctrine of sarfa influenced some Mu'tazila and some who came after them, but the majority rejected it because it weakens the text's intrinsic inimitability {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section c}.

## What the classical books say
[[person/fakhr-al-din-al-razi|Al-Razi]] in Mafatih al-ghayb discusses aspects of i'jaz, criticises sarfa and prefers the rhetorical miracle in the text itself. [[person/mahmud-al-alusi|Al-Alusi]] in Ruh al-ma'ani affirms that the Qur'an is miraculous in itself and that those who hold sarfa lack convincing evidence. [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] and [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]] affirm the standard view of the text's inimitability at the challenge verses, such as 2:23-24 and 17:88. [[person/muhammad-al-tahir-ibn-ashur|Ibn Ashur]] stresses the uniqueness of rhetoric and composition as central to i'jaz {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section c}.

## The meaning of sarfa and the point at issue
Sarfa comes from sarafa, to turn away, and in this subject it means that God turned the Arabs away from opposing the Qur'an. The point at issue, according to the summary, is not whether the Qur'an is a proof, since both sides count it one, but where the inimitability lies: in the text, which no one can match, or in a divine prevention from opposition while the linguistic capacity remains. So in the mainstream account the challenge went unanswered because no one is able, and in al-Nazzam's account the Arabs were able but did not because they were turned away. The summary lays this out in a table of two rows: the mainstream view and al-Nazzam's sarfa.

## How the two views part
The two views agree that the Qur'an is a proof and that the Arabs did not produce its like; they part over the reason for the failure: the mainstream traces it to the superiority of the text, al-Nazzam to a divine act that turned their ability away. A question follows as to whether the challenge concerns the text or the conduct of those challenged. The closest records to this one are [[concept/al-ijaz-al-bayani-inda-al-mufassirin|The inimitability of the Qur'an's language in the commentaries]] and [[concept/nazariyyat-al-ijaz-wal-nazm|The theory of inimitability and composition]].

## What this page does not reach
The books of al-Razi, al-Alusi, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur were not opened, and no text of al-Nazzam's was read; the views come from the research summary the founder approved using on 21 November 2026 without re-verification. No page numbers are given because they vary by edition, and the extent of the influence of sarfa on the Mu'tazila and those after them was not examined.
