## Summary
When something prevents the mother's milk from nursing the child, whether weakness, corruption or thinness of her milk, a wet nurse is chosen on conditions of her age, build, character, breast, the quality of her milk, the time since her delivery and the sex of her child; Ibn Sina sets them out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.205-209}.

## The conditions in outline
When the mother's milk is barred by weakness, corruption or thinness, a wet nurse is chosen for the child on conditions, some about her age, some her build, some her character, some the shape of her breast, some the quality of her milk, some the length of time since her delivery, and some the sex of her child {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.205}.

## Age and build
The best age is between twenty-five and thirty-five years, the age of youth, health and completeness. In build she should be of good colour, with strong neck and chest, broad-chested, muscular with firm flesh, midway between fat and lean, fleshy rather than fatty {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.206}.

## Character and breast
In character she should be of good and praised conduct, slow to bad passions such as anger, distress and cowardice, for these spoil the temperament and may even be passed on by nursing, and her bad temper brings bad care in tending the child {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.206}. As to her breast, it should be full and large but not slack nor excessively large, balanced between firm and soft {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.206}.

## Time since delivery and her child's sex
Her delivery should be recent but not too recent, a month and a half or two months before, she should have borne a boy, the birth should have come at the natural term, and she should not have miscarried nor be accustomed to miscarry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.209}.
