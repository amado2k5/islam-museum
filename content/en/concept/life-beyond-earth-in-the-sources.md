## What this record is for

Neither the Qur'an nor the sound hadith read by the museum contains a plain sentence saying "there is intelligent life on another planet", or the opposite. What the sources do contain is verses about creatures scattered "in the heavens and the earth", about a creation people do not know, and about a plurality of worlds, and the commentators read them in different ways. This record sets out what the sources say and what named commentators said. It neither asserts nor denies that extraterrestrial beings exist.

## What the verses say

- The verse that counts among God's signs the creation of the heavens and the earth and the creatures He dispersed in them, and says He is able to gather them whenever He wills: [[passage/quran-42-29|Ash-Shura 42:29]].
- The verse that lists what God made for riding and adornment and then adds a creation people do not know: [[passage/quran-16-8|An-Nahl 16:8]].
- The verse that glorifies the One who created pairs from what the earth grows, from people themselves and from what they do not know: [[passage/quran-36-36|Ya-Sin 36:36]].
- The verse that says every creature on earth and every bird that flies is a community like people: [[passage/quran-6-38|Al-An'am 6:38]], which speaks of the earth.
- The verse that says those in the heavens and the earth ask Him and that He is every day engaged in some affair: [[passage/quran-55-29|Ar-Rahman 55:29]].
- The verse that says the heavens and the earth and those in them glorify Him: [[passage/quran-17-44|Al-Isra 17:44]].

## How the commentators read the "creature" verse, Ash-Shura 42:29

**Al-Tabari** takes the gathering to be the resurrection, and reports from Mujahid that the creatures meant here are people and angels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 42:29}

**Al-Qurtubi** reports Mujahid's view that angels and people are included, and cites the verse saying God creates what people do not know; he also reports al-Farra' that only what is dispersed on the earth, not in the heaven, is meant, and Abu Ali that the wording means "in one of the two"; the gathering is on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 42:29}

**Ibn Kathir** says the word covers angels, jinn and people and all animals in their different shapes, colours, languages, natures and kinds, that God scattered them through the regions of the earth and the heavens, and that the gathering is the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 42:29}

**Al-Razi** asks how the word "creature" can be applied to angels, and gives three answers: an act may be ascribed to a group though one of them did it; "creeping" means movement, and angels move; and it is "not far-fetched" that God created in the heavens kinds of animals that walk as people walk on the earth. He takes the gathering to be the resurrection and reckoning, and the plural pronoun to point to those with reason. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-quran-tafsir-net on 42:29}

So the commentators read here agree that the verse speaks of creatures in the heaven and the earth together, but the inhabitants of the heaven they name are mostly angels; Ibn Kathir adds jinn; al-Razi leaves open other kinds of animal; and al-Farra', as al-Qurtubi reports him, limits the word to the earth.

## Al-Razi and other worlds

In his commentary on al-Fatiha, where he explains "the worlds" as everything other than God, al-Razi writes that no proof limits bodies to this world. In his reasoning there is an unending void outside the world and God is able to do all that is possible, so He is able to create "a thousand thousand worlds" outside this one, each larger than it and each containing what this one contains: a Throne, a Footstool, heavens, earths, a sun and a moon. He calls the philosophers' arguments that the world is one weak and flimsy, and quotes a line of Abu al-'Ala' al-Ma'arri on the many spheres of God. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-quran-tafsir-net on 1:2}

This is a statement about **power and possibility**, not a report that such worlds exist: al-Razi does not say they do, only that reason does not exclude them and power can reach them. On the verse of the seven earths he adds that the transmitted details about the heavens and earths are rejected by reason unless the report is mass-transmitted. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 PDF p. 354}

## The creation people do not know, An-Nahl 16:8

On An-Nahl 16:8 al-Qurtubi lists readings of the creation people do not know: the majority say it means creation in general; some say kinds of insects and vermin no human has seen; some say what God prepared in Paradise and the Fire; Qatada and al-Suddi say the weevil in clothes and the worm in fruit; and a fifth reading, which he says al-Mawardi gave from the Prophet, is a white earth, a thirty-day journey of the sun across, filled with creatures who do not know that God is disobeyed on the earth and who do not know that Adam or Iblis was created. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 16:8}

Ibn Kathir gives a close report from Ibn Abi al-Dunya's *al-Tafakkur wa-l-I'tibar* (with a journey of forty days), and says: "This is a mursal hadith and very objectionable"; he adds that its narrator Uthman ibn Abi Dahrash is mentioned by Ibn Abi Hatim without criticism or praise. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 65:12} Al-Qurtubi also reports from al-Bayhaqi a statement of al-Sha'bi that God has servants beyond al-Andalus, as far from it as it is from us, who do not know that God is disobeyed. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 16:8}

On Ya-Sin 36:36 al-Qurtubi says that what people do not know may be known to the angels, or to no creature at all. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 36:36} Al-Tabari confines his explanation to things God has not informed them of. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 36:36}

## Modern studies

The subject now has an academic literature. Among it, with their bibliographic details checked in Crossref but their text not yet read for the museum, are Jörg Matthias Determann on Islam, science fiction and extraterrestrial life (2021); the volume *Islamic Theology and Extraterrestrial Life*, edited by Shoaib Ahmed Malik and Determann (2024); David A. Weintraub's chapter "Islam" in *Religions and Extraterrestrial Life* (2014); and Parandis Tajbakhsh's *Islam and Extraterrestrial Life* (2026). {cite:determann-islam-science-fiction-extraterrestrial-life-2021} {cite:malik-determann-islamic-theology-extraterrestrial-life-2024} {cite:weintraub-religions-and-extraterrestrial-life-2014} {cite:tajbakhsh-islam-and-extraterrestrial-life-2026} These are **modern** works, not classical sources, and the museum has not yet summarised their arguments.

:::perspectives
**Sunni: the classical commentators.** The verses speak of creatures in the heaven and the earth; the heaven's inhabitants named in these commentaries are angels for Mujahid and al-Tabari, angels, jinn, people and all animals for Ibn Kathir; al-Razi leaves open kinds of animal in the heavens and the possibility of other worlds through God's power without asserting that they exist; and al-Farra', as al-Qurtubi reports him, limits the word to the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 42:29} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 42:29} {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-quran-tafsir-net on 42:29} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 42:29}

**Sunni: restraint over detail where there is no text.** Ibn Kathir rejects reports about particular creatures beyond what is known unless their chain is sound, and calls one of them "very objectionable"; al-Qurtubi closes his discussion of the earths by saying God knows best what He kept to Himself. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 65:12} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 65:12}

**Academic (modern).** Modern scholarship treats the subject as a live theological and cultural question; the publisher's abstract of Tajbakhsh (2026) says it begins with the history of the plurality-of-worlds debate in Christendom and then turns to the Qur'an and hadith, and that of Malik and Determann (2024) says it gathers Sunni and Shi'i positions. These works have not yet been read. {cite:tajbakhsh-islam-and-extraterrestrial-life-2026} {cite:malik-determann-islamic-theology-extraterrestrial-life-2024}
:::

## What the sources do not say

- No verse and no sound hadith read here names beings on another planet, describes their nature, or reports contact with them.
- Al-An'am 6:38 speaks of the creatures and birds of the earth, not of the heaven.
- Al-Razi's "thousand thousand worlds" belongs to the discussion of power, not to a report of what exists.
- Ibn Kathir called the Ibn Abi al-Dunya report of the "white earth" mursal and very objectionable; no grading of the version al-Mawardi gave was read in the sources the museum consulted.
