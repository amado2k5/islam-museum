## Summary
The chahartaq is a square vaulted chamber in which four large intersecting arches, resting on heavy piers, form a cross around an open square. The square is then turned into an octagon or circle with smaller arches and ribs, so the dome rests directly on the four arches {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.8}.

## In India
The Mughals used the chahartaq widely in mosques and tombs, for example in the western liwans of the Jami' Masjids at Fatehpur Sikri, Shahjahanabad and Lahore, in the Kabuli Bagh Mosque at Panipat and in the square Khusrau Bagh tombs {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.9}.
