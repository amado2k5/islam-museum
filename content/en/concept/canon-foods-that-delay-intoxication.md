## Summary
Ibn Sina says one who means to take his fill of wine does not fill himself with food or eat sweets, but sips a fatty broth, eats a fatty crumbled bread dish and fatty meat, and finishes with salted almonds and lentils, adding that what dries the vapour and what is sticky prevents drunkenness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.238-239}.

## What is eaten with wine
One who wishes to be filled with wine should not fill himself with food or eat sweets; he sips a fatty broth, takes a fatty crumbled bread dish and fatty meat, and accompanies it with salted almonds and lentils and the caper relish. If he eats the cabbage dish and water-cured olives and the like, it helps and assists his drinking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.238-239}.

## Drying and sticky things
The same holds for everything that dries the vapour, such as the seed of the Nabataean cabbage, cumin, dried rue, pennyroyal, naphtha salt and ajowan, and for foods with viscosity and a gluing quality that may thicken the vapour, such as sweet, sticky fats; these hold off drunkenness, though they do not take much wine because they are slow to pass through {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.238-239}.
