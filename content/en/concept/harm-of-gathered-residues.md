## Summary
Ibn Sina lists the ways residues gathered from digestion after digestion do harm: they putrefy and bring diseases of putrefaction, upset the temperament, cause the diseases of fullness, make swellings if they pour into an organ, and their vapours spoil the spirit; he adds that expelling them with drugs is a burden on nature {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.221}.

## The ways they harm
If these residues putrefy they bring the diseases of putrefaction; if their qualities grow intense they bring bad temperament; if their quantity grows they bring the diseases of fullness already described; if they pour into an organ they bring swellings. Their vapours spoil the temperament of the substance of the spirit, so the body is driven to expel them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.221}.

## The cost of expelling them by drugs
He says that in most cases expulsion is accomplished by poisonous drugs that wear down the innate faculty, and that even non-poisonous ones carry some load on nature, citing Hippocrates that a drug purifies and also wounds. Along with the surplus humour they also draw out some of the innate moistures and of the spirit, which weakens the principal and serving organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.221}.
