## Summary
Tarsim is narrowing a person's freedom and limiting his movement. Testimony to the confession of one who is imprisoned or under tarsim is not accepted, since coercion is presumed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4963}

## Definition
In the language, tarsim is from rasm, which means a mark, and is used figuratively for ordering a thing. In the jurists' usage it is narrowing a person and limiting his movement so that he cannot go from one place to another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4963}

## Ruling
Testimony to the confession of one who is imprisoned or under tarsim is not accepted, since the signs of coercion are present, and his confession of a right that carries a penalty is not valid. The work on the Hanbali text says that a claim of coercion into a confession is accepted where a sign shows it, such as a threat the threatener can carry out, restraint, imprisonment or the taking of property. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4963}
