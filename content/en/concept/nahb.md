## Summary
Nahb (plunder) is, in the lexicon, taking by force and dominance in the open; the jurists use it in two senses: taking by force in the open, and taking what the owner has permitted, such as what is scattered at feasts. It differs from embezzlement, usurpation, theft and brigandage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16207} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}

## Definition
Nahb in the lexicon is taking by force and dominance over property; to plunder someone is to take his goods, and "to make plunder" is to leave goods open to be seized. A hadith says that when something was scattered and the people did not take it, the Prophet asked "why do you not plunder?"; Ibn Hajar weakened its chain. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16207} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
The jurists use two senses. The first is taking by force and dominance in the open: the Hanafis say it is taking openly by force from the outskirts of a town or village; the Shafi'is say taking by force and dominance while knowing the property; the Hanbalis say one who relies on strength and takes property as spoils. The second is taking what the owner has permitted, such as what is scattered at feasts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}

## Related terms
**Embezzlement (ikhtilas).** Taking a thing in its owner's presence by quick snatching, in flight, whether the taker comes openly or secretly; plunder does not require quickness. Both are taking another's property without right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
**Usurpation (ghasb).** Taking another's property by force, wrongfully, without brigandage; for the Hanafis, removing the owner's hand from his valued property openly by force. Plunder is narrower, since usurpation may occur without the owner's presence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
**Theft (sariqa).** Taking, by a sane adult, a guarded sum or its value, belonging to another with no claim to it, secretly; plunder is open in the owner's presence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
