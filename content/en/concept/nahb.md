## Summary
Nahb (plunder) is, in the lexicon, taking by force and dominance in the open; the jurists use it in two senses: taking by force in the open, and taking what the owner has permitted, such as what is scattered at feasts. It differs from embezzlement, usurpation, theft and brigandage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16207} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}

## Definition
Nahb in the lexicon is taking by force and dominance over property; to plunder someone is to take his goods, and "to make plunder" is to leave goods open to be seized. A hadith says that when something was scattered and the people did not take it, the Prophet asked "why do you not plunder?"; Ibn Hajar weakened its chain. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16207} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
The jurists use two senses. The first is taking by force and dominance in the open: the Hanafis say it is taking openly by force from the outskirts of a town or village; the Shafi'is say taking by force and dominance while knowing the property; the Hanbalis say one who relies on strength and takes property as spoils. The second is taking what the owner has permitted, such as what is scattered at feasts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}

## Related terms
**Embezzlement (ikhtilas).** Taking a thing in its owner's presence by quick snatching, in flight, whether the taker comes openly or secretly; plunder does not require quickness. Both are taking another's property without right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
**Usurpation (ghasb).** Taking another's property by force, wrongfully, without brigandage; for the Hanafis, removing the owner's hand from his valued property openly by force. Plunder is narrower, since usurpation may occur without the owner's presence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}
**Theft (sariqa).** Taking, by a sane adult, a guarded sum or its value, belonging to another with no claim to it, secretly; plunder is open in the owner's presence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16208}

## Brigandage, the ruling on plunder, resisting the plunderer, and its effect on transactions
**Brigandage (hirabah).** For most jurists, also called road-cutting, it is going out to take property, to kill or to terrify, openly and defiantly, relying on strength and far from help. Its relation to plunder in taking property is that both take another's property without right; brigandage depends on the absence of help, plunder does not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16209}
**The ruling on plunder (taking by force in the open).** Taking another's property without his owner's consent is forbidden: seizing, plundering or stealing it is not lawful, on the verses "do not eat your property among yourselves in falsehood" and "except trade by mutual consent", on the hadith "your blood, your property and your honour are forbidden to you", on the prohibition of plunder and of mutilation, and on "whoever plunders is not of us". Al-Qurtubi says the Sunnis agree that one who takes anything called property, small or great, is thereby a wrongdoer; Ibn Hajar al-Haytami counts seizing others' property by injustice among the grave sins. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16209} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16210}
**The penalty for plunder.** Plunder is a disobedience with no prescribed penalty, on the hadith "there is no amputation for the traitor, the plunderer or the embezzler", so it is not theft or brigandage; a discretionary penalty is due. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16210}
**Resisting the plunderer.** It is lawful, on the hadith "whoever is killed defending his property is a martyr". If the owner is killed he is a martyr; if the plunderer is killed his blood is lost, on the report of the man who asked the Prophet "what if he fights me?" and was told "fight him", and "if he kills me?" "you are a martyr", and "if I kill him?" "he is in the Fire". The jurists differ on repelling an assailant of property, see the entry on assault. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16210} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16211}
**Its effect on transactions.** (a) Picking up stray animals in a time of plunder is disputed; see the entry on lost property. (b) Accepting a deposit in a time of plunder is lawful and recommended for one who trusts his own honesty, and may be obligatory; for depositing a deposit with another in such a time, the Hanafis and Malikis allow it with a trusted guarantor, without liability; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis require returning it to its owner or his agent, and if that fails, to the judge or a trustworthy person. (c) A claim that a deposit was lost by plunder: if the cause was hidden, as theft, the depositary is believed on oath; if the cause was apparent, as fire or a raid or the plunder of an army, proof by general report or sight is needed. (d) A loan repaid in a time of plunder: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis say the lender need not accept it; the Hanbalis add that he need not accept it even if the borrower is harmed, since harm is not removed by harm. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16211} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16212}
