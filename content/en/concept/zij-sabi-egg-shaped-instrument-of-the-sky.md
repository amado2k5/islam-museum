## The copper sphere and the egg
A copper sphere, perfectly round on every side and smooth in surface, is made. Two opposite poles are marked on it, and a line set between them on the back of the sphere halves it. A circle that cuts the sphere in halves is drawn on one of them, with its quarter divided equally; a second circle is drawn across the first, cutting it at two opposite points, so that there are two circles crossing at two points: the equator of the day and the ecliptic. The ecliptic is divided into twelve signs and each sign into thirty parts. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.213-214}

## The measures and the obliquity
The text states that the obliquity of the ecliptic from the equator of the day is about twenty-three parts and thirty-five minutes, and that these are divided into the parts of the instrument. The reckoner draws the signs in their order and starts from the line of the head of Aries. The clear figures are carried over, and the disturbed figures on page 214 are to be checked against the original. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.213-214}
