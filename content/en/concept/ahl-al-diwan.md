## Definition
Diwan is a Persian loan-word meaning a collection of sheets and writings, in which the army and those given grants were written. For the jurists it is the register of those in the state's service who receive a stipend from the public treasury, and the place where it is kept. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3614}
- Ahl al-diwan: those paid from it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3614}

## Its origin
Umar ibn al-Khattab was the first to set it up, according to two accounts: one when Abu Hurayra brought money from Bahrain, and one when the ruler needed to follow the soldiers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3614}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3615}

## Its kinds of recipient
- Soldiers, on regulatory conditions: puberty, freedom, Islam, freedom from disabling conditions, readiness to fight, and freedom from any other trade. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3615}
- Holders of office: governors, judges, scholars, collectors of property, imams and muezzins. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3615}
- The needy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3615}

## Its purposes
Imam al-Haramayn names three groups supported by the imam: the needy, who are the bulk of those due zakat; those whose livelihood the ruler secures so that they may serve Islam, namely the paid soldiers and those who maintain the duties of religion such as judges, muftis and jurists; and the Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib, who receive by right of kinship rather than need. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3615}

## Equality of distribution
- Abu Bakr and Ali made distribution equal, the view of al-Shafi'i and Malik. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3615}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3616}
- Umar and Uthman preferred by precedence in Islam, and Umar added kinship to the Prophet; this view was held by Abu Hanifa, Ahmad and the jurists of Iraq. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3616}

## Relation to the blood-paying group
Some jurists counted the people of the diwan among the group that pays blood-money, which in early times was the kin of the killer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3616}
