## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds that lowering the gaze protects insight, and that many incidents begin with a single glance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:30:00}

## The Qur'anic command
The Sheikh cites the Qur'anic command to believers to lower their gaze and guard their chastity, in [[passage/quran-24-30|the verse in Surat al-Nur]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:30:30}

## An innocent glance
The Sheikh tells the story of a man whose innocent glance went unnoticed until he struck a wall and bled. He uses it as a lesson that a glance can lead to what follows. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:30:00}

## The glance takes insight
The Sheikh holds that a glance takes away the insight of the one who looks, and that many incidents begin with a glance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:31:00}

## Rejecting the passing thought
The Sheikh holds that a passing thought should be rejected from the start. If it is left to grow, it becomes an intention and then a deed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:31:30}

## Insight of the heart
The Sheikh says that whoever lowers his gaze, God gives him insight (needs_source: the saying is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:30:30}

## Guarding the gaze among speech, steps and thoughts
The Sheikh says that scholars warned about four things: speech, the gaze, the steps and the thoughts. He cites the hadith of the night journey, in which a large bull is seen coming out of the eye of a needle and cannot return, and says that a spoken word cannot be taken back. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:24:00}

## Lowering the gaze as insight and trust
The Sheikh refers to the Qur'anic command to believers to lower their gaze, and says the gaze must avoid what is forbidden and must not be used to look down on people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:15:00}
He says that lowering the gaze brings insight and a sense of God's presence, and notes that the Qur'anic address covers believing men and women. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:15:30}
He cites a hadith on theft from one's prayer, and says that lowering the gaze is also a trust. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:05:30}

## Looking at people and their faults
The Sheikh criticises looking down on people for their class, rank or status. He says that Islam equalises people and that only piety distinguishes them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:12:30}
He cites a verse that warns against dwelling on the worldly goods that others have, and says that God's provision is better and more lasting. He says that whoever is dazzled by the wealthy has lost two-thirds of his faith (needs_source: the fraction given by the speaker is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:13:00}
He quotes the sages: whoever wants a friend without fault must live alone, and since everyone has faults, people should be judged by balance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:27:30}
He cites a hadith about a man who looked through a gap in a door into a house, and the hadith shows strong disapproval of his act. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:35:30}

## Screens and the gaze
The Sheikh criticises children who sit before the television for hours. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:17:00} He says that eye doctors hold that an hour before a screen equals eight hours of reading (needs_source: the speaker attributes this figure to doctors and it is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:17:00}
He says that the eyes should be used for what benefits, warns children against wasting their gaze on the internet, and holds that using useful information is a form of obedience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:16:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy suggests that a servant set himself a week in which to train himself to lower his gaze from what God has forbidden, and says that whoever grows used to lowering his gaze sees with the eye of his heart what others do not see. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep11 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep11 00:07:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh holds that the command to lower the gaze is not limited to the forbidden, but includes lowering the gaze from people's faults: husband from wife and wife from husband, brother from brother, neighbour from neighbour, employee from colleague, a lowering of deliberate overlooking and forgiving, so that people's sins are not put under a microscope. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:07:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
## Lowering the gaze and the heart's insight
The Sheikh relates that Hatim al-Asamm visited the house of his teacher [[person/shaqiq-al-balkhi|Shaqiq al-Balkhi]] for thirty years, and Shaqiq's wife thought he was blind because he kept his gaze so lowered; he concludes for young people today that whoever lowers his gaze, God frees his insight, and criticises flicking between forbidden channels (needs_source: the story is the Sheikh's narration). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep17 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep17 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep17 00:04:00} He holds that some people put others' faults under a magnifying lens and their own under a reducing one. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep17 00:04:00}
