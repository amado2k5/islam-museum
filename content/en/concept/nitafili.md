## Nature and selection
It is the spurge called five-leaved {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.582}.

## Actions and properties
Strongly drying without sharpness, pungency or sting; it is applied as a poultice for bleeding and cuts it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Swellings and pustules
It is applied as a poultice for abscesses, scrofula, phlegmatic hardnesses, whitlow and scab {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Joints
It helps pains of the joint and sciatica, and helps hydrocele drunk or applied as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Head, ears and teeth
A decoction of its root is a rinse for the aching tooth and for thrush, and its leaf with wine is for epilepsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Chest and breathing
A decoction is gargled for roughness of the throat, and the juice of its root is for pain of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its root, pressed, helps pain of the liver and jaundice when drunk for some days with salt and honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Excretory organs
Its root helps diarrhoea from ulcers of the intestines and haemorrhoids, and so does a decoction of its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Fevers
Its leaf with water-honey or with wine is for quartan and tertian fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.

## Poisons and bites
The juice of its root is a deadly drug; this is the book's own warning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.583}.
