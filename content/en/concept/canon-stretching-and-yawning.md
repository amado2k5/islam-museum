## Summary
In the thirteenth chapter Ibn Sina treats stretching and yawning: stretching comes from superfluities gathered in the muscles and so often follows sleep, and as they grow it becomes chill and shivering and then fever; yawning is a kind of stretching in the muscles of the jaw and breastbone, bad when frequent out of its time, and wine mixed half and half is good for both when there is no other cause {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.243-244}.

## Stretching
Stretching comes from superfluities gathered in the muscles and so often follows sleep; when those humours grow more it becomes chill and shivering, and when they grow more still they bring fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.

## Yawning
Yawning is a kind of stretching, from a stretching cause arising in the muscles of the jaw and breastbone; it comes to the healthy at the outset without cause, and when frequent out of its time it is poor. Wine mixed in equal parts is good for yawning and stretching when there is no other cause. A phrase at the head of the next page of the printed text, on the use of yawning and the driving off of superfluity, is garbled and is not carried over {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.243-244}.
