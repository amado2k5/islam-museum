## Nature and selection
Known, a kind of pot-herb: garden, wild and water cabbage. The author quotes Dioscorides that the wild grows on the sea coasts and in high places, like the garden kind but whiter and downier, and bitter; if its heart is boiled in pomegranate water it turns sweet and pleasant. Another kind, the western, is far from the garden kind in likeness, with long leaves, and when eaten cooked it loosens the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531}.

## Temperament
The root is moister than the leaf, and the wild is hotter and drier than the garden; as a whole it is hot in the first degree and dry in the second. The wild is more bitter, sharper and further from being a food. A decoction of the root in pomegranate water is pleasant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531}.

## Actions and properties
It ripens, softens and dries, especially when boiled and the first water poured off; the ash of its stalks dries strongly, and it has the property of soothing pains. Its nourishment is little, moister than that of lentils {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531}.

## Swellings and pustules
Wild, sea and garden cabbage ripen hard swellings. The leaf of wild or garden cabbage, pounded fine and applied alone or with barley meal, helps every hot swelling, phlegmatic swellings, erysipelas and hives {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It heals, stops malignant sores from spreading, helps ulcerated scabies, and mixed with salt removes the Persian fire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531}.

## Joints
It helps tremor, is put with fenugreek on gout, and its decoction is poured over painful joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Its decoction and seed slow drunkenness and help dandruff; snuffed, its juice cleans the head. One of its properties is to dry the tongue, and it cleans the face {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.531} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Eyes
It darkens the sight although it goes into eye salves, and Dioscorides said that eating cabbage helped weak sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Chest and breathing
Gargled as juice or decoction it helps quinsy; eating it clears the voice, and chewed with its juice sucked it mends a broken voice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The heart of the cabbage is better for the stomach, and if prepared with salt and water it is worse; the raw leaf eaten with vinegar helps those with spleen trouble. Dioscorides is quoted on the summer-grown kind, which is not suitable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Excretory organs
It brings on urine; its seed in lupin water kills worms, and the ash of its root breaks up stone. Sea cabbage has saltiness and bitterness and so softens and loosens the bowels, especially with fat meat, and its leaf applied helps hot colic. Dioscorides said that lightly boiled and eaten it loosens the bowels, and boiled twice in water and taken it binds them; its juice mixed with the root of the iris called irsa and with natron loosens the bowels. The seed, grown in Egypt in particular, kills worms when drunk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on the menses, and its flower buds do so too; its seed, taken as a pessary after intercourse, spoils the semen; and its flower, made into a pessary and taken by a woman after conception, kills what is in her womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Poisons and bites
Dioscorides said that its juice with wine helps against the sting of the viper; it helps the bite of the rabid dog, and the seed of Egyptian cabbage goes into theriac mixtures {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.
