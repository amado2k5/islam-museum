## Nature and selection
Dioscorides said that the wild and mountain vine has long shoots like those of the vine tendril, leaves like those of the garden nightshade but broader, a hair-like flower, fruit in clusters that turns red when ripe, round berries, and its leaf is eaten when it first sprouts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.535}.

## Actions and properties
The ash of its shoots goes into caustic remedies; vine oil is like rose oil but lacks its fineness, the oil of the juice soothes and warms, and the blossom of the wild is very astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Cosmetic uses
Its sap is for ant warts; the wild vine cleanses freckles and spots, while the cultivated is weak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Vine sap is good for scabies and ringworm, and the fruit of the wild vine prevents swelling of abscesses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Joints
The ash of its pressed residue with vinegar is for a twisted sinew; the ash of its shoots with oil is for a crushed muscle and slack joints, and the water of its ash is drunk for a fall. The oil of the juice is good for muscle pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Its leaf and tendrils are a poultice for hot headache. The root of the black and the white wild vine is among cleansing remedies for earwax and among those that help deafness, and the bark of the wild with honey heals bleeding gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Eyes
The leaves of the vine with barley meal are a poultice for swelling of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Chest and breathing
The juice of the garden leaf is for spitting blood, and so is the fruit of the wild, drunk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its leaf and tendrils with barley meal are a poultice for swelling and inflammation of the stomach, and the juice of its leaf is for stomach pain from heat. The root of the wild vine may be drunk in wine-water and helps dropsy, loosening the water. The fruit of the wild vine is good for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Excretory organs
The juice of its leaf is for dysentery; its sap, gum-like, drunk with wine breaks up stone; and its ash with vinegar is for piles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.

## Poisons and bites
The ash of its pressed residue is an antidote for the bite of vipers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536}.
