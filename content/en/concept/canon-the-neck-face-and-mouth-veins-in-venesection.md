## Summary
Ibn Sina names the two jugular veins opened at the start of leprosy, quinsy, vitiligo and spleen ailments, then the vein of the tip of the nose, whose harm he finds greater than its benefit, and the veins behind the ear, of the lips and mouth, tongue, chin and gums, with their uses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## The jugular veins
Among these veins are the two jugulars, which are opened at the start of leprosy, quinsy, vitiligo that comes of much hot blood, and ailments of the spleen and of the two sides. As he told us before, their opening should be with a lancet that has a hair. As for how it is fixed, the head is inclined toward the side opposite the bleeding so that the vein rises, and the side to which it slips most is noted and it is held from the opposite side {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## The vein of the nose tip
Among them is the vein in the tip of the nose; the place to open it is the cleft at its end which, pressed with the finger, parts in two, and there it is cut, and the blood that runs from it is little. Opening it helps in freckles, dullness of colour, piles and pustules that occur in the nose and itching in it, but it brought on a chronic redness of colour like ringworm which spreads over the face, so its harm is much greater than its benefit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## Behind the ear
The veins under the bone behind the ear, toward the hollow of the nape, are useful to open for the dizziness that comes of thin blood and for earlier pains in the head. The editor's footnote says the bone in question is the one behind the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## The lip and the mouth
Among them are four vessels, a pair on each lip; opening them helps in mouth ulcers, thrush, pains of the gums and their swellings, slackness or ulcers, and in the piles and fissures in them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## The tongue, chin and gum
The vein under the tongue, on the inner side of the chin, is opened in quinsies and swellings of the tonsils. Another is a vein under the tongue itself, opened for heaviness of the tongue that comes of blood, and it must be opened lengthwise, for if opened across its blood is hard to stop. Another is a vein at the chin tuft, opened for foul breath, and another is the gum vein, opened in the treatments of the mouth of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.
