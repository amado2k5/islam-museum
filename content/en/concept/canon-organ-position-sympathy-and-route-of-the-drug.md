## Summary
Ibn Sina says that the position of an organ helps in two ways: knowledge of its sympathies guides the choice of the direction in which the drug draws, and knowledge of its place helps in three respects, its nearness or distance, what is mixed with the drug to speed it there, and the route by which the drug reaches it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.269}.

## Sympathy
For example, if the matter lies in the convex side of the liver it is evacuated by urine, and if it lies in the concave side it is evacuated by purging, because the liver's convexity shares with the urinary organs and its concavity with the intestines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.269}.

## Nearness, mixing and route
A near organ such as the stomach is reached by moderate drugs in the least time with their strength intact; a far one such as the lung is reached only after moderate drugs have lost their strength, so it is increased, as with poultices for sciatica, which need more strength than the illness alone calls for. Diuretics are mixed into drugs for the urinary organs, and saffron into drugs for the heart, to hasten them. As to route, if the ulcer is known to lie in the lower intestines the drug is given by enema, and if it is thought to lie in the upper, by drink {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.269}.
