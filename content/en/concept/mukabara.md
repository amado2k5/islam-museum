## Summary
Mukabara means overpowering and defying. In the jurists' usage it is an aspect of highway robbery (hiraba): an open seizure of property, killing or intimidation that relies on force, far from help. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14920}

## Ruling
Mukabara takes the rule of hiraba in general. The Mudawwana holds that whoever overpowers a man for his property with a weapon in an alley, or enters his womenfolk's quarters in a town, is judged as a highwayman. Killing in such a case is a discretionary punishment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14920}

## Mukabara and the theft limb
The Hanafis require cutting the hand for one who overpowers a man at night and takes his goods, because help at night seldom reaches the householder, but not for a daytime mukabara in a town, where help usually comes. The Malikis treat the one who takes by force as exempt if he claims ownership or admits usurpation, and liable to cutting if he takes from a secure place and then claims ownership. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis treat a group who break into a house at night and prevent the owner calling for help as highwaymen, in the sounder Shafi'i view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14921}
