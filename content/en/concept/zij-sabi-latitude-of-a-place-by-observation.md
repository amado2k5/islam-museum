## Summary
Chapter 14 concerns the latitudes of places by observation: the latitude is the altitude of the north pole there and its distance from the equator. Al-Battani takes the Sun's altitude as it crosses the meridian line, by sighting or by the shadow, subtracts the degree's declination from the altitude if northern and adds it if southern, giving the altitude of the first of Aries or Libra, and its complement to ninety is the latitude. What is taken from a table of cities' latitudes is approximate and not like what is taken by observation. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.47-48}.

## From the noon altitude to the latitude
He says: if you want the latitude of any place you like, which is the altitude of the north pole there and also its distance from the equator, find the Sun's altitude at midday from whichever side, that is, where the Sun crosses the meridian line, by observation or by knowing the shadow. Once you know the altitude by either means, find the declination of the Sun's degree at that time: if northern, subtract it from the altitude; if southern, add it. The altitude, after the addition or subtraction, is the altitude of the first of Aries or the first of Libra in that place. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.47}.

## The complement to ninety and the approximate table
Then he says: subtract it from ninety and what remains is the latitude of that place. If you learn a place's latitude from a table of the cities' latitudes, it is approximate and not as exact as what is taken by observation. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.47-48}.
