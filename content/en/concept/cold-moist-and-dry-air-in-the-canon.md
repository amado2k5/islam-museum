## Summary
Ibn Sina completes his account of altered air by describing cold air, then moist and dry air, and which of them suits the healthy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.126}.

## Cold air
Cold air confines the innate heat inward unless it is so excessive that it drives it deep into the interior, which kills. When not excessive it checks the flow of matters and holds them, but it brings catarrh, weakens the nerve and does severe harm to the windpipe. If it is not severe it strengthens digestion and all the inner actions and stirs the appetite; in sum it suits the healthy better than excessively hot air. Its harms lie in the actions that depend on the nerve, in closing the pores, and in squeezing the filling and gaps of the bones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.126}.

## Moist and dry
Moist air is good and suits most temperaments, improves and softens the colour and the skin and keeps the pores open, though it prepares for putrefaction. Dry air is the opposite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.126}.
