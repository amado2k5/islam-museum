## Summary
In Ibn Ishaq's account the Prophet forbade speaking to the three, "so the Muslims withdrew from speaking to those three men"; separation from their wives was added on the fortieth night. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/175, 178-179}

## In the account
[[event/boycott-of-the-three|The boycott]] lasted fifty nights; in it Abu Qatada did not answer Ka'b ([[event/kaab-and-abu-qatada-at-the-garden-wall|the event]]), then came [[event/order-to-the-three-to-separate-from-their-wives|the order to separate from their wives]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/178-179}

## Notes
- "Boycott" describes the event in the account; naming it "hajr" is the museum's term, not Ibn Ishaq's wording. The juristic treatment of shunning the sinner needs sources (needs_source).
