## Summary
The communal obligation (fard kifaya) is what is required to be brought about by the community of Muslims without regard to who does it: if those who suffice take it up, the rest are released from sin, and if everyone leaves it, none of them is safe from it. Under this section the browser gathers the texts that use the term or state its rule, and the two verses from which al-Shafi'i derived it.

## al-Shafi'i's exposition in al-Risala
Al-Shafi'i makes knowledge a matter of degrees: the knowledge of the general public, of which no adult may remain ignorant, and then a third degree that he describes as "a degree of knowledge which the general public does not reach, and which not every one of the specialists is charged with". Those of the specialists who could reach it are not all permitted to neglect it; if those of them who suffice take it up, another who left it is not held in sin, and the merit in it belongs to whoever took it up over whoever neglected it {cite:risala-shafii p.475}.

Then his interlocutor asks him for his evidence, and he mentions the verses on fighting. He says that they could mean that fighting is upon everyone able to do it, so that no one may hold back from it, like the prayer, the pilgrimage and the alms; and they could mean that the sense of its obligation differs from the obligation of the prayers, and that the obligation in them is aimed at sufficiency, so that whoever takes up the sufficiency has attained the performance of the obligation and the extra merit of the voluntary, and releases whoever held back from sin {cite:risala-shafii p.478}. For the second meaning he cites the verse [[passage/quran-4-95|al-Nisa' 95]], which prefers those who strive over those who sit and promises both groups the best reward {cite:risala-shafii p.479}, and the verse [[passage/quran-9-122|al-Tawba 122]], which tells that the believers were not to go out all together, and that going out and gaining understanding fall to some of them and not others {cite:risala-shafii p.481}.

He then generalises the rule and says: "And so it is with everything in which the obligation is aimed at sufficiency in what occurs: when those of the Muslims who suffice take it up, whoever held back from it is released from sin", and he fears that if they all neglect it together, not one of them who was able to do it would be released from sin {cite:risala-shafii p.481}.

## His examples in al-Risala
Al-Shafi'i counts among this section attending funerals and returning the greeting, and he says that the Muslims have never ceased, from the time God sent His Prophet until today, that the fewest of them gain understanding, some attend funerals, some strive and return the greeting, while others hold back from that {cite:risala-shafii p.483}.

## Among the scholars of legal theory after him
Al-Shawkani mentions the obligation on the community in the division of the obligatory, and says that one who leaves it is not blamed unless no one else has taken it up {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.8}. Al-Ghazali discusses an objection: how can it be said that the communal obligation is upon everyone when the obligation lapses through the act of one? {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.309}. The other meanings of "sufficiency" (kifaya) among the jurists are in the entry on [[concept/kifaya|sufficiency (kifaya)]].
