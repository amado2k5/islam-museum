## Summary
Linguistically, Hijaz comes from the root for barrier, the mountain range that separates the lowland of Tihama from Najd. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6824} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6825} For the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, who limit the Prophetic ruling on the Arabian peninsula to the Hijaz, it covers Mecca, Medina, al-Yamama and its dependencies; the Minhaj lists Ta'if, Wajh, Jedda, Yanbu' and Khaybar among its towns. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6825} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6826} The jurists count four rulings that apply to the Hijaz: no non-Muslim may settle in it, no non-Muslim may be buried in it, no place of worship for non-Muslims may stand in it, and its land pays alms (zakat) with no kharaj tax. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6826} Outside the Hijaz, the rest of the Arabian land such as Yemen and Bahrain is included in the hadith for the Hanafis and Malikis but not for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6826}
