## Summary
Operational planning is the subject of two lessons by [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] in [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]]. He presents them as the complement of [[concept/al-takhtit-al-istratiji|strategic planning]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:02:30} An operational plan usually covers a year, while a strategic plan covers five years or more. Operational planning belongs to a department, section, team or committee. Higher bodies may also have operational plans, but strategic plans belong only to the levels above a department. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:03:30} He says the same method serves a single project, such as a student trip, and makes up much of a feasibility study. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:03:30} He teaches it as six steps built on "SWOT, modified the Suwaidan way", in his words. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:04:30} The method and its figures are his teaching.

## Step 1: SWOT as he reads it
He says SWOT came from Harvard, then passed through Britain and Egypt to the Arab world, where, in his view, it was misread. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:08:30} He rejects the common reading that strengths and weaknesses are internal and opportunities and threats external. For him strengths and weaknesses exist now, inside or outside the organisation. An opportunity is "a positive thing that does not exist, which can be brought about within the period of the plan", and a threat is the negative counterpart. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:10:30} The planning team of five to seven writes three to ten strengths and three to ten weaknesses. It writes no more than three opportunities, because beyond three, he says, they become formal rather than real. Threats come last. That gives at least eight points and at most twenty-six. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:19:00}

## Steps 2 and 3: goals and SMART goals
A goal, in his definition, is "a statement of the result to be achieved, within a quantitative measure and a set date". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:20:30} Turning the analysis into goals is mechanical. Each strength becomes a goal to keep or grow it, each weakness a goal to remedy it, each opportunity a goal to seize it, and each threat a goal to guard against it. That is why, he says, half the work of SWOT lies in the first step. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:25:00} Each goal is then made SMART: specific, measurable, agreed, realistic and time-bound. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:29:00} He says the "A" he learned in America means agreed between the planner and the person who carries out the goal. He calls the Arab reading of it as "achievable" an error. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:33:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:35:30} Realistic means hard but possible. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:37:00} His worked example turns "improving human resources" into "training seventy employees on four basic computer courses within six months". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:40:30}

## Step 4: means
Means are the procedures and activities that reach a goal. "Every goal is a goal for the one who carries it out, but a means for the leadership," he says, and means too must be SMART. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:45:00} He names their sources: one's own experience; competitors' methods, copied and changed by at least fifteen per cent; serious books and references; visits to advanced organisations; and creativity. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:48:00} He warns that without fresh sources, methods in Islamic work, such as a curriculum or a structure, can come to be treated as sacred by habit. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep17 00:45:30} Each goal gets its own page with four columns: the means, who carries it out, the deadline and the cost. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:05:00} Details are left to those who carry it out. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:11:30}

## Steps 5 and 6: timeline and agenda
The timeline gives one page to each month. Every means is copied under its month, with a column marked "done" for follow-up. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:16:00} The load is spread over the year, lighter in summer and Ramadan. Monthly follow-up checks the past month and the coming one. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:18:30} The agenda gives one page to each person or unit, built from the timeline. He keeps these pages on his phone to see who is working. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:22:00} He warns that in the end every other sheet is set aside; only the timeline and the agenda remain. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:13:00}

## Revision and criticism
On people he quotes [[person/jack-welch|Jack Welch]]'s rule from his 2005 book "Winning". Each year staff are sorted into the effective twenty per cent, to be rewarded; the middle seventy, to be trained; and the bottom ten, to be let go. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:24:00} He frames dismissal with a reminder that the employer is not the provider of anyone's livelihood. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:26:00} A twenty-year strategic plan, he says, gets a quick review every year and a full review every five. Next year's operational plan is begun at the start of the current year and approved within about three months. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:36:00} He then sets out criticisms of planning, citing Henry Mintzberg's "The Rise and Fall of Strategic Planning" (1994): that it kills creativity, assumes a stable world, can be ignored by a new leader, and is resisted by those who hold the money. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:37:30} His answer is that planning is a tool in the leader's hand: "planning does not make the failure succeed, but it helps the successful". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep18 00:43:00}
