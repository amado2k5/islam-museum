## Nature
Some people call it aliyun and others alariyun, both names being derived from the curdling of milk, for it curdles it like rennet; it is a plant with a leaf and rods like the leaf and rods of the plant called harinan, bearing a white flower inclined to yellow, fine, dense and abundant and sweet-scented, and it grows in thickets and groves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.621}

## Actions and properties
Its flower, applied as a poultice, benefits an outburst of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.621}

## Wounds and ulcers
So too its flower and leaf benefit burns by fire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.621}

## Joints
It is sometimes mixed with a wax ointment made with rose oil and tempered with salt until it whitens, and so benefits weariness and the pain of exhaustion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.621}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists its root as stirring the desire for intercourse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.621}
