## Nature and selection
A plant that resembles absinthe, but this one is green and has a sticky moisture. One variety has shorter branches and larger leaves, with small fine white and yellow leaves, and it appears in spring and summer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}-{cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Galen is quoted as saying that the two are herbs close in temperament and called by the same name {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold and moist in the first degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Very thinning and opening; its poultice stops the superfluities from being drawn to the organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.

## Uses by organ
Head: as a poultice and as a lotion it helps cold headache. Its boiled decoction is safe, and it helps blockage of the nose and cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. Excretory organs: it breaks up stone in the kidney, promotes menses when sat in its decoction, helps its sores, expels the afterbirth and the foetus, and helps a closed womb by opening it, and a hardened womb when drunk or applied as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}. These are the book's statements on menses and the foetus, recorded as written and not as advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.

## Substitutes
No substitute is named in the passage read here. The measures of drink given by the book are not transcribed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.388}.
