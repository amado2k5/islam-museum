## Summary
In the second chapter on the veins Ibn Sina describes the gate vein: its end sunk in the liver divides in five, its end at the concavity divides in eight, two small and six larger, which feed the stomach, spleen, omentum and intestines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.87-88}.

## Its end within the liver
Its end sunk in the cavity of the liver divides in five and branches until it reaches the convex ends of the liver, and a vein goes from it to the gall bladder; these branches are like the roots of a tree reaching down into the depth of its source {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.87}. The end by its concavity, as soon as it leaves the liver, divides in eight: two small and six larger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.87}.

## The two small parts
One joins the intestine called the twelve-finger, the duodenum, to draw food from it, and may send branches into the body called the pancreas. The other spreads in the lower parts of the stomach and at the gatekeeper, the stomach's lower mouth, to take food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.88}.

## The six larger parts
The first goes to the flat side of the stomach to feed its outside, since the inside meets the food and is fed by contact. The second goes to the spleen; before reaching it, branches feed the pancreas, and after joining it a branch returns to divide in the left side of the stomach. When it passes into the middle of the spleen one part rises and one descends: the rising one spreads in the upper half of the spleen, and one part comes out to the convexity of the stomach and splits in two, one in the outer left of the stomach and one diving to the stomach's mouth to push to it the acrid, sour surplus of black bile so it leaves with the wastes, and to tickle that mouth with a tickling that wakes appetite, as he said earlier; the descending part goes to the lower half of the spleen, and one to the omentum to feed it. The third goes left to the channels of veins around the rectum to absorb what is left of food in the waste. The fourth spreads like hair, some on the right of the stomach's convexity opposite what comes from the spleen, some to the right of the omentum opposite what arrives from the left. The fifth spreads in the channels around the colon to take food. The sixth mostly spreads around the jejunum, and the rest around the thin intestines joined to the caecum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.88}.
