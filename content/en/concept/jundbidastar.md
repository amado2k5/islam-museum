## Nature and selection
It is the testicle of a sea animal, taken as a pair joined at one root, with a thin skin that breaks at the slightest touch {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}. The best is the two joined together, since that is not adulterated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}. Adulteration is with jawshir and gum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}. Whoever takes it must let out the moisture held inside, which is like honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}. The editor's footnote calls it an aromatic fatty substance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: finer and stronger than any hot, drying substance; hot at the end of the third to the fourth degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}. Actions: it dissolves wind; rubbed on the body it warms it, and its waxy inner part is very biting and very heating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: helps hot swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.
Wounds and ulcers: helps deadly ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.
Joints: warms and helps the nerve, and helps wet tremor, wet convulsion, wet lockjaw, numbness and palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.
Head: helps forgetfulness; dissolves cold and windy headaches as a poultice or fumigation; helps cold deafness, and nothing is better for wind in the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.
Respiratory and chest: its steam, inhaled, helps swellings and ailments of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.
Food: given with vinegar for hiccups, and it causes thirst {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417}.
Excretory organs: with vinegar it removes colic, dissolves wind, moves the menses, and expels the afterbirth when taken with fennel and honey after a bleeding; the book says it moves the menses without harm, expels the foetus, and removes the cold and wind of the womb and the cold of the testicle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.417} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}. These are the book's listings, labelled.
Poisons: helps bites of vermin; the book says it is an antidote to choking hellebore {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}.
