## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that when a drug brings colic, disturbance, drowsiness and headache and does not purge, one resorts to an enema and suppositories, drinks three kurum of mastic in lukewarm water and follows with astringents, quince and apples, and that if bad signs appear and the movement is upward, venesection is needed, even two or three days later, for fear the humours move to a principal organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.284}.

## The steps
If the drug does not purge and brings colic, disturbance, drowsiness, headache, stretching and yawning, one resorts to the known enema and suppositories, and the patient drinks three kurum of mastic in lukewarm water; the drug may also be made to work by drinking astringents and then taking quince and apples, which squeeze the mouth of the stomach and what lies beneath it and calm nausea. If the enema does not help and bad signs appear, such as tension of the body and bulging of the eyes, with the movements going upward, venesection is unavoidable. And if the drug does not purge and no bad signs follow, it is still right to follow with venesection, even after two or three days, since otherwise the humours are feared to move to some principal organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.284}.
