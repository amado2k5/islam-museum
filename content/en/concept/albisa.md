## Definition
Libas is what covers the body and protects against heat and cold; albisa is its plural. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3267} The word libus, with the l vowelled, names what is worn, such as armour. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3267}

## Rulings
- Obligatory: what covers the nakedness and protects against heat and cold. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3267}
- Recommended: what gives the basic adornment and shows blessing, and dressing well at gatherings and festivals, following Aisha's report of two garments for Friday, so long as it is not for pride. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3267}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3268}
- Disliked: garb that suggests pride and vanity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3268}
- Forbidden: dress worn for pride, and silk and gold for men, except in necessity or illness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3268}

## Materials
The default is that materials are lawful unless a text forbids them, as silk is for men. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3269} Hides of dead animals become clean and lawful by tanning. Wool, hair and fur of an animal that may be eaten are clean and lawful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3269}
- Predator skins: most jurists allow their use after tanning; Abu Ya'la held them forbidden absolutely, citing the prohibition on riding tigers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3270}

## Colours
- White is recommended and used for shrouds, following the report of Samura ibn Jundab. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3270}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3271}
- Red: some Hanafis and Hanbalis disliked pure red for men; others allowed it if not dyed with saffron or safflower, citing a red garment the Prophet wore on feast days. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3271}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3272}
- Black: permitted to men and women without dislike. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3272}
- Yellow: permitted unless dyed with safflower or saffron. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3272}
- Green: some jurists recommended it, citing the green garments of the people of Paradise in the Qur'an. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3273}
- Striped: permitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3273}
- Saffron-dyed clothing: the Shafi'is forbade it to men and allowed it to women; the Hanafis and Hanbalis disliked it for men. A muhrim may not wear saffron- or safflower-dyed clothes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3274}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3275}

## Images, crosses and verses
- Clothing with images of animals is forbidden, and so is a cross on clothing; pictures without living beings are permitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3273}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3274}
- Praying in clothes with animal images or crosses is forbidden, though the prayer is valid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3274}

## Transparent clothing
Clothing thin enough to show the colour of the skin is forbidden; clothing that shows the shape of a limb while covering the nakedness is disliked. The report of Usama about the Coptic cloth and the undergarment is cited. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3275}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3276}

## Clothing that makes one conspicuous, and imitation
Clothing that makes its wearer conspicuous is disliked, and imitation of unbelievers' dress is disliked, following the hadith that whoever imitates a people is one of them. Such clothing is forbidden where worn for pride or show. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3276}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3277}

## Impure and usurped clothing
- Wearing impure clothing to cover the nakedness outside prayer is permitted. In prayer the schools differ; the Hanbalis require covering with it, while Abu Hanifa distinguishes an impure material from one that is merely soiled. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3277}
- A naked person may not take clothing from its owner by force for prayer, and makes tayammum if he finds none. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3277}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3278}

## Festival clothing and the dress of scholars
- Recommended to dress well at festivals, in new and fine clothes, white being best. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3278}
- Garb that displays wealth or vanity is disliked, and excessive width is disliked by the Malikis and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3279}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3280}
- Scholars are recommended to wear distinctive dress so that people may ask them; the Malikis and Hanbalis dislike excessive width for them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3279}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3280}
- The dhimmis must be distinguished in dress from the Muslims. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3280}

## Other rulings
- Clothing is due in maintenance according to the means of the one who provides it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3280}
- For expiation of an oath, feeding ten poor persons with clothing counts when it is called clothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3280}
- A bankrupt is left the clothes he needs; fine clothes are sold only if the proceeds do not leave him what suits him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3280}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3281}
- The spoils of a slain enemy, including clothes, go to the slayer if the imam so declared. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3281}
- The rule is to begin with the right when dressing and with the left when undressing, and to say the prescribed words on putting on a new garment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3281}
