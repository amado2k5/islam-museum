## Summary
In chapter 27 al-Battani reports that earlier astronomers disagreed on the length of the year; some ancient Egyptians and Babylonians gave it as three hundred and sixty-five days, a quarter, and a part of a hundred and twenty of a day, counting it from the Sun's leaving a fixed star until it returns to it, and he reports Ptolemy's judgement that this view is extremely weak {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}.

## Ptolemy's argument
As reported by al-Battani, Ptolemy said that if this view were allowed, one could equally say that the year is the Sun's leaving Saturn or another wandering planet until it returns to it, and that is a view plainly false. The argument is one of parity: if the measure is a body that itself moves, the measure moves with it {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}. The definition of the year that stands against this view is in [[concept/zij-sabi-year-between-equinox-or-solstice-returns|the year between returns to an equinox or solstice point]]. The passage shows al-Battani using [[person/batlamus|Ptolemy]] and the [[work/al-majisti|Almagest]] as the authority on which his own observation is built {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.65}.
