## Overview
The author clarifies the difference between public ownership and the ownership of the public treasury (bayt al-mal): the public treasury owns resources that the state may dispose of according to the public interest, whereas public ownership is dedicated to the Muslim community as a whole {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 56} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 64}.

## Al-Subki's View
The author quotes Taqi al-Din al-Subki that river land is not the property of the public treasury (bayt al-mal), that the ruler may neither grant it as a fief nor confer ownership of it, and that people share in it like uncultivated land (mawat) {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 64} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 65}.

## The Public Treasury (Bayt al-Mal) in the Author's View (pp. 304–332)
The author notes that zakat has a treasury separate from the public-welfare treasury (bayt mal al-masalih), with independent revenues and expenditures {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 304} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 313}. Expenditures from the public-welfare treasury include salaries, roads, mosques, and redistribution {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 300}. The public treasury inherits when there is no heir, serving as the heir of last resort {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 332}, and pays the blood money (diyah) for a slain person if there is no 'aqilah and no one else {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 331}.
