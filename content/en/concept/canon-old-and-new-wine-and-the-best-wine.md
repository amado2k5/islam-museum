## Summary
Ibn Sina distinguishes old wine, which is in the nature of medicine, from new wine, which harms the liver, and describes the best wine as one between old and new, clear, white tending to red, of pleasant scent, neither sour nor sweet; he names the washed wine and the treatment of a sting after drinking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.

## Old and new
Old wine, Ibn Sina says, is in the position of a medicine and not of a food, while new wine harms the liver and leads to what is called the liver's rising, through its bloating and its loosening of the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.

## The best wine
The best wine is the one between old and new, clear and white tending to red, pleasant in scent, moderate in taste, neither sour nor sweet. The good wine known as the washed is made with three parts of thyme to one of water boiled until a third is gone. One stung by drinking wine sucks pomegranate and drinks cold water afterwards, takes wine of wormwood the next day, and uses the bath after taking a little food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.
