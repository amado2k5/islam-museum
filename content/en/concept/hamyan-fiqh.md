## Summary
Himyan in the lexical sense is a purse in which provision is kept and tied at the waist; it is a loan word and is used in the law in the same sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16563}

## Wearing it in ihram
The Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is permit a pilgrim in ihram to tie the himyan at his waist, relying on a report of Ibn Abbas. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16563}
The Malikis restrict this to provision for the pilgrim and his family, not for trade, and to a tie on the skin rather than on the waist-wrapper. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16564}
Abu Yusuf disliked a silk girdle for the pilgrim, and Ibn Umar, in the sounder of his two reports, disliked tying the himyan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16564}
The Hanbalis permit it only when it holds provision. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16564}

## Spoils and theft
Most jurists count the himyan within the spoils; the Malikis and one Shafi'i view do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16564}
Most jurists hold a thief who cuts a himyan and takes its money liable to the hadd punishment; the Hanafis distinguish a purse outside the sleeve from one reached through the sleeve. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16565}
