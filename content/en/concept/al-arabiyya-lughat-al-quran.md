## Summary
The Qur'an speaks of its own tongue in fifteen verses. Eleven of them describe the Book, or its tongue, or its ruling, as Arabic; four say that a messenger is sent in the tongue of his people, that the Qur'an was made easy in the tongue of the Prophet, and that had it come down upon some of the non-Arabs they would not have believed in it. This page gathers what four Sunni commentators said on these verses: al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi, each statement attributed to its author.

## The verses
The verses that describe Arabic: [[passage/quran-12-2|Yusuf 2]], [[passage/quran-13-37|al-Ra'd 37]], [[passage/quran-16-103|al-Nahl 103]], [[passage/quran-20-113|Ta-Ha 113]], [[passage/quran-26-195|al-Shu'ara 195]], [[passage/quran-39-28|al-Zumar 28]], [[passage/quran-41-3|Fussilat 3]], [[passage/quran-41-44|Fussilat 44]], [[passage/quran-42-7|al-Shura 7]], [[passage/quran-43-3|al-Zukhruf 3]], [[passage/quran-46-12|al-Ahqaf 12]].

The four other verses: [[passage/quran-14-4|Ibrahim 4]], on every messenger being sent in the tongue of his people; [[passage/quran-19-97|Maryam 97]] and [[passage/quran-44-58|al-Dukhan 58]], on the Qur'an being made easy in the tongue of the Prophet; and [[passage/quran-26-198|al-Shu'ara 198]], on the supposition that it came down upon some of the non-Arabs.

## Why it came down in Arabic
**Because those first addressed by it were Arabs.** At the verse of Yusuf al-Tabari says that the Book was sent down as an Arabic recitation to the Arabs because their tongue and speech are Arabic, so it came down in their tongue that they might grasp and understand it {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 12:2}. At the verses of Ibrahim and al-Shura he states that God sends every messenger in the tongue of his people so as to make things clear to them and establish the proof {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 14:4} {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 42:7}. Al-Baghawi says the same {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 14:4}, and al-Qurtubi reports from Sufyan al-Thawri that the book of every prophet came in the tongue of his people {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 43:3}.

**Because Arabic is the most eloquent of languages.** At the verse of Yusuf Ibn Kathir adds a reason that lies in the language itself: in his view the language of the Arabs is the most eloquent, clearest and widest of languages and the fullest in conveying the meanings that stir in the soul. So the noblest of books was sent down in the noblest of languages, upon the noblest of messengers, through the envoy of the noblest of angels, in the noblest region of the earth, and its sending down began in the noblest month of the year, which is Ramadan {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 12:2}. He repeats the point at the verse of al-Dukhan {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 44:58}.

**To cut off excuses.** At the verse of al-Shu'ara al-Tabari holds that mentioning the Arabic tongue at this place informs the polytheists of Quraysh that it came down in their own tongue, so that they could not say that it came down in a tongue other than ours and that they were therefore turning away from it and not listening because they did not understand it. Their turning away, then, was not from lack of understanding but from denial and arrogance {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 26:195}.

**To establish its inimitability.** At the verse of Fussilat al-Qurtubi says that it came down in their tongue so that the meaning of inimitability might be settled by it, since they were the people who knew the kinds of speech best, in verse and in prose. When they were unable to match it, that was among the strongest proofs that it was from God; and had it been in a foreign tongue they would have said that they had no knowledge of that tongue {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 41:44}.

**Then others follow them.** Al-Baghawi mentions that the Prophet was sent from among the Arabs in their tongue and that the other peoples follow them, after which messengers were dispatched to the outlying lands to translate for them {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 14:4}. Al-Qurtubi says that non-Arabs and others have no argument once what they can understand has been translated for them {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 14:4}.

## Had it been foreign
The verse of Fussilat 44 supposes that the Qur'an had come in a foreign tongue. The four commentators report from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Mujahid, al-Suddi, Ibn Abbas and Ikrima that the polytheists would then have said: a foreign book and an Arab messenger? {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 41:44} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 41:44}. In another reading reported from al-Hasan al-Basri the sense is: why was part of it not foreign and part Arabic? Ibn Kathir describes it as more extreme in obstinacy {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 41:44}.

At the verses of al-Shu'ara 198 and 199 the commentators say that their disbelief was not on the side of language: had a foreigner recited it to them they would not have believed in it {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 26:198} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 26:198}.

## "Clear" and "without crookedness"
Al-Baghawi explains "mubin" as eloquent {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 16:103}, al-Qurtubi says that it means the most eloquent that Arabic can be {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 16:103}, and Ibn Kathir says that it is the evident, plain, manifest word that cuts off excuses and establishes the proof {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 26:195}.

As for the description of the Qur'an in the verse of al-Zumar as "without crookedness", al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi report several sayings: not inconsistent, from Ibn Abbas and al-Dahhak; not contradictory, from Uthman ibn Affan; without confusion, from Mujahid; without error of speech, from Bakr ibn Abd Allah al-Muzani; and without doubt, from al-Suddi {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 39:28} {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 39:28}.

## The tongue of Quraysh
At the verse of al-Shu'ara al-Baghawi reports from Ibn Abbas that the clear Arabic tongue is the tongue of Quraysh {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 26:195}. In Sahih al-Bukhari Uthman ibn Affan said to those who copied the codices with Zayd ibn Thabit: if you and Zayd differ on anything of the Qur'an, write it in the tongue of Quraysh, for it came down in their tongue; the report is in the museum's register at [[passage/bukhari-4987|al-Bukhari 4987]].

## Reports the commentators transmit
At the verse of al-Shu'ara Ibn Kathir transmits, from Ibn Abi Hatim, a report that a man said to the Prophet: how eloquent you are, we have never seen anyone more Arab than you; he answered that this was his right and that the Qur'an came down in his tongue. He also transmits from Sufyan al-Thawri the saying that revelation came down only in Arabic and that each prophet then translated it for his people {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 26:195}. The commentator gives these reports with their chains and passes no judgement on them in the passage that was read.

## Translation
Al-Qurtubi took the verse of Fussilat 44 as evidence that the Qur'an is Arabic and that, once rendered from Arabic into another language, it is no longer the Qur'an {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 41:44}.
