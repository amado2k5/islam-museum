## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the crescents are set times for people and for the pilgrimage: the sun marks the day and the moon the month. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.806} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.810}

## The crescent and the set times
Sha'rawi says the crescent is seen at the start of the month and so marks its beginning, and that by it are known the times of Ramadan, the feast, the pilgrimage, a woman's waiting period and the sacred months, while the sun fixes the day. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.811}

## Time and place
Sha'rawi holds that a set time joins time and place: Rabigh is the station for Egyptians whatever the hour, while the fast is fixed by time in any place. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.810}

## Worship turning through the year
Sha'rawi says that reckoning by the moon ties worship to the changing seasons, so that Ramadan is never always in summer or always in winter, and the pilgrimage turns through the months, easing it for people of cold or hot regions. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.812}

## Limits of this batch
Sha'rawi's statements on astronomy, eclipses and the size of the sun were not checked against a scientific source and are not treated as verified here. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.809}
