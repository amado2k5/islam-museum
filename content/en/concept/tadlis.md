## Summary
Tadlis is hiding a defect in goods from the buyer. It is forbidden by text and, in the majority view, gives a right of option in an exchange when the deceived party did not know of the defect before the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4897} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4898}

## Definition and the hadith sense
In sale, tadlis is hiding a defect in the goods from the buyer; the word is also used for concealing the chain of narration in hadith. Among the hadith scholars there are two kinds: concealing the chain, which is reporting from someone one met what one did not hear from him, as if one had heard it; and concealing a teacher's name, which is reporting from a teacher under a name or kunya or description that hides who he is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4897} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4898}

## Related terms
- Khalaba (deception): broader, since it may be by lying as well. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4898}
- Talbis (confusion): broader, since it covers hiding attributes or facts too. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4898}
- Taghrir (enticing into risk) and ghish (cheating) are broader still, since tadlis is limited to concealing a defect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4898} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4899}

## Ruling
Jurists agree that tadlis is forbidden by text: the hadith that the two parties to a sale have the option until they part, and that if they speak truth and make clear, their sale is blessed, but if they lie and hide, the blessing of the sale is taken away; and the hadith "whoever cheats us is not of us". The Prophet is also reported to have said that one who sells a defect without showing it remains under God's displeasure. The judge disciplines the deceiver for the right of God and the right of people. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4898} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4899}

## Tadlis in exchanges
Jurists agree that any tadlis that changes the price gives a right of option, such as tasrijat (holding back a ewe's milk) to make the buyer think she gives much milk, or dyeing goods a desirable colour. They rely on the hadith of the tasriyya, giving the buyer the choice of keeping the animal or returning it with a sa' of dates; other acts that make the buyer expect a quality that is absent are treated alike. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4899}
A right of option does not arise by tadlis alone: the deceived party must not have known of the defect before the contract, and the defect must not be plain. The right applies in every exchange, such as sale and hire. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4899}
Verbal tadlis is treated as acts are, such as lying about the price in trust sales (murabaha, tawliya and hatiyata). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4899}

## Tadlis in marriage
The majority of jurists (Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis) hold that if one spouse deceives the other by hiding a defect that gives a right of option, the deceived spouse has that right. If one spouse stipulates in the contract a quality of perfection, such as Islam, virginity or youth, and it proves absent, the deceived party may annul the marriage. Abu Hanifa and Abu Yusuf hold that neither spouse has a right of annulment for a defect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4899} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4900} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4901}
If the marriage is annulled before consummation the dower falls; if it is annulled after consummation, the wife keeps the dower, and the husband may claim it back from the one who deceived him, according to the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4900} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4901}

## Discipline of the deceiver
The deceiver is disciplined by a discretionary penalty chosen by the judge as deterrent, a point the jurists agree on, as for any sin without a fixed penalty or expiation, in addition to the order to return the goods. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.4901}

## Tadlis in Ibn al-Salah and al-Khatib
[[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] makes "knowing tadlis and the ruling on the mudallas" his twelfth type and divides it in two. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 73} **The first is tadlis of the chain**: relating from someone one met what one did not hear from him, giving the impression of having heard it, or from a contemporary one never met, giving the impression of having met and heard him; there may be one or more intermediaries, and such a narrator avoids "he informed us" or "he told us", saying instead "so-and-so said" or "from so-and-so". {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 73} An example: Ali ibn Khashram relates that they were with [[person/sufyan-ibn-uyayna|Ibn Uyayna]], who said "al-Zuhri"; asked "did al-Zuhri tell you?", he fell silent, then said "al-Zuhri"; asked "did you hear it from al-Zuhri?", he said: no, neither from al-Zuhri nor from anyone who heard it from him — Abd al-Razzaq told me from Ma'mar from al-Zuhri. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 74} **The second is tadlis of teachers**: relating a hadith heard from a teacher but naming him, giving his kunya, lineage or a description by which he is not known, so that he is not recognised — as when [[person/ibn-mujahid|Abu Bakr ibn Mujahid]] the Qur'an reader, narrating from Abd Allah ibn Abi Dawud, said "Abd Allah ibn Abi Abd Allah", and from al-Naqqash said "Muhammad ibn Sanad", after one of his ancestors. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 74}

**The ruling.** The first kind is strongly disliked and condemned by most scholars, [[person/shuba-ibn-al-hajjaj|Shu'ba]] being among its fiercest critics: "tadlis is the brother of lying", and "I would rather commit adultery than practise tadlis" — which Ibn al-Salah takes as hyperbole to deter. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 74} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 75} In [[work/al-kifaya-fi-ilm-al-riwaya|al-Kifaya]] [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|al-Khatib]] gathers these and other sayings, such as [[person/hammad-ibn-zayd|Hammad ibn Zayd]]'s "tadlis is lying", with his appeal to the hadith "one who boasts of what he was not given is like one wearing two garments of falsehood", and [[person/abd-allah-ibn-al-mubarak|Ibn al-Mubarak]]'s "we would rather fall from the sky than practise tadlis on a hadith". {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 355} {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 356}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: a distinction (Ibn al-Salah, the sound view for him)**: what a mudallis relates with an ambiguous expression that does not state hearing takes the ruling of the mursal and its kinds, while what he relates with an expression stating connection, such as "I heard", "he told us" or "he informed us", is accepted and used as proof; the two Sahihs contain a great deal from such narrators — Qatada, al-A'mash, the two Sufyans, Hushaym and others — because tadlis is not lying but a kind of creating an impression. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 75}
- **Sunni: discrediting outright**: a group of hadith scholars and jurists held that one known for it is discredited and his narration is never accepted, whether he states hearing or not. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 75}
:::

The second kind is lighter for Ibn al-Salah: it wastes the identity of the person narrated from and makes it harder to learn about him; how disliked it is depends on the motive — the teacher being untrustworthy, dying late so that lesser men shared in hearing from him, being younger than the narrator, or being narrated from so often that one prefers not to repeat his name the same way; several authors indulged in it, among them al-Khatib, who was fond of it in his works. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 76} The editor Nur al-Din Itr names among works on mudallis narrators Burhan al-Din al-Halabi's al-Tabyin and Ibn Hajar's Ta'rif Ahl al-Taqdis. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 76} See also [[concept/muanan|chains linked by "from"]].
