## Summary
Khafa\' is lexically concealment or disclosure of a thing, one of the words with two opposite senses, and the jurists use it for being hidden; for the usulis it is a word whose meaning is hidden by something in the form of the word or outside it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7732} It is near to ishtibah, confusion, and to jahl, belief in a thing contrary to what it is; hiddenness may cause ignorance of existence or of place. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7732}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7733} The usulis divide words by the degree of hidden meaning into four ranks; the first, the hidden (khafi), is one whose meaning is obscured by something outside the form, as with the word thief which is hidden for the pickpocket and the grave-robber; the hiddenness may add or lessen what the ruling depends on, and the grave-robber is disputed: cut by the majority, not cut by Abu Hanifa and Muhammad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7733} The second rank, the ambiguous (mushkil), is one whose meaning is obscured by entering a form that is not known except by a sign that marks it off. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7733}
