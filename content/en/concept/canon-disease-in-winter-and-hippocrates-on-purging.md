## Summary
Ibn Sina notes that sound bodies seldom fall ill in winter and that, if one does, the cause is great, so treatment and evacuation are hastened, since the innate heat grows strong in winter through confinement; he then reports Hippocrates on preferring purging to venesection and disliking vomiting in winter while approving it in summer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.

## Rare illness and strong innate heat
Sound bodies seldom fall ill in winter; if one does, treatment and evacuation are hastened if required, for illness does not befall in winter unless the cause is great, especially if hot, because the innate heat, the governing agent, grows very strong in winter by what is spared from dissipation and gathers through confinement, and all the natural powers do their work well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.

## Hippocrates' view
Hippocrates is reported to find purging in winter fitting rather than venesection, to dislike vomiting then and approve it in summer, because the humours float in summer and incline to settle in winter, and Ibn Sina says one should follow him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.
