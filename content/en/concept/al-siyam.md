## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that fasting is abstention from the appetites of the belly and of desire from dawn to sunset, a discipline found in earlier religions too. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.764} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.765}

## Fasting as training in steadiness
Sha'rawi says fasting weakens the drive of the material and trains a person to taste steadiness so that it lasts beyond Ramadan, and that Ramadan was chosen so that the effect of steadiness spreads to all times. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.765} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.766}

## The sick and the traveller
Sha'rawi reads the verse as passing the fast to other days after travel or recovery, holds that one who fasts while sick or travelling does not have it counted, and answers those who make the leave optional that what needs no interpretation is preferred. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.767} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.771} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.772}

## The counted days
Sha'rawi says fasting was first set for a counted number of days, which his own judgement inclines to take as three in each month; this is his personal reading, not a text. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.771}

## Limits of this batch
He reports the view that breaking the fast on a journey is optional and rejects it; the jurists' views on breaking the fast on a journey, the academic view and other views are not entered. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.771}
