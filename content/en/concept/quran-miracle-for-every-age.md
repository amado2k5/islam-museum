## Summary
Muhammad Mutawalli al-Sha'rawi holds that the Quran's inimitability does not end with its revelation, but gives each generation a sign in what that generation excels at. This is his view and is attributed to him; other views are not yet presented. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.12}

## A challenge for every age
Sha'rawi says the Quran came challenging the Arabs in language and eloquence, and because it is a religion for all people it had to challenge others in what they excelled at. He cites the opening verses of [[passage/quran-30-1|Al-Rum 1]] and [[passage/quran-30-4|Al-Rum 4]], which reported the Romans' victory after their defeat by the Persians, and counts this as a sign for non-Arabs at the time of revelation. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.12} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.13}

## The example of the spread earth
Sha'rawi cites [[passage/quran-50-7|Qaf 7]] to argue that the word "spread" does not conflict with the knowledge that the earth is round, since the earth appears spread out to an observer at any point on it, which would only be the case if the earth were round. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.13} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.14}

## Author's view
This is Sha'rawi's view and his reasoning, attributed to him here. The views of other scholars on this question are not yet presented and will be added from other sources in a later batch.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh gives two examples of what he calls historical inimitability: [[person/haman|Haman]], who he says was shown by the hieroglyphs to have been a minister of building works, and the prophecy that the Byzantines would defeat the Persians within a few years; he relates that [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] wagered a hundred camels with the polytheists that the Byzantines would win, before wagering was forbidden, and that they did win. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:07:00} He holds that great powers, once defeated, win again only after a generation, citing the two world wars, and that civilisation usually flies on two wings, Persia and Byzantium or America and the Soviet Union, except in the days of Islam's greatness; this is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep28 00:06:30}

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'Wa Yatafakkarun'
[[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] says that because the Qur'an is God's word, preserved in the language of its revelation for fourteen centuries, it must be inimitable in every respect, and that there is 'no angle' from which a neutral person looks at it without seeing some aspect of its inimitability. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep01 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep01 00:04:00} Among these aspects he counts the rhetorical, doctrinal, devotional, moral, legislative, social, scientific, historical, educational, psychological, economic, administrative, unseen, phonetic, affective, preservation, script, challenge, perfection, method, numerical and 'geometric', without claiming they are all. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep02 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep02 00:13:00} He says a verse comes in fixed words in which the people of each age see a meaning, and the meanings widen 'in a harmony that knows no contradiction'; his example is the oath by the earth 'that splits': the early scholars understood the soil splitting for the plant, while he sees in it the network of rifts on the ocean floors. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep17 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep17 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar wa-yatafakkarun/ep17 00:12:00}
