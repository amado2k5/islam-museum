## Summary
The lower jaw alone was given movement, not the upper, and it has three movements, opening, closing and chewing, each with its muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.63-64}.

## Why the lower jaw
Among the reasons for giving the lower jaw movement are that moving the lighter is better, that moving the one free of noble organs is more fitting and safer, and that were the upper jaw easy to move its joint with the head could not be made secure {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.63}.

## The muscles
Closing has two muscles known as the temple muscles, small in humans because the part they move is light, and larger and fiercer in other animals for biting and cutting. They are soft since they lie near the brain, and were buried at their origin in the two temple bones and helped by two muscles inside the mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.63-64}. The fibres of the opening muscles begin from the styloid processes behind the ear and join as one muscle, become a tendon, and spread again into flesh; contracting, they draw the jaw back and down, and natural weight helps, so two suffice. Chewing has two muscles, one on each side, triangular, with different directions of contraction so that grinding and chewing are accomplished {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.64}.
