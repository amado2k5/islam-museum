## Definition
Ma'ruf is what the Law counts good, and munkar is what it counts bad; commanding the first and forbidding the second is the duty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3386} In the usage of the jurists, hisba is commanding right and forbidding wrong, one of the Islamic offices, which falls outside the duties of rulers and judges. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3387}

## Ruling
The imams agree on its legitimacy; al-Nawawi and Ibn Hazm report the consensus that it is obligatory, citing the verse on a community that calls to good and commands right and forbids wrong, and the hadith "whoever of you sees a wrong, let him change it with his hand...". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3387}
- The first view: a communal obligation, the view of most Sunni scholars. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3387}
- The second view: an individual obligation in certain cases, such as a wrong known only to oneself where one can remove it, a wife or child's wrong, and the officer of hisba. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3387}
- The third: recommended, the view of al-Hasan al-Basri and Ibn Shubruma. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3387}
- The fourth: distinctions. What is obligatory to do or to leave is commanded as obligatory, what is recommended as recommended; another view takes the aim of forbidding to be to remove the wrong or reduce it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3388}

## Pillars and conditions
The pillars are the commander, what is commanded and forbidden, the act of commanding, and the one commanded, as al-Ghazali listed them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3388}
- The commander: sanity, belief, and justice, which is disputed; most scholars do not require freedom from every sin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3388}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3389}
- The matter: the command must be to what the Law counts good and the prohibition to what it forbids; the wrong must be present, visible without spying, and agreed to be wrong. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3389}
- The one commanded: he must be in a state that makes the act a wrong for him; sanity and discernment are not required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3389}

## Degrees
The main degrees are three, following the hadith of Abu Sa'id: by the hand, then by the tongue, then by the heart, "and that is the weakest of faith". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3389}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3390} The highest is the hand, and where it leads to carrying weapons the matter is referred to the ruler. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3389}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3390} The duty of heart-disapproval is never lifted; one who fears for himself must avoid the wrongdoer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3390}

## Payment for hisba
Most jurists hold that a pure act of worship, such as the call to prayer or the hajj, may not be hired for; the Hanafis and Hanbalis forbid paying for commanding right. The Shafi'is, Malikis and later Hanafis permit it, citing the hadith that "the most worthy of what you take a fee for is the Book of God". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3390}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3391} An officer appointed to hisba may be given his living from the treasury, as a judge is, while a volunteer is not paid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3391}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh relays a saying that urges doing good and showing kindness both to those who deserve it and to those who do not: whoever does good to the deserving has put it in the right place, and whoever does good to the undeserving is himself a person of kindness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:08:30}. The Sheikh closes with a prayer that God make those present among the people of kindness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:08:30}.

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme "Liqa' al-Iman"
In episodes 10 and 25 of [[work/likaa-al-iman|Liqa' al-Iman]] [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|al-Sha'rawi]] speaks of the hadith on changing wrong by the hand, then the tongue, then the heart. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:03:00} He says the extremists who cite it overlook the condition of ability: changing by hand belongs to whoever has authority over the wrongdoer, as a father over his son, while one over whom you have no authority you change by a kind word. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:02:30} He holds that changing by the heart is not disapproving inwardly while sitting with the wrongdoer, laughing with him and visiting him, but cutting him out of your life: neither congratulating him, nor consoling him, nor greeting him. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:03:30} He says the Prophet built no prison for the wrongdoer; instead he shut the community away from the wrongdoer, and once he is cut off from society outside and within, people keep to the straight way. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep10 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:04:30} He holds that commanding right is a reminder of God's way for whoever has forgotten or grown careless, and that mutual counsel means each alerting his brother in his moment of weakness and accepting the alert in his own. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:04:00}
