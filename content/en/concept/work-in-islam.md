## Overview
Abu'l-Wafa' al-Maraghi holds work a duty, shown by Islam's rules and the conduct of prophets and righteous: David wove palm-leaves, Idris was a tailor, Zachariah a carpenter, Moses a hireling {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 14}. He distinguishes tawakkul — reliance on God after effort — from tawakul, which he counts as incapacity {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 14}. Work is bounded by the law: it must not violate others' rights or harm their interests {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 16}, and he cites the hadith on a man earning by his own hand.
