## The corroding and the fistulas
The corroding ulcer gone far needs its own qalqandiyun named in the Aqrabadhin, and likewise the fistulas, then the astringent drugs are sprinkled on it; among what was tried then is a dentifrice he described (names left out, the tried report carried on the book's attribution). The middling degree: what he described is taken, made into a sanun and used on the middling corrosion and fistula; the gum is pressed with what he described then rinsed with squill vinegar or a vinegar cooked with a leaf of this kind; what he named is also set on the corroded spot and does well, and the putrefaction-barring resolvents he described — if they fail there is no help but the qalqandiyun. Near it is the rub he described, abided on an hour then followed by a rose-oil rinse; discs of it may be made, dried and stored for need. The named cautery also helps, shedding the corrosion and growing sound flesh, then a sanun of this kind is used which grows flesh and firms the gum; venesection of the jaharik (the editor: a temple vein) benefits it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.281-282}
