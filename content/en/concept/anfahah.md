## Nature and selection
The rennets are many, and each rennet will be mentioned in the chapter on the animal that has it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}. The best of them in kind is the hare's rennet {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}.

## Temperament and actions
All of them are hot, dry and fiery {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}. They dissolve every clot of blood, curdled milk and thick humour, and solidify everything that is liquid; all are cutting and all refining, and without doubt they also dry; Galen says: I do not use the sharp of the rennets where astringency is needed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}.

## Uses by organ
All of them, drunk, help epilepsy, especially the rennet of the qawqi (an animal the book does not describe) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}. They dissolve clotted blood in the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}. They dissolve curdled milk in the stomach when drunk with vinegar, and clotted blood in the stomach, and they are bad for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}. They help ulcers of the intestines, especially the rennet of the foal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}. All are antidotal (badzahari) and help against hemlock, and the most suitable for this are the rennets of the kid, the fawn, the young camel and the lamb; they are given for all poisons and stings, with amounts the book gives and which are not reproduced here; and goat's rennet with tila' (boiled grape must) is the antidote for euphorbium {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that they stop every flow and bleeding in women {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}; that when used as a pessary after the purity they help conception, and when drunk before the purity they prevent it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}; and that they help suffocation of the womb, especially the rennet of the qawqi, and suit pains of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.353}; reports attributed to the book, without recommendation.
