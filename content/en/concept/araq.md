## Summary
In the lexicon, araq is the water that flows from the roots of the hair through the skin. Jurists use it in two senses: what exudes from the body, and a spirit distilled from wine {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11568}.

## Human sweat
The jurists hold human sweat to be pure, whatever the person's religion, sobriety, or state of menstruation or major impurity {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11569}.

## Animal sweat
The Hanafis divide animal sweat into pure, impure, disliked and doubtful. Pure is that of edible animals and the horse; impure that of the dog, the pig and predatory beasts; disliked that of the cat, a free hen, predatory birds and house vermin; doubtful that of the donkey and the mule {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11569} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11570}. The Malikis hold the sweat of every living animal to be pure {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11570}. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis make the sweat follow the animal's ruling, though they differ on which animals are impure {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11570} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11571}.

## Spirit distilled from wine
The spirit distilled from wine has the ruling of wine: it is impure, and drinking it incurs the prescribed punishment, on the preferred view even for a single drop {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11571}.
