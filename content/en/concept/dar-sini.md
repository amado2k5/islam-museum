## Nature and selection
Many kinds, named after the places where they grow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.431}. The best is strongly fragrant, sharp to taste and without sting, of a pure colour; the poor kinds are the Isaniya, the Kandariya, the salikhi, and the rotten; a piece from a single root is best, and crumbs are adulteration {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}. The editor's footnote quotes a modern herbal book; that footnote is not used {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot and dry in the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}. Dioscorides is cited: every cinnamon warms and opens, suits every corruption, draws, and its oil is strongly warming and dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.

## Uses by organ
Skin: its powder is put on freckles and lentil spots, and with vinegar on acne {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Wounds: it suits ringworm and ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Joints: its oil is wonderful for tremor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Head: it helps colds; its oil weighs on the head; it cleanses the brain by dissolving its humours, and it calms earache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Eyes: it helps cataract and dimness, eaten and as a collyrium, and removes thick humour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Chest: described as an irritant that helps cough and clears the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Liver: it opens obstructions of the liver and strengthens it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Stomach: it strengthens the stomach, dries its humours, and helps dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.432}.
Excretory organs: it helps pains and swellings of the womb after it is mixed with a little oil, wax and egg white; it drives urine and the menses, and it can cause expulsion; these are labelled book listings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}.
Fevers: it helps the rigors, especially its oil rubbed on {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}.
Poisons: it helps the bites of pests; a poultice with myrrh for a scorpion sting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}.
Substitutes: the astringent qinnawr of salikha, or kababa, or abhal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}.
