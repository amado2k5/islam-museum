## Overview
The author places free resources at the foundation of his analysis of distribution, considering their contribution to production greater than that of economic resources, and noting that the weak rarely benefit from them while the strong increasingly dominate them {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 16} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 17}.

## Their Impact on Zakah
He uses this to illustrate the wisdom of zakah: livestock grazing freely on public pasture are subject to zakah, whereas stall-fed livestock are not subject to zakah according to the majority of jurists, as he notes {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 17}.


## Permissible Public Resources and Their Limits
Permissible public resources such as pasture, water, fire, land and sea game, and forest wood remain shared and do not become the property of a landowner merely by entering their land, like a fish that leaps into a boat or a bird that enters a plot of land {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 222} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}.

The author explains that water, pasture, and fire are shared among people, and that any scheme to withhold surplus water in order to monopolize pasture is prohibited {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221} (see [[concept/water-distribution-in-islam|Water Distribution]] and [[concept/pasture-in-islam|Pasture]]).
