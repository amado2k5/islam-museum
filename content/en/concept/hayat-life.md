## Summary
Hayat (life) is the opposite of death, a temperament that brings sensation and motion; in God's case it is an attribute that suits Him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7431} The article separates it from ruh, nafs, istihlal (crying out at birth, a sign of life) and death. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7431} The first human life began with the breath of the spirit into Adam; the jurists agree that the life that counts for the offspring begins when the spirit is breathed into the foetus, but they differ on the date. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7432} The majority hold that the foetus's life before ensoulment is legal, shown in inheritance and bequest; some hold life begins when the drop settles in the womb, and full legal life begins at a live birth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7432} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7433} Life ends with the departure of the spirit, and one sign of death is the fixed gaze. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7433}
