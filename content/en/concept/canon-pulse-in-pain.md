## Summary
In the sixteenth chapter Ibn Sina shows that pain changes the pulse through its severity, its seat in a principal organ or its long duration, and sets out its stages from stirring up to collapse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.181}.

## The start of pain and its end
Pain at its start stirs power, moves it to resist and defend, and inflames the heat, so the pulse is great, quick and more widely spaced, since need leads to greatness and speed. When the pain wounds power, the pulse begins to go back until it loses greatness and speed, replaced first by extreme frequency, then by smallness, the wormlike and the ant-like pulse; if it increases it leads to wide spacing and then to death {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.181}.
