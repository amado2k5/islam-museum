## Nature and selection
It is the sisimbar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Temperament
Hot in the third degree and dry toward it; it resists putrefactions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Cosmetic uses
It kills lice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Swellings and pustules
It helps inner swellings and phlegmon of severe hardness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Cooked in vinegar and mixed with rose oil it helps forgetfulness when the head is smeared with it, and likewise confusion of the mind, lethargus and phrenitis; cooked with vinegar and set with rose oil on a headache it helps, and a poultice of the leaf of the wild kind is put on the head and forehead for headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Helpful for hiccup drunk with wine, its seed stronger, and it helps cold swellings of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Excretory organs
It helps worms and gourd-seed worms and provokes urine; the wild kind drunk with wine prevents dribbling of urine, expels the stone and helps colic with wine as well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It expels the dead foetus and provokes menses, especially the rock kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.576}.

## Poisons and bites
It helps stings; wasp stings are dressed with it and it is drunk in oxymel for them (the stated measure is not carried over here) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.
