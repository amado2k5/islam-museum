## Overview
For Ibn Rajab, guarding the tongue is the foundation of all the works of religion. He bases this on the hadith of Mu'adh, the twenty-ninth hadith of the Forty. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.602}

## The hadith of Mu'adh
The Prophet told Mu'adh about the gates of good, then about the head of the matter, its pillar, and the peak of its hump. Then he said: "Shall I not tell you the foundation of all that?" He took hold of his tongue and said: "Restrain this upon yourself." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.602}

Mu'adh asked: "Will we be held to account for what we say?" The answer was that people are cast into the Fire on their faces, or on their noses, because of "the harvest of their tongues." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.602}

## Related hadiths
Ibn Rajab compares this hadith with the hadith of Abu Hurayra: "Whoever believes in God and the Last Day, let him say what is good or keep silent," which is the fifteenth hadith; and with the Prophet's gesture to his tongue when Sufyan al-Thaqafi asked him what he should fear most, which is the twenty-first hadith. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

The subject is connected to the chapter on [[concept/adab|good manners]].
