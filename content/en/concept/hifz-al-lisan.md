## Overview
For Ibn Rajab, guarding the tongue is the foundation of all the works of religion. He bases this on the hadith of Mu'adh, the twenty-ninth hadith of the Forty. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.602}

## The hadith of Mu'adh
The Prophet told Mu'adh about the gates of good, then about the head of the matter, its pillar, and the peak of its hump. Then he said: "Shall I not tell you the foundation of all that?" He took hold of his tongue and said: "Restrain this upon yourself." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.602}

Mu'adh asked: "Will we be held to account for what we say?" The answer was that people are cast into the Fire on their faces, or on their noses, because of "the harvest of their tongues." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.602}

## Related hadiths
Ibn Rajab compares this hadith with the hadith of Abu Hurayra: "Whoever believes in God and the Last Day, let him say what is good or keep silent," which is the fifteenth hadith; and with the Prophet's gesture to his tongue when Sufyan al-Thaqafi asked him what he should fear most, which is the twenty-first hadith. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

The subject is connected to the chapter on [[concept/adab|good manners]].

## Guarding the tongue in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons
The speaker cites a hadith in which a single word may weigh heavily for or against a person. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:00:30}
He lists the sins of the tongue: backbiting, gossip, lying, false witness, obscene speech and false oaths, and says the believer must keep his tongue in check. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:17:00}
He says that scholars warned against four things: speech, looks, steps and thoughts. He cites a hadith of the night journey in an image of something that cannot return, and says a spoken word cannot be recalled. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:24:00}

## The effect of speech
He says that a wound from the tongue lasts longer than a wound from the sword, and that a Muslim guards his speech accordingly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:25:00}
He says that a spear wound may heal but a tongue wound may not, and urges repentance and a renewed covenant when one has spoken wrongly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:42:30}
He says that righteousness is a cheerful face and gentle, truthful speech, and that a word may raise its speaker in Paradise or lower him in God's wrath. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:18:30}

## The speaker's view: speech with knowledge and deed
The speaker's view is that the state of a person speaks louder than his words. He gives the example of a son who promised his father to study, while his conduct over the years contradicted what he said. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:26:30}
He says that humility must be practised and not merely spoken of, and cites a saying attributed to al-Shafi'i that he wished God would show the truth through another person's tongue (needs_source: the saying is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:25:30}
He cites a verse against pursuing what one has no knowledge of, and does not name its sura in the recording. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:29:30}
He says people talk too much and fall into what they should avoid, and cites a report from Aisha about how little Abu Bakr used to speak (needs_source: the report is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:42:00}

## Changing wrong with the tongue
He says that changing wrong with words is gentle advice, as when a person advises a spoiled child, and that one who cannot act has a level of faith in disliking the wrong in the heart; he cites a hadith on this. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep05 00:44:00}
He asks whether people are safe from the tale-bearing of listeners, and cites a hadith that a Muslim is one from whose tongue and hands people are safe. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep19 00:30:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series Nafahat Ramadaniyya (the faults of the tongue)
At the close of the series 'the faults of the tongue' the Sheikh gathers the means of guarding the tongue: holding God's sanctities in awe, remembering death, night prayer, training oneself in silence, the company of the righteous, attending to one's own faults and many acts of obedience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:09:00} He cites hadiths that whoever keeps silent is saved, that a person is safe so long as he is silent, and the command to hold one's tongue. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep06 00:05:30} The details are under [[concept/afat-al-lisan|the faults of the tongue]].

## In al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi opens the Book of Guarding the Tongue with the verses [[passage/quran-50-18|50:18]] and [[passage/quran-89-14|89:14]], and says he wished to add to the recommended remembrances the words that are disliked or forbidden, so that the book would cover the rulings of speech as a whole {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 332}. He sets down that every accountable person should keep his tongue from all speech except where its benefit is plain, and that where speaking and silence are equal in benefit the practice is to hold back, because permitted speech can lead on to what is forbidden or disliked; safety, he says, has no equal {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 332}. From the two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayra, he reports: «من كان يؤمن بالله واليوم الآخر فليقل خيرا أو ليصمت», whoever believes in God and the Last Day should say what is good or keep silent. He calls it an explicit text that one should speak only when the speech is good, and quotes [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] that a person should think before speaking {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 332}. From the same, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, that the best of the Muslims is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe; and from Sahih al-Bukhari, from Sahl ibn Sa'd, the pledge of Paradise to one who guards what is between his jaws and between his legs {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 332}. From al-Tirmidhi's book, from Sufyan ibn Abd Allah: «قل ربي الله ثم استقم», say "my Lord is God", then be upright; asked what he feared most for him, the Prophet took hold of his own tongue. From Uqba ibn Amir, on salvation: «أمسك عليك لسانك وليسعك بيتك وابك على خطيئتك», hold your tongue, let your house suffice you and weep over your fault; al-Nawawi quotes al-Tirmidhi's gradings of both {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 333}. From al-Tirmidhi's book, from Mu'adh, the words «كف عليك هذا», restrain this; and from Abu Hurayra that part of the goodness of a person's Islam is leaving what does not concern him {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 334}.
