## Overview
The jurist's exertion of his utmost effort to derive the legal ruling from the legal evidences {cite:nashat-al-fiqh-al-ijtihadi-azhar p. 11}. Al-Sayis holds it began in the age of revelation; its aim is to uncover God's ruling in the case, not to create legislation — binding on the mujtahid and his followers, bound by the Shari'a's general rules {cite:nashat-al-fiqh-al-ijtihadi-azhar p. 24, 26}. Its ways are many: taking the ruling from the texts' literal sense or from their rationale.

## Ijtihad in al-Shawkani's Irshad al-Fuhul

[[person/al-shawkani|Al-Shawkani]] gives the sixth aim of [[work/irshad-al-fuhul|Irshad al-Fuhul]] to independent reasoning and imitation. He derives ijtihad from juhd, effort and capacity, and among its technical definitions quotes: exerting one's capacity to reach a practical legal ruling by derivation; and al-Amidi's: exhausting one's capacity in seeking a probable judgement on a legal ruling until one feels unable to do more. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.252} The mujtahid, for him, is the jurist who exhausts his capacity to reach a probable judgement on a legal ruling: adult, sane, and possessed of a settled ability to draw rulings from their sources. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.252}

He sets out conditions for this. First, knowledge of the texts of the Book and the Sunna that bear on rulings: he quotes al-Ghazali and Ibn al-Arabi that the legal verses number about five hundred, but holds that the verses from which rulings can be drawn are many times that; for the Sunna, the mujtahid must know what the hadith collections contain, such as the six mother books and what is attached to them, be able to find a hadith in its place and distinguish sound, good and weak, without needing to know them by heart. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.252} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.253} Second, knowledge of the questions of consensus; third, knowledge of Arabic — grammar, morphology, semantics and rhetoric — to the point of a settled ability; fourth, knowledge of usul al-fiqh, which he calls "the pillar of the tent of ijtihad"; fifth, knowledge of the abrogating and the abrogated. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.253} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.254} He reports disagreement over requiring theology and positive law, and counts the criticism of narrators under knowledge of the Sunna and analogy under usul al-fiqh. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.254}

The matter of ijtihad, for him, is a practical legal ruling with no decisive proof, which excludes the obligation of the five prayers and zakat and the plain matters on which the imams agree. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.254} On whether an age may be without a mujtahid he answers those who said so and holds ijtihad easier for later scholars; see [[question/can-an-age-be-without-a-mujtahid|Can an age be without an independent reasoner?]]. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256}
