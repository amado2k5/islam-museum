## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Cyprus: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. Cyprus has a particular case here: an isolated variety, Cypriot Maronite Arabic, recognised by the state as a minority language and classed by UNESCO as severely endangered. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Cyprus", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The dates in the summary are 12 November 2008, when Cyprus declared its recognition of Cypriot Maronite Arabic as a minority language under the European Charter; the 2021 census; and the Council of Europe report of 2025. The summary does not tell how the Maronites and their Arabic variety came to the island, and the museum invents no history for that. For a historical reference on Arabic in the Mediterranean islands see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-al-andalus-wa-siqilliyya|Arabic in al-Andalus and Sicily]], noting that it does not treat Cyprus.

## Legal status
The summary says Greek and Turkish are the official languages of Cyprus {cite:wikipedia-languages-of-cyprus}, and that on 12 November 2008 Cyprus formally recognised Cypriot Maronite Arabic as a non-territorial minority language under Part II of the European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages {cite:verdragenbank-cyprus-ecrml-declaration-2008}. In 2025 the Council of Europe noted government action-plan support and school instruction while warning that it remains in danger of extinction {cite:coe-cypriot-maronite-arabic-in-danger-of-extinction-2025}. These are the summary's statements about its pages, which the museum did not read, and the museum does not assess the effect of the plan.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names Cypriot Maronite Arabic, known as Sanna, an isolated variety sharing features with the northern Syrian and Mesopotamian qeltu dialects, heavily reshaped by contact with Cypriot Greek and lacking diglossia with Modern Standard Arabic {cite:wikipedia-cypriot-arabic}. It is the speech of a Maronite community, so for its link with the Levant see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-syria|Arabic in Syria]]. The museum does not call the variety a separate language or a dialect; that is the question of [[concept/tasnif-al-arabiyya-lughatan-am-lahajat|Classifying Arabic]].

## Official figures
According to the summary, no official census and no UN or UNESCO source publishes a separate count of speakers of Cypriot Maronite Arabic. What it found is the 2021 census of the Statistical Service of Cyprus (CYSTAT): 19,663 speakers of Arabic in total, aggregating all varieties including recent migrants {cite:cystat-2021-census-languages}, and 4,486 Maronite Church adherents {cite:cystat-2021-census-religion}. UNESCO classifies Cypriot Arabic as severely endangered {cite:unesco-atlas-languages-in-danger-pf0000187026}. The two figures are not a count of speakers of the variety, and the museum infers none from them.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: Treaty declaration of Cyprus, European Charter for Regional or Minority Languages (12 November 2008) {cite:verdragenbank-cyprus-ecrml-declaration-2008}, Cypriot Maronite Arabic in danger of extinction, among other findings in new report on Cyprus {cite:coe-cypriot-maronite-arabic-in-danger-of-extinction-2025}, CYSTAT 2021 Census, languages table (predefined/40) {cite:cystat-2021-census-languages} and CYSTAT 2021 Census, religion table (predefined/42) {cite:cystat-2021-census-religion}; Reference, academic and press pages: UNESCO Atlas (unesdoc ark 48223/pf0000187026), as named in the research summary {cite:unesco-atlas-languages-in-danger-pf0000187026}; Wikipedia pages: Languages of Cyprus {cite:wikipedia-languages-of-cyprus} and Cypriot Arabic {cite:wikipedia-cypriot-arabic}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
