## Nature and selection
Nature: human blood and pig blood are said to resemble each other; the book records what is reported about blood, most of which is not reliable. Choice: the blood used in remedies must come from a healthy animal whose colour is not dominated by a humour or by putrefaction. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}

## Actions
Horse blood corrodes and rots, and all blood is hard to digest, especially the thick kind. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: rabbit blood is hot and, applied to white patches and freckles, helps. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}
Swellings and pustules: rabbit blood quickly ripens hot swellings, as does goat-buck blood; ox blood is hot on hard swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}
Joints, a reported listing on menstruation: it is said that menstrual blood dripped on gout helps; the book reports this from others. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
Head: pigeon, dove and shefnin blood dripped warm on head cracks and wounds prevents the swelling that follows a fall when mixed with cooled rose oil, as the book reports from Galen. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
Eyes: monitor and chameleon blood strengthens sight. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
Breathing and chest: owl blood is very helpful for asthma, as reported. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
Excretory organs, book listing on conception: it is said that carrying menstrual blood prevents pregnancy; the book gives it as a reported claim. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
Poisons: goat, deer or rabbit blood cooked and drunk with wine helps the harm of the Armenian arrow poison, as the book reports. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
