## Nature and selection
Nature: balsam oil is known and was described earlier. Castor and radish oils are alike in strength, both resolving, castor the stronger, though radish oil is warmer and resembles old oil. Temperament: iris and jasmine oils are hot and dry in the third degree; the oils of anjara and carthamus are hot and moist in the first and second; narcissus oil is hot in the second and moist in the first; khiri oil is hot and moist in the second; almond oil, bitter almond, grapevine tips, rose and apple oils are close in cooling and astringency, as is quince oil; chamomile oil is moderately hot; dill oil is similar and warmer; violet oil cools a little without astringency; rue oil resolves. The book defers the making of oils to the chapter on compound preparations. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.443}
Choice: each oil has its own temperament and strength according to its source; compound oils are described in the chapter on compound preparations. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.444}

## Actions
Actions: bitter almond oil opens; apple and quince oils have astringency and cooling; chamomile oil eases pain, loosens thickening and resolves vapours; iris oil softens, strengthens the organs, ripens and eases pain; myrtle oil tightens and strengthens the organs and cools more than quince; rue oil resolves wind strongly; costus oil helps in the conditions of epidemics. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.444}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: laurel oil for alopecia; myrtle oil tightens and strengthens hair roots and darkens hair; costus oil keeps the youth of hair; almond oil with honey, iris root and melted wax helps wrinkles, freckles and scars; castor oil is good for white patches and freckles; fenugreek oil is good for a spoiled complexion. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.444}
Swellings and pustules: almond oil helps a sprain swelling; iris oil resolves and removes an old hardness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Wounds and ulcers: castor oil for thick pustules and scabies; fenugreek oil for ringworm; myrtle oil helps ulcers; costus oil removes scabies and itching quickly. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Joints: almond oil helps sprain; chamomile oil helps weariness of the limbs; iris oil and dill oil likewise. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Head: almond oil helps headache, throbbing in the ear, ringing and whistling in the ear, most of all for blocked ear and the worms in it; rose oil is very good for inflammation of the brain; laurel and rue oils are good for chronic headache; castor oil helps head ulcers, swellings and earache. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Digestive organs: almond oil is good for the spleen and heavy on the stomach. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Excretory organs, book listing: anjara and carthamus oils loosen; rose oil may loosen where slipperiness is needed and may bind bilious diarrhoea; castor oil purges. Almond oil is good for kidney pains and urine retention. The book says iris oil eases birth and calms womb pains; fenugreek oil helps a hard womb and its swellings and difficult birth; castor oil helps swellings of the anus and the closing of the womb. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Fevers: chamomile oil in long fevers is better than rose oil; dill oil is good for rigor. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.445}
Substitutes: the book names substitutes for each oil, such as for balsam, laurel, iris, anjara, henna, water lily and castor oil, without giving the proportions. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.446}
