## Summary
Khalt is lexically mixing a thing with another, and is wider than liquids; the Kulliyat defines it as joining the parts of two or more things; its rules vary with its subject. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7746} Mixing of zakatable property: if two owners mix their property, by joint ownership or by neighbourhood, some jurists have them pay zakat as one owner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7747} Mixing capital in partnership: the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis hold that partnership is concluded by the contract alone without mixing; the Shafiis require the mixing before the contract to a degree that the goods cannot be told apart, and if it is after, the contract must be repeated. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7747} Mixing by wrongdoing: a usurper or trustee who mixes the property with other goods must pay it if it can be separated; if not, it counts as lost and the duty passes to his liability, whether he mixes it with a like, better or worse thing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7747}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7748} A guardian mixing an orphan\'s property with his own: allowed where it benefits the orphan, and the mixing of food for orphans for the good of all, as the verse on reform of orphans is cited. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7748} Mixing water with a pure thing: it does not lose its purifying power if the mixing cannot be avoided, like weed; it loses it when mixed deliberately. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7748}
