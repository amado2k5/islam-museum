## Summary
Ibn Sina says air is an element of our bodies and spirits, a supply to our spirits and an acting moderator of them, whose moderating belongs to two acts, ventilation and purification, and that while it is moderate and pure it keeps health {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.113-114}.

## Air and the spirit
Air is an element of our bodies and spirits; besides being an element it is a supply that reaches our spirits and is the cause of their amendment, not as an element only but as an agent, that is, a moderator. By spirit he means what he explained earlier, not what the philosophers call the soul. This moderating belongs to two acts, ventilation and purification {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.113}.

## Ventilation
Ventilation is the moderating of the temperament of the hot spirit when it grows excessive, mostly through confinement, and changes; it is gained by breathing in through the lung and through the pulse-breathing pores joined to the arteries. The air around our bodies is very cold relative to the spirit's innate temperament, so it mixes with it and prevents its turning to the fiery, confined state that leads to a bad temperament, by which it loses the readiness to receive the psychic effect that is the cause of life and its moist vaporous substance itself dissolves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.113}.

## Purification
Purification is by air carrying off, when the breath is returned, the smoky vapour handed to it by the discriminating power, whose relation to the spirit is that of the surplus humour to the body. So the moderating is by the coming of air to the spirit at inhalation, and the purifying by its going out at exhalation: inhaled air, to moderate, must be actually cold when it first arrives; when it has changed to the spirit's quality by warming through its long stay, its benefit is gone and new air is needed in its place, so it must be put out to empty the place for its successor and drive out with it the residues of the spirit's substance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.113-114}.

## Moderation and change
While air is moderate and pure, with no foreign substance opposed to the spirit's temperament mixed in it, it works health and keeps it; when it changes it does the opposite. It undergoes natural changes, unnatural changes, and changes outside the natural course that oppose it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.114}.
