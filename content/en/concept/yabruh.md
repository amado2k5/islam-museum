## Nature
It is the root of the wild mandrake, the root of every mandrake, and is shaped like a human figure, which gives it its name. The book says it has two kinds: the female, dark in colour, with leaves like lettuce leaves and a small fruit; and the male, white, with broad leaves like those of chard, a root shaped like the female's but a little longer, and no stalk. The editor's note on the name is a modern herbal reference, not used. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.509}

## Temperament and properties
The book says it is cold in the third degree and dry towards it, with a slight warmth by some opinions. Its root is strongly drying, and its leaves are used dried or moist. It is narcotic, with a sap and a tear, and the sap is stronger than the tear. Its smell also brings on stupor. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.510}

## Surgery
The book records that when a limb had to be removed, stupor was induced so that the patient felt no pain, and that its smell and sap bring on the same stupor. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.511}

## Eye and stomach
Its tear, used in eye remedies, eases severe pain, and its leaf may be applied as a poultice. Taken in excess, the book says, it causes vomiting and a large discharge of phlegm and bile, and beyond a certain amount it kills; the amount is left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.511}

## Adornment, swellings and joints
The book says that rubbing the leaf on the skin spots called al-barash clears them without raising sores, especially when the leaf is fresh, and that its milk removes freckles and sun spots without stinging or burning. It is applied to hard swellings, heals the carbuncle and removes pustules. The root with barley meal is a poultice for pain in the joints and may cure elephantiasis. No amounts or methods of preparation are carried over. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.510}

## Poisoning
The book names ghee and honey as the treatment for its poisoning, and vomiting as helpful. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.511}

## Sleep and drinks (book listing, amounts left out)
The book describes drinks made from its root peel to dispel sleeplessness, and drinks that induce stupor. The amounts and methods are not transcribed. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.510}

## Excretory organs and womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book's account of its tears and seed in the matter of the womb and the foetus is recorded only as a labelled listing held for the founder's ruling. It is not written as advice here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.511}
