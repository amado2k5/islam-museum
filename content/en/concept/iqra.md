## Definition
Iqra' means causing another to read; the jurists keep this lexical sense, whether the aim is listening and remembrance or teaching and memorisation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3184}

## Related terms
Qira'a (reading) and tilawa (recitation) mean the same thing, and recitation is most often used for the Qur'an, though some take it to be wider. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3184}
Mudarasa is two people reading to each other in turn. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3184}
Idara is a group reading a portion each in turn. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3184}

## General ruling
Causing someone to recite for remembrance and for listening to the Qur'an, especially from a person with a good voice, is recommended. The jurists cite a report about Ibn Mas'ud in which the Prophet asked him to recite to him, and stopped him at a point in the surah. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3184}

Iqra' for teaching and memorisation is in general a communal obligation (fard kifaya) on those qualified to carry the religious sciences; what a person needs for himself is an individual obligation. Carrying them means keeping, teaching, reciting and verifying them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3184} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3185}

Related rulings include taking payment for teaching; see the entries on teaching, hire and i'tikaf. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3185}
