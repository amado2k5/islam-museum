## Overview
The Companions, Successors and those after them studied the transmitters' conditions to tell the truthful from the liar — knowing the best-memorized, the most accurate, and the longest-companioned of those above them; they resorted to history as lying grew common, and some set aside days for speaking of the men {cite:manhaj-tawthiq-al-sunna-azhar p. 43}{cite:manhaj-tawthiq-al-sunna-azhar p. 44}. The accepted narrator among them: trustworthy, accurate, comprehending, sane, adult, free of the lapses of honor and the sin — if one condition failed, his narration was rejected.

## In al-Dhahabi's Mizan al-I'tidal
In the introduction to [[work/mizan-al-itidal|Mizan al-I'tidal]], [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] traces the science through a chain of imams: first, in his account, [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan]]; then his students [[person/yahya-ibn-main|Ibn Ma'in]], [[person/ali-ibn-al-madini|Ibn al-Madini]], [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]], al-Fallas and Abu Khaythama; then their students Abu Zur'a, Abu Hatim, [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], Muslim and al-Jawzajani; then al-Nasa'i, Ibn Khuzayma, al-Tirmidhi, al-Dulabi, al-Uqayli, Ibn Hibban, Ibn Adi and others. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1 PDF p. 5} {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1 PDF p. 6}

He ranks the terms of vindication from the highest: "firm, a proof", "firm, a hafiz", "trustworthy, exact", "trustworthy, trustworthy", then "trustworthy", then "truthful", "no harm in him" and "there is no harm in him", then "his place is truthfulness", "good in hadith", "sound in hadith", "a middling shaykh", "a shaykh, fair in hadith", "truthful, God willing" and "fairly sound". {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1 PDF p. 7} The lowest terms of criticism for him are "imposter" and "liar", then "not strong", "not a proof", "there is talk about him", "soft", "of poor memory", "disputed" and the like, which show that a transmitter is discarded, weak, or held in suspense. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1 PDF p. 7} He makes the turn of the year 300 the dividing line between early and late transmitters. {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1 PDF p. 7} He reports al-Bukhari saying that it is not permitted to narrate from anyone of whom he said "munkar al-hadith". {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1 PDF p. 8} On how innovation bears on accepting a report, see [[question/riwayat-ahl-al-bida|narration from people of innovation]].

## In Tadrib al-Rawi: knowing the trustworthy and the weak
[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] makes "knowing the trustworthy and the weak" type sixty-one and calls it one of the most important, since by it sound and weak hadith are known. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 541} In [[work/tadrib-al-rawi|Tadrib al-Rawi]] [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] sorts its literature into three kinds. Some books treat only the weak, such as those of [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]], [[person/abu-jafar-al-uqayli|al-Uqayli]], [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]], al-Saji, [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]], al-Azdi and Ibn Adi's al-Kamil, which listed everyone who had been criticised even if trustworthy and was followed in this by [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] in [[work/mizan-al-itidal|al-Mizan]], except that al-Dhahabi left out the Companions and the followed imams. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 541} Al-Iraqi wrote a supplement to it, [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] produced [[work/lisan-al-mizan|Lisan al-Mizan]], and al-Dhahabi's al-Mughni gives the soundest verdict on each man in a single word. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 542} Other books treat only the trustworthy, such as [[work/al-thiqat-ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban's al-Thiqat]] and those of Ibn Shahin and al-Ijli; and others combine both, such as [[work/al-tarikh-al-kabir|al-Bukhari's Tarikh]], the Tarikh of Ibn Abi Khaythama, Ibn Abi Hatim's [[work/al-jarh-wal-tadil|al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil]] and Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 542} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 543}

**Its legitimacy.** Al-Nawawi says criticism and vindication were permitted to protect the Sharia; al-Suyuti adduces the verse on verifying a report brought by a sinner ([[passage/quran-49-6|al-Hujurat 6]]) and hadiths of praise and of censure. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 543} He notes that a number of Companions and Successors spoke about narrators, and that Salih Jazara's statement that the first to do so was Shu'ba, then al-Qattan, then Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in, means the first to take it up as a task. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 544} He relates that Abu Bakr ibn Khallad asked [[person/yahya-ibn-said-al-qattan|Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan]]: do you not fear that those whose hadith you abandoned will be your adversaries before God? He replied: I would rather have them as my adversaries than have the Messenger of God as my adversary, saying: why did you not defend my hadith against lies? {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 544} And that when Abu Turab al-Nakhshabi told Ahmad "do not backbite the scholars", Ahmad said: woe to you, this is sincere counsel, not backbiting. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 545}

**The duty of care, and critics' mistakes.** Al-Nawawi says the critic must verify, for more than one has erred by censuring men for what does not discredit. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 546} Al-Suyuti quotes [[person/ibn-daqiq-al-id|Ibn Daqiq al-Id]]: the honour of Muslims is one of the pits of Hell, on whose edge two groups stand — the hadith scholars and the judges. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 546} One example is al-Nasa'i's censure of Ahmad ibn Salih al-Misri as "not trustworthy and not reliable", though he was a trustworthy imam on whom al-Bukhari relied; al-Khalili said the hafizes agreed al-Nasa'i was prejudiced against him, Ibn Adi said the cause was that Ahmad had expelled him from his session, and [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] said the eye of displeasure shows faults that have sound explanations within, not that critics knowingly attack what they know to be false. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 546} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 548} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 549} He cites Ibn Hajar that Ibn Ma'in's weakening concerned Ahmad ibn Salih al-Shumumi, not al-Misri. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 549} Ibn Daqiq al-Id lists five ways error enters: whim and personal motive, the worst; differences in creed; the quarrel between Sufis and scholars of the outward law; ignorance of the ranks of the sciences; and acting on suspicion without scrupulousness. He notes that [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Ibn Abd al-Barr]] devoted a chapter to contemporaries' criticism of one another and held that it should be accepted only with clear explanation. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 549} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 550}

**How reliability is known, and the classes of the discredited.** Al-Suyuti quotes Ibn Daqiq al-Id's al-Iqtirah: a narrator's reliability is known when those who narrate from him state it, when he appears in a history of the trustworthy, when al-Bukhari or Muslim include him in their Sahih, or when one who stipulated soundness includes him. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 550} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 551} He quotes [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]]'s al-Madkhal: the discredited fall into ten classes — those who forged hadith; those who switched chains; those whose greed drove them to narrate from men they never met; those who raised Companions' words to the Prophet; those who connected disconnected reports; pious men too absorbed to master hadith, who fell into error; those who narrated from their shaykhs what they had not heard; those who narrated without their original hearing-copies; those who, handed books, narrated them without knowing whether they had heard them; and those whose books were destroyed and who narrated from memory by guesswork, like Ibn Lahi'a. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 552} Closely linked is the following type: [[concept/ikhtilat-al-ruwat|trustworthy men who became confused]].
