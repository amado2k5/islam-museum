## Summary
Ibn Sina gives the causes of unevenness in the pulse and of the saw and double-beat pulses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.175}.

## Unevenness
The cause of unevenness with a steady power is the weight of matter, from food or a humour; with a weak power it is the labour against the illness. Another cause is fullness of the vessels with blood, which bleeding removes. Unevenness is greatest when the blood is sticky and chokes the spirit moving in the arteries, above all when the pile-up is near the heart. Causes that bring it on within a short time are a full stomach and mouth and thinking about something; if a bad humour stays in the stomach the unevenness lasts, and may lead to palpitation so that the pulse becomes palpitating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.175}.

## The saw and the double beat
The cause of the saw pulse is differences in what is poured into the vessel's substance, in its putrefaction, rawness and ripeness, differences in the vessel's hardness and softness, and a swelling in the nervous organs. The cause of the double beat is strong power and need with a hard instrument, which does not yield to the single expansion the power demands, like one who wants to cut something with one stroke and cannot, so strikes again, above all if the need rises suddenly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.175}.
