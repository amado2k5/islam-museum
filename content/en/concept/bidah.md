## Brief
In the extract, [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]] holds that innovations at graves revive an old shirk, and that the innovator believes he honours the prophets and the righteous while he contradicts their way {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.8} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.13}.

## The author's position
He sets out a list that contrasts what the Prophet forbade at graves with what people did in his own time: praying there, lighting lamps, building mosques over graves, raising them, writing on them, and making them festivals {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.8}. He answers those who say that prayer in a graveyard is forbidden because of its impurity: the prohibition is for the shirk that attaches to the one who prays there {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.4}.

## Examples the extract gives
- The report of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri weeping over what had been introduced in his time {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.16} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.17}.
- Umar ibn al-Khattab's cutting of the tree under which the Companions had pledged, so that people would not be tempted by it {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.16} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.19}.
- The hadith of Dhat Anwat, when some Companions asked for a tree on which to hang their weapons, and the Prophet said that this was the way of earlier peoples {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.16}.

## For the reader
These are the author's rulings, presented as his. The general definition of innovation in the scholars and the schools is not yet included and needs its own sources.
