## Nature
The book quotes Dioscorides that it was named malinodiyun because a man named Malinus purged the daughters of Furutus with this plant and they were cured of madness; it is a plant with a green leaf like the plane leaf but smaller, more deeply cut, darker and with roughness, a short stem and a white flower with a purple tint, and thin black roots from one origin like the head of an onion, only its roots being used, and it grows in rough places, caves, hills and hard dry places; the book relates of some people that they sprinkle houses with it, thinking it purifying, that they pray when pulling it up and take care that no eagle passes and sees it dug up, believing that death threatens its digger, and that its smell makes the head heavy so that the digging is quick and precautions are taken beforehand; all of this is an account attributed to Dioscorides, which the book does not endorse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.704}

## Choice
The best is the middling between old and new, fat and lean, ash-coloured, quick to break, not decayed with a web-like inside, sharp in taste and numbing the tongue; the book gives a way of preparing it and using it powdered and the amount of the draught, left out here; and it says that the physician must look to age, habit, season, the present time and the cause that calls for it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.704}

## Temperament
Hot and dry to the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.704}

## Actions and properties
It is dissolving, subtilising and strongly cleansing, so that it eats dead flesh; when it grows at the roots of a vine, the power of its drink becomes purgative; one of the properties of hellebore is that it turns the body from its temperament and gives it a new, youthful one; it suits more men, mannish women, the strong, the young and those with fullness of body and much blood, and does not suit the dropsical and the lax; the book gives seasons suited to it and a regimen before and after, left out here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.704}

## Cosmetics
It is applied with vinegar on bahaq and likewise on wadah {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.704}

## Wounds and ulcers
It is applied with the milk of the black and the white on mange and with vinegar on ringworm and scaling, as an application and an evacuation through it, and it removes the hardness of a hard fistula; the method is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.704-705}

## Joints
It benefits palsy and joint pains, and evacuation through it is a strong remedy for them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.705}

## Organs of the head
Cooked with vinegar and dropped into the ear it calms ringing, rinsing with that vinegar calms toothache, its decoction dropped into the ear of one hard of hearing strengthens it, and it benefits obsession, melancholia, epilepsy, migraine and diseases of the head in general {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.705}

## Ocular organs
It strengthens sight when it enters kohls {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.705}

## Excretory organs
It benefits black bile and its excess and loosens it from the whole body without compulsion, and likewise brings out yellow bile and phlegm, and brings out every superfluity mixed with the blood, even from the farthest part of the body and from the skin; the book gives ways of strengthening its purging and of preparing it, left out here; it is of great benefit for swellings in the intestines and bladder and promotes urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.705}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it brings on menses; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.705}

## Substitutes
The book names madhariyun and gharighun as its substitutes, and Masawayh states that its substitute is kundus; the amounts are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.705}
