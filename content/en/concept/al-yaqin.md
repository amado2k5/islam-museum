## Summary
Sha'rawi sets certainty in three degrees: knowledge of certainty, sight of certainty and truth of certainty. For him, certainty is the settling of a matter in the heart so that it admits no doubt. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.556}

## Knowledge of certainty
Certainty is knowledge when the report comes from someone one trusts. Sha'rawi illustrates it with Abu Bakr, who accepted the account of the night journey, saying that if the Prophet had said it, then it was true. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.556}

## Sight and truth of certainty
Certainty rises to sight of certainty when the matter is seen with the eye, and then to truth of certainty when one enters into the reality of the thing. He uses the warning of the Fire in al-Takathur to show the move from report to sight. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.557}

## Limits of this block
These are Sha'rawi's words alone, from these pages. The positions of Sufi writers and theologians on the stations of certainty, the academic view and other views are not yet entered.
