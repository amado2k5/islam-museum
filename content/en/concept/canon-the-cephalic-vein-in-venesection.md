## Summary
Ibn Sina says the cephalic is the safest vein of the arm, that its lengthwise opening is slower to close because it lies at a joint, and shows how error in it is avoided and what is sought if it cannot be found {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## Lengthwise and across
So it is with the cephalic: its lengthwise opening is slower to close because it is at a joint, while in a vessel that is not at a joint the matter is the reverse. For the sciatic vein, the asilam and other vessels it is best to open them lengthwise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## Avoiding error
Even so, in the cephalic one should move away from the head of the muscle to the soft place and widen the incision, and not follow one incision with another, for it swells. Most of those on whom error fell at the site of the cephalic did not suffer it by a single stroke, however great, but the injury comes by repeating the strokes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## If it cannot be found
Its incision is widened if a second bleeding is wanted, and when it is not found, some of its branches on the outer side of the forearm are sought {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.
