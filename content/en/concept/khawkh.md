## Temperament
Cold at the end of the second degree, moist in the first short of its end {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Actions and properties
Its moisture is quick to putrefy, it is softening with some astringency, the dried the most astringent, there is a checking of flux in it, and the unripe is astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Cosmetics
Its leaf, when applied, removes the smell of lime {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Organs of the head
The water of its leaf is dropped in the ear and kills worms, and its oil benefits migraine and hot and cold pains of the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Organs of nourishment
The ripe is good for the stomach and there is in it a whetting of appetite for food, and it should not be eaten after other food, lest it spoil and be spoiled, but be put before the meal; its dried form is slow to digest and not good as nourishment, though more nourishing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Excretory organs
The navel is poulticed with its leaf and it kills the worms of the belly, and likewise if the juice of its blossoms and leaf is drunk; the ripe loosens the belly and the unripe binds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that some said that it increases desire, and that this is likely to hold in dry, hot bodies; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.713}
