## Nature
Ambergris is thought to be a spring welling up in the sea, and what is said, that it is sea foam or the dung of an animal, is far-fetched; but one whose word I trust told me that he sailed in his youth, and said that when he entered a country on the sea called Bakhakh by them, he was in the forenoon with some people on the shore, and when the sea surged on the shore they would find ambergris in pieces and colours of various kinds, and whoever was first to take it had it; I asked the dwellers of that country about this and its cause, and they said that this is the habit of this sea and it is so constantly, at many times {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.613–614}

## Selection
The best is the ash-grey, strong, salahati one, then the blue, then the yellow, and the worst is the black; it is adulterated with plaster, wax, labdanum and mandah, and the black poor kind is the one often taken from the bellies of the fish that eat it and die {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.614}

## Temperament
Hot and dry, its heat seeming to be in the second degree and its dryness in the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.614}

## Actions and properties
It benefits the old by the gentleness of its warming {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.614}

## Cosmetics
A kind of mandah dyes the hand and is suited to follow up the fading of henna {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.614}

## Organs of the head
It benefits the brain and the senses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.614}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits the heart greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.614}
