## Nature
A well-known tree found in some lands {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Temperament
It is said to be hotter than the walnut; it is hot at the end of the second degree and has some moisture, and some claimed that it is cold, and erred {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Actions and properties
It opens obstructions of the liver by its bitterness and fragrance, has some astringency, and its nourishment is very little {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.634}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is good for the stomach, especially the Syrian kind that resembles the pine nut, for the bitterness it has together with astringency; it opens the obstructions of the liver and the passages of nourishment and especially cleanses them, and its oil benefits pain of the liver arising from moisture and thickness. If someone says that he found in it no great harm or benefit to the stomach, Ibn Sina answers that it prevents nausea and the turning of the stomach and strengthens its mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.635}

## Excretory organs
It neither softens the bowels nor binds them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.635}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits the bites of vermin, especially cooked in strong wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.635}
