## Summary
Ibn Sina knows beneficial vomiting by the lightness, good appetite, pulse and breathing that follow it, and describes the course of vomiting with hellebore from nausea to a flow of saliva, then much phlegm, then a clear fluid; he then names the signs of excess, bulging and red eyes, heavy sweat and loss of voice, and orders the remedy by enema, honey and lukewarm water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.286-287}.

## Beneficial vomiting
Beneficial vomiting is told from the unbeneficial by the lightness that follows it, and by good appetite, good pulse and breathing, and likewise the state of the other powers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.286}.

## Stages of vomiting
It begins with nausea. The worst that troubles with it is a sharp biting in the stomach and a burning if the drug is strong, such as hellebore and what is made from it. Then it starts with a flow of saliva, followed by much phlegm in several bouts, then a clear flowing fluid {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.286}.

## Signs of excess and the remedy
Among the signs of excess are distension or bulging of the eyes with deep redness, heavy sweating and loss of voice; one who has this and is not helped comes to death. The remedy is the enema, honey, lukewarm water and theriac oils such as iris oil, and he is urged to vomit, for if he vomits he will not choke {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.286-287}.
