## Overview
Ridwan is the name that came in hadith for the angel who is the keeper of Paradise, as narrated by Ibn Hibban and al-Hakim in his Mustadrak and others. In hadith it is mentioned that the people of Paradise circumambulate him, and that God said to him "you are pleased, so I have pleased you," and for this he was named Ridwan {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban} {cite:mustadrak-al-hakim}. The degree of these narrations was disputed among the hadith scholars.

## In the Qur'an
- The Qur'an does not name the keeper of Paradise by name; rather it mentions Paradise, its people and its descriptions in many surahs, and mentions "al-Ridwan" in another place with the meaning of divine pleasure itself: {"except those who followed you among them... and al-Ridwan"} — and the difference between the keeper of Paradise named Ridwan and the meaning of pleasure in the language was settled by the exegetes {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
- [[passage/quran-9-72|At-Tawba 72]], [[passage/quran-9-100|At-Tawba 100]], and [[passage/quran-98-8|Al-Bayina 8]] in the description of the people of Paradise and God's pleasure in them.

## In hadith
- Ibn Hibban narrated in his Sahih and al-Hakim in his Mustadrak: "The keeper of Paradise is Ridwan; the people of Paradise circumambulate him. God said to him: you are pleased, so I have pleased you, and for this he was named Ridwan" — and al-Hakim commented on its chain {cite:sahih-ibn-hibban} {cite:mustadrak-al-hakim}.
- In a narration in al-Tirmidhi in the chapter "what came concerning the keeper of Paradise" that he has his companions who circumambulate him {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}.
- Al-Bayhaqi output a like of it in Sha'b al-Iman {cite:mustadrak-al-hakim}.
- Some hadith scholars strengthened it by gathering its paths in their books on weak and fabricated hadiths {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1} {cite:sharh-shudhur-al-dhahab}.

## Meaning of the name
- "Ridwan" in the language is the verbal noun of "radiya", meaning pleasure, and "al-Ridwan" in the Qur'an is the great divine pleasure {cite:mufhim-qurtubi-v7}.
- And the keeper was named that because God said to him "you are pleased, so I have pleased you" in the hadith narration, or because he is the keeper of the abode of pleasure {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## Among the exegetes and hadith scholars
- Ibn Kathir mentions that the keeper of Paradise in the narrated hadith is Ridwan, and that the Qur'an did not name him {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
- Among the hadith scholars there was disagreement over the degree of the narration "Ridwan is the keeper of Paradise": some of them declared it good by gathering its paths, and some weakened it because some of its paths are interrupted; and the principle is that the name is established in the transmitted reports {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v1} {cite:mizan-itidal-azimabadi-v2} {cite:sharh-shudhur-al-dhahab}.

## Notes for researchers
- The keeper of Paradise is not known in the Qur'an by name, unlike the keeper of the Fire whom hadith named Malik; and the difference between the two narrations in degree among the hadith scholars.
- His counterpart on the other side is Malik, the keeper of the Fire, on [[concept/malik-keeper-of-jahannam|Malik's page]].
- The divine "al-Ridwan" and the narrated name may be of one origin in Arabic: the abode of pleasure and the name of its keeper {cite:mufhim-qurtubi-v7}.
