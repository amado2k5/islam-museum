## Overview
Ta'rid is allusion, the opposite of plain statement: speech that may carry the intended sense or another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5412}

## A veiled proposal to a widow
A veiled proposal to a woman in the waiting period for her husband's death is lawful by agreement, on the verse in the Surah of the Cow about alluding to a proposal to women (verse 235). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5413}

## A veiled proposal to a divorced woman
Most jurists allow it to a woman divorced irrevocably in her waiting period; the Malikis, the more known Shafii view and one Hanbali view allow it, while the less known Shafii view and one of Ahmad's views forbid it. The Hanafis forbid it for any woman under a divorce, and link its lawfulness to whether she may leave her house. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5413}

## Examples
Such as «you are beautiful; who would find one like you?» and similar phrases. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5413}

## Allusion to slander
For the Malikis, one who alludes to slander is punished by the hadd if the context shows it, while a father is not so punished for his child; one view of Ahmad agrees. The Hanafis discipline without the hadd, for doubt. The Shafiis hold it is slander only if he meant it and the wording can bear it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5414}

## Allusion to a Muslim's pursuer
It is lawful to allude to a Muslim to kill one who comes to take him back to the abode of unbelief, like Umar's word to Abu Jandal: «the blood of one of them is the blood of a dog». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5414}

## Allusion to a confessor to retract
In the sounder Shafii view the judge may allude to a confessing man to retract, as in theft «perhaps you took it without a keeper»; the Hanafis and Hanbalis recommend it, on the story of Ma'iz. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5414} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5415}

## Reference
Allusion is treated in marriage, waiting periods, penalties, truces and oaths. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5415}
