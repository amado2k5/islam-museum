## Nature
Dioscorides describes a plant of the coasts. When the sea rises it covers the plant, but it is neither under the water nor apart from it. Its leaves resemble those of the plant called atatis, but are thicker, and its stalk is about a span long and split at the top. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.506}

## Colour and root
The book says the plant changes colour three times a day: white in the morning, near purple at midday and bright red in the evening. Its root is white and sweet-smelling, and when tasted it warms the tongue. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.507}

## Temperament
It inclines to heat. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.507}

## Excretory organs
A small measure of it taken in wine purges the belly more than water does and increases the urine. The measure itself is left out of this entry. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.507}

## Poisons
The book says it is taken to ward off the harm of poisons before other antidotes. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.507}
