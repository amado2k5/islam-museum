## Summary
Ibn Sina divides the signs taken from urine into seven genera, colour, consistency, clarity and turbidity, sediment, quantity, smell and froth, and sets aside touch and taste {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.184}.

## The genera
The signs taken from urine are drawn from seven genera: colour, consistency, clarity and turbidity, sediment, quantity in fewness and abundance, smell, and froth. Some people add touch and taste to these genera; Ibn Sina leaves them out, holding apart and finding them repugnant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.184}.

## Defining the genera
By the genus of colour is meant the colours the eye perceives in it, black, white and what lies between; by consistency, its state of thickness and thinness; by clarity and turbidity, its state of how easily or hardly the sight passes into it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.184}.
