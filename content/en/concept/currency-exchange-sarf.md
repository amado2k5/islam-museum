## Summary
The author divides trading in money into two categories: exchanging money for money, and exchanging money for goods, each with its own ruling {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}.

## Money for Money
If the two currencies are of the same kind, there must be hand-to-hand delivery and no disparity in amount {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}. If the two currencies differ, hand-to-hand delivery is still required, but disparity is permitted—meaning the possibility of profit from an immediate exchange, namely currency exchange operations {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}. As for exchanging money for money where one is immediate and the other deferred, or both are deferred, it is not permissible because it is a means to riba al-nasi'ah or riba al-nasa' {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}.

## Note on the Author's Text
In the same passage, the author states that riba al-nasa' is permissible in a loan though not in a sale, and treats an extra return on an interest-free loan (qard hasan) as an act of kindness {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}. This is the author's own statement; it differs from what general works of Islamic jurisprudence establish—that any stipulated excess in a loan is riba—and the book presents no disagreement among legal schools here, so it is not attributed to any specific school {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355}.

## Money for Goods
In exchanging money for goods, sale, disparity in value, deferment, an increase in price for deferment, and a discount for early payment are permissible, so long as the relationship is bilateral between the seller and the buyer {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 355} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}.
