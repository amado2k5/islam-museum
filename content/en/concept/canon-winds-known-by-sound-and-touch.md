## Summary
Ibn Sina completes the ways of knowing winds with sound and touch, and says the difference between a puffing and a wind lies in the manner of motion and not in substance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.

## Sounds
The sounds may come from the winds themselves, such as rumbling, or as is felt in the spleen when its pain comes from wind and is pressed; or the sound may arise on percussion, as the drum-like and the water-skin kinds of abdominal dropsy are told apart by striking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.

## Touch
Touch tells a puffing from a tumour by the stretching with a yielding that is not due to a flowing, fluctuating moisture or a sticky humour. The difference between a puffing and a wind is not in substance but in the form of motion, stillness and agitation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.166}.
