## Overview
Hums: the name Quraysh and their allies gave themselves in imitation of zeal for the religion, and they invented for it novelties in the rites of hajj.

## The story
Quraysh were called the Hums — Quraysh, those born to Quraysh, and their Arab allies — and they used to say: we are God's people, the residents of God's sanctuary and the dwellers of God's House, so all the Arabs must revere us and revere our sanctuary {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

Among the novelties they instituted for themselves: they did not go out to Arafa nor stand at it because it lay outside the sanctuary, standing instead at Muzdalifa; when one of them wore ihram he did not wear the accustomed clothes but stripped or wore a garment next to his skin called al-rimad; when they made tawaf they stripped the men and women; and when one of them wore ihram he did not enter his house by its door but from its back {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## The Qur'an abolished them
God sent down, on standing at Arafa: "Then press on from where the people press on" ([[passage/quran-2-199|al-Baqara 199]]); on nakedness in tawaf: "Take your adornment at every mosque" ([[passage/quran-7-31|al-A'raf 31]]); and on entering by the doors: "It is not righteousness to enter houses from their backs" ([[passage/quran-2-189|al-Baqara 189]]) {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
Quraysh made special hajj rules for themselves in which they took pride; God sent down verses cancelling those rules and returning the hajj to its origin.
