## Summary
Ibn Sina says seven pairs of nerves grow from the brain. The first begins from the depth of the two front ventricles beside the two projections like nipples, by which smell works, and is a great hollow nerve going to the two pupils {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.78}.

## Its course
The one growing on the right goes to the left and the one on the left to the right; they meet in a cross-shaped crossing; the one that grew on the right then passes to the right pupil and the one on the left to the left pupil, and their mouths widen until they enclose the glassy humour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.78}.

## Uses of the crossing
He reports that someone other than [[person/galen|Galen]] said they pass through the cross without turning, and gives three uses for the crossing: that the spirit flowing to one pupil is not barred from flowing to the other if harm befalls one, so each pupil sees better when the other is shut; that the two eyes have a single meeting place where the image of the seen thing becomes one, so sight with both is one sight, and a squinting person sees one thing as two when one pupil is displaced; and that each nerve is propped by the other and rests on it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.78}.
