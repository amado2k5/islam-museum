## Summary
Revival of barren land (ihya' al-mawat) is the cultivation of unused, unowned land, accomplished through plowing, sowing, planting, irrigation, fencing, or building {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}. According to the majority of jurists, full ownership of the land is acquired thereby, whereas according to the Malikis, the Imami Shi'a, and some Hanafis, only usufruct without full right of disposal is acquired {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 225}.

## Definition of Barren Land
Barren land (mawat) is land that is unused, has no owner, and is not the protective buffer (harim) of owned land {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}. The harim is the surrounding area needed to make use of a property, such as the buffer around a house or a well, and custom is usually consulted to determine its extent {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}. The author believes that land revival helps in our era to alleviate housing and food crises {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}.

## Hadiths
The author cites the hadith "Whoever revives dead land, it belongs to him" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 202} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}, the hadith "Whoever develops land that belongs to no one has the greatest right to it" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 214} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}, and the hadith "An unjust root has no right" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 214}. The author does not mention the grading of these hadiths except in a footnote to the hadith "Servants are the servants of God," which al-Suyuti deemed authentic {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 214}.

## Who May Revive Land
The Shafi'is held that land revival by a legally non-responsible minor is valid {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}. Abu Hanifah stipulated the ruler's permission for both a Muslim and a dhimmi {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}, while the Malikis restricted the requirement of permission to land close to settled areas {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 224}.

## Ownership Through Revival
The author presents the disagreement over the legal effect of revival. According to the majority, it confers full ownership {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 225}, whereas according to the Malikis, the Imami Shi'a, and some Hanafis, it confers only usufruct: if the reviver abandons it, it reverts to the ruler to grant to someone else, does not pass to their heirs, and the state may impose kharaj on it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 225}. The author comments on the Maliki argument by noting that it resembles sitting in a mosque or on a public road {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 225}.

## Demarcation
Demarcation (tahjir) is not revival, but the one who marks out the land has prior right to it over others for a period—three years according to the Hanafis, and whatever custom determines according to the Shafi'is and Hanbalis {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 225}.

## Agency in Land Revival
Jurists permit the reviver to appoint an agent to revive land on their behalf, because the principal bears the risk while the worker works for a guaranteed wage {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 225}. The legal schools differ on acquiring unowned public resources through an agent: the Hanafis do not permit it, while the Malikis, the Shafi'is in their stronger view, and the Hanbalis permit it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 235} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 236}.

## Its Connection to Land Grants and Minerals
Revival is the basis of land grants (iqta') in Islam, as an iqta' is permission over land given to one who is capable of reviving it and in proportion to their capacity {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 226}. Reviving a mineral deposit means uncovering it, and if it is surface-exposed it cannot be owned through revival {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 227} (see [[concept/minerals-ownership-in-islam|Ownership of Minerals]] and [[concept/iqta|Land Grants]]).
