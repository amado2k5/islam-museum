## Nature
One kind is a garden kind, and another has its leaves spread on the ground; the most beneficial part of it is its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Choice
The strength of its drink (sharab) is strong in its actions and better, and the preserved in vinegar has its heat broken {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, with a superfluous moisture in it, and for that reason it does not heat the whole body wherever it reaches {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Actions and properties
It benefits all swellings and cold pains, the stirring of winds and flatulence; it has a reddening power and a strong cleansing action {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Joints
It benefits sciatica and joint pain, its root and leaf as a poultice, and it benefits cold pains and bruising of the muscle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Organs of the head
It causes headache, but it dissolves phlegmatic migraine, especially as a fomentation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Organs of the chest
It helps expectoration as a linctus with honey, and is good in action when mixed into the linctuses that cleanse the chest; it is among the things that gladden and strengthen the heart, and a drink (sharab) can be made from it that cleanses the chest and the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that a decoction of its root promotes the flow of the two, especially its drink (sharab); the pronoun is dual as printed, and the book does not here name what is promoted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits the bites of vermin, especially the Egyptian kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.665}
