## Nature and selection
The book names it buqaysa, and the editor's footnote gives its name in a scientific form that is not used here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.400}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.400}. It cleanses and is astringent, and there is moisture in the husk of its fruit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.400}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it brightens the face {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.400}. Wounds and ulcers: it is applied powdered to ulcerated scabies and sticks to wounds because of its astringency and cleansing, especially the bark of its tree. It is sprinkled, and its root in decoction is applied, as are its leaves, to broken bones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.400}. Excretory organs: its thick bark purges phlegm when a measure of it is given in cold water or aromatic drink; the measure is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.400}.
