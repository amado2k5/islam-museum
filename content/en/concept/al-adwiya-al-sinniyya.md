## The forms of the dental drugs
Among the dental drugs: dentifrices, chewables, plasters and mukhabbisat (mixed smears, the editor's gloss) on the teeth or the jaw, mouthwashes, rubs, stuffing things, emplasters, cauteries, extractors, fumigations, snuffs, ear-drops, and evacuations of the matter by design or cupping at the nearest sites; some are resolvent, some cooling, some numbing. The numbing ones used on the teeth are the farthest thing from danger, yet their abundance may corrupt the tooth's substance; the strongly dissolving and heating drugs are used only at need, and any of them or of the numbing reaching the throat is guarded against {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.269}
## The tooth's piercing and the place of vinegar
Often the tooth needs piercing with a fine drill to vent the harming matter and let the drugs reach its depth (carried as the book's description of what is done, not a procedure); vinegar, though harmful to the teeth, enters both the cooled and the warmed tooth-drugs — in the cooled because it cools in substance and penetrates, in the warmed because it penetrates and aids dissolution by cutting, and its harm then is broken by the dental drugs mixed with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.269}
