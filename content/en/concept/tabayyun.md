## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that a Muslim must verify before judging the one who gives the greeting of peace, since Islam is outward and faith is in the heart, and that killing one who professes the creed without verification is a grave error {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2548}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2561}.

## What Sha'rawi says
He says the verse addresses those who believe, since faith is an act of the heart, while Islam is outward and a killer may feign it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2548}. He holds that one who gives the greeting is taken as a Muslim until the contrary is shown, and that enmity makes a man careless about checking {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2557}.

## Occasions of the verse
He recounts how Muhallim ibn Jaththama killed a man of Ashja' out of old enmity, and how the Prophet refused to forgive him and the earth cast his body out; he quotes the Prophet that the earth receives one worse than him {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2556}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2557}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2558}. He recounts Usama ibn Zayd's killing of a man who had professed the creed, and the Prophet's question whether he had looked into the man's heart {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2560}. He also reports the account of Ibn Abbas and the story of al-Miqdad {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2561}.
