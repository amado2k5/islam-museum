## Overview
Ibn Sina defines disease as an abnormal state in the human body entailing of itself a lesion in function — either an abnormal temperament or an abnormal composition {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.103}. He distinguishes it from cause — what precedes and necessarily entails a bodily state — and from symptom, what follows the state: putrefaction is a cause, fever a disease, thirst and headache symptoms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.103}. The causes of diseases are treated in the second lesson of the second book {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.112}.
