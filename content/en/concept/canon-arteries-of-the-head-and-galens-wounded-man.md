## Summary
Ibn Sina names the artery of the temple and the two arteries behind the ears and what is done to them, opening, cutting, drawing out and cautery, then relates from Galen the case of a man wounded in the throat whose artery was struck and whose blood was stopped with a medicine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## The temple artery
Of the arteries in the head there is the artery of the temple, which may be opened, cut through, drawn out or cauterized. This is done to stop sharp thin downflows pouring into the eyes and for the beginning of dispersal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## The two arteries behind the ears
The two arteries behind the ears are opened for kinds of ophthalmia, the beginning of the water in the eye, filminess, night-blindness and chronic headache; their opening is not free of danger and closing is slow with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.

## Galen's wounded man
Galen mentioned that a man wounded in his throat had his artery struck and a good measure of blood ran from him; Galen treated him with a medicine of frankincense, aloe, dragon's blood and myrrh, the blood stopped, and a chronic pain that had been in the region of his hip left him {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.298}.
