## Overview
Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn treats the sanctity of blood as a part of freedom itself: the nation is a single body, the shedding of one person's blood like opening a vein, and retaliation against the murderer like the physician's lancing that keeps the corruption from spreading {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 91}. Retaliation restrains bloodshed and preserves the life of anyone who might otherwise be killed in vengeance; the rules were built on precaution so the wicked find no pretext {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 91} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 92}. He notes the Companions' consensus that a group may be executed for one victim — 'Umar put seven men of San'a' to death for a single killing — though retaliation otherwise demands equality.
