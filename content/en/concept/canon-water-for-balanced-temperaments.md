## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the eighth chapter, on the management of water and drink, with the water that suits balanced temperaments, the harm of water cooled with snow, the experienced physicians' saying on mixing well and river water, and the mending of poor water with vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.236}.

## The water that suits
For balanced temperaments the most fitting water is that of moderate coldness, or water cooled from outside with snow, above all when the snow is poor; the same holds for good snow, since what melts from it harms the nerves, the breathing organs and the viscera as a whole. Only the very sanguine bear it, and if it does not harm at once it harms over the long run, with advancing age {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.236}.

## Well, river and vinegar
The men of experience said that well water and river water should not be combined unless one of them has first run off. Ibn Sina adds that the choice of water has already been shown, as has the mending of poor water, and that mixing it with vinegar improves it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.236}.
