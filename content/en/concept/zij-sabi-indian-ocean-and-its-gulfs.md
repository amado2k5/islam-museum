## Summary
Al-Battani describes the Indian and China sea as stretching west to east from the farthest Abyssinia to the farthest India, eight thousand miles, and names four gulfs: one running from Abyssinian land toward the Berbers called the Berber gulf; the gulf of Ayla, which is the Qulzum sea; the gulf of Fars, which is the sea of Basra; and a further gulf toward the far end of India. He then counts some thirteen hundred and seventy islands in the sea, among them Sarandib. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.30}.

## The gulfs and their lengths
He says the Berber gulf is about five hundred miles long; that the Qulzum sea, the gulf of Ayla, is fourteen hundred; that the gulf of Fars, the sea of Basra, is fourteen hundred; that between those two gulfs lie the lands of Hijaz and Yemen, with fifteen hundred miles between them; and that the Green gulf toward the far end of India is fifteen hundred miles long. The widths in this passage are hard to read in the scan and are left out. See [[place/red-sea|the Red Sea]], [[place/persian-gulf|the Persian Gulf]] and [[place/indian-ocean|the Indian Ocean]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.30}.

## The islands and Sarandib
He says this whole sea, the Indian and China sea, holds thirteen hundred and seventy islands, inhabited and otherwise. Among them he names an island at its far end near the lands of China called Taprobane, which is Sarandib, circled by three thousand miles and lying opposite India to the east, with great mountains and many rivers from which come the red ruby and a stone the colour of the sky, and around it fifty-nine inhabited islands with many towns and villages. See [[place/sri-lanka|Sri Lanka]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.30}.
