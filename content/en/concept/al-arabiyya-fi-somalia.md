## Summary
This page sets out the place of [[language/arabic|Arabic]] in [[place/somalia|Somalia]] as it appears in the Encyclopaedia Britannica article on Somalia and in the Somalia page of The World Factbook. It ascribes each statement to its page and estimates no number of speakers.

## Official status
The encyclopedia says the primary language spoken in Somalia is [[language/somali|Somali]], which is understood across the country and is an official language, and that Arabic is also an official language {cite:britannica-somalia}. The Factbook says: Somali (official), Arabic (official), Italian, English {cite:cia-factbook-somalia}. In its article on the Semitic languages the encyclopedia says Arabic is official in Somalia alongside Somali {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. The three sources agree that Arabic and Somali are both official.

## The standard and the spoken
The encyclopedia says Arabic is mainly spoken in northern Somalia and coastal towns, that many people also speak English and Italian because of the country's colonial history, and that [[language/swahili|Swahili]] is spoken in the south {cite:britannica-somalia}. That describes a geographic distribution of Arabic, not a general spread. The two pages say nothing on a Somali Arabic dialect or on the difference between the standard and the colloquial; for the duality in general see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Fusha and the dialects]]. The museum ascribes to the pages no classification it did not read. The phrase "mainly spoken in northern Somalia and coastal towns" is a geographic description and carries no count or share; no figure for Arabic speakers in Somalia was read in either source, so the size of that community and its standing among the other languages cannot be inferred from the two pages.

## Other languages in the country
The encyclopedia says Somalia adopted a Latin-alphabet-based orthography for Somali in 1973, making it a written language {cite:britannica-somalia}. The Factbook says in its ethnic groups entry that the population is predominantly Somali with lesser numbers of Arabs, Bantus and others {cite:cia-factbook-somalia}. That is the Factbook's description, and it estimates no shares. Compare [[concept/lughat-al-qabail|the languages of the tribes]].

## From the history of the language
The encyclopedia says Mogadishu, the capital and largest city and a major port, is one of the earliest Arab settlements on the East African coast, dating back to the 10th century, and became the capital of independent Somalia in 1960 {cite:britannica-somalia}. That is a statement of history about the city, not about Arabic as a spoken language. For Somalia among the Arab states, and its joining the League in 1974 as the encyclopedia states, see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab states and the League]].
