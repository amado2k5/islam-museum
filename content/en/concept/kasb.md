## Summary
"The deeds of the servants are God's creation and the servants' acquisition (kasb)" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 27}.

## The capacity
Al-Tahawi distinguishes two kinds: "the capacity by which the deed is obligatory — like the tawfiq which the creature may not be described by — accompanies the deed", versus "the capacity of health, scope, ability and soundness of instruments — which precedes the deed, and by it obligation attaches" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 26} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 27} ([[passage/quran-2-286|al-Baqara 286]]).

## The obligation
"God has not obliged them except what they can bear, and they can bear only what He has obliged them" — this, for him, explains "there is no power nor strength save in God" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 27}.

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama argues from the verse "Today every soul is requited for what it has earned" ([[passage/quran-40-17|Ghafir 17]]) that "the servant has a deed and an acquisition, requited for its good with reward and its evil with punishment — occurring by God's decree and determination" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 27}. Before that he says God commands and forbids only one able to do and leave, and that He compelled no one {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 26}. He does not divide capacity as al-Tahawi does.

## In Ibn Taymiyya's al-'Aqida al-Wasitiyya
Ibn Taymiyya does not use the word "kasb" here; he says: "The servants act in truth, and God is the creator of their deeds. The servant is the believer and the unbeliever, the righteous and the wicked, the pray-er and the faster. The servants have power over their deeds and have will, and God is their creator and the creator of their power and their will" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 22}.

## In al-Ash'ari's Maqalat al-Islamiyyin
In "the account of the sum of the saying of the people of hadith and sunna" in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin, Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari presents what the people of hadith and sunna hold, ending: "And in all we have mentioned of their saying we speak, and to it we go" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 350}. He relates there that they "affirmed there is no creator but God, that God creates the servants' evil deeds, that God creates the servants' deeds, and that the servants cannot create anything of them" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 346}; that no one can do a thing before he does it, nor depart from God's knowledge {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 346}. He does not use the word "kasb" here.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the positions of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya, Ibn Qudama's Lum'at al-I'tiqad, Ibn Taymiyya's al-Wasitiyya, and what al-Ash'ari related in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin of the people of hadith and sunna and declared his own — each attributed to its book. The sayings of the Maturidis and the remaining Sunni and non-Sunni schools from their own books have not yet been added; the entry needs specialist review before promotion.
