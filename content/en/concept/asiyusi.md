## Nature and selection
The stone on which forms the salt called the flower of asius. The book suggests it forms from the dew of the sea and the shade that falls on it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}.

## Temperament and actions
Its power, and the power of its flower, opens, promotes flesh and slightly putrefies; it dissolves rotten flesh without burning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: it resolves wounds when applied as a poultice with mastic gum, once they have stuck {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}. Wounds and ulcers: it helps difficult, severe, large and deep ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}. Joints: with barley flour for gout, and soaking the affected limbs in its decoction helps them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}. Chest: licked with honey, it helps ulcers of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}. Food organs: applied with lime and vinegar to the spleen, it helps {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.377}.
