## The first division
Al-Jamzuri says: "the madd is original and derived; call the first the natural — that which stops on no cause and without which the letters cannot be drawn": the natural madd is two counts, the letter cannot stand without it and it is not lengthened for a cause — "any letter other than a hamza or a sukun that comes after a madd is natural" {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## The derived and its rulings
The derived madd hangs on a cause such as a hamza or a sukun; its letters are the three gathered in «نوحيها», and the lin among them requires an open vowel before it. The madd has three rulings {cite:ar-wikisource}: the wajib, when a hamza follows the madd letter within one word — the muttasil; the ja'iz, when a word separates them — the munfasil, and with it the madd 'arid for the stopping sukun and the madd badal where the hamza precedes the madd; and the lazim, when the sukun is original, in joining and in stopping, after the madd.

## The lazim and the openings
The lazim has four divisions: word-bound (kalimi) and letter-bound (harfi), each either muthaqqal with idgham or mukhaffaf without. The harfi appears at the heads of eight suras gathered in «كم عسل نقص», and in the fourteen opening letters gathered in «صله سحيرًا من قطعك» {cite:ar-wikisource}. In the Jazariyya the madd comes as lazim, wajib, ja'iz and qasr in the chapter of madd and qasr {cite:ar-wikisource}.
