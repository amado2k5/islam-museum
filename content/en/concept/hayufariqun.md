## Nature and selection
Nature: stalks and scattered flowers, and a yellowish-red seed like sumac but without its redness. Choice: Galen says it is drunk from its fruit, not from the flower alone. Temperament: hot in the second degree and dry at the end of it. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.447}

## Actions
Resolves swellings, thins, opens and dissolves. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.447}

## Uses by organ
Swellings, wounds and ulcers: a poultice of its leaf helps fire burns and closes great wounds and bad ulcers; ground and sprinkled on flabby and rotting ulcers it helps. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.447}
Joints: helps hip pain and sciatica when boiled with wine; the book says that drunk for forty days in succession it cures sciatica. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.447}
Excretory organs, book listing on the menses: it drives urine and its property is to bring on the menses; its fruit purges black bile. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.447}
Substitutes: adhkhar and the roots of kibr, without the proportions. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.447}
