## Summary
In the fourth chapter Ibn Sina says urine with no smell shows cold and rawness, perhaps the death of the innate heat, that stench shows sores or putrefaction by its setting, sweetness shows blood, strong stench bile and sour stench black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.192}.

## No smell and stench
It is said that the smell of a sick person's urine has never been seen to match that of the healthy. If urine has no smell at all it shows a cold temperament and excessive rawness, and in acute diseases perhaps the death of the innate heat. If it has a stinking smell and there are signs of ripening, the cause is scab and sores in the urinary organs; if there is no ripening it may be that, or it may be putrefaction. If this occurs in acute fevers and is not due to the urinary organs, it is a bad sign {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.192}.

## Sourness and sweetness
If the smell leans to sourness it shows that the putrefaction lies in humours cold in substance over which a foreign heat has gained mastery; in an acute illness it is a sign of death, since it shows the death of the innate heat and the mastery of cold in nature with a foreign heat. A smell leaning to sweetness shows a predominance of blood, a violent stench bile, and a stench leaning to sourness black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.192}.
