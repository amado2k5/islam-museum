## Summary
Al-Battani describes the sea of Egypt and the Romans, the Mediterranean, running from the strait that leaves Oceanus at Gadira opposite al-Andalus east to Tyre and Sidon, five thousand miles long and varying from six hundred to seven hundred and eight hundred miles wide in places. He counts in it one hundred and sixty-two inhabited islands, five of them great: Sardinia, Cyprus, Sicily, Crete and one more, and two gulfs, one to the north and one toward a land whose name is unclear in the scan. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.30-31}.

## Length and width
He bounds the sea in the west by the strait joining Oceanus, and in the east by the coasts of Tyre and Sidon, and puts its length at five thousand miles and its width between six hundred and eight hundred. He says one gulf leaves it to the north near a place whose name is unclear in the scan, eight hundred miles long, called Adrias or something like it, and another toward a land whose name the scan also leaves unclear. The gulf figures and names are not reliably readable. See [[place/mediterranean-sea|the Mediterranean]], [[place/tyre|Tyre]] and [[place/sidon|Sidon]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.30-31}.

## The great islands
He says this whole sea holds a hundred and sixty-two inhabited islands, five of them great: one named in the scan as Qurni, circled by two hundred miles, Sardinia by three hundred, Cyprus by three hundred and fifty, Sicily by five hundred and Crete by three hundred. The circuits are the text's own figures, not measurements one can rely on. See [[place/sardinia|Sardinia]] and [[place/crete|Crete]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.31}.
