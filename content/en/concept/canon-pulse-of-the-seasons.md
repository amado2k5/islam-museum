## Summary
In the ninth chapter Ibn Sina describes the pulse in spring, summer, winter and autumn and in the seasons between {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.177-178}.

## Spring, summer and winter
In spring the pulse is moderate in everything and greater in strength. In summer it is quick and frequent for need, small and weak, because the power dissolves as the spirit dissolves under the overpowering, excessive outer heat. In winter it is more widely spaced, slower and weaker, though small, because the power weakens {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.177}.

## The warm winter, autumn and the seasons between
In some bodies the heat happens to be shut into the depth and gather and strengthen the power, when a hot temperament dominates and resists the cold without yielding to it. In autumn the pulse is uneven and tends to weakness: uneven because the accidental temperament in autumn keeps changing, now to heat, now to cold, and weak for the same reason, since a temperament changing from time to time harms more than a steady one even if bad, and because autumn is a time opposed to the nature of life, heat in it weakening and dryness growing. The pulse of the seasons between seasons matches the seasons that flank them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.177-178}.
