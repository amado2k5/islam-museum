## Overview
Muhammad al-Khidr Husayn treats freedom in wealth under four heads: acquiring it, the middle way, enjoying it, and aggression upon it {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 62}. The Shari'a urges seeking provision by every lawful work that harms no one; preoccupation with trade is blamable when it distracts from duties, not in itself {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 62} {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 63}. The crafts and industries are communal obligations; society falls into four classes — rulers, people of learning, merchants, and artisans and farmers {cite:al-hurriyya-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 64} — and he then details the transfer of wealth.
