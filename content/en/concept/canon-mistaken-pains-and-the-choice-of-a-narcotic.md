## Summary
Ibn Sina warns of the frequent error in pains whose cause is external, then sets out what perplexes the physician between a slow-acting treatment and a fast-acting one of great danger, such as narcotising colic, and gives the standards for choosing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.310}.

## Error about the cause
Error often occurs in pains: their causes are things from outside, such as heat or cold, a bad pillow, a spoiled bed or a fall in drunkenness and the like, and a cause is sought for them in the body and the physician errs. So it must be learned whether there is fullness or not, and whether the known causes of fullness are present {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.310}.

## A cause that came from outside
The cause may have come from outside and then settled within, as with one who drinks cold water and gets a violent pain about his stomach and liver, for whom a bath and deep sleep often suffice; or as with one who takes something hot and gets a great headache, for whom drinking cooled water suffices {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.310}.

## Perplexity between two treatments
The thing from which relief of pain is hoped may act slowly, so that the pain cannot be borne until then, such as evacuating the matter that causes colic pain, held in the fibre of the bowels; or it may act quickly but with great danger, such as narcotising the painful organ in colic. So the physician must have a strong intuition to know which period is longer, how long the strength holds or how long the pain lasts, and which state does more harm, the pain or the expected danger in narcotising. The pain, if it remains, may kill by its violence, while the narcotic may not kill even if it harms in another way {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.310}.

## Choosing the narcotic
The make-up and manner of the narcotic should be considered, the easiest used, and the compound used with its antidotes, unless the matter is very grave, in which case he fears and needs a strong narcotic. Some organs do not mind a narcotic being put on them, such as the teeth, and sometimes drinking is safe, as with drinking a narcotic for an eye pain, which harms the eye less than applying it as kohl {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.310}.

## Colic and sleep
In colic the danger is great, because the matter grows colder, more congealed and more shut. Narcotics may calm pain by inducing sleep, since sleep is one of the causes of pain's calming, especially when hunger is used with it in a material pain. Compound narcotics whose powers are broken by drugs that act as antidotes are safer, such as philonium and the tablets known as the triple one, though they narcotise less. The fresh is stronger in narcotising, the old hardly narcotises, and the middling is middling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.310}.
