Watan, lexically, is a dwelling or the place where a person stays and settles; the folds of sheep, cattle and camels are also called watan. Technically it is the place where a person lives and settles, whether or not he was born there. The mahalla, the quarter where a people lives, is narrower than watan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17527}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17528}

The kinds of watan, by the rules that attach to them: the original homeland (watan asli), the residence homeland (watan iqama), and the dwelling (watan sukna). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17528}

The original homeland: for the Hanafis, the place of a person's birth, marriage or settling; it is also called the family homeland or the homeland of nature. For the Shafi'is and Hanbalis it is the place where a person lives without leaving it in summer or winter except for a need; the Hanbalis in the sounder view add a town where he has a wife or married, on the saying of 'Uthman that whoever marries in a town should pray the prayer of the resident; one report of Ahmad includes a town where he has people or livestock. The Malikis define it as the place of a person's residence with the intention of staying for ever, and the place of a wife he has consummated with, however rarely he lives there. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17528}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17529}

The residence homeland (iqama): for the Hanafis, the place a person goes to with the intention of staying for a period that cancels the rules of travel; the other jurists agree on the meaning and differ on the period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17529}

The dwelling (sukna): for the Hanafis, a place where a person means to stay for less than the period that cancels travel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17529}

Conditions: the original homeland needs permanent building in the customary way (Malikis, Shafi'is, Hanbalis); the Hanafis treat the place of birth, marriage or settling as the original homeland. The residence homeland needs the intention to stay, the relevant period, the taking of the place, its fitness, and that it is not the person's original homeland; the dwelling needs no intention of staying and no actual stay for the period, and must not be an original homeland. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17529}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17530}

What ends a homeland: for the Hanafis an original homeland ends only by moving to another with the family and leaving the old one; the Hanbalis hold it does not end by taking up another original homeland; a man with wives in two towns has two original homelands, which the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis agree. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17530} A residence homeland ends by an original homeland and by travel, and a dwelling ends by an original homeland, a residence and another dwelling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17530}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17531}

Rules attached to the homeland: shortening the prayer, combining prayers, breaking the Ramadan fast, the sacrifice, and the Friday and festival prayers. The resident in his original or residence homeland does not shorten the prayer, and one returning to his original homeland must complete the prayer from the moment he enters. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17531} For travellers the majority allow combining the noon and afternoon prayers and the sunset and night prayers, while the Hanafis deny this. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17531} A traveller may break the Ramadan fast while travelling; if the travel ends in the day he must abstain for the rest of the day and make up the days later. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17531}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17532} On the sacrifice: the Hanafis hold it obligatory and require residence for the obligation, while the majority hold it a sunna and do not require residence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17532}
