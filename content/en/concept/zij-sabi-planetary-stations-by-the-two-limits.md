## The first and second limits
The corrected centre of the planet is entered in the two rows of the retrogression tables that differ by six parts, and the matching values are taken from the table of the planet. The first limit comes from the first table and the second from the second table. If the corrected anomaly lies between the two limits, that is less than the second and more than the first, the planet is retrograde. If it is above the second limit and below the first, the planet moves direct. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.177}

## Counting the days of retrogression
For a retrograde planet the days it has been retrograde are found by subtracting the first limit from the corrected anomaly and dividing the remainder by the planet's daily motion in anomaly. To find how many days remain before it turns direct, the corrected anomaly is subtracted from the second limit; for a direct planet the days until it turns retrograde are found by subtracting the corrected anomaly from the first limit. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.177}
