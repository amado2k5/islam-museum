## What came from turning and looking
What comes from a person turning on himself, looking at turning things or looking from a high place is treated with stillness, rest and sleep if it does not quiet quickly; the book names other means, which are not reproduced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.116}

## The material kind
What comes from blood and humours confined in the body is treated by evacuating blood, which the book calls the best treatment of all the kinds of material vertigo; if mixed humours are with the blood, or the cause is the humours without the blood, evacuation is hastened by what belongs to each humour, hot or mixed; if the cause is in the stomach and the humours in it, vomiting is used and then evacuation according to the strength; and when the humours are known to be simple bile, by what belongs to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.116} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.117}

## The source organ and the cause
If the cause is in another organ, each is treated as due and the head is strengthened; when the matter is known to be in the head alone, it is treated according to the matters, as known in the Canon; if the cause is seen to be a changing bad temperament, its cause and sign must be known as taught, and it is treated by the opposite so that the temperament returns to the natural; if the cause is a blow or a fall, it is treated first as said in its chapter, and if it heals and the vertigo remains, the vertigo is treated as set out; the sufferer must keep from looking at anything that turns fast and from looking down from caves, peaks, hills and high roofs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.117}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are left out here. The local procedures the book names are also left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.116} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.117}
