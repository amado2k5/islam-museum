## The general principle and headache from the sun's heat
The aim in treating this headache is cooling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.55} For the one from the burning of the sun the treatment is this same treatment with added care in tempering and cooling the air and in taking shelter in cool dwellings, and everything that stirs violently is to be avoided, shouting, much thought and hunger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.55} The one from the burning of the sun is easy to change when it first takes on its colour, and if neglected it is not unlikely that its treatment becomes impossible or hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.55} Often the sun causes a headache that is not only from its heating but from its stirring vapours and moving settled humours, and such a one cannot do without evacuations {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.55}

## Bloody matter
As for the one from a hot bloody matter, one must hasten to draw blood as need requires and as the strength allows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.55} If that does not suffice and the pain remains as it was, the vessels throb throughout, and fullness is seen in the head, face and eye, the book describes a further drawing of blood, and reports from Arkighaysh a measure if that does not suffice; their places are not reproduced here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.55-56} The softening of the bowels is to be kept up throughout, and the sufferer fed with cooling foods that produce a cold blood tending somewhat to dryness and thickness and to astringency, unless dryness of the bowels is to be guarded against, for in treating diseases of the head you have much need of looseness of the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.56} What is used of local means is to have cooling without strong moistening, but with some repelling and some astringency {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.56}

## Bilious matter
As for treatment of the one from a bilious matter, if you see in it the least stirring of blood, the treatment is to draw a little blood; otherwise begin with evacuation by slippery softening means that have no harshness or strong squeezing, and by soft clysters {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.56} If the bilious matters are thick, or soaked in the layers of the stomach, not cast out by vomiting nor slid out by slippery purgatives, you need a stronger evacuation, and then change the temperament by what cools and moistens, in the body by foods and drinks, and in the head by the treatments mentioned in the Canon {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.56}

## Intercourse (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
In listing what is to be avoided of everything that stirs violently, Ibn Sina names intercourse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.55}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are not reproduced here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.55-57}
