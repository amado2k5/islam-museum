## Overview
An *utum* (singular of *atam*) is a high, fortified palace-tower. Yathrib before the Hijra, as al-Samhudi presents it, was a city of isolated towers and not of connected houses. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.177}

## The number of towers
Al-Samhudi transmits from Ibn al-Najjar fifty-nine towers belonging to the Jews, and thirteen towers belonging to the Arabs who lived among them before the Aws and the Khazraj. Ibn Zabala gives the names of many of them. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.177}

## Named towers
Among those named in *Wafa al-wafa*: the tower of al-Shab'an of the Banu 'Akwa, the tower of al-Shar'i of the people of al-Shawt, the tower of the people of al-Walij, the two well-known towers called "the two shaykhs" of the Jews, and the two towers of the people of Zabala. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.177}

Among the towers of the Aws and the Khazraj after the humiliation of the Jews: "Waqim" of the Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal, "al-Ra'l," "al-Muyassar" of the Banu Haritha, and "al-Rayyan" of the Banu Mujdha'a. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.202-204}
