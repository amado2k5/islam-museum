## Summary
This page sets out the place of [[language/arabic|Arabic]] in [[place/algeria|Algeria]] as it appears in the Languages section of the Encyclopaedia Britannica article on Algeria and in the Algeria page of The World Factbook. It ascribes each statement to its page and, where the two differ, names both. The constitutional text is added in its original wording, as supplied in a research summary that the museum did not check against the page.

## Official status
The encyclopedia says Arabic became the official national language of Algeria in 1990 {cite:britannica-algeria-languages}, and makes Tamazight official in 2016, as below. The Factbook says: Arabic (official), French (lingua franca), Tamazight (official) {cite:cia-factbook-algeria}. In its article on the Semitic languages the encyclopedia lists Algeria among the places where Arabic is official, with Tamazight {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. The sources agree on official status, and the encyclopedia dates it, which the Factbook does not.

## Constitutional text
The Constitution of Algeria, Article 3. The research summary supplied to the museum gives this Arabic wording: «اللّغة العربيّة هي اللّغة الوطنيّة والرّسميّة.», which the museum translates: "The Arabic language is the national and official language". The summary names the Constitutional Court of Algeria as the official source; the museum did not open that page, so the wording is taken from the summary as supplied, and the founder approved using it so on 21 November 2026 {cite:official-constitution-algeria}.

## Language shares
No official figure: the census of the national statistics office, the ONS, gives none, and the UNSD 2008 holdings contain Arabic and French literacy categories only; the summary notes that the filtered totals returned HTTP 404. A UNESCO GEMR background paper of 2025 cites about 20 to 30% Tamazight, which the summary describes as a spoken-population estimate by the paper's author, given with a disclaimer; it is not an official figure. This section comes from a research summary supplied to the museum; the museum did not open the pages it names, and the founder approved using the summary as supplied on 21 November 2026 {cite:unesco-gemr-2025-algeria-background-paper} {cite:undata-table-27}.

## The standard and the spoken
The encyclopedia says most Algerians speak one of several dialects of vernacular Arabic, generally similar to those spoken in adjacent areas of Morocco and Tunisia, and that Modern Standard Arabic is taught in schools {cite:britannica-algeria-languages}. It names no dialect and divides none, and no classification of them was read in the Factbook. For the duality in general see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Fusha and the dialects]]. Of the policy of Arabization the encyclopedia says that, adopted since independence to promote indigenous Arabic and Islamic cultural values through society, it replaced French by Arabic as the national medium and as the main language of instruction in primary and secondary schools, and that some Amazigh groups resisted it, fearing domination by the Arabic-speaking majority. The Factbook calls French a lingua franca; the two descriptions do not coincide, since the encyclopedia describes Arabic replacing French in teaching and the Factbook describes French's role among people, and nothing that reconciles them was read.

## Other languages in the country
The encyclopedia says the Amazigh language, Tamazight, in several geographic dialects, is spoken by Algeria's ethnic Imazighen, most of whom are also bilingual in Arabic, and that it was granted national status in 2002 and upgraded to an official language in 2016 {cite:britannica-algeria-languages}. The Factbook names among its dialects Kabyle (Taqbaylit), Shawiya (Tacawit), Mzab and Tuareg (Tamahaq), and gives no shares {cite:cia-factbook-algeria}. For the Maghrib as a whole the encyclopedia says about one-sixth of the population still speak one of the Berber languages, most of them in Algeria and Morocco, and that most also speak some form of Arabic {cite:britannica-maghrib}. Compare [[concept/lughat-al-qabail|the languages of the tribes]].

## From the history of the language
For the history of Arabic in the Maghrib in general the encyclopedia says that after the conquest of the 7th and 8th centuries the Arabs imposed on the native peoples the religion of Islam and Arabic, the language of the Qur'an {cite:britannica-maghrib}. For Algeria alone no detailed account was read beyond the policy of Arabization after independence. For the country among the Arab states, and its joining the League in 1962 as the encyclopedia states, see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab states and the League]].
