## Summary
In the epicycle picture al-Battani makes the radius of the epicycle the line between the centres, two parts, four minutes and a half and a quarter, and moves the Sun on the epicycle against the order of the signs by the mean motion each day, while the centre of the epicycle moves with the order of the signs by that same mean motion {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.72-73}.

## Two worked cases
Al-Battani gives two cases: the first when the Sun stands thirty parts from the apogee and the second at a hundred and fifty, and in each he reckons the right-angled triangle whose sides come from the epicycle and from the zodiac. In the first the equation comes out at about fifty-seven minutes and forty-nine seconds, so that the Sun's place on the zodiac is twenty-nine degrees, two minutes and eleven seconds from the apogee; in the second it comes out at about one part, one minute and twenty-four seconds, so that the Sun lies a hundred and fifty-one parts, one minute and twenty-four seconds past the apogee {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.73-74}. These are not all the numbers of the printed text, since some sides of the triangle are in disturbed letters {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.73}.
