## From the side of the body and brain

What a general temperament of the body produces comes from a predominating dryness, a predominating humoural moisture or temperamental one without matter, or a vapour rising from the body and especially the stomach, or a cold with or without matter, or a predominating material or immaterial heat. Or it follows a cause in the brain itself from the known brain diseases, whether in the substance of the brain or in the whole anterior ventricle, such as a compressing blow befalling it so the eye does not see; mostly this is moisture, and excessive bodily and psychic evacuation and repeated evacuations bring the power down and dry the matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.201} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.202}

## From the side of the visual spirit

What belongs to the visual spirit itself may befall it by thinning, thickening, coarsening or dwindling. Abundance is the best and most beneficial state; thinness mostly arises from dryness and may come from violent dispersion on looking at the sun and bright things, and too strong a gathering may lead to a dissolving congestion so it thickens first then thins greatly, as happens with long staying in darkness. Coarseness comes from moisture or a strong gathering that does not lead to using a thinning temperament; the cause may lie in the original constitution. Scantiness may be constitutional, or from severe dryness and frequent evacuations, or from great weakness of the forebrain and hard illnesses, and death draws near if the spirit is dissolved {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.202}

## From the side of the layers, humours, pupil and nerve

The lesion through the layers is mostly in the outer ones, not the deep: either a bad temperament, a vapour held back in them, a surplus moisture mixing with them, or a dryness, roughness and parching befalling them, especially the uvea and cornea; or corruption of their surface by traces of ulcers or by much endured ophthalmia that takes away their clearness; or a foreign colour entering them as jaundice yellows the cornea; or a lesion of redness or peeling of the natural colour. What befalls the pupil and its passage is narrowing beyond nature, widening, or a complete or partial obstruction as in the descent of the water and the foul ulcer. Through the humours: the ice-like humour changing its balanced consistency or leaving its place; the egg-white humour growing too abundant or thick or losing its clearness; the glassy humour harms sight not primarily but by harming the ice-like humour; and the retinal layer harms by breaking its continuity — in part, sight lessens, in whole, it is lost. Through the nerve: an obstruction, a swelling, a widening or a wearing away {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.202} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.203}
