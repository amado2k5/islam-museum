## Overview
Tashmir is lifting a garment or its hem; it is disliked in prayer by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5196}

## Definition and related terms
In the lexicon tashmir means lifting: one rolls up a loincloth or garment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5196} Sadl is letting a garment hang without closing its sides, or laying it over the head or shoulders and letting its ends fall; it is disliked in prayer by agreement, on the hadith of Abu Hurayra forbidding it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5197} Isbal is letting a garment trail to the ground out of pride; it is disliked, on the hadith that God does not look at one who trails his garment in conceit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5197} The sound hadiths also carry a warning against the one who trails, the one who reminds of his gifts, and the one who sells by a false oath. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5197}

## The ruling in prayer
Tucking up in prayer is disliked by agreement, on the prohibition of tucking up garments and hair. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5198} The Malikis dislike it in prayer only when it is done for the prayer; doing it outside prayer, or in prayer without that aim, is not disliked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5198} For them, lifting the hem from the shin for a task that is unfinished when prayer comes is not disliked; the Mudawwana seems to allow it whether or not he returns to the task, while al-Shabibi restricted it to the case of returning to the task. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5198}
