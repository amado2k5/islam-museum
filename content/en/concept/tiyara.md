## Summary
Tiyara (omen-taking) means taking an ill omen from a thing. Originally, they would come upon a bird or a gazelle and startle it. If it went to the right, they proceeded with what they intended and considered it good; if it went to the left, they desisted and took it as an ill omen. They would also take ill omen if a bird or an animal crossed their path {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2/851-857}.

## Related practices
Close to omen-taking are their hanging of a rabbit's ankle-bone, their taking ill omen from certain days, months, animals, houses and women, and their belief in contagion and in the [[concept/hama|hama]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38}.

## Not covered here
The hadith "There is no contagion and no omen-taking" and the rulings on omen-taking will be presented under their own entries. The author refers to Sahih al-Bukhari with the commentary of al-Saharanfuri {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38}.

## For young visitors
They believed that a bird or a day could bring misfortune. Islam taught them that this is superstition.
