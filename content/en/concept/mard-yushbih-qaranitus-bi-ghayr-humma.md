## What some physicians claimed
Some physicians claimed that a disease like qaranitus may occur without fever, and that its being without fever shows, to them, that it has no swelling; they said it is marked by great restlessness and leaping, so that the sufferer cannot keep still and almost climbs walls, with intense vexation, distress, thirst and tightness of breath, and when he drinks water he chokes on it and throws it up {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77} It is said to kill within the day for the most part, and may stretch to four days, and none escapes it; their faces and tongues turn black, their eyes are fixed and their state is that of the anguished, then their movements slacken, their pulse falls and they die, most of them of choking {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77}

## Ibn Sina's view
Ibn Sina said: it is not unlikely that the cause of this is the brain's sharing with another noble organ, such as the muscles of breathing when a great convulsion comes upon them, or another disorder that tends toward quinsy and reaches the brain, so that it confuses and spoils it, mixes the mind and brings thirst by drying the regions of the throat and chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.77}
