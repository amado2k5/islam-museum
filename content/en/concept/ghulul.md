## Summary
A legal and ethical ruling in Islam's laws of war: an absolute prohibition on any taking or concealing of spoil wealth before its just division among the army; the Prophet stressed it at [[place/al-jirana|al-Ji'rana]], warning against even thread and needle {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/135}.

## The scene of the hair-tuft and the warning
Amr ibn Shu'ayb related that when the people crowded upon the Prophet at al-Ji'rana asking for the fay', he leaned against a camel, took a tuft of hair from its hump between two fingers and raised it, saying: "People, by God I have nothing of your fay' — not even this hair-tuft — save the fifth, and the fifth is returned to you. Hand over thread and needle, for ghulul is upon its people a disgrace, a fire and a shame on the Day of Resurrection" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/135}.

A man of the Ansar came with a ball of spun camel hair and said: "I took this to mend my camel's pack-saddle." The Prophet said: "What was mine and Banu Abd al-Muttalib's is yours." The man said: "If it has reached this, I have no need of it" — and threw it down {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/135}.

## For young visitors
The Prophet taught his army complete honesty in distributing wealth, and stressed that no soldier may secretly take anything of the spoils — even a thread and a needle — to guarantee full justice for all.
