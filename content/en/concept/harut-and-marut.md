## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an names them only in [[passage/quran-2-102|al-Baqarah 102]], the verse that tells the story of magic through Babylon's king: God sent down two angels in Babylon — Harut and Marut — and had them teach what people were tempted by of magic, making them a trial.

## Among the commentators
Ibn Kathir details the whole story of the magic, noting the two angels descended to Babylon, taught nothing themselves but permitted its people to learn it, and were themselves tried {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## Notes
Some theologians held they are not angels but devils or men; others interpret them as the two angels who taught magic as a trial, as in the verse. The primary interpretation takes the verse as explicit about their identity {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
