## Summary
Ibn Sina advises that diseases with danger begin with strong treatment, and those without danger rise by degrees to the stronger if the lighter does not suffice, that one drug is not kept to but drugs are changed, and that when the illness is unclear it is left to nature {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.270-271}.

## Strong first or by degrees
Where there is danger and the strength may be lost if the needed treatment is delayed or lightened, the strong treatment is begun first; where there is no danger the physician rises to the stronger if the lighter does not serve. He is warned not to flee from what is right because its effect is slow, nor to persist in an error because its harm goes unnoticed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.

## Changing the drug, and leaving the illness to nature
One treatment with one drug is not to be held to, but drugs are changed, since the familiar is not acted on; every body, indeed every organ, and the body and organ at one time rather than another, has its own way of responding to one drug rather than another. When the illness is unclear, leave it to nature and do not hurry, for nature will either overcome the illness or show it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.270-271}.
