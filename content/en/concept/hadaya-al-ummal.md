## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds that public money is a trust in the hands of the ruler and the official, and that accepting gifts because of one's office comes close to bribery. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:57:30}

## Public money is a trust
The Sheikh holds that the ruler is a trustee over the people's money, not its owner, and that public money must never be plundered. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:15:30}
He cites a saying attributed to the Prophet, that the wealthy should refrain and the poor should take what is fair (needs_source: the saying is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:15:30}

## Gifts to an official
The Sheikh says that a public official may take, under the name of a gift, what in reality belongs to the stories of bribery. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:55:30}
He reports that in England gifts are allowed up to a limit of eighty pounds sterling (needs_source: the figure is not sourced in the recording), and that anything above that is returned to the government and not kept by the official. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:56:00}

## The Sheikh's view on gifts (opinion)
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's view (opinion): year-end gifts that pupils give their teachers can become bribes when they are meant to win favour. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:57:30}
He also says that gifts to officials are embezzlement (ghulul), a saying whose source is not given in the recording (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep12 00:57:30}

## Disguised bribery and private lessons
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's view (opinion): private lessons imposed on parents as a form of extortion are a disguised bribe, and God does not bless them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 01:07:30}
He holds that income gained through bribery brings no blessing, and that unlawful earnings nullify good in their holder. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:52:30}

## Accountability of officials
The Sheikh recounts that Umar ibn al-Khattab, returning from the pilgrimage, consulted the keeper of the treasury about the cost of the pilgrimage. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:13:30}
He tells how Umar examined a report that Abu Hurayra had traded while he was a governor, and how Abu Hurayra was cleared. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:17:30}
He holds that laxity in holding officials to account is a sign of decline. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:17:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh tells the story of Ibn al-Lutbiyya, who came to the Prophet saying 'this is for you and this was given to me', and the Prophet said: why did he not sit in his father's and mother's house and see whether anything would be given to him? He concludes that any gift an employee takes because of his post is a bribe, and that the briber, the bribed and the go-between are cursed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep12 00:09:30}
