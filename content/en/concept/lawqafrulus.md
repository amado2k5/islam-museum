## Nature and selection
An Egyptian stone used by launderers to whiten clothes; soft and quickly dissolved in water. The editor's note, citing al-Mu'tamad, calls it an African stone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Actions and properties
Bole-like, it dries without stinging and is astringent, stopping matter from flowing to an organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Useful for ulcers and abscesses, especially in the soft organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Eyes
It helps the eye ailment called al-gharb and goes into remedies for eye ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Excretory organs
Good for spitting of blood, useful for chronic diarrhoea and bladder pain, and taken up as a pessary or suppository to stop bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.
