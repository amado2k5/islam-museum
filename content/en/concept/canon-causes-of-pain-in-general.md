## Summary
In chapter 19 of the second lesson Ibn Sina defines pain as the sensing of what is unsuitable and limits its causes to two genera: what changes the temperament suddenly, which he calls unequal bad temperament, and what breaks continuity. He shows that a settled, concordant bad temperament does not hurt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.147-148}.

## The two genera
Pain is one of the unnatural states that befall an animal body; it is the sensing of the unsuitable. Its causes are two: a genus that changes the temperament at once, called unequal bad temperament, and a genus that separates continuity. By unequal he means that an organ has in its substance a settled temperament, and then a foreign, opposed temperament comes upon it, so that it becomes hotter or colder than it was; the sensing power feels the arrival of the unsuitable and suffers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.

## Why a concordant temperament does not hurt
A concordant bad temperament is one where the bad temperament has settled in the organ's substance and displaced its original one until it seems the original. This does not hurt, because it is not felt: the sensing power is acted on by an opposite that arrives and changes it, not by a settled state that does not alter it. So a patient with hectic fever feels less of the burning than one with a one-day or tertian fever, though hectic heat is much stronger, since it is fixed in the substance of the original organs, while the tertian heat comes from a humour lying beside organs whose natural temperament is still kept {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.147-148}.

## The bath as an example
Ibn Sina gives a case from health. Someone surprised by a winter bath in hot water, or even tepid, recoils and is troubled, because his body's state is far from it and opposed to it; then he grows used to it and enjoys it. After an hour in the bath his body may become hotter than the water, and if the very first water is poured on him again he shivers, taking it as cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.148}.
