## Summary
Ibn Sina explains speed and frequency of the pulse as need making up for what is lost of greatness, and likens it to a man carrying a heavy load {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.174-175}.

## Making up by speed, then by frequency
If the need is severe, the power strong and the instrument unyielding to greatness through hardness, the pulse must become quick to make up by speed what is lost of greatness. If the power is weak and neither greatness nor speed can be made, it must become frequent to make up by frequency what greatness and speed lost, many beats standing in for one sufficient great one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.

## The example of carrying
It is like a man who must carry something heavy: if he is strong enough to carry it whole he does so; if not he divides it in two and hurries; if not he divides it into many parts and carries each as he can, slowly or quickly. If the power is strong and the instrument yielding but the need more than moderately severe, the power adds speed to greatness, and if the need is greater still it adds frequency to greatness and speed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.174-175}.
