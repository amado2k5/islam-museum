## Nature
Stems and flowers reddish-dark or fine green; the flower is bitter with slight astringency and a sharpness short of bitterness. The leaf is herb-like and creeps on the ground, resembling the leaf of the spice plant, but finer, weaker and more downy; its flower is yellow. The editor's note gives the name as ground pine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Temperament and properties
Hot in the second degree and drying in the third. It opens and cleanses; its cleansing of the inner organs is greater than its heating, and it has a laxative force. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.517}

## Swellings and wounds
Applied to hardened swellings, especially a hard breast; it stops the creeping sensation like ants. With honey it heals wounds and septic ulcers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Joints, liver and digestion
Joints and liver: it helps sciatica, especially when drunk with honey; some say a long course cures sciatica, and it softens hard gout. It opens obstructions of the liver, helps its diseases and the spleen, and helps black jaundice. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling): the book says it opens blockages of the womb, brings on urine and eases its difficulty, brings on the menses, helps pains of the kidneys, and cleanses the womb. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}

## Poisons and substitutes
Useful against the harm of the poison that some call orkastun. The book names two substitutes for it; their weights are left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.518}
