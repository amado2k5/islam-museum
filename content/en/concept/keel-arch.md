## Overview
The keel arch is a pointed arch shaped like an upturned boat; Yeomans counts it a Fatimid innovation that replaced the round arches of the first al-Azhar mosque and remained, structural or decorative, a distinctive feature of Cairene architecture until the Ottoman conquest. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.53} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.54}

## In Fatimid architecture
In the qibla-arcade dome of [[building/al-azhar|al-Azhar]], from the reign of al-Hafiz, the dome rests on keel arches over antique columns braced by wooden tie-beams; Yeomans sees these features as defining Fatimid structure, much lighter than the solid brick piers of Tulunid building. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.53} The keel arch also became a decorative frame: the façade of [[building/al-aqmar-mosque|al-Aqmar]] (1125) has three keel-arched niches with fluted hoods, and its arcades are keel arches on antique columns framed by Kufic bands. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.62} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.64} The portico of the [[building/mosque-of-al-salih-talai|mosque of al-Salih Tala'i]] (1160) has five keel arches on Corinthian columns, and its arcades are keel arches outlined in Kufic. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.67} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.68}

## After the Fatimids
Keel-arched panels decorate the first storey of the minarets of Qaytbay and al-Ghuri at al-Azhar, from the late Mamluk period, and the northern minaret of the mosque of al-Hakim completed by Baybars al-Jashankir. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.55} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.62}
