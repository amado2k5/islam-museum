## Summary
Ibn Sina says the neck was made for the sake of the windpipe, that its vertebrae are seven, and that the first and second have properties the others lack, in the movement of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.48-50}.

## Properties of the neck vertebrae
Since the neck vertebra is carried on those beneath, it must be smaller, for what is carried must be lighter than what carries it. The beginning of the cord is thicker, so the holes in the neck vertebrae must be wider; and since smallness and a wide cavity thin a body, they were made the hardest of the vertebrae. Their spines are small, so they are not prone to break; their wings are large and double-headed; and their joints are looser than those below, since they need movement more than stillness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.48-49}. Each of them except the first has the eleven processes: a spine, two wings, four articular processes pointing up and four pointing down {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.49}.

## The first and second vertebrae
As the text puts it, the movement of the head to right and left is completed at the joint between it and the first vertebra, and its movement forward and back at the joint with the second. The first vertebra has two hollows on its upper projections into which two processes of the skull bone enter. The second has a long hard process called the tooth, growing from its front inner side and passing through the hole of the first, in front of the cord, which strong ligaments screen from it. The first has no spine and no wings, and its nerve leaves through two small holes behind at the two sides. The second has its holes at the sides of the spine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.49-50}.
