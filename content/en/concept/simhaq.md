## Definition
The simhaq is a thin membrane above the skull bone that separates the flesh from the bone. In the usage of most jurists it is a wound to the head that reaches that membrane, cutting the flesh without reaching the bone; the Malikis call it mulatta, and their simhaq is a wound that strips the skin from the flesh. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9823}

## Other head wounds
Wounds to the face or head, the shijaj, vary by severity. Some do not reach the bone, such as the haritha (scratch), the dami'a (weeping), the damiya (bleeding), the ba'ida (incision), the mutalahima (deep flesh-tearing) and the simhaq; others reach the bone, such as the mudiha (which exposes the bone), the hashima (which breaks it), the ama (which reaches the brain lining) and the munaqqila (which displaces the bone pieces). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9824}

## Ruling
For most jurists the simhaq carries no fixed blood-money and no fixed arsh; a just assessment (hukuma) is owed for it, whether intentional or by error, because no text governs it and it is hard to measure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9824} The Malikis, and a view among the Shafi'is and a report from a Hanafi jurist named Muhammad, hold that retaliation is due for it when intentional, since it can be measured. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9824} See the entries on blood-money and retaliation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9824}
