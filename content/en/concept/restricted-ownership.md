## Overview
The author bases the chapter on ownership on the principle that the true owner is God, that human beings are stewards, and that private ownership is recognized but restricted by public rights {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 50} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 56}.

## Public Ownership and Reserves
The author explains that a public reserve (hima) is not the private property of the state and may not be disposed of at will, because Umar said that wealth belongs to God and servants belong to God {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 59} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 60}.

## International Ownership
The author extends the concept to the high seas and international waters, viewing them as the common property of humanity {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 61} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 62}.
