## Overview
In al-Baladhuri, *dhimma* is a written contract of safe-conduct for the People of the Book who made peace with the Muslims. The best known of its forms is the Prophet's document for the people of Najran. It is the basis of the [[concept/jizya|poll tax]] in the order he sets out. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The Najran document
Al-Baladhuri reports that they have the dhimma of God and of His Messenger over themselves, their religion, their land, and their property, the absent and the present among them. Among the conditions of their peace was that they should not be made to abandon their religion or their ranks, that they should not be mustered, and that they should not be taxed on their produce (*ushr*). The text of the document says that no bishop should be moved from his bishopric and no monk from his monastery, that their rights should not be changed, and that no army should tread their land. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.85-89}

Whoever of them consumes usury, the Prophet's dhimma is free of him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.85-89}

## The document of the people of Muqanna
In the document for the people of Muqanna, they have the dhimma of God and of His Messenger, together with safe-conduct and pardon for all bloodshed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.80-81}

## Renewal of the dhimma
Al-Baladhuri reports that the caliphs renewed the dhimma. Abu Bakr wrote to the people of Najran in the manner of the Prophet's document, and 'Uthman described them as a people who have a dhimma when he remitted two hundred garments from them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.85-89}
