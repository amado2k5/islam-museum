## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan defines the vision as 'a mental picture of the organisation at a point in the future', like a balance sheet — a picture of the financial position at a point in time — except that the vision is a future picture of every aspect of the organisation, not only the financial; it is for him the most important element of strategic planning: written rightly the plan may come out right, written wrongly the plan certainly fails {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:07:00}.

## Writing the vision
He gives a practical method: the planning team fixes the key questions (number of employees, branches, sales, profit ratio, main products, rank among competitors, the technology to be used); each member answers them in writing individually; the answers are then collected and shown to all — what is agreed is passed over and what is disputed is discussed and voted on {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:17:00}.
He says this method is not 'scientific' in the sense that two teams given the same information would not produce the same vision, and that management has no scientific method for setting a vision; strategic planning is to him both science and art, and the vision is the artistic part {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:22:30}.
The vision can be summarised in a sentence everyone memorises — like Savola's '555' formula: five billion riyals in five years with five hundred employees — though he considers the step optional {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:20:30}.

## The mission
He defines the mission as a sentence or phrase summarising who we are and what we want, with ten criteria: clear and easy; short, no more than twenty words and preferably nearer ten; describing what the organisation offers, its audience and its strategic axis (what do you sell?); expressing distinction over competitors; containing one to three values at most; specific without vagueness and without numbers or details; achievable within the plan's duration; and inspiring pride in employees and customers {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:33:00}.
He analyses real examples: the mission of his own American Creativity Academy in Kuwait ('the best education in the Middle East in an ethical and creative environment'), McDonald's mission, the Otis elevators mission — which he finds short of distinction and values — and the US Air Force mission, which repeated the notion of 'worldwide' four times deliberately {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:47:30}.

## Vision versus strategic intent
In the fourth episode al-Suwaidan distinguishes two terms people confuse: the strategic vision — five years for small organisations, fifteen to twenty for large ones — which is open to change and review, and the strategic intent: the essential reason for the organisation's existence, the purpose that would not change in a hundred years; the vision 'to make five billion profit', the intent 'to make profit' {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:57:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:39:30}.

## Slogan and logo
He distinguishes vision, mission, slogan and logo: the slogan is advertising that picks one or two values and proclaims them, and can be changed every three to five years {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep02 00:35:00}.

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders": values, vision and mission as steps of the plan
In both filmed leaders' academies al-Suwaidan teaches values, vision and mission as steps of Pfeiffer's planning model, as he adapts it. ([[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|first academy]], lessons 13–14; [[work/leadership-development-academy-2-program|second academy]], sessions 4–5.) He insists that values in management are not ethics. They are whatever the organisation counts as valuable, such as profit, market share, transparency or quality. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep04 00:08:00} He counts himself among those who reject secrecy in their work. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep04 00:15:00} His own definition of vision is "a mental picture of the organisation at a point in the future". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 00:55:00} It is built from key questions that all begin "What is the state of the organisation at the end of the plan?" {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 01:00:00} The detailed vision is set out field by field, for finance, technology and the rest. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep04 00:34:30} As an example of a one-line summary he gives the vision a large Arab oils company agreed at a conference where, he says, he was the keynote speaker. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep13 01:09:00}

A mission, in his definition, is a sentence that sums up who we are and what we want. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 00:03:00} He gives its marks. It is short, at most twenty words and nearer ten; he says no mission he has written has exceeded fourteen. It states the line of business, carries the key values, shows how the organisation stands out from competitors, and makes its reader proud. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep04 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep04 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep05 00:07:00} He says the mission is put on T-shirts and at the school entrance for pride. The plan itself rests on the vision. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 00:12:00} He tests real missions with the trainees, among them that of his own school, which he says took six people six weeks to write. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep05 00:13:30} He also separates the vision of fifteen to twenty years from a "strategic intent" of a hundred years or more. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep14 00:15:00}
