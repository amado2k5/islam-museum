Walad (a child) in the language is one born; it is used for one or many, for a small or a grown child, and for a male or a female. Ibn (a son), in al-Jurjani's definition, is a male born from the seed of another of its kind, and the Mawsu'a notes that the son is male while the child (walad) may be either sex. Bint (a daughter) is the feminine of ibn. Hafid (a grandchild) is a child's child. Sibt (a grandson by a daughter, in the lexicographers' usage) is applied by the Shafi'is to a daughter's child, and by the Hanbalis to both kinds of grandchild. Dhurriyya (descendants) means in the majority's usage the children, daughters and their children, while some Hanbalis exclude the children of daughters. Nasl (offspring) is wider than walad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17905}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17906}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17907}

The child follows the better of its parents in religion, by agreement of the jurists. If one spouse is Muslim the child is Muslim; if one parent converts and the child is young, the child becomes Muslim with him, for the child's benefit. The Hanafis hold that if one parent is a People of the Book and the other a Magian, the child is a People of the Book, since Magianism is worse. The Hanafis require for the child to follow the better parent that the two share one abode. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17907}

The child follows the religion of his parents where the rule of following applies. If the child is in our land and the father in the abode of unbelief, the child is treated as a Muslim by ruling; but the child does not follow a father who is in the abode of Islam while the child is in the abode of unbelief. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17908}

The apostasy of a discerning boy. Abu Yusuf, Zufar, al-Shafi'i and a view of Ahmad hold that it is not valid, since a boy's words have no effect for harm. Abu Hanifa, Muhammad, the Malikis and the known view of Ahmad rule it valid, since Islam depends on sound reason, not puberty. Once his apostasy is established, the rulings of apostasy apply: he does not inherit or pass on an estate, his wife is separated from him, no funeral prayer is made over him, and he is compelled to return to Islam; but he is not killed, because killing is a punishment and he is not of the kind to bear it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17908}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17909}

At birth the call to prayer is recited in the right ear of the newborn, and the summons in the left. In the funeral prayer for a dead person, the father comes first, then the son, then the son's son. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17909}

On leading prayer by the son of an illegitimate union or of li'an, the rulings are set out in their own places. The Hanbalis see no harm in the imamate of a child disowned by li'an if his religion is sound and he is fit to lead; 'A'isha said of the child of illegitimacy that he bears nothing of his parents' sin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17909}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17910}

By agreement, zakat may not be given to one's own child, since the benefits of property are joined between them. A person pays zakat al-fitr for himself and for those whose maintenance is due, including his children; there is a difference on paying it for a child who died or was born after the obligation arose. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17910}

By the majority, a child may perform the pilgrimage on behalf of his parents, on the report of the woman of Khath'am who asked whether she might do it for her aged father. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17910}

A child's lineage is established from the mother by birth, and from the father by the marriage bed, acknowledgement or evidence; it ends only by li'an. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17910}

On sacrifice for a child: if the child is grown, his father is not obliged; if he is a minor without property, the Hanafis report from al-Hasan from Abu Hanifa that it is obligatory, and another narration that it is not obligatory but recommended from the father's own money. If the minor has property, his father or executor sacrifices from it, according to Abu Hanifa and Abu Yusuf, against Muhammad and Zufar. The Malikis make it a sunna for the parent to sacrifice for poor parents and for a young child until the boy reaches puberty; the Shafi'is allow it only for the father and grandfather from their own money. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17910}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17911}

The 'aqiqa, the animal slaughtered for a newborn in thanks to God, is a sunna for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, recommended for the Malikis and permitted for the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17911}
