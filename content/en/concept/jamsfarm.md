## Nature and selection
Its strength is like that of wormwood with the nightshade berry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.427}. The editor's footnote calls it Solomon's basil; that is the editor's name, not the book's description {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.427}.

## Actions
It opens and soothes flatulence and winds in particular {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.427}.

## Uses by organ
Food: it dissolves sticky humours in the stomach and is very good for children's stomachs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.427}.
Excretory organs: it is listed for womb winds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.427}; a book listing, labelled.
