## In amount
As to amount, it may be much against the aperture, blocking it wholly so the eye sees nothing; or little, blocking one side and leaving one open, so that what lies opposite the blocked side is not perceived and what lies opposite the open side is — sometimes half a thing or part of it is seen and the rest only by turning the pupil, sometimes it is seen whole once and not again, according to its place against the block or the opening. This partial block may sit above, or above and below, or in the very centre of the aperture with all around it clear — then the sides of each thing are seen and not its middle, as a hole or pit in its centre; the meaning is that it is not seen, so a darkness is imagined {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.213}
## In quality
As to quality, it differs in consistency — some thin and clear, not veiling light and sun, some very thick — and in colour: air-coloured, gypsum-white, pearl-white, white tending to blue or turquoise and gold, yellow, black, dusty. The most amenable to treatment by colour are the air-coloured, the pearl-white, the slightly blue and the turquoise; the gypsum, green, turbid, deep black and yellow do not accept the couching; and of the thick kinds one may harden until it is no longer water and has no treatment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.213}
## Testing whether it accepts the couching
The most amenable by consistency is the thin one which, watched in bright shade while a finger presses it, scatters quickly then regathers — its removal by the couching is hoped for, though repeating this test clouds the water and makes couching harder. Another test: a cotton is laid on the eye and blown into hard, then removed and one looks quickly for movement in the water — if seen, it is couchable; likewise if closing one eye widens the other ⁦(the couching is a hand-work carried here as the book's description of what is done, not as a procedure)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.213}
