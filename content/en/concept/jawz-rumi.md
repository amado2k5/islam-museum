## Nature and selection
The book says the Roman walnut tree grows in a river called Eridanus; a gum flows from it, and when it comes out it hardens in the river; this is what is called electron {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}. Some call it khawsufurun, which is amber (kahraba): when rubbed it gives a sweet smell, and its colour is like gold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}. The editor's footnote says the substance is kahraba and that the meaning of akiros is not established {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: very hot in the third degree and dry in the first; its gum is very heating and its flower more heating still {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}.

## Uses by organ
Head: Dioscorides says in his book that its fruit, drunk with vinegar, helps someone with epilepsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}.
Joints: a poultice of its leaf with vinegar helps the pulsing caused by gout {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}.
Food: drunk, its gum stops flow from the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.422}.
Excretory organs: likewise, drunk, its gum stops the flow of humours from the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.423}.
