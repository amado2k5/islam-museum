## Summary
Rikaz in the lexicon is what is fixed and hidden in the ground; for the majority it is the treasure buried by people of the pre-Islamic period, and it may name any property, though the Shafi'is restrict it to gold and silver. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8944} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8945} For the majority a mine is not rikaz, while the Hanafis use the word for both mines and hoards. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8945} One fifth is due on rikaz by agreement, following the saying that there is a fifth on buried treasure; it is due on little and much according to the majority, and the Shafi'is require a nisab. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8949} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8950} Anyone who finds it, Muslim, dhimmi, child or madman, owes the fifth according to the schools. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8950} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8951} If it is found on unowned ground, the finder takes the rest after the fifth; if it is on owned ground, most jurists give it to the owner, while Abu Yusuf and Ahmad give it to the finder. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8951} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8952} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8953} The fifth is spent like the booty in the Hanafi, Maliki and Hanbali views, and like zakat in the Shafi'i view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8954} In the abode of war the schools differ on the rule. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8953}
