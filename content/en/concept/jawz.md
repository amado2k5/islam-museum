## Nature and selection
The walnut is known and is hot. Its antidote for those heated by bile is sikanjabin, and for weak stomachs the walnut preserved in vinegar {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.415}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot in the third degree and dry at the start of the second; its dryness is less than its heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.415}. Actions: the roasted walnut is more astringent; its leaf and whole shell are astringent to bleeding; the burnt shell dries without sting; the old walnut oil is like old olive oil, and the old walnut is a strong polish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: the fresh walnut is a poultice for the marks of a blow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Swellings and pustules: the chewed kernel is put on a black ulcerating swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Wounds and ulcers: its gum helps hot ulcers, sprinkled on them or in ointments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Joints: with honey and celery for twisting of a sinew {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Eyes: its oil helps the corrosive ulcer, redness and fistulas around the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Respiratory organs: the juice of its shell and its pith stops choking; it harms a cough, and the old walnut oil causes sore throat. All kinds are used as a poultice on a swollen breast, especially the large royal walnut {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Food: the walnut is heavy to digest and bad for the stomach; the preserved and fresh are better for a cold stomach, when peeled, and the walnut preserved in honey helps a cold stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}. The author adds that it does not suit a hot stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Excretory organs: the book says it is laxative and calms colic and binds; roasted it binds more. The book says its shell stops menstrual bleeding and that its ash, drunk, prevents the menses; these are the book's listings, labelled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}. Eating a lot of it expels worms and tapeworm, and it helps the caecum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
Poisons: with fig and rue it is a remedy for all poisons, and with onion and salt a poultice for the bite of a mad dog and others {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.416}.
