## Summary
World Arabic Language Day is observed each year on 18 December and has been since 2012, according to the research summary supplied to the museum on the United Nations and UNESCO pages. {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day research summary} {cite:unesco-world-arabic-language-day research summary} This page reports what that summary says, with the founder's approval of 21 November 2026 and without re-verification. The same day is treated again with the speaker figures in [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-al-alam-al-yawm|Arabic at the United Nations and UNESCO, and the speaker figures]]; this page keeps to the day, its reason and its date.

## The decision of 2010
The summary says that in 2010 the United Nations department for global communications, then called the Department of Public Information, decided, as part of promoting multilingualism and cultural diversity, to dedicate a day to each of the United Nations' six official languages. {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day research summary} The six are Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian and Spanish; the summary says six without naming them, and the list here is for clarity. It does not say how that decision was issued or who proposed it, and it does not give the day of any language other than Arabic.

## Why 18 December
The summary says 18 December was chosen because on 18 December 1973 the United Nations General Assembly adopted Resolution 3190 (XXVIII), by which Arabic was introduced as an official and working language of the United Nations. {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day research summary} So the date is the anniversary of a General Assembly resolution, not of an event in the history of the language itself. The summary does not give the text of the resolution or what changed in practice after it was adopted.

## Since 2012
The summary says the observance has been held every year since 2012, and refers to a United Nations page and a UNESCO page on the day. {cite:un-world-arabic-language-day research summary} {cite:unesco-world-arabic-language-day research summary} It says nothing of what happened at the first observance in 2012 or who organised it, nor of the programmes of later years. The museum can add nothing to this without reading the two pages.

## Arabic and the Arab world
The day is an international recognition of this language; it passes no judgment on it and does not rank it against others, being one of six such days. Arabic has its origin in [[concept/tarikh-al-lugha-al-arabiyya|the history of Arabic]] and is the language of the countries of [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab world]], and the page [[language/arabic|the Arabic language]] gathers its standing. The academies that work on Arabic and its terminology have pages such as [[group/ittihad-al-majami-al-lughawiyya-al-ilmiyya-al-arabiyya|the Union of Arab Language Academies]].

## What this page does not reach
The source is a research summary supplied to the museum that rests on the United Nations and UNESCO pages, and the founder approved using the summary without re-verification on 21 November 2026. So the text of Resolution 3190, how the decision of 2010 came about and what happened at the yearly observances are not known here. All of the above is attributed to the summary.
