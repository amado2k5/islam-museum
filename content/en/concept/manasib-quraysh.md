## Overview
The government of [[place/mecca|Mecca]] before Islam rested on offices distributed among the clans of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]]. Five of them were gathered into the hand of [[person/qusayy-ibn-kilab|Qusayy ibn Kilab]] and then divided after him. Other offices were shared among the clans until they formed what *al-Rahiq al-Makhtum* calls a mini-state, even a quasi-democratic state, with councils resembling in our age the councils and chambers of a parliament. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32}

## Before Qusayy: the three attributes of Mudar
When [[group/khuzaa|Khuza'a]] held sole authority over Mecca, the tribes of [[group/mudar-ibn-nizar|Mudar]] had three attributes. The first was directing the people from Arafat to Muzdalifa and dispatching them from Mina on the day of departure; it was held by the Banu al-Ghawth ibn Murr of the clans of Ilyas ibn Mudar, who were called [[group/sufa|Sufa]]. The second was the departure from Jam' on the morning of the sacrifice to Mina, which belonged to the Banu [[group/adwan|Adwan]]. The third was the postponement of the sacred months, which belonged to the Banu Tamim ibn 'Adi of the Kinana. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.29} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/44} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/119-122} See [[concept/nasi|the intercalation]]. Qusayy confirmed the custodians, the families of Safwan, 'Adwan, and Murra ibn 'Awf in the offices they held, because he considered it a religion that should not be changed. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.30} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/124-125}

## The five offices of Qusayy
Qusayy held the marks of leadership and honour: {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/130} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/36}
- The presidency of [[building/dar-al-nadwa|Dar al-Nadwa]], where they consulted about the grave affairs that befell them and married off their daughters.
- The standard: no banner of war was tied except in his hand.
- The chamberlainship of the Ka'ba: no one opened its door except him, and he managed its service and custody.
- The provision of water for pilgrims: they filled basins with water for the pilgrims, sweetened with some dates and raisins, and people drank from them when they arrived in Mecca.
- The provision of food for pilgrims: food prepared for the pilgrim in the manner of hospitality. Qusayy imposed on Quraysh a levy they paid from their wealth during the season, and with it he prepared food for the pilgrims, which anyone without means or provisions could eat. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/130}

## The division after Qusayy
Qusayy bequeathed these offices to his eldest son, Abd al-Dar. When [[person/abd-manaf-ibn-qusayy|Abd Manaf]] died, his sons contested them with their cousins the sons of Abd al-Dar. They came to an agreement and divided them: the provision of water and of food to the Banu Abd Manaf, and Dar al-Nadwa, the standard, and the chamberlainship in the hands of the Banu Abd al-Dar. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/129-132} Then the water and food were drawn by lot to [[person/hashim-ibn-abd-manaf|Hashim]], who held them for his whole life, then [[person/al-muttalib-ibn-abd-manaf|al-Muttalib]], then [[person/abd-al-muttalib|Abd al-Muttalib]], and after him his sons, until Islam came and the office passed to [[person/abbas-ibn-abd-al-muttalib|al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.31} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/129-142} The details are in [[event/partition-of-meccan-offices|the partition of the offices]].

## The other offices
Quraysh had offices other than these, which they distributed among themselves. The author counts them from the history of the land of the Qur'an. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32} {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran 2/104-106}
- The *iysar* (the drawing of arrows for divination): entrusted to the Banu [[group/banu-jumah|Jumah]] (see [[concept/azlam|the arrows]]).
- The *tahjir al-amwal* (the arrangement of offerings and vows dedicated to idols), and likewise the settling of disputes: among the Banu [[group/banu-sahm|Sahm]].
- The consultation (*shura*): among the Banu [[group/banu-asad|Asad]].
- The *ashnaq* (the arrangement of blood-money and fines): among the Banu Taym.
- The *iqab* (bearing the national banner): among the Banu [[group/banu-umayya|Umayya]].
- The *qubba* (the arrangement of the camp and the command of horses): among the Banu [[group/banu-makhzum|Makhzum]].
- The embassy (*sifara*): among the Banu [[group/banu-adi|Adi]].

## What is not recorded here
The author's description of this system as a "quasi-democratic mini-state," with a comparison to parliament, is his own view. See his assessment in [[concept/pre-islamic-political-condition|the political condition]] and [[polity/emirate-of-hijaz|the emirate of the Hijaz]].

## For young visitors
Quraysh divided its duties among its families: one gave water to the pilgrims, another fed them, another carried the war banner, and another opened the door of the Ka'ba.
