## Summary
After dotting for vowels with dots, and dotting for letters, the codices still needed vowel signs that would not be confused with letter dots. This is what [[person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi|al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi]] supplied, the vowelling known today. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2250}

## What al-Khalil set down
Al-Suyuti relays that the first to lay down hamza, shadda, rawm and ishmam was al-Khalil. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2245} He says the vowelling by signs derived from letters has become famous, which is what al-Khalil produced; it is more complete, clearer, and is what is practised: the fatha a slanted stroke above the letter, the kasra likewise below it, the damma a small waw above it and tanwin a repeated mark; if it is clear, before a throat letter, it is set above it, and otherwise the two are placed in succession. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2250} The dropped alif and what stands for it are written in place in red, the dropped hamza is written as a hamza without a letter, also in red, a red iqlab sign goes on nun and tanwin before ba', a sukun before throat letters, they are left bare at assimilation and concealment, every quiescent is marked, the assimilated letter is left bare and the next is doubled, except the heavy letter ta' (tah) before ta', which takes a sukun, and the stretch mark of a long vowel goes no further than it. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2250}

## Why signs replaced dots
Khalid al-Sabt says Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan decided to distinguish the shapes of letters by dotting, and so had to replace the first vowelling, which was dots, with a new one, the familiar signs of fatha, kasra, damma and sukun. The reason is that had he kept the first marks as dots and then the others also came as dots, they would resemble each other and confuse; so he distinguished the two groups in this way. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331} Al-Sabt thus sees no conflict in attributing the present vowelling both to al-Khalil and to Abd al-Malik's time, though the details rest on al-Dani's al-Muhkam. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331}

## Colours in early codices
Al-Dani says, as al-Suyuti relays: I do not permit dotting in black because of the alteration it makes to the form of the script, nor gathering various readings in one codex in different colours, which is among the greatest confusion and change of what was written; I think the vowels, tanwin, doubling, sukun and stretch should be in red and the hamzas in yellow. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249} The detail and use of these marks belongs to [[concept/ilm-al-dabt|the science of dabt]].

## What I do not know
I found no report in the sources I read that says exactly when al-Khalil set down his vowelling, nor a text of al-Khalil's own; the attribution is al-Suyuti's, and I read no text on the exact date the new vowelling spread in codices. See [[concept/naqt-al-irab|dotting for vowels]].
