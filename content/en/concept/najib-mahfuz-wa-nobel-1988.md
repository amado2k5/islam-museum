## Summary
In 1988 the Egyptian novelist [[person/naguib-mahfouz|Naguib Mahfouz]], born in 1911 and died in 2006, won the Nobel Prize in Literature, being the first writer in Arabic to win it, according to a research summary supplied to the museum. This page sets out what the summary says of the prize, of Mahfouz's place in the history of the Arabic novel, and of the major works it names {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-summary} {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-press-release}.

## The first Arabic Nobel laureate
The summary describes Mahfouz as the first Arabic-language writer awarded the prize; the sense here is of language, not nationality, for it calls him a writer 'in Arabic'. The prize comes, as the summary has it, from the Swedish Academy, and the pages of the Nobel Prize website are cited for the summary, the press release and an article on the writer {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-summary} {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-press-release} {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article}.

## What the Academy honoured
The summary says the Swedish Academy honoured him for forming an Arabian narrative art that applies to all humanity. The phrase is the summary's wording of the prize announcement, which the museum has not opened. It may be set beside what other pages show, that the Arabic novel had begun about seventy-five years earlier with [[concept/bidayat-al-riwaya-al-arabiyya-zaynab|Zaynab]] in 1913, a figure derived from the two dates {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-summary} {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-press-release}.

## The maturity of the Arabic novel
The summary credits Mahfouz with bringing the Arabic novel to its full maturity, moving from social realism in the Trilogy to symbolic and experimental forms. In social realism he describes life in Cairo; in the symbolic and experimental forms he tries a different narrative construction. This is what the summary says briefly, without analysing his works {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article} {cite:britannica-the-cairo-trilogy}.

## The Trilogy
For the summary the Trilogy is three novels of 1956 and 1957: Bayn al-Qasrayn, Qasr al-Shawq and al-Sukkariyya. It has a record in the museum, [[work/al-thulathiyya|The Cairo Trilogy]], and its reference in the summary is the Britannica page on The Cairo Trilogy. For the summary it is the example of social realism {cite:britannica-the-cairo-trilogy} {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article}.

## The symbolic and experimental works
Of the works of this phase the summary names Awlad Haratina in 1959 and Miramar in 1967. The museum holds a record, [[work/awlad-haratina|Awlad Haratina]], which treats the novel and what was raised around it. This page says nothing on the subject of the two novels, because the summary does not give it {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article}.

## His place among the periods
In the table of the eight periods Mahfouz stands for the modern period with the Trilogy of 1956 to 1957, beside Taha Hussein and his al-Ayyam, in the page [[concept/marahil-al-adab-al-arabi|The eight periods of Arabic literature]] {cite:britannica-naguib-mahfouz}.

## Timeline
Mahfouz was born in 1911, Zaynab appeared in 1913, the Trilogy was published in 1956 and 1957, Awlad Haratina in 1959 and Miramar in 1967; he won the Nobel Prize in 1988 and died in 2006. The difference between his birth and the prize is seventy-seven years, and between the prize and his death eighteen years, both figures computed from the years given and not from the summary. This order shows that all the works the summary names fall between 1956 and 1967, so the page does not treat what he wrote before or after, as the summary does not give it {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article} {cite:britannica-the-cairo-trilogy} {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-summary}.

## The limits of what the summary says
The summary does not explain how Mahfouz shaped his narrative art, nor quote from the Academy's announcement more than the phrase given, nor speak of the effect of the prize on readers or on the Arabic novel after 1988. All of that lies outside what the page says. The museum has two pages that complete the subject: [[concept/marahil-al-adab-al-arabi|The eight periods of Arabic literature]], to set Mahfouz among the periods, and [[concept/bidayat-al-riwaya-al-arabiyya-zaynab|The beginnings of the Arabic novel]] for what came before {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-summary} {cite:nobelprize-literature-1988-press-release}.

The page rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the museum did not open the pages named, and the founder approved relying on it as it stands on 21 November 2026. What is here is attributed to the summary, and the museum has read none of Mahfouz's works nor the prize announcement.
