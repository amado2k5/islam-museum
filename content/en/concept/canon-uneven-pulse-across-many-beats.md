## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the second chapter by dividing the uneven pulse into that uneven across many beats and that uneven within a single beat, and explains the graduated kind of the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.171-172}.

## The graduated kind
Among the pulses uneven across many beats is the graduated one that runs evenly: it starts from one beat and passes to a larger or smaller one and keeps on in that way until it reaches an extreme of decrease or of increase by a like gradation, then breaks off, returning to the first size or retreating from its smallness in a like manner or in a different one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.171-172}.

## Reaching the limit or breaking off
This graduated pulse may reach its limit, may break off short of it, or may pass beyond it. When it breaks off it may do so with a pause in its middle, or it may do the opposite, with something unexpected falling in its middle. The pulse with a pause is the uneven one in which a motion is expected and a rest comes; the one that falls in the middle is that in which a rest is expected and a motion comes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.172}.
