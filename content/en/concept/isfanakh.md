## Nature and selection
Isfanakh is spinach. The editor's footnote gives the identification and calls the word a Persian loan into Arabic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}. The book treats it as well known and gives no description {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold and moist at the end of the first degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}. It is a laxative, and its food value is better than that of purslane {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}. It has a cleansing, washing power and suppresses bile; sometimes the stomach rejects the leaf, so it is made agreeable and then eaten {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}.

## Uses by organ
Chest and lung: it helps hot chest and lung complaints, eaten or applied {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}. Joints: it helps back pains that arise from blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}. Excretory organs: it loosens the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.375}.
