## Overview
Riya is displaying one's deeds to be seen by people; among the forbidden inward acts {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The encyclopedia counts riya with malice, envy, pride and self-admiration among the forbidden heart-acts {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh counts showing off, vanity, envy, arrogance and grudges among the diseases of the heart that must be cleansed {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 01:11:00}. He holds, as his own view, that the one who calls to faith must convey it clearly and not for display, since he is a trustee of people's minds and convictions {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:44:30}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy holds that someone who tells of his good deed tells it the first time out of joy, the second time with a touch of boasting mixed in, and when he repeats it he comes to tell it in order to boast; he gives the example of the man who says he prays all night, fasts by day and gives away half his wealth. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 01:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 01:05:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh quotes the scholars that acting for people's sake is shirk and leaving an act for people's sake is showing off, giving the example of one who prays two rak'as at home in half a minute but in the mosque bows with composure and humility so that people see him, and of one who wants people to know that he prays at night, prays dawn in the mosque and gives charity; he recalls the one who hid his charity so that his left hand did not know what his right had spent. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep22 00:04:00} See: [[question/what-is-ikhlas|sincerity]].

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says the disease of sincerity is showing off (riya') and seeking repute (sum'a), together with deception and fraud. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:04:30} He cites the hadith of Jundub ibn Abd Allah al-Bajali in the two Sahihs that God will expose whoever seeks repute and show up whoever shows off, and says that one who acts so that people see his place and praise him has associated partners with God, because he did not make God his single aim. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:05:30} He cites the hadith of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari in the two Sahihs about the Bedouin who asked about a man who fights for booty, for renown, or to have his place seen; the answer was that whoever fights so that God's word is uppermost is in God's path. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:05:30}

He calls the hadith of the first by whom the Fire is lit a grave hadith, and says Muslim narrated it in his Sahih through Sulayman ibn Yasar from Abu Hurayra: they are three, a martyr, a scholar and a generous rich man, not a fornicator, a killer or a drinker, and each is told he acted so that it would be said of him, and it was said. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:21:00} He then gives al-Tirmidhi's version through Shufayy ibn Mati': he entered Medina, found people around [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] and asked him for a hadith he had heard from the Prophet and understood; Abu Hurayra began three times and each time was overcome by weeping, until the third time he nearly fell and Shufayy held him up. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:22:00} In this version the Qur'an reciter takes the place of the generous rich man. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep03 00:23:30}

He returns to Jundub's hadith in an episode on good character, as evidence that recompense is of the same kind as the deed, saying God makes the show-off heard of in a way he dislikes, and no secret he hides but God brings it out. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:30:00} Episode 10 of the archive repeats episode 3 with these same words. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep10 00:07:30}
