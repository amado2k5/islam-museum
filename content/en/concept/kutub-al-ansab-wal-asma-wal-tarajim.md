This page gathers the references a research file supplied to the museum sets out for the study of Arabic names: the classical books of genealogy, the books of names and kunyas, the biographical dictionaries, the modern academic works, and the online resources and authority files. The summary itself says its references are starting points for research, not edition-specific citations, and that each entry and page number should be checked before publication. The museum did not open the pages cited.
## The sciences of names among the early scholars
Classical scholars developed several specialised disciplines on names: genealogy, names and kunyas, epithets, biography, men of hadith, confusable names and namesakes, set out in [[concept/ilm-al-asma-wal-alqab|onomastics]]. Their importance is that old manuscripts may omit vowel marks and sometimes even the dots of letters, so matching the written name is not enough to identify the person {cite:jstor-schimmel-islamic-names}.

## The modern academic works
The summary lists seven: Annemarie Schimmel's Islamic Names: An Introduction (Edinburgh University Press, 1989), a general English-language introduction; Jacqueline Sublet's Le voile du nom: Essai sur le nom propre arabe (1991); A. F. L. Beeston's Arabic Nomenclature: A Summary Guide for Beginners (Oxford, 1971); Terrence Potter's chapter Arabic and Onomastics in The Cambridge Handbook of Arabic Linguistics (2021); the Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition (Brill, 1960 to 2009); Khayr al-Din al-Zirikli's al-A'lam [[work/alam-al-zirikli|(al-A'lam)]]; and Umar Rida Kahhala's Mu'jam al-Mu'allifin [[person/umar-rida-kahhala|(1957 to 1961)]]. It adds that al-Zirikli, Kahhala and Brockelmann are among the sources professional libraries use to establish authoritative classical Arabic names {cite:jstor-schimmel-islamic-names} {cite:persee-home} {cite:cambridge-core-home} {cite:brill-reference-works-home}.

The Onomasticon Arabicum deserves particular attention: it was developed to reconstruct historical identities from medieval biographical literature, to differentiate individuals bearing similar names and to connect people through shared biographical characteristics {cite:onomasticon-arabicum-cnrs} {cite:bnf-kitcat-arabic-names} {cite:princeton-arabic-naco-manual} {cite:loc-authorities-id-loc-gov} {cite:viaf-org} {cite:nyu-arabic-collections-online} {cite:islamic-manuscript-association}.
## The recommended core collection
The summary recommends a core of al-Sam'ani's al-Ansab, Ibn Durayd's al-Ishtiqaq, Ibn Hazm's Jamharat Ansab al-Arab, Ibn Makula's al-Ikmal, al-Zirikli's al-A'lam, Schimmel's Islamic Names and Sublet's Le voile du nom, which together span classical genealogy, linguistic etymology, disambiguation of names, biographical identification and modern academic onomastics. The open proposal on joining these references to person records is in [[concept/ilm-al-asma-wal-alqab|onomastics]] and awaits the founder's decision.
