## Summary
To explain the Moon's irregular motion, al-Battani imagines four spheres: one level with the zodiac's sphere, an inclined sphere sharing its centre, a sphere moving inside the inclined one, and the Moon's own epicycle whose centre lies on the circle of an eccentric sphere. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.76}.

## What each sphere does
The first two share a centre; the inclined sphere carries the Moon's latitude and lies about five parts from the zodiac's sphere at most. The inner sphere moves inside the inclined one. The fourth is the Moon's epicycle, whose centre travels on the eccentric sphere, and the Moon itself moves along the epicycle. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.76}.

## A note on the text
The OCR of the third sphere's name and some figures is damaged. The order given here is the one the text reads; the exact wording should be checked against Nallino's Latin rendering. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.77}.
