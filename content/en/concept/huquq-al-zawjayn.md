## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy sets out the rights of husband and wife in two lessons of his series on rights: the husband's rights over his wife in the first, and the wife's rights over her husband in the second. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:03:00}

## The husband's rights over his wife
The Sheikh says the husband's rights over his wife are several points, and that he covered six of them in the previous lesson (needs_source: the number is spoken once in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:02:00}
One of these points is that a wife should adorn herself for her husband and be ready for him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:02:30}
He recounts a saying that calls the husband "her paradise and her fire"; the transcript does not make his explanation clear (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:06:00}
He warns that a wife may forget her husband's kindness when she sees something she dislikes, and cites a saying to that effect. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:05:00}
He recounts that a wife who leaves her husband's home without his permission is cursed by the angels until she returns. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:06:30}
He says the Prophet knew from Aisha's manner of swearing whether she was pleased or angry. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:07:30}
He describes the good wife as one who pleases her husband when he looks at her, obeys when he asks, and guards herself and his property when he is away. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:09:00}
He says this does not mean the wife must be a beauty queen, an actress or a performer. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:09:30}
He cites a saying that counts loving and fertile wives among the best of women. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:13:00}
He advises that a wife should not present her husband with a list of complaints when he comes home from work, because he wants a kind word and a gentle smile. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:17:00}
He says a husband wants to see a smile on his wife's face, and recounts the story of Umm Sulaym, who hid the death of her son from her husband until he had eaten. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:16:00}
He counts it among the gravest betrayals for one spouse to reveal what passes between them (needs_source: the subject of the disclosure is unclear in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:18:30}
He says the wife must guard her children and their upbringing, and fear God in regard to her husband. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:22:00}
He says kindness to the husband is shown in kind words, service and care. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:22:30}
He advises that a wife should not admit into her husband's home anyone he dislikes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep01 00:23:00}

## The wife's rights over her husband
The Sheikh begins with the verse that says God created spouses from among ourselves so that they might find rest in each other and set affection and mercy between them. [[passage/quran-30-21|The verse on the creation of spouses, affection and mercy]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:04:00}
He treats the wife as a sign of God to be treated with respect and affection. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:04:30}
He distinguishes affection from mercy, placing affection in the days of engagement, when each spouse adorns themselves for the other. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:06:00}
He refers to the verse that describes spouses as garments for each other, and explains the garment as closeness and intimacy. [[passage/quran-2-187|The verse describing spouses as garments for each other]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:08:00}
He recounts that the Prophet drank from the place where Aisha had drunk, and took food from the place she had bitten. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:05:30}
He says the Prophet played with his family and raced them, and helped in the work of the house. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:13:30}
He explains the verse that likens the wife to a tilled field: as a farmer tends his land with good seed, water and care, so the husband tends his wife. [[passage/quran-2-223|The verse that likens the wife to a tilled field]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:10:30}
He names good companionship as the first general right and says that among its requirements is that the husband not overlook his wife's feelings. [[concept/al-muasharah-bil-maruf|Living with a spouse in kindness]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:17:00}
He holds that a wife is not obliged to serve her husband's mother, and that she should treat her well. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:18:00}
He warns against suspicion and spying on the wife, and cites Jacob's fear for his son Joseph. [[passage/quran-12-13|The verse on Jacob's fear for his son]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:19:30}
He recounts that when the Prophet returned from a journey he did not come to his household at night without warning. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:20:00}
He warns against a husband belittling his wife in front of her family. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:22:30}
He recounts that Aisha, in jealousy, broke a dish of food, and that the Prophet rebuked her gently. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:23:00}
He cites the verse on the Prophet's gentleness and holds that correcting a wife should be done gently. [[passage/quran-3-159|The verse on the Prophet's gentleness]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:24:30}
He describes the good wife as one who guards her home and children, so that the house is orderly and the children clean. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:28:00}
He counts it among the wife's rights that her husband teach her her religion, and that she hear from scholars and learn. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep02 00:45:00}

## From the lecture series Al Naba Al Safi: maintenance and justice between spouses
He describes a husband who spends on his own pleasures and withholds from his wife and children, and says that the outcome is resentment and injustice. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:27:30}
He cites a sacred saying on God forbidding injustice to Himself, and says that marriage becomes obligatory or forbidden depending on the situation of each spouse. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:28:00}
He describes a husband who wrongs his wife, and a wife who seeks divorce after years of delay in court, and calls the husband's failure to take responsibility a calamity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:28:30}

## Kind words before family and children
The Sheikh criticises a wife who belittles her husband before their children, and says that she falls short in her responsibility and that her daughters will copy her. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:11:00}
He cites a verse saying that God created spouses so that each might find rest in the other and set affection and mercy between them, and counts these three as the supports of a Muslim home (needs_source: the sura is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:33:30}

## Patience and mutual support in hardship
The Sheikh says that spouses should build a store of goodwill to draw on in times of crisis, and he criticises a spouse who waits for the other's mistake. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:34:30}
He says that a wife can improve her marriage by improving her relationship with God, and that the same holds for a husband. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep10 00:39:00}
A woman asks about a husband who is heavily pressed and fears for their time and the household's money; the Sheikh tells her not to despair and not to grieve, but to trust God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:41:00}
He asks husbands and wives to ask what the Prophet would have done, rather than react harshly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:41:00}

## Secrets and spying between spouses
He recounts a hadith that one of the gravest betrayals is for a spouse to pass on the private matters of the other, and says this applies to husbands as much as to wives. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:22:00}
He advises that a husband should guard his wife's secrets and honour, and be cheerful at home rather than frowning because of his work, and he says that the Prophet was kind to his family. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:21:30}
He cites the Qur'anic prohibition of spying and a saying of the scholars that when doubt arises one should not investigate (needs_source: the sura is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:38:30}
He mentions a hadith on the disclosure of a secret between spouses, and gives the wives of Noah and Lot as examples of disclosing a secret (needs_source: the hadith is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:08:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh holds that some sons go to extremes after marriage: either they 'marry the wife and divorce father and mother', or they require their wife to kiss their parents' hands and heads as they do; he holds that she is not their daughter and serving them is not her duty, and that the matter should rest on love. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:04:00} He holds that putting parents in one scale and wife in the other is as wrong as adding pens to watches, because each has its own kind of rights: the wife living together in kindness, the parents companionship in kindness, so each is given his due; and he jokes about the man who calls his mother 'living and provided for' and his wife 'a living snake'. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep23 00:06:30}
In the final episode the Sheikh hopes to see the wife welcome her husband and see him off, urging him not to bring anything unlawful into the home because they can bear hunger but not the Fire, and to see the husband kiss his wife's hand or head in front of the children in acknowledgement of her effort, as a practical lesson for them; he relates that [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr]], on hearing that a man is rewarded for the mouthful or drink of water he puts in his wife's mouth, made his wife swear to drink; and he mentions that when spouses shake hands their sins fall away like leaves on a winter day (needs_source: the Abu Dharr report as the Sheikh tells it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:05:00} He hopes to see a son who does not sit before his father, walk ahead of him, or call him by his name. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep30 00:05:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme al-Munjiyat
In al-Munjiyat Omar Abdelkafy counts among the obstacles on the way to God the wife who broadcasts her husband's faults and hides his good, and belittles him before the children, and says that the husband likewise should not diminish his wife; he mentions the wife who shows no mercy and the husband who makes no allowance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:10:30}

## In Mosaad Anwar's programme 'The Prophet's Counsels to Women'
In the eighth episode of [[work/hamasat-mosaad-anwar|'Hamasat: the Prophet's counsels to women']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] takes up the husband's right over his wife. He opens with a hadith he attributes to [[person/ibn-majah|Ibn Majah]] and [[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|al-Hakim]], graded sound, he says, by [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]], meaning that a woman does not fulfil her Lord's right until she fulfils her husband's, and he sees in it the greatness of that right. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:01:30} He counts obedience to the husband in what is right, and good companionship with him, among the greatest causes of a woman's winning Paradise. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:02:00} He cites the hadith in which the Prophet was asked which women are best, attributing it to [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]], [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] and al-Hakim: the answer is the one who pleases her husband when he looks at her, obeys him when he asks, and does not go against him in herself or his wealth in a way he dislikes. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:02:00} He cites a hadith he attributes to [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|al-Tirmidhi]] from [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]], meaning that if anyone were to be commanded to prostrate to another, the wife would be commanded to prostrate to her husband, and the hadith of the aunt of al-Husayn ibn Mihsan, whom the Prophet asked about her standing with her husband before telling her that he was her Paradise and her Fire. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:03:30}

He then lists the rights. The first is obedience in what is right; if the husband orders a sin he is not to be obeyed, for example if he orders her to remove her hijab or to give up prayer. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:04:00} The second is that she does not refuse him intimacy without an excuse, and he cites a hadith he calls agreed upon, on the angels cursing a wife who refuses her husband's bed until morning. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:05:00} He explains the passage of the Farewell Sermon saying that husbands made their wives lawful by God's word: a contract on the Book of God and the Sunna of His Messenger, with a dower, two witnesses and public announcement, is what turns the forbidden into the lawful. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:06:30} Another of the husband's rights, in his account, is that she adorn herself for him. He cites the hadith that a man's best treasure is a righteous wife who pleases him when he looks at her, guards him in his absence, obeys him when he asks, and fulfils his oath when he swears by her, and he notes that in this hadith the Prophet did not describe her by night prayer or daytime fasting. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:07:30} He advises a wife to know when her husband comes home and to prepare an hour before: to wash, put on her best clothes, wear perfume, do her hair, and meet him at the door with a smile and a kind word. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:08:00} He pokes fun at the wife who greets her husband in kitchen clothes with her hair tied up, and at the one who after the honeymoon turns from "a chirping sparrow" into "a roaring lion". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:09:00} He holds that at home a wife may wear for her husband whatever lawful fashion she likes, and that adorning herself for him before the mirror is an act of worship. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:10:30} The husband's rights also include kind companionship and not denying his favour. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:11:30} He states that after marriage the person with the greatest right over a woman is her husband, and over a man his mother, and he advises the wife to give what she owes before asking for what she is owed, since kindness draws kindness. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep08 00:12:30}

In the tenth episode he returns to ingratitude towards the husband through a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari and Muslim from [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]]: the Prophet went out to the prayer-ground on an Eid and told the women to give charity, for he had seen that they were most of the people of the Fire, because they cursed much and were ungrateful to their husbands; the doubt over which of the two Eids it was, he says, is Abu Sa'id's. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep10 00:01:00} He makes not being ungrateful to the husband the third counsel of this hadith, and cites a hadith he attributes to al-Hakim, meaning that God does not look at a woman who does not thank her husband though she cannot do without him. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep10 00:07:30} He tells how the wife of the caliph [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|al-Ma'mun]] saw peasant women wading barefoot in mud and wished to do the same; he had mud kneaded with the finest perfumes for her and she waded in it; later, in a quarrel, she told him she had never seen any good from him, and he asked: "not even on the day of the mud?" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep10 00:08:30} He tells how [[person/zubayda-bint-jafar|Zubayda]] asked [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]] for thirty thousand dinars and he gave them, and before he had risen from his seat she told him she had never seen any good from him; he explains that a dinar is a gold pound. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep10 00:10:00} He cites the hadith that whoever does not thank people does not thank God, and links thanks to the husband with the thanks to parents commanded in the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep10 00:10:30}

In the eleventh episode he speaks of turning a wife against her husband. He cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari and Ahmad from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], disowning whoever corrupts a slave against his master or a wife against her husband, and mentions a version using the verb khabbaba, meaning to corrupt. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:01:00} He holds that Islam has put obstacles before every cause of broken homes and that whoever wrecks homes follows [[person/iblis|Iblis]]; he repeats a hadith he attributes to Muslim in which Iblis crowns the one of his troops who separated a man from his wife, and infers from it Satan's persistence. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:02:30} He says many homes have been wrecked by foolish mothers who urge a daughter to seek divorce with promises of a better husband, leaving her with no suitor, and likewise by the husband's mother who urges her son to divorce his wife, so that the children are lost and he regrets it. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:05:00} He warns of envious women who hint, lie and backbite to wreck a happy wife's home, sometimes out of desire for her husband, and cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari forbidding a woman to seek her sister's divorce in order to take her place. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:06:00} He narrates a hadith he attributes to Muslim: the Prophet came to the house of [[person/fatima|Fatima]] and did not find [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]]; she said he had quarrelled with her and gone out; the Prophet found him asleep in the mosque, covered in dust, brushed the dust off him calling him by the kunya Abu Turab, and brought him home. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:06:30} He praises the Prophet as the best of fathers-in-law, since he neither reproached Ali nor asked Fatima what had happened, and draws from this the etiquette that the family should not intervene so long as the daughter has not complained. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:08:00} He narrates that [[person/asma-bint-abi-bakr|Asma']] complained to her father [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] that [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]] had struck her; he told her to be patient, since al-Zubayr was a righteous man to whom the Prophet had given glad tidings of Paradise, then went and made peace between them; and he advises a father whose daughter complains to hear both sides first. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:09:30} He observes that every mother wants to run her daughter's home as she sees fit, and every father his son's, although the son is now a grown man responsible for his family and times have changed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep11 00:10:30}
