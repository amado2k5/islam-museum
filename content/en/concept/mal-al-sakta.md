## Whom it befalls
Apoplexy mostly befalls those of moist constitutions, moist bodies and moist regimens — especially when, with the moistness, one of hot or dry temperament is struck, for then the affair is hard, since a disease opposed to the temperament only occurs for a great cause; the temperament may be far from it and not susceptible to it, and apoplexy seldom occurs of heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132}

## Between it and palsy
If the matter of palsy spreads in the two sides it makes apoplexy, just as if the matter of apoplexy contracts to one side it makes palsy; most causes of apoplexy lie in the two rear ventricles, and if there is fever with apoplexy there is a swelling in most cases; those who need much venesection for the blackness of their fluid and profit by it lose in the sequel and fall into apoplexy and its like {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132}

## Hippocrates' warning and predisposition
Hippocrates said: whoever is healthy and is seized by a sudden pain in his head and then struck dumb dies before the seventh day, unless a fever comes over him and gives hope — that is, with fever it is hoped the excess will dissolve. Taking sharp drugs hastens and hurries the sluggish humours, and the book records the warning of impending apoplexy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132}

## Fever with it
If the apoplectic is fevered, his affair is awaited until it is disclosed — it may be a crisis — and the term is seventy-two hours; if it is not that, but a fever for a swelling and sepsis, it is deadly. Know that apoplexy and palsy narrow the passages to them, so that the evacuating drugs hardly evacuate from the matter that causes them in particular {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.134}
