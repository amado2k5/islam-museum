## Summary
Ibn Sina counts autumn the most diseased of the seasons, gives the reasons, lists its diseases and the corruption of the humours in it, and says which autumn is best {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.120-121}.

## Its causes
Autumn has many diseases because people go back and forth in a hot sun and then go home to the cold, because of the abundance of fruit and the corruption of the humours it causes, and because the strength was loosened in summer. Humours spoil in it from bad food and from the fine part dissolving while the coarse remains and burns; and whenever nature stirs a humour to drive it out, the cold sends it back into confinement {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.

## The humours
Blood is very scant in autumn, for its temperament is opposed to blood and does not help to make it. Yellow bile is abundant as a remnant of summer, and black bile too, since summer turns humours to ash and autumn chills them. The start of autumn suits the old fairly well, and its end harms them badly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.120-121}.

## Diseases
Its diseases include scaly scab, the skin eruptions called qawabi, cancers, joint pains, mixed fevers and quartan fever; the spleen enlarges; there is dribbling and difficulty of urine, slipping of the bowels, sciatica, a bilious stinging quinsy (in spring it is phlegmatic), dry ileus, apoplexy and lung diseases, pains of the back and thighs, worms in the belly, smallpox, especially in a dry autumn after a hot summer, and madness, from the badness of the bilious humours mixed with black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.121}.

## Who is harmed
Autumn is the worst season for those with ulcers of the lung, the consumptives, and it brings their state to light if its signs had not yet shown; it is among the worst for those with simple hectic fever, because of its drying. It is like a guarantor of the leftovers of summer's diseases. The best autumn is the moistest and rainy, and the dry one is the worst {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.121}.
