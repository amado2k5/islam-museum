## Nature
The roots of perforated cane, said to burn because their tips rub together when the winds blow through them; this happens in the land of India. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Temperament
Cold in the second degree and dry in the third. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Actions and properties
It has astringency and expulsion, and a little dissolving power. Its cooling is stronger, and its dissolving comes from a small bitterness in it; through its dissolving and astringency its drying grows strong. Its powers are mixed, like rose. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Organs of the head
It helps thrush, and it helps the state the book calls tawahhush, a dread or melancholy. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Organs of the eye
It helps hot swellings of the eye. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Organs of the chest
It strengthens the heart and helps hot palpitation. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Organs of digestion
It helps thirst, vomiting and inflammation of the stomach and its weakness, and it prevents yellow bile from pouring into the stomach. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Excretory organs
It prevents yellow humour. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}

## Fevers
It prevents acute fevers. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.498}
