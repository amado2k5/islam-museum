## Definition
A letter's sifa is the manner its sound carries when pronounced; it is what distinguishes letters that share a makhraj or approach one — "when two letters agree in sifat and makhraj they are the identical pair", as the Jazariyya puts it {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## The paired qualities
The Jazariyya enumerates the qualities by their opposites {cite:ar-wikisource}: jahr (voiced) against hams (whispered), the hams letters gathered in «فحثه شخص سكت»; shidda against rakhawa with a middle course between them — the shidda letters «لفظ أجد قت بكت» and the middle letters «لن عمر»; isti'la (raising of the tongue) against istifal, the seven isti'la letters «خص ضغط قظ»; and itbaq (the tongue pressed to the palate) against infitah — the sad, dad, ta and za, which the same matn calls the strongest letters to be made heavy {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## The qualities without opposites
Among the qualities are those with no opposite {cite:ar-wikisource}: the safir (whistle) of the sad, zay and sin; the qalqala (echo) of «قطب جد»; the lin (softness) of the still waw and ya preceded by an open vowel; the inhiraf of the lam and the ra; the takrir of the ra; the tafashshi of the shin; and the istitala of the dad.
