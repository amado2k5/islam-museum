## Summary
In the second chapter Ibn Sina advises giving the old person food in small portions, in two or three meals according to digestion and strength, with well-made bread and honey at the third hour, warns against thick foods that breed black bile and phlegm and against sharp, pungent, drying ones, and describes how mistakes in these are treated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.251-252}.

## Dividing the meals
The old person's food is to be divided little by little and given in two or three rounds according to the strength or weakness of digestion. At the third hour he eats well-made bread with honey; at the seventh, after the bath, something that loosens the bowels, of the kind the author describes; and near nightfall a wholesome food, a little more of it if he is strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.251}.

## What they avoid
They should avoid every thick food that engenders black bile and phlegm, and every sharp, pungent, drying one such as relishes and spices, except as medicine. To the first kind belong salted food, aubergine, dried meat, game, firm-fleshed fish, melon and cucumber; the second kind includes relishes, the pickle made of small salted fish, and milk taken wrongly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.251}.

## Correcting a mistake
If the old do what is not fitting in these matters, they are treated by taking the opposite. Attenuating agents are to be used on them only when it is known that they hold superfluities; once cleansed they are fed on moistening foods, and now and then are given some attenuating things with the meal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.251}.
