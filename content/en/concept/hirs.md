## Summary
In the Sheikh's account, greed is the craving for money and the world. He gives the image of one who drinks sea water and grows thirstier, and holds that wishes never end (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:26:30}

## Needs that never end
He lists needs a person seeks that never end, such as work, marriage, a house and a car, each followed by another (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:26:00}

## The one who drinks sea water
The Sheikh says a person keeps drinking sea water until he is swollen with dropsy and dies, and the drinking only makes him thirstier. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:26:30}

## Large figures
He criticises preoccupation with billions, saying a large number buys nothing great and that a billion does not buy a loaf of bread (his view, needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:27:00}
He gives the example of a student who flies every day to a university in a neighbouring country, which he presents as excess (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:27:30}

## From the series Al Naba Al Safi: desire and servitude to God
The speaker cites a hadith about people who become slaves of the dinar and the dirham, and a Qur'an passage about God's chosen servants; he says the believer serves God alone {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep05 00:23:30}. He cites a verse about one who takes his own desire as his god, and says people follow their desires {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:14:00}. He suggests that a believer should test whether a desire is the Prophet's way, for example when he wants to sleep rather than pray at night {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:15:30}. He cites a verse that whoever fears the Standing before God and restrains the self from desire, paradise is his refuge {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:17:30}.

He says scholars list four signs of contentment: anger restrained by forbearance, poverty restrained by patience, wealth restrained by gratitude, and deeds restrained by sincerity {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:02:30}. He says envy is refusing God's allotment: the believer admires what others have and asks God for the same, while the hypocrite envies {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:25:00}. He says the soul desires revenge, transgression and the taking of rights, and that the eighth demand is the angel of death taking the soul {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:34:30}.

## Wealth as a loan, and the world as a deposit
He says scholars called the world a loan to be returned, like a borrowed book, and that God lends us lives, wealth and health {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:07:30}. He cites a hadith on what remains of a person's wealth: what he eats, wears or gives in charity {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:03:00}. He cites a verse that God bought from believers their selves and wealth in exchange for paradise, and says this is a serious question of ownership {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:13:00}.

He says wealth is lent and returned, passes between people, and that those who respect a person only for his money are not respecting him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:13:00}. He cites a hadith that the best of people is the best to his family, and says gratitude for children, a wife, wealth and work brings more {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:22:00}. He says that health and wealth must be spent in what pleases God, and that one will be asked where wealth came from and how it was spent {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:16:30}. He says money gained wrongly must be purified by returning it or giving it away, and the penitent is a new page with God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:25:30}.

## Gain and the worth of wealth
He says the third lesson of Hatim concerns people who gather wealth by any means, lawful or unlawful {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:18:00}. He says people with rank should use it to help others, since the best people are those most useful to others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:36:30}. He says the fourth axis is spending wealth on the poor, which is their due and set by a known measure (the last clause is unclear in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:45:00}.

He says Qarun claimed his wealth came from his own knowledge when Moses asked for zakat, and that this forgetting of God's favour led to his ruin {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:17:30}. He describes a rich youth and a poor youth boasting of their fathers, and says such pride is foolish {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:18:30}. He quotes a saying attributed to a companion, whose name is unclear in the recording, that people incline to the wealthy, and that a person who loses wealth loses respect, which shows that respect was for wealth (needs_source: the saying is not sourced in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:12:00}. He criticises people who tell stories of their wealthy grandfathers and grieve over losing it, and says they should not dwell on the past {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:18:00}. He recounts Umar's supplication that he not be held to account in the grave for wealth his heirs enjoy, and says an unjust will punishes its writer (needs_source: the supplication is not sourced in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:28:00}.

## True richness and contentment
He tells that Ibn Umm Maktum was blind and poor, yet God rebuked the Prophet on his behalf, and says a poor man in money can be rich in spirit (garbled: part of the passage is unclear in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:11:30}. He says one life is worth more than wealth: wealth exists for the human, not the human for wealth, and asking help when necessary is lawful {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:42:00}. He says a person is never satisfied by the world; the greedy drink more and thirst more, like seawater, and blessing comes from God, so one should spend on others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:11:30}.
