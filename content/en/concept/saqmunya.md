## Nature
The book reports from Dioscorides a plant with three large branches from one root, oily and downy, its leaf like that of ivy but softer and three-cornered, a white round hollow flower like a basket in shape and heavy in scent, and a long thick root as great as the forearm, white and full of milk, the milk being taken from the head of the root. The best is clear, light and soft, and it should not be judged only by whiteness of colour, because that is also so when it is adulterated with spurge milk and bitter-vetch flour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Selection
The best is blue turning to white as if the break of a shell, friable and quick to dissolve in water until it makes it like milk; the jirmaqani kind is poor, and the worst kinds are those from al-Sham and Palestine, which are adulterated with spurge milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the third degree, its heat greater than its dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Actions and properties
It has cleansing and dissolving power, and is an enemy above all of the stomach and the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Cosmetics
It clears vitiligo, leprosy-like patches and freckles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Laid on wounds it dissolves them, and with vinegar it is smeared on ulcerating scab {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Joints
It helps pains of the joints and the hip as a poultice, and sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Organs of the head
The root and its juice are used on chronic headache, with vinegar and rose oil, and mixed with them and placed on the head of one with chronic headache it is said to heal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Organs of the chest
It is among the things that harm the heart {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It harms the stomach and liver greatly, and the book names ways of breaking its force; it distresses and sickens, takes away the appetite for food and causes thirst {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Excretory organs
It purges bile strongly and differs from country to country; the book says the physician must consider the patient's strength, the strength of the chief organs and the air of the place; it harms the intestines, and the root of the plant purges bile and phlegm. The book warns that excess of it is deadly; the measures and weights it gives are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Poisons and bites
It helps against a scorpion's sting, drunk and smeared on the limb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that, carried in a piece of wool, it kills the foetus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.594}.
