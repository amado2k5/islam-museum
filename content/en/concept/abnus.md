## Nature and selection
Wood from a tree brought from the land of the Zanj; according to Dioscorides it is brought from Abyssinia. It is purely black, without layers, and in smoothness resembles a horn that has been shaped; when broken its break is dense and stings the tongue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot and dry in the second degree; some claim that with its heat it cools the heat of the blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}.

## Uses by organ
Selection: the best is the black, even kind without lines, which when set on live coals gives off a pleasant scent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Properties: when rubbed in water it abrades like many stones; it is thinning and clearing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Eyes: it clears films and whiteness; a salve is made from its scrapings, and when its dust is burned and washed, it helps long-standing ulcers of the eye, dry ophthalmia and discharge of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Excretory organs: a source the book names as al-Khuz says it breaks kidney stone; it is also said to resolve wind in the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}.
