## Summary
Ibn Sina gives the fourth chapter of the nerves group to the nerves of the thoracic vertebrae: the first leaves between the first and second vertebrae and travels with the eighth cervical nerve to the hand, and the rest go to the muscles of the shoulder, back, ribs and abdomen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.82-83}.

## First and second pairs
The first pair leaves between the first and second thoracic vertebrae and divides in two: the larger part spreads in the rib and back muscles, the other runs over the first ribs, joins the eighth cervical nerve, and the two go on together to the arms until they reach forearm and palm. The second pair leaves by the next hole; one part goes to the outer upper arm and gives it sensation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.82}.

## The other pairs
The rest of the second pair, with the remaining pairs, gather and head for the shoulder muscles that move its joint and for the back muscles. Branches growing from the thoracic vertebrae that do not reach the shoulder go to the back muscles and to the muscles between the short ribs outside the chest; those growing from the vertebrae of the chest's own ribs go to the intercostal muscles and the abdominal muscles. Pulsing and still vessels run with the branches of these nerves and enter by their exits to the spinal cord {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.82-83}.
