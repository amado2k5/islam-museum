## Overview
For Ibn Rajab, good character (*husn al-khuluq*) is treating people in ways that please them, in word and deed, while withholding harm from them. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 391}

## In the counsel "Fear God wherever you are"
It is the third part of that counsel, whose text reads: "and treat people with good character." It joins [[concept/taqwa|piety]] with the right of God and the rights of His servants. Ibn Rajab holds that combining the two is rare, and that only the perfected among the prophets and the truthful can bear it. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 427}

## Its aim
He says that its aim, as God described the pious, is to give generously and to bear harm. He cites hadiths, among them: "The most complete of believers in faith are the best of them in character." He notes that it is among the heaviest things placed in the scale. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 391}

## Definitions
He relates from Imam Ahmad that good character is not becoming angry, and in another report, bearing with what comes from people. From Ishaq ibn Rahawayh: openness of countenance and not becoming angry. He relates from some scholars: swallowing anger for God's sake, showing cheerfulness, and pardoning those who slip, except for discipline or the application of a legal penalty. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 428-430}

See [[concept/adab|good manners]].

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh says that a person is known by his character: his neighbours at home, his companions on journeys and those he deals with in the markets are where it is seen {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:05:00}. He says that the word for travel (safar) derives from unveiling (isfar), and that character shows on journeys (needs_source: the derivation) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 00:06:00}.

The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that separating economics, art, poetry and literature from religion has produced discourtesy, deviation in conduct and backbiting {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep05 00:52:00}, and that the nation has lost its identity in its character and conduct {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 01:11:30}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] says good character is the navel of the virtues, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:02:00} and explains how its owner reaches the rank of one who fasts and prays at night through the scales of the Day of Judgement: the person of good character has no adversaries, so his good deeds stay in his scale, while the person of bad character is the bankrupt who comes with prayer, fasting and zakat but has insulted one and struck another, so his good deeds are taken and their sins thrown on him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep01 00:03:00} He says the first to suffer from a man's bad character is the man himself, then his wife, because she knows his inner life, then his servants, even his beast. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:13:00}

He says the Prophet won people's hearts by good character, and that when it was proposed to him to kill [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul]], the head of hypocrisy, he refused, giving as his reason what people would say if he killed him, and naming himself in his words by his own name. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:14:00} He points to two fine points in this wording: that the Prophet called himself by his name, and that he said "his companions" in the plural when the man to be killed was one. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:15:30} He explains the first with the hadith of the Makhzumi woman who stole, when Quraysh sent [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]] to intercede for her and the Prophet swore he would carry out the penalty even on his daughter Fatima, naming her and himself by name; al-Heweny sees in the repeated name that in the hudud the ruler is like anyone else. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:18:30} He says the naming in Ibn Ubayy's case was aimed at distant Bedouins who had not tasted the manners of prophethood, and that news swells as it travels until it would be said he had killed all his companions, so the known hypocrite alive was less dangerous than the harm of killing him; this is his understanding. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep14 00:22:30}
