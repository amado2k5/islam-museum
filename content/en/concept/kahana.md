## Summary
The people of the Jahiliyya believed the reports of soothsayers, seers and astrologers {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:mirqat-al-mafatih 2/2-3}.

## Soothsayer, seer and astrologer
A kahin (soothsayer) is one who undertakes to report future events and claims to know secrets. Some soothsayers claim to have a jinn companion who casts news to them. Others claim to perceive the unseen through an understanding given to them. Others claim to know things through premises and causes, by which they infer where things are from the speech, act or state of the one asking them. This last kind is called an 'arraf, such as one who claims to know what was stolen, where the theft took place, where a lost animal is, and the like {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:mirqat-al-mafatih 2/2-3}. A munajjim (astrologer) is one who looks at the stars, meaning the heavenly bodies, and calculates their courses and times, in order to know by them the conditions of the world and the events that will happen in the future {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38}.

## Astrology and the anwa'
The author holds that believing the reports of astrologers is, in reality, faith in the stars. Among their faith in the stars was faith in the [[concept/anwa|anwa']] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:sharh-sahih-muslim-al-nawawi 1/59}.

## Not covered here
The hadith forbidding going to soothsayers and the rulings on soothsaying in jurisprudence will be presented under their own entries. The mentions of soothsayers in the sira (the soothsayer of Quraysh, Shiq and Satih) will be presented in their places.

## For young visitors
The soothsayers of the Jahiliyya claimed to know the unseen through jinn and stars. Islam forbade asking them and believing them.

## The speaker's view: fortune-tellers in the media
The speaker describes fortune-tellers who buy advertising, with sums in the millions, to mislead people (needs_source: the figure of millions is not sourced from the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:03:30} He holds that channels of astrology and fortune-telling should be shut because of the harm they do, that Muslims should not engage with them, and that the world advances in research while Muslims lag behind. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:05:30}

## On believing the soothsayer
The speaker recalls a hadith about the one who goes to a soothsayer and believes him, and counts cup-reading, palm-reading and the reading of horoscopes as belonging to this category; he stresses the severity of the warning. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:04:30} He recalls another hadith about the one who goes to a soothsayer, and says that his prayer is not accepted for forty nights (needs_source: the number and the source of the hadith were not stated in the passage). He says that the answers of such people are mostly false, and warns of what it costs to seek them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:16:30}

## The speaker's view: why people turn to them
The speaker says that weak knowledge, weak religious upbringing and absence from mosques push people towards such figures, who cost them this world and the next; the real remedy, he says, is taqwa and righteousness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:28:00}

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] cites the hadith on whoever goes to a diviner, asks him and believes him, the hadith that whoever takes knowledge from the stars takes a branch of sorcery, and the hadith of the word a jinn snatches, which his ally mixes with a hundred lies. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:11:00} He distinguishes astrology, the claim that the zodiac governs the temperament of those born under it, which he calls a superstition, from the study of astronomy, which Islam favours, arguing that the sun, the nearest star, is 150 million kilometres away and that the shapes of the constellations appear only from the earth. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:08:30} He criticises the horoscope pages of Muslim newspapers, cup-reading, card-reading and astrologers' advertisements in the media. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep16 00:11:30}
