## Summary
The people of the Jahiliyya believed the reports of soothsayers, seers and astrologers {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:mirqat-al-mafatih 2/2-3}.

## Soothsayer, seer and astrologer
A kahin (soothsayer) is one who undertakes to report future events and claims to know secrets. Some soothsayers claim to have a jinn companion who casts news to them. Others claim to perceive the unseen through an understanding given to them. Others claim to know things through premises and causes, by which they infer where things are from the speech, act or state of the one asking them. This last kind is called an 'arraf, such as one who claims to know what was stolen, where the theft took place, where a lost animal is, and the like {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:mirqat-al-mafatih 2/2-3}. A munajjim (astrologer) is one who looks at the stars, meaning the heavenly bodies, and calculates their courses and times, in order to know by them the conditions of the world and the events that will happen in the future {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38}.

## Astrology and the anwa'
The author holds that believing the reports of astrologers is, in reality, faith in the stars. Among their faith in the stars was faith in the [[concept/anwa|anwa']] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.38} {cite:sharh-sahih-muslim-al-nawawi 1/59}.

## Not covered here
The hadith forbidding going to soothsayers and the rulings on soothsaying in jurisprudence will be presented under their own entries. The mentions of soothsayers in the sira (the soothsayer of Quraysh, Shiq and Satih) will be presented in their places.

## For young visitors
The soothsayers of the Jahiliyya claimed to know the unseen through jinn and stars. Islam forbade asking them and believing them.
