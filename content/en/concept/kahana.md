## Overview
Kahana (soothsaying), 'irafa (augury) and telling of hidden events: the Jahiliyya's ways of claiming knowledge of the unseen; Islam annulled them and forbade going to their practitioners.

## The story
The Jahiliyya practiced kinds of unseen-reading: the kahin claims a familiar of the jinn brings him news; the 'arraf claims to know things by signs; the stone-caster turns pebbles and tells of happenings {cite:mirqat-al-mafatih}.

The Prophet forbade going to soothsayers and augurs and believing them, and counted believing that stars cause rain a kind of unbelief in God, in the anwa' hadith {cite:sharh-sahih-muslim-al-nawawi}.

## For young visitors
The Jahiliyya's soothsayers claimed to know the unseen through jinn and stars; Islam forbade asking or believing them.
