## Summary
Ibn Sina says glacier and snow waters are coarse, and standing waters of thickets and marshes, especially open ones, are bad and heavy. They turn cold in winter because of the snows and breed phlegm, and turn warm in summer because of the sun and putrefaction and breed bitterness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.137}.

## Symptoms
He says that those who drink them suffer enlarged spleens, their bowels are held and vomiting is hard, their limbs, shoulders and necks waste, and they may fall into dropsy and pleurisy; madness, haemorrhoids and varicose veins are frequent among them. Their women find conception and childbirth hard, their boys suffer hernia of the scrotum and their elders varicose veins and leg ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.137}.

## Standing water and water drawn from a spring
He adds that standing waters of whatever kind do not agree with the stomach, and that water scooped from a spring is judged much like standing water, though better since it has not stayed long in one place. It does not suit those with fevers or those dominated by bile, but suits ailments needing astringency or ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.137}.
