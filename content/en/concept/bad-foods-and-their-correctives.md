## Summary
Ibn Sina says foods that breed wateriness, thick humour and bile bring fevers; bitter greens may help in winter as bland ones may in summer; one driven to bad foods should take them seldom and mix in their opposites, answering sweet with sour and sour with honey, and the harm of fat with astringents, bitter, salty and pungent things {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.234-235}.

## What they bring
Foods that generate wateriness, thick viscid humour and bilious humour bring fevers, the watery part rotting the blood, the viscid and thick part blocking the channels, and the bilious part heating the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.234}.

## Winter and summer
Bitter greens may be of much use in winter, as bland ones may in summer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.234}.

## One driven to bad food
One who comes to take bad foods should reduce the times, not take them in succession, and mix with them their opposites. If he is hurt by the sweet he drinks over it something sour, such as vinegar, pomegranate, oxymel of vinegar or quince, and keeps up evacuation; if hurt by the sour he takes honey and old drink, before the food turns raw and before digestion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.234}.

## Harm of fat
The harm of fat is likewise made good with astringents such as chestnut, myrtle berry, Syrian carob, jujube and hawthorn, with bitter things such as bitter ginger, and with salty and pungent ones such as condiments, garlic and onion, and the reverse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.234-235}.
