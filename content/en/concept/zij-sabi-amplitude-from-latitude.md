## Summary
At the end of chapter 7 al-Battani teaches how to find the distance of the rising and setting of any degree from the rising and setting of the first of Aries on the horizon circle when the latitude is given: the latitude is taken from ninety, leaving the altitude of the first of Aries, whose chord is found; then the declination of the wanted degree and its chord are taken, multiplied by the radius and divided by the chord of the altitude of the first of Aries, and the arc of the result is the distance. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.34}.

## How the two methods differ
In this method al-Battani uses the place's latitude where the previous passage used the longest day; he does not need the half day-arc, and works from the altitude of the first of Aries, which is the complement of the latitude, the Sun's noon altitude at the equinox in that place. He says the result is the distance of the degree's rising and setting from the rising and setting of the first of Aries on the horizon circle toward the side of the declination, which is the bearing. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.34}.
