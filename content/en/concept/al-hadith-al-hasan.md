## Summary
For [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] the hasan forms the third and fourth kinds of accepted report: if a report falls short of the highest qualities of acceptance and nothing makes up for it, it is good in itself; if an indication tips toward acceptance a report on which judgement is suspended, it is good through another. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 53} The editor, [[person/nur-al-din-itr|Nur al-Din 'Itr]], counts this distinction among the choices of Ibn Hajar on which hadith scholars settled. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 21}

## Good in itself
If accuracy is lighter — that is, less — while the other conditions of the definition of [[concept/al-hadith-al-sahih|the sound report]] hold, the report is good in itself, not through anything outside it; the requirement of the remaining qualities excludes the weak report. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 60} It shares with the sound report in serving as proof, though below it, and like it is divided into ranks. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 60} With multiple chains it is declared sound, becoming sound through another. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 60-61}

## Good through another
This is the report whose goodness comes from support, such as the report of an obscure (mastur) narrator when its chains are multiplied. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 60}

## "Hasan sahih"
Ibn Hajar answers those puzzled that [[person/al-tirmidhi-muhammad-ibn-isa|al-Tirmidhi]] and others combine the two descriptions, though the good falls short of the sound: {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 61}
- When a narrator is alone in relating it, the combination expresses the critic's hesitation over the narrator — whether he meets the conditions of soundness or falls short — as if he said "good or sound", the word of hesitation dropped; on this reading "hasan sahih" ranks below "sahih", since certainty is stronger than hesitation. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 61-62}
- When it is not singular, the two descriptions refer to two chains, one sound and one good; on this reading "hasan sahih" ranks above a singular "sahih", since multiple chains strengthen. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 62}

## Al-Tirmidhi's hasan
Some object that al-Tirmidhi made it a condition of the hasan that it be related through more than one route, yet calls some reports "hasan gharib, known to us only from this route"; Ibn Hajar answers that al-Tirmidhi did not define the hasan in general but only one kind of it, what he calls simply "hasan", citing his words at the end of his book: what we call a good report we mean that its chain is good in our view — every report related whose narrator is not suspected of lying, which is related through more than one route in similar form, and which is not anomalous. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 62-63} He holds that al-Tirmidhi added "in our view" because it was a new term or an obscure one, and did not attribute it to the hadith scholars as [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|al-Khattabi]] did. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 63} The editor identifies al-Tirmidhi's hasan with the good through another. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 62}
