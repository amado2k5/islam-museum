## Nature
The book reports from Dioscorides a well-known plant whose leaf is like fennel but thicker, its stem rougher, with an umbel like dill, a long bitter or pungent fruit quick to decay, and a long root of good scent. A second kind has a leaf like that of the great ivy but smaller and elongated, heads like those of dill and a dense black seed, being more pungent, more fragrant and pleasanter in taste, and growing on high places rich in water. A third kind has a leaf like euphorbia but rougher and thicker, with a larger stem, and grows on rugged ground; some claimed it is the Roman anjudan {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Actions and properties
It dissolves, thins and disperses wind, and so do its root and seed, calming inner pains and melting hard phlegm; it is given to livestock, and the book says it increases their offspring, and drunk in wine it keeps cold from harming the eyelids {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Joints
It helps pains of the back {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Organs of the head
It helps greatly in epilepsy and dullness of the mind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Organs of the chest
It helps asthma, difficulty of breath, orthopnoea and chronic cough, especially the root and seed together, and the root kneaded with honey and licked clears the chest of sticky moistures {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It dissolves wind and calms pains of the viscera, the root digests, especially food, it is good for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Excretory organs
It dissolves windy colic, removes difficulty of urine, helps pains of the viscera and the kidneys, is useful for the kidneys as a whole, and drunk helps dribbling of urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Fevers
It is useful in phlegmatic fever, as is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists it as easing birth in all animals, dissolving pains of the womb and suffocation of the womb, and provoking the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.592}.
