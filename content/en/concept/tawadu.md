## Summary
In the Sheikh's account, humility is the first principle of taqwa: the believer does not look down on anyone, and knows his own faults before those of others. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:13:30}

## Ages of people
The Sheikh lists four principles of taqwa, the first being that a Muslim does not look down on anyone. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:08:30}
He divides people into older, younger and of the same age, and says that everyone falls into one of these groups. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:09:00}
He says that before an older person he sees himself as lesser, since they have served God for longer. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:09:30}

## Knowing one's own faults
He asks why people respect him, and says he knows his own faults with certainty while people judge him by assumption (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:13:30}
When a close relative insults a Muslim, the first thing to ask is whether his own sins invited it; the Sheikh calls this the first lesson (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:14:00}

## Three pillars
He summarises three pillars: humility with people, remembering God when heedless, and keeping company with the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:18:00}

## Sayings of scholars
He cites scholars that a Muslim should not think the enemy has won, that the one who turns away from the truth is at fault, and that God protects those who fear Him (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:14:30}
He tells of a scholar who said he was not righteous, and a shaykh who replied that people loved him; the Sheikh draws a lesson of humility from it (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:17:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh criticises the person who is humble in front of people and his colleagues at work, then enters his home and raises his voice at his family, and asks why he is hard-hearted toward the children and his mother yet soft-hearted toward the manager and the staff {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:15:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)

The Sheikh urges treating a person who is better in God's sight with respect rather than arrogance, and likewise someone younger, noting that older people usually see the young as reckless, and he replies that they are not {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:10:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 10)

The Sheikh depicts a scene of young men each boasting of his father, in which the boy sitting among them answers that he is the son of the man for whom God made the angels prostrate, meaning Adam; he says that someone with money, property and millions should not look down on God's creatures but be humble, since he comes from this earth {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:56:30}.

He then cites lines of verse about graves, in which the poet reflects that the ground he walks on is made of the remains of people, and says he asked himself among the graves where rest and safety lie, and was answered by what he saw of worms playing in the eye sockets of the dead (the Sheikh's own use of the poem to teach humility) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:57:00}.

The verses end by saying that the slave and the king are equal in the grave; the Sheikh then says everyone here is waiting for permission to go to God, and returns to his account of Musa and Harun, who were sent to Pharaoh {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:57:30}.

## The speaker's view: humility and arrogance
He hopes to see humble people, and defines arrogance as despising the opinions of others and believing oneself the cleverest and best. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:18:00}

He says that the more God's blessing grows upon a person, the more humble he should become, and he criticises those who boast of knowledge, wealth or status. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:41:30}

He compares boasting about wealth to the conduct of a wealthy uncle towards his orphaned nephews, and notes that the man of wealth forgot that God gave him what he owns. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:19:00}

He holds that arrogance toward others is a serious flaw, even in people who call themselves Muslim. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep10 00:17:00}

He criticises the arrogant person who believes he is of a different substance and a different mind from other people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:26:00}

## Humility before God and in need
He cites a Qur'anic passage that help came when the believers were in a state of weakness and humbling, and says that a sick person should be humble before God, since humility is a door through which prayers are answered. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:22:00}

He says the ignorant take the modest needy for the rich, that investigating their cases and giving to them with dignity is better, and he cites a Qur'anic verse on the modest. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:43:30}

## From the Prophet's life and the sayings of the early generations
He cites a hadith that modesty is part of faith, and that every religion has a character, the character of Islam being modesty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:32:00}

He cites a hadith that all people descend from Adam; he recounts the humility of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz when a man addressed him, and says humility raises a person in the eyes of others. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:04:00}

He says the Prophet had no fixed seat and sat wherever the gathering ended; a Bedouin visitor openly asked which of them was Muhammad. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:19:00}

He says humility is something practised rather than only spoken, and cites a saying of al-Shafi'i that he wished God would show the truth through the other person's tongue (needs_source: the saying attributed to al-Shafi'i). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:25:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh suggests keeping in mind that the people one deals with are of three kinds: one older, of whom one says he preceded me by ten years in prayer, fasting and charity and so is better than I; one younger, of whom one says I preceded him by ten years in sins and so he is better than I; and one of my age, of whom one says he does not know my sins that God has covered; he holds that it is part of God's covering that sins were given no smell. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:06:30} He holds that one who is arrogant toward people is deluded and petty, and cites that the people of Badr were given victory while they were humble. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep13 00:04:30}

## In Abdul Hamid Kishk's sermons
[[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Abdul Hamid Kishk]] makes humility before God's greatness the first of [[concept/shurut-qabul-al-salah|the conditions for the acceptance of prayer]] in the hadith qudsi he explains, and makes not lording it over creation a separate second condition. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep01 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep04 00:14:00} He explains lording it over creation as harming people, and lists the harm done by the eye, the ear, the tongue, the belly, the hand, the foot and the heart. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:14:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme al-Munjiyat
Omar Abdelkafy describes the Prophet's humility: he sat among his Companions without being marked out, reassured a man who trembled in awe of him, and lived through his humility as one among all. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep07 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep07 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep07 00:04:00} He holds that whomever God raises to office should grow closer to people and to meeting their needs, and that true scholars grow in humility as they grow in knowledge. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep07 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep07 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep07 00:23:00}
