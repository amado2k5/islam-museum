## Summary
This page sets out the beginning of the artistic Arabic novel as a research summary supplied to the museum tells it: Zaynab by Muhammad Husayn Haykal, which its author wrote while studying in France and published in 1913 under the pseudonym 'a peasant Egyptian'. It drew on European Romantic narrative traditions while depicting, for the first time according to the summary, the social difficulties of Egypt's villagers {cite:britannica-muhammad-husayn-haykal} {cite:britannica-arabic-literature-the-short-story}.

## The author and the novel
Its author is [[person/muhammad-husayn-haykal|Muhammad Husayn Haykal]], who has a record in the museum. The novel, as the summary says of Encyclopaedia Britannica, was written in France, where its author was a student, and drew, according to the summary, on European Romantic narrative traditions. It was published in 1913 without his name, signed 'Misri fallah', that is an Egyptian peasant {cite:britannica-muhammad-husayn-haykal} {cite:britannica-arabic-literature-the-short-story}.

## Why it counts as the first novel
The summary notes that narrative experiments had come before, but that Zaynab is widely regarded in literary history as the first true Arabic novel, and that the NobelPrize.org site says so in its article on Naguib Mahfouz. This is a judgement of historiography which the museum does not make its own; it is passed on in the wording 'widely regarded', as in the summary {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article}.

## The earlier narrative experiments
The summary does not name those experiments in this section, but elsewhere in the same file it names two narrative works of the Nahda. One is al-Saq ala al-Saq by Ahmad Faris al-Shidyaq, 1855; the other is Majma al-Bahrayn by Nasif al-Yaziji, 1856, a collection of maqamat. The maqama is a narrative form, and the page [[concept/islah-al-lugha-fi-al-nahda|Language reform in the Nahda]] tells of them. The summary also lists Tayf al-Khayal by Ibn Daniyal, of the thirteenth century, in the Mamluk period, in its list of periods {cite:wikipedia-maqama-en} {cite:wikipedia-nasif-al-yaziji-en} {cite:britannica-arabic-literature-the-short-story}.

## Where the novel stands in the Nahda
Zaynab comes in 1913, late in the Nahda as the summary dates it, 1798 to 1939, and before Naguib Mahfouz, to whom the maturing of the Arabic novel is credited. To see its place, read [[concept/marahil-al-adab-al-arabi|The eight periods of Arabic literature]]; for where the art of the novel ended up according to the summary, read [[concept/najib-mahfuz-wa-nobel-1988|Naguib Mahfouz and the 1988 Nobel Prize]] {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance}.

## The setting of the Nahda
The summary says elsewhere that print culture, journalism and translation forced nineteenth-century writers to adapt classical Arabic from ornate courtly prose into a direct, standardised medium able to carry modern science, politics and mass communication. Into this setting Zaynab came about half a century after al-Shidyaq and al-Yaziji, once the newspaper and the printed book had become the means of publishing. The summary does not link the two explicitly for Zaynab; the link is the page's alone, left for the reader of [[concept/islah-al-lugha-fi-al-nahda|Language reform in the Nahda]] to weigh {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance}.

## What came after
The same file says that Naguib Mahfouz is the one credited with bringing the Arabic novel to full maturity, moving from the social realism of the Trilogy, 1956 to 1957, to symbolic and experimental forms in Awlad Haratina in 1959 and Miramar in 1967. Between Zaynab in 1913 and the Trilogy lie about forty-four years, a figure derived from the two dates; the museum does not claim to give a detailed history of the novel in between, for the summary names none of the works of those years {cite:nobelprize-mahfouz-1988-article} {cite:britannica-arabic-literary-renaissance}.

## What the page does not say
The summary does not give the novel's events or characters, nor the exact date of its writing, nor the language it was written in, so the page says nothing of these. The museum has not opened the articles the summary cites, nor has it read the novel.

## Basis
The page rests on a research summary supplied to the museum, which the founder approved relying on as it stands on 21 November 2026. All that is here is attributed to the summary, including the dates, the title, the pseudonym and the judgement on the novel's priority.
