## Summary
Ibn Sina urges the healthy to watch their sleep, keeping it in measure and in its time, and to guard against the harm of wakefulness; the best sleep, he says, is deep and comes after the food has gone down from the upper belly and the bloating has settled, while sleeping on emptiness or on fullness before the descent is poor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.241}.

## Watching sleep
The healthy ought to attend to sleep, taking it in measure and in its time without excess, and to guard their brains and all their faculties against the harm of wakefulness. A person often forces himself to stay awake, driving sleep away for fear of fainting and loss of strength {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.241}.

## The best sleep
The best sleep is deep, and comes after the food has gone down from the upper belly and any bloating that may follow has settled; so if the descent is slow he should walk a little and then sleep. Sleeping on emptiness is poor and lets strength fall, and sleeping on fullness before the descent from the upper belly is poor because the sleep is not deep but restless, nature is dulled and digestion spoiled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.241}.
