## Summary
Al-Battani teaches reading the horizontal shadow from the altitude in the table by finding the altitude in the altitude rows and taking the shadow beside it, and the altitude from the shadow by finding the shadow's like in the shadow column and taking what stands beside it. If minutes accompany the numbers they are handled by proportional correction as laid down in the chapter on declination, and for the standing shadow the altitude is first taken from ninety. The table is drawn for a gnomon of twelve parts. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.36-37}.

## Reading the table
He says: to find the altitude from a shadow, look for its like in the table's shadow column, and wherever you find it take what stands beside it of the altitude degrees drawn in the altitude row; the result is the altitude for that shadow. So the table is read in both directions, from altitude to shadow and from shadow to altitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.37}.

## Minutes and the standing shadow
He says: if minutes accompany the altitude degrees, or the shadow's fingers, take them by the correction laid down in the chapter on declination: see what share of sixty they are, take the same share of the difference between the whole degree and the degree above it, and subtract it from the whole shadow fingers. If the shadow whose altitude you want carries minutes, look at the shadow you find in the table and subtract it from yours. For the standing shadow subtract the altitude from ninety and take what stands beside it by the same correction. The shadow in the table is drawn for a gnomon of twelve parts, so whatever you work out is for a gnomon of twelve fingers. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.37}.
