## The kinds
The break of continuity in the head is either in the skin and flesh or in the bone: a muwaddiha, a hashima, a muthqila or a simhaq; of the simhaq is the fatra, which is that the membrane juts outward, swells, fattens and becomes like a fatra, and among them are the ama and the ja'ifa, which carry danger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89}

## Wounds that reach the brain
In wounds that reach the membrane of the brain slackness comes on the side of the wound and convulsion on the opposite; if the cut does not reach the ventricles but only the border of the thin membrane it is safer, and when the cut reaches the brain fever and bilious vomiting show, and few of those do well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89} Nearest to safety is the cut that falls in the two front ventricles when it is met quickly; those in the two rear ventricles are harder, and that in the middle is harder than that in the rear and further from returning to the natural state, unless it is slight and little and haste is made to set it right {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89}

## The two schools of physicians
As for the treatment, haste is made to prevent the swelling by what can be borne, and one is referred to what he gave of the treatment of the head wound in skin and flesh in Book IV, and of the treatment of the fracture in the chapter on fracture and setting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89} The physicians have two schools on the displaced fracture of the skull: that of those who incline to the calm, quieting remedies that strongly ease pain, and that of those who see the use of strongly drying remedies; safety was more often with these later men than with the earlier, which is no wonder, for Galen said that the temper of the membrane and the bone is dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are left out here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89}
