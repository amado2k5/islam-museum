## Definition
Fatherhood in Islam is care and responsibility before it is lineage. In the address of the two revelations the father stands over his household: he provides for them, raises them in religion and character, and deals justly among them; in return his children, young and grown, owe him dutifulness and obedience in what is right. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:al-adab-al-mufrad}

## The father in the Qur'an
The Qur'an holds the father in a double stance: it commands dutifulness to him beside the mother in the kindness-to-parents verses of al-Baqara, al-Nisa', al-An'am, al-Isra', Luqman, al-Ahqaf and al-'Ankabut; it makes Ibrahim's prayer for his father and his offspring a model of seeking guidance and forgiveness; it shows Ya'qub counselling his sons at death, asking them what they will worship after him; it writes Luqman's seven counsels to his son — tawhid, prayer, commanding right, patience and humility; it has Zakariyya ask his Lord for an heir who will succeed him and guard his family after him; and it pictures Yusuf raising his parents upon the throne as the fulfilment of a childhood dream. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## The father in the Sunnah
The Sunnah makes the father the middle gate of Paradise: the parent is the middlemost of its gates — let the son keep it or lose it. When a man asked who most deserves good companionship, the answer named the mother three times and then the father. In the Prophet's own life there are living pictures: a man raising Fatima and his sons, carrying al-Hasan and al-Husayn and kissing them and stepping down from the minbar for them, and grieving over Ibrahim until his breath broke. In al-Adab al-Mufrad the chapters of birr al-walidayn are detailed: walk behind them and not ahead of them, never raise your voice above theirs, and let your du'a go out to them. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:al-adab-al-mufrad}

## The father's duties to his children
Fiqh and adab write duties on the father: maintenance of his children within his means until they stand on their own, a good name, the 'aqiqa for the newborn, teaching the boy prayer at seven and disciplining him for it at ten, justice between children in gifts — the Prophet called witnessing favouritism a witnessing of injustice — and raising them upon faith and works: "each of you is a shepherd and each is answerable for his flock." {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:al-adab-al-mufrad}

## Birr and 'uquq
Dutifulness to the parents is among the greatest devotions: kindness to them is paired with the worship of God, a man's striving in their service is a jihad, and the parent's du'a for the child is answered. 'Uquq is its opposite: it is paired with shirk in the hadith of the major sins, and severing the tie bars from Paradise in the report "no cutter enters the Garden." Yet dutifulness stays within what is right: there is no obedience to them in disobedience to the Creator, while keeping them company in this world kindly. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}

## Lineage, custody and the orphan
Islam secures the child's paternity to his father: "the child is for the bed, and for the adulterer the stone" — the standing rule that establishes nasab and bars its wrongful claim. Nursing builds a bond of the same prohibition as lineage. Custody belongs to the mother and then the father by the child's interest. The orphan who lost his father is covered by the sharia: the orphans' wealth is guarded against wasteful and hasty consumption, and the orphan's guardian stands with the Prophet in Paradise "like these two." {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}
