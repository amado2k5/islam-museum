## Nature
A plant like the plant of khusa al-tha'lab, so that some people were confused about the difference between them, one of them saying that this is that and others that this plant is that on account of the likeness of the roots and the plant, the two being near in actions; it is of two kinds, one smaller, a pair, one below and one above, the one soft and the other full, and another kind greater than that {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Actions and properties
In the large kind there is a superfluous moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Swellings and pustules
It dissolves phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Wounds and ulcers
It cleanses ulcers, keeps erysipelas from spreading, opens fistulas and heals malignant and eating ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Organs of the head
It benefits thrush {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that when a man takes the greater of the two he becomes a begetter of males, and when a woman takes the smaller she becomes a bearer of females; it is said that the fresh increases intercourse and the dry cuts it off, each cancelling the action of the other; all of this was said of the greater and the smaller; a statement attributed to the book as passed on from earlier writers, without recommendation or endorsement {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.703}
