## Summary
In the fifth chapter Ibn Sina shows that composite diseases are not diseases that merely happen to be together but those whose joining produces one disease, the example being the tumour, in which all the genera of disease are found, and he divides tumours by their six matters {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.107}.

## What composite means
By composite diseases he does not mean any diseases that happened to be gathered, but diseases that when joined produce from the whole something that is one disease, like the tumour. Pustules are of the genus of tumour: pustules are small tumours as tumours are large pustules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.107}.

## The genera within a tumour
In a tumour there is a disease of temperament, since no tumour arises except from bad temperament with matter; there is a disease of form and composition, since in every tumour there is a fault in shape and size and sometimes also in position; and there is the common disease, broken continuity, since the surplus matters that pour into the swollen organ settle between its parts and push them apart until they take places for themselves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.107}.

## Cause of tumours
Tumour befalls soft organs, and something like it in bones thickens their bulk and increases their moisture. Every tumour without an evident cause has a bodily cause that involves the shifting of matter from one organ to what lies below it, called catarrh. The material cause from which tumours and pustules arise may be submerged in other humours that are harmless in quality; when the good humours are drained, naturally as in a woman nursing her child, or unnaturally as through a wound running good blood, those bad humours remain pure and alone, nature is harmed by them and pushes them away, and sometimes the way it pushes is toward the skin, so that tumours and pustules arise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.107}.

## The six matters
The matters from which tumours arise are six: the four humours, wateriness and wind. A tumour is then either hot or not {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.107}.
