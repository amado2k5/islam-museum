## Summary
Opaque yellow ware was made and used at [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]] in the ninth and tenth centuries, after which its manufacture seems to have died out; its glaze takes colour and opacity from a tin-bearing yellow engobe, and its decoration is almost always in green. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 247}

## Making
Its reddish body is like that of the colour-splashed and black on white wares; the green spots and strokes, from copper, were probably applied after the glaze was poured and often decayed before the rest of the surface; where the glaze has decayed, the yellow is left as a powdery layer the colour of dry mustard. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 247} A coarse ware resembling it is still made at Nishapur, [[work/nishapur-pottery-of-the-early-islamic-period|Wilkinson]] notes, though with black in the decoration and the yellow not covering the whole surface. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 247}

## Origin
Wilkinson holds that it did not originate at Nishapur but probably in Iraq, as an imitation of imported Chinese pieces; it has been found at Ctesiphon and [[place/samarra|Samarra]] in better quality than at Nishapur — harder, thinner, more golden, without the Nishapur greenish cast. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 247} It was also made in Syria, at Tarsus and al-Mina, and found at Dhahran in Arabia. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 247} It was popular at Nishapur in many shapes: bowls, dishes, lamps, pitchers and small jars with decorative lugs. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 247}
