## Summary
In fiqh, blood is the red fluid that runs in the veins of living creatures. Jurists also use the word for retaliation, for the sacrificial offering, and for the blood of menstruation, irregular bleeding and postnatal bleeding. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8233}
The jurists agree that blood is forbidden, impure and may not be eaten or used; they differ on how little of it is excused and on how to define little. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8233}

## Related terms
Sadid is the thin fluid of a wound mixed with blood; some say it is pus mixed with blood. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8233}
Qayh is pure pus with no blood in it; some say it is a watery pus with a trace of blood. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8233}

## Where the rules appear
Bleeding breaks ablution; blood is impure and must be removed from the worshipper's body, clothes and place of prayer; blood of menstruation, irregular bleeding or postnatal bleeding is treated under those entries. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8234}
Bleeding is among the things that break the fast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8234}
As a sacrificial offering owed for breaking a pilgrimage rule, blood is treated under the obligation of the offering for enjoined pilgrimage and combined pilgrimage, and for one who is prevented. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8234}
The jurists also treat blood in food, slaughter, the aqiqa sacrifice and retaliation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8234}
