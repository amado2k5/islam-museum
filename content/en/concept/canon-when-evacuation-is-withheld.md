## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the third chapter, on how and when to evacuate, saying that ten things indicate the right judgment about evacuation; the page names among them fullness, complexion, age, season, the air of the country, habit of evacuation and craft, and he then lists the states that forbid evacuating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.273}.

## Emptiness, strength and temperament
Evacuation is forbidden by emptiness and by weakness of any of the three powers, though weakness of a sensory or motor power may be preferred to the harm of leaving evacuation undone when it is hoped a grave matter can be set right. A hot, dry temperament forbids it, and a cold, moist one too for lack or weakness of heat, whereas a hot, moist one is strongly permissive. Extreme leanness and loose texture forbid it for fear the spirit and strength will dissolve, so a weak, thin person with much bile in the blood is handled gently, not evacuated, and fed what makes good blood leaning to cold and moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.273}.

## Fat, age, season, country and craft
Excessive fat forbids it, for fear cold will take over and the flesh press on the vessels and close them once they are emptied. Bad symptoms forbid it, such as a tendency to sprue and to convulsion, as do an age short of full growth or past it into withering, the very hot or very cold season, a very hot southern country, since most purgatives are sharp, a very cold northern one, little habit of evacuation, and a craft that itself evacuates much, such as bath service and carrying loads {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.273}.
