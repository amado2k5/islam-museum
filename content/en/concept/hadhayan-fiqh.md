## Summary
Hadhayan is speech without sense, in illness or otherwise; technically it is speaking without reflection. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16517}

## Lagh and laghat
Lagh is speech that has no effect on a ruling; laghat is noisy talk whose meaning is intended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16517} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16518}

## The divorce of a delirious man
All scholars agree that the divorce of one who has lost his reason, without intoxication, does not take effect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16518}
The Hanafis hold that the words of one whose speech is mostly delirious carry no weight. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16518}
The Malikis hold that one who divorces in delirium and later says he did not notice is not bound, unless evidence shows his reason was sound; if he says something happened that he did not understand, Ibn Naji holds the divorce binds him, and al-Dardir objects that a sick man may imagine things and speak accordingly. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16518} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16519}

## Effect on uprightness
The Hanafis require little idle talk and delirium as a condition of uprightness; one given to lying or delirium is not accepted as a witness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16519}
