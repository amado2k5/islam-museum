## Summary
Miqadir are measures: the means by which a thing is known as counted, measured by volume, weight or length. Their four kinds are volume (kayl), weight (wazn), length (dhar') and number ('adad). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14857}

## The sa' and the mudd
The sa' is agreed to be four mudds, though the Hanafis differ on the size of the mudd. Most jurists hold that the legal sa' is the smaller sa' of Medina; Abu Hanifa holds that it is the sa' of Iraq. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14858} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14859}

## Rulings that use the sa'
The sa' fixes zakat al-fitr, the expiation for deliberately breaking the Ramadan fast, the expiation for zihar, the ransom for ihram, the expiation for breaking the fast for an excuse, the wife's maintenance, and the amount of water for ablution. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14859}

## The 'araq, faraq, qadah, qirba, qist, qafiz and qullah
The 'araq is fifteen sa'. The faraq is three sa', or sixteen ratls in the Hanbali view; for zakat on honey Ibn Qudama gives ten faraqs and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan five. The qadah is part of a sa'. The qirba seldom exceeds one hundred Baghdadi ratls. The qist is half a sa'. The qafiz is twelve sa' according to al-Qalyubi. The qullah holds two hundred and fifty ratls in the Hanafi, Shafi'i and Hanbali schools and is named in defining abundant standing water. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14859} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14860} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14861} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14862}

## Two qullas and abundant water
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis define abundant standing water as two qullas, citing the hadith that water of two qullas does not carry impurity; the Hanafis define it by what the observer judges abundant, or by whether impurity would not spread from one side to the other in belief, measured at ten cubits by ten. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14862} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14863}

## The kurr, kilaja, makhtum and mudd
The kurr is sixty qafiz and carries no ruling. The kilaja is an Iraqi measure. The makhtum is the sa'. The mudd is a quarter of the sa' by agreement, but its ratl value differs: the majority hold it a ratl and a third in Iraqi measure, the Hanafis two ratls. The mudd governs ablution water, zakat al-fitr and, for some jurists, a wife's maintenance; the hadith of Anas that the Prophet washed with a mudd is cited. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14863} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14864} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14865}

## Zakat al-fitr, the mudi, the mikkuk and the wasq
For zakat al-fitr a sa' of any listed kind is due; the Hanafis require half a sa', two mudds, of wheat. The mudi is a measure of Syria and Egypt. The mikkuk is a sa' and a half for the Hanafis and Malikis, and two and a half for Abu 'Ubayd. The wasq is sixty sa', the load of a camel, and fixes the thresholds for crops and fruit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14865} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14866}
