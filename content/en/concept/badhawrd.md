## Nature and selection
It is the white thistle. It resembles the hasak, but is whiter and has longer spines. Its leaves resemble the leaves of the hamama, but are finer and whiter. The stalk can reach two cubits, the flower is purplish, and the seed is like the seed of safflower but rounder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}.

## Temperament and actions
Its root cools and dries with a dissolving of water. Its seed is hot and light, and some say the whole plant is very hot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.383}. It has a dissolving and opening power, especially in the seed, and it has an astringency that stops bleeding, which is moderate {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: it helps phlegmatic swellings because of its dissolving and astringent power, and it is applied as a poultice with its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Joints: it helps spasm, because of its moderate astringency with dissolving power, and its seed helps children who drink it when the movements of the muscles are disturbed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Head: a gargle with its juice quiets toothache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Chest: it helps spitting of blood, especially its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Food organs: it helps weakness of the stomach and opens obstructions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Excretory organs: it helps chronic diarrhoea, especially of the stomach, and its root promotes urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Fevers: it helps long phlegmatic fevers and fevers caused by weakness of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Poisons: chewed and placed on a scorpion sting, it draws the poison out. Its seed drunk helps the bite of venomous creatures {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}.

## Substitutes
The book names the shahtaraj as its substitute in fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}.
