## Summary
For one who fears the samum, the scorching deadly wind, Ibn Sina prescribes binding nose and mouth with a turban and veil, eating onion in buttermilk beforehand, and sniffing rose oil and gourd-seed oil; if the samum strikes him, he pours cold water over his limbs, washes his face, makes his food of cold vegetables and puts cold oils on his head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.262}.

## Before the samum blows
Whoever fears the samum must bind his nose and mouth with a turban and veil and endure the hardship, first eating onion in buttermilk, especially onion kept or soaked in it overnight, with sniffing of rose oil and gourd-seed oil and sipping of gourd oil, which wards off the expected harm of the samum {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.262}.

## After being struck
If the samum strikes him, he pours cold water over his limbs or washes his face with it, makes his food of cold vegetables, puts cold oils such as rose oil and cold juices on his head, then bathes and avoids intercourse; salted fish helps once his state settles, as does diluted wine, and milk is among the best food for him if he has no fever, while if the fever is an ephemeral one he uses sour buttermilk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.262}.
