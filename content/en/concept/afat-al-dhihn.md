## The three ways
The kinds of harm that fall in the acts of the brain are from two causes and are known in three ways. If a person's sensation is sound and his imagining of the shapes of things in waking and sleep is sound, but the things and states he saw in waking or sleep, which he ought to be able to express, have gone from him, and what he hears or sees does not stay with him, that is a fault in memory and in the back of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96} If there is no fault in that, but he says what ought not to be said, approves what ought not to be approved, hopes for what ought not to be hoped for, seeks what ought not to be sought, does what ought not to be done, and fears what ought not to be feared, and cannot deliberate in what he deliberates, the fault is in thought and in the middle part of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96} If his memory and speech are as they were, and nothing unsound arises in what he does and says, but things sensed are imagined for him, he picks at lint (zi'bir), sees false figures, fires and waters, or else is weak in imagining the shapes of things in sleep and waking, the fault is in imagination, and in the front belly (batn) of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96}

## Combinations and direction
If two of these combine, or three, the fault is in the two bellies or the three {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96} That thought be sick and fall short through sharing in a fault of memory that came first is easier than that thought be sick, after which the sickness of memory follows; this wording is carried as printed, with the word "thought" in both clauses, and is not interpreted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96} What of this leans to deficiency is from cold, and what leans to confusion and disturbance is from heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96} Some claimed that it may lean to deficiency from a deficiency in the substance of the brain, and this is not far-fetched; all of this has its cause either first in the brain itself, or from another organ, and it may be from outside, as a blow or a fall {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.96}
