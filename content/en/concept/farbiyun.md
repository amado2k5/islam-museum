## Nature
Dioscorides, the sage, said that it is the gum of a tree like the cucumber in shape, growing in Linwa in the land of Sadad or in the lands of Muritiya; the tree is full of an excessively pungent, hot and sharp gum, and those who gather it fear it for its great heat, so they take the paunches of sheep, wash them and hang them on the trunk, then pierce the tree from a distance with a spear or javelin, and much gum pours into the paunches, and some pours on the ground too {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.628-629}. It is of two kinds, one clear, like anzarut, and the other lumpy, like dregs, and it is adulterated with anzarut and with gum mixed into it, and testing it by taste is hard because once it stings the tongue the sting lasts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}. The first to come upon this drug and work out its science was Yunas, king of Linwa; its strength changes after some years, and the old inclines to yellow and fairness and dissolves in oil only with difficulty, while the new is the opposite in all this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Choice
The best is the new, clear one, yellow to fair, sharp in smell and very pungent; anything else is adulterated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Temperament
Hot, with a subtle burning and cleansing power; the new is stronger in heating than asafoetida, though no gum is like asafoetida in heating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Joints
Mixed with some drinks made with spices it benefits sciatica and throws off the scales of bone on the same day, but the flesh around the bones must be protected; it is rubbed on palsy and numbness and benefits greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Ocular organs
Used as an eye salve it cleanses and dissolves the blue water in the eye, but its sting lasts all day, and so it is mixed with honey and with other salves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Excretory organs
It benefits the yellow water and cold of the kidneys and benefits those with colic, and, as they said, loosens the viscous phlegm lodged in the hips, the back and the bowels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book reports from the people of Khuz that it closes the mouth of the womb so tightly that it prevents the drugs that cause a foetus to fall {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Poisons and bites
A warning only: the book says that taken in a small quantity over a few days it kills by ulcerating the stomach and the gut; it also passes on a saying of some about the bite of the viper and of vermin, whose method is not repeated here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}
