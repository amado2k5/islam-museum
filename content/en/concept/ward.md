## Nature and selection
Nature: well known, made of a watery and earthy substance, with pungency, astringency and bitterness with a little sweetness; its watery part breaks the heat. It often brings on a cold, and its bitter power holds while it is fresh; when dry its bitterness lessens, so the fresh kind loosens the bowels when drunk. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.451}
Temperament: Galen says the rose is not very cold compared with us, and should be cold in the first degree. Paul says it is made of heat and astringency. Ibn Masawayh says it is dry in the first degree, indeed at the end of the second. Its dryness is at the start of the second, especially the dry kind. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.451}

## Actions
Actions: its drying is stronger than its astringency, because its bitterness is stronger than the astringent taste; it opens and cleanses and calms the movement of yellow bile. Its seed is its most astringent part, and so is the down in its centre; its astringency does not go beyond preventing resolution. The dry kind is more astringent and colder. Some claim it draws out splinters and thorns. Its good juice is that of the whitish petals, dried in the shade and kept. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.452}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it corrects the stench of sweat when used in the bath; some say it removes all warts when used as a powder. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.452}
Wounds and ulcers: helps ulcers, especially chafing between the thighs and in the armpits and groins, and makes flesh grow in deep wounds; some claimed that as a powder it draws out splinters and thorns. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.452}
Head: its moist part and the decoction of its water calm headache; rose oil makes one sneeze; its seed tightens the gums and helps earache. Some say it causes sneezing by holding back vapour, and that the sneeze suits a hot brain. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.452}
Eyes: calms eye pain from heat; the decoction of its dry part is good for thickened eyelids when used as kohl; its oil and juice help; it helps inflammation only when its white outgrowths are cut away. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.452}
Excretory organs: rose water, sipped, helps fainting; its juice and the water of its branches are good for coughing up blood, and so are its buds; it helps pain of the straight gut, and its decoction is injected for ulcers of the bowels; it helps pain of the anus when painted on with a feather; rose oil loosens the bowels. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Book listing, on heat in the womb: it calms womb pain from heat, and the decoction of its dry part does likewise. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Digestive organs: rose is good for the liver and stomach; its preserve with honey strengthens the stomach, it is the julep, and it helps digestion; rose and its juice help a wet stomach; rose oil cools inflammation of the stomach, and rubbing the stomach with the rose itself or its syrup helps. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Digestive organs, and the foul-smelling rose: the foul-smelling kind is hot, and its root is like the caustic ulcer-plant. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.452}
