## Summary
Nai, in the lexicon, is news of a death. The announcer brings or spreads it. Arab tribes used to send a rider to announce the death of a noble man. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15797}
Nadb (praise by calling out) and nawh (wailing with a keen) are related but do not necessarily accompany the announcement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15798}

## Rulings on nai
Form: no fixed wording is prescribed; humble and pleading phrasing is preferred. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15798}
Ruling: opinions range from recommended to forbidden. Ibn al-Arabi divides it into three: informing family is a sunna, calling a crowd to boast is disliked, and an announcement in the manner of wailing is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15799}
Recommended: informing neighbours and friends so they may pray for the dead; the Prophet announced the death of the Negus. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15799} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15800}
Disliked: for the Hanbalis, informing someone who is not a relative, friend or neighbour, or calling out the news; for the Malikis, calling out in a mosque or its door. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15802}
Forbidden: announcements with loud weeping, praise of the dead for boasting, and the pre-Islamic form. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15802} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15803}
Permitted: a plain announcement of the death; calling it out in markets is disputed, and the sounder view is that it is not disliked if no praise or glorification is added. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15801}
