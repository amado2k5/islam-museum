## Nature
Dioscorides said that the kinds of shabb are many and that those entering the treatment of medicine are three, the split, the moist and the rolled; the split is the Yemeni, white toward yellow, astringent with a sourness, as if the blossom of shabb; and a stony kind is found without astringency to the taste, which is not of the kind of shabb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Actions and properties
It has a power of holding back and drying, benefits the flow of every blood, and holds back the flowing and pouring of superfluities; its astringency is greater than the astringency of badhawurd {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Cosmetics
With the water of pitch on scurf, lice, bahr and the stench of the armpit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Wounds and ulcers
With the dregs of wine it serves as an oak-gall for difficult and eating ulcers, and for the eating ulcer and burns of fire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}

## Organs of the head
Its decoction is beneficial when gargled for toothache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.676}
