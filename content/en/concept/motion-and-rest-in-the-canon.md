## Summary
In the twelfth chapter, on the causes that lie in motion and rest, Ibn Sina shows that the action of motion in the human body differs by intensity and weakness, by little and much, and by the rest mixed with it, and that the philosophers count this a division by itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.

## Motion
Intense, abundant, and slight motion mixed with rest all share in exciting heat. But intense motion that is not abundant differs from abundant motion that is not intense, and from abundant motion mixed with rest, in that it heats the body greatly and dissolves less. Abundant motion dissolves gently, beyond what it heats. When either is taken alone and prolonged, the body cools, because the innate heat is excessively dissolved, and also dries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.

## The effect of the material handled
When the motion handles some material, the material may do what helps its action or what lessens it. For example, the motion of the fuller's craft may bring cold or moistures, while the motion of the blacksmith's craft may bring excess heat and dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.

## Rest
Rest always cools, for the innate heat loses its reviving and is stifled in confinement, and it moistens because the superfluities are not dissolved {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.130}.
