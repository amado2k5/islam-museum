## The sign of participation and of the spirit

What comes in company with the body has the signs pointing to the temperament of the whole body; what comes with the brain has one of the signs pointing to a lesion in the brain, with the other senses afflicted too — though it may single out sight most, and smell rather than hearing, as in the compressing blow that falls on the front part of the brain: hearing may remain as it was, the eye stays open and the lid cannot close over it, yet it does not see. The sign of what belongs to the spirit: if it is thin and scant, the thing is seen from near under scrutiny but not from afar; if thin and abundant, it scrutinizes near and far; if excessively thin, a very luminous thing does not stay fixed but is dazzled and dispersed by bright light; if thick and abundant, contemplating the far does not defeat it yet it cannot scrutinize the near ⁦(the disagreement over its explanation lies between the ray-school and those who hold the transparent conveys, and the book says verifying the truth is for the philosophers, not the physicians)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.203}

## The sign of turbidity, the retina and dryness

A turbidity seen from outside may point to the state of the cornea or to the egg-white humour; its owner always sees something like mist between his eyes. If it is seen only opposite the pupil while the rest of the cornea is clear, it points to the egg-white humour; if it covers all parts of the cornea there is no doubt it is in the cornea, and the doubt remains whether the egg-white humour shares it. The egg-white humour may dry so that some parts gather and stop transmitting, and then a hole or holes are seen opposite. The sign of a rupture of the retina's continuity through its whole extent is the sudden loss of sight. And know that every corruption from dryness grows worse with hunger, dissolving exercise, evacuations and the midday heat, and the moist the opposite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.203} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.204}
