## Summary
Ibn Sina recommends after the drug barley water to turn aside the purgative's harm, washed cress with hot water and oil for the cold, phlegmatic person, psyllium with cold water, violet oil and sugar for the hot, flaxseed for the moderate, Armenian clay with pomegranate water for fear of abrasion, and putting off oxymel for two or three days {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.281-282}.

## By temperament
Drinking barley water after the purge turns aside the purgative's harm. A person of cold temperament in whom phlegm prevails over the humours takes after the drug and its action washed cress with hot water and oil; a hot-tempered person uses psyllium with cold water, violet oil, white sugar and julep; the moderate takes flaxseed. Whoever fears abrasion takes Armenian clay with pomegranate water, and what is named must be used after the purging is over or it cuts it short. For anyone who has a fever after a drug, barley water suits best. Oxymel scrapes and should wait two or three days until the intestines regain their strength. On the second day the one purged enters the bath; if he finds it pleasant and enjoys it, that shows the bath is cleansing him of what remains, so he is left there, and if he does not enjoy it and chafes in it he is taken out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.281-282}.

## The weak, the old and wine
Ibn Sina says one with weak bowels may take from purgative drugs a purging tendency, which drags on and calls for many treatments before it is held; likewise the old are feared to be harmed by purging. Wine drunk after purgatives brings on fevers and disturbance. Purging and venesection often leave a pain in the liver, and drinking hot water removes it. Anyone of dry temperament is worn down by a strong drug, and with a weak drug movement should be kept low so that its force is not dissolved {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.282}.
