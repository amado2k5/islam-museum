## Their kinds
The tongue may take hot tumours, phlegmy, windy and hard ones, and cancer; the signs of all are plain on returning to what was said in the tumours' signs; and the tongue may grow great from drinking poisons of this kind (the poisons' names left out) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.258}
## Their treatment
The hot tumours are treated first by evacuation — better in the tongue's tumours than vomiting — then at their start the cooling juices of this kind are held in the mouth; if it does not dissolve nor open, the ripening dissolvers are needed at the last, gargled with, the foods being of the kind that ripens and dissolves; if it suppurates the astringents of this kind are used in the mouth; and what profits the loose phlegmy and the hot that has reached its end is a burnt thing of this kind laid upon it (names and recipes left out) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.258} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.259}
## Galen's report
Galen said: a man's tongue swelled greatly; he was sixty years old and had no custom of venesection, so I did not bleed him, gave him a drug of this kind to drink and meant to wrap his tongue in cold bandages; it was evening and a physician disagreed; that night he saw in a dream to hold a juice of this kind in his mouth and was healed completely, and that agreed with my own counsel (a report the book ascribes to Galen; the names are left out) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.259}
## The hard tumour
If the tumour is hard, the regimen should be softened, the food made good, the thick humours evacuated by what was named in earlier chapters, the mollifying gargles of this kind used, kinds of lenitives held in the mouth and the loosening of the temperament kept up (the names are left out) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.259}
