## Summary
Once the difference is known it is added to ninety degrees if the star's distance from the equator is northern and taken from ninety if it is southern; the result is half the star's day arc. One sixth of that half is the times of its hours above the earth. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.53}.

## Keep it, then take a sixth
The text tells the reader to keep the half day arc because the later chapters all return to it, and then to take a sixth of it as the time of one hour above the earth. The star therefore has six hours on each side of its culmination, like the Sun, in agreement with the earlier division of the day of a degree into twelve seasonal hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.53}.
