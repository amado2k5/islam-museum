## Nature
It is of two kinds, garden and wild. Some call the wild atritulus; it is a thistle like the garden safflower except that its leaf is far longer and grows at the tip of the stem, the rest of the stem bare, with a yellow flower and a thin root of no use; and if its leaf or fruit is ground it is beneficial {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.646}

## Temperament
The wild is hot in the second degree and dry in the third; the known kind is hot in the first and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.646}

## Actions and properties
Its oil is near the oil of anjara but weaker, and it is among the things that curdle milk and separate its wateriness. Masih claimed that it dissolves curdled milk and curdles liquid milk, and its nourishment is very small {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.646}

## Organs of the chest
It cleanses the chest and clears the voice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.646}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Bad for the stomach, and it curdles milk in the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.646}

## Excretory organs
It benefits colic and loosens burnt phlegm when mixed with fig or honey. The oil of the garden kind loosens the belly, and loosening may be sought with the kernel of its seed in broth, or a pill made from it with almond and honey, or a sweet made for that purpose. The measures are detailed in the book and are left out here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.646-647}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it benefits desire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.647}

## Poisons and bites
The leaf of the wild, or its fruit, or both together, given with wine, benefits the scorpion sting; some people claim that the stung person who holds the wild plant or its fruit in his mouth feels no pain, and when he puts it away from himself the pain returns {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.647}
