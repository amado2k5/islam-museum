## Summary
Ibn Sina divides pustules by the same count as tumours, sanguine, bilious, mixed, watery and windy, then in the sixth chapter lists what is counted with diseases though not itself a disease: the afflictions of hair, colour, odour and bodily habit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.

## Pustules
Pustules follow the number of tumours: some sanguine, like smallpox; purely bilious, like bilious hives and the millet-like eruption; mixed, like measles, herpes, corns, scab and warts and others; some watery, like blisters, and some windy, like bubbles. He refers the detail of tumours and pustules to the fourth book {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.

## Hair and colour
The things counted with diseases though outside them, belonging to adornment, are four: one concerning hair, one colour, one smell, and one the bodily habit after colour. The kinds of hair affliction are falling out, shedding, shortness, scarcity, splitting, thinness, thickness, excess of curl, excess of lankness, greying and any change of colour. Afflictions of colour fall in four genera: change through bad temperament with matter, as in jaundice, or without matter, as in the paleness from a purely cold temperament and the yellowness from a purely hot one; change through external causes, as sun, cold and wind scorch the colour; the spreading of foreign-coloured bodies over the skin, like black vitiligo, or their being caught in it, like moles and freckles; and the marks left when a break of continuity heals, like smallpox scars and the scars of ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.

## Smell and habit
Afflictions of smell are such as armpit odour and other foul smells that rise from bodies, and afflictions of habit after colour are either excessive thinness or excessive fatness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.
