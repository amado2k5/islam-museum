## Definition
Bunuwwa — sonhood and daughterhood — is the child's standing toward its parents in Islam, a two-directional bond on which the household is built: upward from child to parents in kindness and reverence, downward from parents to child in upbringing, mercy and maintenance. The Qur'an joins worship of God with kindness to parents in the same verse, makes affection within the house one of His signs, and makes the child a trial as much as a glad tiding. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## The child in the Qur'an: ornament, trial and glad tiding
The Qur'an states that wealth and sons are the ornament of worldly life, that possessions and children are a trial, and that some are given news of a forbearing boy and give thanks and bear patiently. It does not make sonhood a privilege of sons over daughters: against the Jahiliyya scale on which news of a daughter darkened a man's face, the Qur'an answered — and the hadith adds that whoever is tried with daughters and treats them well finds them a screen from the Fire. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## Rights owed by the child and owed to it
Parents are owed kindness, the lowering of the wing, and prayer for them as they raised the child when small — while no obedience is due in disobedience to the Creator. The child is owed a righteous mother chosen for it, a good name, the birth sacrifice, the first feeding, maintenance in fairness, upbringing in faith, and justice between it and its siblings in gifts, as in the report of al-Nu'man ibn Bashir in al-Bukhari. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}
