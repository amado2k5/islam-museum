## Summary
In the lexicon, alaniya comes from proclaiming and showing a thing; it is the opposite of secrecy {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11788}.

## Public and private worship
Some acts of worship are prescribed to be public, such as the call to prayer, the sermon, jihad and enjoining good; some are better kept private, such as silent recitation in the silent prayer; and some may be either, such as charity {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11788} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11789}.

## Charity
An obligatory gift is better shown when there is no fear of display, so that others may follow; a voluntary gift is better hidden where display is feared {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11789} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11790}.

## The faults of narrators and trustees
The faults of narrators, witnesses and those entrusted with charities and endowments must be made public when they affect their fitness; the scholars hold this is not forbidden backbiting {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11789}.

## Restraint of a bankrupt
The judge is recommended to proclaim the restraint of a bankrupt; one Shafi'i view makes it obligatory {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11790}.

## Where to look further
The rules of restraint are treated under «hajr» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11790}.
