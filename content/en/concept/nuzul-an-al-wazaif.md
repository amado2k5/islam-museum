## Summary

This question in [[work/radd-al-muhtar|Radd al-Muhtar]] is a practical one from [[person/ibn-abidin|Ibn Abidin]]'s own time: may the holder of a waqf post, such as imam, preacher, muezzin, carpet-keeper or doorkeeper, give it up to another for money? {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} He treats it under the side headings "no compensation for bare rights", "on compensation for posts and giving them up" and "on particular and general custom". {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21}

## The rule: no compensation for a bare right

He reports from al-Bada'i' that bare rights cannot be transferred as property and may not be settled for money, adding that they are not compensated if destroyed, and reports from al-Sarakhsi's commentary on al-Ziyadat that destroying a bare right brings no liability because compensation for it is void, unless it defeats a confirmed right. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} Among the examples in al-Ashbah: if the pre-emptor settles his right of pre-emption for money, the settlement is void; if a husband pays a wife who has been given the option so that she chooses him, it is void; if he pays one of his wives to give up her turn, it is not binding; and on this basis compensation for waqf posts is not permitted. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} Exceptions are the right of retaliation, the marriage bond and the right over a slave, for which compensation is allowed, as al-Zayla'i says. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21}

## Custom and the shift in fatwa

The school's doctrine does not recognise [[concept/urf|particular custom]], but many gave fatwa recognising it, and on that basis fatwa is given that posts may be given up for money, as in al-Ashbah. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} He reports from [[person/badr-al-din-al-ayni|al-Ayni]]'s fatwas that giving up posts has nothing to rest on, but scholars and judges allowed it out of necessity and required the administrator's approval to avoid disputes. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21} He reports from al-Hamawi that some compared giving up religious posts with a wife's giving her turn to a co-wife, both being mere releases. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21}

## Ibn Abidin's reasoning

Ibn Abidin holds that the permission does not rest on recognising particular custom but on parallels within the school, and that the ban on compensation for a right is not absolute. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22} He reports from al-Biri the reasoning that compensation may be taken by way of releasing the right, as with a legatee of a slave's service who settles it, or a settlement of a thousand for five hundred; and that the rights of pre-emption and of the wife's turn exist only to remove harm, so that once the holder accepts giving them up nothing remains to be compensated, unlike a right that belongs to its holder in its own right. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22} He reports that some argued for permission from [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan ibn Ali]]'s giving up the caliphate to [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] for a return, and says this too is plain. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22} He reports, from the handwriting of a scholar, that the mufti Abu al-Su'ud gave fatwa allowing compensation for the right of tenure and use and that it cannot be reclaimed. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22} He concludes that the question is one of probable judgement, the parallels are alike and there is room for discussion, and that the better course is al-Nahr's: a general release should follow. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22}

## If the recipient is not appointed

Ibn Abidin says the same holds for giving up the right of cultivation called mashadd al-maska and for a timar-holder giving up his timar; if the holder gives it up to another and the sultan does not grant it to the recipient but leaves it with the holder or gives it to a third person, the recipient should be able to reclaim the payment from the holder, since he paid only for the right to pass to him; so it was ruled in the Isma'iliyya and Hamidiyya fatwa collections, against those who ruled there is no reclaiming. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22}

## Key-money for shops

He reports from al-Ashbah that if particular custom is recognised, fatwa should be that the khulu of shops found in some markets of Cairo is binding. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.22} The subject has its own entry: [[concept/khullu-fiqh|khulu]].

## Selling a stipend

In the same passage he reports the fatwa of the author of al-Tanwir on selling a "jamakiyya", a man's stipend from the treasury which he needs as cash before it is paid and sells to another for less: it is not valid, being a sale of a debt to someone other than the debtor. {cite:radd-al-muhtar-bulaq-v4 p.21}
