## Summary
The text sets out several cases. If the two stars are at one longitude and only one has latitude, their distance is the latitude alone. If one has latitude and the other none, like the Sun or any star on the belt of the signs, the difference of longitude is taken as a first side, its half and half-chord are multiplied by the chord of the complement of the latitude and divided by the radius, the result is turned into an arc and doubled to make a second side, and from the two whole chords and the whole chord of the latitude a root is formed whose half is turned into an arc and doubled, which is the distance between the two stars. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.63-64}.

## When both have latitude
If both stars have latitude on one side and the two latitudes are equal, their distance is the arc of the second side; if the latitudes differ, the smaller is taken from the larger and the remainder is the third side, the fourth being equal to it. If the two latitudes are on different sides they are added and the sum is the third side. If both stars stand at one degree their distance is only the parts of latitude, and if neither has latitude the distance is the parts of longitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.64}.
