## The speaker's view: the state of Muslim homes today
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy asks whether Muslim homes today would please the Prophet and the great scholars, and says that some of them have become cramped, dark or lifeless. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:00:30}
He says that homes without mercy, affection and tranquillity become like prisons, and that each member expects the others to attack him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:43:00}
He holds that a safe home is built on gentleness, mutual respect, love and peace, and that each spouse should credit the other when there is need. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:45:30}

## Rest, affection and gratitude
He recounts a hadith that the best of people is the best to his family, and says that gratitude for children, a wife, wealth and work brings more blessing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:22:00}
He refers to a verse on the few grateful servants of God, and gives the family of David as an example of thankfulness (needs_source: the sura is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:12:30}

## Secrets of the home and the gathering
The Sheikh says that secrets spoken in a gathering are a trust, and that only what carries useful knowledge may be repeated. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:07:30}
He says that homes and gatherings have their secrets, and that people should not carry news of gatherings outside. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:23:00}
He recounts a hadith that one of the gravest betrayals is for a spouse to pass on the private matters of the other, and says this applies to husbands as much as to wives. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:22:00}
He cites a hadith that among the greatest betrayals is a husband confiding a secret to his wife and her spreading it, and he gives the wives of Noah and Lot as examples of betraying a secret (needs_source: the hadith is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:08:00}
He says that people should be left to worship God and to live as they choose, and that interrogating them pushes them to lie. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:09:30}

## The speaker's view: spying, suspicion and privacy
The Sheikh criticises spying and prying into people's salaries, purchases and origins, and says that such curiosity betrays a trust. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:06:30}
He criticises relatives on both sides who ask spouses about each other's affairs, money and having children, and says people should be left to live. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:08:30}
He cites the Qur'anic prohibition of spying and a saying of the scholars that when doubt arises one should not investigate (needs_source: the sura is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:38:30}
He says that every spouse who spies brings evil upon his home. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:40:00}
He says that modern devices used to watch a spouse lead to suspicion and bad outcomes, and he urges trust as the foundation of marriage. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:39:00}
He recounts a hadith about a man who looked into a home through a gap, and the Prophet's rebuke of him (needs_source: the hadith is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep21 00:35:30}

## Relatives, in-laws and the family
The Sheikh criticises families who ask a suitor about his job, his salary, his house and his mother, and says that meddling in-laws are the greatest danger to new homes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:08:30}
He says that whoever cuts off his kin loses blessing in his lifespan, his children and his provision. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:29:00}
He urges people to review their wills, and warns that sixty years of good deeds can be undone by one unjust bequest; he says that families who take the rights of others lose their blessing (needs_source: the figure sixty is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:28:30}
He criticises those who complain about prices, the weather, wives, children, in-laws and work, and says this is not beautiful patience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:37:30}

## Hospitality in the home
The Sheikh says that Abraham hurried to prepare a calf for his guests, and that scholars of etiquette treat this as a model of hospitality. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:10:30}
He reports that Ibn Mas'ud loved the guest most, because his provision is with God and the guest brings reward (needs_source: the report is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:11:30}
He recounts from the Qur'anic story, without naming the sura, that Abraham served his guests himself and brought the food close to them, and he takes this as the sign of a host's service to his guest. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep19 00:18:00}
He says that a visitor should not overstay, and quotes an imam, not named in the recording, who said that he never sat with a heavy-spirited person without feeling the weight of it (needs_source: the imam is not named). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:23:30}

## Lawful provision and patience at home
The Sheikh says that a man must feed his family from lawful earnings, and he criticises bribery, seizure of property, misuse of office and taking people's rights. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:26:30}
He tells that after the death of Jabir ibn Abdullah's son, his wife took charge of a matter of a trust, a neighbour's property returned to its owners, and he praises her patience and her good management, so that grief did not spread (needs_source: the story is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:26:30}
He says that voluntary prayer should be made at home, and that zakat may be declared openly while charity should be given in secret. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:34:00}
