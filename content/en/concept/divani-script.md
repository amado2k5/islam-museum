## Summary
Maryam Ekhtiar writes that the main chancery scripts of the [[polity/ottoman-empire|Ottoman Empire]] were the [[concept/ottoman-tughra|tughra]] and divani. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 27}

## Features
Divani — literally "of the [imperial] council" — was the script used in the body of firmans; she describes it as intricate, compact and very difficult to read, its letters and words intertwining in a slight upward slope reminiscent of a ship's bow. The chancery in the Topkapı Palace, Istanbul, drew up and recorded the firmans and official letters, aiming to make documents aesthetically superb to reflect the sultan's power. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 45} The author counts divani among the scripts that arose for administration, some purposely hard to read. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26} Iranian [[concept/shikasta-script|shikasta-nasta'liq]] resembles it in the stacked, rising words at line ends. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 47}
