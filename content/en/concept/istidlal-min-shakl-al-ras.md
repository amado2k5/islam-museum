## Shape
He says that the chapter on the bone of the skull has already made known what the natural shape of the head is and what the bad shape is {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.19} and that badness, when it falls in a part of the head, certainly harms the particular acts of that part of the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.19}

He reports from Galen that the shape he calls musaffat, and the square, are always disapproved, and the one projecting at both ends is disapproved unless the cause is a strength of the forming power that over-acted, the strength of this power being shown by the shape and size of the neck and the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.19} The editor's footnote glosses musaffat as a vessel from which women pour perfume {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.19}
