## Summary
Does the Qur'an contain words that come from outside the tongue of the Arabs? [[person/al-suyuti|Al-Suyuti]] gathered the scholars' views on it in al-Itqan. The majority, among them [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] and [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]], hold that it does not; others hold that it does; and Abu Ubayd joined the two views. The page gives each view under its holders and does not judge between them.

## The question
Al-Suyuti says in al-Itqan, under the type "what occurs in it from outside the language of the Arabs", that he gave the question its own book, al-Muhadhdhab fima waqa'a fi al-Qur'an min al-Mu'arrab, and then summarised that the imams differed on whether the mu'arrab occurs in the Qur'an {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.125}. The mu'arrab, as Abu Ubayd describes it, is a word whose origin is foreign, which reached the Arabs, who pronounced it with their own tongues and turned it into their own words.

## Those who denied that it occurs
Al-Suyuti states that the majority hold it does not occur, among them al-Shafi'i, Ibn Jarir, Abu Ubayda, al-Qadi Abu Bakr and Ibn Faris, who argue from [[passage/quran-12-2|Yusuf 2]] and [[passage/quran-41-44|Fussilat 44]], and that al-Shafi'i was severe in his rejection of whoever said it {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.125}.

In al-Risala, al-Shafi'i reports that someone said the Qur'an contains Arabic and non-Arabic, and answers that the Qur'an shows that nothing of the Book of God is other than in the tongue of the Arabs {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.41}. He explains the opponent's saying by supposing he meant that part of the Qur'an is rare and some Arabs do not know it: the tongue of the Arabs is the widest of tongues and the richest in words, and no one but a prophet encompasses it, yet nothing of it escapes all of them, as the sunan are with the jurists {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.42}. If a non-Arab pronounces something of the Arabs' tongue, he either learned it from them or the two languages coincided, and diverse tongues rarely coincide {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.44}. He then says God confirmed that the Book is Arabic by negating every tongue other than Arabic in two verses, [[passage/quran-16-103|al-Nahl 103]] and [[passage/quran-41-44|Fussilat 44]] {cite:risala-shafii-shakir-1938 p.47}.

Al-Suyuti reports from Abu Ubayda that whoever claims the Qur'an has other than Arabic has made a grave statement, and he gives as example the word for liar in Nabataean. From Ibn Faris: had it contained non-Arabic words, someone might think the Arabs failed only because it brought languages they did not know. From Ibn Jarir: what Ibn Abbas and others said about words being Persian, Abyssinian or Nabataean is only a coincidence of languages. Others said the Arabs mixed with other peoples in their journeys, took some words and used them until they became eloquent Arabic, and the Qur'an came down with them so. Still others said all its words are Arabic, but the tongue is so wide that part of it may be hidden from the great: Ibn Abbas did not know the meaning of fatir and fatih. They cite al-Shafi'i's statement that none but a prophet encompasses the Arabs' tongue {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.125} {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.126}.

## Those who held that it occurs
Al-Suyuti states that others held that it occurs. They answered the verse of Yusuf by saying that a few non-Arabic words do not change that the speech is Arabic, as a Persian poem with an Arabic word in it stays Persian. They read the verse of Fussilat as a question: a foreign speech and an Arab addressee? And they cite the grammarians' refusal of full declension to Ibrahim because of its being a name and foreign. Al-Suyuti says the strongest argument he saw for occurrence, and his own choice, is what Ibn Jarir reports with a sound chain from Abu Maysara the Successor, that the Qur'an has something from every tongue, and the like from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Wahb ibn Munabbih {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.126}.

## Abu Ubayd's reconciliation
Al-Suyuti quotes Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam, after mentioning the jurists' view of occurrence and the grammarians' denial of it: "The right view, to my mind, is one in which both sayings are confirmed." He means that these words are foreign in origin, as the jurists said, but reached the Arabs, who arabised them and turned them into their own words so that they became Arabic; then the Qur'an came down when they had mixed with their speech, so whoever says they are Arabic is truthful and whoever says they are foreign is truthful {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.128}. Al-Suyuti then gives an alphabetical list of words; its first entry is a word that al-Tha'alibi in Fiqh al-Lugha reports as Persian and al-Jawaliqi as Persian and arabised {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.129}.

## In the commentaries
In a report of Sa'id ibn Jubayr that al-Tabari transmits, Quraysh asked why the Qur'an was not sent down both foreign and Arabic, so God sent down in it words from every tongue, among them sijjil, found in [[passage/quran-11-82|Hud 82]] and [[passage/quran-15-74|al-Hijr 74]], which in Persian is sang and gil. Al-Tabari himself prefers the reading as a question {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 41:44}. Al-Qurtubi adds from Sa'id that among such words are firdaws and qistas, which he says are Greek {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 41:44}.

At [[passage/quran-20-1|Ta-Ha 1]], al-Tabari lists the views of the early authorities: "O man" in Nabataean from Ibn Abbas and Ikrima, in Syriac from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Qatada; he chooses "O man" because the word is known among Akk {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 20:1}. Ibn Kathir quotes Abu Salih that it is arabised {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 20:1}. Al-Qurtubi judges: "The correct view is that even if it is found in another language, it is from the Arabs' language", a Yemeni dialect among Akk, Tayyi' and Ukl {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 20:1}.

Under the type "what occurs in other than the Hijazi language", al-Suyuti lists words said to be Yemeni, among them one that Ikrima and Ibn Abbas explained as singing, one that al-Hasan said a man from Yemen told them means the canopy with the couch, and one that al-Dahhak said is Yemeni {cite:itqan-abu-al-fadl-1974 2 p.106}.

## What this page does not reach
Al-Suyuti's book al-Muhadhdhab was not read, and of his alphabetical list only its first entry was read; the books of Ibn Jarir, Abu Ubayda and Abu Ubayd themselves were not read, only their words as al-Suyuti reports them. The chain of Abu Maysara was not examined. Only the first page of the type on non-Hijazi words was read.
