## Summary
Hay'a in the lexical sense is the outward state a thing is in, sensed or understood. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16574} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16575}

## Kinds of prayer acts for the Shafi'is
The Shafi'is divide the acts of prayer into pillars, the sunan that sahw prostration repairs, and the hay'at that it does not repair. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16575} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16576}
The prayer is likened to a person: the pillar to the head, the condition to life, the sunan to the limbs and the hay'at to the hair. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16576}

## Examples of hay'at
By the Shafi'i count there are more than fifteen, including raising the hands at the opening takbir, at bowing and at rising from it, placing the right palm on the left, seeking refuge before recitation, saying amin, glorification in bowing and prostration, and the postures of sitting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16576} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16577} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16578}
The Hanbalis count matters of manner: the fingers, placing the right hand over the left, the gaze at the place of prostration, and measured recitation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16579} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16580}

## Discipline of the dignified
Most jurists lighten the punishment of the dignified, the people of religion and piety, relying on the saying 'pardon the lapses of the dignified except in the hadd punishments'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16580} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16581}
Some admonish but do not discipline a first lapse; Ibn Abd al-Salam of the Shafi'is holds the dignified are not punished at all, and al-Adhra'i disputes him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16581} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16584}
