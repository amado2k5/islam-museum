## Nature
It is said to be badhawurd, growing in mountains and thickets, with a leaf like the leaf of the white khamalaun except that it is finer and whiter, with something like gold on it, and thorny; it has a stalk longer than two cubits and as thick as a thumb, white and hollow, with a thorny head at its tip like the thorns of the sea urchin but smaller and elongated; it has a flower the colour of purple, a seed like the safflower seed but rounder, and a red root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Temperament
Cold and dry in the first degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Actions and properties
It is said that when hung up in a place it drives away vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Swellings and pustules
Its root is used as a poultice for phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Organs of the head
Its root, when cooked and gargled with the decoction, is good for toothache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Joints
Its decoction benefits gout {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Organs of the chest
Its root, when drunk, is good for spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits slackness of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Excretory organs
Its root, when drunk, benefits chronic diarrhoea and promotes urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits the stinging of vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.684}
