## Its reality
This state is a sound a man keeps hearing with no outside cause; its measure against hearing is the measure of the phantasms and darknesses the eye sees with no outside cause. Since sound's cause is a waving arising in air conveyed to the sense, this occurrence of ringing and buzzing must involve a movement of air; and that air, not being outside air, is the inner air — the vapour poured into the cavities {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.225}
## Its causes
This waving is either faint, scarce known, or more. If faint and of the kind bodies can scarce be free of, yet some bodies hear ringing and buzzing from its like and some do not, then it is either from acuteness of the sense in some and not others — as was said of imagining phantasms — or from its weakness, being affected by the least waving as the weak are chilled by the least cold and heated by the least heat; the kinds of weakness are the known kinds of bad temperament. If beyond the faint, beyond what differs between strong and weak, then its cause is a mover waving the vapour beyond the usual moving: either a wind breeding in the head region moving in it, or a hissing of pus breeding in it, a boiling of pus in its parts, or the movement of worms occurring often in its passages {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.225}
## The causes behind these, and its seat
The cause behind these causes is either a turmoil boiling the whole body's humours as in fevers, or an extreme repletion in the body or specially in the head as after much drunkenness, or a turmoil tending to the brain specially as after violent vomiting or after a collision or blow; or not from turmoil of movement but a sticky matter dissolving a little wind so it persists; or from great emptiness — a turmoil in the scattered moisture of the body when nature finds no nourishment and is set on dissolving and stirring it; and the ringing may follow drugs whose way is to confine humours and winds. This ringing may be in the ear itself or by sharing of the stomach and other organs sending these winds to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.225} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.226}
## Its signs
Of the continuous constant kind, the cause is settled in the head; if it quiets then stirs with repletion, emptiness or movement, and at worsening heat or cold, it is by sharing. The sound's shape tells it: like a thing boiling upward — mostly by sharing of body or stomach; or like a thing turning on itself, a rustling of trees — that shows a settled wind; with fever and pain leading to rigor it shows pus gathered; if it forms by continuous faint growth upon growth it is a viscid humour. The kind from acuteness of sense is told by absence of the causes of winds and repletion, hearing remaining, and its stirring at emptiness and hunger; the kind from dryness comes after evacuations and fevers; the kind from weakness is known from past excesses and may come with a hot temperament suddenly and with inflammation, the cold oppositely {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.226}
