## Definition
A saff is a row of worshippers in prayer, one behind another; straightening it means packing the row so there is no gap or defect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10397}

## Straightening and completing the first row
The majority recommend straightening rows; some hold it obligatory, and Ibn Hajar says the prayer of one who does not straighten is still valid. Among the duties is completing the first row before the next, and no one should stand in a row while the row in front of him is incomplete. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10398} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10401}

## The best rows; women and children
The first rows are best for men; women's best rows are the first if they pray without men, and the last if men are present. The imam stands in the middle of the row, with men first, then boys, then women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10399} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10400}

## The row in battle and at a funeral
A fighter obliged to fight may not leave the line when the two lines meet, except to manoeuvre or to join a body of Muslims, and this holds only while the enemy does not exceed twice the number. Rows at a funeral prayer are to be straightened, and not fewer than three. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10402} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10403}
