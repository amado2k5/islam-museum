## Summary
In the lexicon kataf is the broad bone behind the shoulder; it is feminine and applies to humans and other creatures {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13211}. The legal sense does not depart from the lexical one {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13211}.

## Retaliation for cutting the arm at the shoulder
Most jurists hold that retaliation applies to cutting the arm at the shoulder joint, provided that a deep wound into the body is not feared. If it is feared, the injured party may take retaliation at the elbow, as the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold, since he takes what is possible of his right and may take compensation {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13211} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13212}.

## Breaking the shoulder blade
There is no retaliation for breaking the shoulder blade, as for other bones, and no fixed compensation; an assessed sum is owed instead. Its detail belongs to the entries on injuries short of death {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13212}.

## Related rules
- Sadl in prayer: according to the Hanbalis, it is throwing a cloth over both shoulders without bringing one end over the other shoulder; they dislike it, and require the worshipper to put some garment over one shoulder if able, which they make a condition of valid prayer in the preferred view {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13212}.
- Idtiba' in circumambulation: the pilgrim puts his upper garment under his right arm and throws it over his left shoulder, leaving the right shoulder bare; most jurists recommend it in the arrival circumambulation {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13212}.
