## Summary
Nafar, in the lexicon, is setting out or leaving. The pilgrims depart from Mina to Mecca when they finish there. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15855}

## Rulings on the departure
A pilgrim has two departures and may choose either. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15855}
The first departure falls on the second day of the Tashriq. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis require leaving Mina before sunset, the Hanafis before dawn of the third day. A pilgrim who does not leave must stone the three pillars on the third day and spend that night at Mina. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15855}
The second departure is on the third day of the Tashriq, after stoning the three pillars; the rites at Mina end and all pilgrims go to Mecca. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15855} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15856}
Remembrance and supplication are recommended on the way, and so is stopping at al-Muhassab to pray. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15856}
The Qur'an uses the word for seeking knowledge and going out to fight (surat al-Tawba, aya 122 and aya 41, cited only). Al-Qurtubi and others treat seeking knowledge as recommended rather than obligatory in this sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15856}
