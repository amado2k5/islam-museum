## Summary
The programme asks whether the "conspiracy theory" explains the decline the nation lives through. Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy holds that the first cause of defeat is distance from God and from the ethics of "la ilaha illa Allah", that plots exist, but that a nation with immunity is not overcome by them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:48:30}

## What the programme asks
The programme asks whether plots and intrigues affected the decline of the early Muslims, and the Sheikh says he will address the question. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:42:30}
A questioner also asks whether a conspiracy caused the Muslims' defeat in Iraq. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:39:00}

## A saying of the Sheikh
The Sheikh gives a saying he asks the audience to memorise: "Do not think that the enemy has prevailed; it is the friend who has turned away". He explains that the enemy here stands for Satan, whose cunning is weak. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:37:00}

## Distance from God
The Sheikh holds that the cause of the nation's defeat is its distance from its Lord and from the ethics of "la ilaha illa Allah", and that a nation that wants to return from its wandering must return to them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:18:30}

## Immunity
The Sheikh holds that the nation today lacks the immunity the Companions had against plots and intrigues. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:48:30}
He compares this to the body, where a small virus kills a body whose immunity has weakened, and says that defeat came from within, citing the Tatar threat at Damascus. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:57:00}

## In Al-Wasatiyya: conspiracy theory, fact or fancy?
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] devoted the fortieth episode of his programme [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|Al-Wasatiyya]], the last of the programme's episodes in the Assabile archive, to the question: is the conspiracy theory fact, or closer to fancy? He said it is an issue much talked about in the street but little discussed in dialogue and theory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:00:30}

### How the presenter framed it
The presenter said the struggle between truth and falsehood, good and evil, is old, and that the great events that follow one another upon the umma result from that struggle and from the clashing and converging interests of people and states, which he considers natural. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:01:30} He then set out the other view: that these events are not spontaneous action and reaction but parts of a grand conspiracy planned by the West in particular to dominate the umma and stop what it calls "the green peril", and that those who hold this view have evidence they consider scriptural or documentary. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:02:30} The main guest was Ziyad Abu Ghanima, whom the presenter introduced as a preacher, author and veteran journalist on whose books, he said, he had learned. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:03:00}

### The first poll
The presenter asked the studio audience: is the conspiracy theory fact or fancy? with only two choices. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:04:00} 89% voted fact and 11% fancy; the presenter said the result surprised him and that he had hoped for another. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:06:00} Abu Ghanima welcomed the result as further evidence that the umma has been targeted by a conspiracy since the dawn of Islam. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:07:00} These are the votes of that episode's audience, not a representative survey.

### Ziyad Abu Ghanima's evidence
Abu Ghanima began with what he called tangible evidence: the chain of blood from [[person/sumayya-bint-khayyat|Sumayya]], whom he called the first martyr of Islam, then [[person/yasir-ibn-amir|Yasir]], then the martyrs of Bi'r Ma'una, al-Raji' and [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], down to the Inquisition and the Crusades; was all that accident, he asked, or a conspiracy aimed at the whole religion? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:09:00} The presenter objected that these events came from different parties, that a conspiracy theory means one party planning and moving events, and that no single party stands behind all of it except Iblis. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:10:00}

Abu Ghanima then cited verses that in his reading show the religion itself is the target, that the enemies are many and succeed one another, and that the most dangerous of them are the hypocrites from within the umma. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:13:00} He said God Himself called it a conspiracy, citing the verses on scheming (makr), including its description as "mighty", that is, excessive. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:15:30} The presenter asked why a state such as America pursuing its interests, while also pressing Korea and China, should count as a conspiracy against us alone. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:12:00} He answered that other nations have no Islam to be destroyed, so the umma is targeted because its religion is, and that Europe's wars were mostly religious, not economic. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:30:30} Who, he asked, stops the West from getting its interests from us peacefully, and why does it insist on occupying Palestine, Iraq and Afghanistan if there is no conspiracy? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:28:00}

He read the verse "whatever misfortune befalls you" as concerning what a person brings on himself by lack of obedience, not what befalls the umma; but he granted that the mistakes of some of the umma's own people give enemies pretexts, citing the hypocrites' wavering at the [[event/battle-of-the-trench|Battle of the Trench]], which emboldened the besiegers. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:32:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:32:30} He allowed that an adversary engineering our mistake happens in particular cases, not generally; the rule, for him, is that they scheme night and day. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:33:00} Asked whether this talk does not sow despair among the young, he said that on the contrary it pushes the umma to confront the conspiracy, and that the umma does not fear their schemes. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:33:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:02:00}

Later he cited Miles Copeland's *The Game of Nations*, saying its author belonged to the first intelligence network in Cairo, whose aim was to keep Islamists from ruling Egypt; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:52:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:52:30} a statement he attributed to Zbigniew Brzezinski, national security adviser under Carter, on assumptions of US policy towards Muslims; and Carter's order in 1979 to form a CIA committee to study Islamic movements. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:53:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:53:30} These are his account and his attributions, not checked here (needs_source).

### The Protocols, and documents that appear after the event
Asked about the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion*, he said some deny the book, but that what happens in the media, in his view, confirms its content, and he told a story attributing the founder of the Reuters agency to the Jews (his account, needs_source). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:54:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:55:00} The presenter said he had researched Palestine and written a book on it, was now writing a detailed book on the Jews, and had found no researcher in the umma more exact or comprehensive on the question than Dr Abd al-Wahhab al-Messiri; if we reject scholarly research, he asked, what do we rely on? Abu Ghanima answered that he relies on what is in the Protocols having come true. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:56:30} The academic view on the book's origin has not yet been added here.

The presenter then pressed a question several times: why do the documents of a conspiracy only appear after the event, such as a film said to date from the 1980s and a book from 1986, and why are the documents of coming conspiracies not published now? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:57:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:58:30} Abu Ghanima mentioned a plan "to Balkanise the region" which he said a man, since deceased, distributed in 1981, and which he said matches the sectarianism now in Lebanon and Iraq; then he said plainly that he had no answer to the question. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:59:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:59:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:01:00} He added that American studies told them Iraqis would greet them with flowers, and the Iraqis met them with resistance, so not all their studies are right. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:02:00}

- **Khaled Hroub**: voted that there is no conspiracy in the sense of the question, secret rooms charting the whole world against Muslims, while accepting scattered strategic planning; he called the dispute over the name verbal, and said Muslims also plan to spread their call in Europe and America, which others call a conspiracy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:22:30} He called the loss of Palestine strategic planning to strike the Arab and Muslim worlds, which he did not mind calling a conspiracy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:23:00} He argued that the two world wars killed about fifty million, mostly non-Muslims, and that the slave trade carried, in his figure, five to ten million Africans to America, to show that not everything in the world is aimed at Muslims, and he urged leaving behind "relishing the feeling of victimhood". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:24:00}

- **Ahmad Azm (researcher in international relations)**: reported that a BBC poll about twelve months earlier, of Arabs inside and outside the Arab world, found about 49% saying there is an international conspiracy and 51% denying it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:39:00} He said that studying decision-making in each crisis is what shows whether there was plotting, that in recent events officials in some Western states plotted against their own peoples, to pass a decision or for an election, more than against us, and that the giant conspiracy in dark rooms is unrealistic. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:39:30} He called for "Occidental studies" as a counterpart to [[concept/orientalism|Orientalism]], studying the West, China and Japan exactly and realistically, without exaggeration. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:11:30}
- **Yasser Bushnaq (head of a research centre in Washington)**: the presenter introduced him as a colleague he lived alongside for years in Washington, of American and Jordanian nationality. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:47:00} He said relations between Arabs and Muslims in America and the Bush administration began well, that their votes in Florida were decisive in the 2000 election in his account, that American institutions are advanced in planning and execution and have research centres that exploit events and turn obstacles into opportunities, and that the September 2001 attacks were not started by any responsible American party, contrary to what is spread. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:49:00} He held that Muslims need to rise through planning and to found research centres, that the pressures on Muslims in America have political and security causes, and that Islam and the West share points of contact such as freedom of expression, which would benefit Islam more than anything else. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:07:00}

### The second poll and the young people's views
The presenter asked: were the great events that recently struck the umma made by Muslims and exploited by the West, or planned by the West both as act and as exploitation? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:36:00} 22% voted that we made them and 78% that they planned the event and its exploitation; the presenter said the episode is not political but intellectual: to see how the young think. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:37:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:38:00}

{cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:41:30} another defined conspiracy as planning by unclean means; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:42:00} a young woman questioned the BBC poll's method given uneven internet access, and a young man said Muslims themselves gave up Palestine just as others plotted to take it; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:45:00} a participant held that hanging defeats on conspiracy began with setbacks such as 1967, when those responsible blamed outsiders; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:46:30} a participant said talk of conspiracy supplies the "challenge factor" a revival needs; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:08:30} Dr Shaker said hostility to Islam has existed since the Prophet's mission, but the term has swollen in this period of weakness until conspiracy became a peg to hang problems on; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:09:30} and a young woman counted pornographic channels and harmful websites as a conspiracy against the young, to which Ahmad Azm replied that they are destructive but a market, not targeting, and the presenter added that some people simply want profit. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:11:00}

### The presenter's conclusion and Abu Ghanima's last word
Midway, the presenter said he lived seventeen years in America over thirty-five years of contact with it since the Nixon years, and that they have reached such mastery in planning and in exploiting reactions that an observer thinks they made the act itself, so the young are not to be blamed for that belief. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:50:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 00:51:30} Then he asked: if their conspiracies do not frighten us and can fail, what is the use of living in a conspiracy mentality? He called for facing ourselves, treating our weak points and planning for our interests as they plan for theirs. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:04:30}

In his last word Abu Ghanima said talk of conspiracy will not end while Muslims recite the verses on scheming, and that whoever claims the Americans sincerely want to spread democracy misleads, citing their stance on the results of the Palestinian elections. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:14:30}

The presenter closed by saying that those who promote the conspiracy theory and those who reject it share one aim: that the umma should move, become aware, plan and study, not despair or fear. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:15:30} How many research centres do we have compared with theirs, he asked, and how many of us know Hebrew as they know Arabic? They may seek their interests; we have interests, a religion and principles too, and the fault is to remain in an emotional mentality with no achievement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:16:00} He recalled that on returning from the West he found many people speaking about the umma's problems with anguish and few working to solve them, so he decided to offer a distinguished model in education instead of complaining about it, and the same in media, and he urged the young to live "a mentality of giving and production" rather than of grumbling and complaint. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep40 01:17:00}
