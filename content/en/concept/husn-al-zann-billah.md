## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy defines istibshar as expecting good: a person is glad at a righteous child, a good deed, or an obedient daughter. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep10 00:16:00} He criticises the pessimist who sees only thorns and desert, and says that the Muslim community should be optimistic and active. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:04:30}

## The speaker's view: good tidings and expecting good
He recounts the angels bringing Abraham good news of Isaac, and then of Jacob after him, and holds that bushra means that something good has appeared. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep10 00:17:30}
He says that Jacob hoped for God's mercy and was answered, and that a person cannot know where good lies, so should be patient. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:13:00}
He says that Jacob knew God would save Joseph, and that his hope returned when his second son was lost, until the vision was fulfilled. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:31:00}

## The speaker's view: good opinion of God in provision and ownership
Asked about the effect of good opinion of God on provision and ownership, the Sheikh leaves the knowledge to God and says that God knows best. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:33:30}

## The speaker's view: criticism of pessimism and omens
He says that the Arabs used to take omens of good and ill from the flight of birds, and that Islam replaced this with the prayer of istikhara (needs_source: the description of the pre-Islamic practice is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep10 00:04:30}
He contrasts the one who dwells in grief over the past and seeks posts through bribery or what is forbidden with the one who makes matters easy, because he knows that destiny is just and merciful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:30:30}
He cites a hadith about destiny that is used to ground optimism. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:07:30} He holds that despair is not a quality of the believer, and that when hardship grows relief is near. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:11:30}

## The speaker's view: hope and action
He holds that a believer should always expect good, banish depression, do good, and say that tomorrow will be better. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:35:30}
He recounts Ali saying that he lives on hope: if it is fulfilled, that is by God's favour, and if not, he has still lived with it (needs_source: the saying is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:34:30}
He says that one who loses trust in a thing becomes hopeless, while the believer who prays stands in God's presence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:12:00}
