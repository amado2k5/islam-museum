## Brief
The extract of *Ighathat al-Lahfan* gives the view of [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]] on the veneration of graves: praying at them, supplicating and seeking help from their occupants, circling them, building on them and lighting lamps beside them. The author holds that this veneration is the root of idol worship and leads to shirk {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.2}.

## How the author describes the trial
He holds that the trial of graves appeared first among the people of Noah, who devoted themselves to the graves of their righteous men and then made images of them. They combined the trial of graves with the trial of images, which the author treats as the two trials the Prophet warned against {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.2} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.3}. He cites hadith against taking graves as mosques and the report that earlier communities took the graves of their prophets as places of worship {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.3}.

## The arguments in the extract
- Prayer at graves is forbidden. The author claims the Muslims agree on this, and says that praying there resembles prostrating to idols {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.4}.
- The prohibition is not because of the impurity of the graveyard but because of the shirk that attaches to whoever prays there. The author rejects the view that it applies only to graves that have been dug up {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.4}.
- No Companion or Successor is reported to have sought help or intercession at a grave. The author argues that if prayer at graves had any merit, it would have been transmitted {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.15} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.16}.
- He reports that Umar ibn al-Khattab saw Anas ibn Malik praying at a grave and objected, and that Umar cut down the tree under which the Companions had given the pledge once people began visiting it {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.15} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.16}.

## Stages of the trap
Quoting a shaykh of his, the author describes Satan leading people in stages: first a supplication made at the grave, then swearing by its occupant, then seeking help from him, then building, guarding and vowing at it, and finally making it a festival and a place of rites {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.18} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.19}.

## For the reader
This is the author's position, presented as his. The academic view and the views of other jurists on visiting graves and praying beside them are not yet added here; they need their own sources.
