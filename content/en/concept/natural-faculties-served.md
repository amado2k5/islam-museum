## Summary
Ibn Sina divides the natural faculties into serving and served; the served are two genera: one governing food for the survival of the individual, with the nutritive and growing faculties, and one for the survival of the species, with the generative and moulding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.95}.

## Nutritive and growing
The nutritive faculty turns food to resemble the one nourished, to replace what dissolves. The growing faculty enlarges the body's dimensions in natural proportion so that it reaches full growth by the food entering it, and the nutritive serves the growing. Growth occurs only when what comes in exceeds what dissolves, but not everything of that kind is growth: fattening after emaciation at the age of standing is not growth, and growth is only what keeps natural proportion in all dimensions until full growth is reached, after which there is no growth at all, though there may be fattening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.95}.

## The three acts of the nutritive
The nutritive faculty completes its work in three partial acts. The first is acquiring the body's substance, which is blood and the humour that is potentially near to resembling the organ; it fails in the condition called atrophia, the lack of food. The second is adhesion, making that product food in full actuality, that is a part of an organ; it fails in fleshy dropsy. The third is assimilation, making it, once a part of the organ, like it in every respect, even in consistency and colour; it fails in leprosy and vitiligo, where substitution and adhesion are present but assimilation is not. This act belongs to the altering faculty, which is one in humans in genus and differs in species among the similar organs, since each organ by its temperament has a power that alters food to a likeness different from another's; but the altering faculty in the liver has an act common to the whole body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.95-96}.

## Generative and moulding
The generative faculty is of two kinds: one generates sperm in males and females, the other separates the power in the sperm and mixes it in mixtures suited to each organ, giving nerve one temperament, bone another and the arteries another, and physicians call this the altering faculty. The moulding, imprinting faculty is that from which, by its Creator's leave, come the outlining and shaping of organs, their cavities, holes, smoothness, roughness, positions and associations, and in sum the acts bound up with the limits of their sizes. The servants of this faculty, which governs food for the keeping of the species, are the nutritive and growing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.96}.
