## Summary
"The study of followers" (followership) is a new branch of leadership science as [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] presents it in episodes 5 to 8 of his programme [[work/asrar-al-qiyada-al-nabawiyya-program|"Secrets of Prophetic Leadership"]]: there is no leadership without followers and no bad leader without bad followers, so the follower is studied as the leader is. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep03 00:03:00} A follower, for him, is a subordinate with less authority than his superior, or in the general sense anyone who is not the leader. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:11:00} He says Barbara Kellerman's two books *Bad Leadership* and *Followership* are among the few research-based books on the subject, and that neither topic has been studied in Arabic. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:27:30}

## Tools for moving people, and the goal
Leadership for him is moving people towards a goal, and every person has a "key" that moves him; the failed leader uses one key, money or threats, on everyone. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:02:30} The main tools for moving people are five: enticement, intimidation, oratory, persuasion and example; persuasion, he says, was the Prophet's chief tool because its effect outlasts the leader. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:04:30} The followers need not know the goal, nor need it be a good one: he cites the followers of Uyayna ibn Hisn, who moved with him "without asking why or where". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:08:00}

## The follower and the imitator
In a model of his own, al-Suwaidan distinguishes the follower from the imitator, though both count as followers: {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:14:30}
- The follower asks for proof and evidence; for the imitator proof is secondary. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:14:30}
- The follower treats the means as open to change; the imitator makes them sacred. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:15:00}
- The follower criticises a mistake even when it comes from the leader; the imitator gives in to it. He shows a filmed experiment in which, he says, 37% of participants gave in to the mistake entirely. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:19:30}
- The follower judges leaders rationally; the imitator emotionally. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:19:30}
- The follower is not swayed by the collective mind; the imitator goes with the current. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:23:30}
- The follower takes the initiative because he has understood the goal; the imitator waits for orders. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:24:00}
- The follower keeps developing his skills; the imitator's skills stay as he was taught. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:24:00}

## Why people follow
He then sets out a model of the reasons people follow a leader: fear, example, interests, hope, order and stability, persuasion, oratory and charisma. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:27:30} An example, for him, is someone whose words match his deeds and both match his values. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:29:00} He reports Peter Drucker as saying that in more than fifty years with leaders he never found charisma to be a condition of leadership. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:32:30} Every leader is also a follower, he says; a leader sometimes takes account of his followers, but his place is at the front. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:35:30} One of his examples is the Prophet's leaving the Ka'ba unrebuilt on Abraham's foundations out of regard for his people's recent entry into Islam. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:37:00} On the inner circle, he cites [[person/sad-ibn-muadh|Sa'd ibn Mu'adh]] and [[person/sad-ibn-ubada|Sa'd ibn Ubada]] at the Trench, who asked the Prophet whether his offer to Ghatafan was revelation or opinion and then turned it down; see [[concept/consultation-shura|shura]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep05 00:46:00}

## The eight kinds of follower
Western books, al-Suwaidan says, class followers by two criteria and arrive at four or five kinds; he uses three and arrives at eight: leadership ability; "challenge" in a positive sense, that is discussion, giving one's opinion and objecting in order to improve, not out of obstinacy; and effectiveness in the organisation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:08:00} He calls the model his own, not a translation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:02:30}
1. **The partner follower**: high leadership ability, no challenge, high effectiveness. Example: [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr al-Siddiq]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:28:00}
2. **The influential follower**: high on all three. Example: [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:50:30}
3. **The supporting follower**: high leadership ability, no challenge, limited effectiveness. Example: [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan ibn Harb]] after his conversion. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:33:00}
4. **The advising follower**: middling ability, high challenge, high effectiveness. Example: [[person/hubab-ibn-al-mundhir|al-Hubab ibn al-Mundhir]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:41:00}
5. **The committed follower**: low leadership ability, no challenge, high effectiveness. Example: [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr al-Ghifari]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:47:00}
6. **The obstinate follower**: low ability, high challenge, low effectiveness. Example: [[person/al-jadd-ibn-qays|al-Jadd ibn Qays]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:30:30}
7. **The arrogant follower**: high leadership ability, high challenge, nil or negative effectiveness. Example: [[person/abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Abdullah ibn Ubayy]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:46:30}
8. **The marginal follower**: low on all three, and the majority of people. He traces it to ignorance of the leadership's aims, weak interests, leaders' despotism, and weak leadership unable to make use of talent. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:48:30}

The model, he says, helps a leader know whom to rely on, to whom to delegate and whom to restrain. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:05:00} He distinguishes the soldier from the follower: "carry out, then discuss" is right in battle and crisis, but kept up in peacetime it produces imitators, in his view. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep07 00:06:30} "Challenge" is acceptable only together with effectiveness, he holds, so the critic with nothing to show is not treated like the productive critic. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep08 00:31:30} Among his ideas is "the first follower": leadership becomes effective only when he appears, and al-Suwaidan proposes it as a subject for research. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep06 00:32:00}
