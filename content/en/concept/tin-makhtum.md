## Nature
This earth is brought from a red hill at a place called Buhayra, named for its smooth flat ground with no grass and no rock. It is also called the priestly earth and the Kayhani ochre, and it is sold sealed. The account of the place is given on the word of one who saw it, and in earlier days only a priestess took it. Dioscorides says it is earth from a cave of that place, kneaded with goat's blood. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Selection
The best is the one with a smell of dill; it stops blood flowing from the mouth and sticks to the tongue. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Properties
Paul says there is no drug that cuts blood more; it is stronger than the Samos earth, so that the organs cannot bear its strength when there is a very hot swelling, especially in soft parts. It is cooling and drying. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Swellings and pustules
It helps at the start of hot swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Wounds and ulcers
It closes soft wounds and stubborn ulcers, prevents burns from ulcerating, and heals their sores. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Joints
It protects the limbs at a fall and helps them set, and it prevents humours from pouring into them. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Organs of the head
It prevents catarrh, and it prevents the flow of saliva and of gum bleeding. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Organs of the breath
It protects the inner organs at a fall, helps consumption by drying a lung ulcer, and also helps spitting of blood. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Excretory organs
It helps malignant chafing of the bowels when taken by mouth or as an enema, especially after the enema of honey water near to pure, followed by salt water. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502}

## Poisons
It resists poisons and bites, taken with wine and applied with vinegar. The pure earth, when drunk, makes one vomit the poison, especially when taken before it. Galen reports trying it on a mad dog's bite with wine and on a viper bite with vinegar. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.502} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.503}
