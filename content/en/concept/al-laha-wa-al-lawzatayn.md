## The rest of the regimen and the condition of cutting
With the astringent gargling the hot-water gargle must be kept up, for that readies it for the astringents' action and softens it and stops the astringents hardening it; if they leave it hardness or a painful wringing and contraction, the mucilages and what he described are used in it, and it is painted from outside with what has strong drying and astringency. If the two tonsils suppurate, the decoctions named in the quinsy chapter are used; if the pain lasts, purging is repeated, failing which the strongly resolving of what he described; and if it thins in one spot and thickens in another, whatever can be pushed back by rubbing and shrunk by what is lifted to it is preferable. And they must not be cut unless their root has withered — there is great danger in it (the cutting carried as the book's description, not a procedure). And this is a gargle drying and cleaning the ulcers of the naghânigh tumours, of what he described (the editor glossed al-naghânigh as the ear's flesh from the throat's side) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.298}
