## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat presents the "temptation of wealth" as one of the trials described in the Qur'an. Wealth sometimes brings good to its owner and sometimes brings woe and an evil end, and it is human greed and avarice that drive a person to do evil {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 137}. God placed temptation in wealth just as He placed it in spouses and children, and it has been Satan's greatest means of leading humans astray {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 137}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 138}.

## In the Religious and Moral Sphere
The author mentions three forms: preoccupation with wealth until it distracts the heart from remembrance of God, noting that hoarding dulls the heart so a person fails to reflect on the Hereafter {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 138}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 139}; love of extreme spending that departs from moderation or is not in the path of God {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 139}; and fondness for unethical financial dealings with others, such as shortchanging in measure and weight, riba, and bribery {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 140}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 141}.

## In the Economic Sphere
The author holds that a natural economy based on production makes money easy to manage, because the consumer cares about the availability of goods before the abundance of money, whereas in his view the present situation is the reverse, with goods having become subordinate to money {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 142}. He adds that production declined in societies after oil, and that society has reached a state of goods shortages and frozen capital {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 144}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 145}.
