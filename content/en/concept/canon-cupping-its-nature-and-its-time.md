## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter 21 with cupping, comparing it with venesection: it cleanses the skin regions more and draws thin blood better, it helps little in thick-blooded bodies, and he sets its best time in the month and in the day {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.299-300}.

## Compared with venesection
Cupping cleanses the regions of the skin more than venesection does and draws thin blood out more than thick. Its benefit in heavy bodies of thick blood is small, because it does not bring their blood out or draw it as it should, and it causes weakness in the organ that is cupped {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.

## Its time in the month and the day
Cupping is not ordered at the beginning of the month, because the humours have not yet moved or surged, nor at its end, because they have diminished, but in the middle of the month, when the humours surge in step with the growing light in the body of the moon, as the brain swells in the skull and the waters in rivers with tides. Its best hours in the day are the second and the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.

## The bath and people's dislike
Cupping after the bath is to be avoided, except for a person whose blood is thick: he bathes, waits an hour and is then cupped. Most people dislike cupping on the front of the body and fear harm from it to sensation and to the fat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.300}.
