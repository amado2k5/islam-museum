## Temperament
Its heating is between the second and third degree and its drying between the first and second; when burnt it becomes second in both {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Actions and properties
It ripens cold humours, soothes pains and disperses winds, and so does its oil; it has a marked softening, and its temperament is near that of the ripening and opening drugs but hotter; the fresh is stronger in ripening and the dry stronger in dissolving {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Swellings and pustules
It ripens swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Ulcers
Its ash benefits flabby ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Joints
Its oil benefits pains of the nerves and what resembles them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Organs of the head
It induces sleep, especially its oil, and its juice benefits the dark-humoured earache and the drying of the moisture of the ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Ocular organs
Constant eating of it weakens sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Organs of the chest
Shabath and its seed bring on milk, especially in the breasts that give much milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits the hiccup of fullness arising from the floating of food; Galen said that it harms the stomach, and in its seed there is an inducing of vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Excretory organs
It benefits colic, its seed cuts away growing haemorrhoids, and its ash is good for ulcers of the anus and of the male member {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it cuts off semen when used as an enema and by sitting in its water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.678}
