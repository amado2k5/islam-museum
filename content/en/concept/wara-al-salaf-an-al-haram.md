## Summary
This is a record of stories. It tells what the museum holds on the scruple of the Prophet, the Companions and the Successors before the unlawful and the doubtful, each account in a paragraph of its own: a fallen date the Prophet left for fear it was charity, his grandson al-Hasan and the date of the charity, Abu Bakr and food earned by soothsaying, and 'Umar and the milk of the charity camels. Sayings and reports on leaving what is harmless out of caution for what is harmful follow, each attributed to its source.

## In the Sunnah
A date the Prophet found. [[passage/bukhari-api-2055|Sahih al-Bukhari 2055]] relates from Anas that the Prophet passed a fallen date and said that had it not been that it might be charity he would have eaten it {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2055}. It adds that Hammam transmitted it from Abu Hurayra with the wording that he finds a fallen date on his bed {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2055}.

Al-Hasan and the date of the charity. [[passage/bukhari-api-1491|Sahih al-Bukhari 1491]] relates from Abu Hurayra that al-Hasan ibn 'Ali took a date from the dates of the charity and put it in his mouth, and the Prophet said 'kakh, kakh' so that he would throw it out, then said: did you not know that we do not eat charity {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 1491}.

Leaving what is harmless. [[passage/tirmidhi-api-2451|Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2451]] relates from 'Atiyya al-Sa'di, who was among the Companions of the Prophet, that the Messenger of God said a servant does not reach the rank of the God-fearing until he leaves what is harmless out of caution for what is harmful, and Abu 'Isa says it is hasan gharib, known only by this route {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 2451}.

## In the reports of the Companions, the Successors and the scholars
Abu Bakr and the food of soothsaying. 'A'isha says in [[passage/bukhari-api-3842|Sahih al-Bukhari 3842]] that Abu Bakr had a slave who brought him the revenue and he used to eat from it. One day the slave brought something and Abu Bakr ate of it, and the slave said, do you know what this is? What is it, said Abu Bakr. I practised soothsaying for a man in the Jahiliyya, he said, and I am not good at soothsaying, but I deceived him; then he met me and gave me this for it, and this is what you ate from. The recorded excerpt breaks off at what Abu Bakr then did {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3842}.

'Umar and the milk of the charity. [[passage/muwatta-api-606|Muwatta Malik 606]] relates from Aslam that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab drank some milk and liked it, and asked the one who gave it to him where the milk came from. The man told him that he had come to a watering place, which he named, and found some of the charity camels being watered; they milked some for him and he put it in his skin. 'Umar put his hand in and made himself vomit it {cite:muwatta-malik 606}.

Ahmad and a question of scruple. In [[passage/sahaba-safwat-safwa-halal-1-429|Sifat al-Safwa]], from Ahmad ibn 'Abdullah ibn Khalid, Ahmad ibn Hanbal was asked about a question of scruple and said that he sought God's forgiveness and that it was not lawful for him to speak on a question of scruple; the excerpt stops there {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.429}.

Malik ibn Dinar and the lawful date. Abu Nu'aym relates in the [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-2-371|Hilyat al-Awliya']] that Malik ibn Dinar used to say that to give a lawful date in charity was dearer to him than to give a hundred thousand of the forbidden {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.371}.

The heart and the trial. Abu Nu'aym relates that trials are presented to hearts: any heart that drinks them in has a black spot marked on it, and if it rejects them a white spot is marked on it; whoever wishes to know whether the trial has struck him should look, and if he sees as forbidden what he used to see as lawful, or as lawful what he used to see as forbidden, it has struck him {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.273}. The same report appears in [[passage/sahaba-hayat-sahaba-halal-1-530|Hayat al-Sahaba]] {cite:hayat-al-sahaba-kandahlawi-sabbani p.530} and in [[passage/sahaba-safwat-safwa-halal-1-220|Sifat al-Safwa]] {cite:sifat-al-safwa-tartusi-2012 p.220}.

Those who fast and pray. A passage in [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-1-178|the Hilyat al-Awliya']] speaks of people who used to fast and pray but leapt on anything forbidden that came their way, so that God voided their deeds; it is said in answer to a questioner's fear that he might be among them {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.178}.

The degrees of scruple. In the [[passage/ihya-halal-2-132|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]] al-Ghazali quotes Ibrahim ibn Adham that abstinence is obligatory, supererogatory and one of safety: the obligatory is abstaining from the forbidden, the supererogatory from the lawful, and safety from the doubtful {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.132}, and page [[passage/ihya-halal-3-223|223]] repeats it {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.223}. The [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-9-84|Hilyat al-Awliya']] lists groups among the fearful, one of whom abstained from the forbidden for fear of retribution, and says that the abstinence of the fearful is leaving the clearly forbidden and that of the scrupulous is leaving every doubtful thing {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.84}. Ibn Rajab notes in [[passage/jami-ulum-hikam-halal-2-626|Jami' al-'Ulum wa-l-Hikam]] that scrupulous scholars such as Ahmad and Malik avoided applying the word forbidden to what was not certainly forbidden but involved some doubt or disagreement, and quotes al-Nakha'i that they disliked things they did not forbid {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.626}.

In al-Shatibi's [[passage/muwafaqat-halal-2-69|al-Muwafaqat]] the permissible may be a means to something prohibited and so be left insofar as it is a means, as someone said, I leave between me and the forbidden a screen of the lawful, without forbidding it {cite:muwafaqat p.69}, and pages [[passage/muwafaqat-halal-1-189|189]] and [[passage/muwafaqat-halal-2-229|229]] repeat this saying {cite:muwafaqat p.189} {cite:muwafaqat p.229}.
