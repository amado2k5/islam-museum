## Overview
The mujtahid is one who attains the rank of deriving rulings from their proofs {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The encyclopedia presents the age of ijtihad, the ranks of mujtahids and jurists, and the question of the closing of the gate of ijtihad {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam defines the mujtahid as someone who applies independent reasoning (ijtihad) when interpreting Islamic law. The qualifications it names are schooling in the recognised schools of Islamic law and a broad knowledge of the Qur'an and hadith, while in Sunni Islam, the Dictionary states, the title belongs only to the four official law schools' founders, though modern Islamic reformers urge reviving ijtihad to fit new ideas and circumstances. This restriction is the Dictionary's own wording; the card above, drawing on the Kuwaiti encyclopedia, speaks of ranks of mujtahids, and neither account is settled here. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1596}
