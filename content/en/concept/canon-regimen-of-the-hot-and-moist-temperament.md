## Summary
Ibn Sina says that those of hot and moist temperament are liable to putrefaction and to matter flowing to the organs, so their exercise should be gentle and much dissolving, lest pustules arise in the humours; they exercise after evacuation, bathe before the meal, attend to shaking off all superfluities, and take care in spring with venesection and evacuation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.

## Exercise and bath
Those of hot and moist temperament are liable to putrefaction and to matter flowing to the organs, so their exercise should be gentle and much dissolving, lest it bring on a movement that shows pustules in the humours. Those most to avoid exercise are the ones not used to it; it is better that they exercise after evacuation and bathe before the meal. A few letters in this sentence are garbled in the printing, and it is taken by its evident sense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.

## Shaking off superfluities and spring
They are to attend to shaking off all the superfluities, and on entering spring to take precautions with venesection and evacuation. Those whose temperament is hot and dry at the outset are the more entitled to all of this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.256}.
