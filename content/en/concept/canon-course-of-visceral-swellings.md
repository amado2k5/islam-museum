## Summary
Ibn Sina describes what happens in swellings of the viscera when they gather and turn towards abscess, what follows when they fill with pus and burst, and the good signs after bursting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.

## Before suppuration
All swellings of the viscera bring thinness and wasting at the flanks; when they gather and harden, pain becomes very sharp, fever and a rough tongue set in, sleeplessness grows, and the heaviness is great; hardness may be felt, with sudden leanness and sunken eyes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.

## Suppuration and bursting
When the collection forms pus, the surge of fever, pain and throbbing subsides, an itch-like feeling replaces the pain and the heaviness reaches its peak. On bursting, a shivering first appears from the sting of the matter, then fever; the pulse changes, appetite falls, and the matter is driven out by its own route, in spitting, urine or stool. The good sign after bursting is full calm of the fever, easy breathing, a revived faculty and quick expulsion of the matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.167}.
