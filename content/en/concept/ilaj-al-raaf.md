## The rule of staunching and venesection
The crisis-like bleed and what resembles it, coming of itself, is not to be treated until weakness is felt — it may reach four ratls of it — and must be staunched when it exceeds very greatly. What is not of this kind is treated with the staunching medicines; what comes from the body's predisposition and biliousness requires continual evacuation of the bile and correction of the blood by foods and drinks. Venesection is the best thing by which nosebleed is staunched: a narrow letting from the corresponding neighbouring side, especially when swoon supervenes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.237}
## The kinds of stanchers and the management of the hard case
The staunching medicines are either strongly astringent, strongly cooling thickening and congealing, strongly glutinous, sharp and corrosive, of special property, or combining two or three of these; the corrosives, if used, must be used with caution, for they may raise a crust which, when it falls, brings worse than the first ⁦(the names and measures of the astringents, coolers, glutinous and corrosive things, juices, tents, insufflations, embrocations and stuffings are carried only by kind)⁩. As for the hard case arising from boiling heat or bursting arteries: it requires a very narrow venesection of the cephalic on that nostril's side, cupping at the back of the head with a light incision, and on the neighbouring breast applied without incision; sometimes blood must be drawn by venesection to the point of swoon from the cephalic and the shoulder vein behind, which is more telling since it keeps the blood from mounting to the head — when it brings on swoon the bleeding settles on the spot ⁦(carried as the book's description, not as instruction)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.237} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.238} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.239}
## Cupping, binding the limbs and cooling
If the digging is not severe — mere drops or fits — venesection should be little by little in repeated sessions; when the letting has reached sufficiency, turn to thickening the blood by what cools it and what curdles it. The cupping glass cannot resist dominant blood; the blood must first be lessened by venesection, then the glass set — hung on the liver if the bleed is from the right, on the spleen if from the left, on both if from both sides. The extremities must also be bound ⁦(down to the testicles and the breasts of women — a labelled listing set aside for the founder's ruling)⁩; binding the extremities and the ears is extremely effective. Much cold-water douching must be used; the patient may need to sit in ice-cooled water till his limbs darken, or his head to be plastered with dead gypsum or gypsum dissolved in vinegar with ice-cooled waters poured over till benumbed — at the least he holds iced cold water in his mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.239}
## Food, drink and inducing the bleed
Know that a man may live on in his nosebleed till more than twenty — up to twenty-five — ratls of blood have left him, and then die; the swoon that comes of it may be the thing that cuts it off. As for foods: a lentil dish with sumac, vinegar or unripe grape and the like; fresh cheese suits the nosebled, as do milks cooked till thickened and boiled eggs for the one prone to nosebleed from bilious blood — though sour things may harm the nosebled for their cutting and attenuating. A body of the experienced claimed that chickens' brains are among the best of foods for them, indeed among the best of medicines for one whose nosebleed comes of a fall or a blow — but it must be taken much and on repeated occasions ⁦(a claim attributed as in the book)⁩. Wine benefits in that it strengthens and harms in that it stirs the blood: if you are forced to it for strength, mix it and give little; if not forced and the bleed has not approached felling the strength, do not give it. Among the regimen of inducing nosebleed: necessity may justify it, especially in brain diseases — which is why the ancients used to adopt an instrument that scarified the nose to treat many brain diseases ⁦(a report carried as in the book; the instrument and the provoking drugs are carried by kind)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.239} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.240}
