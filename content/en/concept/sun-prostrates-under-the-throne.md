## What the verses say

[[passage/quran-36-38|Ya-Sin 38]] says the sun runs to a resting place of its own and that this is of God's decreeing, and [[passage/quran-36-40|Ya-Sin 40]] says the sun cannot overtake the moon and that each has its own course. [[passage/quran-18-86|al-Kahf 86]] tells that Dhu al-Qarnayn reached where the sun sets and saw it go down in a spring that is muddy or hot, depending on the reading; the commentators touch on this when they reconcile the reports.

## The hadiths

Al-Bukhari relates from Abu Dharr that at sunset the Prophet asked him, "Do you know where it goes?" He replied that God and His Messenger know best, and the Prophet said it goes until it prostrates under the Throne and asks leave, and is given leave; a time is near when it will prostrate and not be accepted and ask leave and not be given it, and be told to go back where it came from, and then it rises from its west; the hadith ties this to the verse in Ya-Sin {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3199}. Muslim has a close hadith from Abu Dharr: the sun runs until it reaches its resting place under the Throne and falls prostrate, and stays so until told to rise and go back where it came from; it returns and rises from its rising place, and this repeats; then it runs without people finding anything strange in it until it reaches its resting place and is told to rise from its west. The Prophet then asked, "Do you know when that is?" and answered that it is when faith will not benefit a soul that had not believed before {cite:sahih-muslim 159}. The hadith has its place on the page [[concept/tulu-al-shams-min-al-maghrib|The sun rising from the west]]. Al-Qurtubi also gives a version in al-Tirmidhi from Abu Dharr, saying Abu Isa judged it hasan sahih, and a report from Ikrima that when the sun sets it enters a niche under the Throne and glorifies God until morning {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 36:38}; the chain of Ikrima's report was not checked here.

## What the commentators said

On Ya-Sin 38 Ibn Kathir gives two readings of the resting place. The first concerns place: it is under the Throne on the side facing the earth, and wherever the sun is it is under the Throne, which is the roof of creatures; the Throne is not a sphere as many astronomers claim but a dome with legs borne by angels, above the world, so that when the sun is at noon in the vault of its sphere it is nearest the Throne, and when it has turned to the opposite point at midnight it is farthest from it, and then it prostrates and asks leave to rise. The second concerns time: the resting place is the end of its course on the Day of Resurrection, when its motion ceases and it is rolled up; Qatada said it means a term it does not pass. He also mentions a view that the resting place is its highest point in summer and its lowest in winter {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 36:38}. He relays from Abdullah ibn Amr that when the sun rises the sins of Adam's children turn it back, and when it sets it greets, prostrates and asks leave until the day it is not given {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 36:38}.

Al-Qurtubi gathers readings: from al-Kalbi, its farthest setting-stations and then its return; from al-Hasan, that the sun has three hundred and sixty risings; and from Ibn Abbas, that when it sets it reaches a place it does not pass and rests under the Throne until it rises, which al-Qurtubi says brings the views together. He then mentions a reading ascribed to Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas that denies any resting place, and rejects it with al-Anbari's argument that the consensus witnesses to the soundness of the other two chains from Ibn Abbas, and with the established hadiths {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 36:38}.

Ibn Hajar, in Fath al-Bari under the chapter on this verse, says (as read from an OCR text of the edition with Ibn Baz's notes, which is poor in places and should be rechecked) that the phrase "under the Throne" appears to mean when it is level with it, that this does not clash with the Kahf verse on the spring, because the setting there is the limit of sight at sunset, and that its prostration under the Throne comes after the setting. He adds that the hadith refutes those who said the resting place is the highest point it reaches, the longest day of the year, and relays a view that it is the end of its term when the world ends. From al-Khattabi he gives two possibilities: that it rests under the Throne in a way we cannot grasp, or that the sense is knowledge of what was asked is in a book in which the beginning and end of the world's affairs are written, and that nightly prostration under the Throne does not hinder its circling. His own reading is that the apparent sense is that the resting happens each day and night at its prostration {cite:fath-al-bari on Surat Ya-Sin}.

In al-Nawawi's commentary on Muslim, in a scanned printed text that is also poor in places, the sun's prostration is said to be with discernment and perception that God creates in it, and several views on the resting place are listed, among them al-Wahidi's {cite:sharh-sahih-muslim-al-nawawi on Muslim 159}.

On the Kahf verse Ibn Kathir says Dhu al-Qarnayn reached the farthest point that can be travelled on the earth in the west, that reaching the place of the sun's setting in the heavens is impossible, that what storytellers relate of his travelling while the sun set behind him has no reality and is mostly the fancies of the People of the Book, and that he saw the sun in his sight setting in the encircling ocean as anyone at a coast sees it, while it never leaves the fourth sphere in which it is fixed. He gives two readings, hami'a meaning mud and hamiya meaning hot, and sees no conflict between them; he doubts that the hadith that the sun sets in God's hot fire is truly the Prophet's, and thinks it may be from Abdullah ibn Amr, from the two pack-loads he found at the Yarmuk {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 18:86}.

:::perspectives
**On how it prostrates.** Al-Nawawi: with discernment and perception that God creates in the sun {cite:sharh-sahih-muslim-al-nawawi on Muslim 159}.

**On the meaning of the resting place.** A place under the Throne (Ibn Kathir, Ibn Hajar); or a time, the end of the world; or the end of its orbit in summer and winter; and from Ibn Abbas the settling under the Throne after setting until it rises {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 36:38} {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 36:38} {cite:fath-al-bari on Surat Ya-Sin}.

**On the setting in the spring.** Ibn Kathir: a view from the sea coast, the sun never leaving its sphere {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 18:86}.
:::

## What the sources do not say

No modern studies of this hadith were read here, and none is attributed to it. The texts read differ on how the sun prostrates and where it rests, and no one view is preferred here. The readings of Ibn Hajar and al-Nawawi rest on scanned texts with defects in places and should be checked against a printed edition before they are quoted.
