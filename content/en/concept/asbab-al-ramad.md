## Kinds with turns, and the causes of its pain
Some kinds of ramad have a cycle and paroxysms by the cycle of the matter's pouring and breeding; pain intensifies either from a biting humour eating the coats, a copious distending matter, or a thick vapour — the difference in these makes the difference in pain; its matter comes from distension, from the head itself, from vessels delivering a bad hot or cold matter to the eye, or from the eye itself — a humour held in her coats corrupting their temper, or a long-standing ramad turning all incoming nourishment to corruption; prominent eyes take great ramad and protrusion more readily for their moisture and open pores; cold tears may abound in some kinds from non-digestion, and ramad often resolves by the natural crisis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.167} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.168}

## Quantity and quality of matter, climes and seasons
Ramad's badness follows the matter's quality, its greatness the matter's quantity; in southern lands it is frequent and quick to resolve — frequent for the flowing of their matter and many vapours, quick for the openness of their pores and the free course of their natures, though a sudden severe cold can draw it out when a gripping barrier chances on the humour's motion; in cold lands and times it is rare but hard — rare for the humours' stillness, hard because once seated it does not dissolve quickly with the channels narrow; when a northern winter is followed by a rainy southern spring and summer, ramad abounds, likewise a damp southern winter filling the body with humours followed by a retaining northern spring; a northern summer is rich in ramad especially after a southern winter; measure hard bodies by northern lands and loose soft bodies by southern; as hot lands are ramad-prone so is the very hot bath — entering it one is near to ramad; and if ramad with a changed state of the eye persists despite right treatment and thorough cleansing, the cause is a bad matter congested in the eye corrupting nourishment, or catarrhs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.168}
