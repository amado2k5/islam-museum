## Summary
The Moon's latitude is its distance from the zodiac belt. It is found by subtracting the head's place from the Moon's true place; what remains is the argument of latitude. Alternatively the argument is found by adding the head's place to the Moon's true place and removing a full circuit if the sum exceeds one. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.113}.

## North and south latitude
If the argument is from zero to one hundred and eighty degrees, the latitude is to the north of the zodiac; from one hundred and eighty to three hundred and sixty it is to the south. The amount of latitude is then read by entering the argument in the sixth table marked for the Moon's latitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.113}.

## Rising and falling in latitude
He says the Moon is north from when it leaves the head until it reaches the tail, and south from the tail back to the head; by this one knows whether it is rising or falling in latitude on its side. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.114}.
