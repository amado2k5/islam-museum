## Summary
The waqwaq arabesque is a design in which the arabesque's tendrils bear human and animal heads in place of leaves; Norah Titley explains that it is named after the mythical tree hung with heads instead of fruit. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 224}

## Earliest example
Titley regards the earliest known example as one in the borders of the [[object/iskandar-sultan-miscellany-bl-add-27261|Iskandar Sultan miscellany]] made at Shiraz around 1410–11, where heads are attached to tendrils in a border design. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 224} {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 233} She cites it within the rich border ornament of that manuscript, where arabesque and geometric motifs far outnumber the small drawings. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 224}
