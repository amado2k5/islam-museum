## Summary
The knowledge that unwraps a dream's symbols into their outcomes; God gave it to Yusuf, who read the king's dream — seven fat cows seven years, seven lean seven years. A door of the unseen granted only to whom it is given.

## In the Qur'an — the model of Joseph
Interpretation reaches its fullest form in the Qur'an in Joseph's story: he was given of the kingdom and taught of the interpretation of narratives — "the stories of the ancients, which We have sent down to you" — and eloquence in judgment [[passage/quran-12-37|Yusuf 37]]. He interpreted the two young men's dream in prison [[passage/quran-12-36|36]], then the king saw in sleep "seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones and seven green ears and seven dry" and asked him: "O king, expound for me my dream, if ye can interpret dreams" [[passage/quran-12-43|Yusuf 43]]. The king then said: "You have brought me a proof from your Lord" [[passage/quran-12-54|54]], and seeing Joseph he placed him over the treasuries of the land; when he said "make me over the treasuries of the land; I am a knowing guardian" [[passage/quran-12-55|55]], Joseph answered "God has kept me in my absence" [[passage/quran-12-64|64]].

From here the exegetes take Joseph's story as the root of this chapter, holding that what Joseph interpreted was by revelation from God, not by the soul's own effort {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the hadith — the limits of interpretation
The hadith makes interpretation part of what remained of prophethood: "The good dream of a righteous man is one forty-sixth of prophethood" {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.545} [[passage/muwatta-31-2746|Muwatta 2746]]; in it, the righteous dream is seen by the man or shown to him, and the Prophet would ask after the morning prayer: did any of you see a dream last night? {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.546} [[passage/muwatta-31-2748|Muwatta 2748]].

When the Prophet was asked about dreams he would listen to what was recounted and be reassured by it or show the correct reading — as with Abdullah ibn Zayd's dream of the call to prayer, which he took up and for which the adhan was instituted {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997} [[passage/muwatta-1-172|Muwatta 1:172]].

## Rules among the people of knowledge
The people of knowledge gathered in the books of interpretation these bounds:

- **Interpretation by the state**: the dreamer's condition is considered — who he is, where he is, and what he was doing — for a dream changes with the change of state {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.
- **Interpretation by the likely**: one dream is not decisive, and no ruling is built on it; a grave matter may appear in sleep as a symbol of good and the reverse, in the view of the exegetes {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997}.
- **Only the people of dreams interpret**: whoever sees a dream does not dictate it to another save with knowledge, and the condition is that it be a righteous dream, not from Satan nor the soul's talk {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.547}.
- **The difference between ru'ya and hulm**: the righteous dream is interpreted or brings glad tidings, while the bad dream from Satan is neither interpreted nor told {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.547} [[passage/muwatta-31-2750|Muwatta 2750]].

## The books of interpretation
The best known in this chapter is [[work/tafsir-al-ahlam-ibn-sirin|Tafsir al-Ahlam]] attributed to [[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Muhammad ibn Sirin]], with chapters on interpreting what people see in sleep of water, sky, earth, animals, kings and more — though its museum card is still a first draft whose sources have not yet been checked on the page {cite:al-aalam}.

In the modern age a debate arose over interpretation from dreams — between those who see in it a door of the unseen and those who warn against attachment to it — which the museum reviews in [[concept/tafsir-al-ahlam-al-yawm|dream interpretation today]] and the fatwa [[work/fatwa-islamweb-dreaming-of-blasphemy|dreaming of blasphemy]].

## Interpretation in fiqh
No legal ruling is built on a dream; if a person sees in sleep what would entail a hadd, a divorce or a vow, nothing attaches until the cause exists in waking {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}. What is built on a dream is glad tidings and reassurance, not obligation.

## Perspectives
- **Ahl al-Sunna:** interpretation is a door of the unseen granted to whom it is granted; no attachment to it is made in rulings.
- **The people of knowledge:** the righteous dream is interpreted by the likely and with caution, and what is not understood is left to God.
