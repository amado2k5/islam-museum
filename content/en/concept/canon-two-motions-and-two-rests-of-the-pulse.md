## Summary
Ibn Sina sets out that every beat is made of two motions and two rests, reports physicians' disagreement over whether contraction can be felt, and cites Galen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.

## The four parts
Each beat is made of an expansion and a contraction, and a rest must come between every two opposite motions, so each beat has four parts: a motion of expansion, a rest between it and contraction, a motion of contraction and a rest between it and expansion; this is shown in natural science {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.168}.

## Feeling contraction
Many physicians hold that the contraction is not felt at all; others say it can be felt in a strong pulse by its strength, in a large one by its height, in a hard one by its resistance and in a slow one by the length of its motion. Galen is reported to have overlooked contraction for a time, then kept practising touch until he noticed it and doors of the pulse opened to him; Ibn Sina holds that in most cases contraction is not felt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.168-169}.
