## Summary
In technical use haqiqa is a word employed for what it was coined to mean in the convention through which speech is conveyed; its counterpart is metaphor, a word used for something other than its coinage because of a link between the two. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7216} Kinaya is speech whose intended meaning is hidden by usage though its literal sense is clear, so it needs intention or context to settle what is meant. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7217} Literal meaning is linguistic when set by the lexicographers, legal when set by the lawgiver, and customary when set by custom; the same word may be literal in one sense and figurative in another, as prayer is literal in the legal sense and figurative in supplication. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7217} Speech is taken literally by default and moved to metaphor only when the literal sense is impossible, hard or abandoned by custom, with branches in bequests, oaths and acknowledgements. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7218} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7219} Most usulis forbid taking the literal and figurative sense together in one use, the Shafiis allow it unless reason forbids; a general metaphor is admissible. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7219}
