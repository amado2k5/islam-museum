## Overview
Ta'dhib is inflicting punishment or harm; the basis is prohibition, since it punishes without right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5406}

## Related terms
Ta'zir is discipline below the fixed penalty; ta'dib is correction by speech or by beating; mutilation is disfiguring a corpse; ta'dhib is wider than them all. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5406} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5407}

## Ruling
Torment is forbidden in principle, on the hadith «God will torment on the Day of Resurrection those who torment people in this world». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5407} Retaliation short of death is carried out by the ruler, and the Hanbalis require the ruler's presence for a killing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5407}

## Lawful torment of persons
It is lawful in the fixed penalties, retaliation and discipline; cauterising is lawful for treatment if needed. Burning enemies in war is lawful when they cannot be taken otherwise, but not once they are under control, on the hadith of Hamza al-Aslami «do not burn him, for none punishes with fire but the Lord of fire». A father may beat his child to discipline him, a teacher with the father's leave, and he is liable if he exceeds; piercing a girl's ear is lawful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5408} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5409}

## The accused
A man of known goodness is not punished by agreement; one of unknown standing is held until his standing is known, on the hadith of Bahz ibn Hakim about the Prophet holding a suspect. One known for wrongdoing may be held and beaten according to some jurists, citing the Prophet's order to Zubayr to punish a man who hid money; the views are that both ruler and judge beat, or only the ruler, or that he is held and not beaten. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5409}

## Captives
Captives may not be tormented, since Islam calls for gentleness toward them and feeding them. They may be held if feared to flee, and threatened or lightly punished when there is hope they will reveal the enemy's secrets. If the enemy tortures Muslim captives, the same may be done to theirs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5410}

## Animals
Branding of zakat and tax-paying animals is lawful; grilling live fish is lawful for the Malikis; beating for training is lawful to need. Forbidden are starving or denying water to an animal, on the hadith of the woman in the Fire for a cat, using a living creature as a target, and cutting the head or skinning a slaughtered animal before it has cooled. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5410} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5411}

## Reference
Torment is treated in crimes, discipline, slaughter, captivity, governance and war. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5411}
