## Summary
The two equinoxes are two points on the ecliptic at which the Sun crosses the celestial equator, so the vernal equinox occurs at the beginning of Aries and the autumnal equinox at the beginning of Libra {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 113}. On the day of the equinox, day and night are equal across the entire Earth {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 107}.

## Position and Naming
The point through which the Sun passes from south to north is called the "point of the vernal equinox," which is the beginning of the sign of Aries, and the other, through which it passes from north to south, is called the "point of the autumnal equinox," which is the beginning of Libra {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 113}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 115}.

## Northern and Southern Signs
Six signs are north of the celestial equator, from the beginning of Aries to the end of Virgo, and six are south of it, from the beginning of Libra to the end of Pisces {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 113}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 149}.

## Equality of Day and Night
Al-Farghani explains that when the Sun is at the two equinoxes, day and night are equal across the entire Earth, because its path on that day lies on the celestial equator, which the horizon divides into two halves {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 107}. The duration of day is longer than night when the Sun is in the northern signs, and the reverse in the southern signs {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 107}.
