## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy takes up polygyny through the story of a man who married two wives without knowing what burdens a husband, and describes the hardship between co-wives. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:29:30} This topic is contested, and the Sheikh's view is set out in the final section.

## The story of the man with two wives
The Sheikh tells of a man who married two wives without knowing what burdens a husband, and then complained of the strain between them (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:29:30}
He recounts that a friend found him sad and said his wife was tired; he then described himself as a lamb between two rams, or a load carried between two women (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:30:30}
He mentions a house with two doors in which a family died in a fire, and a dream in which one of the wives tells him she came out through the narrow door; the transcript breaks off here (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:31:00}
