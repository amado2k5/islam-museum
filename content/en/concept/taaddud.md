## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy takes up polygyny through the story of a man who married two wives without knowing what burdens a husband, and describes the hardship between co-wives. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:29:30} This topic is contested, and the Sheikh's view is set out in the final section.

## The story of the man with two wives
The Sheikh tells of a man who married two wives without knowing what burdens a husband, and then complained of the strain between them (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:29:30}
He recounts that a friend found him sad and said his wife was tired; he then described himself as a lamb between two rams, or a load carried between two women (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:30:30}
He mentions a house with two doors in which a family died in a fire, and a dream in which one of the wives tells him she came out through the narrow door; the transcript breaks off here (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:31:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 02)

The Sheikh speaks of a widow with two or three children and says that she may accept to be half, a third or a quarter of a wife to an already married man, then asks his listeners whether that is so {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:29:00}.

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] gives an episode to polygamy, saying it is a question he had put off until then and that it needs this episode and perhaps two more. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:13:30} What follows is his view and his arguments as he presents them, attributed to him. He begins with the brotherhood between Muhajirun and Ansar: [[person/sad-ibn-al-rabi|Sa'd ibn al-Rabi']] told [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]] that he had two wives and that Abd al-Rahman should see which he liked, and Sa'd would divorce her so that he could marry her; al-Heweny sees in this the logic of faith. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:04:30}

He then sets out the argument of those who oppose polygamy from the hadith in the two Sahihs on Banu Hisham ibn al-Mughira seeking leave to marry their daughter to Ali, and the Prophet's saying that [[person/fatima|Fatima]] is part of him and what hurts her hurts him; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:09:00} he answers that in the same hadith the Prophet says he does not forbid the lawful or permit the forbidden, so the ruling for him is particular to Fatima, and that hurting the Prophet is forbidden by consensus, so no one else may be compared to him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:10:30} He says Ali took no other wife while Fatima lived, until she died six months after her father, and then married several women. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:12:00} He grants that a second marriage hurts the first wife, but holds that its benefit outweighs this, and that the Sharia weighs benefits against harms. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:12:30}

Al-Heweny holds that jealousy is innate in women and excused as long as it does not overstep God's limits, and is otherwise a fault. He cites the hadith of [[person/umm-salama|Umm Salama]] in Sahih Muslim: when the Prophet proposed to her after Abu Salama's death she said she was very jealous, and he said he would pray God to take her jealousy away; al-Heweny says that had jealousy been good he would not have prayed for its removal. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:16:30} He also cites the hadith of [[person/aisha|A'isha]] in Sahih Muslim, when she was jealous and the Prophet asked whether her devil had come to her, {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:17:30} and the hadith of [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]] in al-Nasa'i that the Prophet did not marry women of the Ansar because of their intense jealousy, whose chain he grades sound on Muslim's condition. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:19:00} He ends by saying polygamy has conditions for the man and the woman, and that some men are not fit for it. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep12 00:21:00}
