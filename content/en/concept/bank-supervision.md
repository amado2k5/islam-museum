## Summary
The author states that central banks supervise banks through monetary and banking policies, setting cash reserve ratios, liquidity ratios, interest rates, and collateral requirements {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}. If they grow lax in supervision, banks expand credit and violate regulations, which in his view is one of the causes of the crisis {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 356}.

## Administrative Corruption
The author lists forms of corruption: bribery to obtain loans; manipulation in valuing collateral and setting loan amounts, installment terms, and interest rates; embezzlement of bank funds; and granting loans to influential figures who evade repayment {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 357}. As a result, doubtful and bad debts increase, and bank funds become frozen {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 357}.

## Internal and External Oversight
The author calls for internal and external oversight to prevent bribery, embezzlement, and theft, because the bankruptcy of one bank may drag other banks down with it, causing banks to fail one after another {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 357}.
