## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the ark God made the sign of Talut's kingship was a sacred object for the Children of Israel. It held sakina from God and remains left by the house of Musa and Harun {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1048}.

## Keeping relics
He says that relics tied to events of faith and prophethood matter for belief and should be left as they are, as reminders, rather than counted as idolatry. He holds that rulers should look after them, display them so that they become a source of calm, and advise people not to be drawn into them {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1049} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1050}.

## What the verse says
He notes that the verse does not say the ark would come whole or that it held what Musa was placed in. It says remains, and that angels carry it, which he reads as a sign of its weight {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1050}.

## The staff
He reports that some said the remains were Musa's staff. He finds this reasonable, since the staff was one of the tools of his sign and his people would not have let it go {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1051}.

## Not yet checked
This is Sha'rawi's reading. The exegetical discussion of the ark, the academic view and any non-Sunni view have not been checked, so nothing is attributed to them.

## At Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy (Al-Munjiyat, episode 14)
The Sheikh counts the occurrences of 'sakina' in the Book of God: in the ark among the Children of Israel, its descent on His Messenger and the believers in Surat al-Tawba, and in three places in Surat al-Fath. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:04:00}
He transmits from Ibn Taymiyya that he read the six sakina verses when affairs grew hard on him, and that sakina names three things: what was in the ark, the subtle words spoken on the tongues of truthful people such as al-Hasan al-Basri, and what descended upon the Prophet's and the believers' hearts at Hudaybiyya. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:10:30}
He pauses at Hudaybiyya, where the Companions found sakina in their release from ihram and their rivalry to shave and slaughter first, and the Muslims counted the peace a victory. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:15:30}
He transmits the scholars' three degrees of sakina: the heart's humility in service, then self-accounting, gentleness with people and watchfulness of the Truth, then contentment with God's allotment, citing Abu Dharr's 'steer the ship well, for the sea is deep' on relying on God while striving. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep14 00:21:30}
