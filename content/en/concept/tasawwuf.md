## Overview
The Islamic spiritual heritage of the purification and refinement of the soul. Its orders organised the discipline of the heart and remembrance (dhikr) over centuries. {cite:wikidata}

## On "Al-Wasatiyya": Sufism, spirituality or deviation?
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gave an episode of his programme [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|"Al-Wasatiyya"]] to Sufism. He asked whether Sufism is still widespread today, whether modern Sufism is the same as the old, whether the two sides of the sharp conflict between Salafis and Sufis actually know each other, whether there is scholarly dialogue between them or mutual attack, and what the merits and failings of Sufism are and whether the failings can be remedied. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:02:00} His guests included people who hold the Sufi way and people who oppose it, and he said the young studio audience is not selected: the episode is announced and whoever comes is welcome. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:03:00}

### The guests
- Dr Azmi Taha, professor of Islamic philosophy and a researcher on Sufism. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:08:30}
- Shaykh Isma'il al-Kurdi, shaykh of the Hashimi [[group/shadhiliyya|Shadhili]] order. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:14:00}
- Dr Muhammad Basil al-Ta'i, professor of cosmological physics at Yarmouk University. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:28:00}
- Dr Fakhri, a critic of Sufism, who said he studied at the Islamic University of Madina. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:46:00}
- Dr Abdullah, also a critic. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:49:00}
- Dr Muhammad Nuh al-Qudah, a specialist in the sharia, introduced by the presenter as the son of the mufti of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:50:00}

### The two polls
- The studio audience was asked whether Sufism and Sufis are a widespread phenomenon today or a limited one: 62% said widespread and 38% limited, and the presenter remarked that the subject is therefore something large in the community. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:05:30} One young man said its spread differs from country to country. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:08:00}
- He then asked whether most Sufis today are moderate or deviant, and read the result as 65% moderate and 44% deviant, a total above a hundred as read. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:25:30}

### What is Sufism?
Dr Azmi Taha defined it in three words: emptying oneself of vices (takhalli), adorning oneself with virtues (tahalli), then disclosure (tajalli); he explained disclosure as the point where one who strives to purify himself may reach a measure of unveiling, so that knowledge from God becomes manifest in his heart, what they call inspiration, unveiling and openings. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:10:00} The presenter said the first two words are undisputed and the third is thorny ground, since some may understand unveiling as contact with God without need of revelation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:10:30} Another guest cited an early definition: entering every noble trait and leaving every base one. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:12:00}

Shaykh Isma'il al-Kurdi said there are more than two thousand definitions of Sufism, as [[person/ahmad-zarruq|Shaykh Zarruq]] said, and defined it as practical and inward conduct leading to knowledge and love of God, bound to the Prophet's lawful way, beginning as knowledge and then practice; he linked it to the station of [[concept/ihsan|ihsan]] in the hadith of Jibril. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:15:30}

The presenter said that in his long research he had not found two people agreeing on a definition, but he set out the axes Sufism turns on: renunciation, deepening the heart's acts of worship such as sincerity and turning to God, remembrance by tongue and heart, and then methods of conduct, remembrance and training; he said fiqh is not among Sufism's concerns, which are the refinement of hearts, conduct, character, the relation with God and love of the righteous. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:17:30} He said Sufis date its beginning to the Prophet and his Companions while researchers date it to about 150 AH, with its great spread in the third century, after which it formed orders such as the Shadhiliyya, the [[group/naqshbandiyya|Naqshbandiyya]] and the [[group/qadiriyya|Qadiriyya]], and that some of the major hadith scholars were Sufis; this is his statement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:19:00}

### Why did it spread?
Shaykh Isma'il al-Kurdi held that what drew people was Sufism's spirituality, its inward worship and the sense of nearness to God, of His watch and of humility in prayer, a natural human need; he told of visiting an Islamic centre in Rimini, Italy, where an Italian university teacher who had embraced Islam told him he would not have become Muslim but for Sufism. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:22:00} The presenter added that he lived in the West for seventeen years debating priests and rabbis, and found that speaking of Islam's spirituality and the clarity of its monotheism moved people more than scholarly or logical argument. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:22:30}

### The unity of being
The presenter set out what is attributed to some Sufis as the unity of being (wahdat al-wujud), the heart rising until it, God and the cosmos become one, along with annihilation (fana') and the claim that obligations fall from one who has "arrived". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:29:00} Dr Muhammad Basil al-Ta'i said the sharia is the Sufis' original method, that some Sufis expressed their relation to God and the cosmos in wholly wrong words, that what [[person/bayazid-al-bistami|Abu Yazid al-Bistami]] said of this kind is ecstatic utterance (shath) not to be counted against Sufis, and he explained the unity of being as the effort to be in harmony with the cosmos in movement and worship, giving the example of circling the Ka'ba anticlockwise as the planets turn. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:33:30} A Sufi participant said union with God is unbelief and that he knew no Sufi shaykh who holds it, and Shaykh al-Kurdi cited [[person/abd-al-ghani-al-nabulsi|Abd al-Ghani al-Nabulsi]] that what they mean is the unity of the Real's existence, all creation depending on God. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:35:00} The presenter said that since those present deny it, the sound rational and religious approach is to accept their denial. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:35:30}

### The critics' points
- Dr Fakhri said the problem is not the Sufism of the scholars of the early centuries but the deviation among followers from about the eighth century AH. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:13:30} He cited sayings attributed to al-Bistami and [[person/al-hallaj|al-Hallaj]] on indwelling, and noted that [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]], who purified Sufism, holds a duality between servant and Lord and advises folding away, not repeating, the words of those in ecstasy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:40:30} He criticised what he sees as exaggeration about the Prophet's reality and the claim of his presence at gatherings (hadra), cited [[person/abd-al-wahhab-al-shaarani|al-Sha'rani]] on wonders of [[person/ahmad-al-badawi|Ahmad al-Badawi]] and his festival, and mentioned calling on the dead for help, seeking blessing at graves and circling them. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:46:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:47:30} He said he heard with his own ears, in a Friday sermon at a mosque of one of the orders, that the Prophet is alive in his grave and the deeds of believers are shown to him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:14:00}
- Shaykh al-Kurdi objected to the method: all Sufis cannot be bound by one man's saying, just as one cannot collect errors from Sunni books and call them the Sunni method. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:43:00} The presenter insisted on hearing all the criticisms first and then giving the reply, since airing them is better than leaving them in books to be passed on without answer. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:45:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:48:00}
- Dr Abdullah said Sufi worship is marked by excess and severity and by particular rituals in expressing the relation with God. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:49:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:50:00}

### Dr Muhammad Nuh al-Qudah's reply
- He said judging a thing follows from conceiving it, that most of what he heard judged hearsay rather than facts, and advised going to the books of Sufism, first among them al-Ghazali's [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]]; fiqh without Sufism, he said, is a body without a soul: the jurist teaches the pillars of prayer, but humility in prayer needs training. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:50:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:51:00}
- He rejected the well-known saying that a disciple before his shaykh should be like a corpse before its washer, cited the maxim that one is not a Sufi until one has learned fiqh, distinguished Sufism from some who claim it to gather followers and profits, and counted Sufism as the third part of Islam after creed and fiqh: ethics. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:52:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:11:30}
- He noted that [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] wrote a book called *al-Zuhd*, that [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] discussed "true Sufism" in two volumes of his fatwas, and counted [[person/abd-al-hamid-ii-uthmani|Sultan Abd al-Hamid]], [[person/izz-al-din-ibn-abd-al-salam|al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam]] and [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din al-Ayyubi]] among Sufis; these are his statements. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:53:00} The presenter added that Muslim scholars, Salafis among them, used the term Sufism, and that there is no objection to replacing it with conduct, ethics or spirituality. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:52:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:52:30}
- He said every discipline has terms to be asked of its specialists, and that the doctrine of the unity of being is "unbelief beyond discussion". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:53:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:55:00}
- On the [[practice/mawlid-al-nabi|Mawlid]]: he said standing at the mention of the Prophet's birth is neither obligatory nor worship but courtesy and reverence, so one who does not stand is not at fault; the presenter pressed that acts of worship are fixed by revelation and asked why the Companions did not do it, and he replied that they showed even greater courtesy, and that the Prophet is present by his spirit and knowledge, not his body. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:55:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:56:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:58:30}
- On calling for help and [[concept/tawassul|tawassul]]: he cited the hadith of the blind man; the presenter objected that the added direct address in it is weak according to scholars; the guest then distinguished calling on the person in a grave for help, which he said is forbidden and no part of the religion, from seeking a means to God through the Prophet or the rank of his prophethood, which he holds permissible and the same in the Prophet's life and after his death. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 00:59:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:06:30} The presenter noted that some scholars restrict permission to asking a living person to pray for one. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:06:00}
- On [[concept/tabarruk|seeking blessing]] at graves: he said seeking blessing at graves, rubbing them and circling them is part of neither the religion nor Sufism, that seeking blessing from the Prophet's relics is established, and that the Sufi etiquette for visiting the noble grave is to stand four cubits away; the Sufis present agreed in rejecting such practices, and the presenter said the dialogue was taking excellent steps forward. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:04:30}
- On particular practices such as silence and hunger: he said for some these are a matter of training, not worship, that whoever worships with what God did not prescribe has innovated, and he agreed that such worship is wrong while saying he does not know how widespread it is. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:11:00}
- He closed by saying Sufism has been stabbed with two daggers: by some of its own people who brought into it what does not belong, and by opponents who judged it without knowing it; and that extreme Sufis and extreme Salafis watch over each other so that the matter stays in the middle. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:12:30}

### Last words
- Dr Azmi Taha said true Sufism is not something set against Islam, and listed deviations in practice: reading trust in God as passivity, abandoning learning while waiting for unveiling, sayings contrary to the creed, and exaggeration about the Prophet and the saints; he quoted al-Ghazali that this is a field "with many claimants", and closed by saying every merit found among Sufis is a merit of Islam. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:17:30}
- Shaykh al-Kurdi said al-Sha'rani reported that words were falsely inserted into his books in his lifetime, that Sufi books contain fabrications just as hadith books do, cited [[person/ibn-arabi-muhyi-al-din|Ibn Arabi]] declaring the doctrine of union unbelief, and referred to his creed in the introduction to *al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya*. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:18:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:18:30} The presenter commented that Ibn Arabi has words suggesting union, but that he had read Ibn Taymiyya, the chief critic of Sufis, interpreting his words rather than calling them unbelief, and urged following his approach of not rushing to declare unbelief. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:19:30}
- Shaykh al-Kurdi said most Sufis he has known across Muslim lands are people of learning and piety; the fault in some countries, Egypt among them, is that a learned shaykh's son succeeds him without learning, which brings deviation and veneration of the shaykh; and he knows no Sufi in Syria or Jordan who seeks blessing at graves. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:21:00} On jihad he named the Mahdist movement in Sudan, the Sanusiyya in Libya, [[person/abd-al-qadir-al-jazairi|Abd al-Qadir al-Jaza'iri]], Salah al-Din, [[person/abu-al-hasan-al-shadhili|Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili]] and al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam in fighting the Crusaders as Sufis; this is his statement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:21:30}

### The presenter's summing-up (his view)
The presenter said one episode was not enough, that he had prepared axes he never reached, collective remembrance, particular devotions and the Mawlid in detail, and that he is surprised that Sufis of old did not enter sharp disputes while today they reply sharply when attacked, which in his view is not Sufi character. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:22:30} He held that Sufism is an old Islamic phenomenon under several names, the science of conduct, renunciation as with Imam Ahmad, spirituality, and that it has great merits: it reminds people of God and softens hearts in a dry age, reveres the Prophet, preserved Islam in the communist states, and spread it in South-East Asia without war. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:23:30} He held that some Sufis exaggerate in their dealings with God, the Prophet, graves and saints, was glad that some present hold Sufism and reject these exaggerations, and called for taking from the [[group/salafiyya|Salafis]] their exact following of the Sunna and from the Sufis the softening of hearts, all turning towards the middle way. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep28 01:25:00}

These are the views of the participants in the episode, each attributed to its speaker; the museum does not judge between them.
