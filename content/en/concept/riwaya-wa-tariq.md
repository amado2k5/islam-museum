## Summary
When one says "Nafi's reading", "Hafs's riwaya from Asim" or "the path of Ibn Mujahid", these are three levels in the chain. The **reading** is its attribution to an imam of recitation; the **riwaya** is what a named transmitter carries from him; the **tariq** (path) is the chain of those who took from the transmitter thereafter down to the author who recorded the reading. The usage appears in Ibn al-Jazari's [[work/al-nashr-fi-al-qiraat-al-ashr|al-Nashr]], where he says, for example, 'as for Qunbul, by the two paths of Ibn Mujahid and Ibn Shannabudh from him; Ibn Mujahid by the two paths of al-Samiri and Salih from him'. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 64}

## Reader and transmitter
Ibn al-Jazari explains what it means to attribute a reading to the imams: it is not attribution of invention, opinion or independent judgement but of choice, constancy and adherence. The reader chose the reading in that form as he had recited it, preferred it to others and kept to it until he became known by it and was taken from, so it was attributed to him and not others. Likewise the attribution of variants to Companions means only that one was more exact in it, recited and taught it more, and kept to it and inclined to it. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 61} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 62}

## The paths
A path is how the author links a transmitter's reading to himself through his teachers. Ibn al-Jazari opens his table of chains by saying he will first give 'how I received the books from which I took these readings in text, then follow with the connected oral delivery as it should be', beginning with al-Dani's [[work/al-taysir-fi-al-qiraat-al-sab|al-Taysir]] and its chain. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 67} The paths differ in number from book to book: Ibn al-Jazari notes that al-Shatibiyya and al-Taysir give fourteen paths from the seven, while his own book contains eighty, with other benefits besides. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 66}

## Many paths and few
Ibn al-Jazari names those famed for gathering the most: al-Hudhali in al-Kamil, fifty readings and 1,459 reports and paths; Abu Ma'shar al-Tabari in Suq al-Arus, 1,550 reports and paths; and Isa ibn Abd al-Aziz of Alexandria in al-Jami al-Akbar, seven thousand reports and paths. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 44} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 45} He says people have not ceased writing on many readings and few, relating the irregular and the sound according to what reached them or was sound for them, and none objects to them. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 45}

## A caution
Limiting the short manuals to two transmitters per imam is a teaching convention, not a limit set by law; al-Suyuti quotes al-Mahdawi that whoever limits himself to two transmitters per imam comes to reject a third transmitter's reading when he hears it, though it may be better known. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 525} The plurality of paths should thus be read as breadth of transmission, not weakness of precision.
