## Overview
*Hamm* is the resolve of the heart to act before the act is done. Ibn Rajab explains it in the thirty-seventh hadith of the Forty, and it is connected to the chapter on [[concept/niyya|intention]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.754-755}

## The four cases
The hadith mentions four cases. Whoever resolves on a good deed but does not do it, God writes for him a full good deed. If he does it, ten good deeds are written for him, up to seven hundred times, to many times more. Whoever resolves on an evil deed but does not do it, God writes for him a full good deed. If he does it, one evil deed is written. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.754-755}

## Ibn Rajab's commentary
One is rewarded for resolving on a good deed even if the act does not take place. Resolving on an evil deed is not punished; rather a good deed is written if one leaves it for God's sake, and an evil deed is written only when it is done. He explains that the merit of resolve over a mere passing thought is that it is a settled purpose resembling a firm will. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.754-755}
