## Overview
Al-nasi': the people of the Jahiliyya moved forward and postponed the sacred months to suit the needs of war and trade, until the Hajj coincided with its true time in the tenth year, and then the practice was abrogated by the Qur'an.

## The story
The people of the Jahiliyya would move one month forward and postpone another. They would swap the status of one of the sacred months for another month when the need of war or business called for it. When they wished to perform the nasi', their people stood up at the Hajj season, making Muharram lawful and making Safar forbidden {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

This manipulation continued until the Hajj of the tenth year matched its true time. The Prophet then performed the Farewell Pilgrimage with the months in their original order {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an addresses this practice directly in [[passage/quran-9-37|al-Tawba 37]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
People would change the order of the sacred months whenever they wished. The Qur'an fixed the months at their set number, which does not change.

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
Ibn Ishaq gives the interpretation of the verse on nasi' in Surat al-Tawba. The Arabs would make lawful one of the sacred months and forbid another in its place, to match the count of what God had forbidden. The verse was revealed condemning the practice, and it is not permitted to make lawful what God has forbidden or to forbid what He has made lawful {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/193}. See [[event/hajj-led-by-abu-bakr|Abu Bakr leads the pilgrimage, 9 AH]].

## Nasi' in al-Biruni
In [[work/al-athar-al-baqiya|al-Athar al-Baqiya]], [[person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad|al-Biruni]] says the pre-Islamic Arabs tracked the surplus of the solar year over their lunar year and added a month whenever it amounted to one; this was done by the intercalators of Kinana known as the Qalamis (singular Qalammas), the first of them Hudhayfa ibn Abd ibn Fuqaym, and they took the practice from the Jews about two hundred years before Islam. {cite:athar-baqiya-thaqafa-2008 p.13} {cite:athar-baqiya-thaqafa-2008 p.14} He describes how the intercalator would rise after the pilgrimage, address the assembly, postpone the month and give the next month its name, until the postponement had passed through all twelve months. {cite:athar-baqiya-thaqafa-2008 p.75} He says that when the Prophet emigrated the turn of intercalation had reached Sha'ban, which was called Muharram; then at the Farewell Pilgrimage he declared that time had come round to its form on the day God created the heavens and the earth and recited the verse forbidding nasi' ([[passage/quran-9-37|al-Tawba 37]]), after which intercalation was abandoned and the months moved away from their seasons. {cite:athar-baqiya-thaqafa-2008 p.14} {cite:athar-baqiya-thaqafa-2008 p.75}
