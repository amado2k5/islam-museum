## Nature and selection
Ashna is fine delicate crusts that wind around the oak, pine and walnut trees, and it has a sweet smell; some said that it is brought from the land of India {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}. The good kind is the white, and the black is poor; the book reports Dioscorides as saying that the best is what grows on the shirbin, which is the pine, then what is found on the walnut, and the best is the sweetest of smell and the white leaning to blue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.351}.

## Temperament and actions
It has a slight coldness leaning to dullness and a moderate astringency; some claimed that it is hot in the first degree and dry in the second, and the Khuz said that it is cold and very dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. It has the power of astringency and resolving together with softening, especially the pine kind, whose astringency is moderate, while the oak kind opens obstructions and tightens slack flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. The book names a substitute for it, qardamana {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}.

## Uses by organ
It is applied to hot swellings and soothes them, resolves hardnesses and soothes swellings of soft flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. It enters the oils for fatigue, resolves hardness of the joints, and so does its decoction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. When it is steeped in wine it puts the drinker to sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. It clears the sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. It is helpful for palpitation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}. It holds back vomiting, strengthens the stomach and removes its distension, especially in an astringent drink, and helps a weak pain of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it opens obstructions of the womb, that when a woman sits in its water it helps pain of the womb, and that it promotes the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.352}; reports attributed to the book, without recommendation.
