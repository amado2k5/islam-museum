## Summary
From the year found, al-Battani divides the three hundred and sixty parts of the circle to obtain the Sun's mean motion in a day, then in thirty days, the Egyptian month, then in three hundred and sixty-five days, the Egyptian year, which is three hundred and fifty-nine parts, forty-five minutes and forty-six seconds with further fractions following, and he enters these motions in tables {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.68}.

## The tables
He says he added these motions and entered them in the tables for collected and expanded years and for months, days and hours, by the Arab and the Roman reckonings, so that the knowledge may lead to finding the place of the Sun's motion by its mean motion, called the mean Sun, at any time wanted in either reckoning. The figure for the daily motion itself is scattered in the printed text and is not given; only the value for the Egyptian year and what follows from it is taken from the text {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.68}.
