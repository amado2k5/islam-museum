## Summary
Al-Battani opens chapter 1 by praising God and invoking blessing on the last of the messengers, then calls the craft of the stars among the noblest sciences in rank, one whose parts a person cannot be ignorant of in the religion's rules and customs, for what it gives of the length of years, months and appointed times, the seasons, the lengthening and shortening of day and night, the places and eclipses of the two luminaries and the course of the planets; and because steady study of it leads to proofs of God's oneness and to knowing the Creator's greatness. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.10-11}.

## The opening
The chapter begins by praising God who created creatures by His power, ordered affairs by His will and made them firm by His wisdom, with the two testimonies, then invokes blessing on the last of the messengers, his family and companions and those who follow his path until the Day of Judgement. This is the customary opening of Muslim writings and places a book of calculation and astronomy within the manner of religious books. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.10}.

## The standing of the science
He calls astronomy one of the noblest sciences, finest in its clarity, closest to hearts, the sharpest spur to thought and inquiry, a polishing of understanding and a training of the mind, coming after the knowledge of what a person may not be ignorant of in the religion's rules and customs. Among its benefits he lists knowing the length of years, months and appointed times, the seasons, the growth and shrinking of day and night, the places and eclipses of the two luminaries, the planets' direct and retrograde course, the changes of their aspects and the ranks of their spheres. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.10}.

## Looking at creation as a proof of oneness
Al-Battani ties this science to proving God's oneness and knowing the true greatness of the Creator, the breadth of His dominion, the beauty of His power and the delicacy of His making, and he cites several Qur'anic verses on the creation of the heavens and the earth, the alternation of night and day, the Sun as radiance and the Moon as light, and the measuring of the Moon's stations so that the number of years and the reckoning may be known. This page does not reproduce the verses and only describes their subject. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.10}.
