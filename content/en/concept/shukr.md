## Summary
In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons, gratitude is owed for blessings people overlook: general ones such as air, water, security and health, and private ones such as wealth and a home. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:58:30} He holds that patience and gratitude are the two halves of faith (his statement, needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:12:30}

## General and private blessings
The Sheikh says many people thank God for private blessings, such as wealth, children, a house and a car, and forget general ones such as air and the seas. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:59:00}
He notes that people praise God for rain only when it is withheld, and pray for rain in drought, but do not thank Him in plenty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:58:30}
He gives an example about fish and their breathing that could not be checked (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:59:30}

## The body and provision
He lists the blessings of the body, such as teeth, tongue and stomach, and says a person should ask God for each organ to do its work and thank Him for it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:16:30}
He traces the chain of provision from the farmer to the miller, the baker and the carrier, and thanks God for all of it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:09:30}
He calls for thanks to God for provision and for work. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 01:11:00}

## Security, health and medicine
He reflects on a verse about feeding the hungry and securing the fearful, and holds that security is a blessing that only the one who has lost it fully values. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:23:30} He says whoever follows the news without praising God for what he has has overlooked the blessing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:24:30}
He says that affordable medicine is a blessing, and compares it with a poor patient who cannot pay for it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:27:00}
He says that had we prostrated on burning coals in thanks for security, we would not have given it its due. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:24:00}

## Visiting the sick
The Sheikh urges visits to hospitals and recitation of the opening chapter for the sick, and holds that visits lighten the trial, since many people are afflicted. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:25:30}

## Understanding and ability
He says that reading the Qur'an beautifully in front of someone who does not understand Arabic is a blessing, and that understanding is another. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:28:00}
He thanks God for the ability to lift a leg for ablution, while relatives suffer from arthritis. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:28:30}
He warns against letting blessings become a habit that a person no longer notices. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:35:00}

## Attributing blessings to God
He contrasts the believer who attributes a blessing to God's favour, as he says Solomon did, with one who attributes it to himself, and with the owner of the garden who wronged himself and thought his garden would never perish (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:50:30}

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

He says that gratitude for blessings means spending them in pursuit of God's pleasure and not using them in disobedience to Him, and he calls for avoiding smoking, narcotics and intoxicants {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:30:30}.

He distinguishes praiseworthy reminding of favours, in which God mentions His blessings upon the Prophet [[passage/quran-93-6|Qur'an 93:6]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:30:00}, from blameworthy reminding, in which the giver looks down on others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:30:00}. He sets Pharaoh's reminder to Moses of his upbringing beside a person who reminds his nephew of what he spent on him, and calls for gentleness towards orphans [[passage/quran-93-9|Qur'an 93:9]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:30:30}. He wishes for a family a righteous son and a committed, veiled daughter, and sees the blessing of Islam appearing in the children {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:33:00}.

He relates that the earth, the mountains, the seas and the sky complain of human ingratitude for God's blessings, and ask leave to punish him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:15:00}. He advises that one who is first afflicted should begin by recalling the health and limbs that remain, so as to be patient {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:04:00}. He counts God's blessings from the time of the foetus, such as the umbilical cord and mother's milk, likening them to doors that open as one door closes, and recalls the scholars' saying not to forget the past favours of blessing {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:07:00}.

He gives the example of a man with chickens who thanks God for what remains to him, and says that gratitude preserves a blessing and increases it, citing a saying attributed to the Prophet on the goodness of God's blessing when it is kept well (needs_source: anecdote-unnamed) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:13:00}.

He holds that God has bestowed on people blessings both apparent and hidden, general and private {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:00:30}; that the believer knows the value of a blessing while it exists, while others know it only after it is gone, and that the grateful among God's servants are few [[passage/quran-34-13|Qur'an 34:13]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:01:00}. He draws attention to the blessing of being able to sit and move, which people thank for only when their backs hurt, and he sets out doctors' advice such as limiting weight (needs_source: medical-weight-limit-figure) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:05:00}.

He distinguishes general blessings, such as the sun, the night and sleep, which only the believer thanks for, from private blessings, such as wealth, children and a home, which others also thank for {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:08:30}. He says that one who is afflicted recalls what remains to him of eye, hand, leg, mind, ear and tongue, and so his despair lessens; he gives the example of lands where the sun is not seen for months (needs_source: polar-night-claim) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:12:30}.

He recounts the hadith of three people whom God tested with leprosy, baldness and blindness: whoever gave thanks kept his blessing, whoever denied it lost it, and he notes that pride in a blessing removes it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:11:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh complains, in his own words, about speed bumps and poor roads in some countries, and gives thanks to God for the blessing of good roads in this country {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:20:00}. He says that the poor man who may not own his day's food, the sick person and the detained prisoner, when each is asked how he is, answers with praise of God and finds a blessing in his state, for instance that the prisoner is free to read the Qur'an {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:50:00}. He asks whether any of us prostrated in thanks to God when the weather in the Levant, northern Jordan and northern Lebanon turned mild, at 22 to 25 degrees, easing the lot of the refugees there (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:00:00}. He says he dislikes the word 'stranger' because the earth belongs to God, and he holds that poverty, insecurity and fear make a person a stranger even in his own homeland {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:16:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)

The Sheikh calls on his listeners to remember the afflicted, saying that those present sit safe, hearing the lesson by God's grace, while there are people better than they, more honourable and closer to God, who are displaced in tents or camps {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:22:00}. He speaks of the blessing of security and safety, and then begins an account of a veterinary experiment in which two sheep were brought, one healthy and one sick (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:22:30}. In the account he says the researcher set food before them and then placed a wolf in a cage at some distance, and the sick sheep saw it and was afraid (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:23:00}. The Sheikh thinks, and God knows best, that a man seeing his children go off to pray is a blessing worth noticing {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:26:00}.

He asks: if you see your neighbour's children or your brother's daughters without hijab or prayer, while your own children pray and your daughter wears hijab at fifteen, where is your prostration of thanks to God? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:26:30}. He says that a person whom God keeps healthy, so that incense smoke does not harm him or choke him into coughing and sneezing, and a person who reads the Qur'an in a beautiful voice and knows the senses of the Arabic language, owe a prostration of gratitude {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:27:30}. He says it is God who guides the servant to obedience and makes the doing of good dear to him, and yet the servant then attributes the good to himself although all good is from God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:35:30}.

## Blessings that cannot be counted, and that are often forgotten
The Sheikh says that God's blessings cannot be counted, and that people usually thank God for the blessings that are their own, such as health, wealth, a spouse or a child. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:00:30} He lists general blessings such as the proportion of oxygen in the air, the distance of the Moon and the tides, and says few people thank God for them, and that only the believer notices them (needs_source: the figures and the astronomical descriptions are not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:02:00} He says that blessings cannot be counted, and that gratitude is what keeps them. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:20:30} He lists everyday blessings: air, health, faith, a righteous spouse and children, mosques, and the call to prayer. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:22:30}
He cites a Qur'anic passage on rejoicing in God's favour and on counting His blessings, which cannot be counted (the verse is not named in the notes). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:13:00} He says that few of God's servants are grateful, and takes the family of David as an example of thankfulness. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:12:30} He says that David asked how he could thank God, that the question itself is a blessing, and that most people do not give real thanks. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:44:00} He tells the listener to look at people who have no relatives, no spouse and no helper, to see how many blessings he has. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:10:30} He says that when trial comes, a person should first count the blessings God gave him, such as his mind and his life. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:12:00} He tells of Moses asking how he could thank God; the answer was that Moses had thanked Him, since admitting that he could not thank Him enough is itself thanks. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:38:00}

## What gratitude means and how it appears
He says that saying "alhamdulillah" is one degree of gratitude, and gives a lexical account of gratitude as the visible trace of a blessing on the body (needs_source: the lexical account is the speaker's and has not been checked). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:04:00} He says that verbal thanks is one form of gratitude; thanks for wealth is spending it, and thanks for knowledge is teaching it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:08:30} He says that gratitude for status is to use it to serve people and meet their needs, and reports scholars' definition that gratitude means keeping present blessings and seeking absent ones (needs_source: the scholars' definition is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:09:30}
He says that a person may thank a boss or an official, but that provision comes from God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:12:00} He likens gratitude to tying an animal to a stake so that it does not escape, and cites a Qur'anic passage that gratitude brings increase (the verse is not named in the notes). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:10:00} He cites a Qur'anic passage that gratitude brings increase, and says that the Arabic word for an eye full of water is the root of the word for gratitude. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:02:00}
He says that gratitude fills the heart and shows in the limbs, as the signs of a blessing show on a well-kept horse, and that the greatest blessing is steadfastness in the tongue and in deeds. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:23:30} He says that a grateful Muslim does not backbite, gossip, cheat or harm people, and that the blessing of Islam must show in conduct. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:03:00}
He says that blessings are credited to God's favour and not to the servant's own strength; effort has its place, but the root is God's favour, and a righteous child and an obedient wife are signs of that favour. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:11:30} He cites a Qur'anic passage on thanking or being ungrateful when blessed (the verse is not named in the notes). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:11:00}

## The pillars of gratitude
He names five conditions of gratitude, the first being the submission of the one who thanks to the one who is thanked. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:11:00} He says gratitude requires submission to the giver, love of the giver, recognition of his favour and frequent praise of him, and asks families to memorise this. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:25:00} He divides gratitude into gratitude for what is loved and gratitude for what is disliked, and says that its highest level is that the servant sees only the giver. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:25:30} He compares a driver who boasts of an award from a wealthy boss with the believer, who should take pride in God's favour, and says that gratitude is either servanthood or love. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:42:00}
He says that the first rule of gratitude is submission to the one thanked, and takes as his example a child who asks his father courteously. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:04:00} He says that the second rule is love of the one thanked, and that gratitude must come from the heart and the tongue together. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:05:00} He names the fourth and fifth rules: to remember a deed done sincerely for God, and never to use God's blessings to disobey Him, which he calls the most important rule. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:15:00}

## Gratitude in wealth and family
He says that the absence of blessing from wealth comes from earnings mixed with the forbidden, and that such wealth is cleansed by returning it or giving it away. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:24:30} He says that wealth that lacks blessing despite the payment of zakat should be examined: whether zakat was due, whether it was paid in full, and whether it was given to those entitled to it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:17:00} He gives the example of a father who gives his son a phone, and the son uses it to reveal the father's secrets, and says that blessings must not be used to anger parents or God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:16:00} He says that a community in which rights are lost has no blessing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:24:30} He says that cutting family ties removes blessing from lifespans, children and wealth. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:29:00} He cites a hadith that the best of people is the best to his family, and says that gratitude for children, a wife, wealth and work brings increase. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:22:00} He says that gratitude is shown by using wealth, status or office to serve people, and that the head of a household or a manager is answerable before God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:29:30}
The speaker's view: he criticises a person who thanks God but does not pray or pay zakat, a rich man who hoards, a manager who oppresses those who work for him, and a father who favours some of his children. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:06:00} He blames a husband who denies past kindness and a father who looks down on his wife, and says that children learn these habits. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:05:30}

## Gratitude in trial and hope
He says that a person in trial should feel God's favour, and expect tomorrow to be better than today. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:19:00} He distinguishes the one who thanks in ease (shakir) from the one who thanks in trial (shakur), and holds the second to be the higher rank. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:18:30} He says that the one who thanks in trial praises God when denied what the soul loves, such as wealth, children or a grand house, and that the denial may be good. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:19:00}
He says that gratitude ties blessings down: health, good children, a righteous neighbour, Islam and the call to prayer, which people in Muslim lands take for granted. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:15:30} He cites a hadith that a people who do not keep God's blessings close will lose them, and that once lost they rarely return. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep16 00:16:00} He says that God gives nations hunger and fear when they are ungrateful for blessings (the sura is not named in the notes). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:21:00} He says that a person who takes God's mercy, children, faith, wealth and status as lost, and despairs, becomes ungrateful. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:10:30} He says that the one who truly trusts in God is blessed, and that the sorrowing should not despair, since God is the reliever of distress. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:32:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh distinguishes the grateful (shakir) from the very grateful (shakur): the first thanks God for blessing, the second for blessing and affliction alike; he mentions that in Arabic an animal 'shows gratitude' when the effects of good feeding appear on it, and asks: where are the effects on you of the blessings of fasting, prayer, uprightness and the presence of scholars? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:09:30}

## From the series Qissa wa 'Ibar
In the fourth episode Omar Abdelkafy presents the story of the owner of the two gardens as a Qur'anic parable of the grateful and the ungrateful, holding that the danger is to forget the Giver and remember only the gift, and closes by saying the rich should give thanks and the poor be patient. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep04 00:10:00} In the seventh episode he says that a blessing does not leave if it is thanked for, and slips away if it is not tied by gratitude; he cites a saying of the Prophet to 'A'isha about being good neighbours to God's blessings, and applies it to the individual, the community and the state. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:03:30}
