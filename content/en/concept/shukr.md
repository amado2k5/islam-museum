## Summary
In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons, gratitude is owed for blessings people overlook: general ones such as air, water, security and health, and private ones such as wealth and a home. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:58:30} He holds that patience and gratitude are the two halves of faith (his statement, needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:12:30}

## General and private blessings
The Sheikh says many people thank God for private blessings, such as wealth, children, a house and a car, and forget general ones such as air and the seas. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:59:00}
He notes that people praise God for rain only when it is withheld, and pray for rain in drought, but do not thank Him in plenty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:58:30}
He gives an example about fish and their breathing that could not be checked (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:59:30}

## The body and provision
He lists the blessings of the body, such as teeth, tongue and stomach, and says a person should ask God for each organ to do its work and thank Him for it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:16:30}
He traces the chain of provision from the farmer to the miller, the baker and the carrier, and thanks God for all of it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:09:30}
He calls for thanks to God for provision and for work. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 01:11:00}

## Security, health and medicine
He reflects on a verse about feeding the hungry and securing the fearful, and holds that security is a blessing that only the one who has lost it fully values. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:23:30} He says whoever follows the news without praising God for what he has has overlooked the blessing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:24:30}
He says that affordable medicine is a blessing, and compares it with a poor patient who cannot pay for it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:27:00}
He says that had we prostrated on burning coals in thanks for security, we would not have given it its due. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:24:00}

## Visiting the sick
The Sheikh urges visits to hospitals and recitation of the opening chapter for the sick, and holds that visits lighten the trial, since many people are afflicted. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:25:30}

## Understanding and ability
He says that reading the Qur'an beautifully in front of someone who does not understand Arabic is a blessing, and that understanding is another. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:28:00}
He thanks God for the ability to lift a leg for ablution, while relatives suffer from arthritis. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:28:30}
He warns against letting blessings become a habit that a person no longer notices. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:35:00}

## Attributing blessings to God
He contrasts the believer who attributes a blessing to God's favour, as he says Solomon did, with one who attributes it to himself, and with the owner of the garden who wronged himself and thought his garden would never perish (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:50:30}

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

He says that gratitude for blessings means spending them in pursuit of God's pleasure and not using them in disobedience to Him, and he calls for avoiding smoking, narcotics and intoxicants {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:30:30}.

He distinguishes praiseworthy reminding of favours, in which God mentions His blessings upon the Prophet [[passage/quran-93-6|Qur'an 93:6]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:30:00}, from blameworthy reminding, in which the giver looks down on others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:30:00}. He sets Pharaoh's reminder to Moses of his upbringing beside a person who reminds his nephew of what he spent on him, and calls for gentleness towards orphans [[passage/quran-93-9|Qur'an 93:9]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:30:30}. He wishes for a family a righteous son and a committed, veiled daughter, and sees the blessing of Islam appearing in the children {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep05 00:33:00}.

He relates that the earth, the mountains, the seas and the sky complain of human ingratitude for God's blessings, and ask leave to punish him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:15:00}. He advises that one who is first afflicted should begin by recalling the health and limbs that remain, so as to be patient {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:04:00}. He counts God's blessings from the time of the foetus, such as the umbilical cord and mother's milk, likening them to doors that open as one door closes, and recalls the scholars' saying not to forget the past favours of blessing {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:07:00}.

He gives the example of a man with chickens who thanks God for what remains to him, and says that gratitude preserves a blessing and increases it, citing a saying attributed to the Prophet on the goodness of God's blessing when it is kept well (needs_source: anecdote-unnamed) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:13:00}.

He holds that God has bestowed on people blessings both apparent and hidden, general and private {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:00:30}; that the believer knows the value of a blessing while it exists, while others know it only after it is gone, and that the grateful among God's servants are few [[passage/quran-34-13|Qur'an 34:13]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:01:00}. He draws attention to the blessing of being able to sit and move, which people thank for only when their backs hurt, and he sets out doctors' advice such as limiting weight (needs_source: medical-weight-limit-figure) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:05:00}.

He distinguishes general blessings, such as the sun, the night and sleep, which only the believer thanks for, from private blessings, such as wealth, children and a home, which others also thank for {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:08:30}. He says that one who is afflicted recalls what remains to him of eye, hand, leg, mind, ear and tongue, and so his despair lessens; he gives the example of lands where the sun is not seen for months (needs_source: polar-night-claim) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep12 00:12:30}.

He recounts the hadith of three people whom God tested with leprosy, baldness and blindness: whoever gave thanks kept his blessing, whoever denied it lost it, and he notes that pride in a blessing removes it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep14 00:11:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh complains, in his own words, about speed bumps and poor roads in some countries, and gives thanks to God for the blessing of good roads in this country {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:20:00}. He says that the poor man who may not own his day's food, the sick person and the detained prisoner, when each is asked how he is, answers with praise of God and finds a blessing in his state, for instance that the prisoner is free to read the Qur'an {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:50:00}. He asks whether any of us prostrated in thanks to God when the weather in the Levant, northern Jordan and northern Lebanon turned mild, at 22 to 25 degrees, easing the lot of the refugees there (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:00:00}. He says he dislikes the word 'stranger' because the earth belongs to God, and he holds that poverty, insecurity and fear make a person a stranger even in his own homeland {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:16:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)

The Sheikh calls on his listeners to remember the afflicted, saying that those present sit safe, hearing the lesson by God's grace, while there are people better than they, more honourable and closer to God, who are displaced in tents or camps {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:22:00}. He speaks of the blessing of security and safety, and then begins an account of a veterinary experiment in which two sheep were brought, one healthy and one sick (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:22:30}. In the account he says the researcher set food before them and then placed a wolf in a cage at some distance, and the sick sheep saw it and was afraid (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:23:00}. The Sheikh thinks, and God knows best, that a man seeing his children go off to pray is a blessing worth noticing {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:26:00}.

He asks: if you see your neighbour's children or your brother's daughters without hijab or prayer, while your own children pray and your daughter wears hijab at fifteen, where is your prostration of thanks to God? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:26:30}. He says that a person whom God keeps healthy, so that incense smoke does not harm him or choke him into coughing and sneezing, and a person who reads the Qur'an in a beautiful voice and knows the senses of the Arabic language, owe a prostration of gratitude {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:27:30}. He says it is God who guides the servant to obedience and makes the doing of good dear to him, and yet the servant then attributes the good to himself although all good is from God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:35:30}.
