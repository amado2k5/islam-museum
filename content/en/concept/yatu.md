## Definition and kinds
The book says that yatu' is any plant with a sharp, purgative, cutting and burning milk. Seven well-known kinds are named in the book's Arabic names, among them al-qashr, al-shabram, al-la'iya, al-'urtunitha, al-mahudana, al-mazariyun and bantafilun. Other kinds are noted, such as an ear-of-mouse type, an ivy type and a wild spurge. The milk of al-la'iya is taken as the milk of yatu' in general. The male kind is called hanaqiyas and the others female; the strongest is said to resemble myrtle and is called mur titas. Further kinds are described as rock-dwelling, cucumber-like, coastal, broad-leaved, and the sun-turning kind called al-mushammis, which turns with the sun. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.512}

## Temperament and potency
The milk is hot and dry in the fourth degree, and the other parts of these plants are hot and dry in the second to the third degree. The book adds that the strongest part is the milk, then the seed, then the root, then the leaf. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.513}

## Properties
It is ulcerating and deadly. When it falls into a pond, all the fish float up. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.513}

## Adornment
It removes warts, moles, growths and surplus flesh beside the nails. Its milk removes hair when smeared in the sun; the hair that grows back is weak, and with repeated use it does not regrow at all. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.514}

## Wounds and ulcers
Its roots in vinegar dissolve the hard swelling round piles, remove ringworm, and help putrid and eating ulcers, the book says. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.514}

## Teeth and eye
Dropped on a decaying tooth, its milk crumbles and loosens it, and a root boiled in vinegar used as a mouthwash eases toothache. Its milk also removes the eye film called zufra. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.514}

## Purging and substitutes
The book says its milk clears the piles, and purges phlegm and watery humours, with the milk taken on a base. The amounts and the methods are left out. The book names iris and scammony as substitutes for purging the watery humour in the intestines and the phlegm in the body; the weights are left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.514}
