## Summary
Khasa' is the removal of the two testicles; the jurists use it for taking the testicles without or with the penis; the castrated man is one whose penis is cut off, the impotent (unan) cannot have intercourse, and wijā' is crushing the testicles' vessels without removal. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7643}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7644} Castrating a human is forbidden for child or adult by agreement, on reports of Ibn Mas'ud and Sa'd; the Prophet refused Uthman ibn Maz'un celibacy; a report from Umar says "no castration in Islam"; Ibn Hajar gives as reasons that it defeats the growth of offspring, tortures the self and alters God's creation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7645} For animals: the Hanafis see no harm for their benefit; the Malikis allow it for edible animals without dislike; the Shafiis allow it for edible animals when young, if it does not kill; the Hanbalis allow castrating sheep (one view dislikes it), citing the Prophet's strong prohibition on castrating animals. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7646} On annulment of marriage: the Hanafis treat a castrated husband like an impotent one, with a one-year delay; the Malikis give the wife a choice if he does not ejaculate; the Shafiis have two views; the Hanbalis give no choice if he can have intercourse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7646}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7647} On retaliation for the testicles: most jurists order retaliation when its conditions hold; the Shafiis treat crushing or paralysing them as cutting; the Malikis allow no retaliation for crushing; the Hanafis find no text for retaliation and so require blood-money; the Prophet's letter to Amr ibn Hazm says "blood-money for the testicles". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7647}
