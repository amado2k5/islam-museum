## Summary
Ujra, lexically and in law, is the counterpart of a benefit: what a worker receives for his labour, and what the owner of a thing receives for its use. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1593}

## Conditions
A rent may be anything that may serve as a price: goods, another benefit, or money paid at once or deferred. It must be known to both parties by pointing, by naming or by statement. A contract with an unknown rent is not valid, nor one whose rent is part of the work or of its product, such as hiring a man to skin a sheep for its hide. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1593}

## Wine and pork
Wine, pork and the like may serve as rent only for non-Muslims. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1593}

## Where the topic is treated
Rent is treated under the lease. The taking of a fee for acts of worship is treated under the call to prayer, pilgrimage and jihad; for division under division; for pledge and surety for rent under those contracts; and for price regulation under sales. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1593}
