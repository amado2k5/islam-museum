## Overview
The sabil-kuttab is an attractive two-storey structure: below, a public fountain (sabil) dispensing water through iron window grilles; above, a Qur'an school for children (kuttab), known by its open windows and balconies. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.205} The combination became a feature of fifteenth-century mosque architecture and later appeared as a separate building, a distinctive part of Cairo's townscape. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.205}

## Examples
Two sabil-kuttabs flank the façade of the [[building/farag-ibn-barquq-complex|khanqah of Faraj ibn Barquq]], their sloping wooden canopies, balcony screens and ablaq masonry relieving the weight of the monumental front. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.205} The [[building/funerary-complex-of-sultan-inal|Inal complex]] keeps the remains of one, and in the [[building/emir-qurqumas-complex|Qurqumas complex]] a sabil-kuttab forms the eastern corner. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.215} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.217}
