## Summary
Karaha is, in the lexicon, dislike. In the terminology it is a demand to abstain from an act that is not firm {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13238}.

## Kinds
Karaha divides into dislike of prohibition (tahrim), which is close to forbidden, and which Muhammad ibn al-Hasan calls a probable prohibition; and dislike of reprehension (tanzih), where leaving the act is better {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13239}. If the proof is a probable prohibition, dislike of prohibition is the ruling unless something turns it to recommendation; if the proof is not a prohibition but a non-firm request to leave, it is dislike of reprehension {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13239}.

## Senses of "disliked"
Al-Zarkashi says the word has four senses: the forbidden, as in the Qur'an's "all that was its evil is disliked by your Lord" in Surat al-Isra'; what is forbidden by a reprehensive prohibition; leaving the better; and a thing whose prohibition is doubtful, such as the meat of a predator or a little nabidh. Al-Shafi'i used the word in the sense of prohibition on some matters, and the earlier scholars avoided the word "forbidden" for things that were not clear {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13239} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13240}.

## Leaving the better
Al-Zarkashi says the usul scholars neglected leaving the better, which the jurists treat as a middle ground between dislike and permission {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13240}. The Imam al-Haramayn separates the two: where a prohibition is stated, the act is disliked; where none is stated, it is leaving the better. Al-Zarkashi holds that leaving the better is a grade of dislike, since otherwise the rulings would number six, which is not the known division {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13240} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13241}. Ibn Abidin says that voluntary worship is better performed than left without cause, and that leaving it is not called disliked in the sense of reprehension {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13241}.
