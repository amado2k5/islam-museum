## Summary
Al-mukatabat in the tradition's idiom is the exchange of letters and written messages between kings, sultans, caliphs, governors, envoys and correspondents — what is written, sent, read and answered. This record gathers the genre in the museum, and the [[/en/mukatabat/|Mukatabat Browser]] gathers it with every mention and letter in the Qur'an, the Sunnah, the seerah and every harvested book — and whatever is harvested later.

## The earliest reported correspondence
The oldest correspondence preserved in the museum is the Prophet's letters to the kings: after the Hudaybiyya truce he sent envoys bearing letters sealed with a silver seal «Muhammad, Messenger of God» to Chosroes، Caesar، the Negus، the Muqawqis and the Arab chiefs — as in [[event/letters-to-rulers-and-kings|the letters to rulers and kings]]. Al-Bukhari reports that Heraclius called for his interpreter then for the Prophet's letter and read it: «In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate, from Muhammad, servant of God and His messenger, to Heraclius» in [[passage/bukhari-api-7541|Sahih al-Bukhari 7541]]، and that Heraclius wrote back to the Prophet answering what he understood of the letter. Muslim reports that the Prophet wished to write to Chosroes، Caesar and the Negus but was told they accept no letter without a seal، so he fashioned a silver ring seal engraved «Muhammad، Messenger of God» in [[passage/muslim-api-5482|Sahih Muslim 5482]].

## A letter in the Qur'an
The oldest written text narrated in the museum is Sulayman's letter to the Queen of Sheba: «Indeed it is from Sulayman، and indeed it is: in the name of God، the Merciful، the Compassionate. Indeed upon me [is] that I should thank you» [[passage/quran-27-30|al-Naml 30]] — a letter opening with the sender's name and the basmala، offering her gifts of bracelets and inviting her to Islam.

## The caliphs' and governors' correspondence
Correspondence is not confined to the prophetic era: the caliphs wrote to their governors، factors and commanders as the Muwatta، the Sunan and the history books report — Umar's letter to the people of Egypt، Ali's letter to Muawiya after the Battle of the Camel، al-Hajjaj's letters to Basra، and the Abbasid caliphs' letters to their governors across the realms. States adopted a diwan al-insha' managing this correspondence، a diwan al-barid carrying it، and a diwan al-khatim sealing it — al-Qalqashandi recording in Subh al-a'sha that the chancery diwan was «the first diwan established in Islam» because the Prophet corresponded with his commanders and the kings.

## What is not of this genre
Out of scope: the mukataba contract by which a slave manumits himself in instalments (a chapter of fiqh)، the seal of prophethood، the prophetic message as mission، the sermon and the address (khuṭba، khuṭāb) as preaching، and every written book — only the exchange or dispatch between two parties of kingship and authority.
