## Overview
An administrative-juristic institution that arose in the Islamic state — its root the command of right and forbidding of wrong — held by the muhtasib: oversight of markets, weights and measures, guarding public morals and fighting fraud in sales. The edition-preface of Ma'alim al-Qurba notes the Rightly Guided caliphs held it themselves at first, then appointed muhtasibs as the conquests widened {cite:maalim-qurba-edition-1976 p.5} {cite:maalim-qurba-edition-1976 p.6}.

## Hisba in Ibn Taymiyya
In [[work/al-hisba-fi-al-islam|al-Hisba fi al-Islam]], [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] counts hisba among the Islamic offices - with the police, judgeship and finance - whose common purpose is commanding right and forbidding wrong. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p.11} He holds that the scope of offices is learned from wording, circumstance and custom, with no fixed limit in the law, and that the muhtasib commands right and forbids wrong in what is not reserved to governors, judges and the registry: he orders the five prayers and punishes those who neglect them with beating and prison, not death; oversees imams and muezzins; enjoins Friday and congregational prayer, truthful speech and honesty in trusts; and forbids short measure and fraud in crafts, sales and religion. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p.15} {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p.16} {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p.17} He places the muhtasib among those who stand as obeyed trustees, of whom justice is required. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p.11} {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p.12}
