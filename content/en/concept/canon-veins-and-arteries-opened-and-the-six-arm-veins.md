## Summary
Ibn Sina explains that the vessels that are opened are some veins and some arteries, that arteries are opened least because of the danger of haemorrhage, then names the six veins of the arm and where they are opened, above the elbow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## Arteries
Know that of the vessels that are opened some are veins and some arteries. Arteries are opened least, and the danger that falls on them from the flow of blood is guarded against; the least that can happen is an aneurysm, and that when the incision is very narrow. Yet when haemorrhage from them is made safe they are of great benefit in particular diseases for which they are bled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## The benefit of opening an artery
Most of the benefit of opening an artery comes when the organ next to it has bad diseases whose cause is thin, sharp blood; then, if the neighbouring artery is opened and is not one where there is danger, it is of great benefit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## The six veins
The veins of the arm are six: the cephalic, the median cutaneous vein, the basilic, the funis brachi, the asilam, and the one specially called the axillary, which is a branch of the basilic; the safest of them is the cephalic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.

## Where it is opened
For all three the vessel must be opened above the hollow of the elbow, not below it nor level with it, so that the blood comes out well as it clears and the harms to nerve and artery are avoided {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.294}.
