## Summary
Chapter 19 teaches how to find the day arc of any star in any place, meaning the part of the equator's circle that rises from the star's rising to its setting. The first step is a difference obtained by multiplying the chord of the star's distance from the equator by the chord of the latitude of the place, dividing by the chord of the complement of the latitude, multiplying by the radius and dividing by the chord of the complement of the star's distance; the arc of the result is the difference sought. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.52-53}.

## How the difference is named
The text calls the result a difference belonging to the star's circle; the descriptive word before 'circle' is damaged in the scan and is not reproduced here. The working rests on the half-chords and the complements as in the earlier chapters; what is new is only the entry, which is the star's distance from the equator and not the declination of a degree of the ecliptic. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.53}.
