## Definition
The chadar is a sloping stone ramp in the Mughal garden linking two terraces, down which water rushes, its surface broken with carving or ridges to increase the rippling and sparkle. With unbroken cascades it was one of the means of joining the levels of the terraced gardens of Kashmir. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.141}

## Examples
[[person/john-brookes|Brookes]] notes it at the [[building/shalimar-bagh-srinagar|Shalimar Bagh]]; at the [[building/nishat-bagh|Nishat Bagh]], where every change of level has a cascade or a rushing chadar; and at the [[building/chashma-shahi|Chashma Shahi]], where the water drops down a steep chadar as narrow as a slide. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.141} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.144} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.147} Brookes holds that this Indian tradition of rushing water was later carried back to the water gardens of the Zands and then the Qajars in Persia. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.122}
