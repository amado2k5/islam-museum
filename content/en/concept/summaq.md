## Nature
There is a Khurasani kind and a Syrian kind smaller than the Khurasani, red and lentil-like, and it suits what acacia and rose suit; when cooked in water and its decoction brought to the consistency of honey it suits what the hudad suits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Temperament
Cold in the second degree, dry in the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Actions and properties
Astringent, strengthening and blocking, vinegar being subtler than it; it checks flux, so that some say hanging it does the same, and it checks the flowing of bile to the viscera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Cosmetics
A decoction of the sumac of the tanners blackens the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Swellings and pustules
A blow dressed with it is kept from swelling and from tightness; it helps the whitlow and prevents swellings from growing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Joints
A sprain is bathed with its decoction so that it does not swell {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Organs of the head
It stops discharge from the ear, and its gum put in a decayed tooth calms its pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It tans the stomach and strengthens it, calms thirst, whets the appetite by its sourness and calms bilious nausea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Excretory organs
It stops abrasion, is given as an enema for dysentery, for flow from the womb and for piles, and, put into food, suits one with chronic diarrhoea, ulcer of the intestines and watery flux {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it restrains the menses and flux {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.597}.
