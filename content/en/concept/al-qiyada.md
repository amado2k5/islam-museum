## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan defines leadership as 'the ability to move people toward a goal', and holds that whoever wants to practise pure leadership works only in six roles; everything else is management and is handed to others {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:12:00}.

## The leader's six roles
First, planning: not necessarily planning himself, but making sure the organisation has a strategic and an operational plan into which his vision and ambitions entered {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:12:30}.
Second, opening new horizons for the work — innovation: new markets, products, services and methods, since the manager is too busy with the daily to think deeply {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:13:30}.
Third, building key informal relationships — with officials, funders, major customers, key suppliers and senior staff — and these are not delegated {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:19:30}.
Fourth, solving the chronic problems others have failed to solve {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:20:30}.
Fifth, developing himself and those he supervises, continuously {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:40:30}.
Sixth, appreciation and honouring the distinguished — not to be delegated; he thanks them publicly, and he learnt from the Prophet's sira that he used to grant titles to the distinguished, such as Abu Ubayda, al-Zubayr, Umar and Abu Bakr {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:51:00}.

## Leader and manager
He distinguishes leader from manager: an organisation needs both — one without a leader attends only to the daily, one without a manager only to the distant; and whoever can combine both is advised not to, but to remain the leader and hire a competent, trustworthy manager {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:52:30}.
His rule is that a leader attends no more than fifty per cent of working hours; the rest goes to thinking and building relationships {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:16:00}.
If he keeps one administrative role it is the financial decision — signing the cheques — because watching the money reveals to him all that goes on {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:53:30}.
When founding something new he practises heavy management alongside leadership, then lightens the management over time; the normal span is six months to a year, never beyond two {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:55:30}.
And his advice to whoever wants to lead is to refuse a job description, for the leader is the principal reference entitled to intervene in anything whenever he chooses {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 00:57:30}.

## In "Secrets of Prophetic Leadership": understanding leadership
In his programme [[work/asrar-al-qiyada-al-nabawiyya-program|"Secrets of Prophetic Leadership"]], [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|al-Suwaidan]] repeats the same definition, "the ability to move people towards a goal", and builds on it a model he calls "the model of understanding leadership", with four parts: ability, moving, followers and goal. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:04:30} In this programme he gives the leader eight roles instead of six; see [[concept/adwar-al-qaid-al-thamaniya|the leader's eight roles]].

**Innate or acquired.** After long reflection and training thousands, he estimates that 1% of people are natural-born leaders, 98% can acquire leadership, and 1% neither have it nor can learn it; one who acquires it can come close to the natural-born leader without reaching him, though he may surpass him in some learned skills. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:24:00} His example of a natural-born leader is [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:24:30}

**Leadership and management.** Management for him focuses on tasks, achievement and the short term; leadership on the future, relationships and the long term. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:30:00}

**Links leadership does not require.** In his view leadership is not necessarily tied to knowledge: [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr]] was learned and never led, while [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] was a leader and no scholar. Nor to age: the Prophet put [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]] in command of an army that included Abu Bakr and Umar when Usama was, by his estimate, seventeen or eighteen. Nor to physique, nor to lineage and wealth, for which he cites the story of Talut ([[passage/quran-2-247|al-Baqara 2:247]]). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:30:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:33:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:45:00} On strength and piety he relates Ahmad ibn Hanbal's answer when asked whether to campaign under a strong but sinful commander or a righteous but weak one: the strength of the first serves the Muslims and his sin is on himself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:48:00} He quotes John Adair, whom he describes as a British leading scholar of leadership, on the Prophet's sharing of hardship with his Companions. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep01 00:51:30}

**The Sira as evidence.** He gave episode 2 to a model he calls "the merits of the Sira": it joins religious and worldly reform; it embodies values in a model to follow; its sources are reliable; it is transparent; it is comprehensive; it holds for every age; and it was transmitted collectively without contradiction. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:29:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:35:00} He divides the sources of the Sira into six kinds: the Qur'an and its exegesis, hadith, the maghazi, works of Islamic history, the dala'il (proofs of prophethood), and the shama'il (the Prophet's qualities). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:40:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep02 00:43:00}

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders": making the leader
In the lessons on "making the leader" in [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]], al-Suwaidan says leadership has more than two hundred definitions and offers his own as the two hundred and first: "the ability to move people towards the goal". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:03:00} It has three elements: ability, followers and a goal. In the second academy he counts the act of moving as a fourth. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:12:00} He notes that the definition says nothing about ethics. It describes leadership in general, good or bad. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:05:30} He draws on Arabic: the word for leading comes from leading a riding animal from in front, and its opposite is driving it from behind with a stick. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:12:00} The Qur'an, he says, calls leaders imams rather than using the word for leader. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:55:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:15:00} Leadership looks to the future and works through people, while management wants achievement now and does the work itself. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:16:30}

On whether leadership is innate or acquired he uses a bell curve. He says at most about two per cent of people are born leaders and a similar share at the other end are unfit to lead. About ninety-six per cent are in the middle and can become leaders through training. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:25:00} As examples of born leaders he gives [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]], who commanded the [[event/expedition-of-dhat-al-salasil|expedition of Dhat al-Salasil]] a few months after accepting Islam, with Abu Bakr and Umar among his men. His counter-example is [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr al-Ghifari]], among the earliest Muslims, who never held office. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:15:30} From these he concludes that leadership is tied neither to seniority, nor to knowledge, nor to piety. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:16:30} In the second academy he adds age, citing [[person/usama-ibn-zayd|Usama ibn Zayd]], given command of an army as a young man. He adds sex as well. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:44:30} He also separates general from specialised leadership: [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] led armies but not the community, and Uthman led the community but not armies. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-2/ep06 00:14:30} See also [[concept/al-qiyada-al-takhassusiyya|specialised leadership]].

He then gives the leader six roles. Whoever focuses on them practises leadership, and everything else is management, to be delegated. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:33:00} The six are:
- supervising planning and putting his own vision into the plan; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:21:00}
- opening new horizons; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:22:00}
- building the key relationships, which he says take at least half a leader's time; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:27:30}
- solving only the chronic problems; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:28:30}
- developing himself and his followers; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:30:00}
- recognising achievers, because "recognition is not delegated". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:34:00}
He adds an aphorism of his own: "it is very easy to be busy, but very hard to be effective". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:33:00}

In the next lesson he says the leader presses his view hard but in the end abides by the decision of shura. He illustrates this with the consultation before [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:04:00} He then presents "quartets of useful and harmful leadership traits", sixteen traits in four fields, each with a useful form and a harmful excess. He says he revised them after a theory of 2007. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:06:30} Theorists, he says, disagree on the list of a leader's traits, but all agree on vision. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:09:00} His example of placing the right person is [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] choosing [[person/al-numan-ibn-muqarrin|al-Nu'man ibn Muqarrin]] for [[event/battle-of-nahawand|Nahawand]]. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:32:00} He adds that an effective leader knows when to withdraw. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:46:30} He closes with four further traits of the Muslim leader, beginning with faith and tawhid, with the hereafter as his aim. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep10 00:49:00}
