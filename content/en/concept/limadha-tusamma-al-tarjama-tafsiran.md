## Summary
Why do many scholars not call a translation of the Qur'an the Qur'an, calling it tafsir or a translation of meanings? This page sets out the reasons of three Muslim scholars, al-Zarqani, al-Shatibi and Ibn Taymiyya, and the words of an English translator, Pickthall, in the foreword to his translation. Each view is given under its holder as a research summary supplied to the museum reports it; the books and pages it points to were not opened, and the page does not judge between the views.

## The question and its background
A reader may ask why the jurists insist that a translation is not the Qur'an, as in [[concept/hal-al-tarjama-hiya-al-quran|Is a translation the Qur'an?]], and why translations carry words of meaning and interpretation in their titles. These questions meet the matter of inimitability, in [[concept/ijaz-al-quran|The inimitability of the Qur'an]], and the history of translation, in [[concept/awail-tarjamat-al-quran|The first translations of the Qur'an]] and [[concept/tarajim-al-quran-hasab-al-lugha|Qur'an translations by language]].

## Al-Zarqani: literal and interpretive translation
Muhammad Abd al-Azim al-Zarqani in Manahil al-Irfan, vol. 2, pp. 29-39, 120-133 and 173, distinguishes literal translation, word for word, from interpretive translation. He holds that a translation cannot replicate the Qur'an's Arabic naẓm or its inimitability, and so it must be called tafsir of the Qur'an in another language, not the Qur'an itself {cite:ablibrary-manahil-irfan-zarqani-v2-p32 p.29-39} {cite:eshia-manahil-irfan-42041-2-120 p.120} {cite:quranpedia-manahil-irfan-111-2-173 p.173}.

## Al-Shatibi: primary and secondary meanings
Abu Ishaq [[person/abu-ishaq-al-shatibi|al-Shatibi]] in al-Muwafaqat, vol. 2, distinguishes the primary meaning, the informative one, which may be conveyed in other languages for the sake of reporting, from the secondary meanings, those bound to Arabic usage in the aspects of rhetoric, which cannot be carried across by translation. Manna' al-Qattan reports this in Mabahith fi Ulum al-Qur'an, and an open-access chapter at Taylor & Francis treats it {cite:islamweb-library-shatibi-muwafaqat-99-342 on al-Muwafaqat vol. 2} {cite:taylorfrancis-oa-chapter-10-4324-9781003247784-22 on the open-access chapter} {cite:islamweb-qattan-mabahith-maanawiyya-translation on Manna' al-Qattan}.

## Ibn Taymiyya: translation as an explanation of meaning
[[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] in al-Jawab al-Sahih li-man Baddala Din al-Masih, vol. 2, pp. 52-68, specifically p. 67, states that translating the Qur'an outside prayer is permissible by Muslim consensus in the manner in which its tafsir is permissible. For him, according to the summary, a translation is an explanation of meaning, not a substitute for the revealed Arabic text {cite:salafcenter-2901 on article 2901} {cite:mtafsir-ibn-taymiyya-answers-to-christians-35981 on the forum thread}.

## Pickthall: the Koran cannot be translated
[[person/muhammad-marmaduke-pickthall|Marmaduke Pickthall]] wrote in the translator's foreword to The Meaning of the Glorious Koran (1930), p. vii, that "The Koran cannot be translated", that his English is an attempt to present the meaning of the Koran, and that it "can never take the place of the Koran in Arabic" {cite:byustudies-language-of-god-understanding-the-quran on Pickthall's foreword} {cite:scribd-loss-of-meaning-english-translations-quran on Pickthall's foreword}. This is a translator's remark, not a jurist's ruling, and it is given because it meets the earlier views in what it says.

## Where the views agree and differ
The four statements agree that a translation is an explanation of meaning and not a substitute for the text, and differ in the ground: al-Zarqani gives the Qur'an's naẓm and inimitability; al-Shatibi the distinction of primary and secondary meanings; Ibn Taymiyya the likening of translation to tafsir in permissibility; and Pickthall the translator's inability to carry over the effect. The sources quoted are also uneven, running from numbered copies of books to sites, forums and an academic chapter.

## What this page does not reach
The pages of Manahil al-Irfan, al-Muwafaqat, al-Jawab al-Sahih, Pickthall's foreword and the forums and sites named were not opened; the statements come from a research summary supplied to the museum, and the founder approved using it on 21 November 2026 without re-verification. Volume and page numbers are the summary's and unchecked, and the only wording quoted from a book is two short phrases of Pickthall's, in English.
