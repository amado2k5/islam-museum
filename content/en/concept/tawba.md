## Summary
In Ka'b's account at Ibn Ishaq, "God's repentance" upon the three is declared after the boycott; Ka'b counts as part of his repentance matters touching his wealth and the truthfulness of his speech. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/180-181}

## In the story of the three
The Prophet declared God's repentance upon them after the dawn prayer, and the people congratulated him on it: "Rejoice in God's repentance upon you" ([[event/repentance-of-the-three-accepted|the event]]). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/180}

## What Ka'b counted of his repentance
Ka'b said part of his repentance was to strip off his wealth as alms — the Prophet ordered him to keep some of it — and part of it was to speak only truth as long as he lived ([[event/kaab-offers-his-wealth-and-vows-truthfulness|the event]]). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/180}

## Notes
- This page describes the concept only as it appears in Ka'b's account; the general legal definition of repentance and its rulings need the fiqh and creed sources (needs_source).

## Sha'rawi on repentance and society
Sha'rawi holds that Abraham and Isma'il asked repentance for their descendants because they knew those who came after would sin, and that the law of repentance protects the whole society: if a single sin left the sinner in the Fire forever with no repentance, sinners would grow bolder and people would despair of their salvation. He cites the hadith that every child of Adam errs and the best of those who err are those who repent. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.588}

## Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's account
He explains the narration that God extends His hand by night to forgive the one who sinned by day, and by day to forgive the one who sinned by night. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:04:00} He says paradise is surrounded by hardships and the Fire by desires. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:04:30}
He distinguishes the repentant who stays steady and is excused for minor lapses from the one who repents with his body but not his heart and returns to sin. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:05:30}
He describes a third kind who avoids being reminded of God and flees correction about his sin (needs_source: the wording of this passage is not clear in the automatic transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:06:00}
He warns against delaying repentance: death does not wait, and no one can bring it forward or push it back by a moment. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:12:30} Only good deeds benefit a person in the grave. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:13:00}
He says that if a servant sins and regrets it, even for the blink of an eye, the sin falls away. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:14:30}
He says that the first duty of repentance is to return rights to their owners, and that returning to the truth is better than persisting in falsehood. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep04 00:18:30}

## Repentance in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episodes 2 and 4)
The Sheikh says that if the servant sins, then regrets and asks forgiveness, God forgives him, and that God forgives His servant each time he repents again (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:05:00} He says true repentance begins in the heart and the tongue together, and that the one who repents and then returns gains nothing from the word of repentance alone (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:05:30}
He gives the image of an exam at the mosque door: steadfastness in repentance takes time, and God accepts the penitent after the test (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:06:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)
The Sheikh says God did not burden us with what minds cannot bear, and relates that a man among the Companions came to the Prophet and told him he had done something that belongs to the doors of error (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:02:00}. He says the Prophet did not go into details or motives, but asked whether he had prayed the dawn prayer with them just now, and when he said yes told him that good deeds wipe out bad deeds, closing to him the door of mazes and self-reproach (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:02:30}.

The Sheikh relays from some great scholars that whoever knows God sees him while he sins and then seeks forgiveness is forgiven; he holds this knowledge to be a sign of brokenness and understanding in the servant, and says nobody is ignorant of the sin itself, and he distinguishes between one who sins and one who sins and feels real regret (the wording is incomplete in the text) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:03:00}. He says that whoever commits a sin and regrets it, even for a moment, has the sin fall away faster than the blink of an eye, and that some scholars say regret is repentance {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:03:30}.

He holds that God does not reproach His servant on the Day of Judgement for the sins between them, but conceals them, and he contrasts the servant's good deeds, which he puts between himself and people, with the millions of sins between him and his Lord {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:04:00}. He relates that a man, as he was dying, asked his sons where he was going; he was told to God, and he said: to the Generous? The Sheikh then explains the meaning of pardon (afw) (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:04:30}. He says pardon comes from the wind that blots out a trace by covering the earth with dust, so God wipes out the sin so that it does not show, and he compares it with human pardon, where memory may remain, so that pardon is like a thin layer of dust that any occasion blows away {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:05:00}. The Sheikh says jokingly that when God pardons, the recording angels forget, as if the angels suffered a kind of memory loss, whereas humans bring up the same problem again after years (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:05:30}. He adds that God also makes the human witnesses forget, the brothers and relatives who saw you in the sin or to whom you told it, by occupying them with their own sins, so that nobody tracks your slip (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:06:30}. He says that on the Day of Judgement people are occupied with themselves, and so the recording angels forget; this is divine pardon, while human pardon is temporary (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:07:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)
The Sheikh imagines that God opened a person's inner sight to read the record of the evil deeds of those around him kept by the angel in charge of them, and holds that they would have fled from him, since each of us is known by hidden faults (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:13:00}. He invites listeners to try measuring blood pressure before and after reading Surat Yusuf with only ablution, suggests that one of the doctor sons do it, and says the result will surprise the one who tries it (a claim that needs a source) (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:22:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)
The Sheikh explains the word ma'ab as returning, and says that whenever a person strays he returns, and whenever he errs he returns, hoping that death will come to him in the moment of repentance {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:40:00}. He relates the sense of a hadith: the servant sins once, twice and three times and then returns, and each time he admits that he has a Lord who forgives sins, so God calls His angels to witness that He has forgiven him; the Sheikh says God never closes the door of forgiveness in our faces (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:40:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 8)
The Sheikh says the way out of a crisis, whether personal or general, is nearer to believers in two states: the state of a lasting affliction and the state of committing a sin, and he expects his listeners to be surprised (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:10:30}. He relays that great scholars said they feel closer to God in sin followed by repentance than in obedience, because in obedience Satan makes a person imagine he is like Abu Bakr or Uthman (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:11:00}. He adds that in obedience a person relies on his deed, which is known for its flaw, whereas in sin he relies on God's mercy (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:11:30}. He says that a deed needs sincerity and conformity to the Sunna, that a person cannot be sure of either, since obedience may contain a shortfall or fault, and that therefore one should seek forgiveness (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:12:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 10)
The Sheikh advises that a person should not be a hundred per cent sure that he has been forgiven, and that seeking forgiveness came in Islam after every act of worship, and he mentions a hadith about Arafat {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:14:00}. He relays a saying that whoever thinks he stood at Arafat and God did not forgive him has fabricated a lie against God, yet pilgrims are told to seek forgiveness after leaving, and likewise forgiveness is sought after the salam of the prayer (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:14:30}. He compares this with a guest who has visited a host and says as he leaves: cover what you saw, because the host has seen his shortcomings {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:15:00}. He says this is the speech of great people, and that the worshipper is likewise with God: when the prayer ends he seeks God's forgiveness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:15:30}. He mentions that zakat al-fitr comes at the end of Ramadan as a purification, that zakat purifies wealth at the year's end, and that seeking forgiveness follows Hajj and prayer, so that the believer is in God's company {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:16:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Sharh Ketab al-Fawaed lectures (episode 4)
In a question-and-answer, the Sheikh recounts that he asked about the reality of repentance and was told that it is to forget one's sin (needs_source: the wording of the question and the identity of the speaker of the answer are unclear in the transcript) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep04 00:28:30}.
In the same exchange he explains repentance as a move from a state of dryness, meaning estrangement and hardness of heart, to a state of fidelity to God (his view) (needs_source: some words of the transcript are garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep04 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep04 00:29:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh says that every undertaking needs repentance, and that the basis of repentance is returning rights to their owners, so that justice and mercy spread among people {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep10 00:07:30}. He explains repentance as a return to God, and says that God loves those who repent often {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:06:30}. He says that God replaces the sins of the repentant with good deeds {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep02 00:38:30}.

He says that God forgives sins but does not forgive associating partners with Him, and forgives what lies below that as He wills {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:17:30}.
