## Summary
In Ka'b's account at Ibn Ishaq, "God's repentance" upon the three is declared after the boycott; Ka'b counts as part of his repentance matters touching his wealth and the truthfulness of his speech. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/180-181}

## In the story of the three
The Prophet declared God's repentance upon them after the dawn prayer, and the people congratulated him on it: "Rejoice in God's repentance upon you" ([[event/repentance-of-the-three-accepted|the event]]). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/180}

## What Ka'b counted of his repentance
Ka'b said part of his repentance was to strip off his wealth as alms — the Prophet ordered him to keep some of it — and part of it was to speak only truth as long as he lived ([[event/kaab-offers-his-wealth-and-vows-truthfulness|the event]]). {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/180}

## Notes
- This page describes the concept only as it appears in Ka'b's account; the general legal definition of repentance and its rulings need the fiqh and creed sources (needs_source).

## Sha'rawi on repentance and society
Sha'rawi holds that Abraham and Isma'il asked repentance for their descendants because they knew those who came after would sin, and that the law of repentance protects the whole society: if a single sin left the sinner in the Fire forever with no repentance, sinners would grow bolder and people would despair of their salvation. He cites the hadith that every child of Adam errs and the best of those who err are those who repent. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.588}

## Sha'rawi on public and private repentance
Sha'rawi holds that repentance must match the sin: one who hid his sin may repent in secret, while one who broke a limit before people must declare his repentance before them. He names three stages: God legislated repentance, the servant repents, and God accepts it. He says the hudud are averted by doubts, but one who boasted of a sin and was established against him by witnesses is not left alone. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.677} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.678}
