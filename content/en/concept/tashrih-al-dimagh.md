## Division and temperament
The brain divides into a membranous substance, a marrow-like substance and cavities in it filled with spirit, and the nerves are like branches springing from it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.5}

Its substance was made cold and moist: slightly cold because of the many movements of the nerves and affections of the senses that reach it, and so as to temper the very hot spirit that comes to it from the heart; moist so that movements do not dry it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.6}

It was made soft and fatty. Ibn Sina gives Galen's reason for the softness and then his own: it was made soft in order to be fatty and so that it could nourish the hard nerves gradually {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.6}

The front part is softer and the rear part harder; most of the sensory nerves grow from the front, most of the motor nerves from the rear, and from it grows the spinal marrow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.6}

## The two membranes
The whole brain is covered by two membranes, one thin beside it and one thick beside the bone; they are barriers between the brain and the bone, and the brain may rise to the skull in states such as violent shouting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.7}

The thick membrane is not stuck to the brain nor to the thin one; only the vessels passing through it to the thin one join them, and it is fastened to the skull by membranous ties {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.7}

## The three ventricles
The brain has three ventricles along its length {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.7}

The front ventricle, Ibn Sina says, helps with breathing in, with shaking out the superfluity by sneezing, with distributing most of the sensing spirit, and with the acts of the forming powers among the powers of inner perception {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.7}

The rear ventricle is the origin of the spinal marrow, most of the moving spirit is distributed from it, and there are the acts of the retaining power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.7}

The middle one is like a passage between them, by which the front spirit joins the rear spirit and the remembered images pass {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.7} Since it is a passage leading from forming to retaining, it was the best place for thinking and imagining {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.8}

That these ventricles are places of powers from which these acts come is inferred from the afflictions that befall them: with the affliction of each part its act fails {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.8}

## The spirit, the gland and the two channels
Ibn Sina says the spirit completes its change from the temperament of the heart to that of the brain by being cooked in it: it first reaches the brain's first cavity and is cooked there, then passes to the middle ventricle and is cooked further, and its cooking is completed in the rear ventricle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.8}

Beneath the two ventricles is the place where the two great vessels rising to the brain divide into their branches, and a substance of the glandular kind supports those branches; this gland takes the shape of the branches and so became pine-cone shaped, its head toward the start of the division above {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.8}

For the expulsion of the brain's superfluities there are two channels, one in the front ventricle at the boundary it shares with the next, the other in the middle ventricle; the rear one has no channel of its own {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.9} They meet at a deep passage like a funnel, called the funnel, and it reaches the openings in the upper palate {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.9}
