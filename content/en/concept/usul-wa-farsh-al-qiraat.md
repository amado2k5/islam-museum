## Summary
In the works on the readings, such as al-Nashr and al-Taysir, the author splits the book in two: the chapters of **usul**, covering what runs through the whole Qur'an as one ruling that readers differ over, and then **farsh al-huruf**, arranged by sura, which names in each sura the words that are disputed, one by one. In al-Nashr the usul chapters include "the readers' differences on seeking refuge", "on the basmala", "on the great assimilation" and others, followed by "the chapter on farsh al-huruf". {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 252} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 268} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 283} {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 713}

## What belongs to the usul
Ibn al-Jazari counts among the usul: clear pronunciation and assimilation, rawm and ishmam, thickening and thinning, lengthening and shortening, imala and fath, full pronunciation and easing, substitution and transfer. He says they are not the kind of difference in which wording and meaning vary, because these varied qualities in delivery do not take it out of being one wording. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 181}
Among them is the great assimilation, pronouncing two letters as one doubled letter when the first carries a vowel; it is called great because it occurs so often, or because it affects a vowelled letter before it, and among the ten it is attributed to Abu Amr ibn al-Ala', though it was also reported from al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibn Muhaysin, al-A'mash and others. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 283}

## Imala as an example
Imala and fath are among the plainest usul. Khalid al-Sabt, quoting al-Dani, says imala is a dialect of Arab tribes to which they were drawn by the wish for lightness, and that al-Dani in Ijaz al-Bayan reported consensus on it; he gives al-Hudhali's statement in al-Kamil that imala and thick pronunciation are two dialects, neither older than the other, and the Qur'an came down in both, and that imala is the dialect of Hawazin, Bakr ibn Wa'il and Sa'd ibn Bakr. He notes that the Companions wrote some words in the codices with ya for the imala dialect and some with alif for the fath dialect. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 373}

## Why the distinction matters
Al-Sabt holds that Ibn al-Jazari's reason for leaving this kind out of the modes of difference does not justify leaving it out: difference of dialect is real and readings truly differ by it, and it could be a source of quarrel among readers who did not know that all belong to the seven ahruf; altering the Qur'an is forbidden by what touches its form, its manner of delivery and its dialects, as by what touches its substance. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 181} This is one of the points on which understandings of the seven ahruf differ: see [[question/what-is-meant-by-the-seven-ahruf|What is meant by the seven ahruf?]]

## The usul in teaching delivery
Al-Sabt stresses that the codex alone cannot tell a reader how to deliver things like qalqala, clear pronunciation, concealment, assimilation, rawm and ishmam, and that scholars therefore ruled that codices cannot be relied on alone but the delivery must be taken from a trustworthy memorizer. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 316} That is why the usul are carried by direct recitation and recorded in chapters.
