## Nature
It was said in the chapter on usrub, and this is the qal'i; as for its isfidhaj and the kinds of its preparation, we shall mention them in the Aqrabadhin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}

## Choice
The finest of it is the burnt form and the isfidhaj, and its smell must be guarded against when it is burnt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}

## Temperament
Cold and moist {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}

## Actions and properties
Its burnt form has a subtilising, softening and dissolving power and cuts blood; its isfidhaj is gluey and cooling, with the power of burnt tutiya; and the dross of rasas is like the power of the burnt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}

## Swellings and pustules
When rubbed down with a drink (sharab) or the like, or with one of the cold juices, it benefits swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}

## Wounds and ulcers
It benefits hidden and creeping ulcers, and the isfidhaj fills deep ulcers with flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}

## Poisons and bites
Its isfidhaj, rubbed on the sting of the sea scorpion and the sea dragon, benefits it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.668}
