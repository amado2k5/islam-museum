## Summary
The Qur'an was recited to a people whose craft was speech. The books of commentary preserve reports of what some of them said when they heard it, and of what they did to keep others from hearing it. This page relays those reports as they appear in the commentaries of al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi, with the name of the one from whom each is narrated.

## al-Walid ibn al-Mughira
The verses of sura al-Muddaththir from [[passage/quran-74-11|al-Muddaththir 11]] to [[passage/quran-74-25|al-Muddaththir 25]] came down about him; he is [[person/al-walid-ibn-al-mughira|al-Walid ibn al-Mughira]] of the Makhzum, one of the great men of Quraysh.

**The report of Ikrima.** Al-Tabari narrates from Ikrima that al-Walid came to the Prophet, who recited the Qur'an to him, and he seemed to soften toward it. News of this reached Abu Jahl, who came to him and said: uncle, your people want to collect money for you, for you went to Muhammad to get something from what he has. He said: Quraysh know that I am among the richest of them. He said: then say something about him that tells your people you reject him. He said: and what shall I say? By God, there is no man among you who knows poetry better than I do, nor its rajaz, nor its qasida, nor the poetry of the jinn, and by God what he says resembles none of this. Then he described what he had heard: "its words have a sweetness, it crushes what lies beneath it, and it rises and is not surpassed." Abu Jahl said: your people will not be satisfied with you until you say something about it. He said: leave me until I think. When he had thought he said: this is magic that he takes from others {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 74:17}.

**The report of Ibn Abbas.** In another report in al-Tabari, from Ibn Abbas, al-Walid went in to Abu Bakr and asked him about the Qur'an, then went out to Quraysh and said: how wonderful what he says is; by God it is neither poetry, nor magic, nor the raving of madness. Quraysh took counsel and said: if al-Walid turns, all Quraysh will turn. Abu Jahl said: I will deal with him for you. He came to him and taunted him that his people were saying that he went in to the son of Abu Quhafa only to get some of his food. Al-Walid grew angry and said: its words are nothing but magic handed down {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 74:17}.

**Other reports.** Al-Tabari transmits from Ibn Zayd that al-Walid heard the Prophet praying and went back describing sweet speech that takes hold of hearts; he denied that it was poetry or divination, then said: perhaps it is magic {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 74:24}. He transmits from Mujahid that their gathering was in the Dar al-Nadwa {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 74:18}. In al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi, without naming a narrator, al-Walid heard the Prophet recite the opening of sura Ghafir in the mosque, and put to his people what they were saying about the Prophet: madman, soothsayer, poet, liar; they rejected each by what they knew of him, until he settled on saying: a magician {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 74:18} {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 74:18}.

## Agreeing to drown it out
The verse [[passage/quran-41-26|Fussilat 26]] relates the saying of the disbelievers: do not listen to this Qur'an, and make noise over it. Al-Tabari says that those who said it were the polytheists of Quraysh, and transmits from Mujahid that the noise was whistling, clapping and muddling the speech when the Prophet recited, and that Quraysh used to do this {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 41:26}. In al-Baghawi, from Ibn Abbas, some of them would urge others: when you see Muhammad reciting, oppose him with rajaz, poetry and noise {cite:tafsir-baghawi-quran-com on 41:26}. Al-Qurtubi mentions a saying that they did this when the Qur'an had defeated them {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 41:26}.

## The conversion of Umar ibn al-Khattab
At the opening of sura Ta-Ha al-Qurtubi mentions that the sura is Meccan and came down before the conversion of [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], and he relates the story by way of al-Daraqutni from Anas ibn Malik, and at greater length from Ibn Ishaq: Umar went out with his sword hung on him, intending the Prophet, and Nu'aym ibn Abd Allah met him and told him that his sister Fatima bint al-Khattab and her husband Sa'id ibn Zayd had become Muslims. He came to them, and with them was Khabbab ibn al-Aratt reading to them a sheet containing [[passage/quran-20-1|Ta-Ha]]. He struck Sa'id and hit his sister so that she bled. When he saw the blood he regretted it and asked for the sheet, but his sister refused until he washed. When he read its opening he said: how fine this speech is, and how noble. Then he asked Khabbab to show him the way to the Prophet so that he might become a Muslim {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 20:1}.

Al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Baghawi do not mention the story at this verse in the text that was read.

## Musaylima and Amr ibn al-As
At [[passage/quran-2-24|al-Baqara 24]] Ibn Kathir transmits that Amr ibn al-As went as a delegate to Musaylima before he became a Muslim, and Musaylima asked him what had been sent down upon his companion in Mecca; he recited to him sura al-Asr. Musaylima thought, then claimed that the like of it had been sent down upon him, and produced a speech about the hyrax. Amr answered him that he knew he was lying {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 2:24}.

## How these reports are read
The commentators bring them as evidence that the people of the tongue knew that the Qur'an was not of the kind of their own speech. Ibn Kathir said at the verse of Yunus that those of them who believed did so because of what they knew of its eloquence, sweetness, strength and charm, and that they were the people who knew it best, as the magicians knew that what Moses brought was not magic {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-quran-com on 10:38}.

The books of the Sira themselves were not read on this page, and the reference to the commentaries is by verse.
