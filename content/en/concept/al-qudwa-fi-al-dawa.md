## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that words alone are not enough in preaching, because people see the deed before they hear the word, that a religious scholar has more claim to exemplary conduct than others, and that Islam spread by conduct before it spread by method {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.303} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.305}. See [[passage/quran-61-2|al-Saff 2]] and [[passage/quran-41-33|Fussilat 33]].

## Contradiction
He says that one who forbids wrong and does it is deceitful, and that when word is separated from conduct the call is lost {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.304}. He cites the Prophet's practice of being first to do what he commanded his companions to do {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.305}.

## Umar and his household
He relates that when Umar wanted to regulate a matter, he gathered his family and relatives and announced what he would command, and that whoever opposed would be made an example. He held that this closed the door of discord {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.305}.

## The scholar of religion
He holds that in worldly sciences no one asks a chemist for exemplary conduct, but that a scholar of religion who drinks wine falls in the eyes of people and is no longer listened to {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.305}.

## Spread through conduct
He holds that Islam spread through conduct, gives the example of merchants of good character who spread Islam in China, and holds that the largest number who converted did so through an exemplary way of life {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.305}.

## Not yet checked
This is Sha'rawi's reading of preaching. The China example has not been checked against historical sources, no study of the spread is set out here, and no other tradition's view of the scholar's role is set out. Not yet checked.
