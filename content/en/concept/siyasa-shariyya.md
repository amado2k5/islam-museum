## Overview
The author situates sharia governance (al-siyasah al-shar'iyyah) between legal theory (usul) and substantive jurisprudence (fiqh): it relies on public interest (maslahah), juristic preference (istihsan), blocking the means to harm (sadd al-dhara'i), and removing hardship, which belong to usul, yet it differs from detailed fiqh because it relies on general proofs and universal principles {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 94}.

## From the Book Usul al-Iqtisad al-Islami: Part Two (pp. 101–200 of the PDF)
The author holds that apparent divergence from political texts is not a genuine contradiction, warns against confusing a political text with an immutable text, and states that the ruler's authority to restrict the permissible is part of sharia governance {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 101}.

The author maintains that the majority view on the zakat of money is based on ordinary circumstances, and that in exceptional circumstances additional levies may be imposed {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 116}.
