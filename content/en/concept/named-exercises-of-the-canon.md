## Summary
Ibn Sina lists the kinds of exercise by name, from wrestling bouts, archery and riding to swings and small boats, then the strong exercises of the open ground such as running, wrestling, ball games, polo and lifting a stone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.

## The quick exercises
Among the kinds are the contest, the grappling, boxing, the sprint, fast walking, shooting with the bow, dancing, leaping to catch hold of something, hopping on one foot, fencing with sword and lance, riding horses, and the beating of the hands, in which a person stands on the tips of the feet and moves the arms forward and back quickly; this counts as a quick exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.

## The gentle, soft kinds
Among the gentle and soft kinds are rocking in swings and cradles, standing, sitting or lying, and riding in small boats and skiffs. Stronger than these is riding horses, camels, litters and carts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.

## The strong open-ground kinds
Among the strong exercises of the open ground is running hard across a field to a mark and then backing off, shortening the distance each round until the last stop falls in the middle. Others are shadow-fighting, clapping the palms, leaping, throwing, playing with a large or small ball, playing polo, playing with the bat, wrestling, lifting a stone, galloping horses and reining them in {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.222}.
