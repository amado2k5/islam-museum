## Summary
Ibn Sina sets down that softening nature before purging is a good rule that brings safety, except for someone strongly inclined to loose flux, that a bath some days before the purgative thins the humour and is a good preparation, and that the bath is not entered after the drug, since it draws matter outward and so serves to stop purging rather than help it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.279}.

## Preparing
Ibn Sina recalls that the body must be prepared before the purgative so as to receive it, with the pores widened and nature softened, especially in cold illnesses; softening nature before purging is in general a good, safe rule, except for a person strongly inclined to loose flux, to whose purgative something with emetic power should be added so that it does not hurry down out of the stomach before it has done its work. A bath for some days before the purgative thins the humour and is a good preparation unless something prevents it, with a short interval between the bath and drinking the drug; the bath is not entered after the drug, except in winter, when the first room of the bath may be entered if its heat cannot draw at all but only soften {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.279}.
