## Summary
In chapter 29 al-Battani says that many people and the common folk think days with their nights are equal in time, each day and night being twenty-four hours, which is not so: the mean day with its night is the rising of all three hundred and sixty times of the equator from the horizon or the meridian with the addition of what rises with the fifty-nine minutes the Sun travels by its mean motion in a day and night {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.77-78}.

## The unequal day
The unequal day with its night is the one in which all the three hundred and sixty times of the equator rise with the addition of what rises with the Sun's unequal motion in the day and night, which is either more than fifty-nine minutes or less {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.78}. The difference between the two days is thus a difference between two motions of the Sun, the mean and the true, and not a difference in the time that people measure by hours. This definition is the basis of the whole chapter and the reference for [[concept/zij-sabi-two-causes-of-the-inequality-of-days|the two causes of the inequality of days]] {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.78}.
