## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the second chapter on the colours of urine with the scale of yellows from straw to saffron and the scale of reds, saying that what lies beyond the citron shade shows heat and that reds show a predominance of blood, or of bile where they lean toward saffron {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.184-185}.

## The scale of yellows
The shades of yellow begin with straw, then the fair citron, then the orange yellow, then the fiery shade like the dye of saffron, the deep yellow, then saffron itself, and last the bright red. Everything beyond the citron shade shows heat and varies with its degrees; strong movement, pains, hunger and the cutting off of drinking water can also bring it about {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.184-185}.

## The scale of reds
After these come the shades of red, such as the tawny, the rose, the deep red and the dark red, all of which show a predominance of blood. The more they lean toward saffron, the more bile prevails; the more they lean toward dusk, the more blood prevails. The fiery shade shows heat better than the red and the dark red, just as bile is itself hotter than blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.185}.
