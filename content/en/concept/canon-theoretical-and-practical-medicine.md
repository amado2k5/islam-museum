## Summary
Ibn Sina opens Fann 3 of Book I by dividing medicine into a theoretical part and a practical part, then dividing the practical into the science of preserving health and the science of treatment. He states that Fann 1 finished the general theoretical part and that what follows turns to the practical part in general terms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.

## Theoretical and practical
Medicine divides first into two parts, both knowledge and inquiry. The part called theoretical gives knowledge of opinions only, with no knowledge of action, for example the part that teaches temperaments, humours, powers, kinds of disease, symptoms and causes. The part called practical gives knowledge of how to act and manage, for example how to keep a body healthy in a given state or how to treat a body with a given disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.

## Practical is not the doing itself
Ibn Sina warns that the practical part is not the hands-on doing; it is the part in which the knowledge of doing is learned. He says he has finished the general theoretical part in Fann 1 and will devote the two remaining Fanns to the practical part in general terms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.

## Preserving health and treatment
The practical part has two divisions: the science of managing healthy bodies so that their health is kept, called the preservation of health, and the science of managing a sick body so that it returns to health, called treatment. He begins with a short account of preserving health {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.
