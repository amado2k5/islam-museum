## Nature and selection
A kind of wormwood with a little heat and sharpness; the smaller is sharper and more bitter; they are stalks and a downy white or yellowish flower, full of seed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}. The mountain kind is the smaller {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}. The footnote says that jaadah is a sweet-scented plant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: the smaller is hot in the third degree and dry in the second; the larger is hot and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}. Actions: it opens and thins, and the larger opens all the inner blockages {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: heals fresh wounds, especially the larger, and its dry form helps dry malignant ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}.
Head: causes headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.424}.
Food: with vinegar, a plaster for a swollen spleen and its hardness; it harms the stomach, helps black jaundice, especially the decoction of the larger, helps dropsy; in general it is bad for the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}.
Excretory organs: moves urine and menses, purges, and is very good for worms in the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425} [book listing].
Fevers: helps chronic fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}.
Poisons: helps scorpion stings; a decoction of the larger helps bites of all vermin, and fumigation with it drives vermin away {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425}.
Substitutes: pomegranate-stick bark, fresh, and cinnamon-stick bark, for expelling worms and moving urine and menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.425} [book listing].
