## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that evacuation is called for not by sheer fullness alone but by the gravity of the illness and by fullness in quantity and quality, that better regimen often does without venesection, and that people prone to gout or epilepsy evacuate beforehand by venesection or purging {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.277}.

## Taking precautions
Not every evacuation is needed for sheer fullness; the gravity of the illness and fullness in quality and quantity can call for it too, and better regimen often does the work of the venesection that was due. When something calls for evacuation but an obstacle stands in the way, the only recourse is fasting, sleep and correcting the bad temperament that the fullness brings about. Some evacuation is precautionary, as for a person whose gout or epilepsy comes at a known time, especially in spring: he guards ahead of its time and evacuates in the way that suits his illness, whether by venesection or by purging {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.277}.
