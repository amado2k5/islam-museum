Yawm Arafa is a compound of day and Arafa. Arafa names the well-known standing ground where the pilgrimage is completed; its bound runs from the mountain overlooking Batn Urana to the facing mountains beside the walls of Banu Amir. The day of Arafa is the ninth of Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18029}

Reports on its merit include Aisha's hadith that no day sees God free more servants from the Fire than the day of Arafa; Abd Allah ibn Amr's report that the best supplication is the supplication of the day of Arafa; Jabir's report that no day is better with God, when He descends to the lowest heaven and boasts of the people of earth to the people of heaven; and the report that Satan is never seen smaller, more repelled, meaner or more enraged than on the day of Arafa, except what he was shown on the day of Badr. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18030}

Among them is Umar ibn al-Khattab's report that a Jewish man told him that had the verse of the completion of the religion come down upon the Jews they would have taken that day as a feast, and Umar answered that they knew the day and the place — the Prophet standing at Arafa on a Friday. Some jurists hold that a standing that falls on Friday gives that pilgrimage a merit over others, and in it the Prophet performed his Farewell Pilgrimage, standing when surah al-Ma'ida (verse 3) was revealed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18030}

As for its rules: the standing at Arafa is a pillar of the pilgrimage, its pillar-hood established by the Sunna and consensus — by the Sunna through Abd al-Rahman ibn Ya'mar al-Dili's hadith "the hajj is Arafa; whoever comes on the night of Jam' before dawn has caught the hajj" and Urwa ibn Mudarris's hadith "whoever attends this prayer of ours and stands with us until we move on, having stood at Arafa before that by night or day, has completed his hajj"; and by consensus, for the community has agreed that one who leaves it or delays it past its time has lost the pilgrimage, is released by the acts of umra and owes the hajj next year. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18031}

The standing has two agreed conditions: that it be on the ground of Arafat, and that it fall in the standing time — the ninth of Dhu al-Hijja and the night of the tenth until dawn, so whoever has not stood on any part of Arafa by the dawn has lost the hajj. The Shafi'is add that the stander must be in ihram and fit for worship, so the presence of the unfit — the insane, the fainted, the drunk — does not suffice if their state covers the whole standing time, though they hold the insane person's hajj valid as a supererogatory act like the undiscerning child's. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18031} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18032}

On the standing's time the jurists agree it ends at the dawn of the day of the sacrifice, and differ on its start: the Hanafis and Shafi'is place it at the sun's zenith on the day of Arafa, Malik places it at night, and the Hanbalis from the dawn of Arafa to the dawn of the sacrifice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18032}

The duty of the standing, for the Hanafis, the Hanbalis on the sound view, and the Shafi'is on the view opposing it, is joining night and day for one who stands by day, continuing until sunset; the Shafi'is on the sound view and Ahmad in a narration hold it sunna; the Malikis hold the standing by day obligatory while any standing by night, even a moment, is the pillar itself; and the Shafi'is say one who reaches Arafa only by night is credited with any standing even a moment, with no offering due, since the Prophet declared the hajj caught and complete without naming an offering. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18032}

If one passes beyond Arafa before sunset and does not return, there are three views: the Hanafis, Hanbalis and Shafi'is in one view oblige an offering for leaving the duty of joining night and day; the Shafi'is on the school view recommend it to escape the dispute; the Malikis hold the hajj is lost, since standing an hour after sunset is a pillar not repaired by an offering. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18032} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18033}

If he returns before sunset there is no offering on the Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali views since he has joined night and day — though the Hanafis say the offering lapses only if he returns before the imam departs, Zafar dissenting; al-Karkhi and Ibn Shuja's narration from Abu Hanifa let it lapse even after the imam's departure while the Asl says it does not. If he returns after sunset, the Hanafis, Hanbalis and the Shafi'is' counter-view hold the offering stands since it was confirmed at sunset, while the Malikis and the sounder Shafi'i view see no offering since night and day were joined. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18033}

On error in the standing: an error of place — standing outside Arafa — is invalid by agreement and obliges a make-up whether many or few erred. An error of time by delay, all the people standing on the tenth, is valid on the majority view and Hanafi istihsan, on the reports "your fast is the day you fast, your feast the day you feast, your sacrifice the day you sacrifice" and "Arafa is the day you make known"; Hanafi analogy itself would not credit it. An error by advance, standing on the eighth, is not credited on the Hanafi, Maliki school and sounder Shafi'i views since an advance error rests on no proof and can be guarded against; the Hanbalis, the counter-Shafi'i view and some Malikis credit it on the hadith "the day of Arafa is the day on which the people stand" and by analogy with delay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18034}

On a small group who saw the crescent alone: the Hanafis and Hanbalis on the school view hold their standing does not suffice and they must stand with the body — the Hanafis tying this to the imam and people having stood on the day of sacrifice through confusion, while if the imam stood on the eighth and the witnesses stood by their sighting on the ninth, Muhammad is reported to allow their standing and hajj; a Hanbali narration chosen by Ibn Muflih has the seer stand twice. The Shafi'is state that one who sees it alone must act on his sighting and may not follow the erring many; the Malikis and Shafi'is hold that one whose crescent testimony was rejected must still stand at his own time, like the rejected Ramadan witness who must still fast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18035}

The Hanafis say that if two witnesses testify to the crescent before the imam on the eve of Arafa and he cannot stand in the rest of the night with the people or most of them, he does not act on the testimony and stands the next day after noon, as if they testified after the time; if he can stand before dawn with most of the people it is allowed and his hajj is lost if he does not; likewise if the imam delays the standing for a ground on which opinion is permitted, the standing of those who went before him is invalid. The Shafi'is state that pilgrims who stand on the tenth by mistake are credited, unless they are unusually few, in which case they make it up on the sounder view since there is no general hardship; the counter-view excuses them since they cannot be safe from the like in the make-up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18035} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18036}

On intention: the Hanafis, Malikis, Hanbalis and the sounder of the two Shafi'i faces hold it is not required for a valid standing; al-Kasani states it is valid whether intended or not, unlike tawaf; the Hanafis recommend it. The Hanbalis state that a sane person's standing counts however it came — standing, sitting, mounted or sleeping, even crossing unaware it was Arafa; not valid from the insane, nor on the sounder view from the drunk and the fainted. Al-Nawawi transmits from al-Shafi'i and the companions that what counts is presence on part of Arafat even a subtle moment, given fitness for worship, whether on purpose, heedless, trading, talking, playing, sleeping, or passing unknowing. The Malikis except the passer after the imam's departure, requiring intention and awareness; a Shafi'i face obliges singling the standing out by intention; Abu Thawr requires standing by will. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18036} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18037}

Among the standing's recommended acts is washing for it: the Shafi'is, Hanbalis and Malikis in one view hold it sunna, on reports that Ali, Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Umar washed when setting out to Arafa and because it is a devotion for which creation gathers in one place, like Friday and the two feasts; the Hanafis and the relied-upon Maliki view hold it recommended, and the Shafi'is have one who cannot wash make tayammum. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18037}

Also the sermon of Arafa: two sermons after noon before the prayer, separated by a light sitting as on Friday, on the Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi'i views; the Hanbalis recommend the imam or his deputy give one short sermon, and al-Buhuti argued for shortening it from Salim ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar's word to al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf on the day of Arafa, "if you would hit the Sunna, shorten the sermon and hasten the prayer." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18037} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18038}

Also joining the noon and afternoon prayers early in the noon time with one call and two iqamas, following the Prophet's practice — the Maliki school famously requires two calls — which the majority count among the recommended rites of hajj; on the sounder Shafi'i view it is not a rite but the traveler's licence to join prayers, so they impose travel's conditions, as some Hanbalis say; a third Shafi'i face makes its cause the journey itself, allowing it for the Meccan but not for Arafa's people. The Hanafis impose conditions for the joining: that the prayer be ordered on a presumption of permissibility, the time be the day of Arafa, the place Arafat, the state ihram of hajj, the congregation on Abu Hanifa's view but not his two companions', and, for Abu Hanifa, that the leader be the chief imam or his deputy. The majority dislike supererogatory prayer between the noon and afternoon at Arafa on Jabir's report that the Prophet prayed nothing between them; the Hanafis outside the apparent narration allow none but the sunna of noon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18038} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18039}

Also hastening to the standing: the jurists agree that once the two prayers are done the Sunna is to set out at once for the standing ground and quicken the march; al-Nawawi calls this hastening recommended by consensus, on Salim's report about Ibn Umar and al-Hajjaj on the day of Arafa — "the departure, if you want the Sunna." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18039}

When the sun sets on the day of Arafa the imam and the people depart with calm and dignity, quickening where there is an opening, on Usama's report that the Prophet rode at a gentle pace and sped through gaps; a pilgrim who lingers long without excuse after the imam's departure until nightfall does ill, and if the imam delays and does not move, the people move on, for he has missed the Sunna. The pilgrim remains in a state of purity through the standing — sunna for the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Malikis in one view, recommended for the Hanbalis and on the relied-upon Maliki view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18040}

It is sunna on the Hanafi and Shafi'i view and recommended on the Maliki and Hanbali to stand near the Mountain of Mercy by the great spread black rocks at its foot, that being the description of the Prophet's standing place, or as near as one can; ascending the mountain is not prescribed by consensus as Ibn Taymiyya stated, and al-Nawawi counts the common people's insistence on standing on it — some supposing the standing invalid otherwise — an error against the Sunna; none relied upon named a merit for climbing it except Abu Ja'far al-Tabari and Abu al-Hasan al-Mawardi, who recommended standing on or seeking it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18040} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18041}

Abundant good works are recommended on the day — worship, remembrance, Qur'an reading — on Ibn Abbas's hadith on deeds in the ten days, and the Sunna is much supplication, tahlil, talbiya, seeking forgiveness, repentance, entreaty, Qur'an reading and blessing on the Prophet, for this is the day's office and the greater part and object of the hajj; one prays standing and sitting, hands raised, alone and in company, for oneself, parents, teachers, kin, friends and all Muslims, with much weeping amid the remembrance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18041}

The Sunna is to join the sunset and evening prayers at Muzdalifa, by the jurists' agreement; they differ over one who prayed the sunset prayer on the way. The majority — the Maliki school, Shafi'is, Hanbalis, Abu Yusuf, Ishaq, Abu Thawr, Ibn al-Mundhir, and with them Ata, Urwa, al-Qasim ibn Muhammad and Sa'id ibn Jubayr — hold he left the Sunna but his prayer counts, since whatever two prayers may be joined may be separated. The Hanafis except Abu Yusuf, al-Thawri and Ibn Habib of the Malikis hold delaying the sunset prayer to Muzdalifa obligatory, so one who prayed it on the road repeats it there unless dawn has risen; if he prays dawn before repeating them, they return to validity by Hanafi agreement. The Malikis condition the joining's sunna-hood on having stood with the imam and walked with the people or stayed back by choice; the Shafi'is condition departing from the Sunna on not fearing to lose the chosen time of the evening prayer; the Hanafis require for this joining ihram for hajj, a prior standing at Arafa, the night of the sacrifice as the time, Muzdalifa as the place, and the evening prayer time unless dawn rises. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18042} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18043}

Among the day's disliked things: omitting the iqama for each of the joined prayers, recommended by agreement and disliked to omit by the Hanafis; ihram for umra on the day of Arafa, not disliked on the majority view but disliked by the Hanafis and Ahmad in a narration; and speeding on foot or mounted to a degree that harms, on the hadith "upon you be calm," al-Zayla'i holding that avoiding harm is a duty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18043} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18044}

On shade: the Shafi'is state it is better for the stander not to seek shade but to stand in the sun except for an excuse such as harm or weakened supplication; the Malikis recommend leaving shade during the standing, al-Qurtubi noting that shading in tents is undisputed while shading during the standing itself was disliked by Malik and the people of Medina; the Hanafis allow the pilgrim in ihram to shade himself with house or litter on Jabir's report of the tent at Namira; the Hanbalis on the sounder view forbid the litter and in another narration dislike it. Fasting the day is disliked for the pilgrim on the majority view — Malikis, Shafi'is, Hanbalis — while the Hanafis recommend it for the pilgrim it does not weaken for the standing and supplications; for the non-pilgrim all agree it is recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18044} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18045}

On omitting the Arafa sermon or giving it before noon: the Hanafis state it is disliked — al-Jawhara al-nayyira says it counts but he has done ill; Ibn Habib of the Malikis allows it before noon while Ashhab forbids it and orders it repeated unless the prayer would be missed, al-Baji arguing the sermon is for teaching the pilgrims, not for the prayer, and al-Dasuqi that praying without it or after an early sermon counts by consensus. On entering Arafat before the standing time: Malik disliked the pilgrims going ahead to Arafa before its day or sending their tents ahead; the Shafi'is call it an error, an innovation and a breaking with the Sunna that forfeits many of its practices; the Hanafis say the pilgrim goes after the dawn prayer, this marking mere precedence, so going before dawn is allowed; the Hanbalis recommend going to Mina on the day of Tarwiya, sleeping there, then travelling to Arafa at sunrise and staying at Namira until noon. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18045}

On the way to Arafa and the manner of standing: the pilgrim prays dawn at Mina on the ninth, waits until the sun rises over Mount Thabir, then sets out for Arafat in calm and dignity with talbiya, tahlil and takbir, washing or else making ablution, saying the supplication of setting out, and on sighting the Mountain of Mercy another supplication, then keeps the talbiya until entering Arafa and settles with the people anywhere but the road, near the mountain being best on the Hanafi view; on the Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali view he lodges at Namira until noon to attend the sermon and the joined prayers, busying himself with nothing else between them, and stands at Arafa until sunset facing the qibla with hands raised and spread like a begging pauper as the Prophet's supplication there is described; he voices the known form of the talbiya aloud without excess, keeps his supplications and remembrances low save when guiding followers, repeats each supplication thrice, opens and closes with praise of God and blessing on the Prophet and amen until sunset, and guards outer and inner purity, keeping from the unlawful in food, drink, dress, mount, look and word, on the hadith "this is a day in which whoever masters his hearing, sight and tongue is forgiven." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18046} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18047}

At sunset the imam departs and the people with him without delay, in inward calm and outward gravity; it is recommended to take the road of the two passes, for the Prophet is reported to have taken it, though another road is allowed; he stays in remembrance and humility until Muzdalifa, praying neither sunset nor evening prayer until he enters it, and so completes the pillar of the standing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18047}

Among the recommended supplications at the standing: Abd Allah ibn Amr's hadith that the best supplication is that of the day of Arafa and the best of what he and the prophets before him said is the tahlil; al-Bayhaqi's longer version with the prayer for light in heart, hearing and sight; Ibn Abbas's supplication of the Farewell Pilgrimage "O God, You hear my words and see my place"; and what Ibn Umar used to raise his voice with on the eve of Arafa of tahlil and prayer for guidance, piety and a good provision. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18048} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18049}

On the ta'rif in the towns on the eve of Arafa — people gathering in the towns after the afternoon of the day for supplication, remembrance and entreaty until sunset as the people of Arafa do, al-Tahtawi calling it the people's likening themselves to the standers — the jurists hold three views: the majority, the Hanafis, Malikis, Nafi' the client of Ibn Umar, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, al-Hakam and Hammad, dislike it; al-Tahtawi reads the Hanafi dislike as prohibition-grade since the standing was appointed a devotion in a specific place not to be done elsewhere, like tawaf, Malik said it is not the people's affair and the keys of such things are innovation, Shu'ba reports al-Hakam and Hammad called it newly invented, as did Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, and Ibn Muflih, followed by al-Mardawi, says Ibn Taymiyya saw no ta'rif outside Arafa, held it denounced and its doer astray. The second view: Ahmad permitted it, which is what the Shafi'i expressions yield — Ahmad said there is no harm in the towns' ta'rif on the eve of Arafa, and al-Athram reports his hope that it is harmless since many did it, naming al-Hasan, Bakr, Thabit and Muhammad ibn Wasi' as attending the mosque on the day; Ibn Taymiyya says Ibn Abbas and Amr ibn Hurayth of the Companions and a group of the Basrans and Medinans did it; al-Wana'i of the Shafi'is says there is no dislike, rather a good innovation, a position al-Ushmawi also relied on. The third view, a narration from Ahmad recorded by Ibn Taymiyya among the unique reports, recommends the ta'rif. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18049} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18050}
