## Summary
Hamala is the blood-money or fine a person bears for another; technically it is the debt a man undertakes to pay in order to end a feud between two groups in which lives or property have been lost. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7288} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7289} Guarantee (daman) is wider than hamala, which is guarantee of blood-money or other sums for the sake of peace; kafala is for the Hanafis the joining of the surety's liability to the principal's, and for most jurists an undertaking to produce the person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7289} Hamala is lawful beyond dispute and counted among the noble acts; the Arabs knew it, and its basis is the Quranic command to fear God and mend relations among believers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7289} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7290} Zakat may go to a hamil who borrowed to end a feud: the Hanafis require him to be poor; the Malikis, Shafiis and Hanbalis give him from the share of debtors even if rich, where the burden is crushing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7290} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7291} Most jurists allow the bearer of a hamala to ask others for help until he has paid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7291}
