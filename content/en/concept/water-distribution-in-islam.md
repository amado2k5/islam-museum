## Summary
The author divides water, according to jurists, into three categories: water contained in a vessel or pool, well or spring water, and the water of seas, rivers, valleys, and torrents {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 219}. Each category has its own ruling regarding ownership and distribution.

## Contained Water
There is no disagreement among jurists that contained water may be owned and sold, because its possessor acquired ownership by containment, just as grass and game are owned {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 219}. The author holds that containment may or may not involve labor; whoever considers mere possession a cause of ownership rules that river water entering a person's orchard is owned, whereas whoever considers labor does not rule in favor of ownership, and the latter is the stronger view in his opinion {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 219}.

## Well and Spring Water
The majority of jurists hold that well or spring water is unowned and open to public use, that the owner of the well or spring has first right to it over others, and that they must give away any surplus beyond their needs without charge to those who ask for it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 219}. The hadith states: "Surplus water must not be withheld so as to withhold pasture thereby" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 219}. The author comments with a question: why should beneficiaries take water without charge or expense while the digger bears the cost? He did not place giving away surplus water among the compulsory redistribution tools until the position becomes clear {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 219} (in the footnote).

## Withholding Surplus Water and Bearing Arms
If a landowner withholds water from people when they fear dying of thirst, they have the right to fight him with weapons, and it is reported that Umar said to people who withheld water: "Why did you not take up arms against them?" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 220}. The author states that this is an established right for someone in dire need {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 220}. He cites the hadith of the three whom God will not speak to, among them one who withholds surplus water {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 220}.

## Unowned Public Water
All people have an equal right to public water as shared common property; they may drink from it, water their crops and livestock, and dig irrigation channels so long as they do not harm others {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 220}. Whoever digs a channel has first right to its water, and if any remains beyond their needs and another person needs that surplus, they must give it without charge {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}. As for international waters, this is referred to the chapter on public ownership {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221}.

## Its Connection to Pasture
An owner is forbidden from exploiting public pasture by withholding surplus water, thereby controlling the pasture and selling it through the sale of water; such a stratagem is impermissible {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 221} (see [[concept/pasture-in-islam|pasture]]).
