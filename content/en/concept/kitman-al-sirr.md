## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy counts the keeping of secrets among the first of the [[concept/amanah|trusts]], and gives the example of money deposited with a friend that is returned on time. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:22:00}
He condemns the spreading of what happens in homes and gatherings, and holds that what is said in a gathering is a trust that should not leave it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:23:30}

## The speaker's view: spreading the secrets of homes
He criticises those who spread the secrets of homes among female friends and relatives, and calls this a betrayal that ruins homes and makes light of the Hereafter. He says that some women, in their gatherings, reveal what passes between spouses (needs_source: this detail is spoken once in the recording and has not been verified). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:22:30}
He holds that curiosity divides people and draws them into the secrets of others, and that what is said in a gathering is a trust that should not leave it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:23:30}

## The story of Abu Bakr and Umar
He recounts that Abu Bakr asked Umar whether he had found anything from him, and that Umar admitted he had heard the Prophet mention Hafsa. He counts this as fulfilling the trust of keeping a secret (needs_source: the narration is garbled in some of its wording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:45:30}

## The speaker's view: the chest as a grave for a secret
He reports the saying of the scholars that a person's chest should be a store for a secret that it does not release, then speaks of young men studying in America and of an account about Arab students, which is garbled (needs_source: the saying is not attributed to a named author in the recording, and the account is garbled). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:46:00}
