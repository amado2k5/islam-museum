## Summary
Ibn Sina says one with a weak chest who is short of breath in winter cannot take much wine, that one who wishes to take more should not fill himself with food, and that one filled with both vomits and rinses his mouth with vinegar and honey; he then lists what mends the harm of wine to the liver, the head and the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.237-238}.

## One who wants to take more
One with a weakness in the chest whose breathing narrows in winter cannot take much wine. One who wishes to take more should not fill himself with food and should put into his meal what promotes urine. If fullness from food and drink comes on, he vomits, drinks honey water and vomits again, then washes his mouth with vinegar and honey and his face with cold water {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.237}.

## Mending the harm
One harmed by wine through heat of the body and fever of the liver makes his food something like the sour-grape dish, with pomegranate juice and citron acid as his accompaniments. One harmed in the head takes less and drinks it mixed and clarified, with quince after it. If the stomach is harmed through its heat he takes roasted myrtle berries and sucks camphor tablets and what is astringent and sour; if through its coldness, the accompaniments are cyperus, clove and citron peel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.237-238}.
