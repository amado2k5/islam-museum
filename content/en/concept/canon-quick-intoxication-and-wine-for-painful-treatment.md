## Summary
Ibn Sina closes the chapter on water and drink by describing what is soaked in wine to bring quick drunkenness without harm, and what is put in it for one who needs deep intoxication for a painful treatment of an organ, darnel water and numbing drugs he names {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.240}.

## Quick drunkenness
One who wants to become drunk quickly and without harm soaks lichen or Indian aloeswood in the wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.240}.

## For a painful treatment
One who needs deep intoxication to treat an organ painfully puts darnel water into his wine, or takes fumitory, opium and henbane with nutmeg, musk and raw aloeswood in small amounts, given in the wine as far as need requires, or boils black henbane and mandrake bark in water until it reddens and mixes the wine with it. The printed text is garbled at the listing of the parts, so the quantities are not carried over here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.240}.
