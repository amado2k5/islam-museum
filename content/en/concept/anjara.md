## Nature and selection
Its seed resembles leek seed in colour, but is yellower and shinier, and it burns whatever it touches, down to the intestines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}. A footnote identifies it as al-quraysh and al-kharbaq {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}.

## Temperament and actions
The plant and its seed are hot at the start of the third degree and dry in the second; the seed is less dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}.

## Uses by organ
Properties: it attracts, causes ulcers, resolves, burns strongly; it has a flatulent power and strong cleansing action, and it does not sting ulcers when cooked with meat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}. Swellings and pustules: a poultice with vinegar bursts and helps cysts, helps hard swellings, and its seed helps cancer as a poultice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}. Wounds and ulcers: its ash with salt helps malignant ulcers and cancers, and bites of dogs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}. Joints: its poultice with salt helps twisting of the sinews {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}. Head: its pounded leaves stop nosebleed; its seed opens the obstructions of the sieve-like tissue strongly, and as a poultice helps pull teeth and eases swellings behind the ears {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.365}. Chest: drunk in barley water it cleanses the chest; its leaves boiled in barley water bring up thick humours from the chest, and its seed is stronger, and it removes asthma, orthopnoea and pleurisy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.366}. Excretory organs: it stirs desire, especially its seed with a poultice, and opens the mouth of the womb so it receives semen; the book also states that taken as a pessary or in a decoction with bitter drug it brings on menses and opens the womb. These statements about menses are the book's listing, not a recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.366}. Its tender leaves strengthen the uterus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.366}. It purges phlegm more than safflower oil, and a decoction of its leaves with shell softens the bowel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.366}.
