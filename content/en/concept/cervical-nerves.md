## Summary
Ibn Sina counts eight pairs of nerves from the spinal cord passing through the neck vertebrae, and gives the exit and destination of each {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.81-82}.

## The first four pairs
The first leaves by the two holes of the first vertebra and spreads in the muscles of the head alone; it is small and fine. The second leaves between the first and second holes, brings the sense of touch to the head and reaches the muscles behind the neck and the broad muscle. The third leaves between the second and third and divides in two, reaching the muscles of head and neck and of the cheeks. The fourth leaves between the third and fourth and divides into a small front part and a back part; it is said that a thread like a spider's web from the first part runs along the carotid to the diaphragm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.81} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.82}.

## The fifth to the eighth
The fifth leaves between the fourth and fifth; its smaller front branch reaches the muscles of the cheeks and those that bow the head, and its second branch splits in two, one to the top of the shoulder and one passing to the middle of the diaphragm. The sixth, seventh and eighth leave in order, the eighth at the shared hole between the last neck vertebra and the first of the back, and their branches mix greatly: most of the sixth goes to the surface of the shoulder, most of the seventh to the upper arm, and the eighth, after mixing, reaches the skin of the forearm and arm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.82}.

## The diaphragm
Ibn Sina says the diaphragm was assigned these nerves rather than spinal nerves below them so that what comes to it descends from a height and divides well in it, and their meeting with it was set at its middle, not its edge; since the work of this organ is noble, its nerve was given many origins so that it is not undone by harm to a single one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.82}.
