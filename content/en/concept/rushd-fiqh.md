## Summary
Rushd in the lexicon is sound judgment and right conduct; in the jurists' usage it is the soundness of property for the majority, and of both property and religion for al-Shafi'i. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8736} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8737} It is the last stage of capacity: a person who comes of age sound has full capacity, his guardianship ends and his property is handed to him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8737} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8738} If he comes of age unsound, the majority hold the restriction continues until his soundness is shown, while Abu Hanifa hands over the property at twenty-five years. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8738} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8739} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8741} A child is tested by letting him conduct the kind of transactions his peers conduct, once or more, and the schools differ on the method and the time, the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis mostly before puberty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8739} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8740} The rules of soundness appear in many chapters of law, such as sale, partnership, agency, gift, endowment, marriage, khul' and custody. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8743}
