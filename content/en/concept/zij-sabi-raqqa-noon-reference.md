## Summary
In the closing lines of the foreword al-Battani says he made the extraction of the planets' motions in his tables for the moment of noon of the day being reckoned at the city of al-Raqqa, because that was where the observation and testing took place to verify all this. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11}.

## The place of observation
Al-Battani took [[place/raqqa|al-Raqqa]] as the base of his tables because he observed there and tested his computations there; its noon became the origin of the tables. The city's name appears repeatedly in the zij for this reason, notably in chapter 4 where he measures the Sun's distance from the zenith there. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11} {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.22}.

## What the extraction means
His wording that the planets' motions are extracted from the tables for the time of noon means that a table gives a planet's place at this fixed moment, so a reader must convert the wanted time into equated hours and then into the local time of his own city. The contents list assigns that conversion to chapter 34. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11} {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.8}.
