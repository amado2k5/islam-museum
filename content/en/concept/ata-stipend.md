## Summary
In the author's reading, the stipend ('ata') consists of sums paid from the surplus of the public-welfare treasury (bayt mal al-masalih) to each individual or head of household; it is not a salary or wage in the modern sense, and according to him no one has yet thoroughly established its meaning {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 326} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 327}.

## Origin of the Term and Its Forms
The author notes that the stipend could be in cash or in food; regarding food stipends, 'Umar said he allotted to every Muslim soul each month two mudds of wheat, two qists of vinegar, and two qists of oil, and when asked about slaves he said, "Yes, even the slaves" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 328}. Sometimes the stipend was issued in vouchers called qutut {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 328}.

## Abu Bakr and Equalization
Abu Bakr used to distribute the stipend equally; when asked whether he should favor people of merit and precedence, he replied that merit is rewarded by God, whereas this is a livelihood, in which equality is better than preference {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 327}.

## 'Umar and Differentiation
'Umar differed from Abu Bakr on differentiation, saying that a person has his service in Islam, his precedence, his usefulness, and his need {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 327}. He also said that if he lived until the following year, he would bring the last of the people up to the level of the first so that they would be equal in the stipend, but he died before that {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 328}. The author observes that the criteria varied—the individual (equalization), service, precedence, usefulness, or need—and that the distributive effect appears when the criterion is need or the individual {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 329}.

## Other Cases
'Umar allotted one hundred dirhams to every newborn and raised it to two hundred when the child grew older; at first he only granted it after weaning, then had his herald proclaim that parents should not hasten to wean their children {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 328}. 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz used to pay off the debts of debtors, arrange marriages for those without means, and advance loans to the payers of jizyah so they could cultivate their land {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 348}.
