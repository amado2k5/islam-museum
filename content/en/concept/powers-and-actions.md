## Overview
Ibn Sina treats faculties and acts together in the sixth lesson of the Canon's first book — a discourse and a chapter, the discourse opening with the faculties in six sections {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.94}. Each is defined through the other: every faculty is a principle of an act, every act issues from a faculty {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.94}. The physicians count three genera: the psychic, the natural and the vital {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.94}. Galen and many sages held each faculty has a principal organ as its seat; Aristotle that the heart is the source of them all.
