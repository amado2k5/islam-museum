## Overview
The mosque tower from which the call to prayer is made. It appeared in Umayyad architecture and became the most prominent architectural sign of mosques in the Islamic world. {cite:wikidata}

## Origins and forms in Yeomans
Yeomans reports Creswell's view that the corner minarets Maslama added to the [[building/masjid-amr-fustat|Mosque of Amr]] in 673 are the first recorded minarets, towers barely above the roof with external stairs. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.20} He holds that the earliest minarets arose in Syria, taking their simple rectangular form from church towers, the form that prevailed in North Africa and Spain. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.60} The English word comes from Arabic manara, "place of light or fire"; Hillenbrand suggests that the tiered Egyptian minaret may derive from the Pharos of Alexandria, square, then octagonal, then cylindrical, restored by rulers including Ibn Tulun and destroyed by earthquake in 1307. Creswell dismissed the influence; Yeomans finds it unlikely that architects ignored such a landmark. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.60} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.61}

Cairene forms include the spiral minaret with an external stair at the [[building/masjid-ibn-tulun|Mosque of Ibn Tulun]], after Samarra and Abu Dulaf, and the early Mamluk "mabkhara" (incense-burner) minaret: a rectangular base, an octagonal shaft and a ribbed helmet dome, as in the upper parts of the [[building/mosque-of-al-hakim|al-Hakim]] minarets. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.33} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.62}
