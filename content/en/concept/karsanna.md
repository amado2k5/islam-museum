## Nature
Some say it is a seed smaller than al-malik, the size of a lentil, ribbed rather than flat, dusty grey to yellow in colour, with a taste between mung and lentil, and that cattle feed on it. Al-Khuzi says its seed looks like the quince seed; the author's own view is that it is the al-malik or the wild kind of it, and that it may be whitish-yellow or red. Dioscorides describes a small, fine herb with dusty leaves, and its seeds in husks. The editor's note calls it a kind of plant. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.524}

## Temperament and properties
Hot from the first to the second degree, and dry in the second. It opens and cleanses; it has a bad humour, and its correction is as for lupin. The whitish kind has less medicinal power than the red. When it is boiled twice its cleansing force declines and its earthy part remains, so it gives a dry food. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.525}

## Cosmetic use
A good plaster for vitiligo, freckles and patches; the marks improve the colour. A flour made from it, given to the emaciated, is said to remove wasting. Its decoction poured on chilblains and rubbed in cures them, and it helps the lichen-like eruption. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.525}

## Swellings, wounds and chest
It softens hardness, especially of the breast. With honey it cleans ulcers, helps favus, softens the hardness of sores that kill flesh or a limb, and helps the Persian fire and the honey fire. It helps hardness of the breast and makes thick phlegm easier to bring up. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.525}

## Excretory organs and poisons
Taken in excess it brings blood into the urine because of its strong urine-driving force; it loosens the bowels. Mixed with vinegar and drunk, it helps difficult urine and calms tenesmus and colic. For poisons it is applied in wine to the bite of a viper and the bite of a mad dog. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.525}
