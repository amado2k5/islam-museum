## Overview
Muhammad al-Bahi sees the Islamic law of inheritance as built to disperse inherited wealth, not preserve it: the fixed fractional shares — half, third, quarter, sixth — prevent hoarding and set the heirs to work rather than living off the estate {cite:al-islam-fitrat-allah-azhar p. 135} {cite:al-islam-fitrat-allah-azhar p. 136}. He contrasts the European system that kept title and fortune with a single son — nearer, he says, to immortalizing the person {cite:al-islam-fitrat-allah-azhar p. 137}. On bequests: "no bequest to an heir" keeps wealth from escaping dispersal; a will stops at a third; and leaving heirs rich is better than leaving them a burden.
