## Summary
The horizon circle is the boundary between the part of the sky seen above the earth and the part hidden below it; its pole is the zenith {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 110}. It is among the great circles that bisect the heaven {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 110}.

## Definition and poles
Al-Farghani describes the horizon as the circle separating the visible from the hidden part of the sky, its pole the zenith point {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 110}. Because the earth is very small relative to the heaven, the horizon divides the heaven into two halves {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 153}.

## How the horizon divides the circles
Al-Farghani shows that the horizon bisects only the equinoctial itself among the circles parallel to it; every other parallel is divided into two unequal parts, the northern part above the earth being greater for a northern circle and the southern part below greater for a southern one {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 154}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 155}. This division is why day and night differ in length between summer and winter {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 155}.

## The horizon in different places
At the equator the horizon passes through the poles of the equinoctial, so night and day are always equal {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 153}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 154}. At the pole the horizon coincides with the equinoctial, the heaven turning like a mill parallel to the horizon, and only its northern half appears {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 101}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 161}.
