## Where the disagreement lies
The disagreement is not over beautifying the voice in recitation — that is recommended among the jurists — but over using musical melodies and the maqamat of Arabic song in tilawa; the books of fiqh treat it under the heading "talhin al-Qur'an" {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## The positions
One group forbids or dislikes the practice when the melody takes a letter beyond its measure, adds vowels, or introduces trilling — holding that the forbidden taghani is whatever bends the recitation from the rule of tajwid into the metres of song. Another group permits beautifying the voice even when a melody falls in it, so long as no letter is taken beyond its due and no artifice turns the hearer from the meaning; they note that many of the early readers recited on tunes the listeners recognised without naming them by the maqamat of the musical manuals {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## What is not disputed
The people of performance agree that tajwid is not the accompaniment of instruments, nor stretching the ghunna and mudud beyond their measure, and that Ibn al-Jazari described tajwid itself as "the ornament of the tilawa and the beauty of performance and reading" — placing the beauty in exactness, not in melody {cite:ar-wikisource}.
