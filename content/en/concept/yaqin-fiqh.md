Yaqin (certainty) lexically means knowledge, the removal of doubt and the confirmation of a matter; it is the opposite of doubt. Technically the jurists define it as the heart's firm conviction that a thing has or has not happened. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18001}

Related terms: doubt (shakk) is hesitation between two opposites with no preference, which contradicts certainty. Suspicion (wahm) is a weaker conviction, which is opposed to certainty. Opinion (zann) is a stronger leaning with the possibility of the opposite, which is also opposed to certainty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18001} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18002}

Rulings: the roots of faith, in God, His angels, books, messengers and the Last Day, require certainty and are established only by it, on several verses of the Qur'an that the book names by surah and verse. Verses which use the word zann in the sense of certainty are cited too; the detail belongs to the books of creed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18002} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18003}

Rules of certainty: the jurists framed general rules giving certainty precedence over doubt, opinion and suspicion. The first: certainty is not removed by doubt; what is established by certainty is not lifted except by certainty. Its proof includes the hadith that if one feels something in his belly and is unsure whether anything came out, he should not leave the mosque until he hears a sound or smells something; and the hadith that if one doubts in prayer how many rak'as he has prayed, he should cast out the doubt and build on what is certain. A related hadith on building on the least number is reported by al-Tirmidhi, and Ibn Hajar criticises its chain. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18003} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18004}

Among the rules that follow: the original state persists; so one who is sure he is in purity and doubts that he has broken it remains pure, and one who is sure he is impure and doubts his purity remains impure. The original state of the person is freedom from debt, so a single witness is not accepted to impose a debt unless supported by another cause. If one doubts whether he did something, the presumption is that he did not. If one is sure he did an act and doubts whether it was a little or much, it is taken as little, unless the duty was already engaged, in which case it is discharged only by certainty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18004}

Second: the original state of women's private parts is prohibition; where a woman is both lawful and forbidden, prohibition prevails, so one may not search in such matters. Third: the original state of things is non-existence, as when the worker in a profit-sharing partnership says he made no profit and his word is accepted. See opinion, doubt and suspicion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18004}
