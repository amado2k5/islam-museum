## This chapter (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina makes ishq an obsessional illness like melancholia, which a person has brought on himself by setting his thought on finding certain forms and traits good, whether his desire then helped him in this or did not {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.112}

Its sign is a sunken, dry eye, no tears except in weeping, and a continuous laughing movement of the lid, as if he were looking at something pleasant, hearing glad news or joking; his breath is often cut off and drawn back, he sighs often, and his state changes to joy and laughter, or to grief and weeping, on hearing love poetry, above all on mention of separation and parting; all his members are withered except the eye, his traits have no order, and his pulse is varied and without order, like the pulse of the worried; his pulse and state change at the mention of the beloved in particular and at meeting him suddenly; the book describes a way of knowing the beloved from the pulse, and it is not reproduced here; a word about the lid, printed 'sumayyatihi', is carried as printed and not interpreted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.112}

Ibn Sina relates that he saw one recover health and strength after wasting and long illnesses when he sensed a union with his beloved, and he takes this as proof that nature obeys the imaginations of the soul {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.112}
