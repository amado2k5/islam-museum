## Summary
Ibn Sina divides unevenness in a single part of the pulse into the intermittent, the returning and the continuous, and defines each {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.172}.

## The intermittent and the returning
The intermittent is that which is cut off in one part by a real pause, and the two ends of the part separated by the pause may differ in speed, slowness and likeness. The returning is a large pulse that turned small in one part and then came back. The text here also names the inserted pulse, which is like two beats because they overlap, and the disputants differ over it. The rest of this passage is garbled in the scan and has been left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.172}.

## The continuous
The continuous is that whose unevenness is graded in unbroken continuity with no felt break, in what it changes to, from speed to slowness or the reverse or to moderation, or from largeness or smallness to something it moves toward. It may go on alike, or it may happen that despite its continuity it is more uneven in some parts and less in others {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.172}.
