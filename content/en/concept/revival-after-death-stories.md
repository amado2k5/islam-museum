## Summary
Sha'rawi tells the story of the man who passed a ruined town, whose 'roofs had fallen in', and whom God caused to die for a hundred years and then revived. He ties it to Abraham's question about how God revives {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1130} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1132}.

## Asking how
He holds that asking how does not show doubt. It shows wonder at a fact one does not doubt, as one wonders how the pyramids were built {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1132}.

## Contracted and expanded time
He notes that the food had not changed, which confirmed the man's view that he had stayed a day or part of one, while the donkey had turned to bones, which confirmed the hundred years. God contracts time for one thing and expands it for another {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1133} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1134} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1135}.

## 'Uzayr
He reports that most scholars take the passer-by to be 'Uzayr, who had memorised the Torah, and he tells how the people of the town recognised him. These narratives have not been checked against their sources {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1135} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1136}.

## The people of the cave
He compares the story with the sleep of the People of the Cave and likens the suspension to the winter sleep of snakes {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1137}.

## Not yet checked
