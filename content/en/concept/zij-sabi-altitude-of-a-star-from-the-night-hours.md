## Summary
When the star's distance from the meridian line is known, one takes the returned chord of that distance and subtracts it from the returned chord of the half day arc, multiplies the remainder by the chord of the star's altitude at midheaven, divides by the returned chord of the half day arc, and takes the arc of the result as chords are turned into arcs; that arc is the star's altitude at that hour. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.57}.

## The reverse of the previous chapter
This working is the reverse of the one in chapter 21: there the altitude is known and the elapsed time is derived, and here the time is known and the altitude derived, the tool in both cases being the returned chord. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.57}.
