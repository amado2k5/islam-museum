## Summary
In the eighth and last chapter of the regimen of travellers Ibn Sina says that the sea traveller turns dizzy and is seized by nausea and vomiting in the first days, then settles; he names what prevents nausea, fruits such as quince and apple, celery seed and wormwood, and sour foods that strengthen the mouth of the stomach, such as lentils with vinegar or with verjuice, and bread cooled in a fragrant wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.266}.

## Dizziness and vomiting
The sea traveller may turn dizzy and be turned about, and nausea and vomiting may rise in him, in the first days, then calm and settle; he is left until he vomits, and if it goes to excess it is then checked {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.266}.

## What prevents nausea
There is no harm in preparing so that vomiting does not come, by taking fruits such as quince and apple; drinking celery seed stops nausea from rising and quiets it when it has, and wormwood does likewise; it is also prevented by foods with sourness that strengthen the mouth of the stomach and prevent vapour rising to the head, such as lentils with vinegar or verjuice and a little pennyroyal, or bread cooled in fragrant wine or cold water; and the inside of the nostrils is wiped with white lead {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.266}.
