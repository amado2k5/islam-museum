## Summary
Al-Battani says the ancients took most of this from summer observations at the summer solstice point, and that these are not as exact as observations at one of the two equinox points, especially the autumn equinox, for the clarity of the air then; the reason is that at the solstice the Sun is slow in its declination and at the equinoxes very quick {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.65-66}.

## The argument and the practice
The argument is geometrical and astronomical: at the solstice the declination stays almost still for several days, so the moment of passage is hard to fix, while at an equinox the declination changes quickly and the time of passage is fixed more closely. The text reports that Ptolemy relied only on the autumn observations and measured his result by them, and al-Battani follows this in his own observation at al-Raqqa {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.66}. See [[concept/zij-sabi-hipparchus-and-ptolemy-autumn-equinox-pair|the autumn equinox pair of Hipparchus and Ptolemy]].
