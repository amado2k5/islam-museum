## Summary

The question of taswib and takhti'a belongs to [[concept/ijtihad|ijtihad]] in legal theory: when two mujtahids reason on a question and differ, are both right, or is one right and the other mistaken? It comes up in the life of [[person/ala-al-din-al-kasani|al-Kasani]] in the introduction to the edition of [[work/badai-al-sanai|Bada'i' al-Sana'i']]. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74}

## The debate as the editors relate it

The editors relate that al-Kasani debated this question with a jurist in the land of Rum; the jurist said the report from Abu Hanifa is that every mujtahid is right; al-Kasani said: no, the sound report from Abu Hanifa is that of two mujtahids one is right and one mistaken, and the truth lies on one side, and what you say is the Mu'tazila's doctrine; words passed between them and ended with al-Kasani raising his whip at the jurist, which led to his being sent as an envoy to Aleppo. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74} {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.75}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni, Hanafi: al-Kasani**: one of the two mujtahids is right and the other mistaken, the truth lying on one side; for him this is the sound report from Abu Hanifa. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74}
- **Sunni, Hanafi: the jurist he debated (unnamed)**: the report from Abu Hanifa is that every mujtahid is right. {cite:badai-sanai-ilmiyya-v1 p.74}
- **The Mu'tazili view from their own books: not yet sourced (needs_source)**: al-Kasani ascribed the view that all are right to the Mu'tazila; their view and the legal theorists' detailed treatment are not given here.
:::
