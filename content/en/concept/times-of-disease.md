## Summary
In the seventh chapter Ibn Sina says most diseases have four times: beginning, increase, peak and decline, each a sensible stretch of time with its own rule, and what falls outside them belongs to the times of health {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.

## The four times
Most diseases have four times: the time of beginning, of increase, of peak and of decline, and what falls outside these belongs to the times of health. By beginning and peak he does not mean two ends in which the state of the disease cannot be discerned, but for each a perceptible stretch of time with its own rule. The beginning is the time in which the disease appears and is much alike in its states, its increase not yet discernible. The increase is the time in which its intensifying is seen from time to time. The peak is the time in which the disease stands in all its parts at one state. The decline is the time in which its lessening appears, and the further it goes the plainer the lessening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.

## Total and partial
These times may be reckoned over the disease from its start to its end through its paroxysms, and are then called total times, or paroxysm by paroxysm, and are then called partial times {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.109}.
