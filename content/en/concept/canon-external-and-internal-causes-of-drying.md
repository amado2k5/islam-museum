## Summary
Ibn Sina lists the causes of dissolving and rotting: from outside, air that dissolves and putrefies, and from inside, the innate heat that dissolves our moistures and the foreign heat generated from putrefying food, all working together to dry us, a drying that is necessary and unavoidable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.

## External and internal causes
The external causes are such as air that dissolves and rots. The internal causes are such as the innate heat in us that dissolves our moistures, and the foreign heat generated in us from our food and other things that rot. All these causes cooperate in drying us {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.

## Drying is necessary
Our reaching completion and maturity, and our becoming able to perform our functions, come with a great drying that happens to us and goes on until it is complete. At the start we are extremely moist, and our heat must prevail over that moisture or it would be pent up inside it; so the heat acts on it constantly and dries it constantly, and the first effect of the drying is a move toward balance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.
