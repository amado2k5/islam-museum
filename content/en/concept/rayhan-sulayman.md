## Nature
A plant found on the mountains of Isfahan, resembling fresh dill; it is said that its leaf is like the marshmallow and its small flowers twist round the tree like ivy; it seems there is a disagreement, and the second saying seems to point to the plant called jamsafram, for the common people suppose that Jam is Sulayman {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.661}

## Actions and properties
Subtle and drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.661}

## Swellings and pustules
Smeared on with vinegar it benefits erysipelas, and it is smeared on phlegmatic swellings, its leaf and likewise its oil being smeared on phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.661}

## Wounds and ulcers
Smeared on with vinegar on creeping ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.661}

## Joints
Smeared on gout it benefits it, and this is its special property {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Organs of the head
It benefits facial palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it is taken up with rose oil for pain of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}

## Poisons and bites
It is smeared on the sting of the scorpion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.662}
