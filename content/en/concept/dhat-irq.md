## Summary
Dhat Irq is the pilgrim's station (miqat) for the people of Iraq and for others who pass it, two stages from Mecca. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8371}
All agree that a pilgrim who passes it intending hajj or umra must enter the state of ihram there. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8372}

## Whether it is fixed by the text
All agree that the four other stations are fixed by the text. Whether Dhat Irq is fixed by the text or by judgement is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8372}
The Hanafis, Ibn al-Hattab among the Malikis, the majority of the Shafi'is and the Hanbalis hold it fixed by the text, as does Ata' ibn Abi Rabah; Malik in the Mudawwana and al-Shafi'i in al-Umm hold it fixed by judgement, as do Tawus and Ibn Sirin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8372}
Those who hold it textual cite the report of Jabir on the stations for the people of Iraq, and the report of Aisha, and say that together they strengthen one another. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8372} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8373}
Those who hold it a matter of judgement cite the report of Ibn Umar that Umar fixed it when the two garrison towns were conquered. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8373}
