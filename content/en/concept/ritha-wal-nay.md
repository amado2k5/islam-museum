## Summary
Ritha' is praise of the dead and the recounting of his virtues; na'y is announcing a death to the people; ta'ziya is consoling and steadying the bereaved; and weeping and niyaha are what happens among the family at a loss, with the limits the Sunnah drew. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8622} This record gathers the most famous words on these four subjects from the Qur'an, the Sunnah, the seerah, the reports of the Companions and the Successors, and the harvested books; whatever is harvested later joins it automatically.

## The istirja' and the first blow
The Qur'an gave the afflicted a phrase to say, in [[passage/quran-2-156|al-Baqara]]. Sahih Muslim, no. 2127, has Umm Salama hearing the Prophet say that a servant who is struck by a loss and says «we belong to God and to Him we return; O God, reward me in my loss and give me better in its place» is rewarded and given better; [[passage/muslim-api-2127|the report is in the browser]]. Sahih al-Bukhari, no. 1302, has the Prophet say: «patience is at the first blow»; [[passage/bukhari-api-1302|the text is here]].

## «To God belongs what He took and what He gave»
One of the Prophet's daughters sent for him because her child was dying; he sent back greetings and said: «to God belongs what He took and what He gave, everything with Him has a fixed term; let her be patient and seek reward». Al-Bukhari, no. 1284; [[passage/bukhari-api-1284|the text is in the browser]]. At the death of his son [[event/death-of-ibrahim|Ibrahim]] the Prophet wept, and his tears were mercy and not despair.

## Announcing martyrs and kings
The Prophet announced to his Companions the deaths of [[event/mutah-prophets-grief-and-condolence|Ja'far, Zayd and Ibn Rawaha]] on the day of Mu'ta before their news arrived, his eyes streaming; al-Bukhari, no. 3630, [[passage/bukhari-api-3630|the text is here]]. He announced the Negus on the day he died, and prayed over him in absence. He ordered food made for Ja'far's family, which became the sunna of consoling by deed.

## Elegies for the Prophet and the martyrs
The best-known elegies of the first generation are [[person/hassan-ibn-thabit|Hassan ibn Thabit]]'s verses on [[work/hassan-elegy-for-the-prophet|the Prophet]], on Hamza and on the martyrs of Bir Ma'una, and [[person/kaab-ibn-malik|Ka'b ibn Malik]]'s on the martyrs of Mu'ta; all are in the poetry records with their lines and al-Barquqi's commentary. Ibn Majah, no. 1630, relates Fatima's words on the day the Prophet died: «O my father, how near he has come to his Lord»; [[passage/ibnmajah-api-1630|the text is here]].

## Weeping, wailing and their limits
The prohibition of wailing comes in many reports, among them Ibn Majah no. 1592 on the prohibition of elegies, [[passage/ibnmajah-api-1592|in the browser]], and «do not curse the dead» in al-Bukhari no. 1393, [[passage/bukhari-api-1393|here]]. The jurists distinguish the tears of the eye and the sorrow of the heart, which are permitted, from slapping the cheeks, tearing the garments and raising the voice in lament, and an elegy that recounts virtues without excess from one that stirs up the grief. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8623}
