## Summary
Ibn Sina says the muscles of the face match the moving parts of the face: the forehead, the two eyeballs, the two upper eyelids, the cheek together with the lips, the lips, the two sides of the nose tip and the lower jaw; he details those of the forehead, cheek, lip and nostril {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.61-63}.

## The forehead
The forehead moves by a thin, transverse, membranous muscle spread under the skin of the forehead and so mixed with it that it is almost part of its substance; it meets the moving part without a tendon since what moves is a broad light skin. By its movement the eyebrows rise, and by its relaxing it may help the eye close {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.61}.

## The cheek
The cheek has two movements: one following the lower jaw and one shared with the lip. Its muscle is one in each cheek, broad, made of four parts whose fibres come from four places: from the collarbone, drawing the mouth down obliquely; from the sternum and collarbone, the fibres of right and left crossing, which narrow the mouth and push it forward when they contract; from the acromion of the shoulder, tilting the lip to the two sides; and from the spines of the neck, moving the cheek with the lip following, and in some people reaching the root of the ear and moving it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.62}.

## The lip and the nostril
Besides the muscles it shares with the cheek the lip has four of its own, a pair from above on the line of the cheeks and a pair from below. These suffice to move it alone in the four directions, and they are so mixed into the substance of the lip that the senses cannot tell them apart, since the lip is a soft fleshy organ with no bone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.62-63}. The two sides of the nose tip have two small strong muscles: small so as not to crowd the muscles of cheek and lip, which move more often, and strong to make up for the lack of bone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.63}.
