## Summary
The gharib is the fourth class of report by number of chains for [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]]: one related by a single person alone at any point in the chain. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 45} Together with [[concept/al-hadith-al-mashhur|the mashhur]] and [[concept/al-hadith-al-aziz|the 'aziz]] it is a solitary report (khabar wahid), and solitary reports include accepted and rejected ones. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 46}

## Absolute and relative
- **Absolute (fard mutlaq)**: the singularity lies at the root of the chain, the point on which the chain turns and to which it returns, which is the end where the Companion stands. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 49} {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 51} An example is the prohibition of selling or giving away the bond of clientage (wala'), related only by 'Abd Allah ibn Dinar from [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn 'Umar]]; singularity can also run on, as in the report of the branches of faith, related only by Abu Salih from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] and only by 'Abd Allah ibn Dinar from Abu Salih; al-Bazzar's Musnad and al-Tabarani's al-Mu'jam al-Awsat hold many reports singular at every link. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 51} The editor quotes Muslim: "All people depend on 'Abd Allah ibn Dinar for this report." {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 51}
- **Relative (fard nisbi)**: more than one relate the report from the Companion, then a single person relates it from one of them; the singularity is relative to a particular person, though the report in itself may be well known. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 51-52}

## Gharib and fard in usage
Ibn Hajar says gharib and fard are synonyms in language and terminology, but specialists distinguish them by frequency of use: fard is mostly used of the absolute kind and gharib of the relative; with the verb they make no distinction, saying in both cases "so-and-so alone related it". {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 52} He compares their usage of munqati' and mursal: most hadith scholars distinguish the two nouns, but use only the verb *arsala* for both, which led some to think they did not distinguish them. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 52}

## Singularity and soundness
For Ibn Hajar singularity does not exclude soundness; hence he rejected the claim that a number of narrators is a condition of soundness, pointing out that "actions are by intentions" — the first report in al-Bukhari's Sahih — is singular at several levels. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 44} The editor notes that hadith scholars also call the absolute singular "gharib in chain and text". {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 51}
