## Summary
Sha'rawi tells how Hajar ran between al-Safa and al-Marwa seeking water for her son, and holds that the sa'y joins taking the means with trust in the One who causes. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.668} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.669}

## Trust with the means
Sha'rawi says that Hajar's words "then He will not leave us" tied her to the Causer rather than to the cause. The water then came at the infant's foot, an unusual means that God willed to keep causality in place. Sha'rawi distinguishes trust (tawakkul), an act of the heart, from idleness (tawakkul-less passivity), which stops the limbs from working. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.669}

## Cleansing the place of idols
Sha'rawi reports that idolaters set the idols Isaf on al-Safa and Na'ila on al-Marwa, so that some believers scrupled to run between them. The verse then affirmed that al-Safa and al-Marwa are among God's rites, and that the place is not made unclean by the idols placed there. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.669} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.670}

## What circling means
Sha'rawi reads the word for circling in the verse as going and returning between the two points many times, like a night watchman who walks one street back and forth. He holds that the sa'y is a rite of Hajj and 'Umra. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.671}

## The question of the pillar
Sha'rawi answers those who argue that the sa'y is not a pillar: the verse came because of the believers' scruple, so the denial of blame does not mean the act is optional. The schools' rulings on the sa'y, the academic view and other views are not entered here. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.672}
