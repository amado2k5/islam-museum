## Summary
Ibn Sina holds that the ribs are a shield for the organs of breathing and the upper organs of nutrition, that they were not made one bone, and that the sternum is made of seven bones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.52-53}.

## The ribs
The ribs protect what they surround of the breathing organs and the upper nutritive organs. They were not made one bone, so as not to be heavy, so that a harm that comes does not spread, and so that they expand easily when need increases or when the viscera fill with food and wind. The seven upper ribs enclose what is in them and meet at the sternum, surrounding the ruling organ on every side; they are called the chest ribs, with the two middle ones larger and longer and the ends shorter. Those beside the digestive organs were made like an awl at the back and do not join in front, and they shorten step by step, to widen the place of the stomach so that it is not squeezed when it fills. The five remaining short ribs have heads joined to cartilage, so they do not break and do not meet the soft organs and the diaphragm with their hardness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.52-53}.

## The sternum and the xiphoid
The sternum is made of seven bones, not one, for the reasons already given and so that it helps the breathing organs expand more easily; for that reason it was made brittle and joined by cartilage, and it has seven bones for the seven ribs attached to it. Joined to its lower end is a broad cartilaginous bone called the xiphoid, from its likeness to a dagger, a protection for the mouth of the stomach and a mediator between the sternum and the soft organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.53}.
