## Overview
The Hijri calendar is a lunar calendar whose years are counted from the Prophet's migration from Mecca to Medina. It fixes the dates of Ramadan, the hajj and the two festivals. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434}

## Dating from the Hijra
Reports say that Umar ibn al-Khattab adopted the Hijra as the starting point for dating. They differ over whether this followed a consultation with the Companions, and over which year it was. This disagreement needs a second source before it can be treated as established (needs_source).

## For Sprouts
The Hijri calendar is a lunar calendar that starts from the Prophet's migration to Medina, and it fixes the dates of Ramadan, the Hajj and the feasts.

## For Explorers
The Hijri calendar is counted by the moon and begins in the year the Prophet migrated from Mecca to Medina. It tells us when Ramadan, the Hajj and the feasts fall, and people say things like the year 1447 AH.

## For Scholars
A lunar calendar whose years are counted from the Prophet's migration from Mecca to Medina, and which sets the dates of the acts of worship such as Ramadan, the Hajj and the feasts. The reports say that Umar ibn al-Khattab adopted the Hijra as the starting point of dating, but they differ on why he chose it and on its date, so that needs a second source. The lunar year is about eleven days shorter than the solar year, which is why the seasons of the calendar move through the year. The conversion of dates from Hijri to Gregorian is a derived calculation in the museum, not taken from a source.

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] says the lunar month is more exact than the solar month because the moon is the nearest heavenly body to us, and that the Muslim year is lunar-solar: the moon's circuit of the earth sets the month, and the circuit of the earth and moon around the sun sets the year. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:11:00} The difference between the lunar and solar years, he says, is eleven days, so the lunar months move through the seasons and neither fasting nor the Hajj always falls in one season. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:12:00}
