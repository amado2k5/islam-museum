## Summary
An eccentric sphere is one whose centre does not coincide with the earth's centre, so a body's distance from the earth varies between apogee and perigee. Al-Farghani shows that the spheres of the seven planets have centres eccentric to the earth's in different directions {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 84}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 66}.

## The sun
The sun has one eccentric sphere; its centre's distance from the earth's centre is two and a half of the sixty parts that make the sphere's radius, and this distance is, in al-Farghani's reckoning, the sun's mean distance from the earth {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 66}.

## The moon and the five planets
The moon has two centres: one fixed, and another revolving about the earth's centre at a distance equal to that of the fixed centre. The five wandering planets have three centres on a straight line — the earth's centre, a fixed centre and a moving centre — with equal intervals between them, and Mercury has a third centre moving on one of the other two {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 66}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 65}.

## Its effect on motion
Because the centre is eccentric, a body's apparent speed varies: it appears fastest near the earth and slowest far from it, as al-Farghani shows in the chapter on motions {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 66}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 67}.

## Chapter sixteen in detail
In chapter sixteen al-Farghani shows that each of the seven planets has two centres, and that the centres' distances from the earth's centre are measured in half-radii of the eccentric sphere, which is sixty parts. The sun's distance is two and a half parts; the moon's equals the distance of the fixed centre {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 196}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 197}.
