## Summary
Ibn Sina counts dryness of temperament, of the mesentery and of the air among the strongest causes of emaciation, since a dried mesentery does not take in food; dryness and emaciation are treated with rubbing before the bath, between rough and gentle, until the skin reddens, then a pitch coating, moderate exercise, bathing without delay, drying, a little oil and suitable food, and he refers the full account to the book of adornment in Book Four {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.257}.

## Causes of emaciation
Ibn Sina says that among the strongest causes of emaciation are dryness of temperament, as he will describe, the mesentery and dryness of the air; when the mesentery dries it does not take in food. The opening of this chapter in the printing has garbled words, and it is taken only by its evident sense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.257}.

## The treatment
Dryness and emaciation are treated with rubbing before the bath, between rough and gentle, until the skin reddens, then a coating of pitch, then moderate exercise, then bathing without delay, then drying with dry cloths, a little oil, and the suitable food. If his age, the season and his habit allow, he pours cold water over himself. The limit of the rubbing before the pitch is that swelling not begin in the wasted part, which is close to what was said about enlarging a small organ; the full account is in the book of adornment of Book Four {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.257}.
