## Overview
Ibn Sa'id described the organisation of government in al-Andalus in six offices (*khutat*), as al-Maqqari transmits them. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 252-255}

## The offices
The vizierate: shared among a group, from whom the prince chose a chamberlain (*hajib*). The offices were inherited within certain houses. The secretaryship: at its head the secretary of letters and the keeper of the register (*zimam*); he notes that the latter could not be a Christian or a Jew, because he needed the notables of the people. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 252-255}

The officer in charge of land-tax affairs: he describes him as greater than the vizier, though if he was deceived by his earnings he would be confiscated. The judiciary: he counts it the greatest of the offices among the elite and the common people alike, and its holder is the chief judge of the community. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 252-255}

The police: its head is the governor of the city and the night-watch. The office of market supervision (*ihtisab*): it is entrusted to people of learning and shrewdness, and the market supervisor rode out himself to the markets, while his assistants held the bread scales. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 252-255}

This is Ibn Sa'id's description as al-Maqqari transmits it, from a single source. It needs a further source.
