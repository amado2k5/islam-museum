## Summary
Al-Sallabi collects a large number of maxims and proverbs attributed to 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, considering them valuable educational and outreach material for refining souls and instructing people {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 257}.

## Examples of the Maxims
Among the numbered maxims in the book are: "Night prayer is radiance by day" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 253}, "Blessed is the one who acts upon his knowledge" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 254}, "Opportunity passes like a passing cloud" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 254}, "There is no nobility with bad manners" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 254}, "There is no rest for the envious" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 254}, and "Kindness is a treasure among treasures" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 254}. Among the maxims on speech and companionship are: "A person is hidden beneath his tongue" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 256}, "Whoever has sweet speech has many brothers" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 255}, and "Keep company with the good and you will be safe from the wicked" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 255}. And among the maxims on patience and asceticism: "Helplessness is an affliction, patience is courage, asceticism is wealth, and piety is a shield" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 256}.

## Maxims in the Book
The book cites maxims on human nature and the soul, including the saying that the soul is "prone to evil," and that disciplining it benefits it while neglecting it ruins it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 256}. Another is: "When the world turns favorably toward someone, it lends him the virtues of others" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 256}. The book notes that many of these maxims were transmitted by Ibn Qutaybah, al-Tha'alibi, and al-Murtada al-Nadawi {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 253}.

## Reading of the Book
Al-Sallabi holds that these maxims were among 'Ali's means—alongside sermons, poetry, and exhortations—for guiding and rectifying society {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 257}. Regarding the qualities by which he described the best of God's servants, the book cites his saying: "When they do good, they rejoice; when they do wrong, they seek forgiveness; when they are tested, they are patient; and when they are angered, they forgive" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 257}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book mostly bases the attribution of the numbered maxims to 'Ali on late sources (Manhaj 'Ali ibn Abi Talib fi al-Da'wah ila Allah and the manuscript Nathr al-La'ali); each saying needs to be traced back to the earliest available source.
