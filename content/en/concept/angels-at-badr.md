## Overview
The Qur'an speaks of the reinforcement of the angels on the day of Badr, and [[person/rashid-rida|Rida]] presents in his tafsir of [[passage/quran-8-9|Al-Anfal 9]] to [[passage/quran-8-14|14]] the exegetes' disagreement: did the angels fight, or was their reinforcement good news and a strengthening of hearts? {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.512}

## In the Qur'an
- [[passage/quran-8-9|Al-Anfal 9]]: "when you sought help from your Lord and He answered you: I will reinforce you with a thousand angels, rank upon rank" — in it the reinforcement at Badr with a thousand angels {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- [[passage/quran-8-12|Al-Anfal 12]]: "when your Lord revealed to the angels: I am with you, so steady those who believe; I will cast terror into the hearts of those who disbelieve" — in it that God revealed to the angels that He was with them {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- [[passage/quran-8-56|Al-Anfal 56]]: "those who disbelieve spend their wealth to bar from God's way..." — in it the context of the war {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- [[passage/quran-3-123|Al 'Imran 123]], [[passage/quran-3-124|Al 'Imran 124]], and [[passage/quran-3-125|Al 'Imran 125]]: "And God indeed aided you at Badr while you were weak... when He said to the believers: is it not enough for you that your Lord should reinforce you with three thousand angels, sent down?" — in them the promise of reinforcement with the three thousand {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In hadith
- In a narration of al-Bukhari on the description of the day of Badr "God reinforced you with a thousand angels" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- In the hadith "You saw few..." in which the angels descended on the day of Badr {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.
- In a narration of Muslim that the Prophet said "the angels on the day of Badr..." {cite:sahih-muslim}.
- In the hadith "Jibril came to me with Mika'il..." in some narrations {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}.

## The view of the scholars of inquiry
Rida mentions that the scholars of inquiry were certain the angels did not fight on the day of Badr, and that God's saying in [[passage/quran-8-10|Al-Anfal 10]] about the reinforcement being good news and reassurance for hearts indicates this. He holds that the address in the whole surah is directed to the believers, and that the mention of revelation to the angels came incidentally. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.512}

## The interpretation of inspiration
Rida mentions another view: that God commanded the angels to cast meaning into the hearts of the believers by inspiration, just as Satan casts whisperings. He answers the difficulty by noting that the surah was revealed after the fighting, because the good news of reinforcement came before it and the Prophet informed his companions of it, so the surah's revelation was a reminder of the favour. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.512}

## The reports of the angels fighting
Rida relates from al-Alusi and others an interpretation of the verse as the angels fighting, and says that Ibn Jarir did not turn to these reports nor mention them. He mentions that Ibn Kathir cited from them only the saying of al-Rabi' ibn Anas about the marks of the slain angels, and Rida comments that this report has no chain, and that interpreting the verse as the angels fighting contradicts the Qur'anic text in making the reinforcement good news. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.512} {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.513}

## Ibn Abbas and the scene of Badr
Rida mentions that what is attributed to [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] about the angels fighting is a mursal report without a chain on which an argument rests, and that Ibn Abbas did not witness Badr because of his youth, so his reports about it are mursal even if in the Sahih. {cite:tafsir-manar-v09 p.513}

## With the exegetes
Ibn Kathir details that the angels of reinforcement are those who descended to strengthen the believers and aid them, and mentions that their number at Badr was three thousand {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}. Al-Qurtubi holds that "mardifin" in [[passage/quran-8-9|Al-Anfal 9]] are the angels who followed you {cite:mufhim-qurtubi-v7}.

## Did they fight or only strengthen?
- **The better-known view**: they descended for reinforcement and victory, strengthened the believers, and there is a narration that they fought {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
- **Another view**: they did not fight at Badr but only strengthened the believers {cite:tafsir-manar-v09}.
- The principle in tafsir is that they descended for aid and victory {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## Notes for researchers
- "Angels of Badr" is a technical name; the Qur'an calls them "angels" in [[passage/quran-8-9|Al-Anfal 9]] {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- In it is evidence that the reinforcement was from God, not from the Muslims {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.
