## Summary
Ibn Sina mentions infantile convulsions, whose treatment he gave under diseases of the head, and adds a powder of thyme, castoreum and cumin; then prolapse of the rectum, treated by a decoction of astringents in which the child sits lukewarm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.219}.

## Infantile convulsions
A child may have rih al-sibyan; we gave its treatment in the chapter on diseases of the head, but we mention something that helps them much: equal parts of thyme, castoreum and cumin are ground together and given, the dose being three grains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.219}.

## Rectal prolapse
A child may have prolapse of the rectum, and then pomegranate rinds, fresh myrtle, oak husk, goat's hooves, pomegranate flower, gall-nut, dry rose, burnt horn, Yemeni alum and cumin are taken in equal parts, a dirham of each, boiled hard in water to draw out their strength, and the child sits in the decoction lukewarm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.219}.
