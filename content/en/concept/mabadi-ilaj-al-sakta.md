## By the cause
The treatment of what comes of an outside hurt is the management of that apparent cause; of what comes of a partner organ, the management of the organ that shares with it, as was passed in the Canon and in other chapters {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.133}

## The sanguine
That which is of blood is managed by venesection at the time, letting much blood — for he recovers at once; after venesection he is clystered with what brings the matter down from the head, his regimen is lightened, he is kept to the lightest foods, given to smell what strengthens the brain, and not heated {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.133}

## The phlegmatic
As for what comes of phlegm: if signs of blood are found with it the two managements are joined; strong evacuants and gradual warming with compresses, fomentations and heating unguents are used on it (the drug names, recipes and cupping sites are not carried); one begins with the weakest of the unguents — that being the most successful — otherwise it is increased and moved to the stronger; what rouses is brought near his nose (not carried) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.133}

## The benefit of vomiting
If he can be made to vomit by what is put into his throat — especially when fullness in his stomach is surmised and an indigestion has preceded — he profits greatly by it; vomiting has another benefit, for retching and straining to vomit warms the temperament of heads whose apoplexy is cold and moist; their winds must be eased by what expels them, and they find lightness in it; and there may be haste to put between their teeth what was mentioned before, lest their teeth spoil one another {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.133}

## After evacuation, and feeding
If after evacuation a little of the strengtheners can be given in honey-water or with spices as needed, it is done; if he improves he is made to gargle and sneeze and cupping-glasses are set on what the book states (the sites are not carried); they are swung in a swing and bathed after three weeks, anointed on the bath day with heating oils. Anointing them with hot oils strengthens the pneuma in the nerves and the substance of the nerves that dissolves the residues without violence. The feeding of apoplectics is lighter than that of epileptics: it is best they keep to bread alone in the mornings — bread with dried figs is good for them; drinking on food is among the most harmful things for them; if they mean to sup they may first take a light exercise and stir the relaxed organs; when they have eaten they do not sleep on it quickly but wait until it descends and digests, nor do they keep awake much, for that wearies the brain and releases undigested vapours from the foods; new wine does not suit them for its residues, nor old wine for the speed of its reaching the brain and filling it — the most suitable drink for them is what is between the two {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.133} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.134}
