## Summary
Ibn Sina notes that one subtle matter in treatment is when two opposed demands meet in a single disease, as with a fever that calls for cooling and the obstructions that cause it, which call for heating, or a colic whose matter calls for heating and cutting while its severe pain calls for cooling and numbing, and that not every fullness or bad temper is treated by evacuation and the contrary {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.271}.

## Opposition and good regimen
A disease may call for cooling while its cause calls for heating, as a fever calls for cooling and the obstructions behind it for heating, or the reverse; likewise a disease may call for heating and its symptom for cooling. Not every fullness or bad temper is treated by the contrary of evacuation and opposition, since a sound, attentive regimen is often enough for fullness and bad temper. Strong measures, such as strong purging, incision, cautery and vomiting, are avoided where possible in summer and winter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.271}.
