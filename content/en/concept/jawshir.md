## Nature and selection
A tree whose leaves stand low, like fig leaves, very green, divided and round-cut; its flower is yellow and its scent sweet {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}. Its gum is drawn by splitting the root as the stem first appears; the gum is white and, when dry, its surface goes the colour of saffron {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}. The editor's footnote says it is a tree called the cow's-milk tree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot and dry at the end of the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}. Actions: dissolves wind and softens {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: softens hard swellings; its froth softens pustules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}.
Wounds and ulcers: the root is good for bare bones, and with honey for chronic ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}.
Joints: drunk with the water of the qaratin or with wine for the weakness of muscle from a blow; poultice for sciatica and joint pains and gout. Some say it is bad for the nerve, and that it suits a healthy nerve, not a moist one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.418}.
Head: helps dental erosion when packed in the tooth, and eases its pain; helps headache, epilepsy and the infants' ailment called umm al-subyan {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
Eyes: sharpens the sight when applied as kohl {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
Chest: a poultice of its leaf on pains of the side; helps side pains and cough when they are cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
Food: its juice helps a hard spleen as a poultice or drunk with vinegar, and helps dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
Excretory organs: softens a hard womb, helps urine drip, moves urine and menses, and the book says it kills and expels the foetus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}. These are the book's listings, labelled.
Fevers: drunk with the water of the qaratin for shivering fits and recurring fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
Poisons: with pitch it makes a good ointment and plaster for the bite of a mad dog; with zarawand it is drunk for stings, and its juice likewise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
Substitutes: its substitute is qunna; ushaq is near it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.419}.
