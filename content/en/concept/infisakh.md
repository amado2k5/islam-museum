## Definition
Infisakh is lexically the reflexive of fasakh, meaning undoing and passing away. For the jurists it is the dissolution of a contract either of itself or by the will of the parties or one of them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3520}

## Related terms
- Fasakh: undoing the tie of a contract by the will of one party or both, or by a judge. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3522}
- Iqala: rescinding a contract by the consent of both parties, a voluntary cause of dissolution for binding contracts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3523}
- Intiha': reaching the end of a contract, such as the end of a hire term or the death of a spouse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3521}
- Butlan and fasad: for the Hanafis a void contract is one not lawful in origin or in attribute, and an invalid one is lawful in origin but not in attribute. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3521}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3522}

## Causes
- Voluntary: fasakh, iqala, a judge's decision, and excuses. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3522}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3523}
- Involuntary: the perishing of the object, the death of a party, a change of capacity, a third party's claim (istihqaq), usurpation, and impossibility of performing the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3524}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3531}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3532}

## Perishing of the object
- Immediate contracts such as an outright sale, settlement or gift are not dissolved by the perishing of the object after receipt. Before receipt the views differ: the Malikis hold a sale of measured goods dissolved, while the Hanafis and Shafi'is hold a sale dissolved when the object perishes before receipt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3524}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3525}
- Continuing contracts such as hire, loan and agency are dissolved when the object perishes, whether before or after receipt, and only for the future period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3525}

## Death of a party
- Death does not affect immediate contracts once the transfer is complete; a marriage ends with death by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3526}
- Hire: the majority hold that it is not dissolved by the death of a party; the Hanafis hold it dissolved if the party contracted for himself, since the benefit comes into being little by little. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3526}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3527}
- Non-binding contracts such as loan, agency, partnership and deposit are generally dissolved by the death of a party. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3527}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3528}
- A surety contract is not dissolved by the death of the surety or the principal; a pledge is not dissolved by the death of either party after delivery. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3528}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3529}

## Change of capacity
- Madness, unconsciousness and apostasy dissolve non-binding contracts such as partnership, agency, deposit and loan, by the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3529}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3530}
- Apostasy of a spouse dissolves the marriage by the agreement of the jurists, on the Qur'anic text in sura al-Mumtahana (60:10); the detail differs before and after consummation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3530}

## Istihqaq and usurpation
- Istihqaq is the showing that a thing is the right of another than the seller. For the Hanafis it does not of itself dissolve the sale but suspends it on the consent of the claimant; the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold the sale dissolved. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3531}
- Usurpation of a leased thing: the Hanafis hold the hire dissolved, in the preferred view; the Malikis treat it as the destruction of the object; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis give the hirer an option to dissolve. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3531}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3532}

## Partial dissolution
When the dissolution reaches part of the object, it may reach the whole; this is the question of dividing a deal (tafriq al-safqa), on which the views differ. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3532}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3533}

## Effects
- An immediate contract is undone from its origin, and a continuing one from the moment of dissolution. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3534}
- If the object is unchanged it returns to its owner; if it has changed by increase or decrease, the rules differ. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3534}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3535}
- A loss without fault or neglect falls on the owner, and a loss caused by a party falls on that party; after dissolution the object is held as a trust by the party who is not its owner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3535}
