## Summary
Ibn Sina notes that some drugs suit some temperaments, so scammony acts only weakly in cold countries unless a large dose is used, as is the custom in the Turkic lands, that aromatic drugs are mixed into purgatives to keep the organs' powers, and that something like ginger is combined with a slow drug such as turbith to quicken it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.284}.

## Combining
Some drugs suit some temperaments and others do not: scammony acts only weakly on people of cold countries unless much of it is used, as is its custom in the land of the Turks, and in some countries and bodies it may be needed to use not the bodies of the drugs but their powers. It is necessary to mix aromatic drugs into the purgatives so as to keep the powers of the organs, and fragrant drugs fit well here because they strengthen the animal spirit in every organ. Two drugs may come together, one quick to purge its humour and the other slow, so that the first has finished its action before the second begins; there should then be combined with the slow one something that sets it going quickly, as ginger does for turbith {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.284}.
