## Violent motions
Violent motions are a great inlet in producing lesions of the nerve, beyond what they are in other things, for the nerves are the instruments of motions; violent motions are like stretching by a rope, lifting a heavy thing, and everything with strong extension or compression and constriction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.135}

## Sources of inference
The source of inference for its states is from the acts of sense and motion, from touch in softness and hardness, from the brain's and the spine's sharing with it, and from the pains and materials peculiar to the nerve; most of the signs by which the states of the brain are reached are from impairment of functions and from touch {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.135}

## The moist and the dry
If it is doubtful whether a disease of the nerve is moist or dry, the manner of its onset is considered: if it came suddenly there is no doubt it is moist. The member's drinking up of oil is also considered: if it soaks it up quickly there is no doubt it is dry — provided the member has not been heated by some outside heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.135}
