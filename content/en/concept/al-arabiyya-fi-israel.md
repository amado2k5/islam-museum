## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Israel: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. This page ascribes every statement about law and language to its holder and passes no verdict on the disagreement over it. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Israel", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The summary's sequence is the Palestine Order in Council of 1922, Article 82 of which made Arabic official with Hebrew; the Basic Law of July 2018, which made Hebrew the state's language and gave Arabic a special status; and the survey of 2011-2012 from which the share is taken. The dates are not in dispute in the summary; what the change means it describes in two ways: the law's own wording, which says it does not harm the earlier status, and the description of a constitutional downgrade. This page relays both and does not decide between them. For the wider history see [[concept/tarikh-al-lugha-al-arabiyya|The history of the Arabic language]].

## Legal status
The summary says Arabic was co-official with Hebrew under Article 82 of the Palestine Order in Council of 1922, and that Article 4 of the Basic Law: Israel as the Nation-State of the Jewish People, of July 2018, declares Hebrew the state's language, assigns Arabic a special status, and states that this does not harm the status previously given to Arabic {cite:israel-law-review-nation-state-law-and-arabic}. The summary describes this as a constitutional downgrade, which is its own description; the title of the academic article it relies on asks the same question, whether the law downgrades, replicates or upgrades. The communication of UN Special Rapporteurs, published by Adalah, records an objection to the law {cite:adalah-un-special-rapporteurs-communication-2018}. According to the summary, earlier statutory and judicial protections, such as bilingual signage, Arabic-medium public schools and government services, remain in force {cite:cfr-what-to-know-about-arab-citizens-of-israel}. These statements are ascribed to their holders as the summary relayed them; the museum did not read the law, the article or the communication.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names Palestinian Arabic, with its South Levantine urban and rural dialects; Negev and Galilee Bedouin Arabic; Druze Arabic; and Judeo-Arabic among older first-generation Mizrahi and Maghrebi immigrants {cite:wikipedia-palestinian-arabic}. Wikipedia supports the names and places here; the descriptions are ascribed to the summary. For the same speech outside this frame see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-palestine|Arabic in Palestine]] and [[concept/lahjat-al-sham|The dialect of the Levant]]; for the place [[place/palestine|Palestine]].

## Official figures
From the Social Survey 2011-2012 of the Israel Central Bureau of Statistics (CBS), covering residents aged 20 and over, the summary reports that 18 percent of all residents of that age gave Arabic as their mother tongue, including 3 percent of Jewish residents of the same age {cite:cbs-israel-social-survey-2011-2012}. It found no all-ages total of speakers of Arabic from the CBS, the UN or UNESCO. The museum gives the figure as the summary relayed it, with its age bound and survey years, and does not extend it to others.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: UN Special Rapporteurs communication of 2 November 2018 (hosted by Adalah) {cite:adalah-un-special-rapporteurs-communication-2018} and Social Survey 2011-2012 {cite:cbs-israel-social-survey-2011-2012}; Reference, academic and press pages: Nation-State Law and the Arabic Language in Israel: Downgrading, Replicating or Upgrading? {cite:israel-law-review-nation-state-law-and-arabic} and What to know about Arab citizens of Israel {cite:cfr-what-to-know-about-arab-citizens-of-israel}; Wikipedia pages: Palestinian Arabic {cite:wikipedia-palestinian-arabic}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
