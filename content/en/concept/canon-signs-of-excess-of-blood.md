## Summary
In the seventh chapter Ibn Sina begins the signs of a dominant humour with blood, whose signs are close to those of fullness of the vessels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.164-165}.

## The signs
There is heaviness in the body, especially at the roots of the eyes, in the head and the temples; stretching, yawning and clinging drowsiness; dulled senses and weariness without prior effort; an unaccustomed sweetness in the mouth and a red tongue; boils may appear on the body and blisters in the mouth, and blood flows from places that split easily, such as the nostril, the anus and the gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.164-165}.

## Supporting indications
Temperament, earlier regimen, country, age, habit and a long time since the last venesection also point to it, as do dreams of red things and of much blood flowing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.165}.
