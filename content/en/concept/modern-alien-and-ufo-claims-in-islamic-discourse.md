## A note on what this page is

This page is about a modern discourse. "Extraterrestrials" and "UFOs" are contemporary ways of putting the question, and the classical sources do not pose it in this form, although they do speak of creatures in the heavens and of many worlds; that material is treated on the page [[concept/life-beyond-earth-in-the-sources|Life beyond the earth in the sources]]. What follows are the views of named contemporary writers or platforms; they are not a ruling or a fatwa in the museum's name. After searching the fatwa sites we found no published fatwa on extraterrestrials or UFOs that we could verify at IslamWeb, IslamQA or Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, so none is reported here.

## The verses contemporary writers turn to

Modern discussion usually returns to [[passage/quran-42-29|al-Shura 29]], which says the creation of the heavens and earth is a sign, that God has scattered creatures in them and that He is able to gather them; to [[passage/quran-16-8|al-Nahl 8]], which says God creates what people do not know; and to [[passage/quran-1-2|al-Fatiha 2]], which describes God as lord of the worlds, a theme of the page [[concept/alamin-the-worlds|The worlds]].

## Three positions that were read

**First: revelation neither affirms nor denies.** Spahic Omer wrote an essay, "Islam on Aliens", on the news site of the International Islamic University Malaysia, dated 26 October 2020. He holds that Islam neither affirms nor denies life beyond the earth, that this silence signals the knowledge is not needed, that preoccupation with it distracts from responsibility on earth, and that what the Qur'an says of the Lord of the worlds and of creation people do not know leaves room for worlds we do not know; but he holds revelation to be the only reliable source, notes that angels and jinn inhabit the heavens and earth, that the word for the creatures scattered could be read broadly, and judges alien civilisations rivalling humanity extremely unlikely because humans are God's vicegerents {cite:spahic-omer-islam-on-aliens-2020}. It is an author's opinion essay, not a fatwa or a ruling of the university, and this summary comes from a tool's reading of the page and should be checked against the full text.

**Second: nothing in the Shari'a forbids other creatures, and stories of their landing are myths.** On a question-and-answer page of IslamOnline, "Are there extraterrestrials who descend to the earth?", dated 22 May 2024 by the page, the view of Prof. Abd al-Fattah Idris of al-Azhar is relayed that nothing in the Shari'a prevents other creatures existing in the universe, that God made the earth a dwelling for Adam's offspring, that stories of beings coming down to it are myths, and that no contact has been established if they exist {cite:islamonline-extraterrestrial-visits-2024}. The page attributes the excerpt to the site's AI tool and does not call itself a fatwa, so the attribution of the view to a named person should be rechecked.

**Third: a matter for reason, not for creed.** In an article on the same site, "Life on other planets", dated 22 May 2024 by the page and credited by its page data to Muhammad Abd al-Subhan, the writer says that research on life on other planets is left to human reason, that Islam encourages looking into the dominion of the heavens and earth, that he relays from al-Nasafi that attaching the creature to the heavens and earth together may be attaching a thing to the whole though it is in a part, that if living beings are found in the sky there is no conflict with the verse and if not the verse's meaning stands, that it is not a creed for which one is held to account, and he warns against hurrying to tie the Qur'an to scientific discoveries {cite:islamonline-life-on-other-planets-2024}.

## Academic studies

Jörg Matthias Determann published a book on the culture of astrobiology in the Muslim world, Islam, Science Fiction and Extraterrestrial Life (I.B. Tauris, 2021), reviewed by Merve Tabur in the International Journal of Middle East Studies, vol. 56, no. 4, November 2024, pp. 686-688 {cite:determann-islam-science-fiction-extraterrestrial-life-2021}. A 2024 edited volume, Islamic Theology and Extraterrestrial Life, was edited by Shoaib Ahmed Malik and Jörg Matthias Determann {cite:malik-determann-islamic-theology-extraterrestrial-life-2024}. Only the existence and details of these two works were verified; neither the books nor the review were read, so no view is attributed to them here.

## What was not verified

No published fatwa from a named fatwa body on extraterrestrials or UFOs could be verified, and no classified position on the large fatwa sites was found. What was read is three texts by writers and platforms, two of them dated the same day on one site; it is not a survey of Islamic discourse as a whole. This page does not say that extraterrestrials exist or that they do not; it reports what these writers said.
