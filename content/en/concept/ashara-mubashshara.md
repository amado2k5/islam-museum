## Summary
Al-Tahawi says: "The ten whom the Messenger of God named and gave glad tidings of Paradise — we testify Paradise for them, as the Messenger of God testified for them, and his word is truth" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 29}; he names them Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd, Sa'id, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, describing Abu Ubayda as "the trustee of this umma" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 30}.

## Note
Of the generality of good-doing believers al-Tahawi says: "we do not feel secure for them, nor do we testify Paradise for them" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 21}; the testimony for the ten he builds on the Messenger of God's own testimony {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 29}.

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama says: "We testify Paradise for the ten, as the Prophet testified for them", then names them: Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd, Sa'id, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 38}. Then: "Whoever the Prophet testified Paradise for, we testify it for him" — mentioning al-Hasan, al-Husayn and Thabit ibn Qays {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 39}.

## In Ibn Taymiyya's al-'Aqida al-Wasitiyya
Ibn Taymiyya says: "They testify Paradise for whoever the Messenger of God testified it for — the ten, Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, and others of the Companions" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 24}. Before that he mentions that they believe God said to the people of Badr: "Do as you will, for I have forgiven you", and that "no one who pledged under the tree enters the Fire", as the Prophet reported {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 24}.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the positions of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya, Ibn Qudama's Lum'at al-I'tiqad and Ibn Taymiyya's al-Wasitiyya — each attributed to its book. ## Encyclopaedia of Islam: the lists
The term al-'Ashara al-Mubashshara, "the ten to whom Paradise was promised", does not occur in the canonical hadith, although the conception goes back to it. The traditions usually have the form "Ten will be in Paradise", followed by the names, and the lists differ. The names found in the various forms are Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Sa'id ibn Zayd. In some traditions Muhammad himself is put before these nine (Abu Dawud, Sunna; Ahmad ibn Hanbal); in others Muhammad is absent and the tenth place goes to Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah (Tirmidhi, Manaqib; Ibn Sa'd; Ahmad ibn Hanbal). The entry holds that such conceptions owe their origin to the hierarchic tendencies prominent in the Muslim community, which found expression even in its earliest creeds. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 742}

## In Mosaad Anwar's series 'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise'
In the second episode of [[work/the-ten-promised-paradise-mosaad-anwar|'Those Given Glad Tidings of Paradise']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] dwells on the Companions in general before giving each of those promised Paradise an episode of their own, and he states that this is the second episode of the series {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:01:00}. He says that those promised Paradise are hundreds of Companions, and he describes [[group/sahaba|the Companions]] as the best generation humanity has known, chosen by God to keep His Prophet company, among whom He placed the Prophet's ministers, helpers and in-laws {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:01:30}. He explains that his programme will not cover all the Companions but those among them promised Paradise, whom he calls "the masters of the Companions, and all of them are masters" {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:22:00}. In his view each Companion is a nation in himself, and he names al-Faruq, al-Siddiq, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Ibn Awf, Bilal and Hudhayfa {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:02:00}; they gave their blood and wealth, he says, so that Islam would reach those after them easily {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:02:30}.

He says the verse praising men who kept their covenant with God was revealed about the Companions, and especially about the martyrs of [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], among whom he counts [[person/hamza|Hamza]], [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], whom he calls "the first envoy of Islam", al-Yaman the father of Hudhayfa, Abdullah ibn Haram the father of [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir]], [[person/sad-ibn-al-rabi|Sa'd ibn al-Rabi']], [[person/anas-ibn-al-nadr|Anas ibn al-Nadr]] and Abdullah ibn Jahsh, and he says the martyrs of Uhud were seventy {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:03:30}. He cites the verse on men whom neither trade nor selling distracts from remembering God, and he distinguishes maleness from manliness: not every male, for him, is a man, since manliness is a set of qualities {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:04:30}. He states that the first to be addressed by the two verses describing the community as the middle nation and as the best of nations were the Companions {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:05:30}, and he cites the verse of God's good pleasure with the believers who pledged under the tree at [[event/bayat-al-ridwan|the Pledge of Ridwan]] and the verse on the foremost of the [[group/muhajirun|Muhajirun]] and the [[group/ansar|Ansar]] as proof of God's pleasure with them {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:06:30}, and the verse describing the companions of Muhammad as firm against the disbelievers and merciful among themselves, which mentions their likeness in the Torah and the Gospel {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:07:30}.

He holds that no prophet ever had a companion like Abu Bakr, or like Umar, Uthman, Ali, Hamza or Mus'ab, and that the Companions are the best of humankind after the prophets {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:08:30}. He relates from the two Sahihs, through [[person/imran-ibn-husayn|Imran ibn Husayn]], the hadith that makes the best of people the Prophet's generation and then those who follow them, with Imran unsure whether he named two generations after it or three, and then tells of people coming after them who testify without being asked, betray trust, break their vows and grow fat {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:09:30}; he jokes that the fatness points to a taste for rich dishes such as fatta, kabsa and mandi {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:11:00}. He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari, through [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]], the prohibition of reviling the Companions, and that were one of us to spend the like of Mount Uhud in gold it would not reach a handful spent by one of them, nor half of it {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:11:30}; and he illustrates it: were you to give a hundred thousand tonnes of gold to the poor, and Bilal, Ibn Mas'ud, Ammar or Mus'ab then gave a handful of dates or half of one, his reward would be greater {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:13:30}. He contrasts the Companions who were tortured, flogged and killed for Islam with those born Muslim, and holds that their reward cannot be matched even by one who prays all night and fasts for ever {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:14:00}.

He relates from Sahih Muslim, through Abu Burda, the hadith that makes the stars a safeguard for the sky, the Prophet a safeguard for his Companions and his Companions a safeguard for his community {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:18:00}. He states that the Companions' testimony is accepted because they are upright, neither lying nor flattering, and relates from the two Sahihs, through [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]], the account of two funerals, one praised by the people and the other blamed: the Prophet said the matter was settled for each, then explained to Umar that it meant Paradise for the first and the Fire for the second, and that they were God's witnesses on earth {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:19:00}. He relates from Sahih al-Bukhari, through Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, that bands of fighters will be asked whether there is among them anyone who kept the Prophet's company, and victory will be granted them, then whether there is anyone who kept his Companions' company, then anyone who kept company with those, and he concludes that the presence of the Companions was among the greatest causes of victory {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:20:30}. He lists what he sees as a Muslim's duties towards the Companions: invoking God's pleasure on them, praying for them, loving those who love them, studying their lives, telling their stories to children, taking them as models and naming children after them {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep02 00:22:30}.

In the fifth episode he gives the hadith of the Ten as narrated by [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]] himself, counting in Paradise [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]], [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]], [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]], [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]], [[person/abu-ubayda-ibn-al-jarrah|Abu Ubayda]], [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr]], [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubayd Allah]], [[person/saad-ibn-abi-waqqas|Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas]], [[person/said-ibn-zayd|Sa'id ibn Zayd]] and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:02:30}, and he says that six of the Ten embraced Islam through Abu Bakr within a single week {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:03:30}. He relates from Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, graded hasan sahih as he says, through Sa'id ibn Zayd, that the Prophet climbed Hira with Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd and Ibn Awf, struck the mountain with his foot and bade it be still, for on it stood only a prophet, a siddiq or a martyr {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep05 00:11:00}. In the sixth episode he divides the people of Paradise into two kinds: those testified to by their qualities, such as the truthful believers who pray with humility and fear God, and those testified to by name because a text has come about them, such as the Ten and others {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:05:00}; and he holds that no one is declared bound for Paradise or the Fire without a sound and explicit text, so that for whoever dies a Muslim, however righteous, one hopes without being certain {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar the-ten-promised-paradise/ep06 00:07:00}.
