## Summary
Al-Tahawi says: "The ten whom the Messenger of God named and gave glad tidings of Paradise — we testify Paradise for them, as the Messenger of God testified for them, and his word is truth" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 29}; he names them Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd, Sa'id, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, describing Abu Ubayda as "the trustee of this umma" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 30}.

## Note
Of the generality of good-doing believers al-Tahawi says: "we do not feel secure for them, nor do we testify Paradise for them" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 21}; the testimony for the ten he builds on the Messenger of God's own testimony {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 29}.

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama says: "We testify Paradise for the ten, as the Prophet testified for them", then names them: Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Sa'd, Sa'id, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 38}. Then: "Whoever the Prophet testified Paradise for, we testify it for him" — mentioning al-Hasan, al-Husayn and Thabit ibn Qays {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 39}.

## In Ibn Taymiyya's al-'Aqida al-Wasitiyya
Ibn Taymiyya says: "They testify Paradise for whoever the Messenger of God testified it for — the ten, Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, and others of the Companions" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 24}. Before that he mentions that they believe God said to the people of Badr: "Do as you will, for I have forgiven you", and that "no one who pledged under the tree enters the Fire", as the Prophet reported {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 24}.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the positions of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya, Ibn Qudama's Lum'at al-I'tiqad and Ibn Taymiyya's al-Wasitiyya — each attributed to its book. The sayings of the Ash'aris, Maturidis and the remaining Sunni and non-Sunni schools from their own books have not yet been added; the entry needs specialist review before promotion.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam: the lists
The term al-'Ashara al-Mubashshara, "the ten to whom Paradise was promised", does not occur in the canonical hadith, although the conception goes back to it. The traditions usually have the form "Ten will be in Paradise", followed by the names, and the lists differ. The names found in the various forms are Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha, al-Zubayr, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Sa'id ibn Zayd. In some traditions Muhammad himself is put before these nine (Abu Dawud, Sunna; Ahmad ibn Hanbal); in others Muhammad is absent and the tenth place goes to Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah (Tirmidhi, Manaqib; Ibn Sa'd; Ahmad ibn Hanbal). The entry holds that such conceptions owe their origin to the hierarchic tendencies prominent in the Muslim community, which found expression even in its earliest creeds. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 742}
