## Summary
In chapters 16 and 17 Ibn Sina divides the causes of solution of continuity into internal and external, and says the cause of an ulcer is a tumour that bursts, a wound that opens or pustules that erode {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.146-147}.

## Internal causes
Internal causes include an eroding or burning humour, or a moistening one that relaxes, or a drying one that splits; or windy fullness that stretches or pierces; or a humoral one that stretches by its movement, or one that passes through the body because a strong movement separates it, or one that pierces. All of these arise from violence of movement or abundance of matter, such as a violent movement of the expelling power off the natural course, or movement on fullness. Similar are loud shouting, jumping and the bursting of tumours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.146-147}.

## External causes
External causes are a body that stretches, like a rope or weights; one that cuts, like a sword; one that burns, like fire; one that crushes, like a stone, which, finding emptiness, smashes, or finding fullness, splits the vessels; a body that bores, like an arrow; or one that tears and bites, like a rabid dog, a viper or a human {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.

## Ulcer
The causes of an ulcer are a tumour that bursts, a wound that opens, or pustules that erode {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.147}.
