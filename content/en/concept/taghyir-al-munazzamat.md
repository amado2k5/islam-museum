## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan distils the principles of organizational change into a thirty-question exercise he composed from the conclusions of more than thirty leading management scholars — human estimates that prevail today, not absolutes {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:23:30}.
The key difference between planning and change: planning asks where we are, where we want to go and how to get there; change adds a fourth — what is the expected resistance and how do we overcome it, 'resisting the resistance' — so planning is a part of change {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:29:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:30:30}.

## When and how
Change is required even in a successful organisation meeting its goals, because no organisation lives in a vacuum while its competitors move {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:15:00}.
Change has two drivers, not one: treating and pre-empting dangers, and developing advantages; his example is Sony releasing a replacement for its own product about every three weeks before it 'burns out' in the market — change with no visible danger, to preserve the gap over competitors {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:21:30}.
The prevailing opinion he relays: change everything at once rather than axis by axis, because the parts of the work are interlinked; and a big, sudden change is more effective than a gradual one because two thirds of change efforts fail through internal resistance, and gradualness lets the resistance organise (needs_source: the figure as he gave it) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:26:00}.
Any major error in a step of change may destroy the whole process, so it is planned deeply {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:31:00}.

## The consultants' eight questions
In diagnosing an organisation before change, al-Suwaidan gives the eight questions consultants put — in interviews with fifteen to twenty-five of the leadership tiers, not the staff: What are your most important problems? Who caused them? Who are their parties? Why did they begin? What are your proposals for solution? How could you develop beyond solving problems? What are the main services or products you could add? And what can you learn from your competitors? — saying the science of consulting lies in how and when to ask and how to rephrase {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep05 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep05 00:04:30}.

## Resisting the resistance
Early results are announced even when small — a two per cent gain declared breaks the resistance's argument {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:35:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:36:00}.
Change is not an end in itself regardless of cost: unless it yields clearer output, impact and profit in the end, it serves nothing {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:37:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:38:00}.
Having made a big change does not bar another months later if an unusual opportunity or technology arrives — he recounts that his academy changed its structure six times in three years as results outgrew the plan {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:40:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:41:30}.
Most failure comes from the leadership, not the ranks: when change is serious, the senior leaders are re-evaluated, not the small sections {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:15:00}.
The only effective change is the one focused on changing people — by replacement or by changing mindsets and skills; anything else is short-lived {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:18:00}.
Changing mindsets is the biggest obstacle to change, and he faults training courses for drilling information and skills rather than mindsets {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:11:30}.

## Human nature during change
He states constants he holds unchangeable, to be built on rather than against: employees measure change by the leaders' words and deeds, not the plans — citing that even the Ansar resented the division of the spoils of Hunayn though raised by the Prophet, when Sa'd ibn Ubadah said 'I am but a man among my people' — and that most people put their own interest before the common good, so 'what is in it for me' is computed for everyone affected {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:00:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:04:00}.
Change is painful by nature — losses, tension, jobs shed — and no change-minded leader has gone unharmed {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:11:00}.
The feelings of those affected are honoured to soften resistance — a senior is let go in two steps, adviser then out — and he attributes to senior scholars the criticism that Ali ibn Abi Talib dismissed all of Uthman's governors at once, provoking a total resistance (transmitted from scholars, labelled) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:17:00}.
A heterogeneous group is better at proposing a change project since it yields varied views, while a homogeneous one is better at execution — the best shura council is diverse, the best government homogeneous (his view) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:20:00}.
The elderly are usually an obstacle to change — he estimates only two per cent of them stay creative (needs_source) — and an organisation that grew big fails if it keeps the methods of its smallness {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:13:30}.
The easiest route to radical change is replacing the first person with one of a different management philosophy — for better or worse — though he does not call it the best {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:08:00}.
He faults the Arab mindset for awaiting a 'saving hero' — describing a cartoon of an army ready to the horizon led by a riderless horse captioned 'when will Saladin come?' — a nation that is ready produces its hero; the problem is not a missing person (rant) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:33:30}.

## Quick change versus effective change
Through the 'pharmaceuticals company' exercise he distinguishes two situations: when the new manager must show results within a year or be dismissed, fast-acting fixes take precedence over deep slow ones — restructuring before quality, price-inelastic expensive products, new markets free of competition, spending more rather than cutting, changing the top leaders, the operational plan not the strategic, and reliance on the current second tier of leaders — who know the work best and had been suppressed by the dismissed manager — over outside consultants; when there is no pressure, the long effective-change route is taken {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:28:00}.
He excepts actual collapse: when the company is losing and its reputation is hurt, recruiting from outside beats promoting from inside {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 02:30:00}.
He states that the maximum feasible cost cut without hitting people, quality or marketing is ten per cent, which lifts profits by no more than three per cent, while revenue growth has no ceiling; and he tells of a company down eighty per cent of its capital that a new manager turned around in a year — borrowing to raise salaries and tripling the marketing budget — to a three-hundred-per-cent profit (the speaker's eyewitness account, needs_source) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:53:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:54:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep04 01:55:30}.

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders": a test of twenty-one questions
In two lessons of [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]] al-Suwaidan teaches change in organisations as a true-or-false test of twenty-one questions. He says it sums up two books, one called "Change" and the other "Mastering Change". He refers the trainees to his own book "The Methodology of Change in Organisations", which ends with a reading list. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:09:00} He calls this a human science in which there is no right and wrong. Where scholars disagree, he says, he chose the large majority view. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:10:00} His answers include these:
- A successful organisation still needs change, because the conditions its plans assumed keep moving. He says this point is agreed by all. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:13:00}
- Change is not only problem-solving: it treats weaknesses and develops strengths. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:17:00}
- Sudden change, which in organisations means three to four months, is better than staged change over three to four years, because it gives resistance no time to organise. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:23:30}
- Even with consultants and careful plans, he says, about two-thirds of planned change in American companies fails. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:21:30}
- Planning has three elements: the present, the vision and the route. Change adds a fourth, resistance and how to overcome it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:25:00}
- Even the most capable managers make fatal mistakes, so the idea of the "hero leader and sole chief" should be dropped. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:28:00}
- A new manager should not begin by changing structures and people. He should change people one at a time, because some who seemed weak had been held back by the manager before. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:43:30}
- People judge change by what leaders do and by what is in it for them, and neither can be trained away. He illustrates this with the spoils of [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Hunayn]] and the grievance of the [[group/ansar|Ansar]], whom the Prophet won back. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:49:00}
- Great opportunities come with great risks. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep15 00:55:00}

The second half of the test continues:
- Long-term change rests on creativity, short-term change on improving what exists. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:07:00}
- Replacing the person at the top with someone of a different management philosophy is the easiest way to make a big change, though not necessarily the best. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:10:30}
- No change is possible unless the senior people change, either their persons or their thinking. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:13:00}
- "Change is not for the faint-hearted". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:16:00}
- Changing mindsets is the greatest obstacle. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:17:00}
- Heirs who keep their father's vision without criticising it risk failure. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:23:00}
- A mixed group plans and changes better, and a like-minded group executes better. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:33:30}
- Older people, with few exceptions, are an obstacle to change. He says only two per cent of them stay flexible and creative. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:37:30}
He then gives a second test, "Puzzling Decisions", on short-term change. He separates it from the first test, which concerned long-term change. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:45:00} One of its rules: cutting costs by more than about ten per cent harms quality, service, marketing or morale. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep16 00:51:00}
