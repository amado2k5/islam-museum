## Summary
Hamw lexically is the father of a husband or his brother, or any relative of the husband's side; hama is the husband's mother, and the term runs both ways like in-law. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7329} A mahram in-law, such as the husband's father or son, may look at the woman and be alone with her, on the Quranic permission and the ban on a man spending the night with a married woman unless he is a husband or a mahram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7329} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7330} A non-mahram in-law, such as the husband's brother, has the status of a stranger in looking, being alone, residing and hearing the voice; if looking is forbidden, touching is forbidden too. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7330} The saying 'the in-law is death' is read as applying to non-mahram in-laws and as a strong warning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7330} The entry refers to the headwords stranger, mahram, hamala and kafala. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7330}
