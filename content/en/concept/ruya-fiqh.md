## Summary
A dream (ru'ya) is what a person sees in sleep; it is distinct from inspiration (ilham), from a bad dream (hulm), from a passing thought (khatir) and from revelation (wahy). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8531} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8532}

## General ruling
A sound dream is one of the parts of prophethood. It is more often true for people of righteousness, while for others a dream mixes truth with confused fancies. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8532} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8533}

Seeing the Prophet in a dream is true. Whether his words or acts seen there bind the dreamer is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8534} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8535}

## Where it is discussed
Interpretation (ta'bir) carries a dream from its outward form to its inward meaning, and its principles are drawn from the Qur'an and the Sunna. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8536} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8537}

It is recommended that a dream be told only to a sympathetic or learned person. A dream one likes is met with praise to God and told to others; a disliked one is answered by seeking refuge from its harm and not telling it to anyone. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8538} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8539}

## Rulings touching the dream in fiqh
No legal ruling is built on a dream: if a person sees in sleep what would entail a hadd, a divorce, a vow or an expiation, nothing attaches until the cause exists in waking {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}. What is built on a dream is glad tidings and reassurance, not obligation.

Among the dream's fiqh appendages is [[concept/ihtilam|ihtilam]]: whoever finds in his garment a trace of ihtilam and remembers nothing he saw in sleep owes ghusl from his most recent sleep, and Umar repeated what he prayed after his last sleep but not what preceded it {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.95} [[passage/muwatta-1-126|Muwatta 126]]. Of the woman who sees in sleep what a man sees the report came: "Yes, when she sees the water" {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.96} [[passage/muwatta-1-127|Muwatta 127]] [[passage/muwatta-1-128|Muwatta 128]].

In the chapter of glad tidings fiqh binds the righteous dream to the [[concept/mubashshirat|mubashshirat]] that remain of prophethood after revelation ceased {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.546} [[passage/muwatta-31-2749|Muwatta 2749]], and to interpretation and [[concept/taabir-al-ruya|the science of interpretation]] that God gave Joseph {cite:tanzil-quran}. This chapter connects to [[concept/ahlam|dreams]], [[concept/al-hulm|the bad dream]] and [[concept/kitab-al-ruya|Kitab al-Ru'ya]] in the collections.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 8)
The Sheikh says that when the soul is released in sleep with no barriers, it sees beautiful visions and dreams: it sees a late relative, the Prophet, the Companions and the righteous, and the door of good and transparency opens for it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:28:30}. He gives the example that a person may see his brother living in America and his sister living in South Africa while in his room with his wife beside him who sees nothing, and he says this belongs to the picture of sleep (his view) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:28:30}.

He tells of a man who said his longing for the Messenger of God grew strong one night in the time of Umar, so he asked God to let him see him, slept, and saw him (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:31:00}. He adds that the man saw a tray of fresh dates in the Prophet's hand, who gave him one, which he ate and found sweet, then gave him a second as he woke; the man swore that after waking he still found the sweetness of the dates in his mouth (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:31:30}. The Sheikh describes this as a spiritual, luminous telepathy, and says a person does not always see a plain dream {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:31:30}.

The Sheikh then tells a personal story about the world of spirits concerning his late sheikh: they were sitting in a group when the sheikh said something he did not accept, and he says he was rebellious at the time, it seems {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:33:30}. After he had stayed away from visiting him, he sat one night at his desk at home, fell asleep at the desk, and saw his sheikh come in through the study door; he woke, took it as a sign that he should visit him, prayed the dawn prayer and went (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:34:00}.

## In Ahmad Farid's lessons 'The Boat'
In [[work/the-boat-ahmad-farid|'The Boat']] [[person/ahmad-farid|Ahmad Farid]] counts the good dream among the glad tidings given to believers in this world. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep06 00:02:00} He recalls the hadith that nothing of prophethood remains but the glad tidings, explained as the good dream that a righteous man sees or that is seen for him, and the hadith that the good dream is one of forty-six parts of prophethood. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep06 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep06 00:02:30} Prophethood has ended, he explains, and a part of it remains; but the remaining of a part is not the remaining of prophethood. {cite:assabile-ahmad-farid the-boat/ep06 00:02:30}
