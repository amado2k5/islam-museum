## Nature
Three kinds: sabur-qan, which is natural steel; barmahn; and wrought steel made from barmahn. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.491}
The tutiya of sabur-qan is close to the tutiya of copper. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.491}

## Actions
Its rust is binding and biting; its dross is weaker than its rust, and it is the strongest of all dross in drying. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.491}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: its rust with wine on the dahs patch, a skin patch the book names. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.491}
Swellings and pustules: iron rust with wine on carbuncle and pustules. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.491}
Joints: its rust with wine helps gout. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.491}
Organs of the head: crushed in strong vinegar and boiled in it, that vinegar helps chronic pus flowing from the ear. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Organs of the eye: iron rust is good for roughness of the eyelids and the growth the book calls al-zufra. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Organs of digestion: wine and water in which iron has been quenched help swelling of the spleen and slackness and weakness of the stomach. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Excretory organs: its tutiya has a weak strength of purging water; its rust is a bearable binder; wine in which iron is quenched stops chronic diarrhoea and dysentery, helps relaxed anus and dries piles. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
Labelled listing, set aside for the founder's ruling: the book says its rust stops bleeding from the womb and menstrual bleeding, and that wine in which iron is quenched strengthens sexual power. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.492}
