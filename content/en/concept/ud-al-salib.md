## Nature
Dioscorides claimed that some people call ud al-salib the plant with fingers and others alaysa, meaning in Arabic sweet of scent; it is a plant with a stem from which many branches spread, the leaf of the male like the oak leaf and that of the female like the leaf of the smyrnium, toothed; at the tip of the stem are husks like the husks of the almond, and when they open there appear many small grains red as blood like pomegranate grains, and between those grains others black inclining to purple. The root of the male is as thick as a finger, white, of astringent taste, and the root of the female has branches like the oak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.623}

## Organs of the head
Its seeds, drunk with the water of qaratin, benefit nightmare {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.623}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Eating it as it is benefits burning of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.623}

## Excretory organs
Drunk with wine it benefits pain of the belly, the kidneys and the bladder, and jaundice; cooked in wine and drunk it restrains the bowels; and if children eat or drink it, it takes away the beginning of stone from them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.623}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists its root as given to women whose bodies have not been cleansed of the surplus of the menses after childbirth, benefiting them by promoting it; as benefiting, with wine, pain of the womb; as its red seed, with dark astringent wine, stopping the flow of blood from the womb; and as benefiting, with honeyed wine, the suffocation that comes from pain of the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.623}
