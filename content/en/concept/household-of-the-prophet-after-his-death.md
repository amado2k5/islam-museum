## Summary
This page takes up what the books say of the wives of the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] after his death: their upkeep, their request for an inheritance, their houses around the mosque, and then what they say of two of them. In the pages I read I found no lists of the estates or bequests of the Mothers of the Believers, so I limited myself to what is stated and noted that this part needs other sources. For what the Prophet himself left, see [[concept/estate-of-the-prophet|What the Prophet left at his death]].

## Their upkeep in his life
Ibn Hisham reports that the Prophet divided the Katiba, which is part of Khaybar, among his kin, his wives and men and women of the Muslims, and that his wives had seven hundred wasq of it. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 521} Ibn Kathir says he gave each of his wives eighty wasq of dates and twenty of barley every year. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 640} Fath al-Bari reports that he spent on his family from the property of Banu al-Nadir a year's upkeep, and divided Khaybar and set a part for his family's upkeep. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 238} 'A'isha says that when he died there was nothing in her house a living creature could eat except half a load of barley on a shelf. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3401}

## Their upkeep after him
Abu Hurayra reports that the Prophet said: my heirs shall not divide a dinar; what I leave, after the upkeep of my wives and the cost of my agent, is charity. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 312} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 313} Al-Bukhari puts it in the chapter on the upkeep of the Prophet's wives after his death. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 241} 'A'isha reports that when he died his wives wanted to send 'Uthman to Abu Bakr to ask for their inheritance, and she said to them: did the Messenger of God not say we are not inherited, what we leave is charity? {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 313} Ibn Kathir comments that here is one of the women who would have been heirs, had there been an inheritance, admitting that the Prophet made what he left charity and not an estate, and that the rest of the Mothers of the Believers apparently agreed with her. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 313} Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi report that Abu Bakr told Fatima he had heard the Prophet say a prophet is not inherited, but that he would support whom the Messenger of God supported and spend on whom he spent on. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 317} For the lands that were the source of their upkeep, see [[concept/charitable-lands-of-the-prophet|the Prophet's charitable lands]].

## Their houses
Al-Salihi says the Prophet's houses were nine and that they were around the mosque, and that 'Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-Hudhali saw the houses of his wives when 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz pulled them down on al-Walid's order. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2005} 'Ata' al-Khurasani says that the reading of al-Walid's letter ordering the demolition was a day with more weeping than any he had seen, and that Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab wished they had been left. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2006} For more, see [[building/al-hujra-al-nabawiyya|the Prophet's chamber]]. Al-Salihi also mentions Umm Salama's room, built of mudbrick after the Dumat al-Jandal expedition, and that when the Prophet saw it he told her that the worst thing a Muslim's wealth goes into is building. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 2006}

## What the wives reported of his things
'A'isha is asked what the Prophet's bed was in her house and says it was leather stuffed with palm fibre; Hafsa is asked and says it was a coarse cloth they folded in two for him to sleep on, and that one night they folded it in four to make it softer, and he said: put it back as it was, for its softness kept me from my prayer last night. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 446}

## Zaynab bint Jahsh
Ibn Kathir says Zaynab bint Jahsh was the first of the Mothers of the Believers to die, that she was skilled at crafts, worked with her hands and gave to the poor, and that 'A'isha said she had never seen a woman better in religion, more fearful of God, more truthful, closer to her kin, or greater in trustworthiness and charity. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 105} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 106} He reports that 'Umar sent her an allowance of twelve thousand, which she gave to her relatives, then said: O God, let not 'Umar's gift reach me after this; she died in the year 20, 'Umar prayed over her, she was the first for whom a bier was made, and she was buried at al-Baqi'. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 106} Al-Salihi reports that 'Umar said four takbirs over her and sent to the Prophet's wives asking who should put her in her grave; they said those who used to go in to her in her life; he said the Messenger of God used to say that the quickest of you to join me is the longest in hand, and they used to measure their arms, though it was because she worked with her hands and gave from it in the way of God. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 7905}

## Sawda bint Zam'a
Ibn Kathir says Sawda was the first the Prophet consummated a marriage with after Khadija, that she fasted and prayed much, that when she grew old she gave her day to 'A'isha, and that she died in the caliphate of 'Umar. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 144} In Subul al-Huda, Ahmad reports from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet told his wives in the year of the Farewell Pilgrimage: this, then keep to the mats, and all of them used to make the pilgrimage except Zaynab and Sawda, who said that by God no animal would move them after they had heard that from the Messenger of God. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 7898} Her death is disputed: the well-known view is that she died at the end of 'Umar's caliphate; Ibn Sa'd quotes al-Waqidi that she died in 54 in Mu'awiya's caliphate; al-Baladhuri gives 23 and says 'Umar prayed over her; and it is said that she died in 'Uthman's time. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 7898}

## Note
The pages I read give no estate of any Mother of the Believers and no bequests of theirs; this part needs other sources, such as the Tabaqat books, which are not available in scanned form here.
