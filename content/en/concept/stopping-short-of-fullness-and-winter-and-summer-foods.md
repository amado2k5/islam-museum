## Summary
Ibn Sina says light movement after eating is fitting while heavy emotions and heavy bodily exertion prevent digestion; in winter one eats the more nourishing grains, in summer the opposite; and one should stop while some appetite is left, keeping to habit, since the worst eating is what burdens the stomach and the worst drinking what exceeds moderation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.

## Movement after a meal
Light movement after food, in proportion to what is in the stomach, is fitting, while heavy emotional states and heavy bodily movements prevent digestion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.

## Winter and summer
In winter one should not eat foods of little nourishment such as pulses but those more nourishing and more compact, the grains; in summer the opposite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.

## Stopping before fullness
One should not fill so that no room is left for the residue but hold back with some of the appetite remaining, since that remnant of hunger's demand fades after an hour; and the course of habit is kept, for the worst eating is what weighs on the stomach and the worst drinking what passes moderation and floats in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.229}.
