## Summary
[[work/aqida-tahawiyya|Al-Tahawi]] opens his statement of the creed of the people of the Sunna and the Community by saying: "We say of God's oneness, believing by God's guidance", then enumerates the articles: "God is one without partner; nothing is like Him; nothing overcomes Him; there is no god but He" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 8}.

## God's attributes in the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya
- He is "eternal without beginning, perpetual without end; He does not perish nor cease" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 8}.
- "Imaginations do not reach Him; intellects do not grasp Him; He does not resemble mortals" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 8} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 9}.
- "Living, He does not die; subsisting, He does not sleep" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 9}; "creating without need, providing without cost, causing death without fear, resurrecting without toil" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 9}.
- "He has ever been, with His attributes, eternal before His creation"; the existence of creatures added no attribute He did not have {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 9}; His was the meaning of Lordship with none lorded over, and the meaning of Creator with none created {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 10}.

## Transcendence
Al-Tahawi says: "Whoever does not guard against negation and likening strays and does not attain transcendence"; he describes God with the attributes of oneness and the epithets of singleness — none of His creatures is in His meaning, and He is exalted above bounds, ends, pillars, limbs and instruments; "the six directions do not contain Him as they contain originated things" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 15}.

## Tawhid's place in the creed
Al-Tahawi closes his creed with: God's religion on earth and in heaven is one — Islam — and it is "between excess and falling short, between likening and negation, between compulsion and free will, and between security and despair" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 31} {cite:aqida-tahawiyya p. 32}. For the general meaning see [[concept/islam-din|Islam]] and [[concept/iman|faith]].

## In Ibn Qudama al-Maqdisi's Lum'at al-I'tiqad
Ibn Qudama says God is "described by what He described Himself in His great Book and on the tongue of His noble prophet"; whatever of the attributes has come in the Qur'an or soundly from the Prophet "must be believed, received with submission and acceptance, leaving off confronting it with rejection, interpretation, likening and comparison" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 4}. He does not describe God "by more than He described Himself — without bound and without end" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 6}. He cites the Prophet's question to the maid "Where is God?" and her answer "In heaven" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 13}; says God is above the Throne {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 14}; and relates from Malik on the istiwa': "The istiwa' is not unknown, the how is not conceivable, belief in it is obligatory, and asking about it is an innovation" {cite:lumat-al-itiqad p. 15}.

In al-Tahawi's wording God is transcendent above the six directions; each saying is attributed to its book.

## In Ibn Taymiyya's al-'Aqida al-Wasitiyya
Ibn Taymiyya says: "Part of belief in God is believing what He described Himself by in His Book and what His messenger Muhammad described Him by — without distortion and without negation, without how and without comparison" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 7}; God "joined negation and affirmation in what He described and named Himself by" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 8}. He says: "All this speech God mentioned — that He is above the Throne and that He is with us — is true in its reality and needs no distortion", and "what the Book and Sunna mention of His nearness and withness does not conflict with what is mentioned of His height and aboveness" {cite:aqida-wasitiyya p. 17}.

## In al-Ash'ari's Maqalat al-Islamiyyin
In "the account of the sum of the saying of the people of hadith and sunna" in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin, Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari presents what the people of hadith and sunna hold, ending: "And in all we have mentioned of their saying we speak, and to it we go" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 350}. He relates there that God is "one God, singular, self-sufficing"; that He is "upon His Throne"; that He has "two hands without how", "two eyes without how" and "a face" {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 345}. He says God's names "are not said to be other than God", and that they affirm for God knowledge, hearing, sight and power and do not deny that of Him {cite:maqalat-islamiyyin p. 345}.

## What is not covered here
This entry presents the positions of the 'Aqida al-Tahawiyya, Ibn Qudama's Lum'at al-I'tiqad, Ibn Taymiyya's al-Wasitiyya, and what al-Ash'ari related in Maqalat al-Islamiyyin of the people of hadith and sunna and declared his own — each attributed to its book. The sayings of the Maturidis and the remaining Sunni and non-Sunni schools from their own books have not yet been added; the entry needs specialist review before promotion.

## For Sprouts
Tawhid means that God is one, with no partner and no likeness, and it is the root of faith.

## For Explorers
Tawhid means that God is one, without partner, without anything like Him, and that there is no god but Him. It is the first thing a Muslim is taught, and the basis of all faith.

## For Scholars
Affirming that God is one, without partner or likeness, and denying Him any associate. Al-Tahawi opens his statement of the creed with it, saying that God is one with no partner, nothing like Him, and no god but Him. Scholars of creed divide tawhid into lordship, worship, and names and attributes, a division of later usage, so it is presented here as an explanation of the meaning and not as a sacred text. The museum speaks of tawhid as the creed of Islam, and presents the views of other groups with their attribution. This entry does not judge those who differ on it.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 10)

The Sheikh says that the whole creation bears witness to God's oneness and to His Messenger's messengerhood, and then bears witness for the believers that they are the ones who hold the oneness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:20:00}. He says that the testimony of oneness belongs specially to the believers, meaning everyone who says that there is no god but God, and not merely those who attend the mosque outwardly {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:20:00}.
