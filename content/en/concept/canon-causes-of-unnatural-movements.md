## Summary
In chapter 13 Ibn Sina says unnatural movements arise from weakening dryness such as dry tremor, convulsing dryness such as hiccup, blockages that stop the power reaching a limb, harmful superfluities that hurt by their cold, as in rigor, or by their sting, as in shivering, or wind trapped in a muscle, as in twitching {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.146}.

## The causes
They arise either from weakening dryness, like dry tremor; or convulsing dryness, like hiccup; and from causes that block the path of the power and stop it reaching the organ through blockages, or from superfluities harmful by their cold, as in rigor, or by their sting, as in shivering; or from a sinking and scarcity of the innate heat, so that the superfluity is strengthened in cold and produces wind seeking to dissolve and escape, as in twitching {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.146}.

## Degrees of the harmful matter
Ibn Sina says the harmful matter, if slight and vaporous, produces stretching and yawning; if stronger, it produces fatigue, the tiring kind if the person is still, and other kinds of fatigue that he will describe if moving; if stronger still it produces shivering; and stronger again, rigor. Windy matter trapped in a muscle produces twitching {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.146}.
