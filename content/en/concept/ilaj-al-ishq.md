## This chapter (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina says the treatment is to consider whether his state has led to the burning of a humour, by the signs one knows, and if so to evacuate; then to see to moistening them, giving them sleep and nourishing them with what is good, and to keep them busy with engrossing matters that make them forget what has wasted them (the editor's footnote: adnafahu means that illness clung to him) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.112}

He also says that if the lover is among the sensible, counsel and admonition, and showing him that what he has is an obsession and a kind of madness, are of use; he names other ways of distracting and turning the attachment to someone other than the beloved from among those the law permits, and says these may need to be treated with the regimen of the melancholic, the manic and the qutrub sufferer when their traits and bodily look have passed over to resemble theirs, and that care be taken to moisten their bodies; the particulars of those ways are not reproduced here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.112} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.113}
