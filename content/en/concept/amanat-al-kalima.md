## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy presents the word as a trust, and holds that a person who speaks before people will be held to account for what he says. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:25:00}
A word has weight, and it can raise its speaker or lower him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:00:30}{cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:19:00}

## The word is a trust
The Sheikh says trust is wider than the keeping of money, and he cites a hadith in which the loss of trust is a sign of the Hour. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:03:00}
He mentions a verse on the trust offered to the heavens, the earth and the mountains, which the human being accepted; he does not name the sura of the verse in the recording. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:03:30}
He says the trust is to worship God by His commands and prohibitions, and that acts of worship such as ablution and humility in prayer are trusts. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:04:00}{cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:05:00}
The speaker's view is that everything a person is given is a trust, and that the believer is responsible for upholding his religion in it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:08:00}

## The weight of a word
He cites a hadith on a single word weighing heavily for or against a person. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:00:30}
He says that righteousness is a cheerful face and gentle, truthful speech, and that a word may raise its speaker in Paradise or lower him in God's wrath. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:18:30}
He cites a hadith that a single word may lift a person to high ranks or cast him into the Fire. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:19:00}
He says that scholars warned against four things: speech, looks, steps and thoughts. He cites a hadith of the night journey in an image of something that cannot return, and says a spoken word cannot be recalled. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:24:00}
He says that a wound from the tongue lasts longer than a wound from the sword, and that a Muslim guards his speech accordingly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep02 00:25:00}
He says that a spear wound may heal but a tongue wound may not, and urges repentance and a renewed resolve when one has spoken wrongly. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:42:30}

## Backbiting and tale-bearing
He says that our scholars advised guarding the tongue, and that backbiting and tale-bearing breed resentment and envy among relatives, neighbours and colleagues. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:01:30}
He lists the sins of the tongue: backbiting, gossip, lying, false witness, obscene speech and false oaths. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:17:00}
He defines backbiting and gossip, and says that Muslim homes now broadcast one another's affairs. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:29:00}
He cites a hadith on the believer who eats what is good and does not harm what he touches, and a hadith that the worst of people are those avoided for their harm. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep07 00:25:30}
He cites a hadith that a Muslim is one from whose tongue and hands people are safe, and asks whether people are safe from the tale-bearing of listeners. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep19 00:30:30}
He says that keeping trust with people's money, secrets and honour keeps a person safe. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:17:30}

## Lies
He criticises those who divide lies into white, red and green lies. He says the tongue records every word, and that a lie is a lie. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:27:00}
He lists the lies he regards as permitted: in war, between spouses to keep harmony, and in reconciling disputing parties. He says all other lies are forbidden. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:28:30}
He cites Abu Bakr's answer to the Quraysh as an example of a lie permitted to reconcile people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:30:30}
He recounts a man who refused to deny his fault before the ruler and was released because he spoke the truth (needs_source: the story is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:20:30}

## The speaker's view: speaking before people
The speaker's view is that one who speaks before people must respect their minds, and that he will be held to account for his words. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:25:00}
He holds that honesty in the media is one of the gravest trusts, that a public speaker must verify before speaking, and that covering a Muslim's faults is better. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep01 00:40:00}{cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep19 00:31:00}
He gives the example of a father who heard his son promise to study, while the son's conduct over the years contradicted his words, and says that the tongue of state speaks louder than the tongue of speech. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:26:30}
