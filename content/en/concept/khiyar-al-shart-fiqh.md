## Summary
The option of stipulation (khiyar al-shart) is a compound name for the right, set by stipulation, of one party to choose between confirming and annulling; Ibn Arafa defines it as a sale whose conclusion is held back for a confirmation expected. Other names: the stipulated option (distinguished from the legal option, such as defect), the option of deliberation, and the sale of option. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7923}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7924} Its lawfulness: most jurists hold it lawful and not contrary to the contract, on the report of Ibn Umar and of Habban ibn Munqidh ("when you sell, say: no deceit" and "you have the option three nights"); al-Nawawi and Ibn al-Humam report agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7924}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7925} Wording: no fixed words; any word that conveys the option (consent, will) is enough, and custom counts for the Malikis, as when people have long bargained with an option in a certain sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7925}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7926} Conditions: it must be stipulated in the contract or its session in the Shafii and Hanbali view; the Hanafis allow it to be added later as if at the contract; it must be limited by a known term, since an unlimited option causes gross ignorance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7926}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7927}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7927}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7928} Term: the least is not fixed and may be a moment; for the greatest, Ahmad, Muhammad al-Shaybani, Abu Yusuf and others allow any term by agreement, while Ibn Abi Layla, Ibn Shubruma and others too; Ahmad says he dislikes a long term, and the author of Ghayat al-Muntaha finds a term far beyond custom (such as a thousand years) invalid; Malik limits it by need and what is usual for the sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7928}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7929} Purpose: to let the party consider and test the thing; the term under the Malikis is measured by what the thing needs to be tested and consulted about. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7928}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7929} Term for land: the outer limit is one month plus six days, so the option may run up to thirty-six days; two further days are added so that the buyer, still holding the thing at the end of the term, can return it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7930} Animals: if the option is to learn their food, strength and price, the limit is three days; if to test them within the town, one day or so; outside it, a barid (half a day's march) according to Ibn al-Qasim and two barids according to Ashhab. Clothes, goods and fungibles: three days plus one day. Fruit and vegetables: the term is set by what testing needs before they change. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7930} The third view fixes three days with their nights for any subject, and forbids more. It is the view of Abu Hanifa, Zufar and (in the better-known report) al-Shafii. The sources are the report of Habban ibn Munqidh and the hadith of the sheep with its udder held full (musarrah): whoever buys a sheep so held has the option for three days, as al-Shafii argued. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7930}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7931} Beyond three days: for Abu Hanifa and Zufar the contract is void as fasid; for al-Shafii it is void as batil. Abu Hanifa alone holds that dropping the excess makes the contract valid, as long as the three days have not passed; Zufar disagrees, since continuation follows the original validity. The Malikis add that the term is faulty if it is to consult someone whose view is known only after it ends, or if it greatly exceeds the life of the goods; one extra day does no harm. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7931}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7932} An option with no term has four views. (1) The contract is void or invalid: al-Shafii and, in one report, Ahmad; the Hanafis hold the sale invalid without distinguishing gross from small uncertainty. Abu Hanifa and his companions hold that if the defect is removed within the three days the contract becomes valid; the two companions hold it valid even after three days. (2) The condition is void but the contract stands: one report of Ahmad, and Ibn Abi Layla. (3) The contract stands and the term is set: Malik lets the judge fix the usual term for testing such goods; Ibn Taymiyya suggests three days if they gave none. (4) The contract stands and the option stays open as it arose, the view of Ibn Shubruma and one report of Ahmad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7932}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7933} Permanent option: a condition that makes the option last forever (or for unstated days) invalidates it. Unknown term: invalid, whether the uncertainty is gross (wind, rain, a person's arrival or death, the birth of a pregnant animal) or slight (harvest, threshing, the return of pilgrims). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7933} Connection: the term must start at the contract. If the term is set to begin later (from the end of the month or tomorrow) the sale is void for the Hanafis, Shafiis and Hanbalis; the Hanafis rescue it by counting the gap within the term, as in an option of two days after Ramadan in a contract made at the end of Ramadan. Continuity: the parts of the term must follow one another; for a day held and a day not, the Hanbalis have two views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7933}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7934} Holder: the option holder must be named. If the parties give it to one of them without naming which, or to whomever one of them chooses, it is void according to Ibn Qudama; al-Nawawi holds the sale valid if the option is for one party only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7934}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7935} Contracts: the option belongs only to binding contracts that can be rescinded. Sale is its main field, including sale of trust such as murabaha. The exceptions are salam, exchange of money (sarf) and exchange of ribawi goods of the same kind, for which some Hanbalis require the price to be received in the session; Ibn Abidin notes that excluding salam and sarf strains the rule, and the Malikis allow it in short salam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7935} Other contracts: leasing takes the option in general for Hanafis and Malikis; Shafiis and Hanbalis allow it only in leases on the debt, or in fixed leases whose term does not begin at once. Transfer of debt (hawala): accepted by the Hanafis, refused by the Shafiis and Hanbalis as it is not built on exchange. Division: divided; division of different kinds is by consent, division of the same kind of values takes the option. Guarantee (kafala): the Hanafis allow it, and Abu Hanifa allows more than three days because it is built on latitude. Waqf: allowed by Abu Yusuf, void for Muhammad, and in the Shafii and Hanbali view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7935}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7936} Who may stipulate: the option may be given to either party or to both, the view of most jurists; al-Thawri and Ibn Shubruma limited it to the buyer. It may be given to a third party: the jurists agree this is sound if the third party can speak for himself. The Hanafis base this on juristic preference (istihsan), because a party with little experience may need someone wiser; Zufar objected because the option is part of the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7936}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7937}
