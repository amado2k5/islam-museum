## Its sources
Foetor of the mouth begins either from the gum — its own putrefaction, a relaxation occurring in it, or a putrefaction there without the moistures, this temperament mostly hot — or from the mouth of the stomach for a putrid humour there, bilious or phlegmy; and it may come from the lung's quarters as occurs to the consumptive {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.264}
## What comes from the gum
For what comes from the gum and the 'amur (the 'amur is the flesh oozing from the gum between the teeth, by the copyists' explanation), cleaning the teeth is always to be cared for and washing them of this kind; if that profits in them and they are well, good; if not and there is a surplus of putrefaction, chewing of this kind must be done after it, an astringing thing of this kind laid on the gum, rinsing of this kind and rubbing of this kind; and if stronger than that, chewing of this kind and spitting the spittle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.264}
## When the putrefaction shows
If that does not profit and the putrefaction shows plain, stronger things of this kind are used; if the putrefaction is in the tooth itself, its cure is scraping it if it is at the edge, or filing it with the file, or drawing the tooth if the putrefaction touches the tooth's root (hand-works and instruments carried as the book describes them; the names and recipes left out). If the gum's relaxation is there and the cause is the putrefaction occurring, its treatment is strengthening it with what is told in the gum-relaxation chapter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.265}
## What comes from the putrid humour
If the humour is bilious-putrid in the stomach or the mouth's skin, nothing profits it like the cooling fruits on an empty stomach of this kind, and if they are absent their dried infusion on an empty stomach is used; among what profits is taking sawiq with sugar and snow-water, his food made every cooling cleansing thing not turning into bile. If the humour is phlegmy, vomiting is used first, then what purifies the stomach's mouth named in the stomach chapter; his food is the stews, much water-drinking lessened, fruits and moist herbs abandoned, and his tooth-sticks taken from the bitter cutting trees of this kind (the names, recipes and pills left out) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.265}
