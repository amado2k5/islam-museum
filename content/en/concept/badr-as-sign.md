## Summary
Sha'rawi reads the verse in Al Imran 13 as a visible sign at Badr: the believing group fighting for God overcame the disbelieving group fighting for Satan {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1303}.

## Seeing the two hosts
He sets out the readings of "seeing them twice". He holds that the verses of al-Anfal show the enemy made few before the battle and many during it, so that the believers' courage is kindled and the disbelievers' nerve fails {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1303} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1304} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1305}.

## The caravan and the armed host
He says the believers went out to take the caravan, but God willed victory over the armed host, since a win over the armed is greater than a win over an unguarded caravan {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1307}.

## Stories of the companions
He tells stories of Abu Bakr and his son, of Khalid ibn al-Walid and 'Ikrima, and of Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr and his brother Abu 'Aziz, without tracing them to their sources {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1309} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1310}.
