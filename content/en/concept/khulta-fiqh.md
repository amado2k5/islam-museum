## Summary
Khulta is lexically mixing, and the partner is the mixer; in fiqh there are two kinds: mixing of things, called joint or common mixing, where property belongs to two or more in common and remains undivided, and mixing of attributes, called neighbourly mixing, where each owner\'s property is known and they use the same facilities without separation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7748} The ruling: mixing is lawful in itself and may ease the owners, as when the herds of a village go to one shepherd, or joint farming shares water and labour. The Prophet forbade showing a mixture to reduce zakat, or showing separation for that end, in the report that one does not join what is separate nor separate what is joined for fear of zakat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7748}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7749} On the effect of mixing on zakat: the majority hold it has an effect, citing the report of Anas that two mixing partners settle between them equally; the schools differ on some conditions and on which property is affected. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7749}
