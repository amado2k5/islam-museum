## Summary
Ibn Sina says that distillation and filtration set bad waters right, and where that is impossible, boiling, since boiled water, as the learned attest, causes less wind and passes down faster {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.135}.

## His case for boiling
He answers the physicians who suppose that in boiling the fine part rises and the coarse part stays, so that boiling gains nothing. He holds that water in its own nature is uniform in fineness and coarseness, and is thickened either by severe cold or by an intimate mixture with earthy parts too small to separate. Boiling removes the thickening caused by cold and loosens the parts, so the heavy earthy parts separate and settle and a pure water close to the simple is left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.135-136}.

## The evidence
His proof is that coarse waters left for a long time deposit nothing worth mentioning, whereas boiled they deposit much at once and the remaining water becomes light and clear. He adds that the waters of great valleys such as the river Jayhun are extremely turbid when drawn and clear in a short time, and that boiling is followed by churning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.136}.
