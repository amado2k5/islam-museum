## Summary
"Knowing the shadhdh" is the thirteenth type in [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]], who quotes three definitions and then sets out his own distinctions. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 76}

## Three definitions
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Shafi'i (contradiction)**: Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la reported [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] saying: the shadhdh hadith is not that a trustworthy narrator relates what no one else relates; the shadhdh is that a trustworthy narrator relates a hadith that contradicts what others relate. Al-Khalili reported the same from al-Shafi'i and a group of Hijazi scholars. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 76}
- **Sunni: al-Khalili (a single chain)**: what the hafizes hold is that the shadhdh is a report with only one chain, transmitted by a single shaykh, trustworthy or not; if he is not trustworthy it is abandoned, and if he is, it is suspended and not used as proof. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 77}
- **Sunni: al-Hakim (a trustworthy narrator alone, uncorroborated)**: the shadhdh is a hadith a trustworthy narrator transmits alone, with no corroborating source; it differs from the defective (*mu'allal*) in that the defect of the latter has been identified, while no such defect has been found in the shadhdh. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 77}
:::

## Ibn al-Salah's critique
Ibn al-Salah says what al-Shafi'i judged shadhdh is clearly shadhdh and unacceptable, but the other two definitions run up against reports transmitted alone by upright, retentive hafizes, such as "actions are only by intentions", which Umar alone transmitted, then Alqama ibn Waqqas alone from him, then Muhammad ibn Ibrahim, then Yahya ibn Sa'id, as hadith scholars soundly hold. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 77} Clearer still is Abd Allah ibn Dinar's hadith from Ibn Umar forbidding the sale or gift of clientage, which Abd Allah ibn Dinar alone transmitted. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 78}

## His distinctions
He concludes that when a narrator is alone in something, it is examined: if it contradicts what someone with better memory and precision relates, it is shadhdh and rejected; if there is no contradiction, the narrator is examined — if he is upright, retentive and trusted for precision his solitary report is accepted, and if not, his solitariness weakens him and moves the report out of the sound. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 79} It then falls among degrees: if the solitary narrator is not far below the precise one whose solitariness is accepted, his hadith is deemed fair and not lowered to weak; if he is far below, it is rejected as shadhdh and objectionable (*munkar*). {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 79} So the rejected shadhdh is of two kinds: the solitary report that contradicts, and the solitary report whose narrator lacks the reliability and precision to offset the objection and weakness that solitariness brings. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 79}

## Related notions
The shadhdh is linked with [[concept/al-hadith-al-gharib|the rare and solitary report]] and with [[concept/ziyadat-al-thiqa|additions by trustworthy narrators]], since Ibn al-Salah assigns the contradictory kind of addition to the ruling of the shadhdh. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 86} His next type, the munkar, illustrates an unbearable solitariness with Abu Zukayr Yahya ibn Muhammad ibn Qays's "eat unripe dates with dried dates", a righteous shaykh whom Muslim cites, yet not of a standing to carry a report alone. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 82}
