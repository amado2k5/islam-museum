## Summary
Suftajah is the repayment of a debt in a city other than the city where the debt was contracted, whether written in a deed or unwritten, and it may arise from a loan or a deferred sale {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 262}.

## Cases of Stipulation
If the lender stipulates it and it imposes a burden on the borrower, it is impermissible because it constitutes riba, as the lender benefits by saving transport costs and risks, making the money guaranteed by the borrower until the time and place of repayment {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 263}. If the borrower desires it and the lender does not consent, the lender cannot be compelled to accept it because they would bear the burden of transport {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 263}. If the borrower desires it and the lender agrees to bear the costs of transferring the funds back, it is permissible {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 263}.

## Cases of Benefit
If repayment takes place in the borrower's city, or the borrower has funds there, with no burden on either party and an interest for the lender in recovering the loan there, it is permissible {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 263}. What counts in suftajah is the money of repayment, not the money of the loan; if the borrower transfers the loan funds to another city, that is a burden undertaken for themselves {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 264}.

## Summary
The author summarizes that suftajah is permissible if it imposes no burden on the borrower, and even more clearly permissible if it benefits the borrower {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 264} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 265}. He holds that a lender who lends for their own benefit does not sin, though they do not earn the reward of pure benevolence {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 264}.

## Connection to Riba
The author presents suftajah as an example of distinguishing between a permissible benefit and prohibited riba in a loan {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 263} (see [[concept/riba|Riba]]).
