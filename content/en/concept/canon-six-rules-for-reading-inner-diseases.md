## Summary
Once anatomy is known, Ibn Sina has the physician rest on six rules for reading inner diseases: from harm to functions, from what is evacuated, from pain, from swelling, from position, and from related outward symptoms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.156}.

## The six rules
The first is from harm to functions, whose indication is primary and constant. The second is from what is evacuated, whose indication is constant but not primary: constant because it always brings assent, not primary because it points by way of ripeness or unripeness. The third is from pain, the fourth from swelling, the fifth from position, and the sixth from related outward symptoms, whose indication is neither primary nor constant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.156}.
