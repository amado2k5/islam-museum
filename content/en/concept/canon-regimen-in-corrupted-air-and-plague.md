## Summary
Ibn Sina states that when air is corrupted and plague-ridden it is met by drying the body and adjusting the dwelling with things cooling and moistening in power, or heating against the cause of corruption; that pleasant scents help most; that inhalation of much air is reduced; and that corruption from the ground is told apart from corruption from the air itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.

## Drying and adjusting the dwelling
When air is corrupted and pestilential it must be met by drying the body and adjusting the dwelling with things that cool and moisten by their power, which is the more required in an epidemic, or that heat and act against the cause of the air's corruption {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.

## Scents and breathing
Pleasant scents are of most use in an epidemic, especially when chosen to oppose the temperament; and the need to draw in much air is reduced by distribution and airing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.

## Corruption from the ground or the air
Often the air is corrupted from the ground, and one then sits on beds and seeks very high dwellings and places pierced by winds; often the origin lies in the air itself, from corruption carried from neighbouring airs or from a celestial matter whose manner is hidden from people, and one then takes refuge in cellars, in houses walled on their sides, and in inner chambers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.
