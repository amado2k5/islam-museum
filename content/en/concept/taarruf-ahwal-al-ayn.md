## From touch, motion, veins and colour
It is discerned from her touch: whether the touch finds her hot or cold, hard and dry or soft and moist; from her motion: light motion points to heat or dryness, heavy to cold and moisture; from her veins: gross wide ones point to her heat, fine hidden ones to her cold, empty ones to her dryness, full ones to abundant matter in her; from her colour: every colour points to the matching prevailing humour — red, yellow, leaden and livid {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.163}

## From shape, action, discharge and passions
From her shape: good shape points to strength in her formation, bad shape to the opposite; from her special action: if she sees the faint far and near alike and is not hurt by strong objects of sight she is strong-tempered and balanced; if she perceives the near however small but falls short on the far, her spirit is pure, sound but little ⁦(the book transmits the physicians' talk of the ray and the spirit)⁩; if she perceives the far but not the small brought nearest until moved off, her spirit is turbid and moist and her temper moist; if she is weak in both, her spirit is little and turbid; from what flows from her: if dry and not running she is dry, if running excessively very moist; from her passions: if hurt by heat and relieved by cold a hot distemper, and oppositely; the mean in each of these kinds is balanced, except the excessive excellence of sight — that is the balanced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.163}

## The eye's diseases and its sharers
All kinds of diseases befall the eye — material, simple, structural-instrumental and shared; the eye's states of squinting, closing, opening, colour and tearing carry rulings tied to the acute diseases; the eyes' diseases may be their own or shared, and the nearest sharers are the brain and head, the outer and inner membranes, then the stomach; every disease befalling the eye through sharing the outer membrane is the safest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.164} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.164}
