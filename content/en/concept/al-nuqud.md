## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat discusses money in the chapter "Between Means and Temptation," arguing that the legitimate function of money is as a medium of exchange, and that many verses of the Qur'an command spending and the rapid circulation of wealth while forbidding its hoarding {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 142}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 143}.

## The Ideal Model
The author envisions a society in which farmers, livestock breeders, and poultry producers produce goods, and everyone agrees on a piece of gold or silver to serve as a medium of exchange—having no value in itself, only value in exchange—so that money remains a secondary operation that does not affect the course of production {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 143}. In his view, this model is free of monetary problems and is what Islamic economics, based on natural production, calls for {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 143}.

## Critique of Reality
The author argues that capitalism and communism gave money a value it does not deserve, so that the owner of money came to own everything even without producing, and monopolized what others produced {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 142}. He regards what happened after oil—the shift from natural production to trading in money, transferring funds to European banks, and dumping produce into the sea in pursuit of profit—as manifestations of this deviation {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 144}.

## From the book Usul al-Iqtisad al-Islami: Part Two (pp. 101–200 of the PDF file)
The author notes that money is mentioned in the Qur'an and Sunnah under the names of gold, silver, silver coin, and dirhams, and states that according to the majority of scholars, hoarding gold and silver means failing to pay zakat on them {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 159} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 160}.

The author quotes Ibn Taymiyyah that dirhams and dinars are a means not sought for their own sake, and al-Ghazali's description of them as two stones created to pass from hand to hand, and notes that scholars agreed that minting currency is a function of the state and that money is among the assets subject to zakat and to riba {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 163} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 164}.
