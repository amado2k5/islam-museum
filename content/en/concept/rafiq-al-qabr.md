## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy speaks of death as a passage on which a person takes only his deeds into the grave: his goods stay behind and his family walks him to the grave's edge. He gives narrations and stories about burial and the grave and presents them as his own account; some of them are not sourced in the recording {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:08:00}.

## What the dead leave and what goes with them
He says the dead person's goods stay behind him, his family accompanies him to the grave, and only his deeds go into the grave with him {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:08:00}. He says the shroud is the only worldly thing a person takes with him, and that he enters the grave as he came out of his mother's womb, poor and naked {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:08:30}. He tells that Hatim found that the only beloved who goes into the grave with a person is his good deeds, which become his companion in the grave {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:11:00}.

He says a good person is missed when he is absent and remembered kindly when he dies, and that people should be present for one another {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep07 00:26:30}. He tells that Hasan al-Basri heard a group weeping and laughing at a funeral, and said they were mourning travellers for travellers; he likens life to a train with stations, and says each person will get off at his own station (needs_source: the anecdote is not sourced in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:14:30}.

## The shroud, burial and the funeral
The speaker cites a hadith on the reward of one who attends the funeral prayer and walks with the bier, and says the reward is two qirats, each like a mountain (needs_source: the hadith and the figures are not sourced in the recording). He tells that Ibn Umar regretted the qirats he had missed, and checked the matter with Aisha {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep03 00:31:00}.

He cites a verse on the creation of man from dust and his return to it, and describes throwing dust on the grave at three points {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:09:30}. He tells the story of the man who denied the Hour, whose companion reminded him that he was created from dust and then formed; he says it is from the Qur'an, without naming the place {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep06 00:19:30}.

He urges hastening the burial of the dead, and says that this is recommended {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:10:00}. He tells that two gravediggers in Medina, one digging a niche and the other a shaft, both made istikhara; the one who came first was chosen, and the Prophet was placed in the niche (needs_source: the narration is not sourced in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:38:30}.

## The grave and its companion, in the speaker's account
He tells a hadith about a handsome, fragrant figure that comes to the grave of a righteous person to keep him company until the Hour (needs_source: the hadith is not named in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:12:00}. He gives the counterpart narration, in which an ugly figure comes to keep an evil person company (needs_source: the hadith is not named in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:12:30}.

In one narration he gives, the grave of a believer is widened and his seat in Paradise is shown to him, while the grave of one who did not believe is narrowed; he cites Umar's request to be carried quickly to his grave (needs_source: the narration is not sourced in the recording) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:36:00}. He cites a hadith on hastening the funeral, and says the wicked would be hurried off if people knew what awaited them {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep18 00:36:30}.

He describes the story of a traveller who asked a guide to the city and was led to a graveyard, where everyone had come to the city and none had come back {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:24:00}. He recites a line of verse about sleeping in the grave and the day of reckoning, without naming its author in the recording (needs_source: the author is not named) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep17 00:31:30}.

## The speaker's view: preparing for that day
The speaker criticises people who talk about market prices while standing at a grave, and asks whether anyone has prepared for that day {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:10:30}. He says the grave is a box of deeds, and criticises those who give false witness, swear false oaths and wrong others {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep19 00:33:30}.

## In Abdul Hamid Kishk's sermons
[[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Abdul Hamid Kishk]] says that family and friends leave once the body is laid in the grave, and that only a person's deeds enter the grave with him: if good, they benefit him, and if bad, they forsake him. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:19:30} He says the outside of graves is stone while inside is reward or punishment. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:16:00}
