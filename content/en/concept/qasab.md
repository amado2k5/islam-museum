## Nature
Reed is of many kinds: the solid, from which arrows are made; the female, from which the tongues of flutes are made; the thick-bodied, with many knots, fit for writing; the thick hollow that grows on river banks; the thin hollow, extremely fine, from which mats are made; and the very thick and tall, hard to break, brought from India, from which the lance is made {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Temperament
Very strongly cooling, and its ash is hot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Actions and properties
In its root there is a slight clearing without sharpness, and in its leaf also; as a poultice it draws thorns, reed splinters and arrows from deep in the flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Cosmetics
Its husks and root benefit alopecia and clear away dirt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Swellings and pustules
Its fresh leaf is put on the carbuncle and on hot swellings and benefits them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Joints
It soothes the twisting of the nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Organs of the head
Its flower, if it falls into the ear, causes deafness and lodges, not coming out; and burnt reed benefits scalp scab and ringworm of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Excretory organs
It induces urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that it induces the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}

## Poisons and bites
It benefits the sting of the scorpion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.644}
