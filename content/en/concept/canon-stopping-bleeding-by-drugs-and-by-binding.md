## Summary
Ibn Sina closes chapter 23 by describing the cooling, astringent, sealing and caustic drugs and binding as ways of stopping an evacuation, and then fixes the treatment of bleeding by its cause {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.303}.

## The drugs
Cooling drugs congeal the fluid and take hold of the mouths of the vessels and narrow them; astringents contract the matter and draw the channels together; sealing drugs cause blockages in the mouths of the channels, and if they are hot and drying they work better. Caustics make an eschar that stands over the mouth of the channel and closes it, but with a foreseen harm: the eschar may be torn away and the channel become wider {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.303}.

## Kinds of caustic
Some caustics have astringency, like vitriol, and some have none, like unslaked lime. The astringent kind is wanted where a not-lasting eschar is wanted, the other where the eschar is to fall quickly, and the astringent caustic where a lasting eschar is wanted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.303}.

## Binding
Binding is partly by closing the channel and forcing it together, like binding above the elbow when the bleeder errs in the basilic vein and strikes the artery, and partly by stuffing the mouth of the wound with what blocks the way of the flow, such as hare's fur {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.303}.

## By the cause
Bleeding from opened mouths of vessels is treated with astringents to close them; from a burn, with sealing astringents such as sealed clay; from erosion, with flesh-growing drugs mixed with cleansers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.303}.
