## Nature and selection
The sage Dioscorides says that fern is of two kinds. The male is a plant without leaves, flower or fruit, with a fixed frond on a stalk a cubit long or more; it has a visible root, black and long, with many branches, astringent in taste; it grows in mountain places or rocky places, and its root drives out the tapeworm. Among the ancients some call it qulurhun, some people call it plikhrun, some call it the male plunturis, and in Tabaristan they call it har. The other kind is the female, with a leaf like the male but with many stalks longer than his, and roots broad, long, thick and red, many of them dark, and some red as blood. Whoever wishes to drink it should first eat some garlic, and the male is stronger in action than the other {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.

## Actions and properties
It dries without sting, and has bitterness and astringency {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It heals ulcers, and the female kind is dried, ground and sprinkled on moist ulcers that are hard to heal, and they heal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.

## Excretory organs
Drunk, it kills worms and tapeworm, and its action is made stronger by mixing it with other purgatives; the female kind drunk with wine brings out long worms. Its leaf, when it first comes up, is eaten cooked and loosens the belly. The measures the book gives are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
If a woman drinks it ground she does not conceive, and if a pregnant woman drinks it she miscarries; it may also be dried and applied to the belly, and if drunk it kills the foetus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.
