## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the third chapter, on the consistency of urine, dividing it into thin, thick and moderate, and says very thin urine shows lack of ripening, obstructions, a weak kidney, much water-drinking or severe cold with dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.189}.

## Causes of thinness
The consistency of urine is thin, thick or moderate. Very thin urine shows lack of ripening in every case, or obstructions in the vessels, or a weakness of the kidney and urinary passages so that only the thin is drawn, or much water-drinking, or a very cold temperament with dryness. In acute diseases it shows weakness of the digesting power and no ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.189}.

## Children and persistence
Such thin urine is worse in children than in young men, because their natural urine is thicker than that of young men, they being moister and their bodies drawing moisture more, needing a surplus of matter for growth; so when their urine turns thin in acute fevers they have moved far from their natural state. If it continues it shows destruction, unless good signs agree and the strength holds, when it shows an abscess to come, especially under the region of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.189}.

## In the healthy and at the crisis
So too if it continues in the healthy and does not change, it shows a swelling to arise where they feel pain; most often they also feel pain in the loins and kidneys, showing a readiness for a swelling. If that pain and heaviness does not single out one region but is general, it shows pustules, smallpox and swellings throughout the body. Thinning of the urine at the crisis without gradation warns of a relapse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.189}.
