## Nature and selection
An Egyptian tree that grows only in one place, called Ain Shams in the book. Its leaves and smell are like the rue, but paler, and its stature is that of the tree the book calls hudud {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Its oil is better than its seed, and its seed is stronger than its wood in all respects {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. The oil is taken by making a small cut with an iron after the rising of Sirius, and the seeping liquid is gathered with cotton. No more than a set number of pounds is taken in one year {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. Dioscorides is quoted as saying that the tree grows only in Palestine, in its valley {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}. A test of the oil is that it sets milk when a drop is put on milk. The adulterated oil does not set it, and it may be mixed with some other oils {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.384}-{cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.385}.

## Temperament and actions
Its wood is hot and dry in the second degree, its seed a little hotter, and its oil hotter than both, at the beginning of the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.385}. It opens obstructions and benefits weak organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.385}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: it cleanses ulcers, especially with iris, and draws out the bone's surface flakes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.385}. Joints: taken by mouth it helps sciatica, and its decoction helps spasm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Head: it cleanses the sores of the head and the head itself, and helps epilepsy and dizziness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Eyes: its oil and the plant itself clear the film over the eye and sharpen sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Chest: its wood and seed help pain in the sides, thick asthma, shortness of breath, and cold lung pain, and its seed helps cold pleurisy and cough. Generally it is useful for the organs above the diaphragm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Food organs: it helps weak digestion, its decoction removes bad digestion, cleanses the stomach, and strengthens the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Excretory organs: it promotes urine and helps colic. Its smoke drives out moisture from the womb, helps its cold, and expels the foetus and the afterbirth. Fumigation with it helps all womb pains, its decoction opens the mouth of the womb, and a salve of its oil with rose oil and wax helps cold of the womb and difficult urination. These are the book's statements about the womb and foetus, recorded as its own listing and not as advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Fevers: its oil removes shivering {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. Poisons: it resists poisons and helps the bite of vipers, and its oil drunk with milk helps against hemlock and against vermin in particular {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}.
