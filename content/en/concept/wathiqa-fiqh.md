## Summary
A wathiqa is what is taken to secure a right against loss through denial, forgetting, insolvency or other hazards. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16615} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16616}

## Basis and kinds
Its basis is the Qur'anic verses on debt in Surat al-Baqara and the Prophet's saying that the guarantor is liable. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16616}
There are three kinds: witness against denial, pledge and surety against insolvency, and writing against forgetting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16616} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16617}

## The ruling on writing
Most jurists hold the command to write a recommendation, since Muslims sell on deferred prices without writing or witness; some, including al-Tabari, hold it obligatory. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16617}
The purpose of writing and witness is that a debt whose payment is deferred suffers forgetting and denial. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16617} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16618}

## Pledge and surety
A pledge is property held as security for a debt, to be paid from its price if the debtor cannot pay. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16618}
A surety joins the guarantor's liability to the debtor's, so the right holds against both, and the creditor may claim from either. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16618}
