## Summary
In chapter 27 Ibn Sina treats a corrupted organ by removing the corrupt flesh and then cutting and cauterising, describes how the limit of safety is found in the bone and how it is cut, and says when the physician should flee {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.

## Corrupt flesh
When an organ is corrupted by a bad temperament, with matter or without, and scarification and the paint with what sets right, as set out in the particular books, do not avail, the corrupt flesh on it must be taken away, preferably without iron if possible, since iron may strike splinters of muscle, nerve and pulsing vessels with ruinous effect {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.

## Cutting and cauterising
If that does not avail and the corruption has passed into the flesh, it must be cut away, and the cut is cauterised with boiling oil, by which its dangerous consequence is avoided and the bleeding stops; strange flesh and skin grow over the cut, most like flesh in their hardness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.

## The limit of safety in the bone
When the bone is to be cut, a probe is put in and moved round the bone; where it finds sound attachment the pain grows sharp as the probe goes in, and that is the limit of safety, while where it finds puffiness and weak attachment it is among what must be cut away, sometimes by drilling around the bone to be cut until the drills surround it and it breaks and is severed, sometimes by sawing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.

## Shielding and fleeing
When this is done, the cutter and the borer are kept from the flesh so as not to hurt it. If the bone needing to be cut is a projecting splinter that will not set and cannot be hoped to mend and is feared to spoil what is beside it, the flesh is moved off it, either by an incision and then binding and drawing in the opposite direction, or by other devices, and it is screened from a noble organ with cloths before it is cut. If the bone is like the thigh bone, large, near nerves, arteries and veins and much corrupted, the physician should flee at that point {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.
