## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the sealing of the heart is a judgement that disbelief inside it does not leave and that faith does not enter. It comes after the disbeliever has chosen disbelief and persisted in it, and God knew this before the sealing. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.142} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.143}

## Why the heart comes first
Sha'rawi notes that [[passage/quran-2-7|Al-Baqara 7]] puts the heart before hearing and sight because it is the seat of inner perception, and that sealing means no new perception enters and no old one leaves. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.143}

## Author's view
This is Sha'rawi's reading and is attributed to him. The scan gives no other exegetical or kalam view on the sealing, and none has been checked.

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "God has set a seal on their hearts"
Al-Tabari says the root sense of khatm is stamping or sealing, as one "seals" a letter, and that hearts are vessels for the knowledge placed in them, so sealing them and the hearing is like sealing any other vessel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 258}
He relays from Mujahid that people used to think of the heart as like the palm: with each sin a finger closes until all are closed and then it is stamped, and they called this "ran"; also that sins surround the heart on every side until they meet over it, and their meeting is the stamping; and that ran is milder than stamping, stamping milder than locks, and locks the severest. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 258} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 259}
He mentions a view that the verse only reports their arrogance and refusal to listen to the truth, as one says "so-and-so is deaf to this speech". Al-Tabari prefers what agrees with the report from Abu Hurayra that when a believer sins a black spot forms on his heart; if he repents and seeks forgiveness it is polished, and if he adds sin it grows until it covers the heart, which is the "ran" of the Qur'an. He holds that when sins follow one another and close the heart, the seal from God comes, and faith then has no way in. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 260} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 261}
He argues against holders of the second view that if arrogance and refusal are their own act, the seal, which is God's act, cannot be the same thing; and if the seal is the cause of them, it is something other than their disbelief and arrogance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 261}
