## Summary
"Revenue goes with liability" (al-kharaj bi-al-daman) is a hadith establishing that yield and return belong to whoever guarantees the principal and bears its risk; for the author, it is the foundational principle linking return to risk {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 202}.

## The Hadith
The author notes that the hadith was narrated by al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, the compilers of the four Sunan, and Ibn Hibban, and that it means the yield belongs to the one who bears liability {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 202} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 240}.

## Its Meaning Regarding Defects
The meaning of the hadith in the case the author cites is that if someone buys something that produces a yield, such as a slave or a house, and a defect then appears so they return it, whatever yield accrued belongs to the buyer, because they were the owner bearing liability before the return {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 240} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 284}. Thus it is liability tied to ownership, not mere liability detached from ownership {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 284}.

## Kharaj in the Sense of Tax
Kharaj is used in the sense of jizyah, rent, and yield, and according to Ibn Taymiyyah it is land rent in the view of most scholars when the state is the lessor {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 240}. The author cites this meaning in the section on land (see [[concept/kharaj|Kharaj]]).

## Connection to Profit
The author regards the hadith as a foundational principle that profit follows risk and that risk increases earnings, though he does not make mere guarantee alone entitle one to profit {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 202} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 283} (see [[concept/profit-in-islam|Profit in Islam]]).
