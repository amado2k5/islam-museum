## Overview
*Tanahmir* is a feature in the speech of some people of Yemen, recorded by [[person/al-hamdani|al-Hamdani]] in *Sifat jazirat al-Arab*. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.117}

## What al-Hamdani records
He says that the speech of Saru Himyar and Ja'da contained "something of the tanahmir". They drew out their speech and elided sounds, saying "bi-ibn ma'm" for "bi-ibn al-amm" (with the son of the paternal uncle), and "simma'" for "isma'" (listen). {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.117}

## The eloquence of the tribes
He counted Kinda, Hamdan, and some of the Sadaf as the most eloquent people of Hadramawt and its environs. He said that Mahra resemble non-Arabs in their speech, and that Nahij, Abyan, and Dathina were the most eloquent. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.117}

## Its significance
This page is considered the earliest detailed testimony to the survival of traces of the old Himyaritic language in the speech of the people of the fourth century. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.117}
