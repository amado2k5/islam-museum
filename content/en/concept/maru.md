## Nature and selection
The Indians said it has several kinds: one sweet in smell, marmakhur, hotter and drier; another with less smell, called samusa, which is hot and soft; a third called white maru, balanced and with a gladdening power, which the author thinks is the ox-tongue (lisan al-thawr); a kind called marumahus, hot, dry and thinning; and a kind called mishbahar, which is cold according to the one who described it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.555} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.556}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, then it varies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.556}.

## Actions and properties
All its kinds break wind; fine, they dissolve bloating and phlegm and open cold obstructions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.556}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It is dropped with milk into the aching ear; mishbahar helps hot headache and the other kinds help cold headache; the fragrant kind causes headache, especially smelled over wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.556}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It dissolves phlegm in the stomach, helps stomach pain and strengthens it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.556}.

## Excretory organs
It strengthens the intestines; its seed, if fried, helps excoriation and dysentery, and unfried it loosens phlegm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.556}.
