## Nature and selection
Nature: roots of a papyrus-like plant that mostly grows in ponds and waters, with whitish knots on them; it has an unpleasant smell with a little fragrance, and is sharp and pungent. Galen says only the root is used, and its strength is near that of zurawand and iris. Dioscorides says its leaf is like iris leaf but longer and finer, its roots tangled and bent, and the good kind comes from Qinnasrin. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Choice: the best is the densest, fullest and sweetest in smell. Dioscorides says the best is white, dense, not worm-eaten or loose, full, and sweet-smelling. Temperament: hot and dry at the start of the second degree and toward the middle. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}

## Actions
It resolves bloating and wind, thins, cleanses without sting, and opens. Galen says it has a smell that is not unpleasant; by our perception it is unpleasant. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it clears the complexion and helps white patches and leukoderma. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Joints: helps spasm and muscle injury; its decoction is also used as a wash and as a drink. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Head: helps toothache, and is good for a heavy tongue. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Eyes: it thins a thickening of the cornea and helps white spots; its juice is especially useful, and it clears dim sight. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Chest: its decoction is good for pain of the side and chest. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Digestive organs: helps a cold liver and strengthens it, greatly strengthens the stomach, cleanses the stomach, and helps a hard spleen. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.453}
Excretory organs, book listing on the womb and desire: it helps colic and hernia; its decoction helps womb pain and stirs her desire; it helps gut pain and chafing from cold. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.454}
Poisons: helps the sting of insects. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.454}
Substitutes: for expelling wind, its use for liver and spleen; the book gives cumin and rhubarb as substitutes, without the proportions. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.454}
