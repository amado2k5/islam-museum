## Summary
Ibn Sina names two powers charged with keeping the body, which the physician serves: the natural nutritive power that replaces what dissolves from the body, and the animal pulsating power that replaces what dissolves from the spirit, then the transformative power that turns food into the likeness of what is fed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.202}.

## The two powers
Two powers are charged with this keeping, and the physician serves them. One is natural, the nutritive, which replaces what dissolves from the body, whose substance leans to the earthy and watery. The other is animal, the pulsating power, which replaces what dissolves from the spirit, whose substance is airy and fiery {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.202}.

## The transformative power
Since food is not actually like what is nourished, the transformative power was created to change foods into likeness of the things nourished, indeed into being food in act and in truth, and instruments were created for this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.202}.
