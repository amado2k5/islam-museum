## Summary
In the seventeenth chapter, on the causes that lie in retention and evacuation, Ibn Sina lists why what should be naturally evacuated is held back: weakness of the expelling power, strength of the retaining power, weakness of the digestive power, narrowness of the passages and obstructions in them, thickness, stickiness or abundance of the matter, loss of the sense of any need to expel it, or the turning of nature's power elsewhere, as in crises {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.138}.

## The illnesses that follow
When what ought to be evacuated is retained, illnesses follow: of the illnesses of composition, obstruction, slackness and moist spasm; of the illnesses of temperament, putrefaction, the change of the innate heat into fieriness, its extinction by long confinement followed by cold, and the overwhelming of the body by moisture; of the common illnesses, the splitting and bursting of vessels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.138-139}. He holds that indigestion is among the worst causes of illness, especially when it comes after a habit of emptiness, and that of the composite illnesses come tumours and pustules {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.139}.
