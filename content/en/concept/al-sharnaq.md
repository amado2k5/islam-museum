## Its definition, sign and those it befalls

Sharnaq is an excess of fatty matter arising in the upper eyelid; it weighs the lid down from opening and leaves it as though relaxed, a firm mass that does not move as a fatty wart does. It mostly befalls boys, the moist-tempered and those with much tearing and ophthalmia; among its signs is what appears on pressing the swelling ⁦(the sign line is incomplete in the read copy and is not interpreted)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.196}

## The treatment

It has a manual treatment which the book lays out as the excision of the matter; but a recent, mild sharnaq is often cured by dissolvent medicines without the hand's work. The book warns that caution in the excision is required lest it reach the depth and cause severe pain, an acute swelling and a hard obstructing remnant worse than the sharnaq itself; and something may be cut from the muscle that raises the lid, weakening its opening ⁦(the manual craft is not reproduced)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.196} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.197}
