## Summary
Blood-money in Sha'rawi's commentary on accidental killing: a believing slave is freed, and blood-money is paid to the victim's family in a believing setting. He holds that the kin group (aqila) pays it, not from the killer's ascendants or descendants, as a safeguard against a reaction in society {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2543}.

## What Sha'rawi says
He explains that accidental killing carries no retaliation because the killer did not intend it, and that the blood-money is spread over those harmed by the loss. If the kin know they will share the payment, they learn to protect others' rights {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2543}. He holds that the payment consoles the victim's family, and that forgiving it breeds affection between the families {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2545}.

## Three cases
Sha'rawi sets out three cases in the verse: a believer killed within a believing people, where a freed slave and blood-money are due; a believer killed among a people hostile to the Muslims, where only a freed slave is due, since there is no inheritance between them; and a person from a people bound to the Muslims by treaty, where blood-money and a freed slave are due {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2545}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2546}. This is his reading of the verse; the jurists' rules on these matters are not yet set out from a named source.
