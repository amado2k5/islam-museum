## Temperament
Compounded of hot and cold, both its substances subtle and the cold prevailing; the one with pungency is hotter, otherwise it is cold and moist, and cooking lessens its coldness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Actions and properties
Strongly drying, it prevents the flowing of matters inward, subtilises and cuts; it may be drunk or poured on bleeding if it is external and stops it, prevents a swelling where it is about to arise, helps digestion and opposes phlegm, and is beneficial to the bilious and harmful to the melancholic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Cosmetics
It is applied with honey on marks of blood and benefits, but overuse of it turns yellow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Swellings and pustules
It prevents the arising of swellings and the spread of gangrene, heals erysipelas eaten and as a pouring, benefits felon, and prevents namla and jamra {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Wounds and ulcers
When wool wetted with vinegar is put on wounds it prevents them from swelling, and it benefits the spread of spreading ulcers, mange and ringworm, and benefits a burn by fire faster than anything {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Joints
It is harmful to the nerve, and when applied with sulphur on gout it benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Organs of the head
Mixed with olive oil and rose oil and an unwashed wool wetted with it and put on the head, it benefits hot headache; it firms the gums, and likewise pouring and rinsing with it, especially with alum it benefits looseness of the teeth and their bleeding; the vapour of hot vinegar benefits "difficulty" and sharpens it (so printed, without saying difficulty of what), opens the obstructions of the sieve and dissolves ringing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Ocular organs
It is smeared with honey on the dark patch (kuhba) under the eye, and its habitual use weakens sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.714}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits the uvula, gargling with it prevents the flow of humour to the throat and it heals a fallen uvula, and it is sipped warmed for leeches and chronic cough and for orthopnoea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.715}

## Organs of nourishment
Fit for the hot and moist stomach, strengthening appetite and helping digestion, all of that for the "ladigha" of the stomach (so printed; its sense is not settled); the vapour of vinegar dissolves dropsy, and its habitual use may lead to dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.715}

## Excretory organs
It is given by enema, warmed with salt, for spreading ulcers of the intestines after soft enemas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.715}

## Poisons
It is poured on "al-nahus" (so printed; the sense is not settled), and benefits against opium and hemlock; vinegar made of the wild grape with salt benefits the bite of a rabid dog and the like; and it may be drunk warm after deadly drugs and benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.715}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it cools the womb; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.715}
