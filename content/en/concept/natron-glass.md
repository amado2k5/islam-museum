## Summary
Natron-type glass uses mineral natron as its flux; it was the basis of [[practice/islamic-glassmaking|glassmaking]] in the traditional centres of Egypt and the Levant in Roman and late antique times. {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 20} Scholars divide Mediterranean natron glass into ten major compositional groups, each the likely output of a discrete primary centre active for a limited period, on the assumption that its composition reflects the silica source. {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 20} {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 21}

## In the Islamic period
Natron glass production continued well into the Islamic period; the shift to [[concept/plant-ash-glass|plant-ash glass]] came only at the turn of the ninth century in Syria-Palestine, and considerably later in Egypt and the western Islamic world. {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 22} In Egypt it was still made until about 870; the last glass weight of "Egypt 2" composition dates to the first year of [[person/ahmad-ibn-tulun|Ahmad ibn Tulun]]'s governorship (868). {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 57} {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 234} Nadine Schibille links this to restrictions on natron extraction imposed by the director of finances [[person/ahmad-ibn-al-mudabbir|Ibn al-Mudabbir]], and explains the late transition in Egypt partly by its natural natron deposits. {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 234}

## The Damascus mosaics
Schibille and colleagues analysed 908 tesserae from the mosaics of the [[building/umayyad-mosque|Great Umayyad Mosque]] in Damascus, whose main group she calls "Egypt 1Ax", an Egyptian natron glass. {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 57} {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 58} She sees in this the steady demise of Levantine natron glassmaking against a still-flourishing Egyptian natron industry. {cite:schibille-islamic-glass-2022 p. 233}
