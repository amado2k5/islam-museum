## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat moves from individual spending to spending public funds, which are part of the yield of production, contributions taken from individuals to support public interests, or funds coming from charity or zakat {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 120}. He holds that these funds must be spent in their proper channels, because their impact touches God, humanity, and material resources together, and embezzling public funds is a major crime punished by both God and people in this world and the next {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 120}.

## Controls
The author states that the controls on spending in the Islamic economy differ from other systems because their goal is the human being and human happiness, not profit or material production alone {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 120}. Among these controls are adhering to the bounds of the lawful and unlawful, focusing on interests that serve the public good, and not entering into exploitative projects or companies regardless of the profits {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 120}.

## Priorities
The author lists priorities for public spending, including: caring for orphans, the vulnerable, and widows, and creating jobs for the poor no matter how modest; transport, airports, and roads; supporting knowledge through schools, universities, and research centers; supporting loans for productive projects; healthcare, prevention, and treatment at nominal prices; and finally protecting the homeland {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 121}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 122}. The author regards defending the homeland as a condition for upholding the principles of the Qur'an, including justice, freedom, and God's law {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 121}.
