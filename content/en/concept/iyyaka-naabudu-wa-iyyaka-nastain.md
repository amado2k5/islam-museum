## Summary
In the fifth verse of the Fatiha the address shifts from the third person to the second. Sha'rawi holds that this places the worshipper before God, and that placing "You" first confines worship and seeking help to God alone. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.77} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.78}

## Worship and prostration
Sha'rawi holds that worship is submission to God's method of doing and not doing, and that prostration is the highest form of it, since a person sets the most honoured part of himself on the ground. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.78}

## Seeking help from the Strong
Sha'rawi holds that one who seeks help from another human receives help bounded by that human's humanity, whereas seeking help from the Living, the Self-Subsisting, frees the believer from the humiliation of this world. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.83}

## Sight of faith
Sha'rawi distinguishes eye-sight from faith-sight and holds the latter stronger, as an insight, citing the hadith of ihsan which he attributes to Muslim. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.74} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.75}

## Author's view
This is Sha'rawi's reading and is attributed to him. The scan gives no other grammatical or exegetical view on the shift or the fronting, and none has been checked.

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "iyyaka na'budu"
Al-Tabari reads "iyyaka na'budu" as "to You, O God, we abase ourselves, submit and humble ourselves, acknowledging Your lordship and no other's". He gives a report from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak, that it means "to You we declare the oneness, and fear and hope", but prefers the sense of humility, since worship in Arab speech begins with abasement, as a trodden path is called "mu'abbad". The editor grades the chain as weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 160} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 161}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "wa-iyyaka nasta'in"
He reads it as: "and to You, our Lord, we seek help in our worship and in our obedience in all our affairs, and not anyone else". A believer asks God for help in obedience for what remains of his life, not for what has passed, and God grants this as favour and kindness. The consensus of Muslims that "allahumma inna nasta'inuka" is right and "allahumma la tajur 'alayna" is wrong answers those who hold that the affair is delegated to humans. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 162} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 163}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: the order and the repeated "iyyaka"
Al-Tabari compares the order of the two clauses to a man who says "you met my need and were kind to me" or "you were kind to me and met my need": the order is the same, since each entails the other. He holds the repeated "iyyaka" more eloquent in Arabic, because the pronoun that stands alone before the verb is repeated with each verb, and rejects the comparison with a line of 'Adi ibn Zayd, since "bayna" is not repeated with a single term. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 164} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 165} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 166}
