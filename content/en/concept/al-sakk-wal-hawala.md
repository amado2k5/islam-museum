## Overview
The *sakk* (cheque) and the *hawala* (bill of exchange) were two financial instruments in the Islamic world, as presented by Hasan Ibrahim Hasan in *Tarikh al-Islam*. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.400-401}

## The sakk
The sakk is much like today's cheque. The author cites al-Khwarizmi in *Mafatih al-ulum* that it gathered the names of those entitled to payment, their number, and what each was due. The caliph, sultan, or prince then signed at its foot to authorise payment of these stipends. The word is still used in European languages. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.400-401}

## The hawala
The Arabic *hawala* remains the origin of the word *awal* in Urdu. As evidence of how widely it was used, the boatmen who ferried the Seljuk sultan [[person/malikshah|Malikshah]] and his armies across the river Jayhun received bills of exchange from his vizier [[person/nizam-al-mulk|Nizam al-Mulk]]. They collected their value from the Seljuk agent in Antioch. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim p.400-401}
