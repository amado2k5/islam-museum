## Overview
The *isnad* (chain of transmission) is the series of narrators leading to the text of a hadith, in the form "so-and-so related to us from so-and-so," until the report ends at the Prophet or a Companion. {cite:sahih-muslim}

## Its function
By it the scholars of hadith knew the soundness of a report from its weakness. They studied the narrators for their probity and precision, and they looked into whether the chain was connected or broken. {cite:sahih-muslim}

## In the introduction to Sahih Muslim
Muslim opened his *Sahih* by speaking of the greatness of the isnad and of its protection from falsehood. It has been said that it is one of the distinctions of this community. {cite:sahih-muslim}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's «Difaf al-Kawthar»
At a board, [[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] explains concealment (tadlis) in the chain of transmission. He says tadlis in the language means darkness, or the mixing of light and darkness, and technically a defect in the chain that the narrator hides, which the critic, had he seen it, would have used to weaken the hadith. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:04:00} He lists six kinds: tadlis of the chain, tadlis of levelling (taswiya, also called tajwid), tadlis of teachers' names, tadlis of places, tadlis by cutting off, and tadlis by conjunction; and he estimates that tadlis of the chain alone makes up about ninety-five per cent of what occurs; this is his estimate. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:05:00} He defines it as a narrator relating from a teacher he did hear from something he did not hear from him. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:06:00}

To explain it he sets out high and low chains: a high chain ('uluww) has fewer intermediaries between the narrator and the Prophet, and the fewer the men the nobler the chain; he compares the Prophet to a light-giving lamp whose light grows stronger the nearer one climbs. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:12:00} He gives the example of Malik from Nafi' from Ibn Umar, two men between Malik and the Prophet; al-Shafi'i from Malik, three; Ahmad from al-Shafi'i, four; al-Bukhari from Ahmad, five; and he says hadith scholars would sell their houses and borrow to travel to the ends of the earth for a higher chain. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:14:00} Tadlis, he says, has one of two motives: to keep the chain high, or, worse, because the dropped narrator is weak and naming him would get the report rejected; the concealer says "from" (an) and not "he told me", for if he said "he told me" of what he did not hear he would be a liar. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:17:00}

His board example is a hadith on the merit of prayer with the tooth-stick (siwak) that [[person/ibn-ishaq|Muhammad ibn Ishaq]], author of the Sira, narrates from Mu'awiya ibn Yahya al-Sadafi from al-Zuhri from [[person/urwa-ibn-al-zubayr|Urwa]] from [[person/aisha|A'isha]]: Ibn Ishaq drops al-Sadafi and says "from al-Zuhri". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:20:30} He says Urwa is one of the Seven Jurists, al-Zuhri trustworthy, eminent and an imam, and Ibn Ishaq truthful and fair in hadith, a man the scholars differed over and whom he himself judges usable as proof; {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:22:30} al-Sadafi, by contrast, is abandoned (matruk), very weak, so the critic takes the chain for good when it is worthless. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:26:00} His rule is that a concealer's report is not accepted when he says "from", and is accepted when he states hearing explicitly: "he told me", "he informed me", "I heard". {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny dhifaf-al-kawtar/ep13 00:28:00}
