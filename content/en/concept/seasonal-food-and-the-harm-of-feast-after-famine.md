## Summary
Ibn Sina advises hot food in winter and cold or only slightly warm food in summer, never so hot or cold as to be unbearable; he warns against a feast in plenty followed by hunger in scarcity, and the reverse, and says he saw people die who filled themselves when food widened after a famine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## Hot and cold
In winter one eats food that is hot in actuality, in summer food that is cold or only slightly warm, without the heat or cold reaching what cannot be borne {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## Fullness after hunger
He says nothing is worse than satiety in a time of plenty followed by hunger in a time of dearth, and that the reverse is worse still; he saw people whom food had left short in a famine fill themselves when it grew plentiful, and die {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## Fullness
He adds that extreme fullness is deadly in every case, whether from food or drink; many a man filled himself to excess, choked and died {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.
