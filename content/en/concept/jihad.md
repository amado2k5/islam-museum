## Overview

In fiqh, jihad is fighting the enemy. It is obligatory in general and personally obligatory in certain situations. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6583} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6588}

## Calling to Islam before fighting

When Muslims enter enemy territory they call the unbelievers to Islam first; the schools differ on whether the call is obligatory. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6602} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6603}

## Who goes out

When the two lines meet, those present must stand firm. A son needs his parents' leave unless the jihad has become personally obligatory, and women are discouraged from going out except for need. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6590} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6592} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6597}

## Rules in war

Treachery, embezzling booty and mutilation are forbidden. The jurists allow killing animals and destroying property when needed, and differ on killing the elderly. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6608} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6610} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6615}

## Sieges and truces

When the imam besieges a fortress he must persist until its people accept Islam, pay a sum, or accept an agreed judge. A truce is allowed only for a fixed period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6612} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6613} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6623}
