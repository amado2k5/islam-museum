## Summary
A question in the principles of religion: by what proof is belief established? Shaltut presents that the faith required needs a decisive proof; that a rational argument which ends in sense perception or necessary knowledge yields certainty; and that transmitted text yields certainty when its transmission is mass-attested (mutawatir) and its wording is explicit in meaning. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 89} {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 90}

## Solitary reports
The book notes that the majority of the careful scholars among the theologians and jurists, such as al-Bazdawi, al-Ghazali and al-Isnawi, hold that a solitary report (khabar al-ahad) yields probability, not certainty, so belief cannot be established by it; and that the lawgiver allowed probability in practical rulings. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 100} {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 101}

## Consensus
The book cites al-Shafi'i and Ibn Hazm for the view that the consensus that counts is one with no dissent, and that in matters of belief it is what is known from the religion by necessity. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 112} {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 113}

## Note
The book sets out the position of the careful scholars, and gives a differing view without naming all who hold it. This disagreement remains open in the Sunni tradition. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 91}
