## Summary
Throbbing pain is caused by a hot swelling, not a cold one, since a cold swelling does not hurt unless it turns hot; it arises when the neighbouring organ is sensitive and arteries pulse constantly nearby {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.150}.

## The cause
The cause of throbbing pain is a hot swelling, not a cold one, since a cold one, hard or soft, does not hurt unless it turns hot. The editor's note in the printed edition explains the throbbing as pain from the pulse of the arteries {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.150}.

## The explanation
Throbbing pain comes from a hot swelling when the organ next to it is sensitive and has arteries near it that always beat. While that organ is sound it feels the artery's movement only deeply; once it is pained and swollen, the beating itself hurts {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.150}.
