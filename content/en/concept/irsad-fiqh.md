## Irsad (assigning the revenue of bayt al-mal)
Irsad in the lexicon means preparation: one says he prepared the matter for him. For the jurists it is the imam's assignment of the yield of some bayt al-mal lands to some of its expenditures. The Hanafis also use the term for assigning the revenue of a waqf to pay the debts it incurred for necessary repair. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2188}
There are two views of its real nature. The first holds that irsad is not waqf, as the Hanafis state explicitly and the Shafi'is seem to imply, because one of the conditions for a valid waqf fails: the property must be owned by the donor at the time of the waqf. The one who assigns (the mursid) is the imam or his deputy, who owns nothing of what he assigns. Ibn Abidin says that irsad by the sultan is not a waqf at all, since the sultan does not own it, but a fixing of some of bayt al-mal for some of its entitled persons. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2188}
The second holds that irsad is in essence a waqf, because no condition of waqf fails in it: the sultan who sets aside some bayt al-mal property acts as agent for the Muslims, as the donor's agent would. On this view waqf and irsad do not differ in prior ownership, and they differ only in that irsad is made only by the imam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2188} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2189}
Irsad differs from iqta' (the grant of land by the imam). A grant of ownership gives the grantee the land itself, which irsad does not. A grant of usufruct or of kharaj differs because irsad is perpetual, while the imam may withdraw a usufruct grant from its holder and give it to another; so iqta' is individual and personal, while irsad is general and serves the community. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2189}
Irsad differs from hima. Hima is the imam's setting apart of a dead land itself for the need of others, such as the grazing of the camels of the poll-tax and of alms and the needs of weak Muslims; irsad is the assignment of the yield of some bayt al-mal lands to some of its expenditures. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2189}
Irsad is lawful by the agreement of the scholars, either because it is a waqf and so subject to its rules, or because it secures a public benefit for the Muslims by a lawful means: bayt al-mal property reached the Muslims without fighting, and its expenditure is whatever serves their interests, including scholars and judges. Securing the Muslims' interests is a duty of the imam, so where it cannot be secured without irsad, irsad is obligatory. The Hanafi jurist Ali al-Aqdi said that there is no doubt in its lawfulness, since it brings the right to those entitled to it, and that its obligation is agreed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2189}
Irsad requires an assignor (mursid, with the kasra on the sad), an assigned property (mursad, with the fatha), the purpose on which it is assigned, and a formula. The conditions of each pillar follow on the next page. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2189}
