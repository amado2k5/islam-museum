## Summary
In the nineteenth chapter, the last on the pulse, Ibn Sina sums up the ways in which things contrary to nature change the form of the pulse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.

## Three ways
These things change the pulse in one of three ways: by the bad temperament they bring about, the pulse of each temperament being already known; by pressing power, so that the pulse becomes uneven, and if the pressure is very great without order or measure, the presser being any material excess, swelling or not; or by dissolving power, so that the pulse becomes weak, as severe pain and strongly dissolving psychic pains do {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.182}.
