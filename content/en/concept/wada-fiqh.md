## Summary
Wada' is the verbal noun of taking leave, the seeing-off of a traveller; the one who departs is the one taken leave of, since he is the one parting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16625} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16626}

## What is said to the traveller
A traveller is advised to take leave of his family, brethren and friends and ask their prayers, and the one who takes leave says: 'I entrust you to God, who does not lose what is entrusted to Him.' {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16626}
Ibn Umar reported that the traveller is told: may God keep your faith, your trust and the last of your deeds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16626} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16627}

## Two rak'as before leaving
It is recommended to pray two rak'as on deciding to leave, at home or in the mosque of one's town. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16628} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16629}

## Taking leave of the fighters
The Prophet took leave of an army with prayer for them and walked with them to the Baqi' al-Gharqad before sending them off. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16628}

## Handshake and kissing
It is reported that the Prophet took a man's hand when he took leave of him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16627}
The Hanafis hold that a man kissing another man's mouth is forbidden; the Shafi'is recommend kissing a returning traveller of the same sex. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16627} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16628}
