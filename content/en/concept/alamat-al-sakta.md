## How it differs from coma
The difference between apoplexy and coma is that the apoplectic snores and a lesion enters his breath, while the comatose is not so; the comatose comes gradually from heavy sleep into the coma, while the apoplectic has it come suddenly (see the concept 'the difference between coma and apoplexy') {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132}

## The warning
Apoplexy is preceded in most times by swelling of the jugulars, vertigo, giddiness, darkness of sight, twitching in the whole body, grinding of the teeth in sleep, laziness and heaviness; often the urine is dusky and black with starchy and bran-like sediment; if this recurs it warns of apoplexy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132}

## By the cause
What comes of a hurt, a blow, a fall or a partner organ is known from the principles already repeated; what comes of a swelling is never without some fever, from the signs of swellings already mentioned; what is of blood is shown by the oft-mentioned signs of blood — the face reddened and the eyes very red, the jugulars and neck veins distended, venesection long past and black-bile-generating food taken before; what is of phlegm is shown by the build and the colour of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.132}

## Sign that the ventricles are involved
That the obstruction in apoplexy comprises the ventricles may be shown by this: were it in the rear ventricle alone, sensation at the front of the head and the face would not have to fail {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.131}
