## Summary
Al-Battani opens his zij with a table of contents counting fifty-seven chapters: the foreword, arithmetic, the chords of the circle, the obliquity and the risings of the signs; then the properties of climes, shadow, altitude and hours; then the fixed stars; then the motion of the Sun, Moon and five planets, the eclipses and the calendars; and he closes with two instruments and a final chapter. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.5-9}.

## Chapters 1 to 17
The table lists as chapter 1 the foreword, chapter 2 the division of the circle with multiplication, roots and division, chapter 3 the sizes of the chords of the circle and the half-chords in the tables, chapter 4 the obliquity of the zodiac from the equator of the day, and chapter 5 the risings of the signs in the right sphere. Chapter 6 treats the parallels to the equator and the inhabited earth, chapter 7 the rising and setting points of winter and summer, chapters 8 and 9 the altitude of the pole and the longest day, chapter 10 altitude and shadow, chapter 11 their azimuth, chapter 12 the meridian line, chapter 13 the risings of the signs in each country, chapter 14 latitudes by observation, chapter 15 the Sun's noon altitude, chapter 16 the hours elapsed from the Sun's measure and chapter 17 altitude from the hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.5-6}.

## Chapters 18 to 41
The table gives chapters 18 to 25 to the fixed stars and the planets: their distance from the equator, the half of a star's day arc, the degree rising with it, the hours of the night, its altitude and azimuth. Chapter 26 concerns distances between stars, chapter 27 the length of the solar year and the Sun's mean motion, chapter 28 the Sun's anomaly, chapter 29 the inequality of days with their nights, chapter 30 the lunar spheres and the causes of both eclipses, and chapter 31 the spheres of the five planets. Then come the calendar chapters: 32 on the Arab, Roman, Persian and Coptic eras, 33 on the Sun's place, 34 on the equated hours and 35 on the ascendant and twelve houses; and the lunar chapters 36 to 41: the Moon's true place, the two nodes, its latitude, parallax, its distance and the sighting of the crescent. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.7-8}.

## Chapters 42 to 57
Chapters 42 to 44 cover the conjunctions and oppositions of Sun and Moon and the lunar and solar eclipses by calculation and table. Chapters 45 to 50 take the positions of the five planets, their stations and retrogradations, latitudes, risings and disappearances, the figures the fixed stars make with the Sun, and the distances, diameters and sizes of the bodies. Chapter 51 concerns the motion of the fixed stars by observation, chapter 52 what the talisman-makers say of a motion of the sphere, chapter 53 the year transfers, chapter 54 the conjunctions by latitude, chapter 55 the risings of the signs between the cardinal points, chapter 56 a flat marble dial for the hours of the day, and chapter 57 the close of the book, a sphere-shaped instrument and the two observing instruments. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.8-9}.
