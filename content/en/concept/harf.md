## Nature and selection
Nature: Dioscorides says the best harf seen is from the land of Babylon. Its strength is like that of mustard and radish seed, or of mustard and rocket seed together. Its leaf is less in its actions because of its moisture; once dry it nears its kind and almost equals it. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Temperament: hot and dry in the third degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}

## Actions
Warming, dissolving and ripening, with softening; it dries the pus of scabies. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it holds falling hair when drunk and applied. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Swellings: good for phlegmy swelling; with salt water as a poultice for boils. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Wounds and ulcers: helps ulcerating scabies and the sores called qawabi; with honey it treats the kind the book calls shahdiya, and it removes the malignancy of the Persian burn. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Joints: helps sciatica when drunk and as a poultice with vinegar and barley gruel; it may be given by enema for sciatica with benefit, especially when it moves something mixed with blood; it helps weakness of all the nerves. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Organs of the chest: cleans the lung, helps asthma, and enters asthma remedies and the electuaries made for asthma, because it cuts and thins. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Organs of digestion: warms the stomach and the liver, helps thickness of the spleen, especially when applied as a poultice with honey; it is bad for the stomach. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Excretory organs: it increases sexual power, expels worms and brings the menses. Labelled listing, set aside for the founder's ruling: it expels the foetus. The roasted kind binds; it helps colic and dissolves wind in the intestines. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
Poisons: drunk and applied as a poultice with honey, it helps the bites of vermin; fumigation with it drives vermin away. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.477}
Measured amounts for drinking are left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.476}
