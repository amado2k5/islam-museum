## Nature and selection
Nature: well known. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Choice: the best is the fresh kind, of good colour and pleasant smell, with few white threads, full, sound, quick to dye, not sticky and not crumbling. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Temperament: hot and dry; the heat is in the second degree and the dryness in the first. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}

## Actions
Actions: astringent, dissolving and ripening because of its binding, persuasive astringency; its heat is moderate and it opens. Galen says its heat is stronger than its astringency and its oil is warming. Al-Khuzi says it never changes a humour, but keeps it dry, corrects putrefaction and strengthens the viscera. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: drinking it improves the complexion. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Swellings and pustules: it dissolves swellings, and is applied to redness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Head organs: it causes headache and harms the head; it is drunk with maybakhtaj for hangover; it dulls the senses when given in wine and intoxicates; it helps hot swelling in the ear. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Eye organs: it clears the sight, stops defluxions to the eye, helps a film over the eye, and is used as kohl for blueness acquired from diseases. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Chest organs: it strengthens and gladdens the heart; the delirious and the one with the pleurisy-like fever smell it to sleep, especially its oil; it eases breathing and strengthens the organs of breath. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Digestion organs: it nauseates and lessens appetite by opposing the acid in the stomach, but it strengthens the stomach and liver because of its heat, tanning and astringency. Some say saffron is good for the spleen. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Excretory organs: it stirs desire and drives urine; it helps hardness of the womb with oil; and someone claims he gave it in a long labour and she gave birth at once. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Poisons: it is said that a measured quantity kills by sudden exhilaration; the quantity is left out as a measure. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
Substitutes: costus and the bark of cassia; the proportions are left out as measured quantities. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.464}
