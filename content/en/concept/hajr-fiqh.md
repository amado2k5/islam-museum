## Summary
Hajr in the lexical sense is the opposite of joining; technically it is leaving what must be attended to, and one person's separation from another in body, speech or heart. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16814} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16815}

## Abandoning what is forbidden
One is commanded to abandon what God has forbidden, on the saying 'the emigrant is one who abandons what God has forbidden'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16816}

## Shunning a Muslim brother
It is forbidden to shun a Muslim brother beyond three nights; most jurists allow shunning for three nights, and some count shunning beyond that among the major sins. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16817} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16818}
The better of two who shun each other is the one who greets first. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16822} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16823}

## Letters and greetings
The schools differ on whether shunning ends by written messages to an absent person, and on whether it ends by greeting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16820} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16821} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16822}

## A non-Muslim and a rebellious wife
Shunning a non-Muslim is allowed, and a husband may shun a rebellious wife in bed until she obeys. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16823} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16824}

## Openly and secretly sinful people
Shunning the openly sinful or innovating is recognised as correction; the jurists give several rulings on it, and three on the one who sins in secret. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16824} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16825} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16826} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16827} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16828}

## In Abd al-Rahman Mansour's programme 'Ahalina'
[[person/abd-al-rahman-mansour|Abd al-Rahman Mansour]] counts shunning among the punishments of upbringing in his programme [[work/ahaleena|'Ahalina']], after advice, turning away and a stern rebuke: a parent may shun his child if he knows it will work with him, for example when the child abandons prayer or, in adolescence, uses indecent words. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:05:30} He sets its limit at three days, citing the hadith that it is not lawful for a Muslim to shun his brother for more than three, and saying that a son is first of all a Muslim brother. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:06:00} Shunning, he adds, may also be used with a friend, a brother or a wife. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:05:00} Of his own children, he says, one is brought back from a fault by shunning and another needs a stern word. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep12 00:02:30} His order of punishments is set out in [[concept/tadib-al-awlad|disciplining children]].
