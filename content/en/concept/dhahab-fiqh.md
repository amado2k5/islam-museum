## Summary
Gold is a known metal. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8487}
Men may not wear gold ornaments or a gold ring except from necessity; women may wear gold ornaments. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8488} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8491}
The majority allow a gold tooth, and a gold nose by a text, while the Shafi'is forbid a gold finger made only for ornament. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8489} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8490} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8491}

## Vessels, Qur'an and mosques
Eating and drinking from gold or silver vessels is forbidden, and the majority forbid keeping them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8491} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8492}
Decorating books with gold is forbidden except for the Qur'an, where the schools differ. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8494}

## Zakat and exchange
Zakat is due on gold by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8494}
Gold may be sold for gold only hand to hand and weight for weight, and gold may be sold for silver with unequal weights hand to hand. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8494} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8495}

## Theft
For the amputation of a thief the majority require a quarter of a gold dinar or more; the Hanafis require a full dinar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8497}
