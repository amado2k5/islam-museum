## Summary
Ribh is gain in trade. Its relatives are growth (nama') and income (ghalla), and growth is the wider term. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8607} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8608}

## Lawful and unlawful profit
Lawful profit comes from a lawful transaction such as sale, mudaraba or partnership, and is lawful by consensus when the contract's conditions are kept. Unlawful profit comes from a forbidden transaction, such as riba or gambling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8608}

## Profit on another's property in one's hand
The Hanafis hold that profit does not become lawful for one who dealt with a usurped or deposited property; the Malikis and the preferred Shafi'i view give it to the one who dealt, and the Hanbalis give it to the owner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8608} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8609}

## Profit in mudaraba and partnership
All agree that fixing the share of profit between owner and worker is a condition for the validity of a mudaraba. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8609} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8610} In partnership profit follows what the partners agree, and the jurists differ on whether unequal profit may follow equal capital. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8610}

## Zakat on trade profit
Profit on trade goods is added to the principal within the year for the Malikis, the preferred Shafi'i view and the Hanbalis; Abu Hanifa has each new gain run its own year. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8610} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8611}

## Where to look further
