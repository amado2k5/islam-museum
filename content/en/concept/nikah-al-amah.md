## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi sets out the conditions for marrying a believing slave woman: her owner's permission, a dower paid in the customary way, and marriage to a free woman being preferred. He says the ruling aims to end slavery. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2121}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2125}

## Permission and dower
He says the slave woman is married with her owner's permission, so that the owner knows the marriage right has passed to the husband while ownership of her person stays with him, and that her dower is paid in the customary way as the price of enjoyment and as a mark of her dignity. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2121}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2125}

## Need and fear of sin
He holds that a man able to marry a free woman does not need a slave woman, and explains "al-'anat" as hardship and strain. A footnote lists conditions that some jurists require, without naming them. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2130}

## Penalty and children
He says a slave woman who commits zina after marriage receives half the penalty of a free married woman, and that a child follows the mother's status, so that a child of a slave mother is a slave. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2122}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2125}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2126}
