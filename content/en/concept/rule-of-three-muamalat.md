## Summary
The rule of transactions is a short chapter in [[person/al-khwarizmi|al-Khwarizmi]]'s [[work/al-jabr-wal-muqabala|Book of al-Jabr and al-Muqabala]], stating that all people's dealings, buying, selling, exchange, hire and the like, rest on four numbers spoken by the questioner: the rated quantity (*musa''ar*), the rate (*si'r*), the price (*thaman*) and the priced quantity (*muthamman*). {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.69}

## The rule
The rated quantity is "opposed" to the price and the rate to the priced quantity; three of the four numbers are always known and one is unknown, the one asked about with "how much". {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.69} Multiply the two known opposed numbers together and divide the product by the third known number to obtain the unknown. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.69}

## Examples
- "Ten for six: how much do you get for four?" Ten is the rated quantity, six the rate and four the price; ten times four is forty, divided by six gives six and two-thirds, the priced quantity. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.70}
- "Ten for eight: what is the price of four?" Four times eight is thirty-two, divided by ten gives three and a fifth. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.70}
- A hired man paid ten dirhams a month works six days: the month of thirty days is the rated quantity, ten the rate and six days the priced quantity; ten times six divided by thirty gives two dirhams. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.70}

Al-Khwarizmi says this is how people deal with one another in exchange, measures and weights. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.70} In the manuscript's margin two lines of verse set the rule out for goods measured or weighed; the editors of the Cairo edition quote them. {cite:khwarizmi-jabr-cairo-1939 p.69}
