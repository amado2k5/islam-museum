## Summary
After the larynx Ibn Sina treats the muscles of the pharynx, the hyoid bone and the tongue: two pairs draw the pharynx down, three pairs belong to the hyoid, and nine muscles move the tongue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.66-67}.

## The pharynx
The pharynx has two pairs that draw it down: one mentioned under the larynx, and one growing from the sternum, rising to join the hyoid bone and then the pharynx. The throat proper has as its muscles the two nughnughatan, two muscles at the throat that help swallowing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.66-67}.

## The hyoid bone
The hyoid has muscles of its own and muscles shared with other organs. Its own are three pairs: one coming from the sides of the jaws and joining the straight line on the bone, drawing it toward the jaws; one arising under the chin and passing beneath the tongue to the bone's upper end, also drawing it to the sides of the jaws; and one arising from the styloid processes by the ears and joining the lower end of the straight line {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.67}.

## The tongue
Nine muscles move the tongue: two transverse from the styloid processes joined to its sides; two long ones from the upper parts of the hyoid joined to the root of the tongue; two oblique ones from the low rib of the hyoid passing between the long and the transverse; two flattening and turning ones beneath these, joined to the whole jawbone; and a single muscle joining tongue and hyoid and drawing one to the other {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.67}.
