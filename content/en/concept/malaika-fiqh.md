## Summary
The malak in the lexicon is a subtle luminous body, and "mala'ika" is its plural, and their dwelling is the heavens. Belief in them is a pillar of the creed, and denying their existence is unbelief by the agreement of the Muslims. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14974} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14975}

## The lexical and technical origin
- "Malak" in the language is used for a messenger and for the strong, and from it "muluk" and "mala'ika" is the plural of "malak" {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14974}.
- In the technical sense: a creature of God made of light, dwelling in heaven, who does not eat or drink, does not marry, does not sleep, worships God and obeys Him {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14975}.
- The jurists distinguish the angels from humans in that the angels were created of light, neither eat nor drink, and worship God, while the jinn share with them the ability to hide and to take shapes {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14974}.

## Their creation and attributes
- They were created before Adam; of their attributes they neither eat nor drink, nor sleep, nor marry, and they have the ability to take human forms by God's permission {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14975} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14976}.
- They have wings, measures and command as in the Qur'anic description {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- They do not disobey God in what He commands and do what they are commanded {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- Each of them has a known station {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## Their worship and works
- Their relation to God is one of servanthood and obedience; they do not disdain His worship, and the angels do not descend except by His command {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14976}.
- God may have charged angels with every kind of creature; their chiefs are Jibril with revelation, Mika'il with rain, and Israfil with blowing the trumpet {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14976} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14977}.
- They glorify night and day without flagging, and seek forgiveness for the believers {cite:tanzil-quran}.
- They record the deeds, and are charged with guarding the servants and their destinies {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## Belief in the angels
- The Tahawiyya creed states: "we believe in the angels and the prophets and the Books sent down upon the messengers" {cite:aqida-tahawiyya §72}.
- Ibn Abi al-Izz's commentary elaborates the meaning of belief in them and their attributes {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz}.
- Denying their existence or calling them liars in what the Prophet brought is unbelief in the majority view {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.

## Their excellence
- The jurists agree that the prophets are the best of creation, and that the Companions, the Successors and the righteous are better than the rest of the angels {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.
- They differ on preferring the rest of the angels over ordinary Muslims: Abu Hanifa said ordinary Muslims are better, and Muhammad and Abu Yusuf said the angels are better {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.

## Reviling the angels
- The jurists agree that whoever reviles the angels, mocks them, calls them liars in what they brought, or denies their existence is killed as an unbeliever {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.
- They differ on whether he is asked to repent: the majority say he is, and the Malikis say, in the better-known view, that he is not {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14978}.

## In the context of the unseen
- They were created of light and do not disobey God; among them the Throne-bearers, the noble recorders, and Jibril the trustee of revelation {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14975}.
- "The angels" in the Qur'an are "messengers" in places because they carry His command {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir}.

## Notes for researchers
