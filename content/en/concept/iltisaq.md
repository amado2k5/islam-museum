## Definition
Iltisaq and iltizaq have the same meaning: to stick to a thing. Iltisaq is the joining of one thing to another with no gap, by stickiness, cohesion or contact. The jurists use the word in the same sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3312}

## Ruling
Adhesion may happen by itself, as when houses join or leaves stick to water, or by intent, as when a splint is fixed to a wound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3312}
- A house joining another on a through street gives the neighbour priority of pre-emption for the Hanafis; the other schools do not recognise pre-emption by neighbourhood. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3312}
- A splint fixed to a wound allows wiping over it in ablution. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3312}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3313}
- Some adhesion is obligatory, such as the forehead touching the ground in prostration; some is forbidden, such as two men or two women under one cloth without a barrier. It is disliked with a barrier and without intent of pleasure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3312}

## Related places
The entry touches covering the nakedness in prayer, damage and injury, pre-emption, the dead land revived, and the joining of two parts of the body in purification. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3313}
