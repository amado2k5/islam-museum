## The obstructing temperament
The corrupt temperament is hot, cold, moist or dry; it seems the hot does not bar the effect of sensation unless it reaches the utmost, as in the wasted and the consumptive, who with their heat keep motion and sensation, and the dry is near it in ruling; what bars sensation and motion in most cases is cold with its numbness, and moisture may well make the organ prone to dullness, for among the causes of lapsed motion are cold or moisture without matter — easily made good by warming, and it does not cover most of the body but befalls one organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.137}

## Retention and its causes
Most palsy and relaxation come by the spirit's retention; retention's cause is obstruction or the parting of the pores and passages leading to the organs by cutting; obstruction is by contraction of the pores, or by obstruction from a blocking humour, or by a swelling joining both, so the cause of the relaxation and palsy that cuts the spirit off from the organs is pore contraction, repletion, swelling, or loosening of the single texture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.137}

## Contraction and compression
Pore contraction may come from an outside ligature that can be undone, so the lapse of sensation and motion is an accident passing with the untying; it may come from severe compression as in a blow or fall, and as when the vertebrae tilt and break to one side, right or left, pressing the nerve issuing there, or forward and backward, where what befalls is mostly stretching rather than pressure, for the meeting of the vertebrae fore and aft is not upon the nerve exits, the exits of the nerves not being on the fore and aft sides {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.137}

## Matters, swelling and cutting
The pores may also contract from grossness of the organ's substance, or the flowing moistures that serve it grow gross and run through all the interstices of the nerves or halt at their roots and branches and block the spirit's path; swelling befalls the nerves' roots and branches and blocks the passages; a lengthwise cut of a nerve does not harm sensation and motion, while a crosswise cut bars them from the organs fed by the passages now severed; the spinal cord is like the brain in dividing into two parts, growing out of the brain's two parts, so it is not strange that nature preserves one half and drives the matter into the weaker half, the one more receptive, or the one the blow and shock struck {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.137} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.138}
