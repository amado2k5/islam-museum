## Summary
Ibn Sina says the aim in treating ulcers is drying, and distinguishes the clean from the foul, the simple from the compound, the small from the large, and what has lost skin from what has lost flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.

## The aim
It should be known that the aim in treating ulcers is drying: a clean ulcer is dried only, and a foul one is treated with sharp eating drugs such as calcitar, vitriol, arsenic and lime; if these do not succeed, fire is needed. The remedy compounded of verdigris, wax and oil cleanses by its verdigris and checks excess stinging by its oil and wax, so it is a moderate remedy in this matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.

## The small ulcer
Every ulcer is either simple or compound. A simple one, if small and with nothing eaten from its middle, should have its two lips brought together and bandaged after taking care that nothing, oil or dust, falls between them, and it will join; so too the large one from whose substance nothing has gone and whose parts can be closed one on the other {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.

## The large ulcer
A large one that cannot be closed, whether a cleft or a space filled with pus, or from which some of the substance of the organ has gone, is treated by drying. If only skin has gone, what seals is needed: by itself the astringents, and by accident the sharp drugs when a small known amount is used, such as vitriol and calcitar, which help drying and the making of an eschar more, though a large amount eats and enlarges the ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.

## Growing flesh
If what has gone is flesh, as in deep ulcers, we should not hasten to seal; care is first taken to grow flesh, and flesh grows only with what does not go much beyond the first degree in drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.
