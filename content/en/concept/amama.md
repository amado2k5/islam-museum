## Summary
In the lexicon, amama is a cloth wound round the head {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11807}.

## Forms
The turban is wound with its end let fall on the back, or wound without being tucked under the chin, which is called i'tijar {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11808}.

## The Prophet's turban
It is described as black, with its end let fall between the shoulders, and he is reported to have worn a Qatari cloth {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11809}.

## Prayer, ablution and shroud
Prostration on the fold of a turban is disliked unless there is an excuse. The Hanafis refuse wiping over the turban in ablution; the Malikis allow it where removal would harm, and the Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11811} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11812} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11813}. A shirt and a turban in a shroud are recommended by Abu Hanifa and Malik {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11813}.

## Ihram and discretionary penalty
A turban is forbidden to one in ihram, and removing the turban may be a discretionary penalty {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11813} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11814}.

## Where to look further
Wiping is treated under «mash», prostration under «sujud», and ihram under «ihram» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11812}.
