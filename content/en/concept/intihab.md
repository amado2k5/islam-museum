## Definition
Intihab in the lexical sense is taking a thing by raid and plunder; the jurists define it as taking a thing by force in contest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3456}
It differs from theft by stealth, which relies on speed and concealment; from usurpation, which applies only to a thing that may not be taken; and from pilfering the booty before division. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3456}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3457}

## Kinds and rulings
- Taking what the owner has not allowed: forbidden by agreement, and it carries discretionary punishment, as a kind of usurpation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3457}
- Taking what the owner has thrown open, such as the sweets thrown over a bride: the jurists differ; al-Shawkani forbids it, others dislike it, and the Hanafis, al-Sha'bi and al-Hasan al-Basri allow it while holding that leaving it is better. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3457}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3458}
- Taking what the owner allowed to be shared equally, such as food at a banquet: forbidden, since taking more than one's share is unlawful eating. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3458}
- Effect: one who takes what the owner has allowed owns it by taking it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3458}
