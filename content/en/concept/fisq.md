## Summary
Sha'rawi explains fisq by its original sense: a date that has ripened and shrunk leaves its skin. The religion took the word for one who goes out of God's law, as if the law surrounds a person in all his conduct {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1576}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1577}.

## Partial and greater fisq
Sha'rawi says every disobedient person is called a fasiq. Such a person still believes in the law but has gone out of one part of it. The fisq that the verse speaks of is the greatest kind: turning from the whole community of faith after the covenant and the testimony of prophets, peoples and God {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1577}.

## Why turning away happens
Sha'rawi holds that one who turns from the new religion is not persuaded by God's law and seeks another, and that laws made by people arise from desire and lead to corruption. He supports this with the subjection of creation to human service and with the view that humans alone bear the duty of choice {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1577}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1578}.
