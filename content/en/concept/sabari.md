## Nature
Sabari is said of an excessive madness that comes with a hot bilious sarsam, so that a person, though he has sarsam, raves as one mad, agitated and confused {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.83} Simple qaranitus follows raving and confusion of mind and has no madness with it; if there is madness it is sabari, as if it were mania joined with qaranitus, just as qaranitus is as if it were melancholia joined with swelling and fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.83}

## Cause
Madness often comes first in it and the swelling and fever follow; it is sabari only when qaranitus is from pure red bile and burnt bile, for when it is driven to the brain and makes madness on its first arrival and makes swelling with it or after it, it is the cause of sabari {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.83} In qaranitus the madness is incidental to the swelling, but in sabari the madness and the swelling arise together from the matter, neither the cause of the other, though each may become a cause of increase in the other {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.83} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84}

## Signs and treatment
When sabari begins to show there is long wakefulness, troubled sleep, fright in sleep, leaping, much frequent breath, forgetfulness, an answer unlike the question, redness and agitation of the eyes and heaviness in them as if they had something fallen in them (the editor's footnote glosses qadhayatan as what falls into the eye), sometimes with some yellowness, a feeling of stretching at the nape and a pain from the rising of vapour, and a flow of tears without intention from one eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84} Then, once the disease has settled, the fever hardens, the tongue roughens and dries, and at the end the movements of the lids quiet from weakness and movement grows heavy even in moving the lids; of the madness there remains an interrupted raving with inability to speak and little speech, he mostly goes on picking at fluff and straw, and the pulse grows weaker, smaller and harder from dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84} Its treatment is that of the bilious sarsam with a great addition of moistening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.84}
