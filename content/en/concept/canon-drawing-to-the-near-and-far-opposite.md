## Summary
Ibn Sina says matter is removed from its place in two ways, by drawing to the far opposite and by drawing to the near opposite, using the example of a man bleeding heavily from the mouth and a woman with an excessive flow from her piles, and he requires that the distance lie along one diameter and that the pain of the place be eased first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.275}.

## Near and far
In the first case the matter is turned to the nose by inducing nosebleed, in the second to the womb by bringing down the menses, which is the near opposite. For the far opposite, the blood is drawn in the first case from the vessels and places in the lower body, and in the second from those in the upper body. The far opposite is not set at a distance in two diameters but in one, so if matter lies in the upper right it is drawn to the lower right itself, which is the more proper, and not to the lower left {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.275}.

## Easing pain and gentleness
When matter is to be drawn far, the pain of the place is eased first so it contests the drawing less, since pain itself draws; if the drawing resists, force is not used, because force stirs and thins the matter so that it leans faster toward the painful place. Drawing may suffice even without evacuating, since the drawing itself stops its turning toward the organ, and it is done by binding the opposite organs, by cupping glasses, by reddening drugs and in general by what produces some pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.275}.
