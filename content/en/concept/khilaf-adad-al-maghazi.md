## Overview
The scholars of the campaigns (*maghazi*) differed over the number of the expeditions the Prophet led in person, and over the number of his detachments (*saraya*) and dispatches (*ba'uth*). {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.579}

## Ibn Ishaq's count in the version of Ibn Hisham
Ibn Hisham relates from Ziyad al-Bakka'i, from Ibn Ishaq, that the total of the expeditions the Prophet made in person was twenty-seven. He counts them from Waddan to Tabuk, and he fought in nine of them: Badr, Uhud, the Trench, Qurayza, al-Mustaliq, Khaybar, the Conquest, Hunayn, and al-Ta'if. See [[dataset/ibn-ishaq-ghazawat-list|the list of expeditions]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/256} His dispatches and detachments were thirty-eight, between dispatch and detachment. He begins them with the expedition of Ubayda ibn al-Harith, and mentions that some people place the expedition of Hamza before it. See [[dataset/ibn-ishaq-saraya-list|the list of detachments]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/257}

## Al-Masudi
Al-Masudi mentions that the expeditions the Prophet made in person were twenty-six according to Ibn Ishaq, while others counted them as twenty-seven by separating the expedition of Wadi al-Qura from Khaybar. He says that he fought in nine by agreement of Ibn Ishaq and al-Waqidi, and that al-Waqidi added Wadi al-Qura and the day of al-Ghaba. He says the detachments are said to be thirty-five, or forty-eight, or sixty-six. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.579} A reader will note that the printed Ibn Hisham says twenty-seven, not twenty-six, although his list counts the Umra of al-Qada' as an expedition. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/256}

## Ibn Hisham's note on the number of detachments
At the end of the list of detachments, Ibn Hisham notes that Ibn Ishaq mentioned the dispatch of Khalid ibn al-Walid to Yemen in his narrative but did not count it in the number of dispatches and detachments, so "the number in his words should be thirty-nine" instead of thirty-eight. See [[event/ali-expeditions-to-yemen|the expedition of Ali to Yemen]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/291}
