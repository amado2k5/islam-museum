## Summary
Ibn Sina adds a third way of weighing an organ's strength, the keenness or dullness of its sense: in keenly sensitive nervous organs he avoids drugs of bad quality, biting or harmful, such as the spurges, and he lists three classes of drug to be shunned {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.

## The three classes
The drugs to be shunned are of three classes: dissolvers, those that cool in potency, and those with opposing qualities such as verdigris, white lead, burnt copper and the like. With this the account of choosing a drug by the nature of the organ ends {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.
