## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that God made the whole earth a place of prayer and purity for the community of Muhammad, to widen the means of meeting God, and that barring God's name from mosques or wrecking them is a great wrong. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.537} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.538}

## Easing worship for the community
Before Islam, Sha'rawi says, the followers of each religion prayed only in a place set apart for their faith. God then made the whole earth a place of prayer, so that prayer and voluntary prayers are valid in any place and at any time. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.537} He cites the hadith of five gifts from Jabir, among them that the earth was made a place of prayer and purity. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.538}

## Thanks in prostration
Sha'rawi holds that the discovery of hidden laws in creation calls for prostration in thanks, and that people should not come to rely on causes instead of the Creator. He gives the example of live images and events that people now see as they happen. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.538} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.539}

## The sanctity of mosques
Sha'rawi counts barring God's name from mosques and seeking their ruin as the greatest wrong. He says that God brought shame on the Jews of Medina in this world for such acts, and that the punishment in the next world is great. He adds that a believer who keeps silent or fails to defend the mosques shares in the sin. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.540} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.541}

## Limits of this block
