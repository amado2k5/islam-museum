## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the fifth chapter, on the causes of the pulse, by dividing them into retentive, obligatory and transforming, and counts the three retentive causes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.

## Three kinds of cause
Some causes of the pulse are general, necessary and essential, entering into the pulse's constitution, and are called retentive. Others do not enter into its constitution; of these some are obligatory, changing the pulse's rulings by their own change, and are called the obligatory causes, and others are not obligatory and are called the transforming causes without qualification {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.

## The three retentive causes
The retentive causes are three: the vital power in the heart that moves the pulse, already treated under the vital powers; second, the instrument, the pulsating vessel, treated under the organs; third, the need for cooling, which calls for a known measure of cooling and renews itself with the state of the heat, whether it flares, dies down or is moderate. The actions of these retentive causes change according to the obligatory and transforming causes joined to them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.
