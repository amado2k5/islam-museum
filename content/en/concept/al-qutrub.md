## Nature and signs
It is a kind of melancholia that most often comes in the month of Shubat; it makes a person a runaway from living people, a lover of the neighbourhood of the dead and the graves, with ill intent toward one who takes him by surprise; the sufferer comes out at night and hides and conceals himself by day, all from love of solitude and distance from people; yet he does not settle in one place, but keeps going to and fro and walking this way and that, not knowing where he heads, wary of people, though some of them are not wary, from heedlessness and lack of alertness to what they see; with this he is utterly still, sullen, regretful and grieving, yellow in colour, dry of tongue and thirsty, with sores on his leg that do not heal, his sight is dry, without tears, weak and sunken, all this from the dryness of the temperament of his eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.111}

## The cause
The cause of the leg sores is the corruption of his black-bile matter, the abundant moving of his leg and the descent of matter to it, especially as he stumbles at every turn, something scrapes his leg or a dog bites him, which is a cause of the abundant pouring of matter to his legs so that sores form there and, because they stay as they are along with their causes, do not heal; the cause of this illness is black bile and burnt bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.111}

## The reason for the name
It is called qutrub because the sufferer flees with a flight that has no order and because of his varied walking; the qutrub is a small creature on the surface of water that moves on it in various ways without order, diving and fleeing and then appearing again; it is also said to be another small creature that never rests, or the male of the su'ali (ghouls), or the bald wolf; and, as printed, 'the more fitting for our place is al-qulun' (not interpreted) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.111}
