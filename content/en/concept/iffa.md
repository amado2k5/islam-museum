## Summary
In the lexicon, iffa is restraint from what is unlawful or unbecoming; a man or woman is chaste who refrains from forbidden things {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11670}.

## Ihsan and its senses
The word ihsan has four senses: chastity, marriage, freedom and Islam, so it is wider than chastity {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11671}.

## Restraint from begging
Islam guards human dignity, so begging is forbidden to one who has enough; a truly needy person may ask within limits {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11671}.

## Chastity from adultery
Islam enjoins chastity and forbids what leads to adultery; it directs those able to marry to marry, and those unable to fasting as a protection {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11671} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11672}. One who can afford marriage, whose desire is strong and who fears adultery must marry {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11672}.

## A chaste person and a formerly adulterous partner
The majority hold that one partner's adultery does not bar the chaste person from the other; the Hanbalis require, for a woman, the end of her waiting period and repentance {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11672}.

## Where to look further
Marriage is treated under «nikah», and providing chastity for relatives under «i'faf» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11672}.

## In Mosaad Anwar's programme 'The Prophet's Counsels to Women'
In the second and third episodes of [[work/hamasat-mosaad-anwar|'Hamasat: the Prophet's counsels to women']], [[person/mosaad-anwar|Mosaad Anwar]] speaks about chastity, making the third a continuation of the second under the title «قصص للعفيفات» ('Stories of chaste women'). {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:01:00} He builds the talk on a hadith he attributes to [[person/ibn-hibban|Ibn Hibban]] from [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]], which he says [[person/al-albani-muhammad-nasir-al-din|al-Albani]] graded sound in Sahih al-Jami': a woman who performs the five prayers, fasts Ramadan, guards her chastity and obeys her husband will be told to enter Paradise by whichever of its gates she wishes. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:01:00} He says it holds four counsels — the obligatory prayers, the Ramadan fast each year, obedience in what is not sin, and chastity — and that the eight gates of Paradise call to her, a great reward for little work. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:01:30} He then announces that he will dwell on one of the four: chastity. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:02:00}

He holds that no religion has commanded chastity as Islam has, and that people live in a time when indecency and illicit relations have spread. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:02:30} He cites the verses listing the traits of the successful believers, among them guarding their chastity except with their spouses, and counts this among the causes of success. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:03:00} He mentions the verse that commands restraint for those who find no means to marry, and tells young men and women who cannot marry that their path is restraint, not brothels or pornographic websites. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:04:00} He cites the hadith in which the Prophet addresses young men, attributing it to [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] from [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]]: whoever is able to marry should marry, and whoever is not should fast, for fasting is a shield; {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:04:30} the encyclopedia of fiqh gives the same point in this entry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11671} He reads the verse praising righteous women who guard their husbands in their absence as meaning that a good wife obeys God and her husband in what is not sin, and protects his honour, children and wealth when he travels. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:05:00}

He draws examples from the Qur'an. God praised [[person/maryam|Mary]] for her chastity, and he describes her as untouched by display, mixing or forbidden seclusion. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:05:30} He says the climax of Surat Yusuf is a story of chastity, when al-Aziz's wife tried to seduce [[person/yusuf|Joseph]] and locked the doors. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:06:30} In his account al-Aziz was the second man in the state and his wife the king's niece, of surpassing beauty, so that had Joseph yielded he would have drowned in wealth and silk. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:07:00} He explains Joseph's calling adultery a wrong by the meaning of wrongdoing: putting a thing out of its place. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:07:30} He then cites the hadith of the seven whom God shades in His shade, attributing it to al-Bukhari and Muslim from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]; among them is a man invited by a woman of rank and beauty who refused out of fear of God. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:08:00} He cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bukhari, promising Paradise to one who guards his tongue and his private parts, and urges speech free of obscenity and lies. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:08:30} He cites a hadith he attributes to [[person/al-tabarani|al-Tabarani]], meaning that men's chastity brings their women's chastity and honouring one's father brings one's sons' honouring, and warns the young man who boasts of many affairs that his own honour will be exposed, since in his view recompense matches the deed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:09:30} He illustrates this with a date-seller who lured a woman customer into his storeroom and reached for her before she fled; at home he learned that the water-carrier had done the same to his wife, who told him: "a blow for a blow". {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:10:30} He addresses the Muslim woman as a guarded pearl, protected by Islam from the gaze through the command of [[concept/ghadd-al-basar|lowering the gaze]], and from touch through a hadith holding that being pierced with an iron needle is better than touching a woman one may not touch. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:12:00} He cites the hadith that the best treasure a man can store is a righteous wife, calling her a treasure kept safe until its owner, her husband, comes. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep02 00:12:30}

In the third episode he narrates the hadith of the three men shut in a cave by a rock, each of whom appealed to God by a good deed. The first loved his cousin; when she was in need and asked him for a hundred dinars he made her yield herself the condition, then left her when she reminded him of God, and the rock moved. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:02:00} He concludes that chastity protects its keeper from evil ends. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:03:30} From [[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]]'s [[work/kitab-dhamm-al-hawa|Dhamm al-Hawa]] he relates the story of a rich man who sent his hired worker away on an errand to be alone with the worker's wife and told her to shut every door and window; she replied that one door remained open, the one between them and God, and he desisted. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:08:00} He tells of a Bedouin who solicited a woman on a dark night, saying only the stars could see them; she asked him where the One who made the stars was, and he left ashamed. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:09:00} He tells of a man pursuing his neighbour who was told she would agree now, in the market; when he balked, she said he feared people though God was more worthy of his fear. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:10:00} He says Joseph heads the chaste among men. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:10:30} He narrates that libertines in a tavern wagered a singing-girl a thousand dinars that she could win a kiss from al-Rabi', the Kufan ascetic; she exposed herself to him, he reminded her of death, the washing of the body and the grave, and she repented until she was called "the worshipper of Kufa", and they complained that al-Rabi' had spoiled her for them. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:10:30} From Dr al-Affani's book Tartib al-Afwah he relates the story of Abu Bakr al-Miski, whom a woman lured to her house on the pretext of asking for a fatwa and then threatened to disgrace; he asked to use the privy, smeared himself with filth, and was thrown out as a madman; he then washed, prayed and dreamed that God had made his scent sweet in this world and the next, and for years afterwards musk came from him though he wore no perfume. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:14:00} He closes by urging women to guard their honour and to guard their husbands in their absence, and warns against those who prey on people's honour. {cite:assabile-mosaad-anwar hamasat/ep03 00:15:00}

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Ahl al-Janna'
[[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] gives the eighth episode of his programme [[work/ahl-al-jannah|'Ahl al-Janna']] to the chaste and the lustful, saying the viewer lives in it with a person whose life overcame his desire. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep08 00:02:30} Among his neighbours in Paradise he places [[person/maryam|Mary]] and [[person/talha-ibn-ubaydullah|Talha ibn Ubayd Allah]], one of the ten promised Paradise, whom he quotes as saying he never committed adultery in the Jahiliyya or in Islam, leaving it in the first from distaste and in the second from chastity (needs_source: he names no source for the saying). {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep08 00:05:00} He describes the chaste person as chaste in his glances, his topics and his speech with the other sex. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep08 00:25:00}
