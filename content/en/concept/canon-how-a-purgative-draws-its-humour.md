## Summary
Ibn Sina answers those who say the purgative generates what it draws or draws the thinnest first, and answers Galen's view of likeness in substance, holding that the purgative has a drawing power, that the drawing is along the route to the intestines from which nature pushes outward, and that most of what is drawn comes from the vessels {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.278-279}.

## Drawing and route
Ibn Sina says the purgative purges what it purges by a drawing power that draws that humour itself, so it may draw the thick and leave the thin, as the purgative for black bile does, and the claim that it generates what it draws, or draws the thinnest first, has nothing to it. He reports that Galen sees a likeness in substance between the drug that draws and the humour that is drawn, and says this is not right, since if drawing went by likeness, iron would draw iron and gold would draw gold; he leaves a fuller treatment to those other than the physician. What draws the humours does so along the road by which they are pushed until they reach the intestines, where nature moves to push them out, and it seldom happens that they rise to the stomach. Most of what drugs draw comes from the vessels, except what lies very close by {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.278-279}.
