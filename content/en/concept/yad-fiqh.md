Yad (hand) is feminine; it runs from the shoulder to the fingertips. Its lexical senses include blessing or kindness, power and authority ("the matter is in his hand"), and, in the verse on the poll-tax in surah al-Tawba (verse 29), power over. "Sold hand to hand" means on the spot. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17974}

Part one: the hand as a limb. Cleaning after relief: it is sunna to clean oneself with the left hand, with a stone or water, and disliked with the right without excuse, on the hadith "when one of you urinates, let him not hold his member with his right hand nor clean himself with it". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17974}

Putting the hands into water for purification: all agree that washing the hands before putting them into the vessel is legitimate, for the one seeking purity and for others, and for one who has just woken. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17974}

Washing the hands in wudu' and ghusl: it is sunna to wash them three times to the wrists before putting them into the vessel. All agree that washing them to the elbows is an obligation of wudu', on the verse of surah al-Ma'ida (verse 6). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17975}

Right first: the sunna is to begin with the right hand, and likewise with the feet, on A'isha's report that the Prophet loved to begin on the right in all his affairs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17975}

Removing major impurity from the hand: a major-impure person who dips his hand into water without intending to lift the impurity does not affect the water. If he dips with the intention of lifting it, the Hanafis (by preference), Malikis, one Hanbali view and one Shafi'i view hold the water does not become used. The Hanafis cite the basin at the mosque door from which the Companions of the Bench drew with their hands, and the need; they also cite A'isha washing with the Prophet from one vessel. Abu Yusuf distinguishes a well, where entering is excused for drawing water, from a vessel, where it is not. The Shafi'is and the sounder Hanbali view hold the water becomes used. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17975} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17976}

Wiping the hand with earth in tayammum: the jurists' majority describe it as striking the clean earth with both hands, shaking them, wiping the face, then striking again and wiping each forearm with the other palm, including the elbow. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17976}

Wiping the khuffs with the hand: the Hanafis and Hanbalis hold that the obligation is to wipe the outer surface of the khuffs with the fingers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17976}

Position of the hands in prayer: it is sunna to raise the hands at the opening takbir, on Ibn 'Umar's report that they were raised level with the shoulders. The schools differ on how they are raised, on placing the right on the left, on raising them at bowing and rising, and on placing them on the knees and in prostration. See prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17976} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17977}

Counting verses on the fingers in prayer: the majority (Malikis, Shafi'is, the school's Hanbalis and the two disciples of Abu Hanifa) allow it, obligatory or voluntary, since counting is needed to keep the sunna of the length of recitation; the Malikis limit it to counting for the sake of the prayer. Abu Hanifa and a Hanbali view dislike it, since counting by hand leaves the sunna of the hand and is no act of prayer; one can count inside the heart or outside the prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17977}

Counting the tasbih on the fingers in prayer: the Malikis, the sounder Shafi'i view and the two disciples allow it; the Hanbalis allow it without dislike. Abu Hanifa, al-Hasan al-Basri and a Hanbali narration dislike it, and Ahmad hesitated; a Shafi'i view says it nullifies the prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17977} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17978}

Hand over the mouth during a yawn in prayer: it is recommended, on the hadiths that the yawn comes from Satan, so one should hold it in, or put a hand over the mouth. The Hanafis cover with the back of the left hand, or the right if standing, the Shafi'is use the left and prefer its back, and the Malikis use the back of the left hand but not its palm. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17978} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17979}

Raising the hands in supplication: (a) in the prayer for rain, all agree it is recommended, on the report of Anas about the Friday sermon when the Prophet raised his hands and prayed for rain; the palms face up for asking rain and down for averting calamity. (b) In the qunut: the Hanafis (the sounder view), the Malikis (the famous view) and one Shafi'i view say no, by analogy with the opening and the tashahhud; the Hanbalis and the sounder Shafi'i view and Abu Yusuf in one narration say yes, on the report of Abu Rafi' behind 'Umar. Ibn al-Jallab permits it. (c) wiping the face after the qunut: the Hanafis, the sounder Shafi'i view and one Hanbali narration say no; the Hanbali school and a Shafi'i view say yes, on a hadith of the Prophet wiping his face with his hands, which has a narrator of unknown identity. (d) outside prayer: the Hanafis, one Maliki view, the Shafis and Hanbalis hold raising the hands level with the chest is a good practice; the Hanafis prefer open palms with a gap, and the Shafis sunna palms facing the sky. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17979} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17980}

Supplication: the Shafi'is raise the hands following the sunna, palms up for asking the removal of a calamity and palms down for asking a thing to be obtained. The Hanbalis hold that the etiquette is to open the hands and raise them to the chest, on the hadith "when you ask God, ask with the insides of your palms, not their backs". A Maliki view says a supplicant outside prayer does not raise his hands. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17981}

Wiping the face after supplication outside prayer: the Hanafis (the sounder view), the Shafi'is, the Hanbalis and a Maliki view say one wipes the face at the end; a Maliki view and a Hanafi view, citing a narration with "it is said", call it nothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17981}

Touching the dead person's private parts: the washer must wrap his hand in cloth and cover the private parts with cloth, since looking is forbidden and touching is worse; the Malikis allow bare touch in necessity. The washing of small children is disputed; see washing of the dead. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17981}

Raising the hands at the takbirs of the funeral prayer: all agree on the first takbir. The Shafi'is, the Hanbalis, a Maliki narration and many shaykhs of Balkh among the Hanafis raise them at each takbir; the Hanafis (in the apparent narration) and Malik (in his preferred narration) do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17981} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17982}

Raising the hands on sighting the Ka'ba: the Shafi'is, the Hanbalis, a Hanafi view, Ibn Habib among the Malikis, al-Thawri, Ibn al-Mubarak and Ishaq hold it recommended, citing a hadith that hands are raised only in seven places, which al-Zayla'i reports Shu'ba criticised as broken. The Hanafis in their school's view and the Malikis say no; al-Tahawi calls it disliked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17982}

Touching the Black Stone: the circumambulator touches it with his hands; if the crowd would harm others, he gestures towards it with his hands, since touching is a sunna and harming people is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17982}

Raising the hands at al-Safa and al-Marwa: the Hanafis, Ibn Habib, the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold it a sunna to climb al-Safa, face the House, raise the hands, praise God and pray as one wishes, on the report of Abu Hurayra. The Malikis say leaving the raising is dearer to Malik; al-Qarafi says leaving it is dearer to Malik in all things except the opening of prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17983}

Trimming the nails: a sunna for men and women, on the hadith of the fitra, which lists trimming the nails among its items. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17983}

Henna on the hands: recommended for a married woman; forbidden for men in the Maliki and Shafi'i schools, and in the school's Hanbali view unless needed for treatment, citing the hadith cursing men who imitate women. A Hanafi view, and what Hanbali statements imply, dislike it for men. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17983}

Washing the hands before and after food: it is recommended to wash them after eating, on the hadith "whoever loves God to increase the good in his house, let him perform wudu' when his meal is brought and when it is taken away". The Malikis limit the recommendation to greasy food. The Hanafis, Shafi'is, Hanbalis and al-Nafrawi recommend washing before food even when one is in a state of purity; the Malikis in their school say it is not sunna unless the hand has some dirt, which must be washed if impure and is recommended if clean. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17984} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17985}

The major-impure person eating or drinking: the majority (Hanafis, Shafi'is, Hanbalis) hold it recommended for him to perform a wudu' when he wishes to eat or drink, on A'isha's report; they differ on whether this means the prayer wudu' or only washing the hands, and A'isha's narration that he washed his hands when he wished to eat in a state of major impurity is cited for the second. The Hanafis dislike eating or drinking before washing the hands and mouth, for a man or woman in major impurity, but not for a menstruating woman; the Shafi'is dislike eating or drinking without wudu' for both; the Malikis say no wudu' is required and washing the hands of filth is recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17984} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17985}

Washing the hands with bran or flour: bran is allowed by the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis, since it is not food; flour is allowed by the Hanafis, Shafis and one Maliki view, since people have always done so without objection. The Hanbalis in their school and the Malikis in their preferred view dislike washing the hands with food, even chickpea or lentil flour, the Malikis as a matter of respect for food; they count wheat bran with food but not barley bran. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17985}

Drying the hands on paper: the Hanafis dislike wiping the hands on paper that is blank but fit for writing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17985}

Eating with the fingers: it is sunna to eat with three fingers when eating by hand; a spoon is allowed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17985}

Licking the fingers after eating: the majority hold it a sunna, before wiping with a napkin, on the hadiths "when one of you eats let him lick his fingers, for he does not know in which is the blessing" and "let him not wipe his hand until he licks it or has it licked". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17985} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17986}

Leaning on the hand while eating: the Hanafis hold it allowed if it is not arrogance, and the Zahiriyya calls this the preferred view; the Hanafi Atabiyya dislikes eating or drinking leaning or with the left hand on the ground. The Malikis dislike leaning, reading it as eating tilted on the left elbow or cross-legged. The Shafi'is dislike leaning, and the Hanbalis dislike eating lying down. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17986}

Masturbation (this entry includes sensitive material; the schools' rulings are stated in the book's terms): (1) for no need, the Malikis, Shafi'is, the school's Hanbalis and one Hanafi view hold it forbidden, on the verses of the believers' chastity (surah al-Mu'minun, verse 5, and surah al-Ma'arij, verse 29). The Hanafis in their school and Ahmad in one narration and 'Ata' say it is disliked, and the Hanafis specify that this disliking is the disliking of what is forbidden. (2) to avoid fornication: the Hanafis and the school's Hanbalis hold there is no penalty; Ahmad in one view says it is disliked; the Malikis and Ahmad in another say forbidden even for fear of fornication, since fasting replaces marriage. (3) where it is the only way to avoid fornication: the Hanafis, Hanbalis and Shafi'is allow it; the Malikis forbid it but, if fornication cannot otherwise be avoided, prefer it as the lesser of two harms. (4) by the wife's hand: the Malikis (in the sounder view), the Hanbalis, a Hanafi view and the Shafi'is except al-Qadi Husayn allow it, since she is the place of his enjoyment; a Hanafi view and al-Qadi Husayn dislike it, Ibn 'Abidin calling it a mere disliking. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17986} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17987} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17988}

A man looking at a woman's hands: the majority allow it if desire is not feared. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17988}

Handshakes: a handshake between two men or two women is recommended, on the hadith "no two Muslims meet and shake hands without being forgiven before they part", which al-Mundhiri calls irregular. A man's handshake with a non-related woman is disputed; see the entry on handshake. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17988}

Kissing the hand: the jurists differ; see kissing. Injury to the hand: all agree the hand is taken for a hand in deliberate injury when the conditions of retaliation are met, whatever the size difference. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17988}

Blood-money for the hand: the diyya is due for cutting off both hands when retaliation is not due, and half for one. For the ten fingers, the full diyya is due; each finger carries a tenth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17989}

Theft: the thief's penalty is the cutting of his hand when the conditions are met, on the verse of surah al-Ma'ida (verse 38). In hirabah, cutting the hand and foot from opposite sides is among the penalties. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17989}

Slander by "your hand committed fornication": the Hanafis, the Shafi'is and the school's Hanbalis, and Ashhab among the Malikis, hold there is no hadd, since it is not explicit. The Shafi'is say the speaker is a slanderer if he intends slander. A Shafi'i view and Abu Bakr among the Hanbalis hold it explicit, by analogy with the private part. The Malikis hold it allusive, with a hadd when a sign shows the allusion or the matter is unclear, and explicit if "hand" means the person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17989} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17990}

Adornment on the hand: a man may not wear gold in his hand or make any gold jewellery, except in necessity, such as a golden hand; women may wear gold of all kinds. A man may wear silver as a ring; the schools differ on other silver. Adornment with other materials is disputed; see jewellery and ring. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17990}

Hand as possession: possession is evidence of ownership; one who holds land and claims to own it by purchase or inheritance is believed, and his opponent must bring proof. See possession and disputes over possession. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17990}

A foundling claimed by two: the Shafi'is hold that if one of two claimants has a hand on him, that claimant is preferred; the sounder view is that a hand of picking up does not count, and that otherwise the earlier claim is preferred, or both are placed before a physiognomist. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17990} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17991}

Giving the matter into the wife's hand: "your matter is in your hand" is a delegation of divorce. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17991}

The hand of trust and the hand of guarantee: the trust hand received property with the owner's leave, not in exchange or as security; the guarantee hand received it without leave, in exchange, or as security. See guarantee. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17991}
