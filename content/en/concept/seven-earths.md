## The verse and the hadiths

The verse of al-Talaq says God made seven heavens and that the earth has its match among them, and that the command descends between them so that people may know His power and the reach of His knowledge: [[passage/quran-65-12|At-Talaq 65:12]]. The seven earths are also mentioned in the hadiths on usurping land:

- Al-Bukhari 3198 reports from Sa'id ibn Zayd that Arwa brought a claim against him before Marwan over a right she said he had cut from her; Sa'id said he had heard the Messenger of God say that whoever takes a span of land unjustly will have it hung round his neck on the Day of Resurrection down through seven earths. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3198}
- Al-Bukhari 2453 reports that Aisha told Abu Salama, who had a dispute with some people, to avoid the land, for the Prophet said that whoever wrongs another by a span of land will have it hung round his neck from seven earths. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2453}
- Muslim has Sa'id ibn Zayd's hadith and Abu Hurayra's hadith on the same subject in the Book of Musaqat, which in the in-book numbering of sunnah.com is book 22, hadiths 172 to 176. {cite:sahih-muslim book 22, hadiths 172-176}

Ibn Kathir refers to the two Sahihs for this and adds that in al-Bukhari it is "he was made to sink to seven earths". {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 65:12}

## The commentators: layers, climes or separate lands?

**Al-Qurtubi** says there are two views on the earths: the majority hold seven earths one above another in layers, with between each earth and the next a distance like that between one heaven and the next, and in each earth inhabitants from God's creation; al-Dahhak held seven earths pressed on each other without gaps, unlike the heavens. He says the first is sounder because the reports in al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and others point to it. He records a third view, from al-Kalbi from Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas: seven earths spread out, not one above another, separated by seas, with one sky shading all of them. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 65:12}

Al-Qurtubi quotes al-Mawardi that if the earths are stacked, the call of Islam is for the people of the upper earth and does not bind those in the others, even if they contain rational, discerning beings, and there are two views on whether they see the heaven; and if they are spread out, then if no one on this earth can reach another earth the call is for this earth only, and if some can reach another, it is possible the call binds them and possible it does not, because no text came concerning it. Al-Qurtubi closes: God knows best what He kept to Himself. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 65:12}

**Ibn Kathir** says the earths are seven as is established in the two Sahihs, and that whoever took this to mean seven climes went far afield and contradicted the Qur'an and the hadith without any basis; he mentions Ibn Mas'ud and others on a distance of five hundred years between each earth and the next and a thickness of five hundred years for each. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 65:12} He repeats the judgement in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya and adds that what many of the People of the Book relate, and a group of our scholars took from them, that this earth is of dust and the one under it of iron and another of sulphur stone and so on, is rejected from whoever says it unless it is reported by an infallible with a sound chain. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya vol. 1, PDF p. 25}

**Al-Razi**, on the same verse, says it is not far-fetched that the verse's likeness of the earths to the heavens means seven climes matching the seven heavens and the seven planets, or three layers: pure earth, clay, and a layer partly uncovered, in the sea and on land; and that the other transmitted accounts from the commentators are refused by reason unless transmitted by mass transmission. He reports from Qatada that in every heaven of His heavens and every earth of His earths there is a creation of His, a command of His and a decree of His. {cite:mafatih-al-ghayb-dar-al-hadith-2012-v15 PDF p. 354} Ibn Kathir, without naming anyone, judges the reading as seven climes to be far-fetched and without basis, so the two views are opposed.

**Ibn Hajar**, on hadith 3198, reports from al-Dawudi that the hadith shows the earths are one above another like the heavens; from some theologians that the likeness is in number only and the seven are adjacent; and from Ibn al-Tin, from some, that the earth is one, which Ibn al-Tin called rejected by the Qur'an and the Sunna. Ibn Hajar remarks that perhaps this is the view of adjacency, otherwise it would be an open contradiction. He says the apparent sense of the verse also answers the astronomers who say there is no distance between one earth and the next. {cite:fath-al-bari on hadith 3198, PDF p. 338-339}

## Ibn Abbas's report: a prophet in each earth

Al-Tabari reports from Ibn Abbas, through Shu'ba from 'Amr ibn Murra from Abu al-Duha, on the verse: in each earth is one like Ibrahim, and such creation as is on the earth. Al-Bayhaqi in *al-Asma' wa-l-Sifat* gives a longer chain, through Sharik from 'Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib from Abu al-Duha from Ibn Abbas: seven earths, in each a prophet like your prophet, an Adam like Adam, a Nuh like Nuh, an Ibrahim like Ibrahim and an 'Isa like 'Isa. Then al-Bayhaqi said: "The chain to Ibn Abbas is sound but it is shadhdh, and I do not know a corroborator for Abu al-Duha." {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 65:12}

Ibn Hajar quotes the same words of al-Bayhaqi, says that al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi both transmitted it at length through 'Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib, and says the chain of al-Tabari's shorter version is sound. {cite:fath-al-bari on hadith 3198, PDF p. 338-339} Ibn Kathir, in al-Bidaya wa-l-Nihaya, says the report must, if it is transmitted truly from Ibn Abbas, be taken as something he took from the Isra'iliyyat. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya vol. 1, PDF p. 25}

Al-Tabari and Ibn Kathir also report that Ibn Abbas said to one who asked about the verse that if he told them its explanation they would disbelieve, and that their disbelief would be their denying it; and to another: what makes you sure that if I told you, you would not disbelieve? {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 65:12} The modern editor of an annotated edition of Ibn Kathir's tafsir adds notes on these chains: that al-Bayhaqi's chain includes Sharik al-Qadi, "truthful, makes many mistakes", so the chain is weak in his view, and that such reports are not given by opinion, the condition for accepting them being that the narrator did not narrate from the books of the Children of Israel, and Ibn Abbas is known to have narrated from them. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 65:12}

**Sunni: seven earths pressed together without gaps, or spread out and adjacent.** The view of al-Dahhak and the view of al-Kalbi from Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas in al-Qurtubi's account; Ibn Hajar reports from some theologians that the likeness is in number only and the seven are adjacent. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 65:12} {cite:fath-al-bari on hadith 3198, PDF p. 338-339}

## What the sources do not say

- The sound hadith on the seven earths says only that the usurper is encircled with them; it does not describe them or who is in them.
- The museum did not read a grading of Ibn Abbas's report by al-Dhahabi, nor al-Hakim's own text in *al-Mustadrak*, so nothing is attributed to them except Ibn Hajar's statement that they transmitted it.
- Whether jinn or others live in the other earths is not stated in what was read here beyond al-Qurtubi's general words, "in each earth are inhabitants from God's creation", and the Ibn Abbas report.
