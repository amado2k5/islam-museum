## The rules of evacuation
Ibn Sina says that when we want to evacuate a matter, if the signs show that plentiful blood is with it and there is no deficiency in the blood, whatever the matter is, we begin with venesection {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.26}

If the case is grave and blood prevails we bleed; and we incline to venesection even if other humours also prevail, beginning with it because venesection is an evacuation common to the humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.26} If the matter is blood only, a full venesection suffices; if other humours, then if it is by sharing of the whole body we evacuate the whole body, then bleed the head alone and use the evacuations particular to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.26} We never go ahead with evacuations of the head except after evacuating the whole body, if there is a humour in the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.26}

That is if we know that the matter is ripened, as seen from what is drawn to the head, if it is not very thin or very thick {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.26} If the disease has reached its peak and we have gone ahead with ripening, we evacuate from the head in particular by gargling, if we do not fear an affliction in the lung, the descents drawn down by gargling are not of a sharp stinging humour, the person is not prone to lung diseases and can guard against something bad descending into the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.26} We also use opening smells that make one sneeze, nasal drops and fomentations to draw the matters out of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27}

## Softening, caution and drawing to the opposite side
He says that in evacuating cold humours we take care not to purge the thin and hold back the thick, and our way to this is to evacuate after softening with softening, ripening remedies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27} Whenever we use an evacuation we follow it with softening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27}

In evacuating sharp humours, where at times hot remedies are unavoidable, we take care that a hot ill-temperament is not left behind; rather we strive that it not remain, by making good the looseness they cause and the evacuation by gargling with cooling applications {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27} We avoid using them except with confidence drawn from the patient's habit that what he drinks of it loosens and evacuates him, so that giving it to him is not a cause of destruction or corruption {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27}

If the humours are unripe we first ripen each by what it requires; if the humours are rising from one side or from the whole body we draw to the opposite: if from below or from the whole body we use enemas and suppositories and bind the limbs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27} And we evacuate the organ itself, for example, if it is the stomach, by what is particular to it, and likewise every organ, and manage each by the regimen particular to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27} He says these are general laws in the matter of matters, and that whatever matter is evacuated and an ill-temperament arises from it, we treat by the opposite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27}

## Blood in the head
As for blood, he says venesection varies with its state: whether a plentiful matter has already reached the head, is on its way, or is only feared because of a cause that draws humours around the head, such as external heat or a blow; the more distant the arrival, the farther from the head the evacuation is made, and the drawing reaches as far as the direction of the leg {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.27} If it is by sharing with an organ, the evacuation is made by what the two organs have in common, if one wants to evacuate from both {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.28}

After the blood is taken, evacuants are taken, and enemas are used if need remains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.28} If the complaint is hard, such as a sanguine apoplexy, we resort to venesection {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.28}

Recipes, quantities, the names of the veins and the sites of venesection and cupping, and methods of application are not reproduced here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.26-28}
