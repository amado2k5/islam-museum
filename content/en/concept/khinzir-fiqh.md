## Summary
The pig is forbidden: the community agrees on the prohibition of eating its flesh except in necessity, on the Quranic verse of al-An'am; the Hanbalis give the dog precedence over the pig in necessity, the Malikis give other carrion precedence over pig. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7878}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7879} Its impurity: the Hanafis, Shafiis and Hanbalis hold the pig and all its parts, its semen, saliva and sweat impure; the Malikis hold its substance pure while alive. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7879} Tanning its hide: it is not purified by tanning by agreement; a report from Abu Yusuf says it is; Ibn al-Faras reports a Maliki view that its hide may be used once tanned. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7880} Leftover: impure by the Shafiis, Hanafis and Hanbalis, washed seven times for a dog in the Shafii and Hanbali view and three in the Hanafi; the Malikis hold it pure because its saliva is pure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7880}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7881} Its hair: most jurists hold it impure and forbid its use; for the Malikis it is pure if cut, not plucked; the Hanafis allow its use by cobblers in necessity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7881} Medicine from its parts: forbidden, since medicine from the impure and the forbidden is not allowed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7881} Change of substance: the Hanafis, Malikis and a Hanbali report hold that an impure thing purified by transformation, so a pig turned into salt is clean; the Shafiis and Hanbalis say no, except wine and the hide of carrion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7881} Value: by agreement the pig is not property of value for a Muslim, so its sale and purchase are invalid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7881}
