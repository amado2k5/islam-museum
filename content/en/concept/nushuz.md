## Summary
Nushuz in the lexicon means rising up; a wife is said to be nashiz when she disobeys her husband and withholds herself from him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15706} For the Hanafis it is a wife leaving her husband's house without right; for others it is leaving the obedience owed to the husband, and it may also be attributed to the husband. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15706} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15707}

## General ruling
A wife's nushuz is forbidden in the jurists' view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15707} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15708} Most jurists hold that a nashiz wife loses maintenance and lodging until she returns to obedience. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15712} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15714} The husband may discipline her by admonition, by leaving her in bed and by beating that is not severe, and the jurists differ on the order of these measures. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15716} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15719} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15725} When the dispute between the spouses grows, the judge appoints two arbiters from their families, and the jurists differ on whether they are agents or judges. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15729} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15733}

## Where it is discussed
Maintenance, the rotation of nights and discipline are treated under their own headings, and arbitration in dispute is treated in the entries on divorce and khul'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15716} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15730}
