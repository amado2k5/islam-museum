## Summary
Al-Battani teaches how a surplus is carried to the next higher order whenever it passes sixty, how one is borrowed from the order above and counted as sixty when a subtraction needs it, how whole revolutions of 360 parts are dropped when degrees are added and the remainder kept, and how a revolution is added to the sum when the degrees fall short of the subtraction. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.

## Carrying
Any number gathered in an order by multiplication or addition is divided by sixty, the ratio between the orders of fractions, and the quotient goes back to the order above while the remainder stays in the lower one. This is the rule of carrying in sexagesimal counting, the one we still use for minutes and hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.

## Borrowing in subtraction
When more must be taken from an order than it holds, one is taken from the order above and counted as sixty, added to it, and the subtraction made as needed, and what is left is counted with what remains of the higher order. Sexagesimal subtraction therefore works by borrowing from the order above, as we do today when subtracting minutes. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.

## Dropping whole revolutions
For degrees gathered by adding the parts of motions, the reference is to revolutions: if more than one revolution gathers, the revolution being 360 parts, the revolutions are dropped and the remainder counted; and if more must be taken from the degrees than they can bear, a revolution is added to them and the subtraction made from the total. Celestial motions are therefore reckoned modulo one revolution. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.13}.
