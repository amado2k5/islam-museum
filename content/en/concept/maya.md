## Nature and selection
They said: the moist kind either flows by itself as gum or is drawn out by boiling. What flows by itself is yellow and, when aged, turns toward gold, and it is rare. What is drawn with the bark is the black, drawn by boiling the bark of that tree; what is drawn off is the moist maya, and what remains as dregs and pulp is the dry. The editor's footnote says that maya is lubna {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.567}.

## Actions and properties
We have spoken of the forces of the moist and the dry: they have astringency and drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.567}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Some said it is hot and dry, brings down humidity from the brain and purifies it; this goes against what is established of it, since it causes headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.567}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The dry kind helps moistness of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.567}.

## Excretory organs
The dry maya restrains the bowel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.567}.
