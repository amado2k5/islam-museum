## Summary
Ibn Sina sets out what summer does to the humours and the strength, why diseases run short in it, the diseases of the burning heat, and how a spring-like, a southern and a northern summer differ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.

## Humours and strength
Summer dissolves the humours and weakens the strength and the natural actions through excess of dissolving. Blood and phlegm grow few, yellow bile grows many, and black bile comes at the end; the colour turns yellow from the blood dissolved. Diseases run short because a strong faculty finds the air helping it to dissolve, ripens the matter of the illness and drives it out, while a weak one is weakened more by the heat and the patient dies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.

## Hot-dry and moist summers
A hot, dry summer quickly brings diseases to their decision, while a moist one lengthens them; for that reason most ulcers turn to eating ulcers, and dropsy, slipping of the bowels and loose stool occur {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.

## Diseases of the burning heat
Among the diseases of the heat are burning fever, continuous fever, tertian fever and wasting of the body; among pains, earache and ophthalmia; and erysipelas and its pustules are frequent when the summer has no wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.

## Kinds of summer
In a spring-like summer fevers are mild and not rough, sweat is plentiful and a crisis is to be expected. A southern summer breeds pestilences, smallpox and measles. A northern summer ripens but brings the squeezing diseases, those that arise when matter flows under heat and an outer cold strikes and squeezes it. If a northern summer is dry, phlegmatic people and women benefit, while the bilious suffer dry ophthalmia, long hot fevers and an excess of black bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.120}.
