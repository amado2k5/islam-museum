## Its definition and causes
It is a spot of blood — fresh and red, or old, dying, dark and black — that has flowed from a burst vessel in the eye, by a blow for example or another vessel-bursting cause, from repletion or swelling till it settles in it; among that is the sound(?) and the violent motion — the word is carried as printed; it may also be from the blood's boiling in the vessels. The traumatic tarfa may cause a fine rupture in the pupil, while a rupture in the conjunctiva is safer {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.187}

## The treatment
The bird bloods named in the book are dripped on it; at the beginning something of the repellents like the known clays is mixed with it, and at the end it is mixed with the resolvents; it may be treated with a woman's milk with frankincense and salt water, and a named collyrium very beneficial for it, and it may be poulticed from outside ⁦(the drug, kohl and poultice names and recipes are not carried)⁩. If a rupture occurs with the tarfa in the conjunctiva, the management stated in the pterygium chapter for adhesion is used {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.187} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.188}
