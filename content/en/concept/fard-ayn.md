## Summary
The individual obligation (fard 'ayn) is what is required of each person under religious responsibility individually, so that it does not lapse for him through the act of another. The term was coined by the scholars of legal theory and the jurists, and it does not occur in its wording in the Qur'an or the hadith; but the texts express its meaning in the form "upon every Muslim" and "upon everyone who has reached maturity". Under this section the browser gathers the texts that use the term and the texts that place the duty upon everyone.

## Among the scholars of legal theory
Al-Shawkani divides the obligatory into what is upon individuals and what is upon the community {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.8}. In al-Risala, al-Shafi'i gives as examples of what no one can do in place of another the prayers, the pilgrimage and the alms, and he says that whoever has one of these obligatory upon him is not released from it by another's performing it, because one person's work in this is not written for another {cite:risala-shafii p.478}. Its counterpart is [[concept/fard-kifaya|the communal obligation (fard kifaya)]], and the two together have their place in the entry on [[concept/al-ahkam-al-taklifiyya|the taklifi rulings]].

## In the hadith
- Knowledge: "Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim" [[passage/ibnmajah-api-224|Ibn Majah 224]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Ibn Majah 224}. The wording is explicit for this section: an obligation, and upon every Muslim.
- The Friday ghusl: "The ghusl of Friday is obligatory upon everyone who has reached maturity" [[passage/nasai-api-1375|al-Nasa'i 1375]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Nasai 1375}, and it is in the Muwatta [[passage/muwatta-api-228|al-Muwatta 228]]. The disagreement of the jurists over whether "obligatory" here is taken as a binding duty or as an emphasis on what is recommended is recorded in the books of commentary; the browser gives the wording as it came.
- What was made obligatory upon everyone: the Bedouin's question about what God had made obligatory upon him, and the answer of the Prophet with the five prayers, the fast of Ramadan and the alms [[passage/bukhari-api-1891|al-Bukhari 1891]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Bukhari 1891}.
- The five prayers: "Five prayers God has prescribed for His servants" in the hadith of 'Ubada ibn al-Samit [[passage/abudawud-api-1420|Abu Dawud 1420]] {cite:hadith-api-fawazahmed0 Abu Dawud 1420}.

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an table does not place any verses under this section, because the Qur'an does not use the term; the verses on fasting, prayer, pilgrimage and alms are included in the section on [[concept/fard|the obligations]] by their wording: "it was prescribed for you" in [[passage/quran-2-183|al-Baqara 183]], the timed prescription in [[passage/quran-4-103|al-Nisa' 103]], and the pilgrimage to the House for whoever is able in [[passage/quran-3-97|Al 'Imran 97]].

## What is included and what is not
Included here is every text that contains "individual obligation" or "individually obligatory" or "it becomes specifically binding on him", and every text that makes something obligatory "upon every Muslim" or "upon everyone who has reached maturity" or "upon every free person and slave". Not included is "upon every small and great" when it is meant of age.
