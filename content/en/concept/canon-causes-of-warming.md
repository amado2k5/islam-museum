## Summary
Ibn Sina begins the second part of the second lesson, which gives a cause for each of the bodily symptoms in twenty-nine chapters, with the first chapter on warming agents, which, following Galen, he reduces to five genera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.143-144}.

## The warming causes
Warming agents come in kinds: food moderate in quantity; moderate movement, including moderate exercise, rubbing and pressing; cupping without scarification, since cupping with incision cools by evacuating; movement that tends a little to intensity and frequency without excess; hot food and hot drug; the moderate bath, known to warm through its air; plasters and airs that are not excessive; moderate wakefulness and sleep; anger in every case; grief when not excessive, for in excess it cools; moderate joy; and putrefaction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.143}.

## The five genera
It is Galen's habit to reduce all these causes to five genera: movement that is not excessive, contact with what warms without excess, hot matter, condensation and putrefaction. Condensation on the surface of the body warms by confining vapour, and rarefaction inside the body warms by spreading vapour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.143-144}.
