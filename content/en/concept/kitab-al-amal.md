## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy describes the book of deeds in the sixth lesson of "Al Fawzu Al Moubeen": the sheets fly about and hang from the necks, and people receive their books from different directions. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:24:30} The book is read the way an examination result is read, and it leaves nothing small or great unrecorded. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:41:30}

## The flying sheets and the taking of the books
The Sheikh describes the sheets flying about and hanging from the necks, and says that people take their books from the right, from the left, or from behind the back. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:24:30}

## Reading the book by the measure of an examination
The Sheikh likens the reading of the book to the results of an examination, between the one who passes and the one who fails. He says that the unlettered person reads his book in his own language, and that each person is reckoned according to his deeds (needs_source: the grades and numbers in this illustration are illustrative and have no source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:25:30}

## Counting every small and great thing
The Sheikh describes the Day of Judgement, when people's records are published one by one, and he appeals to a sense from the Qur'an that the book leaves nothing small or great unrecorded. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep06 00:41:30}
