## Summary
Ibn Sina counts drinking on an empty stomach, after exercise or bathing, and while nature is busy with digestion as harmful, and describes the handling of false thirst by patience, sleep, rinsing the mouth and a narrow-necked jug, adding that drinking to full quenching is harmful {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.236-237}.

## When drinking harms
Drinking on an empty stomach, after exercise and after bathing, above all with the belly empty, is harmful; so is yielding to false thirst at night, as happens to the drunk and the hungover, and while nature is occupied with digesting food. It has already been said that drinking to full quenching is very harmful {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.236}.

## Handling false thirst
If he cannot do otherwise, he should make do with cold air and rinsing the mouth with cold water, and if that does not satisfy him, drink from a jug with a narrow neck. The hungover may gain from this. One who cannot hold off drinking on an empty stomach, especially after exercise, drinks beforehand a drink mixed with hot water. Whoever suffers false thirst should know that sleep and enduring the thirst calm it, since nature then dissolves the matter that causes it, and that he should sip the water rather than gulp it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.236-237}.
