## Overview
Prophetic adab is the whole body of good manners that the believer is commanded to observe in his words, his deeds, and his relationship with God and with His servants. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

## According to Ibn Rajab
Ibn Rajab draws from the phrase "whoever believes in God and the Last Day" in the hadith of Abu Hurayra that these qualities belong to [[concept/iman|faith]], and that deeds enter into faith. He divides the three commands of the hadith into what concerns the rights of God, which is speaking good or keeping silent, and what concerns the rights of people, which is honouring the neighbour, refraining from harming him, and honouring the guest. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

He relates from Aswad ibn Asram the Prophet's counsel that a person should say only what is good with his tongue and extend his hand only to what is good, and that uprightness of the tongue is one of the qualities of faith. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

## Foundations of adab
Abu Amr ibn al-Salah relates from Abu Muhammad ibn Abi Zayd, the leading Maliki scholar of his time, that the whole of the manners of goodness branches out from four hadiths: speaking good or keeping silent, leaving aside what does not concern one, not being angry, and loving for one's brother what one loves for oneself. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

The concept is linked to [[concept/husn-al-islam|the goodness of Islam]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}
