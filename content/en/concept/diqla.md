## Nature and selection
There are wild and river kinds. The wild one has a leaf like the ḥamqāʾ plant, but finer, long branches lying on the ground, thorns by the leaves, and grows in waste places {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.437}. The river kind grows on river banks, its branches rise from the ground, its thorns are hidden, and it has a broad, very bitter leaf {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}. The editor's footnote identifies the kind as the plane; that is the editor's name.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot in the third degree and dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}. Actions: very dissolving; its decoction sprinkled in a house kills fleas and termites {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings: its leaf is placed on hard swellings, and is very useful there {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}.
Wounds: good for itch, scabies and spreading sores, especially its leaf juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}.
Joints: a poultice for an old back pain and for the knee {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}.
Head: its flower causes sneezing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}.
Poisons: it is poisonous; it is said to be mixed with wine and rue and drunk to clear pest poisons; the author warns that this is dangerous, and that the plant and its flower are poisonous to people, animals and dogs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.438}.
