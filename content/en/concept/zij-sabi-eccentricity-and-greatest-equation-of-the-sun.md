## Summary
Al-Battani draws the circle of the zodiac with two diameters at right angles and the Sun's eccentric circle inside it, and from the two observed arcs reckons that the distance between the centres is two parts, four minutes and a half and a quarter of a minute, on a scale where the circle's radius is sixty parts, and that the whole variation of the Sun's motion shown by these observations is one part and fifty-nine minutes, approximately {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.69-70}.

## The steps of the reckoning
He starts from the Sun's exceeding, by its mean motion from the first of Aries to the first of Libra, one hundred and eighty parts by three parts, fifty-six minutes and twelve seconds, so that each of the two arcs has half of that, one part, fifty-eight minutes and six seconds; he then draws two perpendiculars on the diameters of the figure, one of two parts and a few minutes and seconds and the other of sixteen minutes and forty-five seconds, approximately, and takes the root of the sum of their squares, which gives the line between the centres as two parts, four minutes and a half and a quarter. The exact figures of the perpendiculars are in disturbed letters in the printed text and are not all given {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.70}. The arc of this line on the circle is the greatest equation, one part and fifty-nine minutes, approximately {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.70}.
