## Summary
Ibn Sina makes thirst one sign of when purging must be stopped, notes that thirst may come from the state of the stomach, the sharpness of the drug or the heat of the matter, and says that when a purge for bile has gone as far as phlegm it has gone too far, and still more so when it reaches black bile, while blood is the gravest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.282-283}.

## Thirst and what comes out
Among the signs by which the time to cut short the purging is known is thirst, and if the purging goes on without thirst one need not fear that excess has occurred. Yet thirst may come not from the amount purged but from the state of the stomach, which thirsts quickly if hot or dry or both, from the drug if it is sharp and stinging, or from the matter itself if hot, as bile is. In any case, when you see thirst has become extreme and the purging slight, stop it. What comes out may itself show the time to stop: someone purging bile who sees the purging has reached phlegm has gone too far, and how much more if it reaches black bile; blood is the gravest and weightiest matter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.282-283}.
