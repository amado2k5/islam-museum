## Selection
The white is better than the black when they are equal in the other qualities of firmness, thinness, sweetness and the like, and what is left after picking for some days is better than what is picked that day {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.622}

## Temperament
The skin of the grape is cold and dry and slow to digest, its flesh hot and moist, and its seed cold and dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.622}

## Actions and properties
What is picked in season causes wind; the grape hung until its skin shrivels is good food and strengthens the body, its nourishment resembling that of the fig in its little badness and much nourishment, though less than the fig; the ripe harms less than the unripe, and if the grape is not digested its nourishment is raw and unready. The nourishment of the grape as it is is more than that of its juice, though its juice passes faster and goes down faster. The astringent grape may be softened by hanging, the sour not, and the raisin is a friend to the liver and the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.622}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The grape and the raisin with its seed are good for pains of the intestine; the raisin benefits the kidneys and the bladder; the grape picked in season stirs the bowels and causes wind, and every grape harms the bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.622}
