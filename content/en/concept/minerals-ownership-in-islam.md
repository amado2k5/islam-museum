## Summary
Linguistically, ma'din (mineral/mine) denotes staying and permanence, and in jurisprudence it refers both to the place of valuable substances in the earth (the mine) and to the substance itself, encompassing gold, silver, iron, copper, oil, and water {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 217} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 218}.

## Types
Minerals are either solid, such as gold and silver, or fluid, such as oil and water; and either surface-exposed, reached without heavy effort, or subterranean, reached with heavy effort {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 218}. They are found in privately owned land, in unowned public land, or in land belonging to the public treasury {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 218}.

## Ruling for Each Case
If the land belongs to the public treasury, the mineral belongs to the public treasury {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 218}. If it is privately owned, there is disagreement between the Malikis and others as noted above in [[concept/minerals-ownership-in-islam|the entry]]: the Malikis consider the mineral public property, while others consider it to follow the land {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 218}. If it is unowned public land, surface minerals cannot be owned through revival or granted as iqta', as they are a benefit without cost like running water and pasture, whereas subterranean minerals can be owned because they are reached only through toil and expense {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 218}.

## Land Grants
If a mineral deposit is surface-exposed, it may not be granted as iqta', because it is shared among Muslims like flowing rivers {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 228}. Al-Abyad ibn Hammal asked the Prophet to grant him the salt of Ma'rib, and when the Prophet was told it was like perennial spring water, he said, "Then no"; as for a subterranean mineral deposit, it may be granted as a concession for use rather than ownership, and in proportion to the grantee's capacity to work it {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 228}.

## Minerals and Land Revival
Reviving a mineral deposit means uncovering it, and a surface-exposed mineral needs no uncovering, so it cannot be owned through revival {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 228} (see [[concept/ihya-al-mawat|Revival of Barren Land]] and [[concept/iqta|Land Grants]]).
