## Its kinds, signs, hopes and fears
The bad quinsy pressing to keep the mouth open and the tongue lolling is called the canine — said sometimes of what is in the muscles inward of the larynx, sometimes of what befalls both muscle-kinds together, sometimes of what tends to convulsion when the matter drives toward the nerves; it may pour toward the heart and kill, or toward the stomach, and every choked man who dies convulses first. The canine quinsy may kill between the first and the fourth day, and quinsies and their like multiply in the wintry spring; suffocation arising in the acute fevers is very bad, and on a crisis day is a feared killer. The general sign of all the quinsy kinds: tightness of breath, the mouth staying open, hard swallowing until water meant to be drunk comes out of the nostrils, protrusion of the eyes, the tongue coming out in the severe kind with weakened motion — and what is said that so-and-so speaks from his nostrils is in truth the reverse, for the one so described in people's habit has blocked nostrils and does not speak from them at all. The pain does not grow strong in the phlegmatic and the hard, and grows strong in the hot. The mark between a tumour in the larynx and one in the oesophagus: if swallowing is possible and breath barred, the tumour is in the larynx, and if the reverse, in the oesophagus. Signs of swift death: the back-of-neck colour changing from its wonted red to white or green, with a cold sweat on armpit and nose-tip — he dies in one of his two days. Signs of hope: the redness moving outward. Signs of the quinsy's transference: wasting and loosening seen in the tumour without bursting outward, with rest — then the pulse is watched: if it turns strong and large and coughing comes, it passes into pneumonia; if the pulse grows very weak and small and uneven and palpitation rages and faintness comes, the matter is pouring toward the heart. The sign of gathering is a little softness past the fourth day. The bloody, bilious, phlegmatic and melancholic kinds each have signs he has set out; and a fixed pain standing a year or two in a man's throat points to a hardened residue in the throat's quarters {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.288}
