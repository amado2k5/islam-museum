## Summary
Rutuba is dampness, the opposite of dryness; the Hanbalis distinguish moisture from wetness in ruling on impurity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8784} Abu Hanifa and the Hanbalis hold a woman's private moisture pure, while the Malikis, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad hold it impure; the Shafi'is divide it into three kinds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8784} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8785} The majority hold the moisture of a pure animal's private part pure, and the Malikis limit this to animals that may be eaten. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8785} The Hanafis and Malikis hold that what touches a moist impurity does not become impure, in the sounder view, while the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold it impure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8785} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8786} Cleaning with stones requires a dry surface, and wet semen is washed while dry semen is rubbed off, according to the Hanafis and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8786}
