## Summary
In the Prophet's time the Qur'an was not left to memorization alone: he had scribes who wrote the revelation by his command. This matters for the history of the codex because it shows that the collection under Abu Bakr was a copying from what had been written in the Prophet's presence, not the beginning of writing. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 312}

## Who they were
Khalid al-Sabt lists among his scribes the four caliphs, Mu'awiya, Aban ibn Sa'id, Khalid ibn al-Walid, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]], Thabit ibn Qays, al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam, Hanzala ibn al-Rabi' and others. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 312} Other reports note that when Abu Bakr charged Zayd with the collection he said: 'You are a young man of sense whom we do not suspect, and you used to write the revelation for the Messenger of God', in [[passage/bukhari-4986|al-Bukhari]]. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 312} A note by the editors of this edition of al-Itqan calls Zayd 'scribe of the revelation, reciter and expert in inheritance'. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 383}

## How the writing went
Al-Sabt relates that when something came down the Prophet would call one of his scribes and order him to write what had come down, even a single word, as in the hadith of al-Bara' ibn Azib that when [[passage/quran-4-95|al-Nisa' 4:95]] came down the Prophet called Zayd, who brought a shoulder-blade and wrote it. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 312} He cites Zayd's saying 'we were with the Prophet composing the Qur'an from scraps', and the Prophet's saying 'whoever has written anything from me other than the Qur'an, let him erase it'. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 312} Some Companions also wrote the Qur'an for themselves. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 242}

## What they wrote on
They wrote on whatever was to hand, on bones, scraps, palm stems and thin stones. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 313} Al-Suyuti relays that Zayd collected the Qur'an from palm-stems, thin stones and men's chests, and that reports mention scraps, pieces of hide, shoulder-blades, ribs and saddle-wood. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 385} See [[concept/adawat-al-kitaba-al-ula|the first writing materials]].

## al-Muhasibi on the reliability of the transmission
Al-Suyuti cites al-Harith al-Muhasibi that writing the Qur'an was not an innovation, since the Prophet commanded its writing, but it was scattered on scraps, shoulder-blades and palm stems, and al-Siddiq only ordered it copied from place to place into one. He asks how trust was placed in the holders of the scraps and the chests of men, and answers: because they produced an inimitable composition and a known arrangement whose recitation they had witnessed from the Prophet for twenty years, so forging what was not part of it was safe; the fear was only of losing something sound. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 385}
