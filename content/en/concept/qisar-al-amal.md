## Overview
Shortening hope is the subject of the fortieth hadith of the Forty: "Be in this world as if you were a stranger or a passer-by." The stranger does not settle in a foreign dwelling, and the passer-by is less attached than he is. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.810-811}

## Ibn Umar's saying
On the hadith Ibn Umar built his saying: "When you are in the evening, do not expect the morning, and when you are in the morning, do not expect the evening; take from your health for your sickness, and from your life for your death." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.810-811}

[[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab]] says that this hadith is a root for shortening hope in this world, and that the believer should not take this world as a home and a homeland in which to settle. It is part of [[concept/zuhd|asceticism]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.810-811}
