## Nature
Kafur has several kinds: the Qansuri, the Rabahi, then the Azad, and the blue Asfarak, which is mixed with its wood and rises from its wood. Some, the book reports, say that the tree is large enough to shade people and that a creature the book calls al-babura keeps to it, so that it could be reached only in a fixed season of the year; some say it grows on sea-facing slopes, and the book places it in the regions of China. The wood is white, very light and brittle, and a trace of the camphor's scent is sometimes caught in its fibres. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.514}

## Temperament
The book says it is cold and dry in the third degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Cosmetic use
Cosmetic use: the book says its use hastens greying of the hair. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Swellings and pustules
Swellings and pustules: it prevents hot, inflamed swellings and pustules. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Organs of the head
Organs of the head: with vinegar, sour-grape juice, myrtle water or the water of badruj, it stops nosebleed. It relieves hot headache in acute fevers; the book says it induces wakefulness, strengthens the senses of those of hot temperament, and helps thrush. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Eye and chest
Eye and chest: it enters remedies for hot ophthalmia and cardiac remedies. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling): the book says it cuts sexual desire and produces kidney stone. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.515}
