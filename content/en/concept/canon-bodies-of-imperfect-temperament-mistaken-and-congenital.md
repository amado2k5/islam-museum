## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the seventeenth chapter, on managing bodies whose temperaments are not excellent, and divides them into the mistaken, which have acquired poor temperaments through long error of regimen though their original was excellent, and the congenital, whose original temperament is not excellent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.250}.

## The two kinds
These bodies are either mistaken or congenital in their makeup. The mistaken are those whose innate temperaments are excellent but which have acquired poor temperaments in time by long error of regimen until they settled in them. The congenital are those whose temperaments are not excellent from the start {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.250}.

## Recognition and treatment
The error of the mistaken is recognised by quality and quantity so that it may be treated by the contrary, and it may be judged from the state of the body's heat. The congenital are those whose state has been spoiled by their first temperament or by their age. The chapter's text on this page is short and nothing more is taken from it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.250}.
