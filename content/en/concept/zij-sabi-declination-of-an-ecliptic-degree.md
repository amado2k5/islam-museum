## Summary
Al-Battani teaches how to compute the declination of any degree of the zodiac from the equator of the day: take the chord of the degrees from the start of Aries to the wanted degree, within ninety, multiply it by the chord of the whole obliquity, divide the product by the radius, sixty, and find the arc of the quotient from the table; that arc is the declination of that degree from the equator in the meridian. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.22-23}.

## The rule
If you want to compute this obliquity, find the share of each degree you wish, up to the full ninety that completes the obliquity, from the beginning of Aries to the end of Gemini. Take the chord of a degree, two or more up to ninety; when you know the chord of the degrees you want, multiply it by the chord of the whole obliquity, divide the product by the radius, sixty, and find the arc of the quotient; that arc is the declination of the degree you wanted from the equator in the meridian. In later notation it is the relation that the sine of the declination equals the sine of the degree times the sine of the greatest obliquity divided by the radius. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.22}.

## The range of the computation and the symmetry of the quarters
He says that once the declination of each degree up to ninety is known, the declination of all the parts of the zodiac is known, since for what passes ninety up to 180 it is that of the ninety degrees set out, reversed; for what passes 180 up to 270 it is that of the ninety in the straight order; and for what passes 270 up to 360 it is that of the ninety reversed. A single quarter suffices to compute. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.22}.
