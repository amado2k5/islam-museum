## Overview
A thesis Ghallab relays from Ernest Renan's *Islam and Science*: that Islam stifled freedom of thought and learning, and that the Arabs knew no thinking until the translations {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 46} {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 47}. It circulated widely in Europe and was published in Egypt without rebuttal at the time; Ghallab returns to answer it {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 47}, presenting Carra de Vaux's account of the Qur'an as a book that poses philosophical questions in inspired form {cite:al-islam-kama-yarahu-al-urubiyyun-azhar p. 49}, and counting Renan among the writers who erred gravely against Islam.
