## Summary
In the lexicon sam' is the sense of the ear, and it also means answering, as in "God hears whoever praises Him"; the technical sense does not depart from it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9824}

## Related terms
Istima' is listening intended to understand or benefit from what is heard; sam' is broader, since it may happen with or without intention. Inssat is silence kept for listening. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9825}

## The general ruling
Hearing is among the blessings God bestowed, and it must be guarded from what He forbade. For most jurists it is nobler than sight, because it is the faculty that receives the address of the law by which one is charged, and it perceives from every direction and in every state. Whoever is to hold the imamate or judgeship must be able to hear: a deaf man may not be appointed imam, nor a judge who cannot hear. Listening to backbiting, obscene speech and forbidden singing is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9825}

## Compensation for loss of hearing by a wound
When hearing is lost through a spreading effect of a wound that carries no retaliation, a full blood-money is owed by agreement of the jurists, for example where the wound is accidental or where equal retaliation is impossible, as with the hashima. Ibn Qudama reports from Ibn al-Mundhir that the common scholars agree that blood-money is due for hearing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9825} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9826} A report from Umar and one from Mu'adh speak of blood-money for hearing; al-Bayhaqi cites a version in other words and weakens its chain. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9826} It is reported that a man struck another with a stone so that he lost his hearing, reason, tongue and marital capacity, and Umar gave a judgement of four blood-moneys. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9826}

## Where the wound carries retaliation
The jurists differ here. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that retaliation is due, the offender being treated in the same manner; if hearing is not lost by that, it is lost by treatment. The Malikis agree on the method but add that if hearing is not lost by retaliation it is not lost by treatment, and then blood-money is owed by the offender or his kin. The Hanafis hold that there is no retaliation for destroying hearing, since it cannot be matched. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9826}
The details are in the entries on retaliation, blood-money and wounds short of death; the basic entry on hearing is under listening and the ear. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9826}
