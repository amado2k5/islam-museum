## Summary
Al-Battani states that some chords, such as that of one degree and its multiples, cannot be derived by calculation along the road of proof as the others are, but it is known by proof that the ratio of the chord of a smaller arc to its arc is greater than that of a larger arc, so he relies on the known chords of a degree and a half and of a half and a quarter, and takes the chord of three quarters of a degree and adds a third of it to make the chord of one degree. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.18}.

## What proof does not reach
He says the chords not known by proof, that of one degree and its doublings, two, four and eight, are not derived by calculation along the road of proof as the other arcs and chords are, but it is known by proof that the chord-to-arc ratio is greater for the smaller arc than for the larger. So what pure proof cannot reach is held between two known limits. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.18}.

## The approximation through a degree and a half
He says the chords of one and a half degrees and of three quarters of a degree are known by proof, that two thirds of the chord of a degree and a half equals the chord of three quarters of a degree with a third of itself added, that there is no perceptible difference between them and no harm to the calculation from it. So when the chord of three quarters is taken and a third of it added, the sum is the chord of one degree. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.18}.

## Its effect on the tables
He closes by saying that once the chord of one degree is known this way, all the chords of the degrees of the semicircle are known too. So the whole table rests on what proof yields of chords and on this one declared approximation, which gives the reading of the tables a clear origin. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.18}.
