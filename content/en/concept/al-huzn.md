## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy distinguishes grief over what has passed (huzn) from worry about what is to come (hamm). He says that grief left untreated grows into harder states, among them kamad, bath, constriction of the chest and distress, and he compares this to a tree that sends out many branches. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:01:30} He says that the disease is grief planted in the heart, and that its cure is love of God and righteous action. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:24:00}

## Grief and worry: the distinction
The Sheikh separates grief over what has happened from worry, which is the anticipation of what may happen. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:03:00} He holds that grief over what is past, such as the loss of a land purchase, is blameworthy. He describes worry as the expectation of harm to work, health or the schooling of one's children, and holds that both turn the heart away from God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:05:30} He cites a hadith in which the Prophet seeks refuge from worry, grief and other conditions of the soul. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:05:30}

## The speaker's view: grief and faith
The Sheikh holds that grief which dominates a person's day is a sign of weak faith, and that strong faith protects against it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:03:00} He gives the example of a man whose father is in surgery and whose wife is distressed, and says that the man's grief shows how weak his faith is. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:12:00} He says that grief weakens faith, that weakened faith weakens the body's immunity, and that small illnesses then overcome people (needs_source: a medical claim not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:35:00}

## Kamad, bath and depression: what grief becomes
He describes kamad as a darkening of the face and brow in a grieving person, and says that it can grow into bath, a state in which a person is no longer gathered within himself, which he distinguishes from ordinary grief. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:01:00} He says that a person who reaches kamad becomes like something of no account, and that this is a disease whose remedy he will set out in its place. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:15:00}
He says that grief sinks into sorrow and then into depression, and that untreated depression brings fear of poverty and panic about the future, which he calls restlessness (needs_source: claims about the course of depression are not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:15:00}
He holds that forgetting God leads a person into depression and despair (needs_source: the causal claim is not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:05:30} He adds that grief over the past leads to depression and illness (needs_source: a medical claim not sourced in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:18:30}
He describes a constricted chest as making the world shrink in the eyes of its owner to the size of the eye of a needle, so that he overlooks blessings such as health and air. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:20:30} He cites a Qur'anic passage in which the Prophet is told not to destroy himself with grief over those who do not believe, and holds that one who reaches this state reaches kamad (the sura is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:24:00}

## The speaker's view: remedies
He advises listeners to hear the stories of the righteous and to rely on God, and holds that worry comes from a lack of reliance; when a problem comes, one should pray. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:28:00} He says that worries such as a job seeker's wait, a son failing at university or a daughter's suitor are solved by supplication, not by grief. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:03:30}
He cites a hadith that the Prophet turned to prayer when distress came upon him, and that patience and prayer are the means of help. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:33:30} He recounts the counsel of a sage among the Arabs: turn away from one's worries, entrust one's affairs to God, and relief comes through prayer, sincerity, seeking forgiveness and charity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:22:00}
He tells of a woman who did not let grief reach her heart, and advises placing a barrier between grief and oneself, and listening to the stories of the righteous. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:18:30}

## The speaker's view: grief gains nothing
He asks what a person gains from prolonged grief, and holds that trials are normal and are met with patience and self-training. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:21:00} He lists real trials, such as a sick wife, a harsh husband, a harmful neighbour, a disobedient son and failure at work, and says that the answer to them is patience and forbearance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:30:30} He says that grief removes blessing from a person's life, and fear destroys his sense of security. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:22:30}
He sums up that grief planted in the heart grows into worry, sorrow and constriction, and that the cure is love of God and righteous action. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep13 00:33:00}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh holds that all psychological illnesses come from two ailments, fear and grief, and says he prepared the programme 'Bustan al-Amal' to remove people's fear and grief; he advises not grieving over the past, which is over, 'a cheque already cashed', and not fearing tomorrow before it comes, a cheque not yet due, and recites verses on turning away from worries and leaving matters to decree (needs_source: the verses as he recites them). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep06 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep06 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep06 00:06:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh asks the grieving and depressed: does anything happen in the universe against God's will? Does your grief bring back the lost one or the money, or heal the sick? He holds that grief only adds worry, tension, headaches and blood pressure and takes away the blessing of life and health, and quotes [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali]] that he lives by hope: if it comes true, it is by God's grace, and if not, he has lived by it for a time. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep21 00:10:00}
