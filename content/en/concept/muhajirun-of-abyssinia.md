## Summary
"The Abyssinian emigrants" is the expression by which Ibn Ishaq gathers the Prophet's companions who emigrated to the land of Abyssinia; he divides them by their return to the Prophet: those carried in the two ships to Khaybar, then "the rest of the Abyssinian emigrants", then the women emigrants, then the children born there. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/3-11}

## The categories and numbers
- Those carried in the two ships: sixteen men. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/5}
- The rest of the emigrants — those who came only after Badr or later, or died in Abyssinia: thirty-four men. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/10}
- The men who died in Abyssinia: seven persons, plus one of the sons. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/10}
- The women emigrants, those who came and those who died: sixteen women, besides their daughters born there. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/10}
- Those born there: five males and five females. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/11-12}
