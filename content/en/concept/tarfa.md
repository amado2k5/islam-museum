## Nature
Dioscorides says this is a known tree that grows by standing water, with a fruit like the flower, similar in texture to lichen. In Egypt and Syria there is a garden tamarisk, like the wild kind in every way except the fruit. Its fruit resembles gall nut, is toothed, and grips the tongue, so it is used in its place in eye medicines and mouth medicines. It suits spitting of blood when drunk, and diarrhoea. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.499}

## Properties
It has astringency, cleansing and purging without strong drying. Its water is a cooling drier; its cleansing is greater than its drying, and its drying comes with astringency. Its fruit is very astringent, and it has a little fineness that the green gall nut lacks. In other cases it is used in place of gall nut. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.499}

## Adornment
Its decoction is used as a wash on lice, which it kills. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.499}

## Swellings and pustules
Its leaf is applied as a poultice to soft swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.499}

## Wounds and ulcers
Its smoke dries moist ulcers; its fruit and its ash dry stubborn ulcers and eat away proud flesh. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.499}

## Organs of the head
A decoction of its leaf in wine, used as a rinse, helps toothache and prevents the teeth from decaying. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.499}

## Organs of the breath
It helps chronic spitting of blood, especially its fruit. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Organs of digestion
Its twigs, steeped in vinegar and applied as a poultice, help the spleen; a drink boiled with its leaf and twigs is given for the spleen, and cups are made from its wood for those with spleen trouble. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Poisons
Its fruit helps the bite of the tarantula. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}

## Excretory organs
It helps chronic diarrhoea. For a flow from the womb the book lists sitting in its decoction, using its seed as a suppository, and drinking its fruit. These are the book's listings, labelled. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.500}
