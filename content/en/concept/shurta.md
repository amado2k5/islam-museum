## Summary
The police (shurtah) during Ali's caliphate held an important, recognized office in the state; Al-Sallabi lists his police commanders and describes their role, which extended beyond law enforcement to social service {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}.

## Police Commanders
Ali's police commanders included Abu al-Hayyaj al-Asadi, Qays ibn Sa'd ibn Ubadah, Ma'qil ibn Qays al-Riyahi, Malik ibn Khubayb al-Yarbu'i, Al-Asbagh ibn Nubatah al-Mujashi'i, and Sa'id ibn Sariyah ibn Murrah al-Khuza'i {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 272}.

## Work of the Police
Al-Sallabi states that the social function of the police included helping the needy, aiding the distressed, guiding the lost, feeding the poor, and offering assistance and gentleness, and that these acts of aid were intended for the sake of God {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 272}. He concludes that the civilizational role of the police in the Rashidun era holds major significance in the book's assessment {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 272}.

## Prison
The book notes that prison was called "al-mukhayyas," because benefit is the opposite of harm and takhyis means taming and disciplining, so both names fulfill the purposes of imprisonment {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}. Prisoners were provided with sustenance consisting of food, condiments, and clothing in winter and summer {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}.

## Example Case
The book recounts that Ali summoned the police in the case of a man who died while traveling with a group, when his property could not be accounted for {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 271}. (See [[event/ali-police-inquiry-missing-merchant]].)

## Notes for Researchers
- In describing the police, the book relies on modern references (Wilayat al-Shurtah fi al-Islam by Al-Humaydani); the original sources should be consulted before generalizing this picture.
