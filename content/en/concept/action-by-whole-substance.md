## Summary
What acts by its substance acts by the specific form that makes it what it is, not by its quality. This form arises after the temperament and is neither heat, cold, moisture nor dryness but resembles colour, smell and soul. Ibn Sina calls this action by the whole of the substances, meaning by the form of the species and not by the four qualities or the temperament that comes from them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.132}.

## Beneficial and harmful
His examples include the attracting power of the magnet and the nature of each kind of animal and plant acquired after the temperament. Its action on the human body may be beneficial, as the peony's stopping of epilepsy, or harmful, as the power of aconite that corrupts the human substance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.132}.
