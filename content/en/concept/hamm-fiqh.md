## Summary
Hamm in the legal sense is the resolve of the heart to do something, good or bad, before it is done; it is stronger than a passing thought. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16554} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16562}

## Resolve to good and evil
Most scholars hold that one who resolves on a good deed without doing it has a full good deed recorded, and one who resolves on an evil deed and leaves it for God's sake has a good deed recorded. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16556} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16557}
The jurists differ on the reward of one who left an evil deed for another reason, such as inability or fear of people. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16557} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16558}

## Firm resolve and accountability
Al-Baqillani and others hold that the resolve that is not recorded is a passing thought without settled purpose; a firm resolve is held against the person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16558} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16559}
The jurists differ on firm resolve to an act of the limbs: many hold the person accountable for it, while others hold it is reproach, not punishment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16559} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16560} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16561}

## Resolve to sin within the Sacred Precinct
Some jurists hold that resolve to sin inside the Meccan Sacred Precinct is held to account even short of firm intent, while others hold the pardon for resolve general in and outside the Precinct. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16561} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16562}

## Resolve to unbelief
Resolving on unbelief, or firmly intending it for the future, is unbelief at once, according to al-Nawawi and others; a passing whisper does not make one an unbeliever. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16562}
