## Summary
Maryam Ekhtiar counts the rise of kufic in the late seventh to early eighth century, and its spread, as a major development in the history of [[concept/islamic-calligraphy|Islamic calligraphy]]. The name comes from [[place/kufa|Kufa]], but it denotes a category of angular scripts with many variations, not necessarily a place of production. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23}

## Features
Kufic followed precise rules for calibrated letters with geometric proportion and regularity, and so replaced [[concept/hijazi-script|hijazi]] as the preferred script for copying the Qur'an, a status it kept until about the mid- to late tenth century. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23} It is marked by clarity, generous spacing and the horizontal extension of letters along the baseline. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23} The earliest dinars, dated 76 AH/696–97 CE, bear Qur'anic verses in an early formal kufic {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23}; see [[concept/quranic-inscriptions-on-coins|Qur'anic inscriptions on coins]] and [[event/abd-al-malik-coinage|the coinage reform]]. A dated architectural example is the inscription around the ambulatory of the [[building/dome-of-the-rock|Dome of the Rock]], 72 AH/691–92 CE. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23}

## Kufic Qur'ans
Qur'ans of the eighth to tenth centuries were mostly written on parchment, in vertical, square and oblong formats; from the second half of the eighth century the oblong format with a few lines a page became standard. Most early kufic Qur'ans were built of quinions — quires of five folded sheets — bound in leather and kept in chests. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23} A bifolium from an Abbasid Qur'an at The Met carries seven lines of bold kufic; the horizontal stretching of letters may also have signalled the reciter to prolong a letter or word, vowels are red or green dots, and consonant points are largely absent. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 32} Once the [[concept/new-style-script|new style]] and the rounded scripts took over, kufic was confined to ornament such as sura headings. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 24}
