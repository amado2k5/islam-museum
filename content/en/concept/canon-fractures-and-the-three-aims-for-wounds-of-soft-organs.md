## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter 28, on the treatment of breaks in continuity, with bones and then soft organs, and shows that three principles are observed: stopping what flows, joining the cleft and preventing putrefaction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.306-307}.

## Large organs
A break in continuity in the large organs is treated by setting and the suitable bandage described in the art of bone-setting, which will come in its place, then by rest and by sealing food from which a gristly nourishment is hoped to arise to tighten and fit the lips of the fracture, such as kafshir. For it is impossible for bone to knit, especially in grown bodies, except in this manner; otherwise it never returns to continuity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.306-307}.

## Soft organs
For a break in continuity in the soft organs the aim in treating it is to keep three principles, if the cause is fixed. First comes cutting off what flows, and cutting off its matter if a neighbouring part has matter. Second is joining the cleft with suitable drugs and foods. Third is preventing putrefaction as far as possible. When one of the three suffices, the care is turned to the others {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.307}.
