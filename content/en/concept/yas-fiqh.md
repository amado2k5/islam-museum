Yas (despair). Lexically a verbal noun from "yasa", meaning despair, the opposite of hope, or the cutting of hope; it also names the age at which menstruation ends, and a barren woman is called yasa. In one dialect it means knowledge. Technically it is the cutting of hope. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17965}

Despair of God's mercy is forbidden and among the great sins, on the verse in surah Yusuf (verse 87). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17966}

Despair of water is a cause for tayammum in the four schools. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17966}

Repentance of one in despair: the majority (Malikis, Shafi'is, and a Hanafi view) hold that the repentance of one who sees the signs of death is not accepted, on the verse in surah al-Nisa (verse 18). The Hanbalis accept repentance until the death rattle, on the hadith of Ibn 'Umar, and Ibn Rajab explains the rattle as the soul leaving the heart so that no intention remains. A second Hanbali view accepts it until the angel is seen, a view of al-Hasan and Mujahid; the narrations of Abu Musa and Ali are cited, and al-Busayri judged the Abu Musa hadith weak. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17966} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17967}

The Hanafis (in the preferred view), the Hanbalis (in the school's view) and some Malikis hold that the repentance of a believing sinner is accepted even at the death rattle, unlike the despair of one who disbelieves at death; a Hanbali view allows repentance while one is still of sound mind, which al-Mardawi prefers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17967}

Age of despair: the jurists differ whether there is a fixed age after which a woman has no menses; some say there is none, others that a set age ends it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17967}

Waiting period: the waiting period of a woman past menstruation through age, and of a girl who has not menstruated and can bear intercourse, is three months. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17967}
