## Summary
Who set language down: is it revelation from God, or a convention the people agreed on? Al-Suyuti gathered the scholars' answers in al-Muzhir, among them [[person/ibn-faris|Ibn Faris]], who held for tawqif, [[person/ibn-jinni|Ibn Jinni]] and his teacher [[person/abu-ali-al-farisi|Abu Ali al-Farisi]], whose words leaned to convention, and others who took a middle course or suspended judgement. The page gives each view under its holder and does not weigh them.

## The definition of language and where the question arises
In al-Khasa'is, Ibn Jinni opens a chapter on what language is and says: "As for its definition, it is sounds by which every people express their purposes." He adds that lugha is a fu'la form from laghawtu, I spoke, with plurals lughat and lughun, and that the disagreement about it he treats in another chapter, whether it is inspiration or convention {cite:khasais-najjar 1 p.34}. In that chapter he says most people of reflection hold that the origin of language is convention and agreement, not revelation or tawqif, and then mentions that Abu Ali once told him it is from God {cite:khasais-najjar 1 p.41}.

## The view of tawqif
In al-Muzhir, al-Suyuti takes up the second question, on who set language: is it tawqif and revelation, or convention and agreement. The first he names is Ibn Faris in Fiqh al-Lugha, quoted as saying: "Know that the language of the Arabs is tawqif." He argues from the verse [[passage/quran-2-31|al-Baqara 31]] and says Ibn Abbas interpreted the names mentioned in it as the names people know, such as animal, earth, plain, mountain, camel and donkey {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.12}. Al-Suyuti then remarks that this is all Ibn Faris's speech, and that he was of Ahl al-Sunna {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.14}.

## The view of convention
Al-Suyuti states that Ibn Jinni and his teacher Abu Ali al-Farisi were both Mu'tazilis, and gives Ibn Jinni's saying that most people of reflection hold the origin of language to be convention, not tawqif. Abu Ali once told him it is from God, citing the verse of al-Baqara; Ibn Jinni replied that it may mean God enabled Adam to set conventions on names, and that in some of his speech Abu Ali held the opposing view {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.14}. Ibn Jinni also mentions an interpretation that God taught Adam the names of everything in every language, Arabic, Persian, Syriac, Hebrew and Greek {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.14}.

Al-Suyuti reports from Ibn Burhan, in al-Wusul ila al-Usul, that the Mu'tazila said all languages are conventional, and that a group said tawqif {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.21}.

## The middle course and the suspension of judgement
Al-Suyuti then gives further views from Ibn Burhan. The Ustadh Abu Ishaq al-Isfara'ini said that the minimum by which a person calls another to convention is tawqif, and the rest may be either tawqif or convention. Al-Qadi Abu Bakr, that is [[person/al-baqillani|al-Baqillani]], said both are possible, and suspended judgement on which occurred, saying he was pausing. In al-Burhan, Imam al-Haramayn holds that the choice is that reason allows both, that there is no decisive textual proof for what happened, and that the verse of al-Baqara is not decisive for either side {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.21} {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.22}.

## The views in brief
The views reported come to five: tawqif, held by Ibn Faris and a group; convention, held by the Mu'tazila as Ibn Burhan reports and by Ibn Jinni; the distinction of al-Isfara'ini, that the first minimum is tawqif and what follows may be either; suspension of judgement, held by al-Baqillani; and the allowing of both by reason, with no decisive proof, held by Imam al-Haramayn. Each view is ascribed here to its holder as al-Suyuti gives it {cite:muzhir-mansur-1998 1 p.21}.

## What this page does not reach
Ibn Faris's words were read as al-Suyuti quotes them, not in his own book. Ibn Burhan's book and Imam al-Haramayn's were not read, only what al-Suyuti transmits from them. On page 20 al-Suyuti gives objections whose authors are not named in the text read, so they are not given here. What Abu Ali al-Farisi wrote himself was not examined.
