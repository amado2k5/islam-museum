## Summary
Ibn Sina divides food into fine, coarse and moderate. The fine yields thin blood and the coarse yields thick blood; each is then divided into richly nourishing and slightly nourishing, and into good chyme and bad chyme {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.134}.

## Examples of fine and coarse
Fine and richly nourishing: wine, meat broth and warmed egg yolk. Coarse and slightly nourishing: cheese, dried meat and aubergine. Coarse and richly nourishing: boiled egg and beef. Fine and slightly nourishing: julep, vegetables of moderate consistency and quality, and fruits such as apples and pomegranates {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.134}.

## Good and bad chyme
Fine, richly nourishing and of good chyme: egg yolk, wine and meat broth. Fine, slightly nourishing and of good chyme: lettuce, apple and pomegranate. Fine, slightly nourishing and of bad chyme: radish, mustard and most vegetables. Fine, richly nourishing and of bad chyme: lung. Coarse, richly nourishing and of good chyme: boiled egg and the meat of a yearling sheep. Coarse, richly nourishing and of bad chyme: beef, duck and horse meat. Coarse, slightly nourishing and of bad chyme: dried meat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.134}.
