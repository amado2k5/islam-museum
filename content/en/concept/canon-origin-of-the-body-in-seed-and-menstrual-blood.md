## Summary
Ibn Sina explains the body's moistness from its first formation: its first principle is two things, the man's seed and the woman's seed with menstrual blood, both fluid and moist, so their first setting is moist, while earthy and fiery parts harden what has set only to a limited degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.

## The two principles
The first principle of our bodies is two things: the man's seed, which he holds to be the better view stands in the place of the agent, and the woman's seed with menstrual blood, which stands in the place of the matter. They share being fluid and moist, but watery and earthy parts are more in the blood and the woman's seed, and airy and fiery parts predominate in the man's seed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.

## A moist setting, hardened only in part
So their first setting had to be moist, even though earthy and fiery parts are present too. The earthy part with its hardness and the fiery part with its ripening help one another and harden and knot what has set somewhat. Yet this does not reach the hardness of stone or glass, from which nothing dissolves or only something imperceptible, which would keep a body safe from the afflictions that come from dissolution. The body is not like that {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.200}.
