## Summary
The second pair divides among the muscles of the eyeball; the third has four branches going to the viscera, face, teeth and tongue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.78-79}.

## The second pair
It begins behind the first and leans outward from it, leaves by the hole in the socket that holds the eyeball and divides among the eyeball's muscles. It has no helper, since the third is given to moving a great organ, the lower jaw {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.78}.

## The third pair
It begins at the boundary between the front and back of the brain, mixes briefly with the fourth, parts from it and divides into four branches. The first leaves at the entry of the carotid vessel and descends past the neck beyond the diaphragm to spread through the viscera below it. The second leaves by a hole in the temple bone and joins a nerve separated from the fifth. The third rises through the hole of the second and divides in three: one part to the muscles of the temples, of chewing, the brow, the forehead and the eyelid; one to the inside of the nose; and one descending in the hollow of the cheekbone and splitting into a branch to the teeth and upper gum and a branch to the skin of the cheek, the nose tip and the upper lip. The fourth passes to the tongue and spreads in its outer layer, giving it its own sense, taste; what is left spreads to the lower teeth and gums and the lower lip {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.78-79}.
