## The roots of treatment and regimen
The treatment of the nazla is confined to: lessening the matter, countering the effective cause, cutting the flow or adjusting it or moving it to another quarter, and forestalling whatever may be bred of it — chaps in the nose, ulcers on the nostril, harshness in the throat — while leaving food-and-drink fullness. Sneezing is harmful at the nazla's first arising; catarrh prevents the ripening of the brain's humours, which ripen only in stillness, yet it also draws other wastes to itself, and after ripening it is of the greatest use for what it evacuates of the ripened surplus. He who suffers zukam and nazla must not sleep on a belly full of food lest his head fill; he must keep warming the head and removing cold from it, shield it from the north wind — especially right after the south wind, for the south fills and loosens it while the north grips and squeezes — drink less iced water, not sleep by day, and bear thirst, hunger and wakefulness as far as possible: this is the root of the treatment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.242}
## Lessening the matter and countering the cause
Laxation and drawing blood are begun with, then laxation after it, when need calls for both; venesection is rarely hastened, especially at the start, except for an unbearable fullness — and the likeliest nazla not to be venesected is one with a cough: if the cough has little expectoration a small repeated letting is indispensable, for it may run to recurrences. A syrup of the known kind is used if there is sleeplessness, otherwise of the other known kind; the clyster draws the surplus down and softens the path. If a stitch is felt with the nazla, piercing it, it shows the matter is inclining to the side: hasten and venesect. Smokes may breed fever and worsen the cough through chest-roughness, not head-matter. Thirst must be endured and broken by a tempered syrup of the known kind with water; for strengthening, barley water and sawiq. No bath while fever is with the nazla; for one whose catarrhs last summer and winter, a pill of the known kind is among the most useful of remedies, and moving the lower members benefits the catarrhs greatly ⁦(the drink, pill and drug names are not carried)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.242} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.243}
## Countering the hot and the cold causes
As for countering the effective cause — if hot: strive to cool the head with what is cooling in power, like entering the fresh bath each dawn fasting, pouring water on the limbs, smearing head, limbs, navel and throat with an oil of the known kind ⁦(the line adding a further site, named by the editor as the male member, is kept as a labelled listing for the founder's ruling)⁩, using a douche made of the known kind, pouring strongly acting coolants on the head, inclining the foods to what is light, cooling and moist, and taking the electuary daily. If cold: strive, as soon as the tickling and sneezing begin, to warm the head and foment it with heated cloths until the heat is felt reaching the brain, keeping the head at that pitch; sometimes salt and a thing of the known kind are needed in it, sometimes fomenting with the hottest bearable water, using ripening dissolving douches, anointing the limbs with hot oils of the known kind, and washing the head with the soap kind ⁦(the oil, water, soap, plaster and reddening-drug names are not carried)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.243}
## Cutting, adjusting and moving the flow, and forestalling
Cutting the flow: by cold congealing gargles of the known kind — cold for the hot nazla, hot for the cold — by smearing the throat with a syrup of the known kind, by holding in the mouth pills made of the known kind without swallowing their water, by drinks with that special property, and by astringent fumigations made to enter the nasal fossa or a fume-holding throat-tent ⁦(the water, drink, fumigation, pill and drug names are not carried)⁩. Adjusting the consistency: linctuses, holding quince-seed in the mouth so its thickness mixes with the thinness of what descends — thickening and congealing it so it does not sink deep and is easy to expectorate — or thinners so its thickness and stickiness do not hurt. If the nazla is cold the bath is not right before ripening; if hot, there is little harm — it even benefits. Moving it to another quarter: treating the throat-bound nazla by drawing it to the nose with sternutatories and all that stings the nostrils, and treating every hot nazla flowing downward with cupping on the nucha ⁦(the cupping site carried as in the book)⁩, and by keeping at douches made of herbs that draw the matter toward the nose. Forestalling: guarding the throat and lung from its harm, mostly by foods ⁦(the food, oil, linctus and drink names are not carried)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.243} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.244} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.245}
