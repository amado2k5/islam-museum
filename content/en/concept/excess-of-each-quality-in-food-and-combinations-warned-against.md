## Summary
Ibn Sina says too much dry food kills appetite and spoils colour, fatty food makes one sluggish, cold food slackens, sour food brings old age and salty food harms the stomach and the eye; he cites Galen on moist food, then relays the warnings of people of experience among the Indians and others against milk with sours and fish with milk, which they said bring chronic illness including leprosy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.235}.

## Excess of a quality
Too much dry food drops the appetite, spoils the colour and dries the nature; too much fatty food makes one sluggish and takes the appetite away; too much cold makes one sluggish and slack; too much sour brings old age, and likewise pungent; and salty harms the stomach and the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.235}.

## Moist and porous
Galen said moist food is that which leaves every quality, as if bland: neither sweet, sour, bitter, pungent, astringent nor salty. Viscid food descends more slowly, cucumber with its skin passes faster than peeled, and likewise bread with bran faster than sifted {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.235}.

## What the experienced said
People of experience among the Indians and others said milk should not be eaten with sours nor fish with milk, since they bring chronic diseases, among them leprosy; nor mung with cheese or with the flesh of birds, nor barley or wheat meal with milk; nor fat that stood in a copper vessel used in cooking, nor meat roasted on castor coals {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.235}.

## Why mixed foods harm
Different foods harm in two ways: they differ in digestion, some of what is eaten digesting and some not, and one can take more of them than of a single dish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.235}.
