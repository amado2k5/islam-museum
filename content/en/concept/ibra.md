## Summary
The Kuwaiti Encyclopedia of Fiqh defines release (ibra') as a person dropping a right he holds against another's liability or in his hands. It distinguishes it from acquittal, mubara'a, istibra', dropping, gift, settlement, acknowledgement, guarantee, remission and waiver. It reports that the jurists differ on whether release is a dropping or a transfer, and that the majority hold it to be a dropping. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1415} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1420} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1421}

## Related terms
Release, in the lexicon, is clearing or distancing oneself from a thing; as a technical term it is a person dropping a right he holds against another's liability or in his hands. Where no one owes the right, as with a right of pre-emption or a bequeathed right of residence, leaving it is a pure dropping, not a release. The Encyclopedia treats dropping as the wider term: every release is a dropping, but not the reverse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1415} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1417}

It distinguishes release from acquittal, which is its effect and may come about through payment, delivery, a stipulation such as an acquittal from defects, or the removal of the cause of liability. It distinguishes it from mubara'a, a name for khul' between spouses in which the wife drops her claims against the husband. It distinguishes it from istibra', which means ascertaining that a womb is clear or cleansing the two passages. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1415} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1416}

Release of a debt to its debtor is, for the majority, the same as a gift of the debt; the Hanafis differ on the power to retract. It is distinguished from settlement, where a partial settlement of an admitted claim resembles release. It is distinguished from acknowledgement: a claim of release is an acknowledgement that the liability exists, together with a claim that it has been dropped. The Encyclopedia notes that the Shafi'is placed most rulings of release under guarantee, since the two are opposites. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1417} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1418} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1419}

## Its ruling
Release is permitted in general, and each of the five legal categories can apply. It is obligatory where a receipt has preceded it, since that is an acknowledgement owed to the one entitled. It is forbidden when it appears within a void contract, since keeping a void arrangement in place is forbidden. It is disliked when a person in terminal illness releases his heir or another for more than a third of his estate, where the heirs permit it. Its general ruling is recommendation, being a form of kindness; al-Khatib al-Shirbini states that release is recommended, so it is treated leniently, unlike guarantee. The Encyclopedia cites the Qur'anic command to grant time to a debtor in hardship, [[passage/quran-2-280|al-Baqara 280]], and reports hadiths urging creditors to release debts, including the report of Jabir on the debts of his father and the reports of Ka'b ibn Malik and Mu'adh ibn Jabal. Some Shafi'is held release of a debt to a debtor in hardship better than a loan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1419} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1420}

Outside these cases, release stays on the default of permissibility that governs most transactions, especially where the releasor cannot recover his right from one who denies it, since kindness does not arise there. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1421}

## Its kinds
Some authors divide release into release by dropping and release by receipt. The second is an acknowledgement of payment and a kind of acknowledgement. The difference matters in guarantee: if the creditor says to the guarantor "I have been paid the money by you", both guarantor and debtor are freed and the guarantor may recover from the debtor, because it is a release by receipt. If he says "you are released from the money" or "I release you", without that wording, it is a dropping with no right of recovery. The Encyclopedia says it has not found this division stated explicitly outside the Hanafi school, although the other schools have cases in which they distinguish the two. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1420} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1421}

## Dropping or transfer
The jurists differ on whether release is a dropping or a transfer of ownership. The majority of Hanafis, a view among Malikis and Shafi'is, and the prevailing Hanbali view hold it to be a dropping; al-Subki argued that if it were a transfer, release of specific things would be valid. Some Shafi'is and Ibn Muflih hold it a transfer in one respect, whose purpose is dropping. Ibn Muflih also reports some Hanbalis holding it a transfer. Ibn al-Sam'ani holds it a transfer by the releasor and a dropping for the released, outside the case of divorce, since a debt counts as property only in the eyes of the creditor. Al-Nawawi holds that no single answer is preferred: the matter is decided case by case. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1421} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1422}

The difference has effects. The Hanafis allowed a creditor to appoint his debtor to release himself, viewing it as a dropping, but not on the view that it is a transfer. The Hanbalis hold that release does not count for zakat, since the requirement of ownership is absent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1422}

## Elements and wording
By the majority, release has four elements: the wording, the releasor, the released, and the matter released. For the Hanafis only the wording is an element. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1422}

Release is effected by any words that clearly show the dropping of the right, expressly or by an indication with a supporting context, whether standalone or within another contract. It must exclude any sense of exchange or mere postponement: "I release you on condition you give me such-and-such" is a settlement for money, and "I release you from the due date of the debt" is a postponement of the demand. A release without limit is for ever by general agreement, so a temporary release such as "I release you from what I have against you for a year" is not valid. Words used include: dropping, transfer, substitution, permitting, remission, reduction, leaving, charity, gift and grant. The Encyclopedia relies on custom and context. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1422} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1423} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1424}

The Encyclopedia cites Qur'anic uses of pardon and charity for release of dower and blood-money, namely [[passage/quran-2-237|al-Baqara 237]], [[passage/quran-4-92|al-Nisa' 92]] and [[passage/quran-2-280|al-Baqara 280]], for the vocabulary only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1423}

## Acceptance and rejection
The jurists differ on whether release needs acceptance. The majority (Hanafis, the sounder Shafi'i view, and the Hanbalis) hold that it does not, since it is a dropping, like divorce, manumission and the dropping of pre-emption or retaliation. Malikis in the sounder view and a Shafi'i view hold that it does need acceptance, since it transfers ownership. Al-Qarafi says the requirement of acceptance is the apparent Maliki position, lest a favour be imposed on people of honour, so that they may accept or refuse. The Hanafis hold that where a contract requires delivery in the session, such as exchange or salam, release from its payment needs acceptance, because it would undo the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1423} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1424} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1425}

Where acceptance is not needed, release is not undone by refusal according to the Hanbalis, the sounder Shafi'i view and the less sound Maliki view. Where acceptance is needed, refusal undoes it according to the sounder Maliki view and the other Shafi'i view. The Hanafis exempt some cases from refusal: release within a referral (hawala), and release of a guarantor on the sounder view, since it is a pure dropping; and where the released person has asked for release before it was given, or has already accepted it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1425} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1426}

## Conditions on the releasor
The releasor must have full capacity to contract and, specifically, the capacity to make a gift: he must be of sound judgment and not restrained for prodigality or indebtedness. He must have authority over the right released, as owner, as an appointed agent, or as an unauthorised agent whose act the owner later approves, on the view that such acts are valid. The reality of the matter counts, not belief: a person who releases a debt of his father believing him alive is valid if the father had in fact died before the release. Consent is required; release under duress is invalid, and a release made in jest is invalid because it acknowledges a discharged liability. The Hanbalis hold that concealing the amount of the debt from the creditor, for fear he would not release if he knew it, vitiates consent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1426} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1427}

Appointment as agent for release needs specific authority; a general authority to contract does not suffice. The Hanafis hold that where an agent is authorised to release and appoints another to do it, the act is not valid. If the creditor appoints a group of debtors to release their creditors and one of them is among them, he does not release himself on the sounder view, unless he is expressly permitted to do so. A person in terminal illness cannot effectively release a debtor where the estate is wholly consumed by debts, since the rights of the creditors are engaged; and where the released person is an heir, the whole release depends on the consent of the heirs, even if the debt is less than a third. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1427} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1428}

## The released and the matter released
Knowledge of the released person is required, and he must be identified. Release of one of several debtors on uncertainty is invalid, except where the releasor means specific debtors or a limited set of people. The released person need not admit the debt; release of a denier is valid, even after he has sworn an oath. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1428}

On whether release of an unknown debt is valid, there are three positions. The majority (Hanafis, Malikis and one Hanbali narration) hold it valid, since release is a gift and the gift of an unknown thing is permitted, and since the ignorance of what is dropped does not lead to dispute. The second, a Hanbali narration, holds it valid if knowledge is impossible, and invalid if the releasee concealed it. The third, the Shafi'i view and one Hanbali narration, holds it invalid in every case. The Shafi'is except two cases: release of an unknown blood-money, and release framed by a limit the releasor is certain lies below the debt. Some Shafi'is treat the unknown as the unknown that is not easily ascertained, not the unknown that is easily ascertained, such as release of one's share of an estate whose total can be known. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1428} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1429}

## Conditions on the release itself
Release must not change a ruling of the law, such as release from the requirement of mutual receipt in exchange, from a right to take back a gift or bequest (with a Maliki dispute), from residence in the period of waiting, or from guardianship over a minor, since whatever changes what the law has set is void. Nor may it cause the loss of another's right, such as releasing a divorced mother from custody when the custodian also has a right. Release requires that the releasor already own the right released, since no one may dispose of another's property without authority or unauthorised action. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1429}
