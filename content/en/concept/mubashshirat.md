## Summary
Mubashshirat are true dreams from God that bring the believer glad tidings; the Prophet said the mubashshirat are what remains of prophethood after revelation ceased {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:muwatta-malik}.

## The texts in the hadith
Muslim relates from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said: "Nothing remains of prophethood except the mubashshirat." They asked: "And what are the mubashshirat?" He said: "The righteous dream." {cite:sahih-muslim}. The Muwatta carries two reports that add a benefit to this matn: from Ata' ibn Yasar, "Nothing will remain of prophethood after me except the mubashshirat." They asked: "And what are they?" He said: "The righteous dream which a righteous man sees, or which is shown to him — one forty-sixth of prophethood" {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.546}; and from Abu Qatada ibn Rib'i, who heard the Prophet say: "The righteous dream is from God, and the bad dream is from Satan" {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.547}.

The Muwatta also relates from Hisham ibn Urwa from his father that he used to say: the verse that speaks of "in this world and the next" [[passage/quran-10-64|Yunus 64]] is the mubashshirat — the righteous dream a righteous man sees, or which is shown to him {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.547}. Thus the hadith binds the mubashshirat to [[concept/ahlam|dreams]] and [[concept/ruya-fiqh|the fiqh of the dream]].

## The mubashshirat in the Qur'an
Hisham ibn Urwa took the glad tidings of [[passage/quran-10-64|Yunus 64]] to be the righteous dream; the verse came in the setting of the Prophet's being commanded to bear patience with the people of Mecca {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.547} {cite:tanzil-quran}. Hence the mubashshirat connect to [[concept/tafsir-al-ahlam-al-yawm|dream interpretation today]] and [[concept/taabir-al-ruya|dream interpretation]].

## In the seerah
The Prophet gave his Companions glad tidings through their dreams and they took heart; among them Ibn Zayd's dream of the adhan, which the Prophet confirmed and by which Umar learned of it [[passage/muwatta-1-172|Muwatta 1:172]], A'isha's dream of three moons [[passage/muwatta-2-623|Muwatta 2:623]], the Companions' dream of Laylat al-Qadr [[passage/muwatta-5-894|Muwatta 894]] [[passage/muwatta-5-895|Muwatta 895]], and the well-attested dream of [[concept/laylat-al-qadr|Laylat al-Qadr]]. When they asked: does revelation disclose anything to you? He said: "Yes, the good dream is one forty-sixth of prophethood", as in the Muwatta's reports {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.545}.

## In practice
It was the Prophet's custom after the morning prayer to ask: did any of you see a dream last night? If a righteous man saw a good dream he gave him glad tidings of it {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.546}. The fiqh books mention the mubashshirat in the chapters of dreams and ritual washing {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## Perspectives
- **Ahl al-Sunna:** the mubashshirat are among God's honours to the believer; no obligation is built on them and their neglect does not make one a disbeliever.
- **The jurists:** the mubashshirat have no effect on legal rulings; they are glad tidings, not a charge.
