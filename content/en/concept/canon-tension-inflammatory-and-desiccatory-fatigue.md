## Summary
Ibn Sina describes three kinds of fatigue: in the tension kind the sufferer feels as if his body were bruised, with heat and stretching; in the inflammatory kind the body is hotter than usual and like something swollen; the desiccatory is a state as if the body had been seized by excessive dryness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.

## The tension kind
In the tension kind the sufferer feels as if his body had been bruised, with heat and stretching, and dislikes movement, even stretching, above all when it comes from weariness. It arises from superfluities held in the muscles which are of good substance and have no sting, or from wind, and lightness or heaviness tells the two apart. It often comes from incomplete sleep; when it comes after full sleep that is a different matter and the worst of the kinds, the severest being what draws the muscle fibres taut in a straight line {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.

## The inflammatory and the desiccatory
In the inflammatory kind the body is hotter than usual and like a swollen body in size and colour, and in being hurt by touch and movement, with stretching felt as well. The desiccatory is a state in which the person feels in his body as if dryness and aridity had overtaken it; it comes from excessive exercise with good chyme followed by a rough restorative rubbing, and may come from dry air, from taking little food and from fasting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.
