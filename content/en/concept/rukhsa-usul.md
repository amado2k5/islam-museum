## Summary
Rukhsa in the lexicon is ease and permission after a prohibition; in usage it is what is made lighter for the person in doing something, for a cause that prevents him from the strict ruling while the forbidding cause remains. It is set against 'azima, and is close to permission and to lifting hardship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8675} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8676} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8677} Its causes are necessity, need, travel, illness, forgetting and compulsion; its kinds are obligatory, recommended, permitted and against the better course for the Shafi'is, and real and figurative for the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8678} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8679} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8680} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8681} Some jurists hold that the strict ruling is better in some cases, such as compelled unbelief when refusing would mean death, and fasting on a journey. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8681} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8682} Analogy from a concession is allowed by the majority and refused by the Hanafis, except Abu Yusuf. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8687} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8688} Taking up concessions merely to escape duty is condemned; taking them for a lawful cause is permitted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8688} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8689}
