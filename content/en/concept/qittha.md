## Choice
Its seed is better than the seed of khiyar, and the best and finest is the ripe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Temperament
Cold and moist up to the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Actions and properties
It calms heat and bile, but its chyme is bad, prone to putrefaction and rouses severe fevers; the watermelon spoils faster than it; its ripe fruit has a cleansing power; the khiyar is further from digestion, goes raw into the vessels and breeds chronic fevers; nankhwah or a strong heat in the stomach repels its harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.657}

## Swellings and pustules
Its leaf, placed with honey on phlegmatic urticaria, benefits it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.657}

## Respiratory organs
When one with a hot fainting smells it, he benefits and revives {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.657}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It quenches thirst and is good for the stomach, though it is rarely digested well; its root drunk in honeyed wine causes the vomiting of a thin humour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.657}

## Excretory organs
It has a power of driving urine and softening, benefits pains of the testes and suits the bladder; it is below the ripe in driving urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.657}

## Poisons and bites
Its leaf benefits the bite of a rabid dog {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.657}
