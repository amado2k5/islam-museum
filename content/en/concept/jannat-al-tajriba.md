## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the garden in which Adam dwelt was a garden of trial for applying the method, not the eternal Garden {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.258}. He argues from the duty it carried, since the Hereafter has no duty {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.260}. See [[passage/quran-7-19|al-A'raf 19]] and [[passage/quran-18-32|al-Kahf 32]].

## Argument from its features
He notes that God allowed Adam and his wife to eat as they pleased, and that the garden had many kinds of provision so that the soul would not tire {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.263}. He holds that the garden was supplied with all that sustains life before they dwelt in it, as the earth was {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.260}.

## The word jannah in the Qur'an
He notes that the Qur'an uses jannah for gardens of this world too, such as the owners of gardens in the story of the Kahf and the people of Saba, and explains the word as covering, because the trees are thick {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.259} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.260}.

## Adam's purpose
He holds that Adam was created for khilafa on earth, and that those of his descendants who are righteous enter the eternal Garden in the Hereafter, so entering the Garden was not the first aim {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.260}.
