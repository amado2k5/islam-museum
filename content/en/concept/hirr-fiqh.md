## Summary
Hirr is the male cat, with hirra for the female; it is a species of the feline family. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16519}

## Purity and remnant
The majority of jurists hold the cat pure, on the Prophet's words that it is not impure and is among those who go about you; a Hanafi view, that of al-Tahawi, holds it impure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16520}
Its remnant is pure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16520}
Its urine and dung are impure in the sounder Hanafi narration, and likewise for the Malikis and Shafi'is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16520} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16521}

## Sale
The majority allow its sale as a pure animal of use; the Shafi'is limit this to the domestic cat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16521}
Some scholars dislike its sale, among them Abu Hurayra and Mujahid, citing the report of Jabir on the price of the dog and the cat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16522}

## Damage it causes
If a cat destroys a bird or food, its owner is liable, according to the Hanbalis and the sounder Shafi'i view, when such habits were known. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16522}

## Killing an attacking cat
The Shafi'is allow killing it only when killing is the only way to repel it, and otherwise require the lightest repulse first. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16523}
Some jurists allow killing a cat whose harm goes beyond the habit of cats and recurs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16523} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16524}

## Eating it
Most Hanafis and the sounder Hanbali view forbid eating its flesh; some Shafi'is allow it, and the Malikis dislike it in one view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16524}
