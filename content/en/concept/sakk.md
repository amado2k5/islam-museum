## Nature and selection
The original sakk is the Chinese made from the emblic; now that this has become scarce, they make it from galls and unripe dates in the manner of the making of ramak {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.587}.

## Temperament
The plain kind is hot in the first degree and dry in the second, and the perfumed kind is hot and dry in the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.587}.

## Actions and properties
Astringent and strengthening to the viscera, and in the perfumed kind there is much dissolving and opening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.587}.

## Joints
It is good for pains of the sinews {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.587}.

## Excretory organs
It restrains the bowels and helps bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.587}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Some claimed that the perfumed sakk increases desire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.587}.

## In more detail
The reform that made the dinar Islamic: until 77 AH the caliphate struck Arab-Sasanian dirhams and Arab-Byzantine fulus with added Arabic legends; in 77 AH Abd al-Malik struck the first purely epigraphic gold dinar at Damascus — 4.25 g, one mithqal, bearing Surat al-Ikhlas and Q 9:33 and no image at all — with epigraphic dirhams following in 79 AH across Damascus, Kufa, Basra and Wasit.
