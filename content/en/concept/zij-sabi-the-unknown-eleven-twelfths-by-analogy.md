## Summary
Al-Battani says what is not known to be settled or ruined is eleven parts of twelve, and that the part holding the known settlement from the equator contains seas and deserts. If asked whether these parts hold plants, animals and settlement, the answer is a matter of reasoning and opinion rather than sight: the Sun, Moon and stars run for us and by their motion bring summer, winter, plants and animals, so the same may be there and it is fitting that it should be. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.32}.

## Argument, not report
The text separates report from opinion: as for the earth's known settlement it does not go past the limits he has named, and what lies beyond no one has seen as far as reach us, but opinion and supposition may fall on what a person of understanding would not deny on the ground of analogy. So the possibility rests on the regularity of the heavenly bodies' motions and not on a traveller's account, and the text states the kind of proof it relies on. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.32}.

## The length of a degree
He adds that the share of one degree of the miles named is about sixty-five miles on the scan's reading, about two days' travel. The figure is slightly doubtful in the scan and does not clearly agree with the total of miles given in the passage on length, so it is held only as a provisional reading, not a verified one. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.32}.
