## Summary
Ma'il is a term of early Qur'an scholarship rather than the name of a script whose reality is settled. Scholars read it in Ibn al-Nadim's list of mushaf scripts in Gustav Flugel's edition of 1871, which was based on a corrupt manuscript; the word Ibn al-Nadim actually used was munabadh or munabidh, from the root nabadha, whose vocalisation and meaning are unclear. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 147}

## From ma'il to hijazi
The hand associated with early Meccan writing was called ma'il (leaning) and then renamed hijazi after the region of north-west Arabia that holds Mecca and Medina. Deroche divided the varied manuscripts called hijazi into four sub-types, the second of which includes the manuscripts formerly designated ma'il. {cite:blair-islamic-calligraphy-2006 p. 148} The Cairo printing read for this museum also gives al-ma'il in the same list {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim-rahmaniyya-cairo p. 21}, so the reading has not disappeared from printed texts.
