## Nature and selection
A herb that resembles the lupin (termus), which is why it is called termus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.376}. The editor's note says it is known in our country as alusin and was once known in Syria as the herb of al-laja {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.376}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot and dry in the first degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.376}. It dries moderately and cleanses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.376}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it helps freckles, and dissolves all of them moderately {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.376}. Poisons: Galen said it is specifically useful against the bite of a rabid dog, and it has cured several people; for this reason it is called alusin in Greek {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.376}.
