## Summary
Ra'y (grazing) is free by default, as the Prophet said that the Muslims share three things: pasture, water and fire; he abolished the grazing preserves of the pre-Islamic period. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8792} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8793} The majority hold that a village may not exclude others from its pasture; the Malikis allow an inhabited town its own reserve. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8793} The jurists differ on grazing in the Haram: the Malikis, Shafi'is and Abu Yusuf allow it, while Abu Hanifa and Muhammad forbid it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8793} A ruler may take no fee for grazing in a protected preserve, because only God and His Messenger may protect land. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8793} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8794} A hired herdsman is trusted and is liable only for negligence or misconduct. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8794} Most jurists forbid taking the milk of another's flock without leave, while the Hanbalis allow it after asking. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8794} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8795}
