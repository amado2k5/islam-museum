## Summary
For the Hanafis the wajib is what is required by evidence that admits doubt; for the majority it is what the law blames one for omitting in some respect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16584}

## Fard and wajib
The Hanafis hold the fard established by certain evidence and the wajib by probable evidence, such as the alms of the breaking of the fast and the sacrifice; for the majority the two terms are synonymous in the law. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16585} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16586}

## Grades of obligation
The Shafi'is grade obligations by the blame attached to omitting them; the Hanafis by the strength of the evidence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16586}

## Kinds
An obligation is individual or collective, specific or optional, timed or untimed; a timed one is narrow or broad, and an obligation may be immediate or delayed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16586} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16587} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16588} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16589} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16590}

## Missed obligations
If an obligation is missed through delay it must be made up or repaired by expiation; the child, the insane and the menstruating are excused making up, out of mercy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16590} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16591}

## Exceeding an obligation
On exceeding an obligation the schools differ: the Hanafis judge by the evidence, the Malikis dislike excess over what the law fixed, and the Shafi'is and Hanbalis distinguish excess that can be separated from excess that cannot. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16591} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16594} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16596}

## Dropping of obligations
For the Shafi'is an obligation may arise and then fall away where the cause and a bar conflict. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16597}
