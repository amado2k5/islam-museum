## Summary
In chapter 23 Ibn Sina limits the causes of pleasure to two genera, what changes the natural temperament suddenly so that it is felt, and what restores natural continuity suddenly, and defines pleasure as sensing the suitable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.151}.

## The two genera
The causes of pleasure are limited to two genera: one is what changes the natural temperament at once so that it is felt; the other is what restores natural continuity at once. Whatever does not happen at once is not felt, and so gives no pleasure {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.151}.

## Pleasure and pain
Pleasure is the sensing of the suitable. Every sensation belongs to the sensing power and arises by its being acted on, so when what acts is suitable or unsuitable the result is pleasure or pain according to what it undergoes. Since touch is the densest of the senses and keeps best what it receives of an unsuitable or suitable effect, in those of dense nature its sensing of the suitable gives more delight, and its sensing of the unsuitable more pain, than what belongs to the other powers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.151}.
