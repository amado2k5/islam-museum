## Summary
Ibn Sina continues the rulings on dwellings by their site: high, sunken, rocky and exposed, snowy mountain, and seaside {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.127-128}.

## High and sunken
The dwellers of high dwellings are healthy, strong, hardy and long-lived. The dwellers of low-lying hollows are always in vexation and grief, especially if the waters are stagnant, marshy or salt, and their waters are bad because of their air {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.

## Rocky and exposed
Their air is very hot in summer and cold in winter; their bodies are hard and compact, hairy, with strongly built joints and dominated by dryness; they sleep little and are ill-tempered, proud and domineering, with valour in wars, intelligence in the crafts and sharpness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.127}.

## Snowy mountain
The dwellers of snowy mountain dwellings are ruled as those of other cold lands, and their lands are airy; while the snow remains, good winds arise from it; when it melts, if the mountains block the winds, the place becomes oppressively hot and humid {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.127-128}.

## Seaside
The heat and cold of these lands are moderate because their moisture resists being acted on and admitting what penetrates it, while in moistness and dryness they incline to moistness without fail. If the land is northern, nearness to the sea and a sunken site make it more moderate; if it is hot and southern, the reverse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.128}.
