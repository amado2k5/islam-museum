## Summary
In the Qur'an, "ta'wil" means the outcome, the end to which a matter returns, and not "interpretation" as it became known among later scholars. [[person/rashid-rida|Muhammad Rashid Rida]] explains this in the introduction to his commentary, citing a passage in Surat al-A'raf: [[passage/quran-7-53|al-A'raf 53]]. {cite:tafsir-manar-v01 p.24} [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]] devotes the first two chapters of [[work/al-sawaiq-al-mursala|al-Sawa'iq al-Mursala]] to the reality of ta'wil, distinguishing three senses of the word and dividing ta'wil into sound and false. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 23} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 27}

## Rida's method of understanding
Rida holds that the interpreter should trace the technical terms that arose in the community after the first centuries, in order to distinguish them from what appears in the Book. He holds that the Qur'an should be explained by the meanings in use at the time of revelation, and that the interpreter should gather the repeated uses of a word in its various places in order to know its meaning there. {cite:tafsir-manar-v01 p.24}

## The root, for Ibn al-Qayyim
Ibn al-Qayyim says ta'wil is the second-form verbal noun of "ala ya'ulu ila", to come to something, so ta'wil is "making something come to"; he quotes [[person/al-jawhari-abu-nasr-ismail-ibn-hammad|al-Jawhari]]'s definition in al-Sihah – "explaining what a thing comes to" – with a line of al-A'sha as evidence. He then lists what came to be called ta'wil: the outcome, since the matter comes to it, as in [[passage/quran-4-59|al-Nisa 59]]; and the reality of the thing reported, since the matter ends in it, as in [[passage/quran-7-53|al-A'raf 53]] – the coming of its ta'wil is the coming of what the messengers foretold of the return, Paradise and Hell. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 23} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 24} The interpretation of dreams is ta'wil in both respects, being their explanation and their outcome, as in Joseph's words in [[passage/quran-12-100|Yusuf 100]]; and so is the final cause and purpose of an act, as in al-Khidr's words to Moses in [[passage/quran-18-78|al-Kahf 78]] and [[passage/quran-18-82|al-Kahf 82]]. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 24}

## The three senses
- **In the Book and the Sunna**: ta'wil is the reality of the meaning to which the words lead, the reality existing in the world. The ta'wil of a report is the reality reported; of the promise and threat, the thing promised or threatened; of what God reports of His attributes, what He Himself is; of a command, the commanded act itself. He cites [[person/aisha|A'isha]]'s report that the Prophet often glorified God and asked forgiveness in his bowing and prostration, "carrying out the Qur'an's ta'wil" – that is, obeying its command. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 24}
- **Among the exegetes and the early scholars of law and hadith**: ta'wil means explanation and exposition, as in [[person/al-tabari|Ibn Jarir]]'s formula "the statement on the ta'wil of His words…" and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]]'s words in his Refutation of the Jahmiyya, "in what you interpreted of the Qur'an against its ta'wil". This sense concerns grasping the meaning in the mind; the first concerns its reality occurring in the world. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 25}
- **Among the theologians and later scholars**: Ibn al-Qayyim says the [[group/mutazila|Mu'tazila]], the [[group/jahmiyya|Jahmiyya]] and other theological schools mean by ta'wil diverting a word from its apparent, literal meaning to a figurative one, and that this is the common usage of later legal theorists and jurists, who therefore say "ta'wil is against the default" and "ta'wil needs evidence". It is this sense over which books were written for and against: [[person/ibn-furak|Ibn Furak]] and Ibn Mahdi al-Tabari wrote interpreting the verses and reports on the attributes, and [[person/abu-yala-ibn-al-farra|al-Qadi Abu Ya'la]] and [[person/ibn-qudama-al-maqdisi|Ibn Qudama]] wrote refuting those interpretations; the editor gives the titles in a footnote. Ibn al-Qayyim says that more than one scholar reported the early generations' consensus against this kind of ta'wil of the attributes – his claim. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 25} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 26}

## Sound ta'wil, for him
Ibn al-Qayyim counts the first two senses as sound ta'wil – the reality of the meaning and what it comes to, or its explanation – covering the clear and the ambiguous, command and report alike. He cites Jabir's words in the hadith of the Farewell Pilgrimage that the Prophet knew the Qur'an's ta'wil, and whatever he did they did. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 27} He discusses the rajaz of [[person/abd-allah-ibn-rawaha|Abd Allah ibn Rawaha]] at the Umra of Fulfilment, "we fought you over its ta'wil", and Ibn Hisham's view that this line belongs to [[person/ammar-ibn-yasir|Ammar ibn Yasir]] on another occasion; if it is Ibn Rawaha's, he says, the ta'wil meant is the fulfilment of the vision of entering the Sacred Mosque in [[passage/quran-48-27|al-Fath 27]]. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 28} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 29} He reads [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]]'s report that the Companions agreed that any property or life lost "by the ta'wil of the Qur'an" in the civil strife was unaccountable as meaning that both parties at the Camel and Siffin fought over their understanding of the Qur'an. As false ta'wil he gives the claim, about the hadith "the rebel party will kill you", that Ammar was killed by those who brought him to battle; the editor's footnote quotes Ibn Hajar on the many chains of the hadith and al-Nawawi that scholars took it as proof that Ali was in the right and the other party were rebels who had exercised judgement and bore no sin. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 29} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 30} He explains Urwa's saying that A'isha and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] "interpreted" when they prayed in full while travelling as their acting on evidence they thought valid, lists what was suggested about it, and holds the Prophet's practice of shortening to be correct. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 30} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 31} His rule: any ta'wil that agrees with what the texts indicate and the Sunna brought is sound, and any that contradicts it is corrupt, in reports and commands alike. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 32}

## Kinds of false ta'wil, for him
Ibn al-Qayyim lists kinds of false ta'wil, with examples drawn from the theologians' interpretations of the texts on the attributes – the examples are his:
1. What the word cannot bear by its lexical meaning. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 32}
2. What it cannot bear in its particular form, dual or plural – for him, interpreting the two hands of [[passage/quran-38-75|Sad 75]] as power. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 32}
3. What the context and construction cannot bear – for him, taking the "coming" of [[passage/quran-6-158|al-An'am 158]] as the coming of some of God's signs, and interpreting the hadiths of the [[concept/ruyat-allah|vision of God]] against their apparent sense. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 32} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 33}
4. A sense not used in the addressee's language, though familiar in later technical usage – "a place where many people's feet have slipped"; his examples are taking the "setting" of [[passage/quran-6-76|al-An'am 76]] as motion, taking "al-Ahad" as that in which nothing is distinguishable from anything else, and taking the rising over the Throne as turning to create it, which he rejects by the hadith that God decreed the measures of creation before creating the heavens, His Throne being upon the water. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 33} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 34}
5. A sense used, but in a construction other than the text's – for him, taking the hand as favour in the verse of Sad: he grants that the Arabs say "so-and-so has a hand (favour) with me", citing Urwa ibn Mas'ud's words to Abu Bakr, but holds that God's attributing the act to Himself, the preposition "with", the dual and the singling out of Adam rule it out; and taking the looking of [[passage/quran-75-23|al-Qiyama 23]] as waiting for reward. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 35} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 36} Here he reports a saying he ascribes to "the Jahmi" about multiplying the meanings of "throne" and "rising", answers it in harsh terms, and holds that rising construed with "over" ('ala) can only mean being high and above. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 36} {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 37}
6. A word regularly used in an apparent sense and never, or rarely, in the interpreted one – carrying it to the unfamiliar sense is, for him, a deception that defeats clear speech. {cite:sawaiq-mursala-ataat-text p. 37}

## A note on the positions
This is Ibn al-Qayyim's treatment of the question from the standpoint of the people of hadith and the Hanbalis on the attributes. The theologians he answers, such as the Ash'aris and the Mu'tazila, allow ta'wil under their own conditions; their views have not yet been presented here from their own books, and what this card says of them is reported by their opponent.
