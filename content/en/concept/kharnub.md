## Choice
The most suitable is the dried Syrian {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Temperament
The Nabati is drier and colder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Actions and properties
The Syrian is drying and astringent, and so is its fruit except that there is sweetness in it and yet it binds; the Nabati is drier and more drying and does not sting, it is eaten fresh, and its humour is bad and heavy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Cosmetics
When warts are rubbed hard with the unripe Nabati kharnub, it removes them altogether {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Organs of the head
Rinsing with its decoction is good for toothache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Organs of nourishment
The fresh Syrian is bad for the stomach and is not digested, and the dry is slower in digestion and descent; the book quotes Galen that the plant of this fruit was not brought to other lands; and the yanbut is good for jaundice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Excretory organs
Sitting in its decoction strengthens the stomach and there is a promoting (idrar) in it, the book not saying of what; especially what is preserved in inspissated grape juice ('aqid al-'inab), and the fresh of the Syrian, loosens, and the dry binds and benefits diarrhoea; the yanbut is good for colic and diarrhoea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that the Nabati benefits excessive flow of menses as a pessary and eaten; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.710}
