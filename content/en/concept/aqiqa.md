## Summary
In the lexicon, aqiqa is the hair a child is born with, and it names the animal sacrificed for a newborn; as a term it is what is slaughtered for a newborn in thanks to God {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11783}.

## Its difference from the festival sacrifice
The aqiqa is slaughtered when the child is born, at any time, while the festival sacrifice is made on the days of sacrifice {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11783} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11784}.

## Its ruling
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold it a confirmed sunna, the Malikis recommended, and the Hanafis allow it as a voluntary act in thanks {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11784}.

## Who owes it and a girl child
The majority hold it for a girl as well as a boy; the Shafi'is ask it from a father who can afford it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11784} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11785}.

## Its time
The Hanafis and Malikis fix it on the seventh day; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis count from the child's complete birth {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11785} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11786}.

## The animals
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis require livestock, two sheep for a boy and one for a girl; the Hanafis and Malikis give one for each {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11786} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11787}.

## Cooking and eating
It is recommended to cook it without breaking its bones, to eat from it, to give it as gifts and in charity {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11787}.

## Where to look further
The naming of the child, the shaving of its head and its circumcision are treated under their own headings {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11787}.
