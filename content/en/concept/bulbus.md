## Nature and selection
A small edible bulb, resembling the narcissus bulb, with leaves like the leek and a flower like the violet. One kind causes vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Some say it is the zayz, and some say it is of the kind called talkhabyaz. The book does not settle its identity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}.

## Temperament and actions
Its nature is close to the onion's, and it is perhaps dry in the first degree with a superfluous moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. It produces wind, disperses, and roughens the tongue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: applied to freckles, especially in the sun, it helps; it also helps the marks of ulcers. It roughens the palate and tongue. Applied with egg yolk on warts, and with oxymel on milky ulcers, it helps {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Wounds and ulcers: it is said that when roasted with the heads of the sayr fish and sprinkled on sores of the chin it removes them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Joints: a poultice with vinegar is suitable for relaxing the middle of the muscles, and is applied for gout and pains of the joints, and alone for a twisted nerve. It is a poultice for a crushed nail or ear, and it is applied with barley gruel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Head: it is a remedy for lichen and sores of the head, and it is applied to fractures of the skull that have not been broken through, and mixed with egg yolk and applied {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Eyes: it is used alone, and with egg yolk for a sty, and with vinegar it is a good remedy for pimples and swellings of the eyelid {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Food organs: the sweet red kind is good for the stomach, and a poultice of it with honey is used for stomach pains. The bitter kind is better and aids digestion and feeds well, though it is not a good food, especially raw; if it is not digested it causes colic and wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. Excretory organs: it stirs desire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}. This statement on desire is recorded as written and not as advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.392}.
