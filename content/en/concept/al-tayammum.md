## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi reads the verse on tayammum as a command from God by which prayer is made lawful when water is absent, not only a clean substitute for washing. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2256}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2260}

## When it applies
He says tayammum applies when water is absent or when an excuse prevents its use. The verse names illness, travel, relieving oneself and touching women, and he holds that a ruling is to be obeyed even when its reason is not understood. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2260}

## Wiping face and hands
He holds that the clean earth is the one whose cleanness is certain, that wiping the face and hands suffices for minor and major impurity, and that tayammum is not merely a stand-in for washing in the sense of cleanliness. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2261}

## Number of strikes
He reports that some scholars held one strike and others two, and that the difference rests on facility. He does not prefer either view in these pages. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2261}
