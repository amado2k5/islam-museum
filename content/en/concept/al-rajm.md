## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi affirms stoning for a married offender on the basis of the Prophet's practice and approval, and holds that a practice is a text stronger than an argument from doubtful wording. He reports and refutes the view that the Qur'an contains no stoning. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2059}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2129}

## The Prophet's practice
He states that the Prophet stoned Ma'iz, al-Ghamidiyya, and a Jewish man and woman, and that his practice carries more weight than the text, since he is the legislator with delegated authority. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2129}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2060}

## "Muhsanat" and the slave woman
He reads "muhsanat" in the verse on slave women as free women. A slave woman receives half the penalty, and stoning cannot be halved, so it does not apply to her. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2126}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2060}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2061}

## The view he reports and answers
He reports that some people held that "muhsanat" means married women and that the Qur'an has no stoning, so only lashing applies, and he answers that the doubtful reading cannot stand against the practice. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2125}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2126}
