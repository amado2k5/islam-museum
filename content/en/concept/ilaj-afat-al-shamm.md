## Treating the bad temperament and the humour
If its cause is a bad temperament it must be treated by the contrary, aiming at the front of the brain with the douches, smellings, inhalations, embrocations and dressings named in the chapter of head treatments. The commonest arising bad temperament is a cold one, either in both front ventricles or in the mamillary pair themselves, and the most useful means for it are snuffs of the hot-oil kind with the known things pounded in ⁦(drug names not carried)⁩. If the cause is a humour in the brain's ventricles it is recognised by what was said in its own causes, and that humour is brought out of it by head-clearing gargles, snuffs, inhalations and soothing smellings and the like; if venesection is needed it is done, all of it returning to the principles given in the treatment of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.236}
## Treating the occlusion and the one-sided sense
If the cause is an occlusion in the sieve bone known as the cribriform, use the opening douches named among the head treatments and inhale them with the known things pounded in; the head must then be kept to the cupping glasses, with gargling by hot opening things. Among the tried items recorded is a seed soaked days in vinegar, ground fine, mixed with oil and dripped into the nose, drawn up as far as possible ⁦(the tried recipes, oils and pounded drugs are carried by kind, not by name or measure)⁩. If the cause in him is piles — he senses the pleasant but not the foul — he is snuffed repeatedly with a named drug until he mends ⁦(as printed; the name is one of the withheld drug names)⁩; the one who senses the foul but not the pleasant is snuffed with a named perfume until his state improves ⁦(as printed)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.236}
