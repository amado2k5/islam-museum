## Summary
Al-Battani lays down the steps: the star's latitude and its degree's declination are added if their sides agree, or the smaller is subtracted from the larger, giving the corrected latitude; the star's distance from the equator is then worked by chords; then the distance of its degree from the head of Cancer or Capricorn is taken, and the difference of its passage to midheaven is worked by chords and added to the right-sphere risings or subtracted from them according to the side of the latitude and the star's place in the signs, so that the degree that comes to midheaven with it is known. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.51-52}.

## The corrected latitude and the star's distance from the equator
He says: if you want to know the distance of a star with latitude from the equator, and with which parts of the signs it comes to midheaven, find the star's latitude and its side and the declination of the degree it is in. If the latitude and declination are on one side, add them together; if on two different sides, subtract the smaller from the larger; the result is the corrected latitude. Know the side on which it falls, then take the chord of this corrected latitude, multiply it by the chord of what remains to complete the whole declination to ninety, divide the product by the chord of what remains to complete the degree to ninety, which is the complement of its declination, take the arc of the result, and the arc is the star's distance from that equator on the side of the corrected latitude. Keep it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.51}.

## The degree's distance from the head of Cancer or Capricorn, and the difference of passage
Then he says: take the distance of the degree in which the star lies from the head of Cancer or the head of Capricorn, whichever the degree is nearer, from the rising times opposite the star's degree in the right sphere. If it is less than ninety, take it as it is; if it is more than two hundred and seventy, subtract it from three hundred and sixty, and what results from either is the star's distance from the head of Capricorn. If it is more than ninety up to one hundred and eighty, subtract it from one hundred and eighty; if it is more than one hundred and eighty up to two hundred and seventy, take away one hundred and eighty from it; the result is the degree's distance from the head of Cancer. Then take the chord of the star's latitude, multiply it by the chord of the whole declination, divide by the chord of the complement of the star's distance from the equator to ninety, multiply the result by the chord of the degree's distance from the head of Cancer or Capricorn in the right sphere, divide by the chord of what remains to complete the degree's declination to ninety, and take the arc; the arc is the difference of the star's passage at midheaven. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.51-52}.

## Adding or taking the difference from the risings, and the degree at midheaven
Then he says: if the star lies between the first of Cancer and the end of Sagittarius and its latitude is northern, add the difference of its passage at midheaven to the rising times opposite the star's degree in the right sphere; if its latitude is southern, subtract the difference of its passage from these times, following the first declination. If the star lies between the first of Capricorn and the end of Gemini and its latitude is northern, subtract the difference of its passage from the stated times; if its latitude is southern, add it to the times. The rising times of the star's degree in the right sphere, after the addition or subtraction, are then taken to the degrees of the signs opposite them in the right sphere's risings, and the one that results is the part of the signs that comes to midheaven with the star, that is, the sign in which you found the number of the times. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.52}.
