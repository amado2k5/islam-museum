## Summary
Ibn Sina says purging runs to excess through weakness of the vessels, wideness of their mouths, the sting of the purgative on them or a bad temperament, and for excess he prescribes binding the limbs above and below, a little theriac or philonium, sweating by bath or steam, astringents, rubbing, cupping, poultices and an astringent, chilled diet {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.283}.

## Treatment
Purging runs to excess through weakness of the vessels, wideness of their mouths, the sting of the purgative upon those mouths, or the bad temperament the body takes from it. When it is excessive, the limbs are bound above and below, beginning at the armpit and the groin and going down from them; a little theriac or philonium is given, and if possible he is made to sweat by the bath or by water steam under his clothes with his head outside; if the sweating is very heavy they are given astringents, rubbed and given perfumed lotions and fruit juices. The outer limbs are rubbed and warmed, even with fire cupping glasses under the ribs and between the shoulders, and poultices of barley meal and astringent waters, with quince oil and mastic oil, are put on the stomach and viscera. Cold air must be avoided since it squeezes them and they purge, and hot air too, since it slackens their strength. His food is astringent, cooled with snow, such as sour grape juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.283}.
