## Nature
The snake comes in many kinds. It is used boiled with water, salt and dill, sometimes with oil added; the broth is as strong as its flesh, and its skin is also used. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Selection: the best flesh is the female's, and the best skin is the male's. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}

## Temperament
Its flesh dries strongly; its heating is not strong; its skin also dries strongly. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}

## Actions
Its special property is that it sends superfluities through to the skin, especially in someone who is not clean; if its flesh is used, it prolongs life, strengthens strength, and preserves the senses and youth. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: the book gives the use of its flesh and broth; the last clause of this line is unreadable in the scan and is left out. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Joints: its broth, made after the head and tail are cut away and cooked, and its flesh when eaten, help pains of the nerves. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Organs of the head: its skin boiled in wine and dropped into the ear calms its pain; a rinse with vinegar in which the skin has been boiled helps toothache; the best skin is the male's. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Skin: it helps leprosy greatly, and when used on alopecia it helps greatly. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Adornment: eating it breeds lice, the book says, and pushes superfluities to the skin. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.494}
Organs of the eye: the snake broth and its flesh, as above, strengthen the sight. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.495}
Organs of the eye: the book says the fat of the viper is said to hold back the descent of water to the eye, and adds that no one would dare to try it. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.495}
Poisons: a viper is split and placed on its own bite, and the pain subsides. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.495}
