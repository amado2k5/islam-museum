## Definition
Ihab is lexically the hide of cattle, sheep or wild animals before it is tanned. The jurists use it more widely, to include human skin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3591}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3592}

## Hide of a lawfully slaughtered animal
- If the animal may be eaten, its hide is pure by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3592}
- For animals not eaten: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that slaughter does not purify the hide, citing the prohibition of sitting on the hides of beasts of prey; the Hanafis and Malikis hold that slaughter purifies it, on the saying that tanning is the slaughter of a hide. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3592}
- The pig is impure in itself by agreement, and the dog for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis; slaughter does not purify them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3592}

## Hide of a carcass
- It is impure by agreement and may not be used before tanning, except that al-Zuhri allowed use before tanning. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3592}
- After tanning there are seven views: none is purified by tanning; all are purified, even the dog and the pig; all edible animals are purified and others are not; only the outside is purified and not the inside. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3592}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3593}

## Killing or selling an animal for its hide
- The Shafi'is forbid killing an animal not eaten for its hide, and forbid its sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3593}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3594}
- The Hanafis and Malikis allow hunting or slaughtering such an animal for the benefit of its hide, and allow its sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3593}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3594}

## Flaying a slaughtered animal
All the jurists agree that flaying a slaughtered animal before its soul has left is disliked, following the prohibition of it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3594}
