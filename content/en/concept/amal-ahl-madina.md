## Summary
Jurists use the phrase 'practice of the people of Medina' for what the people of Medina agreed upon as a practice in the first three centuries, which the Prophet called the best, and which they inherited {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11840}. The majority hold that the agreement of the people of Medina on a practice is not binding on those who disagree; Malik held it binding on others, and it is reported that he meant that their transmission should be preferred {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11840}. The full treatment is in the legal-theory appendix {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11840}.
