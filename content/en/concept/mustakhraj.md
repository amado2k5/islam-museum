## Summary
A *mustakhraj* ("extraction") is a book in which a hadith master later than the author of a hadith collection takes the hadiths of that collection and transmits them through his own chains. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26} In the introduction to [[work/al-minhaj-sharh-sahih-muslim|his commentary on Muslim]], [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] notes that numbers of hadith masters wrote such books on [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]]; they lived after Muslim and reached high chains, some of them having met some of Muslim's own teachers. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26}

## Standing and benefits
Al-Nawawi quotes [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] that these extracted books share in Sahih Muslim's character as sound, though not in all its distinctions. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26} They bring three benefits: higher chains, greater strength for a hadith through more routes, and additional sound and useful wording. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26} Their authors did not undertake to match the original's wording, since they transmit by other chains, so differences occur. {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26}

## Mustakhrajat on Sahih Muslim
Al-Nawawi lists among them: {cite:sharh-nawawi-misriyya-1347-v1 p. 26-27}
- The book of Abu Ja'far Ahmad b. Hamdan al-Naysaburi, the ascetic.
- *Al-Musnad al-Sahih* of Abu Bakr Muhammad b. Muhammad b. Raja' al-Naysaburi, an early master who shares most of Muslim's teachers.
- *Mukhtasar al-Musnad al-Sahih al-Mu'allaf 'ala Kitab Muslim* of Abu 'Awana Ya'qub b. Ishaq al-Isfara'ini, who narrates in it from Yunus b. 'Abd al-A'la and other teachers of Muslim.
- The book of Abu Hamid al-Shariki of Herat, a Shafi'i jurist, who narrates from Abu Ya'la al-Mawsili.
- *Al-Musnad al-Sahih* of Abu Bakr Muhammad b. 'Abd Allah al-Jawzaqi al-Naysaburi.
- *Al-Musnad al-Mustakhraj 'ala Kitab Muslim* of Abu Nu'aym Ahmad b. 'Abd Allah al-Isfahani.
- *Al-Mukharraj 'ala Sahih Muslim* of Abu al-Walid Hassan b. Muhammad al-Qurashi, a Shafi'i jurist.
