## Overview
In his commentary on the sixth hadith, Ibn Rajab divides rulings and things into what is purely lawful (*halal mahd*), what is purely unlawful (*haram mahd*), and matters that are doubtful between them. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.172-173}

## The divisions
In his view, the purely lawful is eating the good things of crops, fruits, and livestock, and marriage and concubinage when the gain comes through a valid contract. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.172-173}

The purely unlawful includes carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, wine, and marriage with forbidden kin; and unlawful gains such as usury, gambling, and usurpation. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.172-173}

The doubtful is what is disputed as to whether it is lawful or unlawful, such as horses, mules, donkeys, the lizard, and drinks whose large quantity intoxicates. Among gains are the questions of *'inah* and *tawarruq*. He notes that this is the explanation of Ahmad, Ishaq, and others of the doubtful matters. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.172-173}

## Whoever guards against doubtful matters
Whoever guards against doubtful matters keeps his religion and his honour clear. Whoever falls into them is like a shepherd grazing around a protected pasture of God's prohibitions. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.172-173}

## Sha'rawi on prohibition and discipline
Sha'rawi holds that God forbids what harms, and that some prohibitions are disciplinary, as God forbade some good things to the Children of Israel for their wrongdoing (see [[passage/quran-4-160|4:160]]). He says custom enters into the meaning of words such as flesh and beast, and that fish and locust are permitted because they have no flowing blood, citing a hadith given in the footnote. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.715} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.716} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.717}
