## Summary
In the lexicon kali is deferral and loan; one says that a debt is kali when its payment is delayed. In the jurists' terms it means debt {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13163}.

## The prohibition of debt for debt
Among the sales forbidden by the law is selling kali for kali, that is, selling debt for debt. The jurists rely on the prohibition, and a report of Ibn Umar says that the Prophet forbade it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13164}. Abu Ubayda explains the phrase as deferral for deferral {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13163}. A version of the prohibition in al-Bayhaqi is weakened by Ibn Hajar in Bulugh al-Maram {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13163}.

Ibn Arafa said that the scholars' acceptance of the hadith makes a search for its chain unnecessary, and Ibn al-Mundhir reports agreement that debt for debt is not permitted {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13164}.

## Reason for the prohibition
Al-Qarafi holds that when a contract burdens the obligations of both parties, claims can come from both sides, which causes many disputes and enmities, so the law forbids it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13164}.

## Debt for a present item
The sale of kali for a present specific item ('ayn) involves detail and disagreement among the jurists, and the book treats it under its entries on forbidden sales and on debt {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13164}.
