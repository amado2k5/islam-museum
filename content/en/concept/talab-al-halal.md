## Summary
This record treats the seeking of the lawful and the purity of one's food: what the museum holds on eating what is lawful and good, on the source of one's provision, and on the effect of the forbidden in food, drink and clothing. It gathers the verses that call to eat of the good things and to seek provision, the reports that say God is pure and accepts only what is pure, and the sayings of the Companions and Successors and the writings of scholars on earning and food.

## In the Qur'an
[[passage/quran-2-168|al-Baqara 168]] addresses all people, telling them to eat what is lawful and good on the earth and not to follow the footsteps of Satan, who is an open enemy to them {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-2-172|al-Baqara 172]] addresses the believers, telling them to eat of the good things God has provided and to give thanks if it is Him they worship {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-5-88|al-Ma'ida 88]] enjoins eating of what God has provided, lawful and good, while keeping duty to Him {cite:tanzil-quran}, and [[passage/quran-16-114|al-Nahl 114]] enjoins eating of what He has provided, lawful and good, and thanking Him for His favour {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-8-69|al-Anfal 69]] says that what they gained may be eaten as lawful and good, with fear of God {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-23-51|al-Mu'minun 51]] addresses the messengers, telling them to eat of the good things and to act rightly, and says God is aware of what they do {cite:tanzil-quran}. [[passage/quran-62-10|al-Jumu'a 10]] says that when the prayer is finished they are to disperse in the land, seek God's bounty and remember Him often {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In the Sunnah
[[passage/muslim-1015|Sahih Muslim 1015]] relates from the Messenger of God that God is pure and accepts only what is pure {cite:sahih-muslim 1015}, and goes on to mention a man who lengthens his journey, dishevelled and dusty, raising his hands to the sky saying 'O Lord, O Lord', while his food is forbidden, his drink is forbidden, his clothing is forbidden and he has been nourished on the forbidden, and the report asks how such a man could be answered {cite:sahih-muslim 1015}. It appears in [[passage/arbaun-10|the Forty Hadith, number 10]] together with the saying that God commanded the believers with what He commanded the messengers, and the book refers to the verses of al-Mu'minun and al-Baqara {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam 10}. [[passage/tirmidhi-api-2989|Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2989]] has the same wording, and Abu 'Isa says it is hasan gharib, known to him through the report of Fudayl ibn Marzuq {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 2989}.

[[passage/tirmidhi-api-614|Jami' al-Tirmidhi 614]] relates that flesh grown from unlawful gain does not thrive without the fire being more fitting for it; Abu 'Isa says it is hasan gharib by this route, that Ayyub ibn 'A'idh al-Ta'i is considered weak and said to have held the view of the Murji'a, and that he asked Muhammad about the report and he knew it only through 'Ubaydullah ibn Musa {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi 614}. [[passage/bukhari-api-2059|Sahih al-Bukhari 2059]] relates from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said a time will come upon people when a man will not care whether what he takes is from the lawful or the forbidden {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2059}; [[passage/bukhari-api-2083|another place in al-Bukhari]] gives close wording concerning wealth {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 2083}; and Abu Nu'aym records it from Abu Hurayra in the [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-7-93|Hilyat al-Awliya']], noting that Ibn Abi Dhi'b was alone in transmitting it from Sa'id al-Maqburi {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.93}.

## In the reports of the Companions, the Successors and the scholars
In [[passage/sahaba-hayat-sahaba-halal-1-534|Hayat al-Sahaba]] al-Kandahlawi quotes Ibn 'Abbas that there is no believer or sinner for whom God has not written his provision from the lawful: if he is patient until it comes, God gives it to him, and if he grows impatient and takes something forbidden, God reduces his lawful provision {cite:hayat-al-sahaba-kandahlawi-sabbani p.534}; the [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-1-326|Hilyat al-Awliya']] of Abu Nu'aym has a text to the same effect {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.326}. Abu Nu'aym also records from Maymun ibn Mihran that the lawful is not safe for a man until he puts between himself and the forbidden a barrier of the lawful {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.84}, and from Abu Hazim that some people avoided much of the lawful because of its distraction, and he asks what then of those who left the lawful in order to ride the forbidden {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.241}. [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-4-49|Another passage]] says that the most abstinent of people in this world, even if given over to it in eagerness, is the one who accepts only lawful and good earning, and the most eager for it is the one who does not care whether his earning is lawful or forbidden {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.49}. A [[passage/sahaba-hilyat-awliya-halal-7-24|written exhortation]] advises that one's food and one's advisers be of those whose earning is lawful, because scruple is the pillar of religion and the completion of the matter of the hereafter {cite:hilyat-al-awliya-dar-al-kutub-al-ilmiyya-1988 p.24}.

In the [[passage/ihya-halal-3-414|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]] al-Ghazali describes a person who reflects on his stomach and sees that he disobeys God through it by eating and drinking, either by eating too much of the lawful, which is disliked by God and strengthens desire, or by eating the forbidden or the doubtful, so he examines where his food, clothing, dwelling and earnings come from {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.414}, and page [[passage/ihya-halal-2-488|488]] repeats the same thought {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.488}. [[passage/ihya-halal-1-391|Elsewhere]] he mentions people who give a few counted dirhams in charity from the lawful and the forbidden while what they take of Muslims' property and doubtful things is greater {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p.391}. In his [[passage/mughni-halal-3-904|al-Mughni]], on the wage for a stud animal's covering, Ibn Qudama says it is forbidden to the one who takes it and not forbidden to the one who gives it, who spent his money to obtain a permitted thing he needs, and he mentions the cupper's earning and calls it foul {cite:mughni-ibn-qudama p.904}.
