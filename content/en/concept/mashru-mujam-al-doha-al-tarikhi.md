## Summary
This page presents the Doha Historical Dictionary project in Doha, Qatar, as a research file supplied to the museum summarises it. The dictionary's website has a separate record at [[work/doha-historical-dictionary|the Doha Historical Dictionary of Arabic]], made in the dictionaries browser, and this page treats the project, that is its history, size and institutional standing by the summary. The museum did not open the pages cited.

## What it is
By the summary it is a comprehensive historical dictionary tracing Arabic words, roots, meanings and semantic and structural changes across about twenty centuries, from the earliest inscriptions and texts onward, built on a large digital corpus of about a billion words and a bibliography {cite:dohadictionary-org-home}. It calls it the major completed historical dictionary of Arabic.
## The portal and the app
The dictionary has an online portal with advanced search, pronunciation tools and related resources, a mobile app, derived studies and sub-dictionaries, and open scholarly participation for corrections and enrichment {cite:dohadictionary-org-home}. The terms of use of the site, which the museum read earlier, are recorded at [[work/doha-historical-dictionary|the site's record]]: nothing is copied from it, and the museum limits itself to description and reference {cite:dohadictionary-terms}.
## Where claims may differ
The summary calls the dictionary the major completed historical dictionary of Arabic, which is the summary's judgement and not the museum's, and the museum does not adopt such a ranking. It gives more than 300,000 entries and more than ten thousand roots, approximate figures in the form given, which may change with each addition. It also mentions the celebration of completion in late 2025 without making clear what was completed.
## Relation to others
The summary says Sharjah's Academy has had some historical interaction with the project around historical lexicography, see [[group/majma-al-lugha-al-arabiyya-bi-al-shariqa|the Arabic Language Academy in Sharjah]], and that [[group/king-salman-global-academy-for-the-arabic-language|the King Salman Academy]] has been the most active national and international hub since 2020 with a digital focus. The dictionaries browser holds what concerns the older dictionaries.
## What the summary does not say
The summary does not name the head of the scientific council, the team or the source of the corpus, and does not state exactly what completion of the project means. The dates given, 2013, 2018 and late 2025, are as it says them, and the museum has not compared them with others.
## The five bodies at a glance
The summary places these bodies together as the principal contemporary official or major institutional efforts centred in the Gulf and the Arabian Peninsula, plus the pan-Arab layer of ALECSO. The table gives in brief what it says of each, and separates what is official, what is commercial and what is a research project.

| Body | Year | Seat | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| King Salman Global Academy for the Arabic Language | 2020 | Riyadh | A government academy by Council of Ministers decree |
| Arabic Language Academy in Sharjah | 2016 | Sharjah | A government academic body by Amiri decree |
| The Arabic Language Centre | 1980 | Dubai | A private commercial teaching institute |
| ALECSO and its Coordination Bureau | 1961 or 1962 for the Bureau | Rabat for the Bureau | A pan-Arab organisation |
| Doha Historical Dictionary | 2013 | Doha | A lexicographic research project |
## A research project, not an academy
The Doha dictionary differs from the other bodies in being a lexicographic research project resting on a corpus and bibliography, not a language body with competences, and in that its stated body is a research and policy centre. It was launched before the academies of Sharjah and Riyadh, in 2013, a chronology that comes out of the summary's numbers.
