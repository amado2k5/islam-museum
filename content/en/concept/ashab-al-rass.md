## What the Qur'an says
The Qur'an names the Companions of al-Rass in only two places. In [[passage/quran-25-38|al-Furqan 38]] it sets them beside 'Ad and Thamud and many generations in between, all destroyed by God; in [[passage/quran-50-12|Qaf 12]] it counts them among those who denied before the Quraysh, with the people of Noah and Thamud. The text goes no further: it does not name their prophet, their land, or how they perished. That is why al-Tabari says he knows of no firm report about them beyond what is transmitted, that they threw their prophet into a pit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 25:38}

## What the word means
In Arabic al-rass is anything dug, such as a well, a grave or a mine. Al-Tabari cites a line of verse by al-Nabigha al-Ja'di about men who dig the "rissas", meaning mine shafts, and concludes from the word's sense that the Companions of al-Rass were a people living at a well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 25:38} Al-Qurtubi adds that Abu 'Ubayda defined it as any well not yet lined, that al-Rass is also the name of a valley in a line of Zuhayr, and that the word can mean reconciling people. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38} Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan gives it as a well and a mine, says Abu Ishaq reported a well in which a prophet was thrown, and notes that some said it was a village in al-Yamama called al-Falaj and others the dwellings of a group of Thamud. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 vol. 3 p. 43}

## What the commentators say
Al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi collect many views. Here they are, each with the person it is attributed to:

- **A people who threw their prophet into a well:** from 'Ikrima, also transmitted by al-Thawri through Abu Bukayr. Al-Tabari gathers this with other views and, on the sense of the word, prefers that they were a people living at a well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 25:38} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 25:38}
- **A village of Thamud:** from Ibn 'Abbas, through Ibn Jurayj. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 25:38}
- **Al-Falaj in al-Yamama:** from Qatada; Ibn Jurayj also reports 'Ikrima that they were at Falaj and were the people of Ya-Sin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 25:38}
- **A well in Azerbaijan:** from 'Ikrima through Ibn 'Abbas. In al-Qurtubi another report from Ibn 'Abbas says they were a people of Azerbaijan who killed prophets, whereupon their trees and crops dried up. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 25:38} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
- **The man of Ya-Sin and the people of Antioch:** Ka'b, in a report Ibn 'Abbas relates, says they killed the man of Ya-Sin and threw him into a well called al-Rass; Muqatil says the same, and al-Suddi says the well was at Antioch, where Habib al-Najjar was killed. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38} On Qaf 12 al-Tabari reports al-Dahhak that al-Rass is a well in which the man of Ya-Sin was killed, and Mujahid that it is a well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 50:12}
- **Worshippers of a pine tree:** from 'Ali; their prophet, of the line of Judah, called down God's curse, the tree withered, they killed him in a well, and a black cloud burned them. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
- **Well-dwellers and herdsmen to whom Shu'ayb was sent:** from Wahb ibn Munabbih, who says the well collapsed under them. Qatada says al-Rass and al-Aykah were two nations to whom Shu'ayb was sent. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
- **A people who killed a prophet:** from al-Kalbi. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
- **A remnant of Thamud**, al-Rass being the abandoned well of the Hajj sura, or **the Companions of the Trench:** al-Qurtubi gives both, then says the first, a well, is the well-known meaning. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
- **The people of Hanzala ibn Safwan al-'Absi:** so al-Mas'udi states, adding that many other views have been given about them. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab OpenITI text vol. 1 p. 189}

## The report of the black slave
Ibn Ishaq relates from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, as a mursal report, that the Prophet said the first to enter Paradise on the Day of Rising would be a black slave who alone believed a prophet God had sent to a town; the townspeople threw the prophet into a well and sealed it with a great rock, the slave gathered firewood and lowered food to him, then slept seven years and seven more, and on waking found that his people had believed and brought their prophet out. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 25:38} Al-Tabari gives it too, and notes that in this report the people believed their prophet, so they should not be the destroyed Companions of al-Rass unless they were destroyed for something they did after him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 25:38} Ibn Kathir says the report has strangeness and something rejected in it, and perhaps an interpolation; al-Qurtubi says al-Mahdawi and al-Tha'labi mention it. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 25:38} {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni view, al-Tabari:** al-rass is any dug thing, so they were a people at a well. As for who they were, he knows of no story of a pit-people the Qur'an names except the Companions of the Trench, whose account he will give at the sura of the Constellations; if they are others, he knows nothing of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-quran-com on 25:38}
- **Sunni view, Ibn Kathir:** he reports that Ibn Jarir chose the Companions of the Trench, calls the black-slave report strange and rejected, and relays Ibn Jarir's point that the people in that report cannot be taken for the destroyed Companions of al-Rass since they believed. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 25:38}
- **Sunni view, al-Qurtubi:** he lists the views and says that the first, a people of a well, is the well-known one. {cite:tafsir-qurtubi-quran-com on 25:38}
:::

## What the text does not say
The Qur'an gives no prophet, date, place or manner of destruction for them. Everything above is the commentators' effort and their reports from earlier generations; I found no raised report with a sound chain naming them in the sources I read. I did not read a dedicated academic study of them and so attribute no view to scholars, and I did not verify any link between al-Rass and the present-day places of that name in Najd, so I make none.
