## Summary
In the fourth chapter Ibn Sina follows the cephalic and axillary veins in the upper arm and forearm, and names the median cubital, the basilic and the salvatella and their branching to the fingers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.91}.

## The cephalic
The first branches of the cephalic, as it faces the upper arm, spread in the skin and the outer parts of the upper arm; then near the elbow joint it divides in three: the cord of the forearm, which runs along the outside of the upper bone of the forearm, then outward toward the bulge of the lower one and spreads in the lower outer parts of the wrist; a second goes to the elbow's bend on the outer forearm and mixes with a branch of the axillary, and from the two comes the akhal, the median cubital; and a third goes deep and mixes in the depth with a branch of the axillary {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.91}.

## The axillary and basilic
The axillary at first sends branches deep into the muscles which vanish in them, except one that reaches the forearm; near the elbow it divides in two: one goes deep and joins the deep branch of the cephalic, and they part, one dropping to the inner side until it reaches the little and ring fingers and half the middle, while a part rises and divides in the outer parts of the hand. The second sends four branches at the forearm: one to the lower forearm and wrist, a second above it likewise, a third in the middle of the forearm, and a fourth, the greatest, which shows and rises and sends branches that join one from the cephalic to form the akhal; the rest is the basilic, which dives deep again {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.91}.

## The akhal
The akhal begins on the inner side and rises over the upper bone of the forearm, then turns outward and forks in the shape of the Greek lambda: the upper fork goes to the end of the upper bone toward the wrist and spreads behind the thumb, between it and the index finger, and in the index; the lower goes to the end of the lower bone and gives three branches: one to the place between middle and index finger, joining a branch of the vessel that comes to the index from above to become one; a second, the salvatella, spreading between middle and ring finger; and a third to the ring and little fingers; all divide in the fingers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.91}.
