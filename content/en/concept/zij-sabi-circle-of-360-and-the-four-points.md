## Summary
In chapter 2 al-Battani reports that the ancients divided the circle of the sphere into 360 parts and gives their reasons: that the number is near the number of days of the year, which the Sun completes by its circuit, and that it has a half, a third, a quarter and other fractions that many numbers lack. He then ties the division to the points of the two equinoxes and two solstices. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.12}.

## Why 360
Al-Battani gives two grounds for the number: its nearness to the days of the year, which are completed as the Sun goes from a point back to it, and its being a number that divides by many fractions, among them a half, a third and a quarter. The degree is therefore a unit the ancients agreed on and not one nature imposes, being a 360th part of the sphere's revolution. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.12}.

## The four points and the seasons
He says they referred the Sun to four points of the sphere that bring about two equinoxes and two solstices and divide the year into four distinct parts, spring, summer, autumn and winter, and each point was assigned to the season that arises from the Sun's passing through it. So each season takes a quarter of the circle, ninety parts. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.12}.

## The spring point as the start
He explains that the spring point is the best of the points and the first to start from, because the day begins to lengthen from it after the equinox while the Sun climbs to the northern half, so the heat grows, and heat suits the start of growth and the coming-to-be of things; so they made the reckoning of the zodiac circle begin there. This is the ancients' reasoning as al-Battani reports it, not one he imposes. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.12}.
