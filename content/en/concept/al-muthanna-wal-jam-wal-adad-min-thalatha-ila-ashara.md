## What this record is
This record gathers two chapters of grammar and morphology: the kinds of dual and plural, and the rule of the numbers three to ten, in which the form of the numeral takes the opposite gender to the counted noun. It is what a research summary supplied to the museum gave, on the root k-t-b, and it connects with the record on [[concept/al-iraab-wal-mamnu-min-al-sarf|case endings and diptotes]] and with that on [[concept/al-asma-al-mushtaqqa|derived nouns]]. The page attributes each example and each page number to the summary as given.

## The numbers three to ten
The summary gives the rule of reverse polarity (mukhālafat al-maʿdūd): the numeral takes the opposite gender to the singular of the counted noun, so that it carries the feminine -at ⁦(ـَة)⁩ when the singular of the counted noun is masculine, and appears bare, in the masculine form, when the singular of the counted noun is feminine. The counted noun, the maʿdūd or tamyīz, stands in the indefinite genitive plural as the second term of an annexation. Its examples:
- masculine singular ⁦(kitāb, كِتَاب)⁩: thalāthatu kutubin ⁦(ثَلَاثَةُ كُتُبٍ)⁩, "three books";
- feminine singular ⁦(maktaba, مَكْتَبَة)⁩: thalāthu maktabātin ⁦(ثَلَاثُ مَكْتَبَاتٍ)⁩, "three libraries".

## How the references differ
For the dual and the plural the summary lists references in three languages and periods: Ryding, contemporary and in English; Wright, earlier, in 1896 and in English; and al-Ghalayini, in Arabic for teaching the young, and the last is cited through an article on Alukah and not from the book itself. For the numerals the citation of al-Ghalayini comes through a paper on SciSpace, whose address the summary gave as a search-engine link, so the museum says the page title is taken from the address. All page numbers are the summary's and the museum did not check them.

## What this page does not reach
The museum opened none of the references. The summary does not give numbers from eleven upwards, the tens, one and two, or the rules of compound numerals, so none is given here. It does not list all the broken-plural patterns, only examples of them, fuʿul, afʿāl and mafāʿil. The museum did not check the vowels written in the examples. The record does not decide the differences among grammarians on the rule of reversal and its explanation.
