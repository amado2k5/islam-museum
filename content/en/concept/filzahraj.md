## Nature
It is said to be the tree of the huḍuḍ, with a fruit like pepper; huḍuḍ is made from it, and another kind is made from barberry and from the Arab one, and the power of filzahraj is near that of the huḍuḍ made from it, and slightly weaker {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.629}

## Cosmetics
Applied alone or with oil it strengthens the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its branches are cooked in vinegar and drunk for the spleen, and benefit greatly, and likewise for jaundice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}

## Excretory organs
Its fruit, drunk in a small quantity, loosens a great deal of phlegmatic humour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists that a decoction of its leaf and branches brings on the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.630}
