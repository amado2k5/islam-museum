## Summary
The author states that Islam encourages taking legitimate risks through worldly social security, so that if a Muslim suffers a loss in their work or is struck by a calamity, they are not abandoned, but rather receive aid from society and the state; he adds that modern cooperative insurance models aim to restore people to their previous standing {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}.

## Overview
In the author's view, social solidarity (takaful) is social aid that precedes or follows a loss; it is considered one of the factors that facilitate taking risks, and he refers to it as "social security," "social solidarity," or "Islamic insurance" {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}.

## From Loss to Aid
If a Muslim suffers a loss in their work or experiences a calamity or disaster, "they are not left neglected or uncared for," but receive assistance and consolation from society and the state {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}. He views this as an encouragement to production, as it reduces the fear of failure.

## Modern Insurance Models
He notes that Muslims today are interested in cooperative insurance models that insure not only against poverty but also against harm, in order to restore people to their former level of wealth and productive capacity, citing in a footnote Muhammad Nejatullah Siddiqi's book on insurance {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 117}. This is the author's view, and he does not present contract details in this section.
