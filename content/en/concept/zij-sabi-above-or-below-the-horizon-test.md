## Above and below the earth
If the degree lies between the rising degree and the setting degree in the order of the signs, it is below the earth; if it lies between the setting degree and the rising degree in that order, it is above the earth. A second test uses the half-duration of the star above the earth: if it is more than the time between the midheaven and the degree culminating with it on the straight sphere, the star is above the earth; if less, below it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.203-204}

## The distance of the degree from the cusp
The distance of a star or degree from the midheaven or the nadir is found in seasonal hours. To find its distance from the rising or the setting point, these hours are subtracted from six, and the remainder is its distance from the cusp. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.204}
