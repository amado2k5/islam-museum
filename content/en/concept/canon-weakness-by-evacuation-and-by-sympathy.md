## Summary
Among the causes of weakness Ibn Sina lists those tied to evacuation, such as bleeding, diarrhoea, draining dropsical water, heavy sweating, excess exercise, pains and much hunger, then weakness following the weakness of another organ and the unequal strength of organs by creation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.152-153}.

## Evacuation
Among the causes of weakness are those connected with evacuation: loss of blood; diarrhoea, especially of thin humours; tapping dropsical water when much of it is let out; a large flow of matter all at once from an abscess, or its bursting of itself; heavy sweat; and excessive exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.152-153}.

## Pains and change of temperament
Pains too dissolve the spirit, even where the temperament has changed. Some have more effect, such as pain at the mouth of the stomach, whether stretching or irritating. Any pain near the region of the heart, and fevers, weaken by dissolving and by drawing from the body and the spirit. Change of temperament and wide pores help bring about weakness by dissolving, and much hunger is of this kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.153}.

## Following another organ, and unequal organs
Sometimes the weakness of the whole body follows the weakness of another organ, as when harm reaching the mouth of the stomach dissolves its strength; and a man whose heart and brain are strongly affected by slight harms is quick to dissolve and to weary at the least thing. A cause may also be much suffering of illnesses, and some organs are weaker in creation than others, like the lung and the brain, so that they take in what the strong by creation drives from itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.153}.
