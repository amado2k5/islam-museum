## Summary
For the knee Ibn Sina lists three straightening muscles in front of the thigh and bending muscles; for the ankle, lifting and lowering muscles; and for the toes many muscles; he closes the group by saying the muscles of the whole body number five hundred and twenty-nine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.75-77}.

## The knee
The muscles moving the knee joint are three set in front of the thigh, the largest muscles of the thigh itself, whose action is straightening; one of them is double with two heads, and the other two unite into a single transverse tendon that wraps the patella, secures it, joins the top of the shank and straightens the knee by drawing the shank out. For bending there is a narrow long muscle from the flank and pubic bones, and three, inner, outer and middle; and in the knee joint a muscle as if buried in its bend {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.75}.

## The foot and toes
The great lifting muscle of the foot lies in front of the inner reed and joins near the base of the big toe; another from the head of the outer reed joins near the base of the little toe. The lowering muscles are a pair arising from the head of the thigh bone and descending to fill the back of the shank, from which grows one of the greatest tendons, the heel tendon joined to the heel bone, drawing it back; it is the cause of the foot's stability on the ground. If these muscles or their tendon are harmed, the foot is lamed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.76}. The toes have many bending muscles, and in the sole there are ten that escaped the anatomists, first made known by [[person/galen|Galen]], two for each toe. They are so intermixed that when one is harmed the others weaken, which is why bending of some toes alone becomes hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.76-77}.

## Count of the body's muscles
After enumerating the muscles of all the organs Ibn Sina says that all the muscles of the body come to five hundred and twenty-nine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.77}.
