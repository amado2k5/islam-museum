## Selection
The best fish in its body is not very large, not hard or dry in flesh, not so fatty that it crumbles, and neither slimy nor insipid; its taste is pleasant, its fat neither excessive nor thick, and it does not quickly rot once taken from the water. Among the kinds the shababit is the best, then the bunni and the marmahij, the sea sajj is not bad, the rijz and the summ are heavy, the kan'ad is good and the farsiyuk very good. As to the habitat, the best is the fish that lives in rocky places, then sandy ones, and in sweet flowing waters free of filth and mud, not of marshes or small lakes; the sea fish is praised and light, best of all the one found only in the sea and the deep, and one that lives in open water where winds ruffle it is better, and one that lives in much-troubled water is better because it needs more exercise. As to feeding, the fish that feeds on good herbage and plant roots is better than one that feeds on refuse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.605}

## Kinds and ways of eating
The best way to eat fish is the isfidbaj, then grilled on the pan; fried suits those with strong stomachs, with spices; the grilled is more nourishing and slower in descent and the boiled the opposite. As to the salted, the best is the fresh and recently salted, and the most praised is the one pickled with vinegar and spices; the water in which salted fish is boiled, especially the jirri, is strongly cleansing and enters drying enemas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Temperament
All fish is cold and moist, though some is warmer relative to the temperament of fish, then the kawsaj, the jirri and the marmahij; the salted is hot and dry and the older it grows the more so, and the brine of salted fish resembles muri in its states {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Actions and properties
The fresh generates watery phlegm and relaxes the nerves and suits only a very hot stomach, and its blood tends to thinness; near Bayt al-Maqdis there is a fish known as sayfyanus, and if the ash of its skin is dusted into the eyes of cattle it removes their whiteness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Wounds and ulcers
The burnt head of the samaris removes excess flesh in ulcers and stops their spreading, the water of salted fish benefits putrid ulcers and washes them, and sahnat and small fish are good in treating putrid ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Joints
An enema of the brine of the salted, repeated, benefits greatly in pain of the hip, and the fresh relaxes the nerves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Organs of the head
The small fish that the people of al-Sham call sir benefits, in the muri made from it as a rinse, one with malignant thrush, and the living ra'ad, brought near the head of one with a splitting headache, numbs the head to the sense of the pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Ocular organs
The skin of the sayfyanus is rubbed on mangy eyelids and benefits, its burnt skin enters eye drugs and, used as a collyrium with salt, removes pterygium; eating it fried brings a film over the eye, indeed all fish does {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.606}

## Organs of the chest
Fresh jirri cleans the windpipe and clears the voice, and so does the salted; the heads of small salted dried fish are good for a swollen uvula, and fish glue put into broths prevents spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.607}

## Excretory organs
The crop of the sayfyanus loosens the bowels though it is hard to digest, the flesh of jirri loosens the bowels when eaten fresh, every fish broth loosens the bowels, the heads of small salted dried fish are a good treatment for fissure of the anus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.607}

## Poisons and bites
The head of salted samarus, burnt, is placed on the bite of a rabid dog and the sting of a scorpion and benefits, and so does any fish; the broth of every fish benefits against poisons drunk and bitten, and the flesh of qunyun, as a poultice, benefits against the bite of a rabid dog and the bite of vermin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.607}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists the sakk, the marmahij, the qaws and the jirri as all increasing desire {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.607}
