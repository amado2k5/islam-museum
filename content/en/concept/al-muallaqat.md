## Summary
This page gathers the poets of the Mu'allaqat: the seven on whom the best-known recension agrees, which a research summary supplied to the museum says Ibn Abd Rabbih transmitted and al-Zawzani commented on, and the three that ten-ode recensions add, which the summary ascribes to recensions such as those of Abu Ubayda, Ibn Qutayba and al-Tibrizi. Each poet has a tribe and an approximate date as the summary tabulates them; where a date is approximate it is so in the source, and the museum has not recomputed it. The tables are credited to the Britannica page on the Mu'allaqat {cite:britannica-al-muallaqat}.

## The seven poets
1. [[person/imru-al-qays-ibn-hujr|Imru al-Qays]] of Kinda, died about 500 to 550 CE according to the summary {cite:britannica-imru-al-qays}.

2. [[person/tarafa-ibn-al-abd|Tarafa ibn al-Abd]] of Bakr ibn Wa'il, active in the mid-sixth century, about 543 to 569 {cite:britannica-tarafa-ibn-al-abd}.

3. [[person/zuhayr-ibn-abi-sulma|Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma]] of Muzayna, who lived among Ghatafan, about 520 to 609 {cite:britannica-zuhayr-ibn-abi-sulma}.

4. [[person/labid-ibn-rabia|Labid ibn Rabi'a]] of the Banu Amir of Hawazin, about 560 to 661 {cite:britannica-labid}.

5. [[person/amr-ibn-kulthum|Amr ibn Kulthum]] of Taghlib, in the sixth century, about 526 to 584 {cite:britannica-amr-ibn-kulthum}.

6. [[person/antara-ibn-shaddad|Antara ibn Shaddad]] of the Banu Abs, about 525 to 608 {cite:britannica-antarah-ibn-shaddad}.

7. [[person/al-harith-ibn-hilliza-al-yashkuri|al-Harith ibn Hilliza]] of Yashkur of Bakr ibn Wa'il, died about 574 to 580 {cite:wikipedia-al-harith-ibn-hilliza-al-yashkuri-en}. The Wikipedia page is used here only for this fact about the poet, as the summary itself gives it.

## The three added in the ten-ode recensions
The summary names three poets added in the ten-ode recensions. The first is al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani of Dhubyan of Ghatafan, about 600 CE {cite:britannica-al-nabighah-al-dhubyani}. The second is al-Asha, Maymun ibn Qays, of Qays ibn Tha'laba of Bakr ibn Wa'il, from before 570 to about 625 or 630 {cite:britannica-al-asha}. The third is [[person/ubayd-ibn-al-abras|Abid ibn al-Abras]] of the Banu Asad, about 500 to 554, whose reference in the summary is his diwan in Lyall's edition held on the Internet Archive {cite:archive-diwans-abid-ibn-al-abras-lyall}. The museum holds no person record for al-Nabigha yet, so his name carries no link.

## What the numbers mean
Seven and ten mean that the list differs with the recension, and the museum does not rule which list is right. What matters to a student of Arabic is that these odes are among the sources to which grammarians and philologists turned as evidence of eloquent speech, which the page [[concept/al-shir-al-jahili-ka-shahid-lughawi|Pre-Islamic verse as linguistic evidence]] sets out and [[concept/shiir-diwan-al-arab|Poetry as the Arabs' register]] places in a wider frame. Each of the seven odes has its own record in the museum under its poet's name.

## By tribe
Three of the poets belong to Bakr ibn Wa'il: Tarafa, al-Harith ibn Hilliza of the Yashkur branch, and al-Asha of the Qays ibn Tha'laba branch. Ghatafan gathers Zuhayr, who was of Muzayna and lived among it, and al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani of Dhubyan. Kinda has Imru al-Qays alone, Hawazin has Labid of the Banu Amir, Taghlib has Amr ibn Kulthum, the Banu Abs have Antara, and the Banu Asad have Abid ibn al-Abras. As the summary's table shows, most of the poets thus come from the tribes of Rabi'a and of Qays Aylan; the museum does not claim that the lists were drawn up with this in mind, so this is a remark on the table and no judgement on the recension {cite:britannica-al-muallaqat}.

## By time
The earliest dates in the table are those of Imru al-Qays and Abid ibn al-Abras, about 500 CE for both, and the latest is Labid, whose life in the table runs to about 661, forty years or more after the rise of Islam. Between the two ends stand Zuhayr to about 609, Antara to about 608, and al-Asha to about 625 or 630. So the odes of the list do not belong to one generation, and some of their authors lived into the Islamic age, which the summary records in the lives of Labid and al-Asha without setting out any account of their conversion, so this page says nothing on it {cite:britannica-labid} {cite:britannica-al-asha}.

## Basis
This page rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the museum did not open the pages it cites, and the founder approved relying on it as it stands on 21 November 2026. Everything here is therefore what the summary reports, not what the museum has verified; dates are approximate as given, and no poet's diwan or any ode's text was read. The page quotes no verse from any of them. What is ascribed to a particular recension, such as al-Tibrizi's or Ibn Qutayba's, is attributed to the summary alone.
