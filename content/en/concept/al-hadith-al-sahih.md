## Summary
In [[work/nuzhat-al-nazar|Nuzhat al-Nazar]] [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] defines the report sound in itself as "a solitary report transmitted by an upright narrator of complete accuracy, with a connected chain, free of hidden defect and anomaly". {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 52} It is the first of four kinds of accepted report: sound in itself, sound through another, [[concept/al-hadith-al-hasan|good]] in itself and good through another. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 53} The editor, [[person/nur-al-din-itr|Nur al-Din 'Itr]], counts these qualifiers — "in itself" and "through another" — among Ibn Hajar's choices that became standard after him. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 21}

## The elements of the definition
- **Upright ('adl)**: one with a settled disposition that keeps him to piety and decorum, piety meaning avoidance of bad deeds — association of others with God, open sin or reprehensible innovation. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 53}
- **Accuracy (dabt)**: accuracy of memory, retaining what he heard so that he can recall it at will; and accuracy of the written record, keeping his book safe from the time he heard and corrected it until he transmits from it; "complete" marks the highest degree. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 53}
- **Connected**: a chain with no gap, each narrator having heard the report from his teacher. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 54}
- **Hidden defect (mu'allal)**: one containing a concealed, damaging flaw. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 54}
- **Anomalous (shadhdh)**: one in which a narrator contradicts someone weightier (see the anomalous and the denounced). {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 54}

## Ranks and the soundest chains
Sound reports differ in rank as these qualities differ in strength, since they produce the predominant probability on which soundness rests. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 54} The highest rank includes what some masters called the soundest chains, such as al-Zuhri from Salim ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar from his father, Muhammad ibn Sirin from 'Abida ibn 'Amr from 'Ali, and Ibrahim al-Nakha'i from 'Alqama from Ibn Mas'ud; below them, Burayd ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Abi Burda from his grandfather from his father Abu Musa; below that, narrators whose solitary reports count as good, such as Muhammad ibn Ishaq from 'Asim ibn 'Umar from Jabir, and 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb from his father from his grandfather. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 55-56} The view relied on is not to call any single chain absolutely "the soundest", though the sum of what the masters said shows these chains to be preferable to others. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 56-57}

## The two Sahihs compared
- To these ranks belongs the precedence of what al-Bukhari and Muslim both give over what only one gives, and of what only al-Bukhari gives over what only Muslim gives. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 57}
- The majority declared [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhari's Sahih]] the sounder, and no one is known to have stated the opposite; Abu 'Ali al-Naysaburi's "there is nothing under the sky sounder than Muslim's book" denies that anything is sounder, not that anything is equal; and some Maghribis' preference for [[work/sahih-muslim|Muslim's Sahih]] concerns its presentation and arrangement. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 57}
- He gives the grounds of al-Bukhari's precedence: in connection, al-Bukhari required proof that a narrator met his source at least once, while Muslim was content with contemporaneity; in uprightness and accuracy, Muslim's criticised narrators are more numerous; in anomaly and defect, fewer of al-Bukhari's reports were criticised. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 58} He quotes [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]]: "But for al-Bukhari, Muslim would neither have come nor gone." {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 59}
- The ranks then run: al-Bukhari, Muslim, what meets the conditions of both, of al-Bukhari, of Muslim, and what meets neither; a lower class may outrank a higher one through other indications, as when a well-known report in Muslim supported by indications is preferred to an absolutely singular one in al-Bukhari. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 59-60}

## Sound through another
If accuracy is lighter while the other conditions hold, the report is [[concept/al-hadith-al-hasan|good in itself]], and with multiple chains it is declared sound, because the combined form has a strength that makes up for the shortfall in the narrator's accuracy. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 60-61}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Shafi'i): Ibn Hajar.** Al-Bukhari's Sahih is sounder than Muslim's in connection, uprightness and accuracy of narrators, and the smaller number of criticised reports; this is the majority view. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 57-58}

**Sunni: Abu 'Ali al-Naysaburi and some Maghribis, as Ibn Hajar reports them.** Abu 'Ali is reported to have said no book under the sky is sounder than Muslim's, and some Maghribis to have preferred it; Ibn Hajar reads the first as denying superiority rather than equality and the second as concerning arrangement and presentation. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 57}
:::
