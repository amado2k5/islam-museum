## Nature
Some said it is the awsaj, and a kind of it is called dog's ulayq, with a fruit like an olive, woolly inside, found in the country of Shahrazur and in the country of Fasus; the author holds that ulayq is a plant other than the awsaj, because Dioscorides made clear in his book what ulayq is and what awsaj is, the two differing in growth and in actions. He said that ulayq is a well-known plant, and one kind grows on Mount Andi from which it takes this name, its branches being much softer than the first ulayq, with small thorns, and another kind has no thorns at all; its action is like that of the former, but it is superior in that its flower, pounded fine with honey and smeared on the eye, benefits a hot swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Selection
Its juice thickened by drying in the sun is stronger in action {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Temperament
It is cold and dry, and its ripe fruit has some heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Actions and properties
It is astringent and drying in all its parts, the leaf less so because of its wateriness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Cosmetics
A decoction of its branches with their leaves dyes the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Swellings and pustules
A poultice of it and its leaf prevents the spread of herpes and is good on erysipelas, and in the root of the ulayq there is a subtlety together with astringency, and for that reason it breaks up stones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Wounds and ulcers
It benefits ulcers on the head and heals wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Organs of the head
If its leaves are chewed they firm the gums and heal thrush, and so does its ripe fruit; the juice of its fruit and leaf heals hot pains of the mouth, its leaf heals ulcers of the head, and taking much of the fruit of the ulayq causes headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.616}

## Ocular organs
It benefits protrusion of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.617}

## Organs of the chest
Its parts benefit spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.617}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The weak stomach that receives matter is given a poultice of its leaf, which strengthens it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.617}

## Excretory organs
It restrains the bowels; the dog's ulayq, when the wool in its fruit is removed and it is cooked, has a decoction that restrains the bowels, and as a poultice it benefits piles growing in the anus from which blood flows; it and its flower benefit ulcers of the intestine and loose bowels, and it breaks up stones by the subtlety in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.617}

## Poisons and bites
It suits the bite of the animal known as qurtis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.617}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists the dog's ulayq as cutting the chronic flow of moisture from the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.617}
