## Summary
Nawm in the lexical sense is calm and stillness; technically it is the relaxing of the brain's nerves through vapours that rise from the stomach. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16667} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16668}

## Sleep, drowsiness, stupor and fainting
Nu'as is the first stage of sleep, sina a drowsiness that does not remove reason, and fainting the loss of sensation and movement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16668}

## Its rulings
Sleep is permitted in itself; it may be obligatory when a duty depends on it, recommended when one dozes in prayer or recitation, forbidden when one sleeps through the prayer time, and disliked in some cases, such as sleeping after the afternoon prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16668} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16669}

## Recommended conduct before sleep and on waking
Before sleep one is recommended to put out the lamps, close the water-skin and the door, and lay the right hand under the cheek. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16670}
On waking one is recommended to recite the prescribed formulas and to wash the hands three times before putting them into a vessel. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16671}

## Sleep and ablution
Sleep breaks the ablution in general, according to the majority, relying on the saying 'the eyes are the binding of the buttocks'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16672}

## The sleeper's acts
The sleeper's words in legal acts are void: his consecration, divorce and admission do not take effect, except for what needs no intention, such as standing at Arafat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16673} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16674}

## Injury and destruction of property
An injury by a sleeper is treated as an error: the diya falls on his kin and expiation on him; in destruction of property he is liable like one who is awake. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16675} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16676}

## Sleep in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episodes 1, 2, 9 and 10)
The Sheikh counts sleep among the blessings, says excess in permissible things harms a person, and warns against exaggeration in religion (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:40:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 00:41:00} He says that if a person dozes in prayer he should sleep until the drowsiness passes, since he may curse himself without noticing (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 01:09:00}
He says that sleep before midnight is more beneficial than sleep after it, attributing this to scholars without verification (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 01:10:00} He says sleep is linked to growth hormone, a claim that could not be checked (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep01 01:10:30} He urges early sleep, holding that staying up late wastes health (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:48:30}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)
The Sheikh explains that sleep is rest, meaning calm, health and renewed energy, and that the night is a garment [[passage/quran-78-9|An-Naba 9]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:36:00}. He says doctors know that the longest night is that of the sick and worried person [[passage/quran-78-10|An-Naba 10]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:36:00}. He counts the night as a garment and sleep as a blessing, and notes that the day was not called life [[passage/quran-78-11|An-Naba 11]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:49:00}. He says that life belongs to the hereafter, to acts of obedience, to production and to good works {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:49:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 8)
The Sheikh says that in sleep the soul rests from the body, and that a part of it rises to the highest assembly [[passage/quran-39-42|Az-Zumar 42]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:28:00}. He notes that the body turns right and left and swallows saliva so that life continues during sleep {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep08 00:28:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 9)
The Sheikh says that sleep is the lesser death {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:11:30}. He notes that people may sleep side by side and each see what the other does not: a wife may see a lovely dream while her husband, asleep without remembrance of God or ablution, sees something else {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:12:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 10)
The Sheikh explains that the Sahira is the name of the place in which there is no sleep, and that sleep belongs to this world [[passage/quran-79-14|An-Nazi'at 14]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:13:00}. He holds that long and short sleep are measured by the abundance and fewness of sins {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:13:00}. He says that whoever sleeps a great deal should know his sins are many, and holds that the people of Paradise do not sleep because they have no sins {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:13:30}. He says that sleep is tied to one's concern {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep10 00:13:30}.

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] explains the Companions' report that the Prophet disliked sleeping before isha and talking after it except for a need, such as an army setting out or a guest arriving, and sees in it agreement with the natural order of night and day. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:09:00} He says the pineal gland secretes melatonin in the dark and in the first hours of the night, and that cells of the optic chiasm stop its secretion when light falls on the retina, so that for him the best hours of sleep run from after isha to dawn. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep14 00:09:30}
