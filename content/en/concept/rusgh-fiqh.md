## Summary
Rusgh in the lexicon is the joint between the forearm and the palm; in the jurists' usage it marks the limit of washing the hands in ablution and wiping them in tayammum, and the place of the cut for theft. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8731} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8733} It is a sunna to wash the hands up to the wrists at the start of ablution. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8731} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8732} The jurists differ on wiping in tayammum: the Hanafis, the Shafi'is in the school's view and some Malikis take the elbows; the Hanbalis, a view of Abu Hanifa, most Malikis and the older Shafi'i view take the wrists; al-Zuhri and Muhammad ibn Maslama take the shoulders. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8732} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8733} The jurists of the cities hold that a thief's hand is cut from the wrist. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8733}
