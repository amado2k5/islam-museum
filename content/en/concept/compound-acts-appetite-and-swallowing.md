## Summary
In the last chapter of the part on faculties Ibn Sina shows that some single acts are completed by one power, like digestion, and some by two, like the appetite for food, swallowing and the passage of food, and that the origin of an act may be a power together with a quality {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.101-102}.

## Appetite for food
The appetite for food is completed by a natural attractive power and a sensing power in the mouth of the stomach. The attractive does it by moving the lengthwise fibre, demanding what it draws and sucking in the moistures present; the sensing does it by sensing this affection and the gnawing of black bile that rouses appetite. It takes two powers because if the sensing one is afflicted, the meaning called hunger and appetite is void and one does not desire food though the body needs it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.101}.

## Swallowing and the passage of food
Swallowing likewise needs two powers: the natural attractive and the voluntary attractive; the first completes its act through the lengthwise fibre at the stomach's mouth and the gullet, the second through the fibre of the swallowing muscles. If one of them fails, swallowing becomes hard, and so it does if neither has failed but one has not yet stirred to act, which is why it is hard to swallow what we do not desire. The passage of food is completed by a pushing power of the organ it leaves and a drawing power of the organ it goes to, and the same is true of expelling waste by the two outlets {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.101-102}.

## A power with a quality
Sometimes the origin of an act is two powers, one psychic and one natural, and sometimes a power and a quality, such as cooling that blocks matter, which helps the expulsive power to resist the humour pouring toward an organ, hold it back and push it away. The cold quality blocks in two ways by itself, by thickening the substance of what pours and narrowing the pores, and in a third way by accident, by quenching the drawing heat. The drawing quality draws by the opposite of these means; compulsion of the void draws first the fine and then the thick, while the natural drawing power draws what is most fitting or specific to it, and sometimes the thicker is the most fitting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.102}.
