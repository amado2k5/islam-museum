## Summary
Rafd in the lexicon is abandonment; in the jurists' usage it is treating what has been done of the act and its intention as if it had not been. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8802} It differs from annulment, from defect and from voiding. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8802} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8803} For the majority, abandoning the intention of ablution after it is complete has no effect, while abandoning the intention of prayer during it voids it; the Malikis, Hanbalis and one Shafi'i view hold that abandoning the intention of fasting voids the fast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8803} The Hanafis hold that abandoning ihram does not void it, and if a Meccan who has begun the lesser pilgrimage adds the greater one, the Hanafis require him to abandon one of them with a sacrifice and a makeup. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8804} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8805} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8806}
