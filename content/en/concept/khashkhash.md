## Nature
The book quotes Dioscorides that of it there is the garden, from whose seed bread is made that is eaten in health and which is used with honey in place of sesame, its heads elongated and its seed white; the wild, its heads broader and its seed black; and a third wild kind, smaller and more repulsive; the power of the three kinds is cooling; the book describes how opium and a weaker preparation are obtained, a description left out here; there is also a horned sea kind with fruit like horns in a curved husk and black seed, growing on the shores of the sea, and some erred in thinking mamitha is drawn from it; and the buttery khashkhash, named because it resembles butter in whiteness, whose fruit is gathered when it has reached its full size {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.699-700}

## Choice
The best and safest is the white; the best of its gum is the dense, heavy, strongly scented, bitter in taste, easy to dissolve, soft, smooth and white, not rough or granular, which melts in the sun and kindles near a lamp flame; the book says it is adulterated with mamitha, the juice of wild lettuce, gum or fat, and describes the signs of each; it quotes a physician of the Greeks that one with pain of the eye or ear should be spared this drug because it darkens the eye and makes the hearing heavy, Adriyus that without adulteration it would blind whoever applies it as kohl, and another that it is of use only through its smell for inducing sleep and harmful in everything else; the book holds that they erred and argues from what appeared in trials {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Temperament
The garden is cold and dry in the second degree, the black in the third, and it is said to the fourth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Actions and properties
The kinds of khashkhash are cooling and there is no nourishment in it; the black is thickening and drying; the horned sea kind is cleansing, cutting and strongly cleansing; and its wild flower clears the marks of ulcers in the eye of cattle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Swellings and pustules
Its kinds, except the sea kind, may be applied on erysipelas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Wounds and ulcers
The leaf of the horned shore kind is of benefit for dirty ulcers, eats excess flesh by its cleansing and removes scabs, and so does its flower, but it does not suit exposed ulcers for its excess of cleansing; of the wild a poultice with oil is made on ulcers and removes them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Joints
The sea kind is applied with milk on gout with benefit, and when the root of the wild is cooked in water and drunk it benefits sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Organs of the head
It is soporific, especially the black, and numbing; carried on a wick it brings sleep and prevents catarrh, and a sleepless person is helped when his forehead is poulticed with it, and likewise when washed with its decoction; the buttery used as an emetic helps the epileptic in that it cleanses their stomach in particular; its oil with rose oil is good for headache when the head is rubbed with it, though the book says that avoiding it where possible is better; and its decoction may be dropped into an ear of severe pain to calm it; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.700}

## Ocular organs
The cold kind is used in severe pains of the eye at need, and there is danger in it, as the book said of opium, unless it is mixed with some drugs that prevent its harm, when its harm is less; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.700-701}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits hot cough, catarrhs to the chest and spitting of blood, and an electuary is made of it of great benefit for that, especially when mixed with acacia and the juice of goat's beard; Ibn Masa said that the seed of the black clears the chest, while the husk is, it appears, one that makes expectoration difficult {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.701}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits the moistures of the stomach; the horned sea kind, its root cooked in water, benefits liver ailments and one with a thick humour in the belly, and the seed of the buttery kind causes vomiting, the same being said of the wild; the extent of the cooking is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.701}

## Excretory organs
The white and black, pounded fine and given with a dark astringent drink, cut chronic diarrhoea, though its nature is not without a loosening power; its strong decoction used as an enema benefits dysentery, its seed drunk with a qaratin drink softens the bowel, the buttery given as a drink causes vomiting and its seed loosens phlegm and raw matter, and so does the seed of an Egyptian kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.701}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that the seed of the garden kind with honey increases semen; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.701}
