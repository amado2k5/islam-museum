## Summary
Ibn Sina shows how to tell whether blackness comes from burning, cold, collapse of strength or a crisis, by the urine that went before, the state of the sediment, the smell and what follows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.186}.

## Burning and cold
Blackness from burning is recognised by yellow and red urine before it, a sediment that clings and is poorly formed, and a leaning to saffron, yellow or dusk. Blackness from cold is recognised by urine before it leaning to green and dullness, a small gathered sediment as if dry, and a purer black. The two temperaments may also be told apart by smell: strong smell with black urine shows heat, and no smell or a weak one shows cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.186}.

## Collapse and crisis
Blackness from the collapse of the innate strength is recognised by the collapse and slackening of strength that follows it; when nature is utterly defeated there is no smell. Blackness by way of purging and crisis is recognised by unripe watery urine before it, a lightness in the body after it and a large, abundant amount. Its occasions include the end of spring, the resolving of spleen ailments, pains of the back and womb, black-bilious fevers and the stopping of an accustomed flow from the anus or of the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.186}.
