Yasir lexically means easy or slight, and it is the small amount; its opposite is 'usr, hardship. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17997}

Slight impurities: the jurists differ on which slight impurities are excused, whether the slight is measured by custom or by a dirham, and where the excuse applies (prayer, the garment, the body or the place). See the entries on excusing and impurity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17998}

Slight movement in prayer: all agree a slight movement or action does not break prayer. The Prophet is reported to have prayed carrying Umama, the daughter of his daughter Zaynab, putting her down when he prostrated and taking her up when he stood; he ordered the killing of the two black things in prayer, the snake and the scorpion; he took off his sandals in prayer; and he wrapped himself in his garment in prayer. The limit is disputed: some say custom decides, others say the observer decides, so that whatever leaves the observer in doubt whether one is in prayer is slight. See prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17998}

Slight speech in prayer: all agree that deliberate speech breaks prayer, slight or long, unless it is to correct the prayer. The hadith of Zayd ibn Arqam records that they spoke in prayer until the command to stand devoutly in silence was revealed (surah al-Baqara, verse 238). Whether speech to correct the prayer breaks it is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17999}

Slight pause in the Fatiha: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that a long deliberate silence during the Fatiha breaks the recitation, and it must be begun again, since it shows withdrawal; the Hanbalis confine this to the imam and the one praying alone. An unintended slight silence does not require a restart. The Shafi'is add that a slight silence intended to break the recitation does break it, on the sounder view; the slight is what custom allows, such as breathing or resting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17999}

Slight gap before the prostration of forgetfulness: those who place it between the tashahhud and the salam hold that one who forgets it and says salam, then remembers after a slight gap, may prostrate if he wishes, on Ibn Mas'ud's report that the Prophet prostrated for forgetfulness after the salam and the speech. If he omits it deliberately, the sounder Shafi'i view is that it is lost by the salam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18000}

Slight gap in contracts: a slight gap between offer and acceptance does not spoil the contract, since it does not show withdrawal; an unrelated speech, even slight, spoils it, in the Shafi'i view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18000}

Slight gap in the exception: a valid exception must be joined to what it excepts, but a slight pause for breath, stammering or recollection does not break the join, since custom does not count it a gap. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18000}

Slight break between sucklings: the entry continues on p.18000 with the rule on a child who turns from the breast briefly; the rest is not yet drafted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.18000}
