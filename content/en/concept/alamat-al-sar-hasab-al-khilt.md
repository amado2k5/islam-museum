## The phlegmatic
The sign of that whose cause is phlegm: the saliva is sharp, frothy, thick and copious; there is something like molten glass in the urine; cowardice, fright, laziness, heaviness and forgetfulness abound in him. It may also be known from the vomit, the colour of the foam and the colour of the blood, from age and country and the past causes of foods and regimens, and from what stillness and rest show, and the colour of face and eye. If the phlegm is moreover raw and cold, forgetfulness is greater and the epilepsy, lethargy, dullness and heaviness of head and body more loosening and weakening — a very bad kind. As for that which comes of salty phlegm, the lethargy in it is less, the cold of the brain lighter, and the movements sounder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.123}

## The melancholic
The sign of that whose cause is black bile is black-bile vomit — either like black blood, or burnt sharp matter, or acid matter from which the ground bubbles; its sufferer's temper inclines to confusion in his mind and to the melancholic state, and his intellect does not clear when he wakes. Black bile is also inferred from the colour of face and eye, dryness of nostril and tongue, and the black-bile-generating regimens. If the black bile comes of natural blood, the epilepsy is with relaxation, little speech and stillness, and its sufferer is a man of quiet calm thoughts; if it is of the kind of burnt yellow bile — the harif — his confusion is madness-like with much speech and shouting, his epilepsy disturbed and quick to pass, sometimes with fever, especially if his black bile is thin; if it is of sanguine black blood, his states are with laughter. The substance of the black bile is known from the vomit: like the lees of blood — natural black bile; like the lees of wine — burnt black bile; rough and astringent — the utmost cold and dryness; thin and acid with foam — it bubbles on the ground; thick — without foam {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.123} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.124}

## The sanguine and the bilious
As for the sign of that whose cause is blood: if it acts by boiling and motion rather than quantity, it shows little effect before the epilepsy in colour and jugulars, but heaviness, dullness, relaxation, copious saliva and mucus appear from it as from phlegm — but with heat and redness in the eye and a sanguine vapour over the head; if it acts by quantity there is throbbing of the jugulars with the signs and a prior state like choking. The sign of that due to a bilious matter — which is rare — is that the hurt and anguish from it are sharpest, the convulsion least, its duration shortest, but the motions are the most disturbed; it is indicated by vomit, inflammation, severe confusion of the intellect and yellowness of the colour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.124}
