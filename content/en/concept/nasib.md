## Summary
Nasib, in the lexicon, is a share or portion of anything, and the part that is divided. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15742} Every nasib is a fixed share, but not every fixed share is a nasib. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15742}

## Rulings on the share
In inheritance the law fixes the shares of heirs: a half, a quarter, an eighth, two-thirds, a third and a sixth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15742} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15743}
In partnership a partner may dispose of his share on differing views; his hand is a trust, so he is liable only for transgression or negligence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15743}
In division the fee is distributed by shares in the majority view, and in dividing property the share follows the property's layout. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15743} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15744}
In pre-emption the pre-emptor acquires the share in ways the jurists dispute, including the treatment of buildings made by the buyer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15744} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15745}
When one of two co-owners frees his share of a joint slave, a wealthy owner frees the whole slave and owes the other partner the value of that partner's share, according to the Malikis, Shafi'is and the usual Hanbali view; a poor owner frees only his share. Abu Hanifa differs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15745}

## Where it is discussed
Inheritance, partnership, division, pre-emption and manumission are treated under their own entries. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15743} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15745}
