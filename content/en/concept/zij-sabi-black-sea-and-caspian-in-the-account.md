## Summary
Al-Battani introduces the sea of Bontos, running from Laziqa to great Constantinople, one thousand and sixty miles long and three hundred wide; into it from the north runs the river Tanais out of a lake, and at Constantinople a gulf leaves it like a river and pours into the sea of Egypt, about three miles wide there. He also names the sea of Jurjan, the sea of al-Bab, eight hundred miles from west to east and six hundred wide. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.31}.

## The sea of Bontos
He says the sea of Bontos runs from Laziqa to great Constantinople and is a vast sea even though it is called a lake; he gives three hundred miles from east to west and a hundred wide for the lake-like water named with it, and at Constantinople a gulf bursts out of it, running like a river into the sea of Egypt, about three miles wide there, with Constantinople on it. The figures in this passage run together in the scan, so nothing beyond what is stated here is relied on. See [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.31}.

## The sea of Jurjan
He names the sea of Jurjan, the sea of al-Bab, eight hundred miles long from west to east and six hundred wide, with two islands in it. The text gives the sea two names, Jurjan and al-Bab, without explaining either. See [[place/caspian-sea|the Caspian Sea]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.31}.
