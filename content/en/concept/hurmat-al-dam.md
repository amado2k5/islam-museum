## Overview
The sanctity of a Muslim's blood is the prohibition of the blood of one who bears witness that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is the Messenger of God, except by one of three things. This is Ibn Rajab's commentary on the fourteenth hadith of the Forty. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.291-313} The sanctity rests on [[concept/shahada|the testimony of faith]].

## The three things
In the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud in the two Sahihs: "The married adulterer, a life for a life, and the one who abandons his religion and separates from the community." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.291-313}

In the version of Uthman in al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i, and Ibn Majah: "Apostasy after his Islam, or adultery after his marriage, or killing a soul without a soul." One of al-Nasa'i's versions states the ruling for each of them. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.291-313}

## What Ibn Rajab relates
Ibn Rajab calls these things the right of Islam by which blood may be made lawful to shed. He reports that the killing for each of them is agreed upon among Muslims. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.291-313}

He reports the consensus that the punishment of the married adulterer is stoning to death, and that the Prophet stoned Ma'iz and al-Ghamidiyya. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.291-313}

He notes that stoning is among the rulings whose wording was abrogated from the Qur'an, and that Ibn Abbas derived it from a verse in al-Ma'ida. In a report of Umar, he feared that a time would come when some people would say they do not find stoning in the Book of God. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.291-313}

What is not recorded here: the positions of the schools on the details of each of these cases. The verse is not reproduced here; it needs a further source.
