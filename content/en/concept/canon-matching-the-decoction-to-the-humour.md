## Summary
Ibn Sina lays down that pills are drunk in a decoction of like kind: the bile purgative in fumitory, the black-bile purgative in dodder of thyme and polypody, and the phlegm purgative in centaury, taken lukewarm with the stomach and feet of the drinker kept warm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.280}.

## Decoction and warmth
Ibn Sina reports the view that pills which are to be given in decoctions should be given in a decoction of their own kind: a pill that purges bile in a decoction of fumitory, for instance, one for black bile in a decoction of dodder of thyme or polypody, and one that brings out phlegm in a decoction such as centaury. The decoction should be drunk lukewarm, or the pill taken in lukewarm water, and the drinker's stomach and feet kept warm; when his breath has settled he rises and moves a little at a time, for this movement helps. He sips hot water from time to time as much as lets the drug purge, bring itself out and have its force broken {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.280}.
