## The commendation of teaching
Al-Bukhari has from Uthman ibn Affan that the Prophet said: "the best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5027), and the hadith adds that Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami taught recitation in Uthman's governorate until al-Hajjaj {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. And from Masruq: Abdullah ibn Amr mentioned Ibn Mas'ud and said: I heard the Prophet say "take the Qur'an from four: from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, Salim the mawla of Abu Hudhayfa, Mu'adh ibn Jabal and Ubayy ibn Ka'b" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4999 and 3808, Sahih Muslim 2464) {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim}.

## The muqri' of Medina
The Prophet sent Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr to Medina before the Hijra with the men of the first Aqaba, to teach them the religion and recite the Qur'an to them; he was called al-muqri', the first to bear the name, as Wafa' al-wafa' records. He stayed first with As'ad ibn Zurara then with Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, until people entered Islam in every house of the Ansar {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa}.

## Transmission and review
The teaching of the Qur'an was mouth to mouth: the teacher recited, the learner heard and repeated until he mastered it, and the reader presented his reading to his teacher. The books of tabaqat report that Jibril reviewed the Qur'an with the Prophet once a year, and twice in the year of his death, in which he also kept i'tikaf twenty nights — as Abu Hurayra reports (Sahih al-Bukhari 4998) {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. From these two principles — talaqqi and 'ard — the later craft of ijaza and isnad in recitation arose {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.
