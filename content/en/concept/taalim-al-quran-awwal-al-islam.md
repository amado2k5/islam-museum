## The commendation of teaching
Al-Bukhari has from Uthman ibn Affan that the Prophet said: "the best of you are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}, and from Ibn Mas'ud that he said: "take the Qur'an from four: from Ibn Umm Abd — meaning himself — Salim the mawla of Abu Hudhayfa, Ubayy ibn Ka'b and Mu'adh ibn Jabal" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## The muqri' of Medina
The Prophet sent Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr to Medina before the Hijra with the men of the first Aqaba, to teach them the religion and recite the Qur'an to them; he was called al-muqri', the first to bear the name, as Wafa' al-wafa' records. He stayed first with As'ad ibn Zurara then with Sa'd ibn Mu'adh, until people entered Islam in every house of the Ansar {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa}.

## Transmission and review
The teaching of the Qur'an was mouth to mouth: the teacher recited, the learner heard and repeated until he mastered it, and the reader presented his reading to his teacher. The books of tabaqat report that the Prophet reviewed the Qur'an with Jibril every Ramadan, and twice in the year of his death, as Ibn 'Abbas reports {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. From these two principles — talaqqi and 'ard — the later craft of ijaza and isnad in recitation arose {cite:ibn-sad-tabaqat-bewley}.
