## Ascending and descending
The latitudes of the northern planets are added, or subtracted if they differ, to give the latitude of the wanted planet on the side where it falls. For Venus and Mercury the centre is corrected by the third table and read in the half where it falls, and for Mercury the anomaly is corrected by one tenth of the deviation. Whether the planet is rising or falling is found by comparing its latitude ten days apart: if north and increasing it is rising, if north and decreasing falling; if south and increasing falling, if south and decreasing rising. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.179-180}

## A second test by the anomaly
For Saturn, Jupiter and Mars there is a second test: with a northern latitude and anomaly less than qaf, 180 degrees, the planet is rising; if more, falling. With a southern latitude and anomaly less than qaf it is falling; if more, rising. For Venus and Mercury the text says that their quick motion round the Sun makes their state hard to fix except on the earlier side. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.180}
