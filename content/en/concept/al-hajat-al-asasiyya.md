## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat holds that basic human needs—food, clothing, water, and shelter—must be guaranteed for the general public, and that a sound economy contributes to securing them {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 67}.

## Partnership in Water, Pasture, and Fire
The author cites the hadith "People are partners in three things: water, pasture, and fire," and considers that the Messenger recommended this, without mentioning the source or grade of the hadith {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 67}. He argues that the modern age may add shelter, clothing, and whatever is considered essential in its time {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 67}.

## The Difference Between Essential and Non-Essential
The author considers confusing basic needs with non-essential ones to be a cause of imbalance in societies: some people cannot obtain enough to satisfy their basic needs, while others go beyond them to luxuries, a disparity that increases class conflict and economic ills {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 57}. See [[concept/al-nudra-al-iqtisadiyya|Economic Scarcity]].

## Note
The hadith is mentioned here as a citation by the author without a ruling on it, and the reader needs to check its grade in hadith collections before relying on it.

## From Zaydan Qa'dat's Book: pp. 83–164 of the PDF (Basic Needs in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat considers basic needs to be what occupies a person's concern regarding food, clothing, and shelter, and holds that shelter may, under changing circumstances, become one of the shared necessities like water, pasture, and fire {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 115}. Among the needs guaranteed by private ownership he counts food, a means of transport, shelter, medical treatment, and education, noting that some of these are non-material and require material and financial support {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 152} {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 153}.

## According to the Author (pages 301–323)
The author counts essential needs as food, medicine, clothing, housing, and marriage, without extravagance or stinginess {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 302}. According to al-Juwayni, they include staple food, meat, medicine, fruit, clothing befitting dignity, and housing, excluding marriage, and he holds that need reaches the level of sufficiency rather than bare survival {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 322} {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 323}. The author also reports from 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz that housing, a servant, a horse, and household furnishings are included in basic need, so neither the house nor the servant may be sold to pay off a debt {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 348}.
