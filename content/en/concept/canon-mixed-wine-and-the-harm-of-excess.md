## Summary
Ibn Sina says mixed wine relaxes and moistens the stomach and intoxicates faster yet clears the skin, that wine on an empty stomach or after excessive movement harms the brain and nerve, that repeated drunkenness is very bad, that much wine turns in some stomachs to bad bile and in others to sharp vinegar, and that most of the harm falls on the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.

## Mixed wine
Mixed wine relaxes and moistens the stomach and intoxicates faster because the watery part carries it through, yet it clears the skin and refines the psychic faculties. The prudent avoid wine on an empty stomach, before the organs of the moist have had their fill of water, or after excessive movement, for these two harm the brain and nerve and bring on convulsion and confusion of mind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.

## Excess and its management
Repeated drunkenness is very bad: it spoils the temper of the liver and brain, weakens the nerve and brings nervous diseases, apoplexy and sudden death. Much wine turns into bad bile in some stomachs and into sharp vinegar in others, and the harm of both is great. Some have held that intoxication once or twice a month does good. Since most of the harm falls on the brain, one of weak brain drinks only a little, mixed. For one who has filled himself the right course is to hurry to vomit; if that is not easy he drinks much water, alone or with honey, then after vomiting bathes, anoints himself with plenty of oil and sleeps {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.
