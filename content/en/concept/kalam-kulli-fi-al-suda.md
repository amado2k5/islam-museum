## Definition of headache and its cause
Headache is pain in the organs of the head, and every pain has as its cause a sudden change of temperament, a break of continuity, or both together {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.44} The change of temperament is one of the sixteen known kinds, and the moist does not cause pain unless it comes with a matter that moves and breaks the continuity {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.44} The two causes of pain come together in swellings, whose kinds, as the reader has learned, are four {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.44}

## Where the cause may lie
All of this may be in the substance of the brain itself, in the membrane that surrounds it, in the two sides that surround it, in the vessels, or in the membranes outside the skull because of the connections known from the anatomy already described {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.44} The editor's footnote glosses al-mutif as al-muhit, "surrounding" {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.44} The harmful cause in any of these organs may be settled in the organ itself, or it may be there by the sharing of another organ with it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.44-45}

## Headache by sharing with another organ or the whole body
The sharing may be with an organ joined to the organs of the head by interlacing nerves, such as the stomach, the diaphragm and other organs if there are any; or with an organ joined to the brain by interlacing vessels, veins and arteries, such as the heart, the liver and the spleen; or with an organ that adjoins it in another way, such as the lung placed beneath it, so that its affliction passes to the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.45} Or it may be with an organ that shares with one organ on one side and with the brain on the other, as the brain shares with the kidney in its pains; or with the whole body, as happens in fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.45} What comes by sharing may come in cycles and fits that follow the cycles and fits of the cause in the sharing organ, as with the stomach when bilious or other matters pour into it in cycles, and as with the cycles of the kinds of fever {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.45}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
In listing the organs joined to the organs of the head by interlacing nerves, Ibn Sina names the womb together with the stomach and the diaphragm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.45}
