## Summary
Khullu is lexically emptiness; in later books it names the usufruct a tenant owns over a waqf property in return for paying the administrator funds to repair it, with a fixed share of its use and the rent paid for the rest for the beneficiaries. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7800} It also names the right of a tenant of state land to keep it if he has built, planted or filled it, and the cash paid for giving up that right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7800}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7801} Related terms: hukr (the rent on waqf land for one who built or planted), farigh and ifrigh (giving up a right for a price), jadak (movable goods of a tenant attached to a shop or not), kirdar (what a farmer builds or plants with leave), murassad (a debt fixed on the waqf by the tenant\'s repair), and mashd al-maska (the right to farm another\'s land in later Hanafi usage). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7801}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7802}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7802} The nature of khullu: it is ownership of the usufruct, not of use, so its holder may let, give or lend as he likes; the Malikis distinguish that the owner of use uses the thing by its quality, whereas the owner of usufruct may transfer; khullu passes by inheritance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7803} Ways khullu arises on waqf property: by agreement of founder or administrator with the tenant; the Maliki cases include a crumbling waqf let to someone who repairs it, the mosque with shops whose rent cannot repair it, a barren waqf land on which the tenant builds, and shops whose use is sold before the endowment; Shaykh Ulaysh held that the third case is practised. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7803}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7804}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7804}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7805}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7805}
