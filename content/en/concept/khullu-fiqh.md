## Summary
Khullu is lexically emptiness; in later books it names the usufruct a tenant owns over a waqf property in return for paying the administrator funds to repair it, with a fixed share of its use and the rent paid for the rest for the beneficiaries. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7800} It also names the right of a tenant of state land to keep it if he has built, planted or filled it, and the cash paid for giving up that right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7800}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7801} Related terms: hukr (the rent on waqf land for one who built or planted), farigh and ifrigh (giving up a right for a price), jadak (movable goods of a tenant attached to a shop or not), kirdar (what a farmer builds or plants with leave), murassad (a debt fixed on the waqf by the tenant\'s repair), and mashd al-maska (the right to farm another\'s land in later Hanafi usage). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7801}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7802}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7802} The nature of khullu: it is ownership of the usufruct, not of use, so its holder may let, give or lend as he likes; the Malikis distinguish that the owner of use uses the thing by its quality, whereas the owner of usufruct may transfer; khullu passes by inheritance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7803} Ways khullu arises on waqf property: by agreement of founder or administrator with the tenant; the Maliki cases include a crumbling waqf let to someone who repairs it, the mosque with shops whose rent cannot repair it, a barren waqf land on which the tenant builds, and shops whose use is sold before the endowment; Shaykh Ulaysh held that the third case is practised. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7803}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7804}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7804}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7805}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7805} Sale of usufruct apart from the property: the Hanbalis allow selling part of a waqf to repair the rest when it cannot be let, the founder and the beneficiary being one; Buhuti holds that khullu cannot be created for money in every case; a person who takes a property from a previous tenant for money does not become owner of the khullu, as Ibn Abidin argues. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7806} Hanafis: khullu falls under the sale of bare rights; the Hanafi texts split on the validity of such sale on custom, with the Shahid and most texts against it; many muftis allowed it by custom; al-Hamawi leaned against it; al-Amadi made it binding by necessity, analogous to the sale of trust. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7806}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7807}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7807} Malikis: the fatwa of al-Laqani on ownership, inheritance and sale of khullu was followed; al-Hamawi says it has no text from Malik; al-Gharqawi says it is derived from the texts; on continuation by custom acts as a condition, and al-Dusuqi and al-Bannani base continuation on the public interest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7807}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7808}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7808}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7809} Rent: the rent for waqf property is the going rate; the Hanafis hold that a large sum paid to get a lower rent does not count, as it harms the waqf. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7809}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7810}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7810} Conditions for khullu by Maliki law: the sum paid must go to the waqf\'s needs, no income must already cover the repair, and the spending must be proved. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7810}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7811}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7811} The holder may sell, let, pledge, give, lend or bequeath; Buhuti holds bought khullu is held in common, so it may be sold, given or used for debt but not let. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7811}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7812}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7811}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7812} Pre-emption: where several tenants built on a lease-land and one sells his part of the building, the others have pre-emption; Abu al-Saud allows it in plantings, and Ibn Abidin calls it an error against the text. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7812} Gift or endowment of khullu: most later Malikis allow it, since part of the usufruct is waqf; the Shirwani, Hanbalis and al-Ajhuri forbid it, since a waqf cannot be made of a waqf; Ulaysh turned to permit it, and it was practised in Egypt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7812}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7813}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7813} Hanafis: a building apart from its land may not be endowed by many, though it may be by custom; Ibn Nujaym allowed the sale and endowment of buildings and plantings on leased land; a filling of earth may not be endowed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7813}
