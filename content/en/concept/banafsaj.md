## Nature and selection
Its root is well known and close in its actions to the rest of the plant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.386}. A modern herbal note in the footnote is not used in this entry.

## Temperament and actions
Cold and moist in the first degree. Some say it is hot in the first degree, but there is no doubt that its leaf is cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}. It is said to produce a moderate blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: it calms hot swellings as a poultice with barley gruel, and so does its leaf {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}. Wounds and ulcers: violet oil is a good liniment for scabies {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}. Head: it calms bloody headache by smell and by application {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}. Eyes: it helps hot inflammation of the eye, as a liniment and as a drink {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}. Chest: it helps hot cough and softens the chest, especially the form preserved with sugar. Its drink helps pleurisy and lung disease, and the book says it is better than julep for these {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}. Excretory organs: its drink helps pain of the kidneys and promotes urine. Its dried form purges yellow bile, and its drink softens the bowels gently and helps protrusion of the anus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.387}.
