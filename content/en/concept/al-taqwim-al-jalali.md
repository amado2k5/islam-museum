## Summary
The Jalali calendar is a Persian solar calendar reformed by a group of astronomers during the reign of Sultan Jalal al-Dawlah Malikshah ibn Alp Arslan al-Saljuqi, after whom it is also called "al-Malikshahi." Accounts of it appear in Jacobus Golius's commentary on the Persian month names in the book, not in al-Farghani's own text {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 276}.

## Reason for the Reform
Golius mentions, citing Qutb al-Din, that the reform took place at the Sultan's court, where a number of sages—among them 'Umar al-Khayyam—gathered and requested that the true solar year be adopted instead of the old Persian year, and that the beginning of the year be when the sun enters the sign of Aries, which is the Royal Nowruz {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 276}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 277}.

## The Leap Year
In the Jalali calendar, a leap year occurs every four years, except that the fifth year is made a leap year after seven or eight leap cycles, and it is noted that this cycle keeps the year close to the sun. Golius compares this rule with the Gregorian calendar, noting that three days are omitted every four hundred years {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 278}. The date of the reform itself does not appear in the text read, so no specific year is mentioned here.

## Months and Days
The months in the Jalali calendar are named after the old Persian months with "al-Jalali" added for distinction, corresponding to the old names with "al-Qadim" (the Old), such as "Farwardin al-Jalali" and "Farwardin al-Qadim" {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 276}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 277}. Some astronomers made the months uniformly thirty days long, adding the extra days to the end of Isfandarmudh {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 278}.
