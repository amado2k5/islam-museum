## Nature and selection
Nature: Dioscorides says zanjabil has small roots like those of sa'd, whitish in colour, with a taste like pepper and a pleasant smell, though it lacks pepper's subtlety; it is a root of a plant. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.455}
The book adds that one kind is called dog's ginger, which the people of Tabaristan call filfilak. It grows in pools, small springs and slow waters, with a knotted stem reaching the length of the knee, and leaves like mint but larger, whiter and softer, hot in taste and with a pleasant but not perfumed smell; its small fruits gather in bunches like a cluster. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
It is also said to be liable to spoil because of its superfluous moisture; its heating lasts longer than pepper's because it is dense. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
Temperament: hot at the end of the third degree and dry in the second; it has a superfluous moisture by which the book says it increases semen. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}

## Actions
Actions: its heat is strong, but it warms only after a time because of its superfluous moisture; its heating is strong and softening and dissolves flatulence. When steeped in honey it takes up some of its superfluous moisture and dries more. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}

## Uses by organ
Head organs: the book says it increases memory and clears moisture from the regions of the head. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
Eye organs: it clears darkness of the sight caused by moisture, as kohl and as a drink. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
Digestion: it aids digestion, suits the cold of the liver and the stomach, and dries the wetness of the stomach and the humours that arise there from eating fruit. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
Excretory organs: the book says it stirs desire and loosens the belly gently. Al-Khuzi says it binds; the book's author replies that it helps when the cause is poor digestion and a slipping of viscous humour. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
Poisons: it helps against the venom of vermin. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.456}
