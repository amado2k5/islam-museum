## Nature and temperament
Nature: a kind of kerkand. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.484}
Temperament: al-Khuzi says cold and moist; Masih, a physician the book names, says it is like asparagus in its actions, hot and moist in the first degree; another says hot in the first and moist in the second; it is attributed to Galen that it is hot at the end of the second. The author himself holds that its kinds are many and differ in nature. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}

## Actions
It cleanses a little and dries, and has fineness. Al-Khuzi says it breeds black bile, a view the book calls far off. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: its liniment helps the patch the book calls the fox's disease; its water kills lice when the head is washed, and removes armpit stench by bringing out foul urine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}
Swellings: dissolves swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}
Wounds: its water helps hard itching. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}
Organs of the head: its water removes scurf. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}
Organs of digestion: causes nausea, especially the wild kind, and especially its root and gum, which are the kerkand. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}
Excretory organs: increases sexual power, brings urine, brings foul urine out, loosens the bowels, brings out phlegm, and often binds the belly when drunk in wine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.485}
