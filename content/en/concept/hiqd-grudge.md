## Summary
Hiqd is a settled resentment: anger held back when one cannot yet take revenge, which turns inward and becomes enmity, suspicion or a wish for retaliation. It differs from envy (wishing a blessing removed from another) and from anger itself, of which it is the residue. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7172} Its ruling follows its motive: resentment born of envy and rancour without right is blameworthy, and the Qur'an censures hypocrites who bite their fingertips in rage at the believers' unity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7173} The saying 'the believer is not a grudge-holder' is traced by al-Iraqi to al-Fudayl ibn Iyad and not to the Prophet. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7173} Gifts and handshakes are named as remedies. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7174} Resentment toward an oppressor who cannot be stopped, or toward an unbeliever harming Muslims, is not blameworthy; the wronged may forgive or take his right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7174}
