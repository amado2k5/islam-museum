## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the third lesson, on symptoms and signs, by saying that the signs of any of the three states point to something present, past or future, and he quotes Galen on who benefits from each: the patient, the physician or both {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Pointing to the present
A sign of a present matter benefits the patient alone, in what he should do, as Ibn Sina reports in Galen's name {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Pointing to the past
A sign of a past matter benefits the physician alone, again in Galen's words as Ibn Sina gives them, because it shows how far he has advanced in his craft and so raises trust in his counsel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.

## Pointing to the future
A sign of a future matter benefits both: the physician reads in it his advance in knowledge, and the patient learns from it what his regimen requires {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.
