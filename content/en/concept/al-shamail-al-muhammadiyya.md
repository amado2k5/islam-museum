## Overview
The *Shama'il* is a branch of [[concept/al-hadith-al-nabawi|hadith]] that gathers what is reported about the appearance and character of the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]]. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}

## What the chapter gathers
It covers his hair, his gait, his speech, his food, his gatherings, his laughter, and his weeping: his physical and moral description as transmitted by his family and companions. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}

## Al-Tirmidhi's book
The best known of his works is the *Shama'il Muhammadiyya*, with which al-Tirmidhi concluded his *Jami'*. It came to be circulated and explained independently, until it became a discipline in its own right. {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi}

## Its origin
The core of the chapter is the description of Anas ibn Malik in al-Bukhari, who said that he was of medium height among the people, neither tall nor short, with a fair complexion that was neither dazzlingly white nor dark. He said that there were not twenty white hairs on his head and beard. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3547}

## For young visitors
Hadith scholars wrote books that describe what the Prophet looked like and how he behaved, so that Muslims could know his manners.
