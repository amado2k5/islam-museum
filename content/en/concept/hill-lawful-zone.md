## Summary
Hill lexically is what lies outside the sacred precinct, the counterpart of haram, and names a man who has left the state of ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7270} In the jurists' use the lawful is what the lawgiver permits; what the law leaves silent is pardoned, and things are presumed permitted before revelation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7270} The markers of the sanctuary (a'lam) set by Abraham mark the limit: inside them is haram, outside them is hill, where hunting is lawful for one not in ihram. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7270} For taking ihram for umra, the Hanafis and Hanbalis prefer al-Tan'im, while the Malikis and Shafiis prefer al-Ji'rana, then al-Tan'im, then al-Hudaybiya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7271} Hunting in the sanctuary is forbidden, and the majority look to the place of the game rather than the hunter. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7272} On Medina the majority forbid hunting there and require only seeking forgiveness, while the Shafiis in an old view require compensation through the hunter's spoils. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7272} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7273} The four sacred months were times of forbidden fighting before Islam; the postponement practice was abolished. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7273} Hill is also the state after leaving ihram, when what ihram forbade becomes lawful again. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7273}
