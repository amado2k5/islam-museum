## Summary
Al-Battani remarks that the observation is the more exact the nearer the Sun is to a solstitial point, whichever solstice it is, because the Sun's motion in declination is slow between the two observations, that is, the two shadow marks. He then states that the moment of midday is not strictly fixed, and that this method finds it or the time nearest to it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.39}.

## Why accuracy grows near a solstice
He says: the nearer the Sun is to a solstitial point, whichever solstice it is, the more exact the observation, because of the slowness of the Sun's motion in declination between the two observations, that is, the two shadow observations. This means that when the Sun is far from the solstices its declination changes between morning and afternoon, so the two points marked on the circle are not truly symmetrical; at a solstice that change is small. The explanation is the present reading of his statement, not his wording. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.39}.

## Midday is not strictly fixed
He says: it is known that the time of midday is not strictly fixed, but by this method the time of midday, or the time nearest to it, is found. The scan reads disorderly at the reason that follows, so it is not given. He then promises an example of the circle described, the figure on which the circle, the rod, the two marks of the shadow's tip and the meridian line are drawn. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.39}.
