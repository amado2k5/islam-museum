## Summary
Ibn Sina explains natural death: the innate heat dries the moistures until they are spent, so it becomes, incidentally, a cause of putting itself out, like a lamp that goes out when its fuel is used up; when the drying is complete the heat is extinguished and natural death follows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.

## Drying grows, heat shrinks
Once the body reaches the balanced degree of dryness and heat, the drying is no longer as before but stronger, because there is less material and so it yields more readily; the drying keeps increasing until the moistures are spent. As the drying grows, the heat declines, so the inability to replace the moisture that dissolves keeps growing, and the drying increases in two ways: less matter arriving, and the moisture itself shrinking as heat dissolves it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.

## The lamp
A lamp has two moistures, water and oil; it stands by one and is put out by the other. In the same way the innate heat stands by the innate moisture and is smothered by the foreign moisture, which rises from weak digestion and is like the lamp's watery moisture. When the drying is complete the heat goes out and natural death comes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.

## Why the body lasts
Ibn Sina says the body lasts as long as it does not because the first natural moisture withstood the dissolving by the heat of the world and of its own body, but because the body replaces what dissolves from it, and that replacement is food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.201}.
