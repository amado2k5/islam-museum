## The three things
If it is from a black-bile matter settled in the brain, the pillar of its treatment is three things: first, evacuating the matter, which may be by vomiting, except for one whose stomach is weak, whom you do not make vomit in this illness at all, not even in the hypochondriac kind; second, that moistening be joined to the evacuation throughout, lest the humour thicken through a bare dissolving with no softening in it, or thicken through what moistens with no dissolving in it; third, strengthening the heart: if a cold temperament is felt, with the hot gladdening remedies, if one leaning to heat, with the moderate ones, and if the heat is very intense, with cold gladdening remedies that are not excessively cold, which is known from the pulse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.107} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.108}

## Evacuation
On evacuation in detail Ibn Sina says that if the vessels are seen to be full and the black bile is bloody, he begins with evacuating blood, unless great weakness is feared or it is known that the matters are slight and in the brain only and dryness prevails over the temperament; if the humour is truly black and cold, its evacuation is by named medicines in stages, from ripening to the lighter and then the stronger; vomiting is also used when there is something in the stomach that adds to the illness and the stomach is not very weak; if the matter leans to bilious bile, it is evacuated by what belongs to bile, moistening is increased and warming reduced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.108} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.109}

## When the cause is in another organ
If the cause of melancholia is a swelling in the stomach and the viscera, or a hot, burning temperament in them, that is dealt with and the head is strengthened lest it receive what reaches it from elsewhere; if the cause is in the hypochondrium, the winds and rumblings or the hot swelling there are treated as due, by what is said in the chapter on swellings, and the spleen is strengthened lest it send what is in it to the brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.109} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.110}

## What benefits by itself
The melancholic often gain from cooling things insofar as they are moistening, opposed to the dryness of black bile, and keep back the generation of the winds and vapours that harm by rising to the head; the benefit of the cold is not a slight, disease-cutting benefit, but cold that is moist generates no black bile and its matter is cut off; what benefits by itself is the evacuation of black bile, and the rule of treating melancholia is to be thorough in moistening and, with that, not to fall short in evacuating the black bile; you are not to be misled by the benefit some of them get from phlegm they bring up or pass, for that is because the abundance and the pressing of humours on one another is lifted from them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.110}

## Spoiled food and occupation
Whenever food spoils in the bellies of the melancholic, they are to be brought to throw it up, especially when they feel a sourness in the mouth, and they are forbidden to eat other food on top of it; the melancholic must be occupied with something, whatever it is, and have someone he is shy before and finds agreeable beside him, and be occupied with listening, and nothing harms him more than idleness and solitude; they are often grieved by events that befall them or fear something, and occupy themselves with it away from the thought, and recover, for their very turning from the thought is a genuine treatment for them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.110}

## What is left out
In these places the book gives recipes, quantities, the names of drugs, plants and foods, the names of veins, methods of application and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and none of it is reproduced here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.108} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.109} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.110}

## Menses (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Ibn Sina says that if the cause is a flow held back, of menstruation, it is to be brought on, and he gives this in the treatment of melancholia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.110}
