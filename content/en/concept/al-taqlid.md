## Overview
Shaykh Shaltut rejects taqlid — inheriting beliefs and opinions from fathers and grandfathers without inquiry or proof — noting the Qur'an blamed it in many verses {cite:azhar-quran-building-society-shaltut p. 57}. Sticking to the ancients' opinions merely because they came earlier collides with the law of intellectual growth as with the growth of creatures, and robs man of his faculty of telling truth from falsehood {cite:azhar-quran-building-society-shaltut p. 58}{cite:azhar-quran-building-society-shaltut p. 59} — a crime, he calls it, against human nature.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
Taqlid is defined in The Oxford Dictionary of Islam as imitation — keeping to legal precedent, inherited conduct and received doctrines — and reformers often contrast it with ijtihad, independent reasoning from revelation. Although precedent has binding force in classical Islamic law, the entry says, modern reformers have made the word a term of abuse for cultural and intellectual stagnation and a refusal of new ideas, criticism of it flowing from fundamentalist and modernist camps alike. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2339}
