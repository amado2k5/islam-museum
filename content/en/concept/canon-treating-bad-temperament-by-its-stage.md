## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the second chapter, on treating the diseases of bad temperament, saying that when there is no matter only the temperament is changed, and when there is matter it is evacuated and the temperament changed afterwards if the bad state remains, and he divides treatment into three kinds according to whether the bad temperament is established, coming into being, or about to come {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.271-272}.

## With and without matter
Where there is no matter only the bad temperament is changed; where there is matter it is evacuated, and evacuation alone may be enough if the bad temperament does not remain because of its earlier hold, or it may not be enough if the bad temperament remains, and then the temperament must be changed after evacuation is finished {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.271}.

## The three kinds
The bad temperament is either established, treated by the contrary absolutely, which is treatment proper; or at the point of coming into being, mended by treatment together with taking precaution by stopping the cause; or about to come into being, needing only the cause to be stopped, which is called precaution beforehand. An example of treatment is treating the putrefaction of quartan fever with theriac and giving cold water in tertian fever to quench it. An example of the two together is evacuation in quartan fever with hellebore and in tertian with scammony when the aim is to prevent the start of an attack. An example of precaution alone is evacuating, with hellebore, a person ready for quartan fever through a surfeit of black bile, and with scammony one ready for tertian through yellow bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.271-272}.
