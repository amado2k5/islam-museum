## Summary
'Umariyya is the name of two cases in inheritance: a husband with both parents, or a wife with both parents, named after 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, who was the first to decide them {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11836}.

## The mother's share
The mother takes a third where the deceased has no child or grandchild and the parents alone inherit, and a sixth where there is a child or two or more siblings {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11836} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11837}.

## The ruling
'Umar and the majority held that the mother takes a third of what remains after the spouse's share; the estate is counted as six shares with a husband and twelve with a wife. Ibn 'Abbas, Zayd ibn Thabit and Abu Bakr al-Asamm differed {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11837} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11838}.
