## Summary
In lexical use hikma is the knowledge of things as they are and acting on it; in the Quran it is applied to prophethood. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7234} For the usulis hikma is the benefit that follows from tying a ruling to its cause or occasion (securing a good or removing or reducing a harm), and it also names the fitting quality that suits the ruling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7234} A cause (sabab) is an apparent, regular attribute that defines a ruling; an impediment (mani') is what, when present, excludes the cause or ruling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7234} The illa is the attribute the lawgiver made the criterion of a ruling; the hikma is the benefit itself, which may be hard to measure or hidden, so the usulis dispute whether a ruling may be tied to the hikma. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7235}
