## Definition
Alsagh is one who has a speech defect, a stammer that turns a letter into another, such as r into l or g, or s into th. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3315} Related terms are al-art, one who merges one letter into another, al-thaththa'a, one who repeats the taa, and al-fafa'a, one who repeats the fa. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3315}

## Rulings
- The defect is a fault in a prayer leader. The majority count the speaker with the illiterate in leading prayer, so a fluent person may not follow him, but he may lead others with the same defect. Some Malikis and the Hanbali judge al-Qadi hold his leading valid with dislike. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3315}
- The Shafi'is require, for a speech-defective person to lead another with the same defect, that the defect fall on one word only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3315}
- Some jurists forbid following one who swaps one letter for another. Ibn Taymiyya gives two views on swapping the dad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3315}
- In a compensation for injury that causes the defect, the diya is reckoned in proportion to the defect; if the injury prevents speech altogether, some jurists hold the full diya due. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3316}
- The defect may affect divorce, as when a man says "tali" for "talaq" by mistake. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3316}
