## Summary
Contentment with God's decree is the last subject [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]] treats in the Book of Love, Longing, Intimacy and Contentment, the sixth book of the quarter of saving things in [[work/ihya-ulum-al-din|Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din]]. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7703} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7952} He says he will set out its merit, then its reality and how it can exist in what goes against desire, then what is thought to belong to its perfection but does not, such as giving up supplication and keeping silent about sins. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7952}

## Its merit
Al-Ghazali cites the verses on contentment from both sides, and holds that the utmost of excellence is God's being pleased with His servant, which is the reward for the servant's being content with God. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7952} He observes that the Qur'an raised God's good pleasure above the Gardens of Eden, as it raised the remembrance of God above prayer, and concludes that the good pleasure of the Lord of paradise is higher than paradise and the final goal of its people. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7953} He quotes [[person/sahl-al-tustari|Sahl]]: the servants' share of certainty is in proportion to their share of contentment, and their share of contentment in proportion to their life with God. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7969}

## Its reality: how can one be content with what hurts?
Al-Ghazali says that whoever denies that contentment is possible in what goes against desire, holding that only patience is possible there, does so because he denies love. Once love of God and absorption in Him are established, love produces contentment with the Beloved's acts in two ways: {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7969}

- **The feeling of pain falls away:** like the warrior wounded in his anger or fear who does not feel it; the man whose heart is so occupied that the cupper or barber finishes without his noticing; and the lover absorbed in contemplating the beloved. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7969} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7970} He holds that love can grow many times over, as pain can, and that no beauty compares with the beauty of the divine presence. He relates that the wife of Fath al-Mawsili stumbled and broke a nail and laughed, saying the sweetness of its reward had removed the bitterness of its pain, and that Sahl used to treat others for an illness he had while not treating himself. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7971} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7972}
- **The pain is felt but accepted by reason while disliked by nature:** like one who asks the bloodletter to bleed or cup him, feels the pain, accepts it and is grateful; or the traveller seeking profit, for whom the hardship of the road is sweetened by love of its fruit. So one struck by affliction who is certain that its reward exceeds what he lost is content with it and thanks God for it. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7972} He allows that love may grow so strong that the lover's share is in what the Beloved wills and is pleased with, for no further aim. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7973}

## What contentment is not: supplication and rejecting sin
:::perspectives
- **Ahl al-Sunna: al-Ghazali in the Ihya' (the author's view).** Al-Ghazali has a chapter showing that supplication does not contradict contentment, and that hating sins, detesting those who commit them and working to remove them by enjoining good and forbidding wrong do not contradict it either. He says some "idle, deluded" people claimed that sins and unbelief are of God's decree and so one must be content with them, and calls this "ignorance of interpretation and heedlessness of the secrets of the law". {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7991} He argues that supplication is an act of worship; that the Prophet and the other prophets supplicated often; that God censured people content with the life of this world; from the report "whoever witnesses a wrong and approves of it is as if he had done it"; and from a saying attributed to [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]] that a servant may be absent from a wrong yet bear the same burden as its doer if he hears of it and approves. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7992} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7993} Al-Iraqi notes that the hadith "one who points to evil is like its doer" was transmitted by al-Daylami from Anas "with a very weak chain". {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7993}
- **Not given here:** the views of those al-Ghazali alludes to who held that giving up supplication is part of perfect contentment, and the academic view; not yet documented with a defined source. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7952}
:::

## Links to other themes
Al-Ghazali makes contentment a fruit of [[concept/hubb-allah|love of God]], pairs it in the same book with longing and [[concept/al-uns-bi-allah|intimacy with God]], and counts "the content one" among the states of [[concept/faqr|the poor man]] in the Book of Poverty and Renunciation. {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7930} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7969} {cite:ihya-shamela-dar-al-marifa p. 7186}

## For young visitors
Contentment means accepting what God decrees with a calm heart. Al-Ghazali says contentment does not mean giving up prayer for help, or keeping quiet about what is wrong.
