## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that Islam is a religion that keeps its covenants: one who takes refuge with a people bound by treaty to the Muslims is not to be attacked, nor one who comes to the Muslims with a heart too strained to fight them or his own people {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2533}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2534}.

## What Sha'rawi says
He explains that the verse makes an exception to the order to seize and kill for one who reaches a people bound by treaty with the Muslims, since the Muslims had given tribes covenants to protect whoever sought refuge with them {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2533}. He cites the covenant between the Prophet and Hilal ibn Uwaymir al-Aslami: neither side would help the other's enemy, and whoever reached Hilal had the same protection as Hilal {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2534}.

## One whose heart is strained
He reads the exception as covering one who comes to the Muslims admitting his weakness, unable to fight them or his own people, whom the Prophet accepts {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2534}. He holds that a hypocrite who migrates is received by the breadth of faith {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2533}. This is his view; the jurists' rules on covenants and protected status are not yet set out from a named source.
