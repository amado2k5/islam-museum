## A wind that entered from outside
As for headache from a wind that passed into the head from outside, consider whether the wind was hot, of summer, or cold, of winter, and then consider where it entered {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.64} If it was hot it is treated by what cools and dissolves gently, and if a hot ill-temperament follows, it is treated with gentleness, beginning with what is less cold and increasing if that does not help; if it was cold the treatment is warm and dissolving, and everything that puffs and loosens the bowels is avoided {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.64}

## Bad vapours from outside
The treatment of bad vapours that reach one from outside is the same: they are cold only in the lesser number of cases, such as the vapours of spoiled bathhouse places, and in most they are hot, and moderate means dissolve them; if much of them is held back and giddiness and vertigo are imagined, moderate pleasant smells are breathed, and if he feels severe heat, the cold among them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.64} For the cold ones warm pleasant smells help; if the vapours are smoky there is need of strong moistening; and if a fault and ill-temperament arise from them, as happens with the smoke of sulphur and the smoke of arsenic and the like, what moistens one and cools the other is used {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.64}

## Pleasant and foul smells
As for the one from pleasant smells, if they were hot and harmed by their heat and not by dryness alone, it is treated with cold pleasant smells; if they harmed by drying and dryness besides, the treatment is not to stop there but to make good with what is moist and cooling; and the reverse likewise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.65} As for the headache from foul smells, its treatment is by pleasant ones opposed to them in temperament, and if those smells dry, care is taken that the smells set against them are moistening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.65}

## Note
Recipes, quantities, the names of drugs and plants, the names of veins and the sites of venesection, cupping and cautery, and methods of application are not reproduced here. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.64-65}
