## Nature and selection
The ash of lead and anak; anak, when burned hard, becomes sirang and gains extra fineness. All the isfidajat are made with vinegar; they may also be made with salts and in many other ways {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}. A footnote describes it as a lead compound and says it is toxic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.369}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}.

## Uses by organ
The kind made with vinegar is very thinning and penetrating; the other kinds are less thinning; all irritate, especially sirang. Swellings: it softens cold and hard swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Wounds and ulcers: it enters salves and fills ulcers, grows flesh in them, and eats away bad flesh; sirang especially eats bad flesh and grows flesh strongly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Eyes: it helps pustules of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Excretory organs: it is among the remedies for fissure of the anus and helps greatly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. Poisons: it is a poison, and its treatment is described in the chapter on poisons {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}.
