## Nature
The origin of tutiya is a smoke that rises where lead and copper are freed from the stones they are mixed with; sometimes iqlimiya rises and what rises of it is good tutiya, and its deposit is iqlimiya called suqudiyun; tutiya is white, yellow, green, thin, thick, or tending to red, and all of these are made in the land of Kirman; the Indian is the washing of tutiya that gathers like dregs under the water that washes it, and that is suqudiyun; the difference between suqudiyun and tutiya is that tutiya rises and the other stays below. It is also said that in the sea there is a round animal, hard in its khadaj (the printed word, unclear), that dies in the sea and the waves cast it to the shore, and tutiya is made of it, and it is very subtle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Choice
The best is the white volatile, then the yellow, then the pistachio-coloured Kirmani, and the freshest of all is the finest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Temperament
Cold in the first degree, dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Actions and properties
It dries without stinging, and its washed form is the best of the dryers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Cosmetics
It benefits body odour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Wounds and ulcers
Its washed form benefits ulcers, even cancerous ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Ocular organs
It benefits eye pain and prevents the wicked superfluities held in the veins of the eye from passing into its tunics, especially the washed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}

## Excretory organs
It benefits ulcers of the stomach and of the male organs, and their swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.688}
