## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, with a superfluous moisture in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Actions and properties
It has a warming, astringent, restraining power, and is one of the finest of the eaten herbs in substance; if sprigs of it are left in milk the milk does not curdle, and its juice drunk with vinegar cuts the flow of blood from the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.577}.

## Swellings and pustules
With barley meal it is a poultice for abscesses, and it is not to be confused with pennyroyal, for pennyroyal has no astringency and has an excessive dissolving, warming and drying that harms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It is put as a poultice on the forehead for headache, especially with barley meal; it is rubbed on the roughness of the tongue and the roughness goes; its juice is mixed with water of qaratin and dropped into painful ears {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.

## Chest and breathing
It stops spitting and flow of blood and, as a poultice, sets the milk in the breast and calms its swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It strengthens and warms the stomach, calms hiccup, prevents phlegmatic and bloody vomiting and helps jaundice, especially its syrup. A word in this sentence is unclear in the copy and is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.

## Excretory organs
It firms the vessels of the semen and kills worms; sprigs of it drunk with pomegranate seeds calm cholera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It aids desire through an inflating quality from its garden moisture that pennyroyal lacks, and used as a pessary before intercourse it prevents conception {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.

## Poisons and bites
Helpful for the bite of a rabid dog, its seed especially {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.578}.
