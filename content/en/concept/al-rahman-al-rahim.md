## Summary
Al-Rahman and al-Rahim are two of God's names, appearing in the basmala and in the Fatiha. Sha'rawi holds that their meaning shifts with their place: in the basmala they recall forgiveness, and in the Fatiha they are joined to God's lordship over the worlds. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.52} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.54}

## In the basmala
Sha'rawi says the two names in the basmala recall mercy and forgiveness, so that one who has disobeyed is not ashamed to seek God's help, since God is approached through the door of mercy. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.52}

## In the Fatiha
In the Fatiha the names follow "Lord of the worlds", so the mercy of lordship reaches believer and unbeliever alike, all receive their favours in this world, the disobedient are given time, and the door of repentance stays open. Sha'rawi holds that God made His mercy precede His anger. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.54}

## The mercy of creation
Sha'rawi cites a hadith qudsi, which he attributes to Ahmad's Musnad, in which the sky, the seas and the mountains ask leave to punish the son of Adam for his disobedience, and God says to leave them, for perhaps they will repent; if they repent He is their beloved, and if not He is their physician. He uses this to argue that creation serves the human by God's subjection of it despite disobedience. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.66}

## Author's view
This is Sha'rawi's reading and is attributed to him. The grading of the hadith on p.66 has not been checked here, and no other view on the two names is given in the scan. The edition is unverified; page numbers are scan pages.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari begins with the form: "al-Rahman" is fa'lan from "rahima", and "al-Rahim" is fa'il from it. The Arabs often build names from the pattern "fa'ila yaf'alu" into fa'lan, as in ghadban from ghadib, sakran from sakir and atshan from atash, so rahman comes from rahima; al-Rahim takes the fa'il pattern although its base vowel differs, because it carries praise, and the Arabs shape names of praise or blame on fa'il. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 126}
Asked why two names of mercy are repeated when one could stand for the other, he answers that each word has a meaning the other does not carry, and that scholars of Arabic agree that rahman departs further from the pattern of fa'il than rahim, so that describing God by it signifies more. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 127}
From the reports he gives two accounts of the difference: one from al-Arzami, that rahman is for all creation and rahim for the believers; and a hadith from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that attributes to Jesus the words "rahman of this world and the next, rahim of the next". The editor grades both chains as weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 127}
Al-Tabari holds both senses sound and sees no reason to prefer one. Rahman is general: God's mercy in this world is provision, blessings, rain drawn by the clouds, plant growth and sound bodies and minds, which believer and unbeliever share, and in the next world it is His justice, in which no one is wronged by the weight of a mote. Rahim is particular: in this world it is the guidance that brings believers to obedience, and in the next the lasting bliss reserved for them. He cites the uncountable blessings of [[passage/quran-14-34|Ibrahim 34]] and God's mercy to the believers in [[passage/quran-33-43|al-Ahzab 43]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 128} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 129}
He then gives a report from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak, that rahman is tender toward whoever God wishes to show mercy, and rahim is gentle toward whoever is gentle to him. The editor notes that the wording may have been altered by a copyist or printer. Al-Tabari judges the view ascribed to the Prophet through al-Arzami closer to the meaning, while agreeing with Ibn Abbas that each name has a sense the other lacks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 129} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 130}
He reports from [[person/ata-al-khurasani|Ata al-Khurasani]] that rahman was among the names of God that no creature bears, and when Musaylima took it, God joined the two names, "al-Rahman al-Rahim", to set His name apart from the name of another. Al-Tabari finds this view not corrupt in meaning, and holds that God may reserve the pairing to Himself as a distinction from His creatures. The editor notes that Ata is trusted by some and weakened by others, and that the transmitter from him is unidentified. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 130} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 131}
Against the claim that the Arabs did not know the name rahman, he argues from the unbelievers' question "and what is rahman?" in [[passage/quran-25-60|al-Furqan 60]], and from the people of the Book who knew the Prophet as they know their sons [[passage/quran-2-146|al-Baqara 146]]: they denied what they recognised. He cites two lines of pre-Islamic verse containing the name; the editor notes the attribution of one is disputed and calls the claim of ignorance baseless. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 131}
He also records the view that the two names are synonyms, rahman meaning "possessor of mercy" and rahim "the one who shows mercy", citing parallel word pairs like nadman and nadim. He objects that this view separates the two meanings and then merges them again, which undoes its own distinction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 132}
