## Definition
Inkar is lexically ignorance of a thing, negation, and changing a wrong; for the jurists it means denial (jahd) and changing what is wrong. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3547}

## Related terms
- Jahd and juhud: the opposite of acknowledgement; some say juhud is denial with knowledge. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3548}
- Nafy: negation, the opposite of affirmation; all denial is negation, but not all negation is denial. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3548}
- Nukul: refusal to swear. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3548}
- Ruju': retracting after one has acted. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3548}
- Istinkar: counting a thing as wrong, or asking about what one denies. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3548}

## How denial is made
- By clear speech that admits only denial, such as "I have not been lent what you claim". Words such as "you have no right over me" are disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3549}
- By refusing both to affirm and deny: the two disciples of Abu Hanifa hold it denial, while the Hanbalis and some Malikis hold it the same as refusal. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3549}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3550}
- By silence: Abu Yusuf, the fatwa among the Hanafis, and the Shafi'is treat it as denial; the Malikis and Hanbalis treat it as refusal, and some Hanbalis hold the judge may detain the person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3550}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3551}

## Proof and oath
- The proof lies on the claimant and the oath on the one who denies, on the hadith that this is the way of judgement in general. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3553}
- If the claimant brings proof, he wins. If the denier takes the oath and clears himself, the claim fails; if he refuses, the Hanafis and Hanbalis judge against him, while the Malikis and Shafi'is do not judge against him unless the oath is returned to the claimant. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3553}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3554}
- The Malikis require two conditions for compelling a denier to swear: a dealing between the parties, and in claims of wrongful taking a known reputation for the like. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3554}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3555}
- Rights of God: the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is compel the oath when there is suspicion, while the Hanbalis do not compel it in rights of God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3555}
- Rights of people: the jurists agree on the oath in property, and differ in other matters; for a trustee there are three views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3555}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3556}
- Marriage, return to a wife, and the like: Abu Hanifa does not compel the oath; Abu Yusuf and Muhammad do. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3556}

## Denial of what is true
A defendant may deny a claim if he owes nothing and is in the wrong; he may not deny a claim he knows to be just. The Hanafis allow two exceptions: a claim of an old defect in goods, and an executor's denial of the dead man's debt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3557}

## Depositary who denies
If a depositary denies a deposit after its owner asks for it, he becomes liable for it, and is liable if it perishes after his denial. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3557}

## Retraction of an acknowledgement
- An acknowledgement of a right of God, such as fornication: the Hanafis and Hanbalis allow retraction; the Shafi'is have views; Ashhab of the Malikis allows retraction only for an excuse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3556}
- An acknowledgement of a right of people cannot be retracted; a person who confesses theft and then retracts, owes the property but no hand is cut. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3556}

## Denial of a sale or marriage
Denial of a binding contract does not dissolve it unless the other party accepts the dissolution, and denial of marriage is neither a divorce nor a dissolution, by the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3557}

## Denial of apostasy
For the Hanafis, a denial of an apostasy proved by evidence counts as repentance, and he is not pursued; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis judge him an apostate and require him to repent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3558}

## Compromise with denial
The majority, the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis allow it, and al-Shafi'i forbids it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3558}

## Denial of matters of religion
A Muslim may not deny a matter of religion; but one is not declared an unbeliever for denying it unless the matter is agreed upon and known to all Muslims as part of religion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3558}
