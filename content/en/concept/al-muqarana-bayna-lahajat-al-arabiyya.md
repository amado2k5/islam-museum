## What this record is
This record is a side-by-side comparison of Modern Standard Arabic and six Arabic dialects in four basic expressions: asking how someone is, asking what someone wants, and the numbers two and three. It condenses the sixty expressions and words that a research summary supplied to the museum gathered, whose detail is in the pages of the six dialects: [[concept/lahjat-misr|Egyptian]], [[concept/lahjat-al-sham|Levantine]], [[concept/lahjat-al-khalij|Gulf]], [[concept/lahjat-al-hijaz|Hijazi]], [[concept/lahjat-al-maghrib|Maghrebi]] and [[concept/lahjat-al-yaman|Yemeni]]. For the general frame see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Standard Arabic and the spoken dialects today]] and [[concept/al-izdiwajiyya-al-lughawiyya-fi-al-arabiyya|Diglossia in Arabic]].

## Background: six dialects, one reference variety for each
The summary chooses for each dialect group one reference variety, because a single dialect varies much within itself:

| Dialect | Arabic name | Reference variety |
|---|---|---|
| Egyptian Arabic | اللهجة المصرية | Cairene Arabic |
| Levantine Arabic | اللهجة الشامية | urban Levantine Arabic |
| Gulf Arabic | اللهجة الخليجية | Kuwaiti and Gulf Arabic |
| Hijazi Arabic | اللهجة الحجازية | urban Hijazi Arabic, associated with Jeddah, Mecca and Medina |
| Moroccan Arabic (Darija) | الدارجة المغربية | Moroccan Darija |
| Yemeni Arabic | اللهجة اليمنية | Yemeni Arabic, with regional qualifications |

The summary says questions are generally addressed to one male speaker, that transliteration is normalized for comparison and Arabic spelling may be normalized where a source uses phonetic transcription, and that the MSA equivalents are supplied for comparison and are not necessarily quotations from the dialect sources.

## The comparison table
The differences become particularly apparent in these four expressions:

| Dialect | How are you? | What do you want? | Two | Three |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MSA (Modern Standard Arabic) | كيف حالك؟ | ماذا تريد؟ | اثنان | ثلاثة |
| Egyptian Arabic | إزيّك؟ | عاوز إيه؟ | اتنين | تلاتة |
| Levantine Arabic | كيفك؟ | شو بدّك؟ | تنين | تلاتة |
| Gulf Arabic | شلونك؟ | شنو تبي؟ | إثنين | ثلاثة |
| Hijazi Arabic | كيف حالك؟ | إيش تبغى؟ | اثنين | ثلاثة |
| Moroccan Arabic (Darija) | كيف داير؟ | شنو بغيتي؟ | جوج | تلاتة |
| Yemeni Arabic | كيف حالك؟ | إيش تشتي؟ | اثنين | ثلاثة |

## Reading the table
For "how are you?", MSA, Hijazi and Yemeni coincide in the table, while Egyptian has its own izzayyak, Levantine kifak, Gulf shlonak and Moroccan kif dayr. For "what do you want?" the forms vary: ʿāwiz ēh, shū biddak, shinu tibī, ēsh tibgha, shnu bghīti and ēsh tishtī. For "two" the table has itnēn, tnēn, ithnēn and ithnayn, with Moroccan alone having jūj. For "three" Egyptian, Levantine and Moroccan are pronounced talāta with t, while Gulf, Hijazi and Yemeni keep the th in the spelling. The summary warns, however, that Arabic spelling does not always reflect every phonetic difference: the FSI textbook writes the Hijazi ثلاثة and transcribes it talāta; and it says many Gulf varieties keep the interdental pronunciation of ث. The detail is in the dialect pages.

## What this page does not reach
The museum opened none of the pages named and did not read the specialist books, so everything above is attributed to the summary. What is said of one dialect is not ascribed to another: Moroccan here is Moroccan Darija alone and not all Maghrebi dialects, and the Yemeni examples are not universal across Yemeni varieties. The summary gave no speaker numbers in this section and no evidence from the heritage. The page does not decide whether these varieties are to be called dialects or languages.

## Where this page comes from
