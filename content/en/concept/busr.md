## Nature and selection
Busr is the date before it softens; balah is the palm fruit called busr while it is soft, called balah while it is green, and rutab when it ripens {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.393}. Both digest slowly; the soft kind digests less. Their food value is small {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}.

## Temperament and actions
Both are cold and dry in the second degree, and the busr is more astringent than the qasb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}. Both cause wind, especially when water is drunk after them; they produce bowel rumbling and obstruction, and much of them produces thick humours in the body. Boiled busr calms the flame while keeping the innate heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}.

## Uses by organ
Head: busr causes headache and its excess quiets, and both are good for the gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}. Chest: both are harmful to the chest and lungs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}. Food organs: both tighten the stomach and cause obstruction of the liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}. Excretory organs: each binds the belly, especially when mixed with vinegar or astringent wine. Balah promotes urine, and drunk with astringent vinegar it stops discharge from the womb and bleeding piles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}. Fevers: frequent use leads to rigor and shivering {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.394}.
