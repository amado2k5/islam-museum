## Nature and selection
Two kinds, small and large. Dioscorides said it is called many-ribbed and seven-ribbed, the large one's leaf larger and the small one's smaller; its substance is composed of watery and earthy parts, by the watery it cools and by the earthy it binds. The most useful is the larger; the fruit and the root are close in nature to the leaf but drier and less cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Temperament
Its root is drier and less moist; its cold falls short of numbing and its dryness short of stinging, so it is the best for ulcers, being fine, especially when dried. Galen said it is cold and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Actions and properties
Its leaf is astringent and repelling through the cold watery part in it; it stops the flow of blood and dries it, without stinging, and so helps old and fresh boils, and nothing is better. It has a measure of opening from a cleansing in it, and its root is hung on the neck of one with scrofula {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Swellings and pustules
Good for hot swellings, burns, the ant-sore, hives, erysipelas, swellings at the base of the ear, and scrofula {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Good for malignant ulcers, the spreading Persian fire, chronic ulcers and deep wounds, and foremost in these matters; with cimolia and white lead it helps when put on erysipelas {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Good for earache from heat; a decoction of its root as a rinse is for toothache; a lentil dish with plantain in place of chard helps epilepsy. The juice of its leaf dropped in the ear quiets pain, its root chewed and the juice rinsed in the mouth quiets toothache, and its leaf water cures thrush {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Eyes
It helps ophthalmia, and eye salves are dissolved in its juice and so help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Chest and breathing
Its seed is for spitting blood, and a lentil dish into which it is put in place of chard helps asthma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.542}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its root, seed and leaf are used in treating obstructions of the liver and kidneys, and a lentil dish with it in place of chard helps dropsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Excretory organs
Good for ulcers of the intestines and bilious diarrhoea, its seed drunk and its juice as an enema; it stops the bleeding of piles, and its leaf is drunk in boiled wine for pain of the bladder and kidneys {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Fevers
It is said to help the tertian fever, that is the ghibb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.

## Poisons and bites
It is laid with salt on the bite of a rabid dog {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.543}.
