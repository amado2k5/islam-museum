## Summary
The taklifi rulings (al-ahkam al-taklifiyya) are the five descriptions that the scholars of legal theory and jurisprudence apply to every act of a person under religious responsibility: the obligatory, the recommended, the permitted, the disliked and the forbidden. The Rulings Browser gathers what the museum's sources say about these degrees and arranges them on a single scale, from the act that is required decisively to the act whose avoidance is required decisively. The museum describes what the texts say and what the scholars say, and issues no ruling.

## al-Ghazali's fivefold division
In al-Mustasfa, al-Ghazali says that the established rulings on the acts of those under responsibility fall into five divisions: the obligatory, the forbidden, the permitted, the recommended and the disliked. He explains the basis of the division: the address of the Lawgiver either comes requiring an act, or requiring an abstention, or giving a choice between acting and abstaining. If it comes requiring an act, it is a command: if it is joined to a warning of punishment for omitting it, it is the obligatory (wajib); if not, it is the recommended (nadb). If it comes requiring abstention: if it warns of punishment for doing the act, it is prohibition (hazr); otherwise it is dislike (karaha). And what comes as a choice is permission (ibaha) {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.297}. This is the division that the scale at the head of the browser draws.

## The definition of the obligatory and its synonym
In Irshad al-Fuhul, al-Shawkani defines the obligatory in technical usage as that for which the doer is praised and the one who leaves it is blamed, in certain respects. He says that this qualification brings in the obligation with a choice and the obligation on the community, and that the obligatory is divided into the specified and the optional, the narrow and the extended, and the individual and the communal; and that the majority treat the fard as its synonym {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.8}. In al-Burhan, al-Juwayni reports other definitions of the obligatory and criticises them, among them that it is what God threatened with punishment for leaving {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p.156}. The Hanafis distinguish between [[concept/fard|the obligation (fard)]] and the necessary (wajib), as recorded in the entry on [[concept/wajib-fiqh|the necessary (wajib)]].

## The disliked and its meanings
Al-Ghazali notes that the word "disliked" (makruh) is shared in the usage of the jurists among several meanings. One is the forbidden: al-Shafi'i often says "and I dislike this" while meaning prohibition. The second is what is forbidden with a prohibition of refinement, which signals that leaving it is better than doing it even though there is no punishment, just as the recommended is what signals that doing it is better than leaving it. The third is leaving what is preferable even though it was not forbidden, such as leaving the forenoon prayer {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.303}. Al-Juwayni says that the scholars were unsettled over the meaning of the disliked, and he reports their views and criticises them {cite:burhan-juwayni-mostafa p.157}. The details are in the entries on [[concept/makruh|the disliked (makruh)]] and [[concept/karaha|dislike (karaha)]].

## The individual obligation and the communal obligation
The scholars of legal theory divide the obligatory according to whom it is required of, into [[concept/fard-ayn|the individual obligation (fard 'ayn)]] and [[concept/fard-kifaya|the communal obligation (fard kifaya)]]. The earliest exposition of it among the museum's sources is the discourse of al-Shafi'i in al-Risala, where he makes of knowledge a degree that the general public does not reach and that not every one of the specialists is charged with: if those who suffice take it up, the others who leave it are not held in sin {cite:risala-shafii p.475}. Al-Ghazali discusses how the communal obligation can be on everyone while it lapses through the act of one {cite:mustasfa-hafiz-v1 p.309}.

## How the texts are gathered
The verses of the Qur'an are taken from a table of references that names the verses whose wording carries one of these sections; the text of each verse is in its own entry, from the Tanzil text, and is not written here. The other texts, from the hadith of the two Sahihs, the Sunan and the Muwatta, and from the books of legal theory, jurisprudence, commentaries, biographies and tabaqat, are picked up when the site is built by a guarded lexicon of terms, so every book harvested later enters from the day its passages arrive. A bare prohibition is not classified, because whether it is forbidding or disliking is a jurist's reading; so a hadith of prohibition enters only if its wording names the degree.
