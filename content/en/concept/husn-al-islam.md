## Overview
Among the goodness of a person's Islam, as in the twelfth hadith of the Forty, is leaving what does not concern him of speech and deed. Ibn Rajab explains it on pages 268 to 271. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.268-271}

## The meaning of "concerns him"
Ibn Rajab explains that "concerns him" means what his care is attached to and is among his aims. Leaving it is by the ruling of the Law, not by the ruling of desire and the soul's pursuit. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.269-270}

## What leaving includes
In Ibn Rajab's view, the goodness of Islam requires leaving forbidden things, doubtful things, reprehensible things, and the excess of permissible things that one does not need. This reaches the level of [[concept/ihsan|excellence]], and from watchfulness and excellence comes modesty before God. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.270-271}

## Four hadiths
He calls it a great root of the principles of good manners. He relates from Abu Amr ibn al-Salah, from Abu Muhammad ibn Abi Zayd, that the branches of good manners spring from four hadiths: "Whoever believes in God and the Last Day, let him say what is good or keep silent"; this hadith; "Do not become angry"; and "the believer loves for his brother what he loves for himself." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.270}
