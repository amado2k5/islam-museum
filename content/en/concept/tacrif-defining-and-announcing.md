## Overview
Ta'rif is making known and clear; the usulis use it for a definition that includes and excludes, and the jurists in the lexical sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5415}

## The gathering on the day of Arafat in towns
Ibn Abbas and Amr ibn Huraith did it in Basra; Ahmad allowed it without recommending it, while Abu Hanifa and Malik disliked it and some called it an innovation. Raising the voice excessively in mosques on that day, or in other kinds of recitation, is disliked. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5416} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5417}

## Announcing a found thing
Announcing a found thing is obligatory for the three imams, the sounder view for the Shafiis, whether the finder means to own it or keep it; one Shafii view makes it unnecessary when the intention is only to keep it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5417}

## Identifying the claimed thing and the defendant
Identifying the claimed thing and the defendant is a condition of hearing a claim by agreement, since the purpose of a claim is to bind to a right and a right cannot be enforced on something unknown. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5417}

## Reference
The detail is in the entries on the found thing (luqata) and on claim (da'wa). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5417}
