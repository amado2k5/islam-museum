## Summary
This page sets out the place of [[language/arabic|Arabic]] in [[place/comoros|Comoros]] as it appears in the Encyclopaedia Britannica article on Comoros and in the Comoros page of The World Factbook. It ascribes each statement to its page and estimates no number of speakers.

## Official status
The encyclopedia says the official and national languages of Comoros are Comorian, which it calls Shikomor, French and Arabic {cite:britannica-comoros}. The Factbook says: Arabic (official), French (official), Shikomoro (official; similar to Swahili), Comorian {cite:cia-factbook-comoros}. The two sources agree that Arabic, French and Comorian are official. The encyclopedia does not list Comoros in its article on the Semitic languages among the places where Arabic is official, although the League article lists it as a member since 1993; the two pages do not explain the difference.

## The standard and the spoken
The Factbook names Comorian twice in its list, once as Shikomoro with a note that it is official and similar to Swahili and once as Comorian; nothing was read that explains the repetition, and the page does not assume they are one language or two. The encyclopedia says most of the islands' inhabitants speak island-specific varieties of Comorian, a Bantu language related to [[language/swahili|Swahili]] and written in Arabic script, and that French is the language of administration {cite:britannica-comoros}. The two pages say nothing on a spoken Arabic in the islands or on the difference between the standard and the colloquial; for the duality in general see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Fusha and the dialects]].

## Other languages in the country
The encyclopedia names one dialect of Comorian, Shingazidja, spoken on Grande Comore and appearing in the government-sponsored weekly newspaper Al Watwan {cite:britannica-comoros}. The Factbook names the ethnic groups as Antalote, Cafre, Makoa, Oimatsaha and Sakalava {cite:cia-factbook-comoros}; these are the Factbook's names as printed and carry no shares. The encyclopedia says the islanders reflect a diversity of origins, with Malay immigrants and Arab and Persian traders mixed with peoples from Madagascar and various African peoples. Compare [[concept/lughat-al-qabail|the languages of the tribes]].

## From the history of the language
The encyclopedia says the culture of the islands combines African, Arabic, Malagasy and French influences, that Islam is the state religion and that most Comorians are Sunni Muslims {cite:britannica-comoros}. The writing of Comorian in Arabic script is what the encyclopedia mentions as the visible mark of Arabic on the language. For the islands' place in the Arab world see [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|the Arab states and the League]], which records the islands joining the League in 1993.
