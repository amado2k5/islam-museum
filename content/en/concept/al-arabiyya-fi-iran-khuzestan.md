## Summary
This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum says about Arabic in Khuzestan, Iran: its legal status, the varieties spoken and the official figures, each as the summary gives it. Local spoken Arabic has no official administrative status there, while Article 16, the summary says, requires the teaching of Qur'anic Arabic in secondary schools. The museum did not open the pages the summary names, and the founder approved using it as supplied on 21 November 2026. Where the summary says that no official source or figure was found, this page says exactly that and supplies no number of its own.

## What this page is
This page is an article on the subject "Arabic in Khuzestan, Iran", in the world chapter of the Arabic browser, one of the pages that treat Arabic outside the states counted as Arab. It answers three questions: what is the legal status of Arabic there, which varieties of it are spoken, and what official figures have been published on speakers, if any. The museum keeps here to what it keeps to on all its pages: a describing voice, not a commanding one, every statement ascribed to its page, and no verdict on a disputed question. All the material comes from one research summary, so the page describes what the summary said and does not claim to have read the sources.

## Background and dates
The summary's date here is the 1989 constitution of Iran, with its Articles 15 and 16; it does not tell the history of Arabic in Khuzestan. That history does have a link in the museum's pages: the pages on the dīwāns date the change of administration from Middle Persian to Arabic in Iraq in 697 and in Khurasan in 741-742, see [[concept/taarib-al-dawawin|The Arabisation of the dīwāns]]. The museum does not say that this is the origin of the Arabic of Khuzestan, since the summary does not say so.

## Legal status
According to the summary, Article 15 of the Constitution of Iran makes Persian the sole official language and permits regional and ethnic languages in the press, the mass media and the teaching of literature alongside Persian, applied in practice only in a limited way {cite:constituteproject-iran-1989} {cite:learnpersianonline-languages-of-iran}. Article 16 requires the teaching of Classical or Qur'anic Arabic in secondary schools {cite:constituteproject-iran-1989}. Local spoken Arabic has no official administrative status. This is the summary's statement; the museum did not read the text of the two articles.

## Varieties spoken
The summary names Khuzestani Arabic, a variety of South Mesopotamian (gələt) Arabic spoken across Ahwaz, Abadan, Khorramshahr, Shadegan, Howeyzeh, Bostan and Susangerd, with heavy contact with Persian and Bakhtiari Lori {cite:jipa-khuzestani-arabic} {cite:brill-book-9789004510241-khuzestan}. For the parent group in the western neighbour see [[concept/al-arabiyya-fi-iraq|Arabic in Iraq]]; for the places see [[place/khuzestan-province|Khuzestan]] and [[place/al-ahwaz|Ahwaz]].

## Official figures
The summary found no official government census and no UN or UNESCO count of speakers. It explains this by saying that the Statistical Center of Iran does not publish language breakdowns {cite:fanack-population-of-iran}, a statement of the page as the summary relayed it. The museum therefore gives no count and no share for speakers of Arabic in Khuzestan and takes no number from any other source.

## How the sources differ
The pages named for this record are of different kinds. Official texts and statistics: Constitution of Iran (1989) {cite:constituteproject-iran-1989}; Reference, academic and press pages: Languages of Iran {cite:learnpersianonline-languages-of-iran}, Khuzestani Arabic {cite:jipa-khuzestani-arabic}, Brill book page 9789004510241 (named for Khuzestani Arabic) {cite:brill-book-9789004510241-khuzestan} and Population of Iran {cite:fanack-population-of-iran}. The official texts and statistics speak for the state or the statistical office; the reference, academic and press pages describe and interpret; Wikipedia supports only names, places and dates. Where the kinds differ, the museum reports each in its own voice without choosing between them, and it opened none of them.
