## Summary
Opening chapter 7, on the management of what is eaten, Ibn Sina says whoever guards his health should not make medicinal foods such as pulses and fruit the substance of his diet; he prefers kid, young calves, lambs, cleaned wheat and a sweet suited to the temperament, and names figs, grapes and dates as the fruits closest to real food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.227-228}.

## Medicinal foods
The guardian of health strives not to make the substance of his food of the foods that act like medicines, such as pulses and fruit, because the fine ones burn the blood and the coarse ones breed phlegm and weigh the body down {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.227}.

## The fitting diet
His food should rather be meat, above all kid, young calves and lambs; wheat cleaned of impurities from a sound crop that no blight touched; a sweet suited to the temperament; and a good aromatic drink. Nothing else is to be heeded except as treatment or as forethought in preserving health {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.227}.

## Fruit
The fruits most like food are figs, sound ripe grapes that are very sweet, and dates in the lands where they are customary. If a surplus arises from them, he says, one should hasten to evacuate it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.227-228}.
