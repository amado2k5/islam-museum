## Summary
In the anatomy of the skull Ibn Sina distinguishes three true sutures and two false ones, and names each by its shape {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.44}.

## The true sutures
The first is the one shared with the forehead, arched in shape, called the coronal. The second divides the head lengthwise in a straight line and is called the sagittal; seen from where it joins the coronal it is called the skewer-like, since it is like an arch with a straight line standing in its middle. The third is shared between the back of the head and its base, shaped like an angle whose point meets the end of the sagittal, and is called the lambdoid because it looks like the letter lambda in the writing of the Greeks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.44}.

## The two false sutures
The two false sutures run along the head parallel to the sagittal on either side and do not sink fully into the bone, so they are called the scaly sutures {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.44}. The editor gives the Latin or English term in the margin for each Arabic name {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.44}.
