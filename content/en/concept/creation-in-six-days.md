## What the verses say

The Qur'an says repeatedly that God created the heavens and the earth in six days, and in several places that He then settled on the Throne: [[passage/quran-7-54|al-A'raf 54]], [[passage/quran-10-3|Yunus 3]], [[passage/quran-25-59|al-Furqan 59]], [[passage/quran-32-4|al-Sajda 4]]. [[passage/quran-11-7|Hud 7]] adds that His Throne was upon the water before that. Surat Fussilat gives the division: the earth in two days [[passage/quran-41-9|Fussilat 9]]; the mountains set in it and its provisions measured in four days in all [[passage/quran-41-10|Fussilat 10]]; the seven heavens completed in two days [[passage/quran-41-12|Fussilat 12]]; between them comes the mention of smoke [[passage/quran-41-11|Fussilat 11]], treated on the page [[concept/smoke-rending-and-expansion|Smoke, rending and expansion]].

## What is meant by a day?

Ibn Kathir says people differed over whether each of these days is like the days that first come to mind, or each is like a thousand years, as Mujahid and Ahmad ibn Hanbal stated, and as is related from al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:54}. Al-Qurtubi notes that a day in the language runs from sunrise to sunset and that there is no day without a sun; he then reports one view that these are days of the hereafter, each a thousand years, to magnify the creation of the heavens and earth, and another that they are days of this world, the first Sunday and the last Friday, from Mujahid and others. On the wisdom of the gradual pace he says God could have said "be" and it would be, but wished to teach His servants gentleness and deliberation and to show His power to the angels step by step {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 7:54}. Ibn Kathir assigns the days at the Fussilat verses thus: the earth in two days, Sunday and Monday, then the heavens in two further days, Thursday and Friday, and says nothing was created on Saturday because it is the seventh day {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:54} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 41:12}.

Ibn Kathir also relays Ibn Abbas's answer, as al-Bukhari has it from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, to someone who found a clash between the earth being created before the heaven in Fussilat and the spreading of the earth coming after it in Surat al-Nazi'at: the earth was created in two days, then the heaven, then the earth was spread by bringing out its water, pasture and mountains in two further days {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 41:9}.

## The hadith on the days in Muslim, and what was said of it

Muslim relates from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet took him by the hand and said that God created the clay on Saturday {cite:sahih-muslim 2789}; it continues with the mountains on Sunday, trees on Monday, what entails toil on Tuesday, light on Wednesday, the spreading of animals on Thursday, and Adam after the afternoon of Friday. Ibn Kathir observes that this covers seven days where the Qur'an says six, and says that al-Bukhari and more than one of the hadith masters spoke against it and made it Abu Hurayra's transmission from [[person/kab-al-ahbar|Ka'b al-Ahbar]] rather than a statement of the Prophet; elsewhere he says al-Bukhari gave it a defect in al-Tarikh al-Kabir, saying some said it is from Ka'b and that this is sounder {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:54} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 32:4}. Against that, Muslim included it in his Sahih, and al-Nasa'i has a close version in which God settled on the Throne on the seventh day {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 32:4}.

Ibn Kathir also relays from Ibn Jarir a report from Ibn Abbas in which the Jews asked the Prophet about the creation of the heavens and earth and he answered with a different apportioning: the earth on Sunday and Monday, the mountains on Tuesday, trees, water and habitation on Wednesday, the heaven on Thursday, and on Friday the stars, sun, moon and angels; the Jews then said that He rested, and the Prophet became very angry {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 41:12}. Only Ibn Kathir's text up to that point was read here; the chain and scholars' assessment of this report were not checked.

## The Throne upon the water

Al-Bukhari has it from Imran ibn Husayn that people from Yemen asked the Prophet about the beginning of this matter and he said: "God was, and nothing else was", His Throne was on the water, He wrote everything in the Dhikr, and then created the heavens and earth {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 3191}. Ibn Kathir gives Ahmad's version, which says He wrote it in the [[concept/al-lawh-al-mahfuz|Preserved Tablet]], Muslim's report from Abdullah ibn Amr that God decreed the measures of creatures fifty thousand years before creating the heavens and earth while His Throne was on the water, and the hadith of Abu Razin asking where our Lord was before creating His creation, of which Ibn Kathir says al-Tirmidhi called it hasan {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 11:7}. Among early sayings he has Mujahid, Wahb ibn Munabbih and Qatada that the Throne was on the water before anything was created; from al-Rabi' ibn Anas that when the heavens and earth were created the water was divided in two, half under the Throne, which he identifies with the sea spoken of in Surat al-Tur; and from Ibn Abbas that the Throne is called arsh because of its height {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 11:7}. The museum has pages on [[concept/al-arsh|the Throne]] and [[concept/istiwa-ala-al-arsh|settling on the Throne]].

:::perspectives
**First Sunni position: the hadith is the Prophet's.** Muslim recorded the seven-day hadith in his Sahih from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet, and al-Nasa'i has it in a close form {cite:sahih-muslim 2789} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 32:4}.

**Second Sunni position: the hadith is Ka'b's.** Al-Bukhari and others of the hadith masters made it Abu Hurayra's report from Ka'b al-Ahbar rather than the Prophet's; Ibn Kathir's reason is that it covers seven days while the Qur'an says six {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:54} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 32:4}.

**On the length of a day.** Days like ours; each a thousand years; or days of the hereafter said for magnification {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:54} {cite:quran-com-tafsir-api al-Qurtubi on 7:54}.
:::

## What the sources do not say

The verses do not state the length of these days, and the commentators do not agree on the order of everything within them; the sources read here give no estimate in years for the age of the heavens and earth. This page is not a link between the verses and modern scientific estimates of the age of the universe; for what has been said on that, see [[concept/smoke-rending-and-expansion|Smoke, rending and expansion]]. Ibn Kathir himself, at the settling on the Throne, notes that people have said very many things and that he follows the way of the early generations, letting it pass as it came {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir on 7:54}.
