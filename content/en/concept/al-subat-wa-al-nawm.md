## Definition
Coma (subat) is said of excessive heavy sleep, not of every excessive heavy one, but of that whose heaviness is in both length and kind, so that its length is longer and its form stronger and waking from it hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89} Of sleep, some is natural in its measure and kind, some is heavy, and some is sunk-in coma {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89} Sleep, in sum, is the return of the psychic pneuma from the instruments of sense and movement to an origin, with which the instruments are idle from returning in act within them, save what is needed for the staying of life, as in the instruments of breathing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89}

## Natural sleep
Natural sleep absolutely is that whose return comes with the sinking of the vital pneuma inward to ripen the food, the psychic pneuma following it, and that too which is for rest, so that the pneuma may gather to itself while it is nourished and grows and its substance increases and it gains the replacement of what dissolved from it in wakefulness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.89} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90} Close to this is what comes to one who is nearing the turn of his disease for the better, for deep sleep comes to him and shows the calming of his disease, though in the healthy it shows nothing good {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90} It also comes to one who has evacuated much by medicine, and that sleep is of use to him and restores his power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90}

## Sleep that is not natural
A sleep that is not natural absolutely may come when the return to the origin is from excessive dissolving of the pneuma, whose substance cannot bear expanding for want of an excess over what suffices the roots, as is the case with fatigue and strong exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90} A sleep that is not natural may also come when the return of the psychic pneuma from the instruments is because of a cooling agent opposed to the substance of the pneuma, either from outside or from cooling medicines, so that the instruments gain a cold that is against the passing of the pneuma, and this is numbness (khadar) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90} It may come too because of a moistening agent of the instruments that muddies the substance of the pneuma, blocks its paths and slackens the substance of nerve and muscle with a slackness followed by blockage and closure, and this is the sleep of drunkenness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90} Close to this is what comes from indigestion and the long stay of food in the stomach, and the coma of these is removed by vomiting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.90}
