## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents situational leadership through a table of his own design: the leader plays two roles — directing (instructions: what, how, when, where, at what cost) and encouraging (motivation, appreciation, granting authority, listening, sharing in decisions, teaching) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:00:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:01:30}.

## The four leadership styles
From the mix of much or little direction and encouragement come four styles: the director (directs much, encourages little), the coach (directs and encourages much), the encourager (directs little, encourages much), and the delegator (neither) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:04:00}.

## The subordinate types
He classifies subordinates by two measures — competence and enthusiasm — on a given task, not as a general evaluation: the beginner (enthusiastic, not competent), the intermediate (neither), the advanced (competent, not enthusiastic) and the expert (both) {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:11:00}.
The matching rule: the beginner needs the director, the intermediate needs the coach, the advanced needs the encourager to raise his enthusiasm, and the expert needs to be shielded from interference and delegated to — and a leader who leads everyone in one style is a failed leader {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:16:00}.
The best leader, to him, is the one who brings those with him to expert level, so that his style with them becomes delegation: providing them authority and resources and protecting them {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:15:00}.

## Delegation is for the expert alone
He ties the theory to delegation: a task is delegated only to someone competent and enthusiastic for it; whoever delegates otherwise has only himself to blame, and whoever forces a person onto a task should not expect success, for he is not enthusiastic for it in the first place {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:21:00}.
He applies the rule to raising children too: a child is assessed in each subject separately — he may be able and fond of one subject and otherwise in another — and is led in each by the fitting style, until he is competent and enthusiastic in all and needs not even supervision {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep03 01:19:00}.

## In "Secrets of Prophetic Leadership"
In episode 25 of his programme [[work/asrar-al-qiyada-al-nabawiyya-program|"Secrets of Prophetic Leadership"]], [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|al-Suwaidan]] makes situational leadership the skill that trains a leader to analyse situations, one of the two competencies of the role of motivation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:38:00} A "situation" for him is a person in a task; a leader suited to one task or stage may not suit another; his two levers are directing and supporting; and followers are classed by their competence and enthusiasm for the particular task. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:22:00} He gives an example from the Sira for each type, as he tells it:
- **The beginner** (low competence, high enthusiasm): [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]], sent to Yemen to call people to Islam, where no one converted in six months; then Ali was sent and Hamdan accepted Islam. The remedy is to train the person or change the task. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:26:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:28:00}
- **The trainee** (low on both): the Ansari who disputed with al-Zubayr over water. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:29:30}
- **The frustrated** (high competence, low enthusiasm): [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] after his conversion, when he found no role that suited him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:33:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:34:00}
- **The empowered** (high on both): [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] as judge; only this type is to be delegated to. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:35:30}

A leader who treats everyone alike fails, he says. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan asrar-al-qyada-al-nbawya/ep25 00:36:00}

## In "The Academy for Preparing Leaders": four styles for four kinds of follower
In the ninth lesson of [[work/leadership-development-academy-1-program|the Leaders' Academy]] al-Suwaidan calls situational leadership the best, in his view, of thirteen main theories of leadership. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:36:30} The leader has two roles, directing and supporting, which he explains in some detail. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:37:00} Combining them gives four styles: the director, the coach, the supporter and the delegator. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:39:30} Followers are measured on two criteria, competence and enthusiasm, and always in a particular task, never in general. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:43:00} That gives four kinds of follower: the beginner, the intermediate, the advanced and the expert. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:47:00}

He follows a young civil engineer through her career to show how she moves through these stages. He says one of the strongest ways to motivate is to give a person authority. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:47:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:49:00} The beginner, who is keen but not yet capable, needs direction rather than praise. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:51:00} His key sentence is: "the effective leader chooses his style according to the situation", where the situation means "the person in a particular task". He who leads everyone in one way, he says, is not effective. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:53:00} He applies it at home, subject by subject: a child who is good at mathematics but dislikes it needs encouragement, not direction. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:55:00} Forcing a person, he says, is failure in leadership. The leader's task is to raise competence before handing over a task, so that everyone becomes an expert in his own work. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan leadership-development-academy-1/ep09 00:56:30}
