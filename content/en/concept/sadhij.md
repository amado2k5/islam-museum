## Nature and selection
It is close in strength to sunbul but softer; it is leaves that appear on the face of the water and stalks like shahisfaram, with a flower that crumbles; it grows in India in waters that stand in muddy ground and floats on the face of the water like the plant known as duckweed, without attachment to a root. Some have supposed it to be the leaf of Indian nardin because it resembles it in strength, and its oil has the strength of the oil of chamomile and of saffron, indeed stronger. Dioscorides said that some people err in supposing it the leaf of nardin from the likeness of smell, since many things have a smell like that of nardin, such as valerian, asarum and sweet flag; it is not as they thought, but sadhij is another kind growing in India. When the water dries in summer the land there is burnt with wood kindled on the spot, for if that is not done the leaf does not grow. One kind of sadhij is crumbling and of poor smell, and the strength of that kind is like the strength of nardin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.585}.

The best is the fresh kind inclining to white that does not crumble, with a pervasive smell like nardin, and neither spoilt nor salty nor slack {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Actions and properties
Put among clothes it keeps them from moths, it is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Adornment
It sweetens the breath when held under the tongue and prevents decay {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Swellings and pustules
It is cooked in rose water and, after grinding, applied as a poultice to a hot swelling; it is a good remedy for hot swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is much more helpful to the stomach and liver than nardin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Eyes
Sadhij is fit for hot swellings of the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Excretory organs
It is more strongly diuretic than nardin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.

## Substitutes
Its substitute is talisfar or sunbul; the proportions the book gives are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.586}.
