## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that what is evacuated or held back points in several ways: an unnatural retention, or an unnatural evacuation, either of the organ's own substance, read by its kind, amount or colour, or of something else, read by its coming out at all, its quality, its substance, its amount or its direction {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.

## Retention
What is evacuated or held back may point by an unnatural retention, as when something that should be passed is held, in one whose urine or stool is retained {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.

## Matter from the organ's own substance
Unnatural evacuation of the organ's own substance points in three ways: by its kind, as shed lining points to erosion in the windpipe of the lung; by its amount, as the scab that comes away in abrasion points to an ulcer in the large intestine if thick and in the small if thin; and by its colour, as red scaly sediment points to fleshy organs such as the kidney, and white to sinewy ones such as the bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.

## Matter not from the organ
What is not of the organ's substance points in other ways: that it does not usually come out, as sound humours and blood when they appear; that its quality is not natural, as spoiled blood; that its substance is not natural at all, as a stone; that its amount is not natural though it usually comes out, as too little or too much stool and urine; that its colour is unnatural, as black stool and urine; or that its direction is unnatural, as stool coming up from above in ileus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.157}.
