## Summary
Al-Battani shows how the direction of the declination is known from the Sun's place on the zodiac, whether it is growing or shrinking and whether the Sun is rising or falling: from the start of Aries to ninety the declination grows and the Sun climbs toward the north; from ninety to 180 it shrinks and the Sun falls from the north; from 180 to 270 it grows and the Sun descends toward the south; and from 270 to 360 it shrinks and the Sun rises in the south. He then says the ancients divided the declination into six grades in rising and falling. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.23}.

## The four quarters and the Sun's motion
When the declination's share is from the start of Aries to ninety, the declination grows and the Sun rises in the north; from ninety to 180 it shrinks and the Sun falls from the north; from 180 to 270 it grows and the Sun descends to the south; and from 270 to the full 360 it shrinks and the Sun climbs in the south. The numbers are written in the printed text in letter-numerals and look disturbed, so this entry follows the fourfold division that agrees with the rest of the passage. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.23}.

## North and south
He says in short that when the share of the declination is in the first half of the circle, from the start of Aries to 180, the declination is northern, and when in the second half, from 180 to 360, it is southern. By this computation one knows the Sun's declination, its direction, and its rising and falling. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.23}.

## The six grades
He says the ancients divided the declination into six grades in rising and falling and made each fifteen degrees of the Sun's course in each of the four quarters a grade of rising or falling up to the full ninety that completes the six grades. When the Sun is in the first fifteen degrees of a quarter they say it is in the first grade, in the second fifteen the second, and so on to the sixth. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.23}.
