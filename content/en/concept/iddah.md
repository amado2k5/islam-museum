## Overview
Iddah is the waiting period a woman observes after divorce or widowhood; the Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia treats it across the marriage and divorce chapters {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The encyclopedia examines the term of the iddah, the supervening iddah, residence in the iddah home, and the effect of miscarriage on the iddah {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## For Scholars
Idda is the term a woman observes after divorce or widowhood; the Kuwaiti fiqh encyclopaedia treats it in the marriage and divorce chapters, covering the term's length, the interim idda, dwelling in the idda house, and the effect of miscarriage on it.

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme "Liqa' al-Iman"
In episode 15 of [[work/likaa-al-iman|Liqa' al-Iman]] the presenter notes that the waiting period of a widow is four months and ten days, and asks its wisdom now that science can tell what is in the womb. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep15 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep15 00:00:30} [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] says the waiting period was set to establish that the womb is free, so that no man waters another's crop, but not for that alone: the woman is not a beast, and if it were only for the womb it would not have been set at three menstrual cycles, which lengthen the term. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep15 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep15 00:01:00} He holds that divorce comes from aversion while death does not, so affection and longing remain, and the widow's waiting period needed a longer span for the soul to settle. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep15 00:04:30}
