## Summary
Mukus is the plural of maks, which in the lexicon means loss and injustice. It names the dues taken from sellers in markets in the pre-Islamic period and the toll taken from merchants entering a town. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14939}

## Related terms
The 'ushur is the tithe on trade or on alms; jibaya is collection in general; daraib are levies; kharaj is the levy on land; the mukus are levies on goods. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14940} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14941}

## Ruling
Some are blameworthy and forbidden, others are not. The half tithe Umar imposed on the trade of the protected people, and the tithe on the property of the hostile people that the Companions allowed, are not among the blameworthy. A report from Uqba ibn 'Amir says that the one who takes a toll does not enter Paradise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14941}

## Related rulings
Most jurists hold that a toll paid cannot be counted as zakat. Testimony on tolls is allowed to return rights to their owners. Asked about dealing with one whose wealth is mostly unlawful, Ibn Taymiyya answered that such dealing is doubtful and is not judged forbidden unless one knows he gives what is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14942}

## The hajj
The Malikis weigh whether the road is safe for property: a light toll that does not strip the traveller leaves the hajj binding in the sounder view, while a crushing toll cancels the obligation. The Hanafis do not count a toll on the road as an excuse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14942}
