## Overview
The grammatical dispute began quietly between al-Ru'asi in Kufa and [[person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi|al-Khalil]] in Basra, and then grew sharper between [[person/al-kisai|al-Kisa'i]] in Kufa and [[person/sibawayh|Sibawayh]] in Basra. {cite:duha-islam}

The source holds that this scholarly partisanship appears to have rested on a political partisanship between the two cities. It was a development of grammar after its emergence; see [[concept/ibtida-al-nahw|the beginning of grammar]]. {cite:duha-islam}

## The differences
The school of Basra laid down general rules to be followed exactly, said of the anomalous (*shadhdh*) that it is "preserved but not made a basis for analogy," and preferred analogy. {cite:duha-islam}

The Kufans, by contrast, held that everything that came from the Arabs should be respected and admitted even when it contradicted the general rules, and they made the anomaly the basis of a rule. The source notes, citing al-Suyuti, that al-Kisa'i would hear an anomaly and make it a principle; this is al-Suyuti's judgement, not the museum's. {cite:duha-islam}
