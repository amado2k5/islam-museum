## Summary
Al-Battani shows that a wandering planet appears to go backward when it is in the nearer, lower half of its epicycle, because its equation changes in one day by more than its mean motion. Its place on the zodiac then falls behind its earlier place, and it resumes forward motion after passing the tangent point of the epicycle. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.98}.

## Not a motion of the planet itself
He holds that the planet has no backward motion in itself; the appearance comes from the changing centres of its spheres and its position on the epicycle. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.98}.
