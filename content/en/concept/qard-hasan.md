## Summary
On al-Baqara 245, Sha'rawi holds that spending in God's cause is a loan to God. He takes a good loan to be one made from lawful money, and says its reward is multiplied by God's measure, not ours {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1040} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1041}.

## Why it is called a loan
Sha'rawi says that the word for loan means biting off a piece with the teeth. God uses the word because He knows how hard it is for a person to part with wealth. The lender hands over money hoping for its return, and God undertakes to repay it many times over {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1040}.

## To whom the loan is made
Sha'rawi says that giving to a needy person is in effect a loan to God, since God calls each servant into being and undertakes his provision. In spending for the cause, he says, the giver gives to God directly, not to a named person but to the general cause {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1040}.

## The good loan and charity
He says the good loan comes from lawful money, and cites the saying about a woman who gave in charity from unlawful earnings: would that she had neither sinned nor given {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1041}. He reports a view that the loan's reward is greater than charity's, because charity is given once and lost, while the loan keeps the self attached and each act of patience brings a good deed. He does not name who holds this view {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1041}.

## Spending from what God gave
On al-Baqara 254 he holds that one spends from God's provision: the mind, the limbs and the material all belong to God, and the poor may one day be oneself {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1083}. He distinguishes goods used directly from the price, which is not used directly, and says gold does not feed the hungry {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1084}. He then says that on the Day there is no sale, no close friendship and no intercession except by God's leave, so spending must come before it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1085}.
