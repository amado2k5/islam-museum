## Summary
Ibn Sina counts the paint among the treatments that reach the disease itself, says a drug may have a fine and a dense power and the fine is the more needed, distinguishes the cohesive plaster from the flowing paint, and notes cloths fumigated with raw aloeswood for the principal organs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## The paint reaches the disease
The paint is among the treatments that reach the disease itself. A drug may have two powers, a fine one and a dense one, and the need for the fine is greater than the need for the dense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## The plaster
If its density balances its fineness, then when it is used as a plaster its fine part passes in and the dense part is held back, and one benefits from what passes, as coriander does with groats in dressing scrofulous swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## Plaster and paint
Plasters are like paints, except that plasters are cohesive and paints flow, and paints are often applied with cloths {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.

## Principal organs
On the principal organs such as the liver and heart, if nothing prevents, cloths fumigated with raw aloeswood help, and they give the paints an aromatic power that the principal organs love {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.289}.
