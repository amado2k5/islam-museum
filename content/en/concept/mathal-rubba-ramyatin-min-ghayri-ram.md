## The proverb and its meaning
The proverb 'Many a shot is from one who is no archer' is one of the Arabic proverbs recorded in the old proverb books, as a research summary supplied to the museum sets out in a choice of ten. Its meaning, in the summary, is unexpected success by a novice who is no master of the craft. It belongs to the matter of the great Arabic proverb collections treated in the page [[concept/kutub-al-amthal-al-arabiyya|The Arabic proverb books]] {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p16} {cite:noonpost-16943}.

## The story as the books tell it
Hakim ibn Abd Yaghuth al-Minqari was a renowned marksman, and he swore to hunt a wild cow but missed repeatedly. His young son asked for the bow and hit the target with the first shot, and his father made his remark. {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p16} {cite:noonpost-16943}

## The old sources
The summary places the proverb in Majma al-Amthal, volume 1, page 299, number 1581, with a KetabOnline page and the Noonpost article. Volume, page and serial numbers stand as in the summary, and the museum has not checked them {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p16} {cite:noonpost-16943}. Majma al-Amthal is the work of Abu al-Fadl al-Maydani, who died in 518 AH, 1124 CE, and holds according to the summary over 6,000 proverbs; the summary tabulates it with Abu Hilal al-Askari's Jamharat al-Amthal and al-Zamakhshari's al-Mustaqsa {cite:arabictypinglab-arabic-proverbs}.

## When the proverb is used
The proverb is used for what fits its meaning, that is unexpected success by a novice who is no master of the craft, and the hearer calls its tale to mind even when it is not told. The proverb books agree in keeping a proverb together with its story, because the story explains why it was said, and the sense it is applied to may widen with use; but the summary gives no other instances of its use, so the page gives none.

## A note on the nature of the story
The story here is as the books tell it. It is one of the widely repeated traditional reports, and the museum cannot establish that it happened; it is a narrative told to explain the proverb, not confirmed history. The museum has not opened any of the books and pages named.
