## Overview
Taqlid in usul usage is accepting another's saying without proof; the Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia gives it a dedicated entry {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The encyclopedia's taqlid entry treats its senses and rulings among the jurists {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}; the founding of usul in al-Shafi'i's Risala is the frame in which the taqlid–ijtihad contest was later fought {cite:risala-shafii}.

## Taqlid in al-Shawkani's Irshad al-Fuhul

In [[work/irshad-al-fuhul|Irshad al-Fuhul]], [[person/al-shawkani|al-Shawkani]] derives taqlid from qilada, a necklace, as though the imitator hung the ruling on the mujtahid's neck; technically it is acting on another's word without proof, which excludes acting on the Prophet's word and on consensus, a layperson's turning to a mufti and a judge's turning to the testimony of upright witnesses, since in these a proof stands. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.267} He quotes Ibn al-Humam's definition in al-Tahrir — acting without proof on the word of one whose word is not itself a proof — and prefers it. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.267}

On imitation in matters of creed he reports that the majority forbade it, and mentions the claim that one who imitates in creed is not a believer, which he rejects, holding that the Companions who did not reach the level of ijtihad were sufficed by a general faith; he quotes Ibn al-Sam'ani rejecting the demand that ordinary people know the principles of faith by the theologians' method. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.268} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.269}

On positive law he reports three positions: forbidden altogether, which he quotes al-Qarafi as the view of Malik and most scholars, and Ibn Hazm claiming a consensus against taqlid and reporting its prohibition from Malik, al-Shafi'i and Abu Hanifa; obligatory altogether, which he attributes to some of the Hashwiyya; and a distinction, obligatory for the layperson and forbidden for the mujtahid, which he reports from many followers of the four imams. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.269} Al-Shawkani holds that prohibition is the view of the majority if not a consensus, and places between ijtihad and taqlid a middle way: the unlearned asking the learned about the law's ruling on his case, not about his opinion; he reads the verse "ask the people of remembrance" ([[passage/quran-16-43|al-Nahl 43]]) as concerning the question whether prophets were men. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.269} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.270} For a full treatment he refers to his treatise [[work/al-qawl-al-mufid-fi-adillat-al-ijtihad-wa-al-taqlid|al-Qawl al-Mufid fi Hukm al-Taqlid]]. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.269} A related question has its own entry: [[question/must-a-layperson-follow-one-madhhab|Must a layperson keep to a single school of law?]].
