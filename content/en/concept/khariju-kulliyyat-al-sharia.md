## Summary
The programme [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|Al-Wasatiyya]], presented by [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] on Al-Resalah television, gave an episode to "Graduates of the sharia colleges and the challenges of the present". {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:00:30} Opening it, the presenter said the world is changing fast, the sharia colleges graduate thousands, and the question is whether they meet what the present needs. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:01:00} He set out two opposing views: one that attacks the graduates and sees a break between them and reality, technology and the internet, and one that sees their performance and their link with life and technology improving. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:01:30} He stressed the subject's weight because the mosque imams and Friday preachers are graduates of these colleges. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:02:30}

## Scope of the discussion
The presenter limited the discussion to bachelor-level graduates of a sharia college or institute, not doctorate holders who become professors, because a bachelor graduate usually becomes a teacher or an imam and preacher, and they are the largest numbers. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:13:00}

## The guests
- Dr Jamil Ahmad Allam, dean of the Institute of Islamic Studies in Alexandria, Zagazig and Menoufia, who introduced himself as a former member of the Muslim World League and a lecturer at Islamic centres in Europe. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:10:00}
- Majd Makki, a researcher in the religious sciences who supervises the website of the League of Syrian Scholars, one of the Syrian scholars living in Saudi Arabia, who has taken part in preparing curricula. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:10:30}
- Dr Ahmad ibn Rashid ibn Sa'id, professor of political communication at King Saud University in Riyadh, journalist, writer and poet; his master's thesis was on how the American elite press covered the Palestinian intifada, and his doctorate, in Britain, was on the Friday sermon as political communication. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:11:00}
- Dr Abd al-Hamid, formerly professor at the College of Islamic Sciences of the University of Baghdad and a former dean of a sharia college in Yemen, who presents two programmes on the channel. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:12:00}
- The professor of comparative fiqh at the Faculty of Sharia and Law of [[building/al-azhar|al-Azhar]] and its former dean, a member of the Islamic Research Academy, the Assembly of Muslim Jurists of America and the International Union of Muslim Scholars; his name is unclear in the transcript and is not written here. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:12:00}

## First poll: the graduates' level
The presenter put a question to the channel's viewers, answered by text message, with three choices: well grounded in knowledge and in reality; grounded in knowledge but weak in knowing reality; or weak in both. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:03:30} Its results were not read out in the episode. He asked the young studio audience about the graduates' level in meeting what is asked of them; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:05:00} only 14% said excellent, 37% average and 49% weak, which the presenter called a tragedy and a big problem. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:07:30}

## Views of the young audience
- One young man held that their study is purely theoretical and academic, does not reach into society or renew preaching, and that many well-known "new preachers" are not sharia specialists. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:08:00}
- Another held that sharia study has turned from a mission into a job, entered for the certificate and employment. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:09:00}
- A third held that moral and educational formation is missing from religious study, which separates the student from the spiritual path and stays theoretical and weak in practice. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:10:00}

- **Political awareness and the changes of the age:** one guest saw a flight from engaging with politics; Dr Ahmad ibn Rashid saw the shaykhs' avoidance of politics as falling into "the secular trap", since the literature of the Islamic movements raised people on the idea that Islam is religion and state. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:22:00} He mentioned a shaykh who ruled that resisters in Palestine and Iraq must not resist the occupation until they had the ruler's permission; another guest replied that this does not represent the scholars. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:25:00} The Azhar professor saw them as close to good because sources of knowledge have multiplied, and the presenter distinguished between being informed and being aware. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:23:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:24:00}

## Second poll: causes of the weakness
The presenter asked the young audience for the biggest cause, with four choices: the students' level, the professors' level, the curricula, or the lack of incentives. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:34:30} 14% chose the students, 9% the professors, 56% the curricula and 21% the lack of incentives. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:36:30}
- A young woman said the problem is the random selection of students: sharia is mostly entered by those not admitted elsewhere, and the gifted student's future after it is uncertain. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:37:30}
- Abir, a graduate of a four-year religious institute, said her professors were excellent and the curricula weak, and that in her third year she was surprised to find creed and da'wa topics dropped and replaced, which left her frustrated. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:39:00}
- A young man saw the root cause as the politicisation of religion, with curricula set by government policies and decisions. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:39:30}

### Incentives
Dr Abd al-Hamid held that students choose a college by its financial return, and that if incentives rose the able would crowd into the sharia colleges and professors and curricula would improve. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:40:30} He said that in some states, giving Israel as an example, the religious scholar draws one of the highest salaries. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:41:00} The Azhar professor agreed that incentives are the biggest cause, called for every sharia college to teach the subjects of contemporary life, and for the student's weakness at the preparatory and secondary stages to be addressed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:43:00}

### The student and admission
Dr Ahmad ibn Rashid said the idea that the Gulf student outdoes the Egyptian is a myth overtaken by time, and that curricula have an enormous effect when the Arab-Israeli conflict or its religious character is removed from them, yet a student's own readiness can overcome shallow curricula. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:44:00} He said admission to the sharia college of Imam University requires, as he put it, around 80 or 85 percent in secondary school, a high Arabic grade and interviews. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:44:30} Dr Jamil Allam said graduates of private colleges are without doubt better than those of state colleges; that the problem is the student entering a state college through the admissions office rather than by aptitude; and that the old Azhar produced outstanding scholars without incentives or state funding. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:49:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:50:00}

### Curricula and length of study
The Azhar professor said that as dean he introduced a course on "contemporary fiqh issues" covering surrogacy, choosing the sex of a child, assisted fertilisation and cosmetic surgery, and proposed it for every sharia college. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:52:00} Dr Abd al-Hamid held that four years cannot make a scholar, while the early scholars studied in the mosques from dawn to night for twelve years, so the bachelor's degree is for him a first step on the road. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:53:00} Dr Ahmad ibn Rashid held that shallowness is common to students of every field, recalling that he once asked a media student, during the Bosnian war, who was the aggressor and who the victim between the Serbs and the Muslims, and got no answer. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:53:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:54:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:54:30}

## Third poll: private colleges
The presenter asked the young audience whether it would be better to turn the sharia colleges into private colleges free of official tutelage, so that only willing students come and administration and curricula are strong, warning that the question has large implications. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:55:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:56:00} 80% supported it and 20% rejected it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:57:30}
- Dr Abd al-Hamid, who ran a sharia college for six years, held that a private college's students would be better because only those who want learning come, but called for both kinds to remain, in competition. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:57:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:59:00}
- The Azhar professor disagreed: a private college carries its owners' policy, which may be extreme or commercial, while a state college follows the state's laws and its curricula are open to all; he mentioned a private institute in Cairo that grants degrees and was never recognised. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 00:59:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:00:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:01:00}
- Majd Makki held that the problem is the selection of students and professors, called for combining both, for raising the standing of the student of knowledge in society's eyes, and for endowments that free him for study. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:02:00} He gave the example of an Indian scholar who, he said, died in 1304 aged thirty-nine leaving three hundred books, because a wealthy Muslim freed him with four hundred rupees a month when four rupees sufficed others. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:03:00} (needs_source: the scholar's name is unclear in the transcript; the details match [[person/abd-al-hayy-al-lakhnawi|Abd al-Hayy al-Lakhnawi]], not confirmed by listening.)
- Dr Ahmad ibn Rashid said he had written two articles years earlier as an exchange of letters: one from private education to state education saying it needed a "perestroika", and a reply from state education titled "lovers of money do not raise generations"; he concluded that competition between the two is healthy. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:03:30}

### A private institute as a model
Asked for the marks of a successful sharia college, Dr Jamil Allam said his institute's regulations combine what is taught at al-Azhar, the sharia departments of Egyptian universities and Dar al-Ulum, and add psychology, education, research methods, practical research training and foreign languages, with a languages branch teaching fifteen languages and an institute for the Qur'an. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:05:30} He said he admits only the gifted, whom he defined as a Muslim who wants to know his religion in order to express it in a contemporary way, and turns away anyone without real desire even if he would pay, because this is not a business. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:05:30} He called for freeing sharia colleges from the state's general policy, so that foreign aid does not push the state to interfere in curricula, and for the professor's freedom, and said that he refused support and so kept freedom over curricula. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:07:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:08:00} The Azhar professor replied that college departments set the curricula and pass them up until they become regulations, without state interference. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:09:30}

### The International Islamic University Malaysia
The presenter described the International Islamic University in Malaysia, which he said he had visited and where he saw a robotics laboratory; it has a rule that no sharia student graduates without a second supporting specialisation to work in, so as not to earn a living from religious knowledge, and no medicine or engineering student graduates without a supporting specialisation in sharia. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:10:30} Dr Abd al-Hamid held that this does not produce a well-grounded scholar, because knowledge needs specialisation, follow-up and continued study. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep20 01:11:30}
