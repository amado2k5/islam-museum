## Summary
Ibn Sina warns the exerciser to keep the heat of exercise from reaching a weak organ except as a by-effect, giving the example of someone with varicose veins who should not move the legs much but carry the exercise to the upper body, and rules that a weak body's exercise is weak and a strong body's strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.

## The varicose example
The exerciser must guard against the heat of exercise reaching what is weak among his organs, except in passing. Someone troubled with varicose veins should not move his legs much but little, and should carry his exercise to the upper body, neck, head and trunk, so that the effect of the exercise reaches the legs from above {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.

## Measured by strength
A weak body's exercise is weak, and a strong body's exercise is strong {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.224}.
