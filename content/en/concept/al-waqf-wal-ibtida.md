## The chapter
Ibn al-Jazari says: "and after your tajwid of the letters there is no doing without knowing the stoppings and the beginnings" — the waqf is the reciter's cutting the sound at the end of a word to breathe or to part the meaning, and the ibtida' is what the recitation is begun with after the stop {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## The kinds of waqf
The matn divides the stop after completion into three ranks: tamm — what has no attachment to what follows; kafi — what has a verbal but not a semantic attachment; hasan — what has a semantic attachment and may still be stopped on; then the qabih — whose meaning is incomplete and is stopped on only out of necessity, the beginning being made before it. And it closes: "there is no waqf in the Qur'an that is obligatory, and none forbidden, except what has a cause" {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## The waqf marks of the codices
The codices of the tradition place marks at the stopping places — the mim of the obligatory stop, the lam of the no-stop, the jim of the permissible, qala and sila and the three dots — and their detail differs with the printing houses. They are a copying convention, not a binding text, and the last word stays with the scholars' explanations of the places {cite:ar-wikisource}.
