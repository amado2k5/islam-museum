## Summary
[[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]] gave an episode of his programme [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|"Al-Wasatiyya"]] to changing and developing the religion curricula in the Arab world. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:00:30} The young studio audience rated how effective these curricula are: 43% weak, 29% acceptable, 16% good and only 12% excellent. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:08:30} The presenter then went through ten aims he sees for religion curricula and asked his guests to rate each. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:12:00} The archived copy of the episode ends at about the fifty-fourth minute, before the close.

## The episode's questions
The presenter began by saying that everyone learned the basics of the religion from school curricula, and that extraordinary effort lies behind them; but he asked whether they are effective in reaching their aims, or whether, with other things, they contributed to the community's backwardness, some people even holding that parts of them in some states helped spread extremism and sectarianism. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:01:30} He asked where the fault lies: in the curricula themselves, in the teacher, or in media and society that pull down what the school builds. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:02:00} He asked why developing them was considered only when the West demanded it after the September attacks, why the change deleted only the texts on jihad, the Jews and wala' and bara', and whether curricula owned by official bodies with political calculations can be developed at all. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:03:00}

## The account of the review committee
Ali Labban said Egypt's religion curricula were set by a committee for reviewing religious textbooks chaired by an American he named as Dr Linda Lambert, who required the curricula to bring out what Islam shares with other religions and the books to be reviewed by followers of other religions; so riba, wine, jihad and ruling were left out, and prayer came to be explained as exercise and ablution as hygiene. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:10:30} This is his account on the programme; neither the committee's name nor its chair has been checked in another source.

## The presenter's ten aims
The presenter said that, as his own judgement, he had set ten main aims for religion curricula, on which he would write them if he were writing them, and asked his guests to rate the curricula they knew on each aim in one word before discussion: {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:12:30}

1. **Rooting the creed**: love of God and His Messenger, attachment to the hereafter, awareness of God's watch. Dr Jamil Allam found this present in Saudi Arabia and Yemen and weak in Egypt; Dr Khalid al-Qasim rated it very good in the Saudi curricula. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:14:00}
2. **Understanding the Qur'an and the Sunna**: how to read and understand them and tell sound from weak. Some guests found it better in the Saudi and Yemeni curricula and absent in Egypt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:15:00}
3. **Understanding the aims and general rules of the sharia**: preserving religion, life and honour. The guests agreed it is weak, even in Saudi Arabia, and the presenter said there is a clear problem here everywhere. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:15:30}
4. **The rulings a Muslim needs in daily life**: worship and dealings such as marriage, divorce and sales. Ratings ranged from "very weak and very superficial" through "acceptable" to "very good", and Salah Abd al-Sami' held that the rulings are written in the textbooks but the means of practice are missing. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:17:00}
5. **The middle way and respect for other views**, rejecting extremism, terrorism and sectarianism and seeking common ground with people of another school, sect or religion. Ali Labban said, ironically, that after the deletions this side is "more than excellent"; Salah Abd al-Sami' rated it weak; and the presenter asked whether the curricula do the opposite with other Islamic schools. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:18:30}
6. **Understanding and analysing history**, including that of the prophets and the Companions, without sanctifying what is not sacred. Ali Labban said Islamic history has been erased from the books, and gave figures: the Islamic education books keep only six lines on Umar ibn al-Khattab, four on Khalid ibn al-Walid and ten pages on the whole prophetic era, against thirty-four pages on Napoleon in the history books; these are his figures. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:20:30}
7. **Instilling pride** in Islam, its history and civilisation. One guest held that the curricula instil a narrow national pride, not an Islamic one; another that they form in the child a habit of acceptance without voicing an opinion; Dr Khalid al-Qasim rated this good in Saudi Arabia. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:23:00}
8. **Instilling values and morals**: truthfulness and trustworthiness. The presenter recalled noticing, while living in the West, that a Muslim would give you an appointment and not keep it while an American kept it to the minute, and asked whether that came from the person or from what was planted in him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:23:30} The guests held that the curricula speak about morals in theory but do not instil them, and that the school's educational climate completes the curriculum. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:24:30}
9. **The civilisational role** of the community and of the individual Muslim, rather than reducing religion to acts of worship. The guests held that Islam's civilisational contribution is wholly absent from educational discourse, and that the sira and history are presented as wars, battles and disputes without the side of building. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:25:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:26:30}
10. **Civic participation**: human rights and duties, the rights of ruler and ruled, and taking part in politics, the economy and civil society. The presenter said Islam, as he understands it, plants this clearly; the guests held that it is absent or was deleted, leaving only acts of worship. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:27:30}

The presenter concluded that there is a very large problem in religion curricula, and that the discussion had turned on the Egyptian and Saudi curricula in particular but reflected others too, some with bigger problems. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:27:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:28:00}

## Where is the fault?
- **A creed emptied out**: Ali Labban repeated that the weakness of creed in the curricula comes from the international committee that dismissed curriculum experts, and called for bringing back the competent, lifting American pressure and freeing the author. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:32:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:33:00} He held that the West, through an international body, asked for curricula to be emptied of what offends it and promised the same in return, but our governments were able to change while Western governments cannot intervene in their own education. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:38:00}
- **No overall picture of the religion**: Dr Jamil Allam held that what is missing is an overall picture of Islam: the features that set it apart, its comprehensiveness and fitness for every field of life, its universality, and its answer to the great human questions. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:36:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:37:30} He said religion is the key to human development, so whoever wants a renaissance must begin with it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:36:30}
- **Information without feeling or conduct**: Salah Abd al-Sami' distinguished "Islamic education", a wider concept, from "religious education", and held that building a Muslim character rests on understanding, feeling and conduct, while the textbooks he has examined stop at understanding. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:40:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:41:00}
- **Rote learning and the aims of the sharia**: Dr Khalid al-Qasim agreed that there is heavy emphasis on rote learning, said we copied the heritage of earlier generations, part of which is the ijtihad of a particular age, and held that the aims of legislation matter greatly for understanding rulings, creed and dealings with others but receive weak attention. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:42:00}

## Criticisms of the Saudi curricula, relayed
The presenter said he was relaying criticisms he does not adopt, that Saudi Arabia's religion curricula are among the deepest in the Islamic world and the largest, taking about 27% of the curriculum, and that their merits stand. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:42:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:43:00} Among the criticisms he relayed: mobilisation against opponents reaching even the [[concept/ashariyya|Ash'aris]], who are Sunnis; describing anyone who adopts capitalism as a hypocrite outside the faith; so much talk of shirk, unbelief and innovation that he told of a father advising his son to write "shirk" for every creed question and "haram" for every fiqh question; an insulting description of Western civilisation; and calling traffic week an innovation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:44:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:45:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:45:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:46:00}

Dr Khalid al-Qasim replied that not all of this is so: there is no declaration of the Ash'aris as unbelievers in the curricula, and he studied creed at every stage; but he acknowledged a kind of charged mobilisation and repeated charges of innovation and sin, and that a student of that age cannot tell a Sufi who holds the unity of being from one who does not. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:46:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:47:00} He said capitalism is a different system that may be called contrary, agreed with the presenter that calling its adherent an open hypocrite is a hard word, and concluded that the curricula need review. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:48:30}

## What was changed?
The presenter asked Ali Labban, as a member of the People's Assembly, whether the recent change really focused on removing jihad, enmity towards the Jews and wala' and bara', as Islamists had argued in several Arab parliaments. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:49:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:49:30} He said the change covered that and more, in Egypt and some ten states including Pakistan and Iran, and gave examples from the UAE: a lesson on the role of wind in pollinating flowers kept the idea and dropped the verse, a lesson on water development dropped the rain prayer, and he said the prohibition of wine and riba and what was said about the Crusades were also removed; these are his examples. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:49:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:50:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:51:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:51:30}

Salah Abd al-Sami' said every Arab state without exception has held conferences to develop education, that their participants are citizens of those states, and called for leaving conspiracy theory aside and looking at the results. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:52:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:53:00} Dr Khalid al-Qasim said the change in the Kingdom was small: it did not touch jihad or creed, a subject called "Fiqh and Conduct" was added, religion subjects were reduced at some stages, and this came from pressure at home rather than in response to Western pressure. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:53:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep27 00:54:00}
