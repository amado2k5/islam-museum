## Nature
Tuth is of two kinds, one the sweet firsad, which goes the way of the fig in ripening but is worse in nourishment, less, worse for the blood and worse for the stomach, having all the conditions of the fig but below it; as for the bitter known as the Shami tuth, most of the book's discussion falls on it; and its unripe, when dried, takes the place of sumac {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Temperament
The sweet is hot and moist, and the sour Shami tends to cold and moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Actions and properties
It has astringency and cooling, and its juice is astringent, especially when cooked in a copper vessel, and it prevents the flow of matter to the organs, especially the unripe, the unripe being like sumac {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Cosmetics
When its leaf is cooked with vine leaf and black fig leaf in rain water it blackens the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Swellings and pustules
The sour holds back swellings of the throat and mouth, and its leaf benefits the throat disease and quinsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Wounds and ulcers
The sour kind benefits malignant ulcers by drying, and so does its juice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Organs of the head
The syrup of the sour is of benefit for pustules of the mouth, a decoction of its root loosens the teeth, and rinsing with the juice of the sour's leaf is good for an aching tooth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Tuth is bad for the stomach and spoils in it, especially the firsad, so all its kinds should be eaten before food and on a stomach without corruption; the Shami does not harm a bilious stomach and has no badness or nausea, its nourishment is little, and it stirs appetite, slips food along and sends it out quickly; in sum it descends quickly from the stomach but slowly from the intestine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Excretory organs
The salted, dried, astringent kind of tuth strongly holds the bowel and benefits dysentery, its tear loosens the bowels, in its bark there is cleansing and loosening and its loosening is the greater; in the sweet tuth there is quick descent, either through its moisture or through the sharpness of what is mixed with it; it may prevent chronic diarrhoea and ulcers of the intestine, especially dried, in all kinds of tuth there is a promotion of urine, and the Shami, though quick from the stomach, is slow from the intestines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}

## Poisons and bites
The bark of tuth is a theriac for hemlock, the juice of its leaf, drunk, benefits stings of the spider, and it softens the bowel through its stickiness and bloating; the amount is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.695}
