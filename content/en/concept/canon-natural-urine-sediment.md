## Summary
Opening chapter six on kinds of sediment, Ibn Sina describes the natural, praiseworthy sediment: white, continuous, even, round and smooth, a sign of the whole body's maturation of its matter. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.193}.

## Description and comparison
The natural sediment shows ordinary digestion and maturation; it is white, settled, continuous, uniform and even, round, smooth and fine, like the sediment of rose water. Its sign of maturation of matter in the whole body is like that of white, smooth pus for a swelling, except that pus is dense and this is fine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.193}.

## Colours and how it differs from pus and rawness
He says the best colour other than white is red, then yellow, then arsenic-yellow, and that the bad begins with the lentil colour; whiteness may come from wind mixed in, not from maturation. The sediment resembles thin pus and thin raw matter, but pus differs by its stench and raw matter by its compact parts, while the good sediment is distinguished by fineness and lightness. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.193}.
