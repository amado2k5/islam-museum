## Summary
Chapter 7 gives the rulings for how a year is put together, that is, what follows when a spring of one kind meets a winter, summer or autumn of another, southern or northern, moist or dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.121-122}.

## A moist winter and a southern spring
When winter is very moist, women due in spring miscarry on the slightest cause, and if they deliver the newborn are weak, die or fall sick; ophthalmia, bloody flux and catarrh abound, above all among the old. If a rainy southern spring follows a northern winter, summer brings many hot fevers, ophthalmia, loose bowels and bloody flux, with much putrefaction and its fevers. This is worst for women and boys, and those who survive fall into quartan fever, dropsy, pains of the spleen and a weak liver; but if rain falls and a north wind blows at the rising of Sirius in their summer, improvement is hoped for and the diseases dissolve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.121-122}.

## A dry summer and a rainy autumn
When a rainy southern autumn follows a dry northern summer, bodies are ready for winter headache, cough, hoarseness and consumption, since they catch cold often. When a rainy northern autumn follows a dry southern summer, headache is frequent, then catarrh, cough and hoarseness. A southern summer followed by a northern autumn brings the squeezing diseases and retention. If both are southern and moist, moistures abound, winter brings the squeezing diseases, and the holding in of matter may lead to putrefactive diseases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.122}.

## Two dry northern seasons
If summer and autumn are both dry and northern, those who suffer from moisture and from sciatica gain, while others meet dry ophthalmia, chronic catarrh, hot fevers and melancholia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.122}.

## Other combinations
A cold rainy winter causes burning of the urine. When the summer heat and dryness are great there are quinsies, some fatal and some not, difficulty of urine, measles, smallpox, ophthalmia, corrupt blood, retention of the menses and spitting of blood. A dry winter after a dry spring is bad. Pestilence spoils trees and plants, so the animals that graze on them spoil, and so do the people who eat those animals {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.122}.
