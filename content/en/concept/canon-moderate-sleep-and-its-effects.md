## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the ninth chapter, on sleep and waking, by saying the cause of natural sleep was dealt with in its place, then shows that moderate sleep lets the natural faculty do its work, rests the psychic faculty and holds back the dissolving of the spirit, so that food is digested and weakness made good, and that it moistens and warms and most benefits the old {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.240-241}.

## What sleep does
The cause of natural sleep, of deep sleep and of their opposite, waking, was spoken of in its place and will be spoken of in the particular medicine. Here it is said that moderate sleep lets the natural faculty carry out its acts, rests the psychic faculty and increases its substance, so that by relaxing it may even stop the spirit dissolving, whichever spirit it is. Thus the digestible foods already named are digested, and the weakness left by kinds of dissolving, from fatigue, from coitus, from anger and the like, is made good {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.240}.

## For the old
Moderate sleep, when it meets balanced humours in quantity and quality, moistens and warms, and is the most useful thing for the old, for it keeps their moisture for them and brings it back {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.240-241}.
