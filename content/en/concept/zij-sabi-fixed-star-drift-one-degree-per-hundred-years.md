## The distance between the two observations
The text says that Ptolemy's observations gave a drift of one degree every hundred years, and that the interval between Ptolemy's observations and those they were compared with was too short to show a clear change in such a motion. The gap between Ptolemy's observation and the one compared with it was about two hundred years, and as time lengthened the drift showed as one degree in every sixty-six years. The difference between the two rates accounts for the excess. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.195-196}

## The reason for the difference
The text puts the difference between the two rates down to the fact that the motion does not show over a short interval, and that the discrepancy grows with time. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.196}
