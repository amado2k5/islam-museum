## Summary
In the fifth chapter Ibn Sina shows that the lumbar nerves, those of the lower back, send one part to the back muscles and one to the abdominal muscles, and that the two lowest pairs send large branches to the legs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.83}.

## Their course
The lumbar nerves share this: one part reaches the back muscles, another the abdominal muscles and the muscles lining the back, but the upper three mingle with the nerves descending from the brain and the others do not. The two lowest pairs send large branches toward the legs, joined by a branch from the third pair and one from the first sacral nerve; yet these two branches do not pass the hip joint but spread in its muscles, while the others pass it to the legs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.83}.

## How they differ from the nerves of the arms
The nerves of thighs and legs differ from those of the arms in that they do not all gather and incline deep inward, since the thigh joins the hip unlike the way the upper arm joins the shoulder, nor is its attachment to the origin of its nerves like that one; so these nerves head straight for the leg, some on the inner side, some on the outer, some plunging hidden under the muscles. Because the muscles growing from the pubic bone have no path to the legs from behind the body or from the inner thighs, given the many muscles and vessels there, a part of the nerve belonging to the leg muscles was made to run in the channel descending to the testicles until it turns to the pubic muscles and then descends to the knee muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.83}.
