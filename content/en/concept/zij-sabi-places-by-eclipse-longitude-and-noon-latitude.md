## Summary
Al-Battani says the longitudes and latitudes of cities are as set down in the well-known Book of the Form of the Earth: they began the longitudes at the Inhabited Islands in the western ocean and went east, by the times of lunar eclipses especially, one coming earlier than another in different cities, and took the latitudes from the Sun's measure at noon. He notes that the book lists ninety-four cities and has faults in longitudes and latitudes, and says he will restate what is needed. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.32}.

## Longitude from eclipses
He explains that those who set the longitudes made noon in one city precede noon in a city west of it by parts of the equator's times equal to the interval between the eclipses in the two cities, and that they took some of it, roughly, from the reports of those who travel the roads. So the method for longitude is to compare the hours at which one lunar eclipse is seen in two cities and convert the difference into parts of the equator. The passage gives no particular examples. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.32}.

## Latitude from the Sun, and the Book of the Form of the Earth
He says they took the latitudes from the Sun's measure at noon in the cities, knowing how far or near each stood to the zenith point as set out in earlier chapters, and so found each city's distance from the equator; under each city they put its approximate distance from the Khalidat islands in length and from the equator in latitude. He fixed this on the plan he found in the well-known Book of the Form of the Earth, and listed the middles of the known cities and districts separately, as Ptolemy did, ninety-four cities in all. The text does not name the book's author, and none is assigned here. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.32}.
