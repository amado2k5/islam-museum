## Overview
What is passed over in silence (*maskut 'anhu*) is what no text has mentioned either prohibiting or requiring. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

The hadith of Abu Tha'laba al-Khushani divides legal obligation into four kinds: obligations, which must not be neglected; limits, which must not be exceeded; prohibitions, which must not be violated; and things God passed over in silence out of mercy to people, not out of forgetfulness, so they are not to be searched for. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

A report from Abu al-Darda' says that what is passed over in silence is pardoned, and one from Salman as a *mawquf* (ending at a Companion) says that it is among what God has pardoned. The original position regarding what is passed over in silence is the absence of prohibition, as Ibn Rajab sets out. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}

It is based on the chapter on [[concept/halal-haram|the lawful and the unlawful]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam}
