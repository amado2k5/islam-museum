## Summary
Al-Battani teaches the reverse: whoever wants the arc of a known chord looks for the chord's like in the table, and where he finds it or what is nearest below it takes what faces it in the first row of numbers; then he subtracts the chord found from his own, multiplies the remainder by thirty minutes, divides by the difference between the found chord and the next one, and adds the result to the kept arc. He names this operation taqwis al-awtar. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.20}.

## Looking the chord up
He says whoever wants the arcs from these chords should look for the chord's like in the table, and where he finds it, or the nearest value below it, take what faces it in the first of the two rows of numbers, which is the arc wanted. The reading thus runs from chords to numbers after having run from numbers to chords. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.20}.

## Correction by thirty minutes
He then subtracts the chord found in the tables from the chord in hand, multiplies the remainder by thirty minutes, divides the product by the difference between the chord found and the one after it, adds the minutes and seconds that come out to the arc he kept, and the result is the arc of the half-chord he wanted. If he prefers he can see what share of the difference between the two chords the leftover is and take the same share of thirty minutes; the meaning is the same. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.20}.
