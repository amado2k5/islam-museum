## In Abd al-Rahman Mansour's programme 'Ahalina'
[[person/abd-al-rahman-mansour|Abd al-Rahman Mansour]] gives the last surviving episode of his programme [[work/ahaleena|'Ahalina']] to punishment in upbringing. Punishment in Islam, he holds, has an aim and a direction: it is not revenge on the child, nor harming him, nor applying a hadd to him; it is one of the many means of Islamic upbringing, joined with mercy and compassion and bound by the limits of the Sharia. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:02:00} He says some fathers strike ten blows in one place until the child swells or his arm breaks, and calls this a calamity. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:02:30}

He ranks punishments from the lightest to the heaviest. First come advice, guidance and reminding: he cites the boy whose hand wandered about the dish, whom the Prophet taught to say God's name, eat with his right hand and eat from what was in front of him, without harshness. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:03:30} Then turning away: he cites from Sahih al-Jami' that when the Prophet learned that someone of his household had lied he turned away from him until he repented, and suggests teaching the child the two rak'as of repentance. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:04:00} Then a stern rebuke, citing the Prophet's rebuke of [[person/hasan-ibn-ali|al-Hasan ibn Ali]] when he took a date from the charity dates. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:04:30} Then [[concept/hajr-fiqh|shunning]], for three days at most, for example when a child abandons prayer or uses foul language in adolescence. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:06:00} Then reprimand, which for him is severity of speech without insult, cursing or abuse. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:06:30} Then hanging the whip where the household can see it; he cites a hadith on this from Sahih al-Jami' and says the point is that the child be deterred by seeing it, not beaten with it. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:07:30} Children differ, he observes: a look is enough for one, turning away for another, and some are set right only by striking. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep12 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep12 00:02:30}

Striking he makes the last means, once all before it are exhausted. People, he says, are divided between supporters who give the father absolute power and opponents who forbid it, while the method of Islam is a middle way that allows it within the narrowest limits under rules of the Sharia: a light smack, joined with mercy. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:10:00} One of his rules is that a child is never struck before the age of ten, on the evidence of the hadith on teaching prayer at seven and striking for it at ten, which he repeats in other episodes. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep06 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep06 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep06 00:08:30} He separates "striking to discipline" from "striking in revenge": the first is a gentle touch on the child's hand with a reminder of God, the second a father emptying his anger on his child, and in his view most people who strike do so in revenge. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep06 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep06 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep06 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:08:30} He offers himself as an example: a teacher who threatened him as a boy left him unable to recite al-Fatiha in a Qur'an competition and made him hate school. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:13:30} At the end of the episode he promises another on how to strike and its rules, which is not in the archive. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:13:30}
