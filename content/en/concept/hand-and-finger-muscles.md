## Summary
Some muscles of the fingers lie in the hand and some in the forearm; gathered all in the hand they would be heavy with flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.70-71}.

## Extending and drawing down
All the finger-straightening muscles lie on the forearm, as do those that move the fingers down. Of the straighteners, one in the middle of the back of the forearm sends tendons to the four fingers; the tendons are round and strong and widen only on reaching the limb. Three muscles draw down: one sends two tendons to the little and ring fingers, one two tendons to the middle and index, and the third a tendon to the thumb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.71}.

## The benders
Some benders lie on the forearm and some inside the palm. Of the three on the forearm the noblest, the lowest, is buried and joined to the lower forearm bone, since its action is noblest and its place must be safest; its tendon divides into five, one to the inner side of each finger. The second, above it and smaller, sends tendons to the middle joints. The third is not for bending; its tendon spreads over the palm to give it feeling, to stop hair growing there and to support the palm in its work {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.71}.

## Muscles of the palm
The palm itself holds eighteen muscles in two rows: seven in the lower row, none for bending, five for lifting and two for lowering, and eleven in the upper, eight bending the first joint of the four fingers and three belonging to the thumb. Ibn Sina says only [[person/galen|Galen]] knew the muscles of the upper row {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.71-72}.
