## Overview
Hoarding (ihtikar), for al-Maraghi, is withholding goods to shrink supply, raise prices and let the hoarder dictate sales — the crisis-time "black market," its practitioner a diseased limb of the community {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 34}. He cites the hadith "whoever hoards is a sinner," while noting in the margin that some of the chapter's wording is weak {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 34}. From Ibn Hajar al-Haytami: the ruler may compel the hoarder to sell in necessity {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 36}; from Abu Yusuf: hoarding covers not only food but medicine, clothing, fuel and building materials {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 35}.
