## Summary
Is prayer valid when a translation of the Qur'an is recited instead of its Arabic wording? This is one of the questions the four Sunni schools of law discussed: the Hanafis report their imam Abu Hanifa's view and what he returned to, while the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis do not accept a translation in prayer. The page gives each school's view under its holders as it appears in their books and in the fatwa houses, and does not judge between them. It rests on a research summary supplied to the museum; the books and fatwas it points to were not opened.

## The question and its background
The question concerns the recitation of al-Fatiha and whatever else is recited in prayer: whether its translation, in Persian for example, stands in for its Arabic wording, whether the ruling differs between one who knows Arabic and one who does not, and whether the Arabic text may be recited and its translation added. The Hanafi books carry a root of the question: the summary says that Salman al-Farisi translated al-Fatiha into Persian for Persians who had newly become Muslim, to use until their tongues were at ease with Arabic, that al-Sarakhsi in al-Mabsut and al-Ayni in al-Bināya report it, and that Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya cites it in Fatwa 7281 {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-7281 on Fatwa 7281}. The history of the translations themselves is in [[concept/awail-tarjamat-al-quran|The first translations of the Qur'an]].

## The Hanafis
Al-Kasani in Bada'i al-Sana'i reports that Abu Hanifa first held reciting the Persian translation in prayer valid even for one able to recite Arabic, while his two companions Abu Yusuf and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Shaybani confined validity to one unable to recite Arabic {cite:ablibrary-badai-sanai-kasani-p113 p.113}. Ibn Abidin in Radd al-Muhtar reports that later Hanafi authorities transmit Abu Hanifa's retraction to his companions' view, and that one who recites the Arabic and then adds its translation inside prayer invalidates the prayer {cite:islamweb-fatwa-111542 on Radd al-Muhtar 1/484}. Dar al-Ifta al-Misriyya also gives the Hanafi view in its Fatwa 3106 on translating the Qur'an inside prayer {cite:dar-alifta-fatwa-3106 on Fatwa 3106}.

## The Malikis
The IIUCS Journal article and IslamWeb Fatwa 35811 quote al-Dasuqi's Hashiya on the Sharh al-Kabir: reciting a translation is not valid in prayer in any case, whether the worshipper is able in Arabic or unable. The one unable follows an imam who recites in Arabic; if no imam is available, al-Fatiha is waived and he occupies himself with Arabic dhikr and tasbih {cite:banglajol-iiucs-arabic-prayer-article-2681 on al-Dasuqi} {cite:islamweb-fatwa-35811 on al-Dasuqi}.

## The Shafi'is
Al-Nawawi in al-Majmu' Sharh al-Muhadhdhab, vol. 3, pp. 326-327 and 342-343, states that reciting in other than Arabic is not valid in prayer or outside it, whether the reciter is able in Arabic or not, and that whoever substitutes a translation for the Qur'an invalidates his prayer {cite:islamweb-majmu-nawawi-v3-quran-non-arabic p.326-327, 342-343}.

## The Hanbalis
Ibn Qudama in al-Mughni, vol. 1, p. 352, states that recitation in translation does not suffice in prayer whether the worshipper recites Arabic well or not, and that an Arabic synonym may not be put in place of the wording either {cite:ketabonline-mughni-ibn-qudama-v1-p352 p.352} {cite:islamweb-fatwa-111542 on the Hanbali position}.

## Where the views agree and differ
According to these sources the four schools agree that a translation is not the Qur'an itself and that one unable in Arabic is a special case: the Hanafis, in the view of Abu Hanifa's two companions and his own retraction, make translation a temporary allowance for the unable, while the Malikis give such a person following an Arabic-reciting imam and then dhikr instead. They differ over the one able in Arabic only with the early Hanafi position. Whether a translation is the Qur'an is taken up in [[concept/hal-al-tarjama-hiya-al-quran|Is a translation the Qur'an?]], and the duty to learn Arabic in [[concept/wujub-taallum-al-arabiyya-lil-ibada|The duty to learn Arabic for worship]].
