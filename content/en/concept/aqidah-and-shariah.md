## Summary
Mahmud Shaltut presents Islam as a division into two branches: belief ('aqida), the theoretical side that must be believed first, and law (shari'a), the systems God laid down for the human being's relationship with his Lord, with his fellow humans, with the cosmos and with life. In this presentation, belief is the foundation on which the law is built. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 31}

## In the book
Shaltut makes the Qur'an the common source of both branches. He holds that law without belief lacks the moral force that carries a person to obey, and that whoever takes up one of the two and neglects the other is not a Muslim in the judgement of Islam. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 33} {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 34}

## Note
This is the author's own presentation of his view in describing contemporary Sunni doctrine, not a final ruling binding on every school. {cite:al-islam-aqida-wa-shariah-azhar p. 31}
