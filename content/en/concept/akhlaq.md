Akhlaq is the settled disposition of the soul from which actions issue easily, without deliberation: if good actions issue from it the character is good, and if bad ones issue it is bad — the definition given by Ibn al-Qayyim and those who followed him in the ethics books {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

Islam made its perfection the purpose of the prophetic mission itself. The Prophet said, "I was sent to perfect noble character," and "the most complete of believers in faith are the best of them in character" {cite:riyad-al-salihin}, and God described him as being upon a tremendous character {cite:tanzil-quran}.

Its doors, as the Qur'an and the Sunnah detail them: truthfulness, trustworthiness and faithfulness to covenant; patience and gratitude; humility, modesty, forbearance and pardon; sincerity, altruism and generosity; kindness to parents, kinship ties and good neighbourliness; withholding harm from people and doing them good. Opposite them stand the vices: lying, treachery, pride, ostentation, envy, miserliness, backbiting, slander, mockery, anger, injustice, disobedience to parents, severing kinship, hypocrisy and cheating {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:riyad-al-salihin}.
