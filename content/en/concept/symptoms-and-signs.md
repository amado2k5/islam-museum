## Overview
For Ibn Sina a symptom is that which follows the state of disease and is abnormal — it may oppose the normal, like the pain of colic, or not {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.103}. Symptoms and signs are treated in the third lesson of the second book — eleven chapters and two discourses opening with general remarks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}. As he relays from Galen, a sign indicates something present — profiting the patient; or past — profiting the physician in establishing what came before; or future — profiting both {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.154}.
