## Definition
In the lexicon summ is a deadly substance, plural sumum or sammam, and "poisoned" means containing poison; to poison food is to put poison in it. The technical sense does not depart from the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9828}

## Antidote and remedy
The tiryaq is the antidote to poisons; a hadith speaks of the Aliyya date as a cure, and the word is applied to any medicine or electuary used to repel poison. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9828} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829} A remedy (dawa') is what a sick person is treated with by suitable means. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9828}

## Taking poison
By agreement of the jurists it is forbidden to take a poison that kills without need, on the basis of the verses in al-Baqara (195) and al-Nisa' (29). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9828}

## Purity of poison
The Hanbalis state without distinction that poison is impure, whether solid or not, and whether it comes from plants that are otherwise pure and are forbidden only for harm, or from snakes, scorpions and other venomous creatures. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9828}
The Shafi'is distinguish between tree and plant poisons forbidden only for harm to health, and substances mixed with impurity or derived from an impure source, such as the venom of snakes and scorpions. They say prayer is invalidated by a snake's bite, since its poison shows at the bite, while the scorpion's sting does not invalidate it in their preferred view, since its sting penetrates into the flesh. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829}
The Malikis hold that the venom of snakes, scorpions and other venomous creatures is pure, like the saliva of any living creature, provided it does not carry impurity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829} The Hanafis' phrasing implies that their saliva is impure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829} The full treatment is under impurities. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829}

## Sale of poison
By agreement it is not lawful to sell a deadly poison that has no permitted benefit, or that is mixed with impurity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829}
Where it has a lawful benefit and no impurity, the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is allow its sale, whether it comes from herbs or from snakes. The Hanbalis distinguish: they forbid the sale of snake venom because it has no permitted benefit; poison of herbs is not sold if it has no use or if a small amount kills, but is sold if it has use such as medicine. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9829} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9830} See the entry on sale. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9830}

## Treatment with poison
Treatment with poison is allowed even by those who hold it impure, when safety from its harm is likely and benefit is hoped, to avoid the lesser of two harms, on condition that a trustworthy Muslim physician reports it or the treated person knows it, and that no substitute is available. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9830}

## Killing by poison
Most jurists hold that if a poisoned meal is given to a non-discerning child or a mentally ill person and he dies, retaliation is due from the one who served it, provided he knew the poison would generally kill, whether or not he told him it was poisoned. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9830}
If a sane adult is coerced into eating poisoned food that he did not know was poisoned, retaliation is due on the coercer; if he knew, there is none, as when he is forced to kill himself. If the poison is poured into his throat, retaliation is due even on an adult, since he was compelled and had no choice. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9830} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9831}
If a poisoned meal is served to a sane adult who eats it and dies, there is no retaliation or blood-money by agreement if he knew; if he did not know, the jurists differ. The Shafi'is say there is blood-money of semi-intentional killing and in one view retaliation for the deception; the Malikis and Hanbalis require retaliation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9831}
If someone slips poison into the food of a discerning or adult person who usually eats it, and that person eats it unknowingly, semi-intentional blood-money is due. If someone puts poison into his own food and another who habitually enters eats it, the loss is void. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9831}
If one treats a wound from a guaranteed injury with a deadly poison and dies, the wounder bears no retaliation for the death nor blood-money, but owes the wound by retaliation or arsh. The Hanafis say there is no retaliation for death by poison at all: one who serves poisoned food unknowingly is not retaliated against, but is disciplined; if poison is poured or forced, blood-money is due from the offender's kin as semi-intentional. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9831}
