## What the pain, ripening and rheum tell
The pains arising in the eye are of two kinds: a biting eating pain and a distending one; the biting points to corruption and sharpness in the matter's quality, the distending to its quantity or to wind; rheum pointing to ripening or to thick matter — the kind that attends ripening, touches the eye a little at first and clears quickly is the laudable; small grains are a lesser sign of good, smallness pointing to slow ripening; when the lids begin sticking together ripeness has come, just as a watery flow means it is still early {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.168} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.169}

## Telling the matter's path
Takaddur is known by its lightness, its cause and the absence of an inceptive swelling; what is ramad from a catarrh descending from brain to eye by the outer membrane veiling the head shows a distended brow, running outer veins, swelling hurrying to the lid and redness and pulsation in the brow; if from the inner membranes none of that shows and instead there is sneezing and itch in mouth and nose; if with the stomach's sharing, as stated above {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.169}

## Signs of the four kinds and the windy
Sanguineous ramad is told by the eye's colour, the running vessel, pulsing temples and the rest of blood's signs around the brain; it does not weep much but runs with rheum and sticks shut in sleep; the choleric is told by sharper pricking, a more intense burning pain, less redness, a thin hot tear that may ulcerate or may be absent like the sanguineous, and no sticking in sleep; of this kind is the humra striking the eye — one of the malign tempers — which may cauterise and ulcerate the eye like a roaming fly, and there is a sharply itching choleric kind with little redness and rheum, no swelling or flow worth mention, from a little sharp matter; the phlegmy is told by strong heaviness, little heat, faint redness — the white being sovereign — rheum, sticking in sleep, irritation, the face and complexion sharing, and nausea if it began from the stomach; phlegmy ramad may swell the conjunctiva over the black like a tent of swelling, yet without tears, only rheum; the melancholic shows heaviness with gloom, dryness, persistence and little sticking; the windy carries distension only, without heaviness or flow, and the distension may bring redness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.169}
