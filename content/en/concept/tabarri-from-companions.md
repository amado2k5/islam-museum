## Summary
Al-Sallabi presents passages from Twelver Shia books indicating, in his presentation, that they declared most of the Companions unbelievers and mandated disavowal of the first three caliphs, Mu'awiyah, and the Mothers of the Believers. In return, he presents Sunni proofs for the uprightness of the Companions and the prohibition of reviling them, along with statements attributed to Ali and his sons that contradict that stance. This material presents what is in those books and the author's response, not a museum judgment between the two sides. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 238}

## Excommunication (Takfir) as Presented by the Author
The author quotes Al-Qadi Abd al-Jabbar as saying that the Imamiyyah held that the Twelve Imams' leadership was established by an explicit text whose denier is an unbeliever, and thus they declared the Prophet's Companions unbelievers. He quotes Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi that most of the Imamiyyah claimed the Companions apostatized after the Prophet except Ali, his two sons, and a small group. He quotes Ibn Taymiyyah that the Rafidah say the Emigrants (Muhajirun) and Helpers (Ansar) concealed the text and thus became unbelievers except for a small number. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 238}

The author cites from Al-Kulayni a report from Al-Fudayl ibn Yasar on the authority of Abu Ja'far: "All the people became people of ignorance (jahiliyyah) except four: Ali, Al-Miqdad, Salman, and Abu Dharr," and that the narrator asked about Ammar, to which he replied: if you mean those untouched by anything, it is these three. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 239}

The author states that the same books pass judgment of apostasy on members of the Prophet's household and his wives, that Ibn Abbas was described as foolish in certain reports, and that Al-Kulayni explicitly stated that whoever does not believe in the Twelve Imams is an unbeliever even if he is an Alid Fatimid. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 239}

## Slandering Aisha and Condemning Rulers, Judges, and Scholars
The author notes that Tafsir al-Qummi contains slander against Aisha; he quotes Ibn Kathir that scholars unanimously agreed that whoever reviles her with what God declared her innocent of is an unbeliever for opposing the Quran, and Al-Qurtubi that whoever denies what God cleared her of denies God. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 240}

The author cites from Al-Kafi that every government other than that of the Imamiyyah is invalid, that its holders are tyrants (tawaghit), and that judges sought judgment from false authority. He states that Al-Kulayni transmitted from Abu Ja'far that Abu Hanifah and Sufyan al-Thawri were "those who bar from God's religion," that Al-Kulayni transmitted the excommunication of scholars such as Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, Malik, Al-Shafi'i, and Ahmad, and that Ibn Taymiyyah explained the stance of these Shia toward the early generations (salaf). {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 241} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 242} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 243}

The author mentions rulings regarding the people of Medina, Mecca, Egypt, and Syria found in Al-Kafi and Bihar al-Anwar, considering them rulings in need of critique. He quotes Abu Zur'ah al-Razi that whoever disparages any of the Prophet's Companions is a heretic (zindiq), because the Book and the Sunnah were transmitted to us only by the Companions. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 350}

## What Sunni Scholars Say About Uprightness and Prohibition
The author presents the Sunni consensus on the uprightness ('adalah) of the Companions, listing his proofs from God's words "You are the best nation" and "The foremost, the first," and from the hadith "Do not revile my Companions." He quotes Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, Ibn Abd al-Barr, Ibn al-Salah, Al-Nawawi, Al-Iraqi, Ibn Hajar, and Ibn Kathir as reporting consensus on the uprightness of the Companions, including those involved in the civil strife, whom consensus deems upright out of good faith. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 343} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 344} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 345} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 346}

The author quotes Malik that whoever hates any of the Prophet's Companions or harbors malice toward them in his heart has no right to the Muslims' fay' (state revenue); he mentions that Ibn Umar disavowed the Qadariyyah, and quotes Ahmad and Abu Zur'ah al-Razi that whoever disparages any of the Companions is suspect or a heretic. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 348} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 350}

## The Hadith of the Basin (Al-Hawd)
The author presents the hadith "People will be driven away from the Basin, and it will be said: they turned back on their heels," noting that hadith scholars applied it to the Bedouin apostates after the Prophet, to innovators and sinners, and to the hypocrites, and that it does not include Companions who fought the apostates. He cites the Prophet's words "I do not see any of them escaping except like straying camels," arguing that the pronoun refers to the people being driven away, not to all the Companions. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 338} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 339} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 341}

## Statements of Ali and His Sons as Narrated by the Author
The author narrates from Ali that he said: "I saw the Companions of Muhammad, and I see no one resembling them," describing their worship, and that he said Abu Bakr was "the trustee of the grateful." He narrates that Ali ibn al-Husayn prayed for the Companions in his prayer. He narrates from Al-Hasan that when asked about the Shia claiming Ali would be resurrected before the Resurrection, he said: "They lied; by God, these are not Shia; had we known he would be resurrected, we would not have married off his wives." He narrates from Muhammad al-Baqir that he told Jabir al-Ju'fi he disavowed people who revile Abu Bakr and Umar, and from Ja'far al-Sadiq the statement "May God disown whoever disowns Abu Bakr and Umar," which Al-Dhahabi reports as widely transmitted from Al-Sadiq. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 350} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 374} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 375} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 376}

## Attributed Prayers of Cursing
The author mentions that Shia books contain a supplication called "Du'a Sanamay Quraysh" (the Supplication of the Two Idols of Quraysh) attributed to Ali that asks for the cursing of Abu Bakr and Umar, that it is encouraged among them, and that commentaries on it have reached ten in number. He quotes Al-Khomeini in Kashf al-Asrar saying they have no concern with the two elders (Abu Bakr and Umar) and the violations they committed. The author presents reports attributed to Zayn al-Abidin on the merit of cursing al-jibt and al-taghut, and states that some among the Imamiyyah seek closeness to God by cursing the two elders. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 331} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 332} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 333}

The author presents the call of some contemporary Shia scholars for rapprochement, noting that their stances were not met by Shia scholars in Iraq and Iran with changes to the books containing such attacks. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 380} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 381} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 383}

## What Is Not Included Here
The response of contemporary Shia scholars to what the author attributed to them is not quoted here; it is absent from the book and requires a direct Shia source. Material concerning Mu'awiyah and the remaining historical reports on the civil strife appear in the first part of the book and the second part itself and were not repeated here.
