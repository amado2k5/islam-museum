## Its kinds
It has three kinds: first, the lid contracts and does not cover the white — either congenital or from a cut hitting the lid — and an eye like it is called the hare-eye. Second, the middle kind: not covering some of the white, called shortness of the lid, with the first's cause but less. Third: the upper lid not closing on the lower — from a gland, or from superfluous flesh that grew at the start, or from a convulsion occurring to the lid from an ulcer that healed on it and does not let the upper lid close on the lower; all of that may be from convulsion of the lid-closing muscles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.195}

## The treatment
The kind from shortness of the lid is treated by slitting without suturing, healing after skin flesh grows — this for the first and second kinds, more and less; the kind from a gland or superfluous flesh is taken with iron, and likewise the kind from the remainder of an ulcer that healed shortening the lid — treated with iron, opened and healed; the convulsion kind is treated as convulsion in its two kinds ⁦(the iron-work carried as the book's description, not as procedure)⁩ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.195} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.196}
