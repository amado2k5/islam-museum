## Summary
Khanq is lexically squeezing the throat until death; the jurists use it for any means: a cord, the hands, or stopping the mouth and nose. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7884} Hunting and slaughter: by agreement an animal that is to be slaughtered is not lawful when strangled; the verse on the strangled animal in al-Ma'ida is cited; game dead from strangling is not lawful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7884}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7885} Homicide: the majority (Malikis, Shafiis, Hanbalis and the two companions) impose retaliation for an intentional strangling, since it usually kills; Abu Hanifa imposes no retaliation for strangling as it is semi-intentional, and holds that one who strangles repeatedly may be killed by discretion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7885} Oaths: the Hanafis and Hanbalis count striking a wife as including strangling, so an oath not to strike her is broken by strangling; the Shafiis do not count it as striking by custom. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7885}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7886}
