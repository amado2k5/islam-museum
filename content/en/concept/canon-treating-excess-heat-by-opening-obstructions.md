## Summary
Ibn Sina holds that whoever treats excess heat by opening obstructions should avoid excessive cooling, lest the blockage harden further, beginning with what cleanses, then a cooling cleanser such as barley water and chicory water, then a moderate remedy, then one with slight warmth, since the benefit of opening is greater than the harm of heating {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.272}.

## Gentleness and the harm of quenching
He should proceed gently, treating first with what cleanses; if a cooling cleanser such as barley water or chicory water suffices, well and good; if not, with a moderate remedy; if not, with one that has slight warmth, for the benefit of its opening, in cooling, is greater than the harm of its heating, which is easily quenched after the opening. Excessive quenching may prevent the ripening of the sharp humours; strong quenching lowers the strength, above all one already weakened by illness, and may bring on other diseases, either from a simple cold bad temper or with matters opposed to those it set right {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.272}.
