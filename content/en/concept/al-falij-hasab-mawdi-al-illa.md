## The seats of the lesion
Know that if the lesion and the matter making the palsy is in one side of the brain's ventricles it covers the whole half of the body with the half of the face, and likewise if in the passages of the one side; were it in both ventricle sides or their passages it would be apoplexy; if at the root of the spinal cord the whole body is palsied without the face's organs, and numbness may join it in what the neck's sense reaches; if in one side of the cord's root that whole side relaxes without the face; if descending from the root, wholly or in a side, what follows that nerve among the organs relaxes; if not of the cord but of a nerve, what belongs to that nerve relaxes — whether in all of it, half or part — relaxing what moves by that diseased nerve, through matter, loosening of texture or swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.138}

## Palsy as crisis of colic
Some palsy comes as the crisis of colic, and sensation often remains with it, for the matter lies in the nerves of motion not of sense; some of the ancients said colic was general for some years and killed most, and whoever escaped escaped with a chronic palsy, as though nature shook off that matter going to the intestines and turned it outward, too gross to pass by sweat, so it settled in the nerves and made palsy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.138}

## Remnants, seasons and lands
Most of this happens with sensation preserved, when nature cannot complete the evacuation and remnants of matter stay around the brain, then nature drives it by a drive of weight, not a drive of full evacuation, producing palsy and the like; palsy most often befalls in the deep cold of winter, may befall in spring from the motion of repletion, and in the southern lands for whoever reaches fifty years or so, by catarrhs descending from their heads, for the southern temperament much fills the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.138}

## Pulse, urine and the two sides
The palsied man's pulse is weak, slow and varying; if the illness exhausts the power the pulse weakens, quickens and unordered dropped beats occur; the urine is mostly white, sometimes very watery from the liver's weakness at separating blood from wateriness, or the vessels' weakness at drawing blood, or for a pain with it, or another concomitant disease; it may happen that the sound side burns as if in fire while the palsied side is cold as snow, the two sides' pulses differing, and it may go so far that the eye of that side shrinks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.138} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.139}
