## Takaddur and its outside causes
Ramad has a true kind and one resembling it called takaddur, takhatthur and khathr — a heating, moistening affection arising from outside causes that stir and redden it: the sun, a burning brand, burning one-day fever, dust, smoke, sometimes cold by its contracting, a blow by its stirring, the violent wind by its slap; all that is a light stirring attending the cause and not lingering after it, and left alone it passes when the cause passes; in Greek it is called tartasis, but if a bodily or incidental cause seconds the first it can rage and turn into a true manifest swelling, as one-day fevers turn into other fevers, and at the start of that turn it is called in Greek lequoikima {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.167}

## True ramad and the itch kind
One kind of ramad follows the itch in the eye, its cause being a scratch in the eye; at first it runs takaddur's course, and its treatment is possible only after scratching the itch; ramad in general is a swelling in the conjunctiva — either a simple swelling within bounds in the running of vessels, discharge and pain, or a great one beyond bounds, in which the white mounts over the pupil, covers it and prevents closing: chemosis, known here as wardinj; it often befalls boys for the abundance of their matter and the weakness of their eyes, and it is not from hot matter alone but phlegmy and melancholic too {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.167}

## Its humours and whence the matter pours
Since true ramad is a swelling in the conjunctiva and every swelling is from blood, yellow bile, phlegm, black bile or wind, ramad's cause is never outside these; the swelling humour may be bred in the conjunctiva or come to it from the brain as a catarrh by way of the outer membrane veiling the head or the inner membranes; sometimes the arteries pour their superfluities into it; and the matter may come from the other organs, especially when a distemper has weakened the eye and made it receptive of lesions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.167}
