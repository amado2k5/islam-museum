## Summary
As a term the Dahriyya are a group of disbelievers who held that time is eternal and attributed events to it, denying the existence of a Creator who chooses, and denying the Hereafter, resurrection and recompense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8239}
The Qur'an reports the view of this group in the surah of al-Jathiya; al-Razi explains that they attributed life and death to the influence of natures, with no need for a chosen agent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8239}

## Related terms
Most jurists define the zindiq as one who conceals disbelief and shows Islam; some say he is one who follows no religion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8239}
The mulhid attacks religion while claiming Islam, or interprets its necessities to suit his desires. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8239}
The hypocrite (munafiq) hides disbelief in his heart and shows Islam in speech. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8239}
The apostate (murtadd) turns back from Islam by uttering a word of disbelief or doing an act that implies it, after believing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8240}
All of these share disbelief with the Dahri. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8240}

## Ruling
An original disbeliever of this kind, who never embraced Islam, is a harbi if he lives in the abode of war, a musta'min if he lives in the abode of Islam under a temporary safe-conduct, and a dhimmi if he lives there under a lasting contract of protection; each has its own entry. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8240}
A Muslim who then declares the eternity of time and denies that events are attributed to the chosen Creator is an apostate, and his ruling is treated under apostasy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8240}
