## Nature and selection
Nature: a metallic substance, green, yellow or red. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Choice: the best is the ground, sulphur-smelling kind; the finest is the yellow Armenian kind, golden and in thin plates like yellow mica. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Temperament: hot in the third degree and dry in the second. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}

## Actions
Actions: all of it rots and stings; the red is better than the qaldqiyun. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it depilates, and with the riteinaj it treats the alopecia the book calls the fox's disease. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Wounds and ulcers: it is placed with fat on wounds. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Swellings and pustules: with fat and oil it is used for itch and for moist and rotting favus, and it burns the skin. It is smeared with myrrh for lice and marks of blood, and with pitch for marks of the nails, and it is sometimes used with pitch for lice. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Head organs: the cerate made from it, especially from the red, helps gangrene in the nose and mouth and their sores. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Respiratory organs: given to those with suppuration, with honey water; fumigated with riteinaj for chronic cough and spitting of pus, and it enters remedies for asthma. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Excretory organs: smeared with rose oil for pustules and for haemorrhoids of the anus. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
Poisons: the sublimated form is deadly. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.460}
