## Summary
In the sixth chapter Ibn Sina shows what the retentive causes alone bring about in the greatness and smallness of the pulse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.

## Greatness
When the instrument is yielding through softness, the power strong and the need for cooling severe, the pulse is great. Need is the most helpful of the three in this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.

## Smallness and its differences
If the power is weak, smallness of the pulse follows without fail, and if the instrument is also hard and the need slight, it is smaller still. Hardness too produces smallness, but smallness from hardness is told apart from smallness from weakness in being hard, not weak, and not excessively short and low. Little need also produces smallness, but without weakness, since power without need takes little from the moderate {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.174}.
