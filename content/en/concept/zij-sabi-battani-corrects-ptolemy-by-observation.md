## Summary
In the foreword al-Battani says he long studied the books on planetary motions, the errors of their authors, and what had shifted with the obliquity of the zodiac, the lengths of the years, the times of the seasons and the conjunctions of the two luminaries, and that he tried to correct these on the school of Ptolemy in the Almagest, following his lead; then he fixed what he found by observation and by calculating the two eclipses and corrected the motions and places of the planets on the zodiac. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11}.

## Following Ptolemy
He says he followed what [[person/batlamus|Ptolemy]] laid down in the [[work/al-majisti|Almagest]], because Ptolemy had covered the matter from all sides and shown its causes by geometrical and numerical proof whose soundness cannot be turned aside. It is al-Battani's own acknowledgement that the Almagest is the root and reference of his craft, and that his work is a correction from inside Ptolemy's school and not a break with it. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11}.

## The door left open to later correction
Al-Battani reports that Ptolemy himself said later observers might correct his observations as he had corrected Hipparchus and his peers, since the craft is lofty and heavenly and is grasped only approximately. The statement keeps the door open to correction by later observations, and it is al-Battani's warrant for setting his new observations in place of older ones where they differ. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11}.

## What he adds
He says he composed a book making clear what was obscure, opening what was shut and explaining what was difficult in the roots of this science and what had strayed in its branches, smoothing the road of guidance for those who come after him, correcting in it the planets' motions and places on the zodiac belt by observation and by calculating the two eclipses, and adding the further operations that are needed. This is his own description of the project of [[work/al-zij-al-sabi|al-Zij al-Sabi]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.11}.
