## Summary
Mubalagha, in lexicon, is striving and not falling short, or extravagance {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13823}. The jurists agree that thoroughness in rinsing the mouth and nose is sunna for one who is not fasting; for the fasting person it is disliked, on the hadith of Laqit ibn Sabra: be thorough in snuffing water unless you are fasting {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13823}. Most jurists recommend washing more than the obligatory parts in ablution, on the report of Abu Hurayra and the glowing faces and limbs of the community; the Hanbalis mean by thoroughness rubbing the places where water does not settle, the Malikis hold rubbing obligatory {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13823-13824}. Most recommend rubbing the heels, and extravagance in washing is disliked {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13824-13825}. The Malikis and Shafi'is recommend raising the voice in the call to prayer without strain; the Hanafis call it sunna and the Hanbalis make it a pillar, raised as far as one can {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13825}. On praise, al-Nawawi says the prohibition applies to exaggeration and to someone whom praise may corrupt; praise is not forbidden for a person of settled faith and sense {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13825}.
