## Overview
The science of computing star positions, the zodiac sphere and the stations and motions of the planets. The Sabian Zij defines its subject as the computation of the stars, the zodiac sphere and the stations of the planets and the like, covering the sphere's graduation, chord computations, inclination, rising-times, latitudes, shadow, altitude and distances {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.1} {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.5}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam gives the Arabic name of the subject as ilm al-hay'a and says astronomy in the Muslim world combined Greek, Persian and Indian contributions and rested on the system of Ptolemy of the second century, which it improved markedly. In the thirteenth century [[person/nasir-al-din-al-tusi|Nasir al-Din al-Tusi]] devised a geometric device now known as the Tusi couple, which let astronomers explain the planets' observed motions while keeping to uniform circular motion. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e233}

In the fourteenth century Ibn al-Shatir, a mosque timekeeper in Damascus, refined al-Tusi's work further and built lunar and Mercury models that turn up again in the work of Copernicus two centuries afterwards. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e233} See [[person/ibn-al-shatir|Ibn al-Shatir]].
