## Overview
In Ibn Rajab's commentary, envy (*hasad*) is the wish for another's blessing to be removed, or dislike of his excellence. It is the first of the four prohibitions in the thirty-fifth hadith of the Forty: "Do not envy one another, do not inflate prices against one another, do not hate one another, and do not turn away from one another." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

## Its place in the hadith
Ibn Rajab holds that these prohibitions regulate the side of the heart in the brotherhood of Islam, and that they precede the prohibitions concerning dealings. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

Envy is linked to *najash*, which is raising the price of a commodity without intending to buy it, in order to deceive, and to hatred and turning away from one another. All of these are diseases of the heart that produce the severing of brotherhood. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

The hadith pairs them with belittling a fellow Muslim, and it counts that as enough evil for its possessor. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

Envy belongs to the chapter on [[concept/khuluq|character]] in the museum's classification.

## Examples in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lesson
The Sheikh gives examples of the wish and the comparison that trouble the heart over blessings: a person says he has a righteous child while another has none, or a healthy child while another's child is ill (needs_source: the classification of these examples as envy is not clear in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:29:30}

## Envy in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episode 5)
The Sheikh says that patience with those around you, including relatives who envy you, is patience that is needed (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:45:00} He holds that envy is broken by doing good to the one who envies you and increasing kindness to him (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:45:30}
He mentions seeking refuge from the whisperings of Satan, and repelling evil with what is better (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:46:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:47:00}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)

The Sheikh contrasts the emotions of this world with those of the afterlife. He says a man may see his cousin driving an expensive car and, if he is content, accept his own lot and thank God for his health, while in someone else's heart sensitivity and envy may stir, and he counts all this among the emotions of this world {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:03:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 6)

The Sheikh jokes that if he became a manager his first decision would be to jail the employee who informs on colleagues to the boss, and then adds that the universe is not in a person's hands {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:24:30}. In the same jest he imagines the air tanks placed in his hands so that he rations air among his family and relatives, with the mother-in-law mentioned at the end of the scene, and then stresses that sustenance comes from God's bounty alone {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep06 00:25:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that many people spend their lives seeking money and rank, and in resentment and envy of others, instead of asking what they have given to God's cause {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep09 00:13:30}. He says that the story of Qarun repeats throughout history, since people today look at the world's billionaires and wish for what they have been given {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:15:00}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme al-Munjiyat
Omar Abdelkafy says that the envier is not content with God's apportioning, that envy brings two calamities on the one who harbours it, and cites the verses 'Say to the one who spends the night envying me'. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep03 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep03 00:20:30}

## From the series Qissa wa 'Ibar
In the seventh episode, at Qarun's people wishing for the like of what he had, Omar Abdelkafy distinguishes envy from ghibta: to wish you had the like of someone's wealth, knowledge or character is ghibta, which a believer may feel; to wish the blessing would leave him and come to you, or simply leave him, is envy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:08:00} He quotes verses saying that the envier has been ill-mannered with God by not accepting what He gave, and says that the envious never lead, and grow more distressed whenever another's blessing grows. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy silsilat-qissa-wa-ebra/ep07 00:08:30}
