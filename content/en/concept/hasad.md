## Overview
In Ibn Rajab's commentary, envy (*hasad*) is the wish for another's blessing to be removed, or dislike of his excellence. It is the first of the four prohibitions in the thirty-fifth hadith of the Forty: "Do not envy one another, do not inflate prices against one another, do not hate one another, and do not turn away from one another." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

## Its place in the hadith
Ibn Rajab holds that these prohibitions regulate the side of the heart in the brotherhood of Islam, and that they precede the prohibitions concerning dealings. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

Envy is linked to *najash*, which is raising the price of a commodity without intending to buy it, in order to deceive, and to hatred and turning away from one another. All of these are diseases of the heart that produce the severing of brotherhood. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

The hadith pairs them with belittling a fellow Muslim, and it counts that as enough evil for its possessor. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

Envy belongs to the chapter on [[concept/khuluq|character]] in the museum's classification.

The Sheikh gives examples of the wish and the comparison that trouble the heart over blessings: a person says he has a righteous child while another has none, or a healthy child while another's child is ill (needs_source: the classification of these examples as envy is not clear in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:29:30}

## Envy in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episode 5)
The Sheikh says that patience with those around you, including relatives who envy you, is patience that is needed (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:45:00} He holds that envy is broken by doing good to the one who envies you and increasing kindness to him (his view). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:45:30}
