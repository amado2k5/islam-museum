## Nature and selection
Nature: the Chinese kind is like the pistachio, the Shahri kind like red castor dotted with black, and the Indian kind smaller than the Chinese and larger than the Shahri. Its kernel is greyish yellow; its property is that the kernel shrinks with time until it vanishes, and in its own land it lasts longer. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.441}
Choice: the Chinese kind is the best and strongest, then the Indian; the Shahri is poor, slow, causes distress and colic. The Chinese must be peeled with a blade and not touched with the lip, since it removes the lip's colour and causes a leukoderma-like mark; the fine tongue that comes out when peeled is discarded and the kernel taken. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.441}
Temperament: very hot. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}

## Actions
In excess it purges. It is not given alone; it is mixed with starch and saffron, and not with spurge or every sharp drug, but with things like turbith, ass milk, wormwood juice, indigo seed and turmeric. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}

## Uses by organ
Excretory organs: it purges the humours, black bile and phlegm lodged in the joints; the book says it is given only in a cold land and to a cold temperament. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.442}
