## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that a drug's strength follows the strength of the disease and that the stage of the disease changes treatment: a swelling at its start is met with what repels, at its end with what dissolves, and in between with both, and an acute disease has its regimen made moderately light at the start and thoroughly light toward the end {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.

## Size and stage
Someone whose accidental heat is strong needs a colder drug, and someone whose cold is strong a warmer one; if neither is strong, a weaker drug is enough. An acute disease has its regimen lightened moderately at the start and thoroughly toward the end, while a chronic one is not lightened at the start as at the end, though many chronic diseases other than fevers are dissolved by a light regimen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.

## Matter and air
If the patient has abundant, agitated matter, evacuation is done at the start without waiting for ripening; if the matter is moderate, it is ripened and then evacuated. The air is among the things whose condition most needs attention, whether it helps the drug or the disease {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.
