## Nature and selection
It is a moisture that clings to the hair and beard of goats that graze on a plant known as qasus: dew falls on it and dampness settles, mingling with the exudate of the plant's leaf; when the goat's hair is coated with it and holds it, it is taken from the goat and becomes ladhan. The pure kind clings to the beard and to hair raised from the ground; the poor kind clings to the hooves and was trodden with sand and earth. The editor's note calls it a gum of the flowers of the qustus plants {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.536} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Temperament
The best is the fatty, heavy Cyprian kind, sweet-smelling and yellowish, with no sand, which dissolves wholly in oil and leaves no dregs; the black, pitchy kind is not good. It is hot at the end of the first degree and dry in the second, and the kind from southern lands is hotter. Al-Khuzi said it is cold and astringent, and it is not so {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Actions and properties
Very fine, with slight astringency, ripening thick sticky moistures and dissolving them moderately; it has a drawing, warming power that opens the mouths of the vessels, and goes into remedies that soothe pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Cosmetic uses
It makes hair grow, thickens, multiplies and keeps it, especially with myrtle oil and with wine. It can help in early baldness and in falling and shedding of hair, but does not reach the point of curing alopecia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Wounds and ulcers
In the Kata Genos it is said that ladanum heals what is hard to heal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It is dropped with rose oil into a painful ear and goes into the treatment of headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Chest and breathing
It helps cough {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Excretory organs
Drunk with old wine it binds the bowels and brings on urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that as a pessary it dissolves swellings of the womb, and as a fumigation through a funnel it brings out the dead foetus and the afterbirth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.537}.
