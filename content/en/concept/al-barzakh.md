## Summary
The barrier between this world and the next where souls remain until they are raised — with the grave's bliss and punishment as the texts report.

## In the texts
[[passage/quran-23-100|a barzakh until raised]] · [[passage/quran-55-20|a barrier neither transgresses]]

## Note
In ar-Rahman it names the barrier between two seas — the word for every veiled partition.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam says the barzakh is mentioned briefly in the Qur'an and is understood in popular imagination as the period between a person's death and resurrection and as a barrier between the dead and the realm of the living. It adds that some traditions it calls questionable suggest that what the dead experience in the barzakh depends on how they behaved in life, and that modern Muslim thinkers give the barzakh less weight and turn instead to personal accountability and the Day of Judgment. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e306}

The two phrases, popular imagination and questionable traditions, are the Dictionary's own judgements, in a general reference for non-specialists; the Sunni account of the grave's bliss and punishment rests on the sources cited above, the Tahawiyya creed and Ibn Kathir's commentary. The modern view the entry credits to modern thinkers is reported here as the entry's attribution and is not adopted by this record.
