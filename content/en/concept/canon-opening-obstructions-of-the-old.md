## Summary
In the fourth chapter Ibn Sina describes treating the obstructions that come upon the old, the easiest being those from drinking wine, with pennyroyal and long pepper and pepper scattered on the wine, with garlic and onion for those used to them, and with theriac; they are then moistened by the bath, anointing and foods; honey drink is a safeguard against obstructions and joint pain, celery seed is added for the urinary organs, something stronger for stony obstruction, and remedies for the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.253}.

## Opening remedies
If obstructions come upon them, the easiest being those from drinking wine, they are opened with pennyroyal and long pepper, with pepper scattered on the wine, and if they are accustomed to garlic and onion they use them. Theriac benefits them much, above all when obstructions arise, as do the compounds called Athanasia and Ambrosia; but afterwards they must be moistened by bathing, anointing and foods such as meat broth with groats and barley {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.253}.

## Honey drink, the urinary organs and the lung
Using honey drink benefits them and secures them against obstructions and joint pain, provided that, when a blockage is felt in an organ or its readiness for one, something particular to the organ is added: celery seed and its root for the urinary organs; if the blockage is stony, a stronger decoction such as parsley; and if the obstructions are in the lung, maidenhair, hyssop, cassia bark and the like {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.253}.
