## Summary
In the lexicon, afw means removing or pardoning, and also abundance, going away and giving; the jurists use it mainly for dropping or overlooking a right {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11674}.

## Its ruling depends on the right
Where the right is purely the individual's, pardon is recommended; where it belongs to God, as in the fixed punishments, there is no pardon once the matter is before the judge {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11675}.

## In worship
The schools disagree on pardon for small amounts of impurity such as blood, pus and street mud, on the interval between the two thresholds of the camel and cattle tax, on some things that do not break the fast, and on forgotten wearing of sewn clothes or perfume during pilgrimage {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11676} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11679} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11680}.

## In contracts
Pardon of pre-emption, of a debt, and of a dower are treated; a wife may pardon all or part of her dower {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11680} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11681}.

## In retaliation
Pardon for retaliation is lawful and recommended. If one of several heirs pardons, retaliation falls and the others' shares become money. The schools differ on the effect of a pardon given after injury and before death, on who may pardon, and on settling for money {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11681} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11683} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11689} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11690}.

## In fixed and discretionary punishments
There is no pardon for the fixed punishments of adultery and theft; the jurists differ on slander. Pardon in discretionary punishment (ta'zir) is allowed where it concerns a person's right or the ruler's discretion {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11691} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11692} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11693}.

## Where to look further
Retaliation is treated under «qisas», fixed punishments under «hadd», and discretionary punishment under «ta'zir» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11675}.

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Ahl al-Janna'
[[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] gives the seventeenth episode of his programme [[work/ahl-al-jannah|'Ahl al-Janna']] to the forgiving and the vengeful: he cites a hadith he attributes to al-Bayhaqi's 'Shu'ab al-Iman' from Abu Hurayra, that Moses asked his Lord who of His creatures was dearest to Him and was told the one who forgives when he has power. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:04:00} He relates that the woman who poisoned the Prophet's sheep admitted she had wanted to kill him and that he forgave her (needs_source: the reports differ on her fate). {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:04:30} He quotes Aisha as saying no one ever wronged him and he took revenge for himself. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:05:30} He distinguishes the tolerant, who takes his right and then removes hatred from his heart; the pardoner, who forgoes his right; and the vengeful, who repays harm with more, and reads verses of Surat al-Shura on the three. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ahl-al-jannah/ep17 00:09:30}
