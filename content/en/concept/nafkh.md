## Summary
Nafkh, in the lexicon, is sending out air; one blows into a horn to make it sound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15852}
Nafas is wider than nafkh, and tajashshu (belching) is narrower. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15852} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15853}

## Rulings on blowing
Food and drink: most jurists dislike blowing into them, on the hadith forbidding breathing into a vessel; Abu Yusuf dislikes it only if it makes a sound; al-Mardawi sees no dislike when eating needs it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15852} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15853}
In prayer: deliberate blowing invalidates it in general. The Hanafis require it to be audible; the Malikis treat blowing from the mouth as invalidating; the sounder Shafi'i view requires two letters to appear with knowledge of the ruling; the Hanbalis dislike it without two letters and invalidate it with them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15853} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15854}
Ensoulment: from the time the spirit is breathed into the foetus it is a person for inheritance, the ghurra compensation and the prohibition of abortion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15854}
The trumpet: blowing it is a matter of belief, as the Qur'an states (cited by surah and aya only). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15854} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15855}
Instruments of entertainment: some jurists allow blowing them in particular cases, others forbid it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15855}
