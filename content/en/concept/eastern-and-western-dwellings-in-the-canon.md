## Summary
Ibn Sina compares the city open to the east with the city open to the west and prefers the first {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.

## Eastern
A city open to the east and set facing it is healthy and of good air; the sun rises on its people early in the day and their air clears, and then the sun leaves them with the air already clear, while gentle winds that the sun sends blow on them and follow it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.

## Western
A city open to the west and screened from the east is not reached by the sun for a while, and as soon as the sun reaches it, it begins to move away rather than nearer, so it neither refines nor dries its air but leaves it moist and thick. If it sends winds, it sends western ones and at night, so its rulings are those of lands of moist temperament, moderate heat and thickness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.

## On those who liken it to spring
He says that but for the density of the air it would resemble the nature of spring, yet it falls far short of the health of the air of eastern lands, so one should not heed whoever flatly asserts that its power is the power of spring; relative to other lands it is very good. A blameworthy point is that the sun reaches them only when it already dominates the heating of the region through its height, rising on them all at once after the cold of the night, and because of the moisture of their air their voices are hoarse, especially in autumn, from their catarrhs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.129}.
