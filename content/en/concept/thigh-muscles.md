## Summary
Ibn Sina holds that the greatest muscles of the thigh are those that straighten it and then those that bend it, since these two movements are its noblest actions, straightening being the better since standing is accomplished by it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.74}.

## The straighteners
Among the straightening muscles is the greatest of all the muscles of the body, arising from the pubic bone and the hip bone and winding around the whole thigh from inside and behind to the knee. Its fibres begin at different places and so its actions vary: those arising from below the pubic bone straighten with a lean inward, those arising from the hip bone straighten the thigh directly. Another covers the whole hip joint behind, with three heads and two ends, arising from the flank, the hip and the coccyx {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.74}.

## Bending, drawing in and turning
Among the benders is a straight muscle descending from two origins, one at the end of the back and one at the flank bone, one from the pubic bone, and one stretched obliquely beside it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.74-75}. For drawing inward there is a muscle growing from the pubic bone that runs so long it reaches the knee; for drawing outward two muscles; and for turning two, one from the outer side of the pubic bone and one from its inner side {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.75}.
