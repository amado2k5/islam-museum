## Nature
A thick wood brought from the edge of China, of three kinds, yellow, red, and another yellow inclining to white that some people call maqasiri, which has more fragrance than the two kinds named {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.637-638}

## Choice
Galen and Ibn Masawayh said the red is stronger, some said the yellow is stronger, and others said the maqasiri is better and stronger {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Temperament
Cold at the end of the second degree, dry in the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Actions and properties
It checks flux, especially the red {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Swellings and pustules
It dissolves hot swellings, especially the red, and is smeared on erysipelas, where it benefits {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Organs of the head
It benefits headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Organs of the chest
It benefits the palpitation that comes in fevers, applied and drunk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It benefits weakness of a hot stomach, applied and drunk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}

## Fevers
It benefits hot fevers, especially the white maqasiri {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.638}
