## Summary
Al-Battani fixes the length of the inhabited earth from the Inhabited Islands, six islands in the western ocean, to the farthest limit of China, and finds it twelve hours, half the circle of the earth. Its latitude he takes from the equator to the island of Thule, where the longest day reaches twenty hours: about sixty parts, a sixth of the earth's circle, so the inhabited part seen is about half a sixth of the earth, one part in twelve. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.29-30}.

## Length, from the Inhabited Islands to China
He says the ancients took the limits of the length from the Inhabited Islands, called the Khalidat, six islands in the western ocean, to the farthest settlement of China, and found it twelve hours; they concluded that when the Sun sets at the farthest settlement of China its first rising falls on the first of those islands, and when it sets there its first rising is on China's farthest limit, so the length of the inhabited world is half the earth's circle. He gives it in the miles used in surveying the earth, but the figure is not readable in the scan, so it is not given here. See [[place/china|China]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.29}.

## Latitude, from the equator to Thule
They then looked to the latitude and found the settled land runs north from the equator to the island of Thule, where the longest day is twenty hours. They say the equator crosses from east to west between India and Abyssinia on an island south of the equator of the day, and the line crossing it from north to south at the midpoint between the western islands and China is the dome of the earth. The latitude is about sixty parts, a sixth of the circle; multiplied by the half-length, the settled land shown comes to about half a sixth of the earth, one part in twelve. See [[place/india|India]] and [[place/abyssinia|Abyssinia]]. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.29-30}.
