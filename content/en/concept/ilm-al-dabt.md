## Summary
Once the matter had reached the vowelling that is now familiar, scholars regulated it and fixed its use so that no codex would differ from another; this was called 'dabt'. In Lisan al-Arab, as al-Sabt cites it, Abu Hatim says: I 'shakaltu' the book, I vowel it, so it is 'mashkul', if you bound it with vowels; in al-Qamus 'shakala' the book means he dotted it, as if he removed ambiguity from it; and then 'shakl' became current for what befalls letters of vowel or sukun. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 330} Al-Dani has two books on it, named in the notes of the Tahdhib: al-Muhkam fi Naqt al-Masahif and al-Naqt. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 331}

## Al-Dani on colours
Al-Suyuti relays from al-Dani: I do not permit dotting in black, for the change it makes to the form of the script, nor gathering various readings in one codex in different colours, for it is among the greatest confusion and alteration of what was written; I think the vowels, tanwin, doubling, sukun and stretch should be in red, and hamzas in yellow. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249} For him dabt does not change the first script of the codex but sits over it in a contrasting colour, so that the original is separated from the mark.

## Current rules of dabt
Al-Suyuti sums up al-Khalil's marking: fatha a slanted stroke above the letter, kasra below, damma a small waw above, tanwin a repeated mark, set over it before a throat letter when clear and otherwise in succession; a dropped alif and what stands for it are written in place in red, the dropped hamza likewise; a red iqlab sign goes on nun and tanwin before ba' and a sukun before throat letters; they are left bare at assimilation and concealment; every quiescent letter is marked; and the assimilated letter is left bare with the next doubled. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2250} See [[concept/shakl-al-khalil|al-Khalil's vowel signs]].

## Opinions on placing the marks
Ibn Mujahid said, as al-Suyuti relays: nothing should be vowelled except what is ambiguous. Al-Nawawi said: dotting and vowelling the codex is recommended, for it protects it from error and corruption. Al-Jurjani of the Shafi'is, in al-Shafi, said: writing an explanation of the words of the Qur'an between its lines is among the blameworthy. Al-Bayhaqi said: among the etiquette of the Qur'an is that it be honoured, written spaced in the best hand, not small, its letters not crammed, and nothing mixed with it that is not part of it, such as the number of verses, the prostrations, tens, stops, differences of reading and the meanings of verses. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2248} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2249} These opinions belong to the dispute over [[concept/tajrid-al-mushaf|keeping the codex bare]].

## Dabt does not replace direct teaching
Al-Sabt points out that however its spelling and marking are arranged, the codex cannot tell the reader how to deliver, for example, the openings of suras, rawm or ishmam; the press may err and some rules of tajwid may be hidden from the reader; so codices alone cannot be relied on, but delivery must be verified by taking from a trustworthy memorizer. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 316} See [[concept/tajwid|tajwid]].
