## Summary
Ibn Sina advises the traveller to proceed by degrees in exercise and to accustom himself beforehand to wakefulness, hunger and thirst, to make his food small in bulk and rich in nourishment, and to avoid vegetables and fruit; he then names foods that help endure hunger, prescribes purslane seed in vinegar for thirst, and warns against thirst-provoking foods {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.261-262}.

## The habit of hunger and wakefulness
The traveller should proceed by degrees and exercise a little beyond his habit; if he will need wakefulness he accustoms himself to it little by little, and likewise if he reckons that hunger or thirst will come; he gets used to the food he means to eat on the journey, makes it small in bulk and rich in nourishment, and gives up vegetables, fruit and anything generating a watery humour unless needed for treatment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.261}.

## Foods that help endure hunger
A traveller may need to bear hunger until appetite lessens, and he is helped by foods made from roasted liver and the like, sometimes shaped into balls with viscous matter, strong melted fats, almonds and almond oil and fats such as beef fat, so that after one of them he endures hunger for a good while; and it is said that whoever drinks a ratl of violet oil with some wax melted in it to the consistency of a salve will not crave food for ten days {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.261}.

## Enduring thirst
The traveller carries the thirst-quenching remedies set out in the third book, above all purslane seed, of which three dirhams are drunk with vinegar; he gives up thirst-provoking foods such as fish, caper, salted and sweet things, speaks little and is gentle with the little he has; and water with vinegar suffices in small amount where water is scarce, as does drinking mucilage of psyllium seed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.261-262}.
