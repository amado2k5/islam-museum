## Nature and selection
The name applies to ashaqq (gum ammoniacum), already discussed; it also applies to something made from boys' urine ground in a copper mortar and set in the sun until it thickens; and there is a mineral kind that forms in the mine from a vapour dissolved in its waters and then set. This last is the one the book treats {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}. The best is pure and clean, especially the naturally grown; the made kind is stronger and finer, then the burnt mineral kind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Temperament
Hot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Actions and properties
Cleansing, astringent and warming, it presses gently and stings a little; it dissolves and dries strongly, its dissolving being stronger than its sting, and so is its drying; it melts without much stinging. The made kind dries more and stings less because of its extra fineness, and the mineral kind grows finer when burnt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It dissolves flesh and is a good remedy for wounds that are hard to close {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Emetic and astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.545}.
