## The treatments
If the matter is a hot surplus, evacuation and venesection of the jaharik are used (the editor: a temple vein, carried as the book names it), opening with cooled mouthwashes holding some astringency of this kind, then mouth-oils of this kind — the mastic-tree oil having a wonderful strong power to calm the pain of gum tumours, especially recent ones, suppressing without roughening (the book's own praise of what it names). Then what he described of the resolvents of this kind; if the hot tumour is deep — called parulis, a Greek word meaning 'the deep' by the editor — it is resolved not by drugs but by suppuration; it may need the iron's treatment, and its substance may end by growing new flesh. When it suppurates what he described is used; a gum that keeps swelling, tumour on, not healing, needs cautery — best is the boiled oil taken on wool wrapped on a probe, repeated until it shrinks and whitens (carried as the book's description, not a procedure). If the tumour is of a surplus moisture, hot-oil mouthwashes with what he described open it, then the named strong resolvents {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.280-281}
