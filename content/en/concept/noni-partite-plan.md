## Summary
The noni-partite plan divides a building, by two intersecting lines, into nine parts: a domed octagonal chamber at the centre, open halls or flat-roofed aiwans between, and double-storeyed octagonal chambers at the corners, linked by axial and diagonal passages {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.13}.

## Timurid origin
Rezavi cites the tomb of Abu Nasr Parsa at Balkh (c. 1460) and other Timurid examples. Their direct descendants in India include the Sabz Burj, the Nili Gumbad and Humayun's Tomb in Delhi {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.13}.

## In India
The author singles out Humayun's Tomb and the Taj Mahal, and notes square variants such as the Tomb of I'timad-ud-Daulah at Agra {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.14} {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.15}.
