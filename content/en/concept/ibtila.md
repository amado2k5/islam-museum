## Summary
Ibtila is God's testing of people, as in the verse in al-Baqara 143 which speaks of God's knowledge of who follows the Messenger. [[person/rashid-rida|Muhammad Rashid Rida]] presents two readings of the word "to know" in that verse: that the act is ascribed to God in the Arabic manner of attributing an act to the head of a group, and that knowledge here is knowledge of an event on which a reward follows. {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.7} {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.8}

## Attribution to the head
Rida relates from [[person/muhammad-abduh|the Master Imam, Muhammad Abduh]] that the Arabs attribute an act to their leader even if he did not do it himself, as when they say that the emir conquered the city. So the testing may be ascribed to God in the plural form, and its meaning is to inform people of what will come from them. On this reading, the meaning is that God tests them so that the steadfast and the hypocrite become visible. {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.7} {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.8}

## Knowledge of what occurs
The other reading is that God knows things before they occur, that they will occur, and knows them after they have occurred, that they have occurred, and the reward follows what has occurred. The change is in the object of knowledge, not in the knowing. Rida cites verses of al-Kahf and Luqman on the division of God's knowledge and His words, and prefers the expression "knowledge of the unseen and knowledge of the witnessed". {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.8} {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.9}

## Trial and testing in the Qur'an
Rida holds that God purifies what is in hearts through the trials with which people are tested, and cites verses of al-'Ankabut. In his account, the trial does not lie in the matter itself, but in what follows from it: the denial of the ignorant and the steadfastness of believers. {cite:tafsir-manar-v02 p.8} [[passage/quran-2-143|Al-Baqara 143]]

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 5)

The Sheikh explains that the original sense of fitna is putting a piece of gold mixed with impurities into the fire to refine it, and notes that those before us were tested in their turn {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:32:00}.

He relates that the Companions asked the Prophet to pray for them, and that he told them one of those before them would be sawn from the parting of his hair to his heels and it would not turn him from saying there is no god but God; the Sheikh asks whether we took the religion with such hardship {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:32:30}.

He recounts from Sa'd about the expedition to Tabuk that they marched until they ate the qatad, a thorny desert tree, and when their provisions ran out they would pound its thorns with stones, swallow them and drink water over them {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep05 00:33:00}.

## The speaker's view: the world as a house of trial
He says that the world is a house of trial, not of honour, and that people are tested to see who gives thanks and who is patient. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:11:30}
He likens God's trial to a loving father's surgery on his child, which the child may see as harm but which is mercy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:18:00}
He holds that trial comes from the Creator, the Wise, the All-Knowing, who lifts it only by His decree. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:27:00}
He says that God tests a servant in order to bring him back to Himself, and contrasts this with Pharaoh's arrogance. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:28:30}

## The speaker's view: rejoicing in trial and degrees of gratitude
He tells every afflicted person not to think that he alone is afflicted, to rejoice in trial as nearness to God, and to know that the angels say amen to his prayer. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:38:00}
He cites a hadith that the prophets are tested most, then the righteous, and that trial is in proportion to a person's faith. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:08:00}
He distinguishes the one who gives thanks in ease from the one who gives thanks in trial, and holds the second to be of higher rank. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:18:30}
He holds that the portions are apportioned in advance, and that the rich are tested with gratitude and the poor with patience. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:35:30}
He says that God tests people with wealth and poverty, health and illness, and that calamities may remove sins. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep12 00:21:30}
He says that life never lacks the alternation of ease and hardship, so one should thank God in ease and be patient in hardship. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep22 00:01:00}

## The speaker's view: illness and trial
He calls the belief that illness brings death closer, and that health keeps it away, a mistaken belief. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:03:00} He says that illness does not bring death, and that the afflicted should rejoice in God's favour and know that God loves him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:38:30}
He says that the illness of a relative brings the family together, so that those who were separated draw close and visit the sick. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:14:00}
He says that a child's illness may be a test or a purification, and that a parent should not assume the illness is the final word. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:21:30}
He cites a hadith on visiting the sick, in which the visitor is enveloped in mercy. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:20:00}
He says that the sick person is nearer to God, and cites a hadith that one should supplicate with humility rather than with conditions. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep08 00:26:30}

## The speaker's view: trials of everyday life
He lists the trials of everyday life: a disobedient child, a spouse with a harsh tongue, a difficult neighbour, and an oppressive boss. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:25:30}
He mentions the trials people face from others' tongues, from unjust employers, and from neighbours who hide good and spread evil. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:10:30}
He holds that a narrowed livelihood teaches a person to call on God, unlike a secure salary, which makes its owner forget his Lord. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep23 00:17:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy explains Ibn Ata Allah's maxim that what lightens the pain of affliction is knowing that God is the one who tests, and says that the most sorely tried of people are the prophets, then the scholars, then the next best and the next; one should praise God so long as the affliction is not in one's religion. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep13 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep13 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep13 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep13 00:27:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh holds that all people are tested, and that ibtila' does not mean calamity but examination: God tests one with illness and another with office; he holds that [[person/sulayman|Sulayman]] was among the most severely tested by kingship, standing and wealth, and said 'this is of my Lord's bounty, to test me whether I give thanks or am ungrateful'. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep02 00:02:30} In a following episode he mentions that when God loves a servant He tests him; if he is patient He chooses him, and if he is content He selects him; and that whoever passes forty days without a test should review his accounts. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep03 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep03 00:09:30} See: [[concept/al-istirja|istirja']].

## In Abdul Hamid Kishk's sermons
[[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Abdul Hamid Kishk]] mentions a hadith qudsi whose sense is that God does not take a servant He wishes to have mercy on until He has expiated his sins through illness of body, narrow provision, or calamity in wealth or children, so that he meets Him pure as on the day his mother bore him. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:18:00} He mentions that God is shy, on the Day of Resurrection, to set up a scale or open a register for one who was tried in himself, his wealth or his child and met it with beautiful patience, and admits him to Paradise without reckoning. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep07 00:19:30} He mentions the hadith of the righteous child who dies and whose father is patient, for whom a House of Praise is built in Paradise. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep06 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep06 00:15:30}
