## Nature
Some call it saqulugandarion. It is a small plant lying on the ground, about a span long, found mostly on sea coasts, with no leaves. Its stems bear many small red berries, about the size of a wheat grain, sharp at their ends and astringent. Some people pound the seed, make cakes from it and keep them for need. The editor's note gives the name sea grape. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.509}

## Excretory and womb listing (labelled, held for the founder's ruling)
Dioscorides says that when about ten of the berries are mixed with wine, they help long diarrhoea and chronic discharges of fluid from the womb. The amount is left out; the womb listing is recorded as the book's report only. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.509}
