## Nature and selection
It is well known, and paper is made from it; it has the power of paper, and the burnt form dries more {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.410}. The editor's footnote says it is a plant like the palm, from which mats are made.

## Temperament and actions
Cold and dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.410}.

## Uses by organ
Actions: helps bleeding, and its ash stops it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.410}.

Wounds and ulcers: sprinkled on fresh wounds it closes them; it may be steeped in vinegar and dried; it enters the treatment of fistula and all creeping ulcers and wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.410}.

Head: its ash helps mouth sores {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Breath: its ash holds back spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.

Excretory organs: taken, wrapped in flax, and left until dry, then applied to piles, it helps them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.411}.
