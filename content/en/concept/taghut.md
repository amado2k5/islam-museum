## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that taghut comes from the root of tyranny and that its form signals intensity. It includes Satan, the cleric, the sorcerer and the unjust ruler {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1115}.

## Its forms
He says taghut may be Satan, or someone who takes to himself the right to legislate, declaring others unbelievers or believers according to desire, or a sorcerer or charlatan, or anyone who exceeds the limit in a matter {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1115}.

## The gradual rise of tyranny
He holds that tyranny begins by testing the ground and grows if people endure it, and that obedience in small matters increases the tyrant's power over the obedient {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1116}.

## Singular and plural
He explains that taghut is treated as singular in the verse, so that it covers one or several, as one says "a just person" for one or many {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1120} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1121}.
