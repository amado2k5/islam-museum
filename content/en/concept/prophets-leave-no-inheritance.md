## Summary
The Prophet is reported to have said: "We are not inherited from; what we leave is charity", and in a wording that covers all prophets: "We, the company of prophets, are not inherited from". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 609} This record sets out how Sunni scholars understood the hadith and how they reconciled it with the verses of al-Naml ([[passage/quran-27-16|Qur'an 27:16]]) and Maryam ([[passage/quran-19-6|Qur'an 19:6]]), which mention [[person/sulayman|Sulayman]] inheriting from [[person/dawud|Dawud]] and [[person/zakariyya|Zakariyya]] asking for an heir, and how they understood the other saying that scholars are the heirs of the prophets. It presents the Sunni understanding only, from the books read; the point on which others differ is recorded in [[question/do-prophets-leave-an-inheritance|the question "Do prophets leave an inheritance?"]].

## The hadith and its transmitters
Ibn Kathir says that the hadith "We are not inherited from; what we leave is charity" is agreed on in the two Sahih collections through several routes from a group of Companions, and that Abu Bakr used it to refuse to hand what had been the Prophet's own to his heirs: his daughter Fatima, his nine wives and his uncle al-Abbas. Among those who agreed with him in relating it from the Prophet he lists Umar ibn al-Khattab, Uthman ibn Affan, Ali ibn Abi Talib, al-Abbas, Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Talha, al-Zubayr, Abu Hurayra and others. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 666} The editor of Ibn Kathir's Tafsir refers it to al-Bukhari no. 3094, Muslim no. 1757 and al-Tirmidhi no. 1610, from the hadith of Umar. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2926} The editor of the Stories of the Prophets notes that he did not find in al-Tirmidhi the general wording "we, the company of prophets" that Ibn Kathir ascribes to him, only "we are not inherited from; what we leave is charity". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 666}

The hadith appears in the museum's hadith texts in two places. In Sahih al-Bukhari ([[passage/bukhari-4033|Bukhari 4033]]), in the Book of the Fifth, Umar tells al-Abbas and Ali, who are disputing about the Prophet's property, that the Prophet had three special holdings: the land of Banu al-Nadir, kept for his needs; a part of Khaybar divided in three; and Fadak for wayfarers; and he rules between them that what the Prophet left is charity and not inherited. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4033} In the Muwatta ([[passage/muwatta-31-2840|Muwatta 2840]]), Aisha reports that when the Prophet died his wives sent Uthman ibn Affan to Abu Bakr to ask for their inheritance, and Aisha reminded them of the Prophet's saying that he is not inherited from and what he left is charity. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.592} See also [[event/fatima-inheritance-claim-fadak|Fatima's claim to the Prophet's inheritance and Fadak]] and [[event/umar-judges-ali-abbas-inheritance|Umar judges between Ali and al-Abbas]].

## How they reconciled it with the verses of al-Naml and Maryam
The commentators quoted in the books read here say that what is meant by Sulayman inheriting from Dawud and by Zakariyya's request for an heir is inheritance of prophethood and knowledge, not of wealth. Ibn Kathir says on [[passage/quran-27-16|the verse of al-Naml]] that Sulayman inherited from his father prophethood and kingship, not wealth, since Dawud had other sons and Sulayman would not have been singled out for wealth over them, and since the sound report is that the prophets' property is not inherited. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 609} On [[passage/quran-19-6|the verse of Maryam]] he adds four points: that the hadith is established from a group of Companions; that al-Tirmidhi has a general wording; that this world was too low in the prophets' eyes for them to hoard for it or to ask for children to take it after them; and that Zakariyya was a carpenter eating from the work of his hand, so he is not thought to have left an estate. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 666} In his Tafsir he says that since the hadith is in the two Sahih collections through more than one route, the inheritance in the verse has to be inheritance of prophethood; had wealth been meant, Sulayman would not have been singled out from among his brothers, and there would have been little point in mentioning it, since it is known that a son inherits from his father. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 2926}

Al-Qurtubi says that the people of knowledge of Qur'anic interpretation hold this, quotes Ibn Abd al-Barr that any view contrary to the Prophet's saying is "rejected and abandoned", and says the hadith enters the transmitted commentary as a specification of the verse: Sulayman did not inherit wealth Dawud left, but wisdom and knowledge, and so did Yahya. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4330} He gives al-Nahhas's three answers on "inherit": prophethood, wisdom or wealth; inheritance of knowledge and wisdom he calls a good view, and the hadith that scholars are the heirs of the prophets. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4333}

## "The scholars are the heirs of the prophets"
Al-Qurtubi quotes from the Book of Abu Dawud a saying that the scholars are the heirs of the prophets, and that the prophets did not leave a dinar or a dirham as inheritance but left knowledge. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4330} Ibn Atiyya, on the verse of al-Naml, says that the saying "the scholars are the heirs of the prophets" is a parallel to calling the passing of prophethood and kingship to Sulayman an "inheritance", figuratively. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5106}

## Ways of understanding the wording
Al-Qurtubi gives, from Abu Umar, two readings of "we are not inherited from; what we leave is charity": the first, held by the majority, is that the Prophet is not inherited from and what he left is charity; the other is that our Prophet in particular was not inherited from because God specially made all his property charity as an addition to his excellence, as he was singled out in marriage for things allowed to him and forbidden to others, and this is the view of some people of Basra, among them Ibn Ulayya; the rest of the Muslim scholars hold the first. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4333} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4334} Al-Nahhas has another view: he does not see in the hadith proof against prophets in general being inherited from, since a person may speak of himself in the plural, and it may mean "we do not leave an inheritance, what we left being charity", because the Prophet left nothing to be inherited and what God allowed him in his life was from the fifth. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4333} Ibn Atiyya says the hadith may not intend generality but describe the usual way of the prophets, even though some of them did leave their wealth to heirs, Zakariyya on the best-known view, like saying "we Muslims are busy with worship"; al-Qurtubi replies that the first view is the sounder, because of the general wording "we, the company of prophets, are not inherited from", from which nothing is excepted without evidence. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 5106}

## Those who said otherwise
Ibn Kathir says that some of the Shia held that what is meant by inheritance in the verse of Maryam is inheritance of wealth, that Ibn Jarir agreed with them in this verse and reported it from Abu Salih among the early generation, and then he answers it with the four points. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 665} Al-Qurtubi quotes from Ibn Atiyya that most commentators hold that Zakariyya meant inheritance of wealth, that the hadith may not be general, and that the more fitting for Zakariyya, in his own view, is inheritance of knowledge and religion. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 4330} The reading of those outside the Sunni tradition and its argument are not presented here from their own sources; see [[question/do-prophets-leave-an-inheritance|the recorded question]].
