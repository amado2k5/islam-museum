## Summary
The first motion is the motion of the whole; by it night and day occur, the whole heaven turning once from east to west each day and night {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 117}. It is the first of the two motions seen in the heaven {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 117}.

## Its poles and equator
The heaven turns on two fixed poles, one toward the north and the other opposite toward the south {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 117}. The equinoctial is the great circle midway between the poles, dividing the heavenly sphere into halves {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 148}.

## Proof of it
Al-Farghani argues its rotation from the fact that the planets near the north pole — Capricornus, the Farqadân and Banat Na'sh — revolve about one point in parallel circles: the nearer to that point the smaller its circle and the slower its motion {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 124}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 140}. Another proof is that the circumpolar stars show part of their circuit in the shape of the sphere {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 139}.

## Its effect on the earth
Al-Farghani counts the variation of day and night length with latitude as an effect of this motion together with the zodiac's inclination, showing that the sun in the northern signs rises at the polar places circling the horizon {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 101}.
