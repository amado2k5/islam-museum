## Summary
Ibn Sina holds that the strength of an organ is considered through its rank: strong drugs are not risked on the principal organs where it can be avoided, lest harm spread to the whole body, so the brain and liver are not emptied of all they need to lose at one stroke nor chilled hard, and the heart most deserves this care {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.269-270}.

## Examples of the care
When the liver is poulticed with dissolving drugs, the poultice is not without astringents of pleasant smell to preserve strength, and the same holds for what is drunk for its sake. The care extends to an organ that shares with a principal one without being principal, such as the stomach and lung, which is why very cold water is not given in fevers when the stomach is weak. Using relaxants on the principal organs and those next to them, pure, is on the whole very dangerous {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.270}.
