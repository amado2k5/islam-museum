## Lexical sense
In language sanad is the part of a mountain that rises above its foot, and whatever is leaned on and relied upon, such as a wall; from this a debt certificate is also called a sanad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9835}

## The sanad as a written instrument
The technical sense is a written instrument by which rights are secured. It is valid for proving rights before a ruler or judge. The Majalla requires that it state what establishes the right, for example the amount of the debt in figures and in words; that it bear the name of the person entitled; and that it be sealed by the debtor and signed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9835}
It must be free from forgery, erasure and alteration, and experts must confirm that the hand is the scribe's. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9835} The Majalla also says that the hand and seal alone are not enough, but a sound instrument free from any suspicion of forgery is acted on. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9835}

## The sanad as a written confession
When a sanad meets the conditions for its validity it counts as a confession in writing. The Majalla holds that one who writes a sanad, or has it written and returns it signed or sealed, is bound as if he had spoken; and one who orders a scribe to write that he owes a sum to another, and signs or seals it, has made a written confession. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9835} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9836}
See the entries on confession, proof, documentation and forgery. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9836}

## The sanad as a chain of narrators
Sanad also names the chain of transmitters leading to a hadith's text; the conditions for accepting the chain were treated under the entry on isnad. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9836}
