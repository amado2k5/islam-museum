## Nature and selection
Well known. Salt has bitterness and astringency, and the bitter is close to borax; there is a brittle kind, a mined kind, a Darani like crystal, and a naphthic kind whose blackness comes from naphtha in it, which when smoked until the naphtha flies off remains like the Darani; there is an Indian black salt whose blackness is not from naphtha but in its substance. Sea salt melts as soon as water touches it, and land salt does not {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, and whatever is more bitter is hotter {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Actions and properties
Cleansing, dissolving, astringent and drying, through its dissolving and its astringency, its astringency being the strongest of its acts; it increases winds; the burnt is more drying and dissolving; it prevents putrefaction and helps thickness of humours. Its flower is finer than it and than its burnt form, and its dust is close to both. The mined is less dissolving and less fine, and when washed many times it dries without stinging. All of this melts congealed humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Cosmetic uses
Burnt salt cleans the teeth of pits and, as a paint, removes the blackness of blood wherever it is {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Swellings and pustules
With honey and raisins it is a poultice for boils, with calamint and honey for phlegmatic swellings, and it stops herpes from spreading {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It eats excess flesh and helps ulcerated scab and ringworm; it is smeared with oil and vinegar near a fire to make the skin sweat and so calms itch, especially the phlegmatic; with oil on a burn from fire it prevents blistering {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Joints
With flour and honey on twisted sinew; gout is bound with it; mixed with oil it is rubbed on for fatigue {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Painted with colocynth fat on pustules of the head; the Andarani sharpens the mind; salt firms slack gums, especially the Darani, and with vinegar it is a poultice for earache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Eyes
It eats excess flesh in the eyelids and pterygium; its flower especially helps film and whiteness; salt with oil and honey is bound on the eye and dissolves the blood bruise clotted in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Chest and breathing
Andarani and naphthic salt and all its kinds cut the sticky phlegm in the chest, and a palate rub with the naphthic with honey and vinegar helps quinsy and swelling of the uvula {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.571}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Salt helps vomiting, especially the naphthic and the Andarani, and helps pains of the cold stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Excretory organs
All salt eases the passing of stool and the descent of food; the naphthic purges putrid phlegm, water, bile and black bile and is strong in enemas; the intensely black kind that is not naphthic loosens phlegm and black bile; the bitter salt also loosens black bile strongly; the Andarani loosens raw phlegm strongly and loosens black bile. It helps purgative remedies to uproot black bile and sticky moistures from the parts of the organ, and with mountain calamint, butter and leaven it is used for phlegmatic swellings of the testicles, likewise with calamint and honey, and it helps ulcers of the penis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Poisons and bites
Bound on with flaxseed for a scorpion sting; with mountain calamint, hyssop and honey for the bite of the muqarrana; with vinegar and honey for the bite of the many-legged centipede and for wasps; and with sikanjabin for the harm of opium and deadly mushrooms {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.
