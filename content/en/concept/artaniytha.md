## Nature
Its root is what is used, and it was said to be the bakhur Maryam. Dioscorides said that it has husks like those of the chickpea, a leaf like that of cabbage and a black root like that of the turnip; this is not the description of what we know in our time, for what is known as artaniytha is a dense short thorn with a white root with which wool is washed of dirt; Dioscorides said that it grows in farms among wheat, and the properties we mention belong to this, and it seems the mistake is the translator's {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.610–611}

## Actions and properties
Dissolving and cutting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}

## Joints
It is good for pains of the hips {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}

## Organs of the head
It causes thirst and is strongly opening to the body and to obstruction of the sieve bone {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}

## Organs of the chest
It drives off hiccup {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}

## Poisons and bites
Its decoction is applied to stings, and so is drinking it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}

## Substitutes
The substitutes the book gives for it, in expulsion and in benefit against poisons, are long birthwort, citron seed and nutinj {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists it as causing the foetus to be lost {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.611}
