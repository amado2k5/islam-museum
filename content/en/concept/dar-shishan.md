## Nature and selection
A tree of thickness with many thorns, its bark pungent, its flower hot and its wood astringent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}. The best is heavy, with a red to purplish colour under the bark, fragrant in smell and taste; the white and odourless is poor {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}. The editor's footnote gives the identity as the kind called qandul, and says Ibn Sina called it usfalatos {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}; the identity is queued for outside research.

## Temperament and actions
Temperament: hot in the first degree and dry at the end of the second, though some say the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}. Actions: it warms and is astringent, dissolves winds and stops flows {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.433}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: it helps spreading and putrid ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
Joints: it is especially helpful for relaxation of the nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
Head: good for a foul nose, made into a wick; its decoction is a gargle for thrush and for keeping the teeth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
Chest: its decoction stops the spitting of blood from the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
Food: it helps flatulence in the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
Excretory organs: its decoction binds the belly and helps flatulence in the bowel and difficult urination; the book says it can expel a foetus; sprinkled on sores of the perineum and the testicles, it helps their hardness and their spreading; these are labelled book listings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
Substitutes: the fruit of yanbut, and for the nerve effect, asarum and daronj {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.434}.
