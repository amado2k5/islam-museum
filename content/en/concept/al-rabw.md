## The signs of asthma
If the asthma's cause is humours and moistures in the windpipe itself, there is narrowness at the start of breathing with throat-clearing, wheezing, a standing matter held, and the spitting of something from a near place; if the humours are from a catarrh it is all at once, else little by little. If in the rough vessels, the pulse's unevenness persists palpitatingly and may lead to a settled killing palpitation — most of the asthmatics' pulse is palpitant. If it is outside the space there is no cough; if by sharing of the sources, what went before points to it; if by sharing of the neighbours, its worsening on the stirring and filling of a matter in them points to it; if from a tumour's bursting all at once into the breathing organs, the tumour and gathering that preceded point to it, then what arose of the bursting; if from dryness, the thirst and the utter absence of spitting point to it, and its lessening on taking what moistens; if from a wind, the lightness of the chest's quarters with a narrowness changing by the taking of inflating things points to it; and if the cause is the lung temperament's cold — as in the old — it begins little by little and settles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.316}
