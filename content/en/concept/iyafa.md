## Summary
In the lexicon, iyafa means disliking food or drink, and also the augury of birds: reading omens of good or ill from their names, cries and flight paths {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11950}. Augury by birds was an Arab custom in the pre-Islamic era; Islam abolished it, returned the matter to God's absolute will and to the fixed laws of creation {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11950}. A report counts augury, bird-omens and the drawing of lines as a form of falsehood, a matter treated under the heading of omens {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11951}. In the sense of aversion to food, a report from Khalid ibn al-Walid tells of a dish of lizard set before the Prophet, which he withdrew from because it was not common in his people's land and he was not used to it; Khalid then ate it in his presence and he did not forbid it, which shows it is lawful and that his withdrawal was from unfamiliarity {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11951}.
