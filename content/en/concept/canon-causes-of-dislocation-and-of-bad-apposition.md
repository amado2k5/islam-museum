## Summary
In chapters 10 to 12 Ibn Sina says displacement of position comes from stretching, violent movement, a relaxing and moistening cause, or a cause that spoils the substance of the ligament, and that bad apposition that prevents bringing together or moving apart of limbs is caused by thickness, scar of an ulcer, spasm, slackness, drying and hardening of humour in the joint, or birth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.145-146}.

## Dislocation and displacement
Displacement of position arises from stretching, as when a person pulls a limb and stretches it until it dislocates; or from violent movement leaning on a support that moves the limb from its place, as when a foot is turned over; or from a relaxing, moistening cause, as occurs in hernia; or from a cause that spoils the substance of the ligament by eating or putrefying it, as occurs in leprosy and sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.145-146}.

## Bad apposition preventing approach
Its cause is thickness, the scar of an ulcer, spasm, slackness, or the drying and hardening of humour in the joint, or it is congenital {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.146}.

## Bad apposition preventing separation
Its cause is thickness, the knitting of the scar of an ulcer, spasm, or it is congenital {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.146}.
