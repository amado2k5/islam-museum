## Summary
In the lexicon, nikah means intercourse or the contract without intercourse, and each school defines it in its own terms. The jurists differ on its literal sense in three views, and the difference changes some rulings. This part is partial. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}

## Definition
Nikah is, in the lexicon, a verbal noun of nakaha; "nakaha al-mar'a" means he married her, and also he had intercourse with her. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}
The Hanafis define it as a contract that gives enjoyment of a woman by intention; the Malikis as a contract making lawful the enjoyment of a woman who is not forbidden to the man. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}
The Shafi'is define it as a contract that carries permission of intercourse by the words nikah or tazwij, and the Hanbalis as the contract of tazwij. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}

## The literal sense
The jurists give three views. The first is that the word is literal for intercourse and figurative for the contract; this is the sounder Hanafi view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16035}
The second is that it is literal for the contract and figurative for intercourse; this is the Maliki view, the Shafi'i view in its sounder form, and the sounder Hanbali view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16035}
The third is that it is literal for both, whether as a shared word or a shared meaning; this is a Hanafi view, Bahram's view among the Malikis, and a view among the Shafi'is and Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16035} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16036}

## Effect of the disagreement on rulings
The disagreement changes some rulings. In the Hanafi view, and the sounder Hanbali view, a man who commits zina with a woman makes her forbidden to his ascendants and descendants; the Shafi'is and, by the stronger view, the Malikis hold that zina does not create affinity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16036}
If a man swears not to marry, or ties a divorce to marriage, the oath is broken and the divorce takes effect by intercourse according to those who take nikah as intercourse, and by the contract according to those who take it as the contract. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16037}
The Hanafis distinguish: if a man says to his wife, "If I marry you, you are divorced," it applies on intercourse, but to a stranger it applies on the contract. The Shafi'is say that an oath not to marry is broken by the contract, not by intercourse, unless he intended intercourse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16037}

## Related terms
Khitba is the request for a woman in marriage; its technical sense does not depart from the lexical one, and it comes before nikah. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16037}
Sifah is illicit intercourse; it is the opposite of nikah, because intercourse in sifah is forbidden and in nikah lawful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16037}
Talaq is the ending of the marriage tie by the word of divorce or similar, or, in the Hanbali wording, the release of the tie or part of it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16037} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16038}
