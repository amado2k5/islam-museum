## Summary
Ibn Sina treats what follows vomiting: lingering sharp irritation of the stomach with a fatty broth quick to digest and a rub of violet oil mixed with wallflower oil and a little wax; and tetanus, lethargy and loss of voice with binding the limbs and fomenting the stomach with oil in which rue has been boiled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.288}.

## Lingering irritation of the stomach
The severe irritation that remains in the stomach is driven off by drinking a fatty broth quick to digest and rubbing the place with violet oil mixed with wallflower oil and a little wax {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.288}.

## Tension and pain
For the tension and pain under the ribs he names softening oils and cupping glasses; the wording around them is broken in the print, so only what is clear is given {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.288}.

## Tetanus and lethargy
For tetanus, the cold diseases, lethargy and loss of voice that may follow, it helps to bind and tie the limbs and foment the stomach with oil in which rue and squirting cucumber were boiled, and give honey and hot water to drink; for the lethargic the same is used and poured into his ear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.288}.
