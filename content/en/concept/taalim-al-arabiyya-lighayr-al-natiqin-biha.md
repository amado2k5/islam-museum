## Summary
This record gives what a research summary supplied to the museum says of teaching Arabic as a foreign language in our time: how teaching moved from grammar and translation to the four skills and the integrated approach, where Arabic is taught, and by what scales a learner's level is measured. Each statement is attributed to its page as the summary reported it, and the page says at its end what it did not reach. For the setting in which the standard is taught beside the spoken see [[concept/al-izdiwajiyya-al-lughawiyya-fi-al-arabiyya|Diglossia in Arabic]], and for the heritage's praise of learning Arabic see [[concept/fadl-taallum-al-arabiyya|The merit of learning Arabic]].

## Background
The question rests on what has been said above, that Arabic in use is two things, a standard learned through education and dialects acquired by upbringing. The summary says that teaching Arabic to non-native speakers, TAFL, moved from a philology-based method of grammar-translation that taught only modern standard Arabic to communicative teaching of the four skills: listening, speaking, reading and writing {cite:dokumen-teaching-arabic-as-a-foreign-language} {cite:illinois-ideals-tafl-study}. The paragraph rests on the book Teaching Arabic as a Foreign Language: Origins, Developments and Current Directions and on a University of Illinois study that the summary calls "the TAFL study".

## The integrated approach
The summary names an approach popularised by textbooks such as Al-Kitaab, the "integrated approach" (al-tariqa al-takamuliyya), which teaches modern standard Arabic and from the start a regional spoken dialect beside it, typically Egyptian or Levantine {cite:dokumen-teaching-arabic-as-a-foreign-language} {cite:illinois-ideals-tafl-study}. The learner is not left between a standard that is not heard in the street and a colloquial that is not written, but learns both together, a practical answer to the duality set out on the previous page.

## Institutions
The summary names these bodies, as their pages give them:
- The Arabic Language Institute (ALI) of the American University in Cairo, founded in 1921, with the Center for Arabic Study Abroad (CASA); the summary says El-Said Badawi also founded there the MA in teaching Arabic as a foreign language in 1977 {cite:auc-department-of-arabic-language-instruction} {cite:aata-language-programs}.
- The Middlebury College School of Arabic, a summer programme in Vermont with Middlebury Schools Abroad in Amman and Alexandria, known for a strict pledge to speak only Arabic {cite:aata-language-programs} {cite:talkpal-best-universities-for-studying-arabic}.
- The Qasid Arabic Institute in Amman and Al-Azhar University in Cairo, beside major Western university programmes such as Georgetown, SOAS and Edinburgh {cite:buruj-academy-learn-arabic-in-the-middle-east} {cite:talkpal-best-universities-for-studying-arabic}.

## Proficiency scales and tests
The summary sets out scales and tests that measure a learner's level, which the table gives:

| Test | Body | Year | Scale and skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| OPI / OPIc | ACTFL | 2024 (guidelines) | Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, Superior, Distinguished; live interview (OPI) or computer-delivered (OPIc), in MSA and dialects |
| Hamzah ⁦(همزة)⁩ | KSGAAL | December 2023 | aligned with CEFR A2 to C1; listening, reading, writing and speaking |
| ALPT | Arab Academy | 2002 | computer-adaptive, five skills |
| CIMA | Institut du monde arabe, Paris | 2018 | CEFR A1 to C2 |
| AL-ARABIYYA-TEST | Universität Leipzig | not given | CEFR A1 to C2 |

In the summary's detail: the ACTFL guidelines are the main American framework, measured by the live Oral Proficiency Interview (OPI) and the computer-delivered OPIc {cite:actfl-proficiency-guidelines-overview} {cite:actfl-opi} {cite:actfl-opic}. The Hamzah test was launched in December 2023 by the King Salman Global Academy for the Arabic Language and aligns with CEFR A2 to C1 {cite:ksgaal-news-language-testing-conference} {cite:wikipedia-hamza-test}. The ALPT is a computer-adaptive five-skill test developed by the Arab Academy in 2002 {cite:pixel-online-advancing-arabic-language-assessment} {cite:preply-arabic-language-tests}. CIMA, the Certificat international de maîtrise en arabe, was founded by the Institut du monde arabe in Paris in 2018, and AL-ARABIYYA-TEST comes from the University of Leipzig; both certify proficiency on the CEFR scale from A1 to C2 {cite:wikipedia-list-of-language-proficiency-tests} {cite:preply-arabic-language-tests}.

## How the sources differ
The sources are of several kinds: a scholarly book, university and official pages, the site of a professional association, and with them blogs and a commercial language-learning site. The summary lists them all without distinction, and the page keeps them all, by the founder's decision of 21 November 2026, ascribing to each only what it alone says. Where pages agree, as several do on the launch date of Hamzah, this is said without ranking them.

## What this page does not reach
The museum opened none of the pages named. The summary gives no numbers of learners, no ranking of universities and no comparison of the tests in difficulty or cost, so none is given here. It does not name the authors of Al-Kitaab or its publisher, so none is ascribed. The dates and places of founding of ALPT and CIMA are as the summary gave them and were not checked. The page does not decide the dispute over whether the colloquial should be taught with the standard or after it.
