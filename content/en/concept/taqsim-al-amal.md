## Summary
Tareq al-Suwaidan presents division of labour as the principle with which the West opened modern management science, drawn from Adam Smith's pin-factory story {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:23:00}.

## Its origin in his account
He recounts that Smith watched the workers of a pin factory, each performing every stage, then rearranged the work so that each worker had a single step, and output rose tenfold without changing the workers, machines or materials {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:22:30}.
He calls division of labour a great theory in the world of industry, but one that dealt mankind a severe blow when it was carried into the world of services, where an applicant is stalled between one clerk and the next {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:23:30}.

## His alternative: reengineering and the complete employee
He says humanity treated this flaw with 'administrative reengineering' (reengineering, arabised as hindara), which redistributes work by process rather than by division of labour, producing the 'complete employee' who finishes a whole transaction {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:24:30}.
He advises anyone working in services, government or companies, not to use division of labour and to rely on the complete employee instead, who is better and faster {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan administrative-processus-abilities/ep01 00:25:00}.
