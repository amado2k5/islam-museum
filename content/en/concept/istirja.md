## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that saying the formula of istirja' at a misfortune acknowledges that the person is God's, accepts the trial and hopes for reward. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.663} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.664}

## Misfortune between one's own act and justice
Sha'rawi distinguishes a misfortune to which the person contributed, over which he should not grieve, from one caused by another, whose justice or injustice must be considered. A just trial expiates a fault, and an unjust one is avenged by God for the wronged. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.664}

## The transmitted prayer
Sha'rawi cites the prayer "O God, reward me in my misfortune and give me something better in its place", and holds that it is answered with better after the loss. If someone forgets it at the time and remembers it later, he still has its reward. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.665}

## Umm Salama's story
Sha'rawi tells how Umm Salama, after the death of her husband Abu Salama, was then asked in marriage by the Prophet once her waiting period ended, and offers it as a witness to the prayer's effect. The story is given without a chain in the text and its original source was not checked here. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.665}
