## Summary
Rifq in the lexicon is gentleness, softness in the act and firmness in the purpose; it is the opposite of harshness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8815} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8816} In general it is recommended, but it is obligatory with parents and forbidden with fighting unbelievers. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8816} God has laid on His servants only what they can bear and has granted them concessions, and the believer is told to take from the supererogatory what he can keep up. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8816} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8817} Kindness to parents and neighbours is commanded; the imam should shorten the prayer for the weak; in crowded rites one should not push others; and in changing wrong one should be gentle with the ignorant. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8817} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8818} The Prophet urged kindness to servants, feeding them from one's own food, and forbade torturing animals and shooting at living creatures for a target; the owner must feed the animal and may not overload it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8818} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8819} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8820} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8821}

## In Abd al-Rahman Mansour's programme 'Ahalina'
[[person/abd-al-rahman-mansour|Abd al-Rahman Mansour]] repeats in his programme [[work/ahaleena|'Ahalina']] the hadith that gentleness is never in anything without adorning it, nor removed from anything without disfiguring it, and makes it the rule for dealing with children over prayer, especially in late childhood and adolescence. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep10 00:12:30} Severity, harshness and coercion, he says, do not bring the hoped-for result, and a child scolded and beaten over prayer comes to hate prayer. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep07 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep11 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:02:30} He describes an angry father whose pupils widen and whose neck veins swell as he gestures at a seven-year-old, and urges him to be gentle. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:06:00} He advises easy, kind, calm speech with a child of five to seven, and correcting a child's mistakes in a soft, calm way. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep08 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep08 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep11 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep11 00:08:30} He asks that the imam and staff of a mosque be gentle with small children and give them sweets instead of scolding. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep07 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep07 00:12:00} Even striking, when it is resorted to, must in his view be joined with mercy, compassion and gentleness. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep13 00:09:30}
