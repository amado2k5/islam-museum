## Nature and selection
One kind is Roman and white, another Nabataean and inclining to black. Its tree is composed of little water and much earth, and it is finer and more useful than frankincense. The best is the white, shining and clean; it is corrected by dissolving it and leaving it in vinegar for days, then drying {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, less warming and drying than frankincense {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Actions and properties
Astringent and dissolving; every part of its tree is astringent, its roots and their bark stand in for acacia and hypocist, as does the juice of its leaf, and a strongly astringent oil is made from its fruit. Galen thinks that all its parts have softening as well as astringency; the blackish Nabataean is less astringent and more drying, so it suits what needs strong dissolving. Its oil is very fine and melts phlegm, and it is less sharp and dense than other gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Cosmetic uses
It goes into tooth powders and cosmetic washes and gives them a good effect {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Swellings and pustules
By its astringency and softening it helps swellings of the viscera; the black Nabataean suits inner hardnesses better, and it is useful for the herpetic swellings called namliyya {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Its juice and the decoction of its leaf hold back the sa'iya eruption; the oil of its tree helps scab, even the scab of cattle and dogs; the decoction of the leaf and its juice poured on ulcers make flesh grow, and on broken bones they knit them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Chewing it draws phlegm from the head and cleans it, and rinsing with it firms the gums {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Eyes
It is used to stick down an inverted eyelash {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Chest and breathing
It helps cough and spitting of blood, especially a decoction of its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It strengthens the stomach and liver, stirs the appetite and sweetens the stomach and liver {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Excretory organs
It strengthens the liver and intestines and helps their swellings, and a decoction of its root and bark helps diarrhoea, dysentery and excoriation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book lists its leaf for bleeding from the womb, all pains of the womb, bad discharges from it and prolapse of the womb and the anus, and so its tree oil and seed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.554}.
