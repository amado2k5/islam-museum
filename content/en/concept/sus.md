## Temperament
Its root is moderate, and if it leans to anything it leans to heat and moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Swellings and pustules
Its juice is put on the whitlow, and so is its root {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Wounds and ulcers
Its juice is for wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Organs of the chest
It softens and cleans the windpipe, helps lung and throat, and clears the voice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It calms thirst by its moisture, and likewise helps inflammation of the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Excretory organs
The book lists the root for the pterygium, its juice stronger, and for burning of urine and for ulcers and scab of the kidneys and bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.

## Fevers
It helps old fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.593}.
