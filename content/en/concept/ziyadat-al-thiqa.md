## Overview
*Ziyadat al-thiqa* is a word or phrase in the text or chain of a hadith that a trustworthy narrator alone adds, as the editor Nur al-Din 'Itr defines it; [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] in [[work/nuzhat-al-nazar|*Nuzhat al-Nazar*]] discusses additions to the text in particular. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 63}

## When it is accepted
Ibn Hajar holds that an addition by a narrator of sound or good hadith is accepted unless it contradicts the report of someone more reliable who did not mention it. Either the addition does not conflict with the report of those who omitted it, and then it is accepted without qualification, since it counts as a separate hadith that a trustworthy narrator alone transmits; or it conflicts with it, so that accepting it means rejecting the other report, and then the two are weighed and the stronger accepted and the weaker rejected. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 63-64}

The editor points to a third class between these two, such as an added word that restricts a general statement or qualifies an unrestricted one, and reads Ibn Hajar as accepting it, since accepting it does not entail rejecting the other report; he attributes acceptance to al-Shafi'i, Malik and Ahmad and rejection to Abu Hanifa, and gives as an example Nu'aym al-Mujmir's addition that Abu Hurayra recited the basmala aloud when Nu'aym prayed behind him. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 64}

## Against unqualified acceptance
Ibn Hajar notes that a group of scholars is known for accepting additions without distinction, and says this is inconsistent with the method of the hadith scholars, who require that a [[concept/al-hadith-al-sahih|sound report]] not be irregular (*shadhdh*) and define irregularity as a trustworthy narrator contradicting a more reliable one. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 64}

He says that what is reported of the early hadith imams, such as 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Yahya al-Qattan, [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]], Yahya ibn Ma'in, 'Ali ibn al-Madini, [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], Abu Zur'a, Abu Hatim, al-Nasa'i and [[person/al-daraqutni|al-Daraqutni]], is the weighing of reports in the matter of additions and elsewhere, and that none of them is known to have accepted additions without qualification. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 65}

He finds it stranger still that many Shafi'is accept additions without qualification when [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]'s own text points the other way: al-Shafi'i treated a narrator's report being shorter than the hafiz's report as evidence of his care, and anything else as damaging to his hadith, which includes additions; had he accepted additions without qualification, they would not have damaged their transmitter. {cite:nuzhat-al-nazar-itr-bushra-2011 p. 65-66}
