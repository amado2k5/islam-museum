## Summary
"Knowing the additions of trustworthy narrators and their ruling" is the sixteenth type in [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]], who calls it a subtle art worth attention and notes that Abu Bakr ibn Ziyad al-Naysaburi, Abu Nu'aym al-Jurjani and Abu al-Walid al-Qurashi were known for knowing the legally significant extra wordings in hadiths. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 85}

## The views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: the majority (acceptance)**: [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|Al-Khatib]] reported that most jurists and hadith scholars accept an addition from a trustworthy narrator even when he is alone in it, whether one person related the hadith once without it and once with it, or the addition comes from someone other than the one who related it without it. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 85}
- **Sunni: rejection outright**: some hadith scholars rejected additions altogether. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 85}
- **Sunni: a distinction**: others rejected an addition from the same narrator who also related it without it, and accepted it from a different narrator. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 85}
:::

## Ibn al-Salah's division
Ibn al-Salah divides what a trustworthy narrator relates alone into three. First, what contradicts and conflicts with what the other trustworthy narrators relate: it is rejected, as with [[concept/shadhdh|the shadhdh]]. Second, what involves no conflict at all with others' narrations, such as a whole hadith that a trustworthy narrator alone relates: it is accepted, and al-Khatib claimed the scholars agree on it. Third, what lies between, such as an extra phrase in a hadith that the other narrators of it omit. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 86}

An example of the third is [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]]'s hadith from Nafi' from Ibn Umar that zakat al-fitr is due "from every free person or slave, male or female, from among the Muslims": al-Tirmidhi noted that Malik alone among the trustworthy added "from among the Muslims", while Ubayd Allah ibn Umar, Ayyub and others relate it without, yet several imams, among them [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]], adopted it. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 86} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 87} Another is "the earth was made a place of prayer for us, and its soil was made pure for us", where Abu Malik Sa'd ibn Tariq al-Ashja'i alone adds "its soil"; this resembles the first kind, since the group's version is general and the solitary one specific, and the second, since there is no conflict. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 87}

## Connected and mursal, raised and stopped
Closely related is a hadith that some trustworthy narrators relate as mursal and others as connected, such as "no marriage without a guardian", which Isra'il ibn Yunus and others relate from his grandfather [[person/abu-ishaq-al-sabii|Abu Ishaq]] from Abu Burda from Abu Musa, connected, while Sufyan al-Thawri and Shu'ba relate it from Abu Ishaq from Abu Burda from the Prophet, mursal. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 71} Al-Khatib reported that most hadith scholars give the ruling to the mursal version, some to the majority, some to the better memory, and some to whoever connected it if he is upright and precise — which al-Khatib said is the correct view. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 71} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 72} {cite:kifaya-khatib-hyderabad-1357 p. 411} Ibn al-Salah endorsed this in law and legal theory and noted that [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]], asked about "no marriage without a guardian", ruled for those who connected it, saying "an addition from a trustworthy narrator is accepted", even though those who gave it as mursal were Shu'ba and Sufyan, mountains of memory and precision. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 72} Likewise when some raise a hadith to the Prophet and others stop it at a Companion, the sounder ruling goes to the trustworthy narrator's addition of connection or raising, since he affirms and the other is silent — and even if the other denied it, the affirmer comes first because he knew what the other missed. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 72} Ibn al-Salah grants that connected and mursal versions involve a kind of conflict, since giving a hadith as mursal is a kind of criticism and preferring it resembles preferring censure to vindication; the answer is that censure is preferred for its added knowledge, and here the added knowledge lies with the one who connected. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 88}
