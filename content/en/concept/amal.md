## Summary
In the lexicon, 'amal is a trade and an act; in the scholars' general usage it covers the acts of the limbs and of the heart, and some define it as bringing a thing about by speech or by deed {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11839}. The five rulings govern it: what the Lawgiver requires as binding is obligatory, what he requires without binding is recommended, what he forbids bindingly is forbidden, what he discourages without binding is disliked, and what he leaves to choice is permitted {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11839}. The servant is rewarded for obedience and punished for disobedience, unless God pardons him {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11839}.

## The dignity of work, in Omar Abdelkafy's view
The Sheikh holds that in Islam there is no high work and low work so long as the work is honest, saying he would like to kiss the head of the street sweeper on behalf of the neighbourhood because he performs a branch of faith; he mentions the man who goes beneath the ground to repair the sewers, criticises 'class' and 'the Arab contempt' for some kinds of work, and notes that Arab identity cards record one's 'mihna', which he relates to 'mahana', humiliation. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:05:00}
He cites that [[person/dawud|Dawud]] and [[person/sulayman|Sulayman]] were prophets, kings and wealthy, yet were told 'work, O family of Dawud, in gratitude'; the hadith of the hand roughened by work that God and His Messenger love; and the report that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] struck with his whip a man who sat idle in the mosque, citing a crippled crow that was fed, and told him: why not be the healthy crow that feeds it? He holds that degrees are not the measure of success, telling of a woman with three doctorates who forgot to marry until forty-four and said 'take all my degrees and give me the title of mother', and relates a 'testament' he ascribes to Marilyn Monroe wishing for a husband and children instead of fame (needs_source: both stories as the Sheikh tells them; the attribution of the testament is not established); he advises parents not to grieve if a child does not succeed in studies, for he may excel in something else. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep20 00:10:00}
