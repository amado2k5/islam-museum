## Summary
Ibn Sina says the matter easiest to evacuate is that in the vessels, while what lies in organs and joints is hard to bring out; that someone evacuated must not rush to much raw food; that leaving a remnant of the matter is less harmful than pressing on until the strength gives way; and that evacuation is done little by little when the matter is sticky or tightly mixed with blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.275-276}.

## The limit and the return of a habit
Leaving a remnant of the matter needing evacuation is less harmful than pressing the evacuation until the strength gives way, and nature often dissolves that remnant. So long as the humour evacuated is of the proper kind and the patient bears it, excess need not be feared; sometimes evacuation to the point of fainting is needed for someone strong with much matter. Where the stopping of an accustomed diarrhoea has brought on an illness, resuming that evacuation mostly cures it, as for someone in whom the stopping of earwax or nasal mucus has led to blockages, whose return takes them away {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.275-276}.

## Food and gradualness
Someone who has been evacuated must not hurry to eat much raw food, which nature would draw in undigested; if something is needed it is little by little, so that what enters is well digested. When the matter is very sticky or tightly mixed with blood and cannot be evacuated at once, it is evacuated little by little, as in sciatica, chronic joint pains, cancer, chronic scab and chronic boils {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.275-276}.
