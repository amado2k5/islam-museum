## Nature and selection
A soft, porous marine body like felt. It is said to be an animal that moves over what it clings to and does not leave it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. The fresh kind is stronger and dries more, because of the strength of the sea's nature {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot in the first degree and dry in the second; its stony part is close to it in nature and less hot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}.

## Uses by organ
It dries strongly, especially the fresh kind burned with oil; its ash stops bleeding from a cut or a lancing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. Burned with oil it cauterises the place and stops bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. Swellings: it dries phlegmatic swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. Wounds and ulcers: dipped in vinegar and laid on wounds it heals them, and boiled with honey it heals deep ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. Chest: burned with oil it is good for treating the spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. Excretory organs: the book says its stony part crushes bladder stone, in the view of others than Galen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}.
