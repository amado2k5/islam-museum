## Nature and selection
A herb whose power, according to Galen, is close to that of the herb used to polish glass. The name covers two herbs: one that Galen describes, which smells of mallow and has no firmness; and another that Dioscorides describes, a creeping herb with fine stems, cultivated, with a sky-blue flower and seed like coriander seed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}. A footnote says the kinds are many and all help poisons and swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.370}.

## Temperament and actions
The first has no astringency; the other is drying and reddening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}.

## Uses by organ
Wounds and ulcers: the kind Dioscorides described draws out thorns and the membrane of the viscera, joins wounds and cleans ulcers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}. Head: it helps epilepsy when drunk, and facial palsy strongly when inhaled as snuff {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.371}.
