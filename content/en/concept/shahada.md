## Summary
"The two testimonies" are the testimony that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger — the first thing the Prophet mentioned in his saying "Islam is built on five" {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.192}. They are one of [[concept/five-pillars|the five pillars of Islam]].

## The word shahada and its senses
The Encyclopaedia of Islam, second edition, treats shahada as the verbal noun of the verb shahida, whose meanings run in a sequence: to be present somewhere, as the opposite of being absent; from that, to see with one's own eyes and be a witness of an event; from that, to bear witness to what one has seen; and finally to attest or certify something in general {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 210}. The article then separates the uses of the noun into four.

## Four uses of the term
In the first, shahada can mean what is seen, what is visible. The article links this to the Qur'anic description of God as knowing both the unseen and the seen {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 210}.

In the second and more common use, it is the act of witnessing, in which a person declares that what he saw or claims to have seen really took place. The article lists the Qur'anic settings of this use as a debt, a bequest, an accusation of adultery and a divorce, and notes that legal language developed from this starting point; it refers readers to the entry on the witness, [[concept/shahid|shahid]] {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 210}. The museum's record of testimony in law is [[concept/testimony-shahada|testimony]].

The third use, the subject of this record, is the religious one. The article says it is not directly Qur'anic but is implicit in several verses, and that in it shahada means the Islamic profession of faith, the act of declaring that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is the Messenger of God. It adds that one sometimes speaks in this case of al-shahadatan, "the two shahadas", and refers readers to the entry on the [[concept/tashahhud-testimony-in-prayer|tashahhud]] {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 210} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 211}.

The fourth use extends the third. The article says shahada can denote the supreme way of affirming the Islamic faith, that of the martyr in the cause of Islam, and refers to the entries on the place of martyrdom and on the martyr, [[concept/martyr-shahid|martyr]] {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 9 p. 211}.

## The first shahada and the word kalima
In its entry on kalima, the spoken word, the same encyclopaedia records a related reading. In the Qur'anic passage that says Abraham made an everlasting word among his descendants, the commentators, among them [[person/al-baydawi-abd-allah-ibn-umar|al-Baydawi]], usually stressed that the word meant an affirmation of the Oneness of God, which the entry calls the equivalent of the first shahada {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 545}. The entry also treats the divine word more widely, including the title of [[person/isa-ibn-maryam|Jesus]] as a word from God and the discussion of [[concept/kalam-allah|the speech of God]], and these topics are described in their own records rather than here {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 545}.

## What the article does not say
The article is a short headword entry. It does not set out the conditions attached to the profession or any difference between the schools about it, and so the museum records none from this source. The sections above from the creed and hadith sources and from the lecture series remain the record's other witnesses.

## In Gabriel's hadith
When the Prophet defined [[concept/islam-din|Islam]] for Gabriel he said: "That you worship God and associate nothing with Him" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}.

## What is not covered here
The creed books treat the meaning of "there is no god but God", its conditions and the categories of tawhid; that will be added in a later batch from the creed sources with their authors' sayings, and is not presented here since it is not in the sources consulted.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh says that the highest branch of faith is the saying "there is no god but God", that the first of the branches is the testimony, and that Abu Jahl understood its meaning {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:39:00}. He says that one Companion treated trustworthiness, truthfulness and refusing false witness as part of the meaning of "there is no god but God", so that for him prayer was not separated from trustworthiness {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:09:30}.

The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that the believer who prays carries the ethics of "there is no god but God" {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep06 00:15:30}, and that the words of the testimony that come out at a critical moment are a sign of a heart full of faith {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 00:17:00}.
