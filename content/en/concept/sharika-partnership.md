## Definition
In language sharika is the mixing of two shares. The technical sense has two kinds: ownership partnership, in which two or more hold one thing or its equivalent in common, and contract partnership, by which two properties are mixed in fact or by ruling so that each partner may dispose of the other's share. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9992}

## Ownership partnership
Ownership partnership divides into a debt held jointly (such as a sum owed by a merchant to two people) and a non-debt partnership (in goods or benefits); and into voluntary and compulsory forms, the compulsory form arising when bags burst and their contents mix. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9993} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9994}
If one partner mixes another's property with his own without permission, the Hanafis, Malikis, most Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that he owns it and owes its equivalent; some jurists hold the property stays shared. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9994}
Each partner may sell his share to his partner, and a shared property is not given away without division unless it cannot be divided. A partner may sell his share to an outsider without consent according to the Hanafis and Shafi'is, except where harm follows. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9995}
A partner may not use the shared property in the presence of the other without his leave; one who plants shared land without leave is treated as a usurper. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9996} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9997}
For upkeep of an indivisible shared thing, such as a single animal, a well or a canal, the partner may be compelled to share the cost according to the Hanafis and Malikis, and the Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold so in one view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9997} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9998} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9999}
When a partner collects part of a shared debt, it counts as collected for the whole, and the silent partner may claim his share from the collector. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10001} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10002}

## Contract partnership: mufawada and 'inan
By equality, contract partnership is mufawada (full partnership), in which the Hanafis require equality in capital, disposal, profit, surety and capacity to act, and 'inan (limited), in which no such equality is required; the Hanbalis do not require it in 'inan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10010}
The Malikis do not require equality in mufawada and let each partner authorise the other to act without consulting him at each step. The Hanbalis give mufawada two senses: all four kinds together, or joint liability for everything that falls to both, provided neither takes rare gains or fines. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10011} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10012}
By generality, the Hanafis divide it into unrestricted and restricted. In mufawada every trade is open to the partners; the Malikis alone recognise the compelled partnership, in which a bidder may enter a purchase in the market, subject to conditions such as trading in the place of purchase and not outbidding the buyer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10012} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10013}

## Formation
The contract is formed by offer and acceptance. The Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis accept an action that shows consent in place of words, while the Shafi'is do not accept action in place of words. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10013} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10014}

## General conditions
A partnership requires that the matter contracted on be deputisable, and that each partner be competent to appoint and be appointed, so a minor not licensed to trade cannot enter one; and it cannot be formed over free-for-all acts such as cutting firewood, gathering wood or hunting, according to the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10015} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10016}
The profit must be a known proportion of the whole; if not, the contract is void. A fixed sum of profit for one partner is void: the Hanafis hold a fixed wage paid from outside the capital valid while the condition is void, and Ibn al-Mundhir reports agreement that no partnership stands with a fixed sum of profit for one partner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10017}
Mufawada requires capacity for surety; a minor cannot enter it even with his guardian's leave. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10018}
The special conditions of mufawada, of the partnerships of labour and credit, the rights and duties of the partners, the ending of the partnership and the invalid partnership are not yet summarised here and will be added in a later pass. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10019}
