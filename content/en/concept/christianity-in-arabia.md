## Overview
In *al-Rahiq al-Makhtum*, Christianity came to the lands of the Arabs through the occupation of [[polity/aksumite-kingdom|Abyssinia]] and of [[polity/byzantine-empire|the Romans]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 6/297}

## Yemen and Najran
The first Abyssinian occupation of Yemen was in 340 CE and lasted until 378 CE, and in that time Christian missionary activity entered the lands of Yemen. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 6/297} Around this time an ascetic man whose prayers were answered and who had miracles, called [[person/faymiyun|Faymiyun]], entered [[place/najran|Najran]], invited them to the Christian faith, and the people of Najran saw in him signs of his truthfulness and of the truth of his religion, so that they responded to Christianity and adopted it. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/31-34} See [[group/christians-of-najran|the Christians of Najran]] and [[event/massacre-of-najran|the trench]].

## Abraha and the Yemeni Ka'ba
When the Abyssinians occupied Yemen in reaction to what Dhu Nuwas had done, and [[person/abraha|Abraha]] established his rule over it, he began spreading Christianity with the greatest energy and on the widest scale. He built a church in Yemen called the Yemeni Ka'ba ([[building/al-qullays|al-Qullays]]), and he wanted to divert the pilgrimage of the Arabs to it and to demolish the House of God in Mecca. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}

## The Arabs of the north
The Arabs of [[group/ghassan|the Ghassanids]], the tribes of [[group/banu-taghlib|Taghlib]] and [[group/tayy|Tayy]], and others adopted Christianity because of their proximity to the Romans, and some kings of [[polity/lakhmid-kingdom|al-Hira]] also adopted it. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}

## The author's assessment
The author holds that when Islam came, Christianity had reverted to a paganism that was hard to understand, and had created confusion between God and man. It had no real influence in the souls of the Arabs who professed it, because its teachings were distant from the way of life they were accustomed to. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.42}

## What is not recorded here
The last paragraph is the author's judgement from within the Sunni framework. The views of modern scholarship, and of the Christians themselves, on the Christians of the Peninsula and their sects have not yet been added. See also [[concept/kutub-al-nasara|the books of the Christians]].

## For young visitors
Before Islam there were Arab Christians in Yemen, Najran, and the north of the Peninsula. Their religion came to them from Abyssinia and from the Romans.
