## Summary
Ibn Sina tells thick clear urine from thin by the motion of its waves and its froth, and says a thin urine heavily dyed owes the dye not to ripening but to a mixing of bile, since ripening shows in consistency before colour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.190}.

## Thick clear urine
Thick urine may be clear and transparent or turbid. The difference between thick clear urine and thin is that when it is set in waves by shaking, the waving parts of thick clear urine do not shrink but large waves arise in it, moving slowly, and when it foams its froth has many bubbles that are slow to burst. Such urine comes from well-digested phlegm, or from yolk-like bile if it has a yellow dye, and if it has no dye it shows the dissolving of vitreous phlegm, which is often so in the urine of epileptics {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.190}.

## Thin dyed urine
Thin urine heavily dyed is known not to owe its dye to ripening, else ripening would have worked on its consistency first; it comes from a mixing of bile, for the first act of ripening is the setting of consistency and then the dye. Ripening in consistency is a better sign than in colour, so thin yellow urine that lasts through an acute illness shows evil and a slackening of the digesting power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.190}.
