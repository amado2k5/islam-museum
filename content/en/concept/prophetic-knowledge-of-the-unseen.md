## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that the Prophet's knowledge of the news of earlier prophets, though he was unlettered, is one of the signs that his prophethood is true {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1066} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1067}.

## Unlettered and unteached
He says the Prophet could not read or write and sat with no teacher, and that no one claimed he had learned from anyone on his trading journeys, since that never happened {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1066} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1067}.

## The rejected charge
He reports that some polytheists claimed the Prophet sat with a young man at al-Marwa who taught him. He answers that the tongue they point to is foreign while the Qur'an is in clear Arabic {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1067}.

## The verses of absence
He reads the verses that say "you were not there" (in al-Qasas, Al Imran, Hud and al-Shura) as presenting the news as witnessed, though it comes by revelation {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1067} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1068} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1069}.
