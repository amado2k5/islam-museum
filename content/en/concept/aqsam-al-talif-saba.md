## Overview
In *Kashf al-zunun*, in the chapter on authors and their works, Haji Khalifa says that authorship has seven divisions, and that no reasonable scholar writes except in one of them. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.115-117}

## The seven divisions
An author may present something no one has preceded him in, which he invents; or something incomplete, which he completes; or something obscure, which he explains; or something long, which he abridges without harming its meanings; or something scattered, which he gathers; or something mixed, which he arranges; or something whose author made an error, which he corrects. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.115-117}

## The benefits of a book
Whoever writes in a field that others have preceded him in should ensure that his book has five benefits: drawing out what was difficult, gathering what was scattered, explaining what was obscure, good arrangement and composition, or removing superfluity and excess length. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.115-117}

## The eight heads
It has been the custom of authors to set out at the head of each book the headings that announce it, which they called the *ru'us*. There are eight: the purpose, the benefit, the title, the author, the kind of knowledge, and others. {cite:kashf-zunun-furqan p.115-117}
