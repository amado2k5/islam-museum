## Nature and selection
Galen said some is fresh and some dry, its power compound, the earthy bitter part predominating along with a tepid watery part and a slight astringent roughness. The author holds that its watery part is cold, not tepid, unless a fine hot substance mingles with it and leaves quickly. Hunayn said Galen denied coldness to coriander in opposition to Dioscorides, and the author says that Rufus, Archigenes and others testified to its coldness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Temperament
Cold from the end of the first degree to the third, dry in the second according to Ibn Juraij, or rather the third. The author holds that the dry kind leans to a slight warming. Galen said all of it leans toward warming, perhaps because of a fine substance that dissolves and does not remain when drunk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Actions and properties
It has astringency and numbing, and its juice with milk quiets every violent throbbing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Swellings and pustules
It helps hot swellings, with white lead, vinegar and rose oil; with honey and oil for hives and the Persian fire; and with bean flour, barley meal or chickpea flour for scrofula. The author records Galen's question, if it dissolves scrofula how can it be cold, and answers that it is by a property or by a fine penetrating substance in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It helps dizziness from a bilious or phlegmatic vapour, and epilepsy from the same; its property is to keep vapour from the head, so it is put in the food of the epileptic whose trouble comes from stomach vapour. Much of it, fresh or dry, muddles the mind; the fresh puts to sleep and stops nosebleed; the dry as a powder and a rinse of the fresh juice help thrush {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Eyes
It brings darkness to the sight; its juice dropped in quiets throbbing in the eye, especially with women's milk, and a poultice of its leaf keeps matter from flowing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Chest and breathing
It helps hot palpitation and, drunk with plantain water, stops spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Slow to digest, it strengthens a hot stomach and, fried, stops vomiting. It is said to quiet sour belching after a meal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Excretory organs
Its seed, roasted, binds the bowels. Fresh coriander with honey and oil helps hot swellings of the testicles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that fresh or dry it breaks sexual power and erection and dries the semen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.

## Poisons and bites
Its juice, drunk in a large quantity, is said to kill by bringing on distress {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.534}.
