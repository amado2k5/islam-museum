## Definition
Ikhtilaf al-dar (difference of abodes). In the lexicon dar is the place, comprising the open ground and the building, and it is also used for a city; for the jurists the difference of the two abodes means the difference of the two states to which the two persons belong. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1952}
A difference of abodes between two Muslims has no effect, since all the lands of Islam are one abode; al-Sarakhsi said: people of justice inherit from one another, because the abode of Islam is an abode of rulings, and difference of power and ownership does not separate the abode among Muslims, since Islam's ruling joins them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1953}
What al-Attabi said, that one who became Muslim without migrating to us does not inherit from an original Muslim, is answered by Ibn Abidin: that was at the start of Islam when migration was obligatory, and God denied guardianship between one who migrated and one who did not; now the rule of migration is abrogated, on the saying «there is no migration after the conquest». {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1953}
Al-Sarakhsi said: the abode of war is not an abode of rulings but of domination; with the difference of power and ownership the abodes differ among them, and when their abodes differ, inheritance ceases; likewise those who come to us under safe-conduct, who are each taken as in the power of the realm from which he came. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1953}
The dhimmis are of the abode of Islam, and so differ in abode from the people of war. The people of war among themselves may share an abode or not; Ibn Abidin: their difference is by the difference of power and of rule, so inheritance ends, since it is built on protection and guardianship; but if there is mutual support against their enemies, the abode and inheritance stand. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1953}
Kinds of difference of abodes in the Hanafi school: two abodes may differ in fact only, in ruling only, or in both. In fact only: a safe-conduct holder in our land and a warrior in theirs, whose abodes differ in fact but who are one in ruling, since the safe-conduct holder is in ruling of the people of war. In ruling only: a safe-conduct holder and a dhimmi in our land, who are in two abodes in ruling, since the safe-conduct holder is in ruling of the people of war as he may return. In both: a warrior in their land and a dhimmi in ours, like two warriors in two different abodes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954}
Inheritance: difference of abodes in ruling only or in ruling and fact is one of the bars to inheritance for the Hanafis, so a dhimmi does not inherit from a warrior or safe-conduct holder, nor they from him, even if their religion is one; and a warrior does not inherit from a warrior if their abodes differ. Inheritance stands among safe-conduct holders of our land from the same abode, and between a safe-conduct holder in our land and a warrior in theirs since their abode is one in ruling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954}
The Shafi'is are close: no inheritance between a dhimmi and a warrior; the safe-conduct holder and the treaty-holder are, on the sounder view, in the ruling of the dhimmis, so they inherit from and to the dhimmi, but not from the warriors; another view makes them like warriors. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954}
The Hanbalis and Malikis hold that difference of abodes does not prevent inheritance as long as the religions agree; one Hanbali view, of Abu Ya'la, is that a warrior does not inherit from a dhimmi nor a dhimmi from a warrior, while the people of the abode of Islam and of war inherit from a safe-conduct holder. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954}
The religion of a child: a child follows the better of his parents in religion, and the Hanafis require for following that abode be one for the follower and the followed; if the child is in the abode of war and his father in the abode of Islam, and the father becomes Muslim, the child does not follow him; but if the father is in the abode of war and becomes Muslim and the child is in the abode of Islam, the child follows him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954}
Separation of spouses: the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that separation does not occur merely through difference of abodes; the Hanafis hold that a difference of the spouses' abodes, in fact and in ruling, requires separation; so if a warrior married a warrior woman and one of them entered the abode of Islam and became Muslim... {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1954}
