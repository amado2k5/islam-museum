## Treating the bleeding arising from it
Treating the bleeding after cutting the uvula and the tonsils: cupping-glasses are set on the neck and the two breasts, and venesection of the participating lower veins — like the axillary and the like — is done as a drawing-bleeding; the restraining singles and the smears used for it are what he described, and the truly cooling are used — ice-water and the known cold astringent juices. Of the tried things having a specific property in this chapter is a drug witnessed for by one known among the scholars as Diyukhânis — it is al-kûhârik — and also lamb's-tongue juice when used, above all with what he described of the tablets and the sealed clay; and nothing hot of them must be used, only the truly cold, for heat by its drawing undoes the drug's action (the venesection and cupping carried as the book names them, not procedures; the testimony and tried-thing sayings carried on their attribution as they came) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.300}
