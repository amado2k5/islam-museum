## Definition
Ulu is a word that stays attached to a following noun; its singular is dhu. Ulu al-amr means those in authority: the leaders and the learned. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3328} The lexical amr is a request for an act from a higher to a lower, and it also means a matter or a state of affairs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3328}

## Interpretation
The phrase in the Qur'an (Nisa 4:59) has two main views. The first is the people of the Qur'an and knowledge, the view of Malik, Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and Ata', because the origin of the command and the judgement lies with them. The second is the rulers and governors, which al-Tabari preferred, since reports are sound that obedience is owed to the imams and governors in what is God's obedience and in the public interest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3328}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3329}

## Conditions
The caliph, the highest rank of those in authority, must be a Muslim, free, adult, sane, male, learned, upright and capable. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3329} Capacity includes the power to enforce the penalties, sound senses and limbs, courage, and judgement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3329} Those below the caliph have fewer conditions, measured by strength and trustworthiness. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3329}

## Duties of the subjects
- Obedience within the limits of the Law, as the verse of Nisa (4:59) and the saying "no obedience to a creature in disobedience of the Creator" show. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3330}
- Support in what is not sinful, and counsel, as the saying "religion is counsel" teaches. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3330}
- Consultation with people of judgement, which the Qur'an commends. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3331}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3332}

## Duties of those in authority
- Guarding the religion on its settled bases, enforcing rulings between litigants, and protecting the state. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3330}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3331}
- Enforcing penalties, guarding the frontiers, fighting those who oppose Islam after the call, fighting rebels, brigands and highwaymen, and concluding treaties and covenants of protection. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3331}
- Appointing viziers, governors, judges, leaders of the pilgrimage and army commanders, setting stipends from the treasury, and choosing trustworthy agents. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3331}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3332}
- Acting in the public interest, following the rule that the ruler's management of subjects depends on the public interest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3330}
