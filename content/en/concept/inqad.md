## Definition
Inqad is lexically the opposite of dissolution, with the senses of obligation, binding and confirmation. For the jurists it depends on the subject: the forming of a worship is its valid beginning, the forming of a child is the mother's conception, and the forming of a contract is the binding of offer to acceptance in the manner the law requires. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3510}

## Forming and validity
Validity is reached only when all pillars and conditions are complete; forming may occur before the conditions are complete. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3510}

## How forming occurs
- By words: the contract formulas the law recognises, such as marriage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3510}
- By act: the mu'atah, which most jurists accept. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3510}
- By allusion with intent: for acts a person can conclude alone, such as divorce, manumission and release. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3510}
- In jest: marriage, divorce and return take effect in jest, on a report that these three are serious whether meant seriously or not; sales and similar contracts are not formed in jest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3511}
- By sign: most contracts are formed by the sign of a mute; most jurists do not accept the sign of one able to speak except with an excuse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3511}

## Void and invalid acts
What is void, whether worship or contract, is by agreement not formed. The forming of invalid transactions is disputed; the Hanafis hold that an invalid contract is formed but not valid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3511}

## Forming the imamate and offices
The imamate is formed by the choice of those who loose and bind, or by the appointment of the holder to his successor together with their allegiance. Muslims agree on the validity of such an appointment, since Abu Bakr appointed Umar and Umar left the matter to a council. Offices are formed by speech in person, and by writing in absence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3511}
