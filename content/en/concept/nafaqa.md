## Summary
Nafaqa, in the lexicon, is what is spent. As a term it is what a person needs to sustain life in the usual way without extravagance; it is owed for marriage, kinship and ownership. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15863}

## A wife's maintenance
The proofs are the Qur'an, the Sunna, consensus and reason, since the wife is held for her husband and barred from earning for his right. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15864}
The cause: a sound contract (the Hanafis, the sounder Maliki view and the older Shafi'i view), or her yielding herself (the majority, the Malikis and Hanbalis). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15865} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15866}
Conditions: she must be of age or fit for intercourse, must yield herself when asked, and the marriage must be sound. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15866} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15867}
Those with no maintenance: the disobedient wife, one separated by her own sin, and, in the Shafi'i view, one who is divorced irrevocably and not pregnant. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15867} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15868}
Amount: sufficiency (Hanafis, Malikis, most Hanbalis); a fixed measure (the Shafi'i view); customary amounts (Malikis); or what the judge fixes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15868} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15870}
Means: the husband's means (Hanafis and Shafi'is), the wife's (some Hanafis), or both (Malikis and Hanbalis). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15870} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15871}
Kinds: food, clothing and housing, and, as the schools differ, medicine, a servant and cleaning things. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15872} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15873}
Sickness, imprisonment and absence: the sick wife keeps her maintenance; for an imprisoned husband the schools differ; for an absent husband it is owed from his property, with the judge's order. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15877} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15878} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15879} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15880}
The divorced and widowed: a revocably divorced woman has maintenance and housing; an irrevocably divorced one has housing only unless pregnant; a widow has no maintenance except when pregnant, on some views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15886} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15887} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15888}
Travel: for obligatory pilgrimage without her husband the majority differ; leaving without permission voids the maintenance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15891} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15893}
Separation for non-maintenance: the Malikis, the sounder Shafi'i view and the Hanbali view allow the wife to seek separation; the Hanafis do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15896} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15897} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15898}
Debt: whether maintenance becomes a debt by judgment, agreement or lapse of time; the Hanafis require a judgment or agreement, the others treat it as a debt once it falls due and is refused. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15899} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15900}

## Relatives
Ascendants and descendants: owed by agreement; the schools differ on the conditions of religion, inheritance and need. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15901} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15903}
Several ascendants or descendants: owed by the father alone when he can pay; otherwise shared by heirs according to their shares (Hanafis and Hanbalis), or by the nearest and males (Shafi'is). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15910} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15911}
Collaterals: owed to a mahram relative by the Hanafis and to any heir by the Hanbalis; not owed by the Malikis or Shafi'is. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15913} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15914}
Mixed ascendants, descendants and collaterals: the Hanafis prefer the nearer and the descent link; the Hanbalis follow inheritance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15915} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15918}
Insolvency: the means that bring the duty are measured as the surplus above one day's and night's food (majority) or the zakat threshold (Hanafis). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15918} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15919} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15920}

## Slaves, animals, loans, lost property, pledges, the helpless
Slaves: food and clothing by sufficiency, owed by the master. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15923}
Animals: owed by the owner; the Hanbalis and Shafi'is compel the owner to feed, sell, hire or slaughter. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15923} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15924}
Loan, deposit and pledge: the owner bears the upkeep of a loaned item in the sounder view, the owner of a deposit and the pledgor of a pledge. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15925} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15928}
Lost property: the finder's position depends on the judge's leave and on whether the item lasts a year. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15926} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15927}
Foundling, orphan and the helpless poor: maintained from their own property, then their relatives, then the treasury. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15928} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15929}
