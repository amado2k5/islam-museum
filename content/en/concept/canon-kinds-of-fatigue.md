## Summary
In the twelfth chapter Ibn Sina counts the three kinds of fatigue, ulcerous, tension and inflammatory, and a fourth, the desiccatory, and describes the signs of the ulcerous kind and its cause in thin sharp superfluities or the melting of flesh and fat by hard movement {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.242-243}.

## The kinds
Ibn Sina says the kinds of fatigue are three, with a fourth added, and its ways of arising are two. The three are the ulcerous, the tension and the inflammatory, and the one added is the fatigue called desiccatory {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.242}.

## The ulcerous kind
In the ulcerous kind the person feels in the skin something like the touch of sores, or in the depth of the skin; it may be felt on touch, or by the sufferer as he moves, and he may feel pricks like thorns. They dislike movements, even stretching, or stretch weakly; when it grows strong they have chills, and if it grows further, shivering and fever. Its cause is an abundance of thin sharp superfluities or the melting of flesh and fat by hard movement, in short bad humours spread in the vessels; the printed text is garbled in part of this passage, so only what reads clearly is given {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.242-243}.
