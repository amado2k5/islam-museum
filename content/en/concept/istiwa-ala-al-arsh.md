## Summary
Sha'rawi warns against reading the istiwa attributed to God in the human sense. God's attributes are not like the attributes of creatures: as God's life, knowledge and power are not measured by human life, knowledge and power, so istiwa is not measured by human sitting {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.233}.

## The comparison
He notes that kings sit on their thrones and a person sits on a chair, then holds that the word istiwa in God's case is not to be taken as that {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.233}. He argues that God's life is not like human life, and that his knowledge and power are not like human knowledge and power {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.233}.

## Reason and limit
He holds that the human mind cannot grasp an act related to God's essence, and so one says "glory be to God" at every act related to God's self {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.233}. He adds that this speech is God's own, and that the speaker is God {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.233}.

## In al-Ash'ari's al-Ibana
In [[work/al-ibana-ashari|al-Ibana]], [[person/al-ashari|Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari]] has a "chapter on the rising over the Throne". He says God is risen over His Throne as He said, and argues from the verses on good words ascending to Him, raising and ascent, from Pharaoh's wish to look up to the god of Moses, and from the fact that Muslims raise their hands towards the sky in prayer. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 33-34} He answers the explanation of istiwa' as taking control and subduing, which he ascribes to the Mu'tazila, Jahmiyya and Haruriyya: God controls all things, so if istiwa' were control the Throne would have nothing special in it. {cite:al-ibana-ashari-dar-ibn-zaydun p. 34}
