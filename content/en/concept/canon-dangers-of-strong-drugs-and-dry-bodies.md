## Summary
Ibn Sina warns of strong drugs such as hellebore, which convulse a clean body, stir the moisture of a full one so that it chokes, and draw to the viscera what is hard to expel, tells that a dry, hard-fleshed body be moistened beforehand with fatty foods, and mentions the harm of poisonous spurges and barley meal as a remedy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.281}.

## The danger
If a dry, hard-fleshed body must be evacuated with a strong drug such as hellebore, one should first take great care to moisten it with fatty foods. Strong drugs such as hellebore are very dangerous in general, for they convulse a clean body, stir the moisture of a full body in a choking way and draw to the viscera what is hard to expel. The harm of the poisonous spurges such as mazarion and spurge is cut short with curdled milk if they go too far. A drug often leaves its smell in the stomach so that it seems still to be there, and its remedy is barley meal to wash it out, the most fitting of the powders {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.281}.
