# The limits of God and the major sins

The Qur'an speaks of the 'limits of God' (hudud Allah) at the points of its great rulings: in divorce, «these are the limits of God, so do not transgress them» (al-Baqara 229); in fasting, «these are the limits of God, so do not approach them» (al-Baqara 187); and it warns «whoever transgresses the limits of God has wronged himself» (al-Talaq 1) and «whoever transgresses the limits of God — it is those who are the wrongdoers» (al-Baqara 229). In the jurists' idiom, 'hadd' then became the name of a penalty fixed by the Law as due to God: the hadd of fornication, of slander, of theft, of wine, of apostasy and of brigandage, with the discretionary punishment and, for some, retaliation attached — the widening from the Qur'anic phrase to the fiqh chapters recorded by the Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia's hudud entry {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

'Kaba'ir' is the plural of kabira: the sins the Law treats with its severest prohibition, some of them answered by a hadd in this world or a special warning for the next. The Qur'an divides the major from the minor in «those who avoid the major sins and immoralities, except the slips» (al-Najm 32) and «if you avoid the major sins you are forbidden, We will expiate your ill-deeds» (al-Nisa 31). In the Sunnah, the hadith «avoid the seven destroyers» of Sahih al-Bukhari (2766) and Sahih Muslim (89) names: associating partners with God, sorcery, killing the soul God forbade except by right, consuming usury, consuming the orphan's wealth, fleeing the day of battle, and slandering chaste, believing, unaware women {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

The two themes meet where the great sins are what the hudud answer in this world or warn against in the next: fornication, theft, slander and wine are major sins by consensus, each with a fixed penalty in the Law; and the major sins that carry no hadd — shirk, disobeying parents, consuming usury — keep their warning in the hereafter and their door of repentance open.
