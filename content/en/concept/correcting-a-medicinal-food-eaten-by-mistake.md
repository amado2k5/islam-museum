## Summary
Ibn Sina says that when a medicinal food has been taken by mistake its digestion and ripening must be managed and the expected imbalance guarded against by following it with its opposite: a cold one such as qiththa, cucumber or gourd is corrected with garlic and leek, a hot one with cucumber and purslane, and a blocking one with what opens and evacuates, then a fitting fast {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.228-229}.

## The principle
If the error occurs and something of the medicinal foods is eaten, its digestion and ripening should be managed, and the temperamental disorder expected from it guarded against by using its opposite right after, until it is digested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## Cold, hot and blocking
If it is cold, like qiththa, cucumber and gourd, it is balanced by its opposite such as garlic and leek; if hot, again by its opposite such as qiththa and purslane; if it is of the blocking kind, one uses what opens and evacuates, then fasts a fitting fast {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.

## Afterwards
Nothing more is eaten until the appetite is true and the stomach and upper bowels are empty of the first food {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.228}.
