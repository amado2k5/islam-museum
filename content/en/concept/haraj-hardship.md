## Summary
Hardship (haraj) means narrowness in the lexicon; jurists use it for whatever causes narrowness to the body, the soul, or both. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6981} For the usulis it is a graded notion, and only what the lawgiver has counted as hardship is taken into account. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6981} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6982} It is distinguished from the concession (rukhsa), a permission granted for an excuse while the forbidding cause remains; from the strict rule (azima), what God imposes on the servants; from toil (mashaqqa); from necessity (darura), a state in which one would die or nearly die without what is forbidden; and from need (haja), a lack that, if unmet, produces great corruption. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6982} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6983} Hardship is removed by the law, on the basis of the verses "God desires ease for you and not hardship" and "He has laid no hardship on you in religion"; the maxims "hardship brings ease" and "necessities permit forbidden things" are drawn from this. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6983}
