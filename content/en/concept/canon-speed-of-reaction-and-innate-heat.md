## Summary
The sixth genus is how quickly an organ is acted on: if it warms quickly it is hot in temperament, if it cools quickly, cold. Ibn Sina answers the objection that a thing is acted on by its opposite, then explains the innate heat as nature's instrument for warding off harm and keeping the humours from rotting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.161-162}.

## The rule
If an organ warms quickly and without difficulty it is hot in temperament, since change toward the kindred kind is easier than change to the opposite; if it cools quickly, the reverse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.161}.

## The objection and the reply
Someone objects that a thing is acted on by its opposite, not by its like. Ibn Sina replies that the like that does not act is one whose quality is the same in kind and nature as the thing's; the hotter is not like the colder, and what is not the hotter is cold relative to the hotter, so it is acted on as cold in comparison, and also by the colder. The difference is that one increases its quality and helps its strongest part, the other lessens it, so change toward what increases its quality is easier {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.161}.

## The innate heat
The innate heat is nature's instrument. It wards off the harm of incoming heat by moving the spirit to repel it, clear its vapour, dissolve it and burn its matter, and it wards off incoming cold by opposition, so that hot poisons are resisted only by the innate heat. It shields the innate moistures from the foreign heat; when strong, nature can handle the moistures by ripening and digesting and keep them in health; when weak, the foreign heat takes hold of them, moves them in a foreign way, and putrefaction results {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.161-162}.

## No innate cold
The innate heat is an instrument for all the faculties, and cold works against it and helps only by accident; so one says innate heat and not innate cold, and cold is not credited with the household management of the body that heat is {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.162}.
