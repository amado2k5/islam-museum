## Summary
Not all of the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]]'s belongings were an inheritance to be divided; his Companions kept particular things after him and told reports about them: a cup with Anas, sandals, a waist-wrap and a cloak with 'A'isha, a short spear, a minbar. This page gives each item with its report. Items that have a record of their own are linked to it: [[object/khatam-al-nabi|the ring]], [[object/sayf-al-nabi|the sword]], [[object/qadah-al-nabi|the cup]], [[object/naal-al-nabi|the sandal]] and [[object/kisa-al-nabi|the cloak]]. For the general lists see [[concept/weapons-mounts-and-belongings-of-the-prophet|the Prophet's weapons, mounts and belongings]].

## The cup with Anas
Ahmad reports from 'Asim that he saw the Prophet's cup with Anas, with a silver band on it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 397} In the version al-Bayhaqi gives through al-Bukhari, 'Asim al-Ahwal saw the cup with Anas cracked and bound with a silver chain, a fine broad cup of nudar wood, and Anas said he had given the Prophet drink in it more times than he could say. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 397} From Ibn Sirin it is reported that there was an iron ring on it, and Anas wanted to put a ring of gold or silver in its place, but Abu Talha told him not to change anything the Messenger of God had made, so he left it. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 397} Hajjaj ibn Hassan says they were with Anas when he called for a vessel with three iron bands and an iron ring, took it from a black case, and they poured water in, drank, poured it over their heads and faces and invoked blessing on the Prophet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 397}

## The small staff buried with Anas
Ibn Kathir cites al-Bazzar that Anas had a small staff of the Prophet's, and when he died it was buried with him between his side and his shirt. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 395} Al-Bazzar says he knows it only through Mukhawwal ibn Rashid, truthful but with Shi'i leanings, and al-Bayhaqi says he is a Shi'i who brings reports no one else brings; on the next page it is said that the weakness in his reports is plain. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 395} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 396}

## The sandals
Al-Bukhari reports from 'Isa ibn Tahman that Anas brought out two bare sandals with two straps, and Thabit al-Bunani said these are the Prophet's sandals. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 396} Al-Tirmidhi in the Shama'il has from Ibn 'Abbas and Abu Hurayra that his sandal had two straps, and that the first to tie a single knot was 'Uthman. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 396} Ibn Kathir adds that about the year 600 a merchant became known for owning a single sandal said to be the Prophet's; al-Malik al-Ashraf Musa ibn al-'Adil wanted it for a large sum and the owner refused, but after his death it passed to al-Ashraf, who, when he built the Ashrafiyya hadith house, put it in a cabinet there with a servant paid forty dirhams a month, and by his account it was still there in his own time. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 396} See [[object/naal-al-nabi|the Prophet's sandal]].

## The waist-wrap and cloak with 'A'isha
Abu Burda reports that he went in to 'A'isha, and she brought out a thick Yemeni waist-wrap and a cloak of the kind called mulabbada, and swore by God that the Messenger of God was taken in these two garments. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3402} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3403} Ibn Kathir gives it and says that what became of these three entries after that is not known. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 398} He had said earlier that a red quilt he used to pray on was laid under him in his grave. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 398} 'A'isha and Ibn 'Abbas report that when death came upon him he began to throw a cloak of his over his face, and uncovered it when he was distressed. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 398}

## The short spear
Al-Tabarani, as al-Salihi gives it, reports that the Negus sent the Prophet three short spears; he kept one, gave one to 'Ali and one to 'Umar, and Bilal would walk with his in front of him at the two festivals so he could pray towards it. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4958} Ibn al-Qayyim describes the 'anaza as a small thing like a staff planted in front of him at festivals as a marker. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43}

## The minbar
Sahl ibn Sa'd reports that the Prophet sent to a woman to tell her carpenter slave to make some timbers for him to sit on when he spoke to the people; he made them from the tamarisk of al-Ghaba, and the Prophet prayed on them, said the takbir, bowed, then came down backwards and prostrated at its foot, and told the people he had done this so they would follow him and learn his prayer. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 1123} Ibn al-Athir says a Byzantine carpenter named Baqum or Baqul, client of Sa'id ibn al-'As, made the minbar with three steps, and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says the chain is not firm. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 99} Ibn Hajar says the maker's name is disputed and the chain of that report is weak. {cite:isaba scan vol. 1 p. 401} The editor of Subul al-Huda cites 'Abd al-Razzaq al-Fuwati that a fire in Sha'ban 654 AH struck the Prophet's mosque and his chamber and burned the minbar that dated from his time, and the caliph al-Musta'sim sent men to repair what had burned. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 1995}
