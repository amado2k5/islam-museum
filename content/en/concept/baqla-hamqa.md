## Nature and selection
Well known {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}. Selection: its juice is its strongest part {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold in the third degree and moist at the end of the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}. It is astringent, stops bleeding and chronic flow; its food value is small, and it is very strong against bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: rubbed on warts, it pulls them off by its property rather than its quality {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Swellings and pustules: a poultice for hot swellings and pustules that fear rot, and for redness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Head: a head wash mixed with wine helps head pustules; it removes a tooth by smoothing its roughness, and eases hot throbbing headache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Eyes: helps eye inflammation and enters kohls; too much of it causes a film over the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Breath: its juice helps spitting of blood, by its astringent strength {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Stomach and liver: helps inflammation of the stomach, drunk or applied, and inflamed liver, prevents bilious vomiting and weakens the appetite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Excretory organs: injected for abrasion of the intestines and bilious diarrhoea, and helps pains of the kidney and bladder and their sores {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}. Masarjawayh claimed it increases desire, which he thought holds in hot temperaments and not dry ones, and that it holds the flow of the menses {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}. Roasted and eaten, it stops diarrhoea {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.

Fevers: helps hot fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.405}.
