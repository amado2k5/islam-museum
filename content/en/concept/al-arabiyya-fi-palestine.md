## Summary
This page sets out the place of [[language/arabic|Arabic]] in [[place/palestine|Palestine]] from three sources that were read: the text of the Palestinian Basic Law, the Encyclopaedia Britannica article on Palestine, and The World Factbook pages for the West Bank and the Gaza Strip. It estimates no number of speakers and decides nothing on the classification of the dialect, because what was read does not say it. The constitutional text is added in its original wording, as supplied in a research summary that the museum did not check against the page.

## Official status
Article 4 of the Palestinian Basic Law, as the Constitute Project site shows it and dates it to 2003 with a 2005 revision, states that Arabic is the official language; its first paragraph says Islam is the official religion in Palestine {cite:constitute-palestine-basic-law-2005}. The Encyclopaedia Britannica, in its article on the Semitic languages, lists the Palestinian Authority among the bodies where Arabic is official {cite:britannica-semitic-languages}. So official status here rests on a founding text the reader can read, and the encyclopedia agrees with it.

## Constitutional text
The Amended Basic Law of 2003, Article 4, paragraph 3. The research summary supplied to the museum gives this Arabic wording: «اللغة العربية هي اللغة الرسمية.», which the museum translates: "The Arabic language is the official language". The summary names the Palestinian National Information Center, WAFA as the official source; the museum did not open that page, so the wording is taken from the summary as supplied, and the founder approved using it so on 21 November 2026 {cite:official-constitution-palestine}. The earlier citation of the Basic Law on the Constitute Project, which says the same, is kept beside it; nothing in the summary contradicts it.

## Language shares
No official figure. Language was not collected in the 2017 and 2007 censuses or by UNSD. Issuing bodies and years named in the summary: PCBS / UNSD, 2017. This section comes from a research summary supplied to the museum; the museum did not open the pages it names, and the founder approved using the summary as supplied on 21 November 2026 {cite:pcbs-palestine-census-2017-metadata} {cite:undata-table-27}.

## The standard and the spoken
The World Factbook's Languages entry for the West Bank lists Arabic, Hebrew and English, describing Hebrew as spoken by Israeli settlers and many Palestinians and English as widely understood; this is the Factbook's text and its description {cite:cia-factbook-west-bank}. Its Gaza Strip page lists the same three, but says of Hebrew only that many Palestinians speak it, without mentioning settlers {cite:cia-factbook-gaza-strip}. The Factbook prints no percentage for any language. No comparison of the standard and the colloquial in Palestine was read in these sources; for that duality in general see [[concept/al-fusha-wal-lahajat|Fusha and the dialects]].

## Other languages in the country
The only languages besides Arabic that the reading reached in Palestine are those the Factbook names: Hebrew and English, as set out above. No page on minority languages or ancient languages of the country was read, so this page ascribes nothing to them. For a comparison with countries where the languages of peoples hold a written place see [[concept/lughat-al-qabail|the languages of the tribes]].

## From the history of the language
The Encyclopaedia Britannica says the word Palestine came into English from Greek through Latin; that the Romans used it administratively in the 2nd century CE in the phrase Syria Palaestina; that the Byzantine provinces were called Palaestina Prima, Secunda and Salutaris or Tertia; and that Palaestina Prima was administered as a jund, a military district, by the Arabic name Filastin in the early Islamic era {cite:britannica-palestine}. That last sentence is what the encyclopedia says about Arabic in the name; it is not a history of the dialect. For the country's place in the [[concept/al-alam-al-arabi|Arab world]] see that page.

## What this page did not reach
Not read in these sources: the dialect group Palestinians speak, the share of Arabic speakers, or the state of the standard language in schools. Nor was a classification by Ethnologue, Glottolog or the University of Manchester dialects site read; the last could not be loaded because its certificate was invalid at the time. The Factbook was read in copies archived by the Internet Archive on 29 December 2025 for the West Bank and 31 December 2025 for Gaza, because the agency's site no longer serves the pages. These questions are left to anyone who finds a source that can be read.
