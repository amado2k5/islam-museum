## Definition
Insat is, lexically and in usage, silence for listening; some define it simply as silence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3505}

## Related terms
- Istima': the intent to hear in order to grasp or benefit from what is heard. Insat is silence with that intent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3505}
- Sama': hearing, which does not require intent toward what is heard, whereas insat does. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3506}

## Where insat applies
- The Friday sermon: the majority hold silent attention obligatory on those who attend; the matter has disputes and detail. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3506}
- The Eid sermons: the Hanafis and Shafi'is treat them as the Friday sermon; the Malikis hold it recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3506}
- Qur'an recitation: attentive silence is sought by the law both within prayer when the imam recites aloud and outside it, on the basis of sura al-A'raf (7:204). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3506}
