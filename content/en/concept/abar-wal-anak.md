## Nature and selection
These are black lead. It holds much watery substance that cold has congealed, with some airy and earthy parts that are not very abundant {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. The sign of its moisture, as Galen argued, is how quickly it melts; the sign of its airiness is how thin it is, since it swells and puffs up when left in the dew of the ground {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}.

## Temperament and actions
Cold and moist in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. It is very cooling for swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}.

## Uses by organ
Swellings and pustules: the book describes a preparation by grinding, but that passage is garbled in the scan and is left out. Its cooling power helps hot swellings, and also malignant ulcers and ulcers of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.360}. Wounds and ulcers: its ground and burning forms, as described, help malignant wounds, cancerous ulcers and joint ulcers, especially when washed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.361}. Joints: the ground and burning forms help ulcers of the joints, and when bound on twisted joints and their glands they help {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.361}. Eyes: the calcined form helps their ulcers, especially when washed, and dry ophthalmia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.361}. Chest: the calcined form helps ulcers of the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.361}. Excretory organs: the ground and burning forms help piles; a plate of it bound on cotton prevents frequent nocturnal emissions and calms sexual desire, and both help ulcers and swellings of the penis and the testicles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.361}.
