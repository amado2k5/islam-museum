## Nature and selection
Al-as is well known; it has bitterness with astringency and sweetness, and a coldness that comes from its astringency, and its banak is stronger; it has an earthy substance and a little fine substance, and it is said that its oil has all the benefit listed for it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.345}. The strongest is the blackish, especially the Khusrawani with round leaves, especially the mountain kind of all; the best of its flower is the white, then the juice of the leaf, and the juice of the fruit is better; when its juice ages it weakens {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.345}.

## Temperament and actions
It has a gentle heat, but cold predominates, and its astringency is greater than its coldness; it seems that its cold is in the first degree and its dryness near the second {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.345}. It holds back diarrhoea, sweat and every bleeding, and dries the humours under the skin; a lotion of its decoction on bones hastens their setting, its burnt ash serves in place of tutia to perfume the body, and its nourishment is slight; and among drinks there is none that binds and helps pains of the lung and cough except its drink {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.345}.

## Uses by organ
Its oil, juice and decoction strengthen the roots of the hair, stop it falling, lengthen it and darken it, especially its seed; its dry leaf prevents the odour of the armpits and groins, its ash serves in place of tutia and clears kalaf and freckles and clears bahaq {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.345}. It soothes hot swellings, erysipelas, nimla, pustules, ulcers, whatever is on the palms, and burns by fire, and so do its drink, its leaf as a poultice, its oil and ointments; its dry powder helps when dusted on a whitlow, and its fruit boiled in wine as a poultice heals ulcers of the palms and feet and burns by fire and prevents blistering {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.345}. A poultice of its fruit suits flaccidity of the joints {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. It stops nosebleed, clears scurf (hazaz), dries ulcers of the head and of the ear and their pus when its water is dropped in, and its drink helps slackness of the gums; its leaf, boiled in wine and applied as a poultice, soothes severe headache, and its drink, drunk before wine, prevents the hangover {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. It soothes ophthalmia and protrusion of the eye, and when boiled with barley meal it heals swellings of the eye, and its ash enters the drugs for pterygium {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. It strengthens the heart and removes palpitation, its fruit prevents cough by its sweetness, and binds the bowel of one whose bowel is loose by its astringency, and its fruit and its rubb help spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. It strengthens the stomach, especially its rubb, and its seed stops the flow of superfluities to the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. The juice of its fruit promotes urine, and it prevents burning of the urine and of the bladder, its water binds the bowel and holds back bilious and black-bilious diarrhoea, and as a poultice it helps piles and swelling of the testicle {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}. It helps the bite of the rutayla spider, and so does its fruit drunk in wine, and likewise the sting of the scorpion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}.

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that al-as is good for holding back the passage of the menses, that the decoction of its fruit helps the flow of humours of the womb, and that its decoction helps prolapse of the anus and the womb {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.346}; reports attributed to the book, without recommendation.
