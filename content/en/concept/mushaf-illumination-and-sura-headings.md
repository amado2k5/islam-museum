## Summary
Maryam Ekhtiar writes that the structure, divisions and phonetic requirements of the Qur'an offered calligraphers, illuminators and bookbinders endless occasions to show their talent; some copies are sparsely ornamented and others lavishly illuminated in gold and bright pigments, and ornamental devices highlight the headings of suras, vocalise the text and cue the reciter when to pause, prostrate or keep count. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 65}

## Structure and divisions
She notes that the Qur'an has 114 suras, arranged with one exception from the longest to the shortest, and can be divided into thirty juz', sixty hizb and seven manzils for the days of the week. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 65} Some mushafs open with decorated carpet pages, and sometimes a decorative heading, a sarlawh, above the first verse stands in for them. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 66}

## Sura headings and medallions
As early as the eighth century, she says, Qur'ans in hijazi script had decorated sura headings, and after the tenth century illuminated medallions were added in the margins; marginal devices marked larger divisions such as the thirty juz' that let the whole text be read in a month, especially in Ramadan, and sometimes each of the thirty sections was bound as a separate volume. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 66} See [[concept/hijazi-script|Hijazi script]].

## Bindings
Bindings of mushafs were often as elaborate as the illumination, usually of fine leather that was tooled or stamped, gilded, painted and sometimes inset with precious stones. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 66}
