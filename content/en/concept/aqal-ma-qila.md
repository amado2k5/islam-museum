## Definition
In usul, taking the least of what was said means that when companions disagreed on a set amount, and gave several sayings, the least is taken unless evidence points higher. The example is their disagreement on the blood-money of a Jew: equal to a Muslim's, half, or a third. Taking a third is taking the least said. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3232}
It is close to taking the lightest, which differs in quantity and in kind. Its opposite is taking the most. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3232}

## Ruling
Usulis disagree on whether it is a proof. al-Shafi'i and al-Baqillani held it valid, and al-Qadi Abd al-Wahhab is said to have reported agreement among some. Ibn Hazm denied it and reported the view that one should take the most to be certain of discharging the obligation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3232}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3233}

## Where it is treated
It is discussed under istidlal, which in usul means proofs that are neither text, consensus nor analogy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3232}
