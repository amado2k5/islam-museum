## Summary
If the nurse's milk is scanty the cause is sought in a bad temperament of her body or breast, then it is mended by diet and by milk-increasing foods such as barley gruels, fennel, dill, nigella and the udders of sheep and goats; if the milk is so much that it harms, it is reduced by less food and a dressing on the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.207-209}.

## Looking for the cause
If her milk is scanty, its cause is considered: whether a hot bad temperament in her whole body or in her breast, which is learned from the signs in earlier chapters and by touching the breast. If the cause is that she takes too little food she is fed gruels made from barley, bran and grains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.207-208}.

## Milk-increasing remedies
Fennel root and seed, dill and nigella go into her gruels and foods. It is said that eating the udders of sheep and goats with their milk is very useful, and a dirham weight of dried termites or earthworms in barley water over successive days has been tried. Among what increases milk are an ounce of cow's butter with a little neat wine drunk, or sesame flour mixed with wine, strained and given, and the breasts dressed with spikenard dregs with oil and ass's milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.208-209}.

## Further recipes
An ounce of the inside of boiled aubergine rubbed up in wine is given; bran and radish are boiled in wine with leek seed; or three ounces of dill seed with seeds of fenugreek and alfalfa are mixed with fennel juice, honey and butter and drunk. Eating much mint also increases milk {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.209}.

## Harmful excess
If the milk is such that it harms and spoils from abundance, through being pent up and thickened, it is reduced by less food and by taking what gives little nourishment, and the chest and body are dressed with cumin and vinegar, or clay and vinegar, or lentils cooked in vinegar, with salt water drunk after {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.209}.
