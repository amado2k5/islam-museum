## The moderate brain and Galen's view
The brain moderate in its temperament is the one strong in the sensory, governing and motor acts, moderate in what is expelled from it; its hair is fair in childhood, red in youth and goes toward black when growth is complete; it is middling in curliness and lankness of hair, in its growth and the time of its youth, each in its time, its greying neither hasty nor later than the natural time, and baldness does not hasten to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23}

Galen holds that heat generates mixing of reason and delirium, to which are added levity, the quick arising of changes of mind and the variety of resolves; that cold generates dullness and stillness of movement, to which are added slowness of understanding, difficulty in thinking and laziness; that dryness brings sleeplessness; and that moisture brings deep sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23} with conditions he states, among them that it not be from the borax-like moistures of old men {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23}

Galen holds that the sign that a temperament prevails without matter is the absence of flow of superfluities along with the sign of ill-temperament, and that it prevails with matter, the flow of superfluities {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23} Ibn Sina says this holds if there is no blockage or weakness of the expelling power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23}

## Signs of the hot, the cold, the dry and the moist
The signs of heat in the temperament of the brain are its turning black quickly after being fair, its curliness and quick baldness, quick fullness of the head and its heaviness from causes that befall such as smells, harm from sharp smells, little sleep with lightness, visible veins in the eyes, a certain sharpness of mind and quick changing of opinions and resolves as with boys, and redness of colour {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23}

The signs of the cold temperament are an increase of expelling of superfluities, lank hair, little blackness and quick greying, quick reaction to afflictions from the slightest cause, hidden veins, the coming of catarrh and many descents to the eyes, and much sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 pp.23-24} and a look like that of a drowsy person, slow in the movement of the lids {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.24}

The signs of the dry temperament are clear channels for superfluities and clearness of the senses, strength in staying awake, strong hair and its quick growth, quick baldness and curliness of the hair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.24}

The signs of the moist temperament are lank hair and slow growth of it, slow baldness and murkiness of the senses, many superfluities and descents, and deep sleep {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.24}

## Birth and the belly (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Among the signs of heat in the temperament of the brain, Ibn Sina says, is quick growth of the hair at birth or in the belly, and its blackness at the start {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.23}
