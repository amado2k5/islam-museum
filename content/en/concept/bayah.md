## Overview
The bay'a in legal usage is the contract of submission and obedience; the Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia treats its entries from the contract of bay'a to its oaths {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The encyclopedia covers the bay'a contract to two imams and the bay'a oath among its entries {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
In the account of The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, the bayah is an oath of allegiance sworn to a leader. It describes it as an unwritten agreement made for the subjects by prominent figures of the tribe, on the understanding that their loyalty lasts only while the leader keeps to particular duties toward those he rules. Such delegates are usually religious scholars and political leaders. The Dictionary says the practice continues in places like Saudi Arabia and Morocco, and adds that, like other ideas of classical tribal Islam, it is changing as traditional tribal communities become modern administrative states. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e316}
