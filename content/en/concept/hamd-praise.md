## Summary
Hamd is lexically the opposite of blame and means thanks, contentment and full praise in speech for a voluntary excellence; it is narrower than praise and wider than thanks. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7292} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7294} The fullest form is 'praise belongs to God, Lord of the worlds', which opens the Quran; the expression 'praise that matches His bounties and multiplies them' is also cited. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7308} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7309} Praise is due to God alone, a person may not praise himself, and praising another for what he does not have is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7294} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7295} Opening important affairs with praise of God is recommended, in speeches, writings and openings of prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7295} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7296} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7297} Praise during bowing, prostrating and rising from bowing is disputed among the schools; the follower says 'our Lord, to You be praise', while the lone prayer joins the two in the Shafii view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7297} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7298} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7299} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7300} Praise after prayer is recommended; in the Friday, festival, rain-prayer and eclipse sermons the schools differ on whether it is required. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7301} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7302} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7303} Praise is recommended at a sudden blessing or the removal of a harm, quietly before one afflicted, after sneezing, at morning and evening, at the loss of a child, at seeing what one loves or dislikes, on entering the market, before the mirror and on mounting a beast. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7304} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7305} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7306} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7307} The entry closes at the next headword, hamdala, which is a cross-reference to hamd, and then ḥaml (حمل). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7309}
