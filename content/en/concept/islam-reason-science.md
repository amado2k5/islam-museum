## Overview
Muhammad Farid Wajdi presents Islam as calling to reflection, thinking, demanding proof and casting off blind imitation — the whole Qur'an rests on these principles {cite:al-islam-din-amm-khalid-azhar p. 73}. He cites a hadith that religion is reason; a marginal note records al-Nasa'i judged it baseless {cite:al-islam-din-amm-khalid-azhar p. 74} — so no hadith is ascribed to the Prophet without verifying its grade. The author holds Islam spread "with neither summons nor compulsion" — his own view, presented alongside others.
