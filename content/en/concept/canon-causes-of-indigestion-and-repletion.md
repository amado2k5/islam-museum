## Summary
In chapter 28 Ibn Sina traces indigestion and repletion to evident causes from outside, such as excess moistening, much bathing after food and hindrances to dissolving like ease, and to inner causes such as weak expelling power, strong retaining power or narrow passages {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.152}.

## From outside
Indigestion comes either from outside, from the evident causes, such as using what moistens so strongly that the body no longer needs the moistening of food and drink, so that when both come together matter grows in the body and nature's handling of it spoils, like too much bathing, especially after eating. Also the hindrances to dissolving, such as ease, giving up exercise and evacuation, luxury in food and drink, and bad regimen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.152}.

## From inside
Or it comes from within, as weakness of the expelling power, or strength of the retaining power, so that humours are held back and not driven out, or narrow passages. The printed text here also names the weakness of another power, which the scan renders unclearly, so it is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.152}.
