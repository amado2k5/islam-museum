## Summary
Ibn Sina begins chapter 2, on the management of suckling, by saying the baby should nurse from its mother's milk where possible, because it is most like what fed it in the womb, though it is preferred that another woman nurse it first so that the mother's temperament settles, and that it lick honey before nursing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.204-205}.

## The mother's milk
The baby should be nursed as far as possible on its mother's milk, for that is the food most like the substance of its earlier food in the womb, her menstrual blood, which is the very thing that turns into milk, and it is most receptive and familiar to it. Experience has shown that taking its mother's nipple is of great benefit in driving off what harms it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.204-205}.

## The first nursing
It is enough to nurse it twice or three times a day, and not to start with much. It is preferred that the first woman to nurse it be someone other than its mother, so that her temperament settles; best of all is that it lick honey and then nurse. Two or three milkings are drawn off in the early day before the nipple is given, above all when the milk has a fault {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.205}.

## Motion and music
Two useful things should go with the child to strengthen its temperament: gentle rocking, and the music and chanting customarily used to lull children to sleep. How well it takes to them shows its readiness for exercise and music, one for its body and the other for its soul {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.205}.
