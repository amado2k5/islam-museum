## Summary
In the lexicon, irs is the making of a marriage and the building of the home; some say it is the name of the wedding feast alone. Zifaf is the handing over of the bride to her husband, a narrower word than irs {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11544}.

## Attendance during the wedding
The Malikis (in the preferred view) and the Shafi'is hold that the bridegroom may not leave congregational prayer and works of piety during the wedding days except at night; the Hanbalis allow him to stay with his bride {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11545}.

## The wedding feast and accepting it
The wedding feast (walima) is prescribed by consensus. For most jurists it is a confirmed sunna, but accepting the invitation is obligatory {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11545} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11546}.

## Congratulations and prayer
Congratulating the couple and praying for them is recommended; the Shafi'is disliked saying 'with harmony and children' {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11546} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11547}. The husband is recommended to take his bride by the forelock and pray {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11547}.

## Drums and the division of nights
Announcing the marriage with the drum is recommended {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11547}. The majority give a new virgin bride seven nights and a widow or divorcee three, with disagreement {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11548}.

## Where it is discussed
The rules of the feast are treated under walima and the division of nights under the entry on dividing nights between wives {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11548}.
