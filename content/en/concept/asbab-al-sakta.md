## Compression and fullness
This obstruction is either by compression or by fullness. Compression is that something painful or harmful reaches the brain so that it moves the motion of contraction away from it, or that the quality reaching it is by nature constricting and condensing, like severe cold. Fullness is either swelling fullness or not; swelling fullness is that a matter arises there and blocks both by the fullness and by the stretching, and this counts among the kinds of apoplexy whether the matter is hot or cold; that which comes without swelling is the more frequent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.131}

## The sites and kinds of the matter
It is either in the brain itself and near it, in the pneuma's passages out of the brain, or in the pneuma's passages toward the brain. What is in the passages out of the brain and in the brain is either a sanguine humour pouring into the brain's ventricles at once, or a phlegmatic humour — which is the prevailing majority. What is in the passages toward the brain happens when the arteries and veins are blocked by extreme fullness and abundance of blood so the pneuma has no outlet and soon chokes; from it occurs what occurs when the two carotids are pressed — loss of sense and motion — for the like of that, when it comes from a bodily cause, does the like deed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.131}

## The usage of the word, and its resolving into palsy
They sometimes say 'sakta' and mean by it the palsy general to both sides together. Apoplexy resolves in most cases into palsy, because when nature is too weak to drive the matter from both sides together, it drives it to the more receptive and weaker of the two sides and pushes it through the gaps of the passages, removing it from the brain and its ventricles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.131}
