## Where this appears
[[passage/quran-4-1|al-Nisa 4:1]] says God created people from a single soul. At this verse Tafsir al-Manar, dictated by Muhammad Abduh and written by Rashid Rida, takes up whether all people descend from the Adam of tradition. It reports that some researchers hold each kind of human to have its own father, and says that "O children of Adam" does not conflict with this, since it is enough for the address to be to those who were Adam's descendants at the time of revelation. It adds that it was said earlier, in the commentary on the story of Adam at the start of al-Baqara, that before him there was on earth a kind of this genus who corrupted it and shed blood. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 p. 264} This text is my only basis here; it is a modern secondary work, so I attribute to Ibn Arabi and others only what Rashid Rida relays from them.

## What al-Manar relays from the Imamis and the Sufis
Rashid Rida takes from al-Alusi's Ruh al-Ma'ani that the Imamis and the Sufis are reported to have spoken of many Adams before the Adam known to the People of the Book. He gives the Imami author of Jami' al-Akhbar as reporting that God created thirty Adams before Adam, a thousand years between each, and that the world lay waste for fifty thousand years and was then settled for fifty thousand; Ibn Babawayh in al-Tawhid from al-Sadiq, that God created a million Adams; and Maytham in his commentary on Nahj al-Balagha, from Muhammad al-Baqir, that a million Adams or more passed before our Adam. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 p. 264} Then: "the Greatest Shaykh" in the Futuhat said something that on its face implies another Adam forty thousand years before Adam, and there are other statements on the matter in the Futuhat and elsewhere; he also notes that Zayn al-Arab declared one who affirms several Adams an unbeliever, which Rashid Rida rejects as reckless takfir. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 pp. 264-265} I take the "Greatest Shaykh" to be Ibn Arabi, as the usage is understood, but I did not read the Futuhat and consulted no scholarly study such as William Chittick's, so I do not say what Ibn Arabi meant.

## Muhammad Abduh's view as Rashid Rida reports it
Rashid Rida says his teacher had two opinions: that the plain sense of the verse resists the single soul being Adam, father of all humans or not; and that the Qur'an has no decisive text that all humans descend from Adam, "human" here meaning the speaking, upright animal called man. On this view what researchers say of several origins of humankind does not contradict the Qur'an. Rashid Rida insists this does not prevent one who believes Adam is father of all from believing so, and that Abduh never said the Qur'an denies it. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 p. 265} Before this he recalls what al-Razi gave on al-A'raf 189: three readings of the single soul, a parable (from al-Qaffal), Quraysh with Qusayy as the soul, or Adam. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 p. 264}

:::perspectives
- **Sufi and Imami reports as relayed in al-Manar:** several Adams before Adam, with differing numbers in the reports, among them what is attributed to Ibn Arabi. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 pp. 264-265}
- **Majority Sunni exegetes on the verse:** the single soul is Adam, father of humankind; for Rashid Rida this is a given among the majority rather than something the verse spells out. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 p. 264}
- **Modern view, Muhammad Abduh:** the Qur'an has no decisive text that all humans descend from Adam; a believer may still hold Adam to be father of all. {cite:tafsir-manar-v04 p. 265}
:::

## What I do not say
I give no verdict on these views and do not ascribe them to the Sunni tradition at large. I found no other verified source for modern Muslim writers' debates on "before Adam" beyond al-Manar, so I add no names I have not checked. The report from Ibn Abbas about earths each with its own Adam belongs to the record on the seven earths, which I did not read here.
