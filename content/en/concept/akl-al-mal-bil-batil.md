## Summary
Sha'rawi holds that movement in wealth is governed by the lasting truth, not by the passing falsehood, and that consuming by falsehood includes theft, seizure, fraud, betrayal of trust and bribery. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.797} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.798}

## Falsehood and truth
Sha'rawi says falsehood is what passes away, and truth what lasts; falsehood may rise and float, as scum does on water, but it does not endure. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.798} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.799}

## Whoever eats by falsehood goes hungry in truth
Sha'rawi holds that God may afflict one who eats by falsehood with an illness that keeps him from the lawful and good, so that the blessing is in his hand but he cannot enjoy it lawfully. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.802}

## A ruler does not make the forbidden lawful
Sha'rawi reports that if governments permit usury or other things God forbade, the believer may not follow them in it, since a ruler does not make lawful what God forbade; the footnote names Bukhari and Muslim for the hadith on judgement by the outward plea. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.803} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.804}

## Limits of this batch
The jurists' rules of transactions, the academic view and other views are not entered. Sha'rawi's examples of arts and work in his time are his own view. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.800}
