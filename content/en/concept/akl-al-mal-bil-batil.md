## Summary
Al-Sha'rawi reads "do not consume your wealth among yourselves in falsehood" as a command to each person not to take another's wealth without right, and he treats trade by mutual consent as the lawful way. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2142}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2143}

## What counts as falsehood
He names usury, theft, embezzlement, bribery, cheating and gambling, and holds that taking by force or by shame without consent is not lawful. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2144}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2145}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2146}

## Eating in the houses of relatives and friends
He recounts that the Muslims were scrupulous about eating at their brothers' houses, until the verse of al-Nur lifted that restriction, and he separates this from eating wealth in falsehood. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2144}

## Protecting society
He holds that protecting each person's wealth protects the movement of the whole society, since a person who is secure in the fruit of his work works more. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2142}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2143}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p. 2145}
