## Overview
Al-Maraghi presents agriculture as the oldest livelihood — Ibn Khaldun's simplest and most natural of crafts {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 27} {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 28}. Islam urges it as the source of food for man and beast, and warns against working usurped land {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 28}. Whoever revives dead land has the best claim to it; whoever leaves his land idle three years may see another cultivate it — conditioned on the ruler's leave {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 29}. On water he warns against withholding its surplus from crops, and retells the dispute over the Harra's watercourse between al-Zubayr and the Ansari.
