## Summary
This page gathers what is said of grammar from three sides: [[person/ibn-jinni|Ibn Jinni]]'s definition of it and the origin of the word, what [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] holds of its importance and the reason it was set down, and [[person/abd-al-qahir-al-jurjani|Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani]]'s view that nazm is working by the meanings of grammar. The origin of the science and its two schools have their own pages, to which the reader is referred, and each statement here is ascribed to its author as it stands in the pages read.

## Ibn Jinni's definition
In al-Khasa'is, in the chapter on grammar, Ibn Jinni defines nahw as "following the manner of Arab speech in its inflection", in i'rab and other things such as dual, plural, diminutive, broken plural, annexation, relative adjective and composition. Its aim is that one who is not of the people of Arabic may reach them in eloquence and speak it though not one of them, and that if any of them deviates from it he is returned to it. He notes that nahw was originally a general verbal noun from nahawtu nahwan, I aimed, and was later given to this science, as the words fiqh and Bayt Allah were {cite:khasais-najjar 1 p.35}.

## The importance of grammar for Ibn Khaldun
Ibn Khaldun holds that grammar is the most important of the sciences of the Arabic tongue, because by it the principles of intended meanings are shown by indication, so that the agent is known from the object and the subject from the predicate, and without it the root of conveying meaning would be unknown. He compares it with lexicon and holds it more important, because most of lexicon stayed as it was while inflection changed entirely and left no trace {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.753}.

## Why it was set down
Ibn Khaldun relates that the Arabs' faculty was the best of faculties, and that vowels and particles pointed to meaning. When they left the Hijaz and mixed with the non-Arabs, the faculty changed through what hearing took in, and the scholars feared it would be corrupted and the Qur'an and hadith become closed to understanding. So they derived from the course of Arab speech rules, among them the nominative for the agent and the accusative for the object, and called the change at the end of the word i'rab and its cause the 'amil {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.754}.

## Who set it down
Ibn Khaldun says the first to write on it was [[person/abu-al-aswad-al-duali|Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali]], and it is said at Ali's suggestion; then people wrote until [[person/al-khalil-ibn-ahmad-al-farahidi|al-Khalil ibn Ahmad]] came in the days of al-Rashid, refined its chapters and completed them, and [[person/sibawayh|Sibawayh]] took from him and completed its branches in his famous book {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.754}. Abu Ali al-Farisi and al-Zajjaj wrote short books for learners, then the dispute arose between the Kufans and the Basrans; among later books he names the Tashil of [[person/ibn-malik-al-jayyani|Ibn Malik]] and the Mufassal of [[person/al-zamakhshari|al-Zamakhshari]] {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-shahada-1981 1 p.755}. For the reports on the first setting down see [[concept/ibtida-al-nahw|the beginning of grammar]], and for the two schools [[concept/madrasat-al-nahw-al-basriyya-kufiyya|the Basran and Kufan school]].

## Grammar and nazm
Abd al-Qahir al-Jurjani makes grammar the basis of nazm, saying in Dala'il al-I'jaz: "Nazm is only that you put your speech in the position that the science of grammar requires." He explains that the composer seeks only to look at the aspects and differences of each chapter of grammar, such as the variants of the predicate in Zayd is departing, Zayd departs, departs Zayd and Zayd is the departing one, and the variants of conditional sentences, working by its rules and ways and leaving none of them out {cite:dalail-al-ijaz-shakir-1992 1 p.81}. Grammar is thus tied in his work to the cause of inimitability set out in [[concept/nazariyyat-al-ijaz-wal-nazm|the books on inimitability]].

## Grammar and morphology
In al-Munsif, Ibn Jinni says that tasrif is intermediate between grammar and lexicon, that tasrif is for knowing the stable selves of words and grammar for their changing states, and that books of grammar usually have tasrif at their end {cite:munsif-ibn-jinni-1954 p.4}. Tasrif has its own page, [[concept/ilm-al-sarf|Arabic morphology]].

## What this page does not reach
Sibawayh's book was not read, nor al-Khalil's works, nor the later books of grammar that Ibn Khaldun mentions; only what he says about them was read. The details of the dispute between the Kufans and the Basrans are not given here because it has a page of its own. Of al-Khasa'is only the page of the definition was read, of Dala'il al-I'jaz only the place cited, and of al-Munsif only its first pages. This page does not decide between what these authors say about the rise of grammar and the reports on its beginning set out on its own page.
