# Wala' and bara' among Muslims

## The episode and the question
An episode of [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|Al-Wasatiyya]], presented by [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]], discussed applying the concept of wala' and bara' (loyalty and disavowal) inside the Muslim fold: among movements, schools and sects, and towards secular Muslims, the religiously lax and advocates of peace with Israel. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:02:30} The Assabile archive carries this episode twice: as episode 9, and as episode 26, which is the same broadcast with the channel's commercial breaks; time anchors from both copies are given here. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:00:30}

The presenter said the concept is present in the Qur'an and abundantly in the Sunna, and that issues, fatwas and positions have been built on it; the programme had discussed wala' and bara' with non-Muslims in an earlier episode, and this one concerned Muslims themselves, because applying it among them had led to division and even to takfir. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:07:30} He asked: does it apply to movements, schools, sects, people of innovation and extremists in Sufism and Shi'ism? May one love and support them, pray behind them, join their celebrations and marry among them? And on the other side, does it apply to secular Muslims, advocates of peace with Israel, and those who neither pray nor fast? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:02:30}

The presenter reminded viewers that the Al-Resalah channel can be watched worldwide on its website, with a live stream, an archive of episodes and their transcripts, and that in this free discussion programme the guests' and young people's opinions are their own and do not represent the channel. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:10:00} He put a question to television viewers, to be answered by text message: is it true that wala' and bara' may not be applied among Muslims but only towards non-Muslims? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:04:00}

## First poll: whom does Muslim unity include?
The presenter asked the studio audience whether Muslim unity includes all Muslims of every kind, Sunnis only, or Sunnis together with the moderates of others. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:05:00} Sixty-six percent chose all Muslims, 15% Sunnis only, and 19% Sunnis and the moderates of others. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:08:30} A student, Samah Ibrahim, said she chose all Muslims, citing the Qur'anic command to hold fast together to the rope of God; another student thought some sects have nothing of Islam but the name. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:10:30}

## What the concept means: love and support, and disavowal is not war
Abd al-Rahman al-Barr, a professor of hadith at al-Azhar University, summed up the earlier episode's conclusions: wala' is love and support and bara' its opposite; it applies to those who fight Muslims; and it can be divided, so that a Muslim is loyal in one respect and disavows in another, depending on the issue and the situation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:11:30} He said the community's condition shapes some conduct: when weak it needs to hold to what marks its identity; when strong, some leniency is acceptable to win hearts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:12:00} He stressed that disavowal does not mean war or enmity: a Muslim may disavow a non-Muslim's unbelief without fighting him, and war is only for the combatant. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:12:00}

Al-Barr supported unity including everyone whose Islam is established: a deviant's deviation is disavowed while his Islam remains, for which he is still owed loyalty, advice and a call to leave his error. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:13:00} He cited the first hadith in Sahih Muslim, which reports Abd Allah ibn Umar's disavowal of the Qadariyya, and said they were an innovating sect that deviated in understanding the pillar of belief in divine decree but did not thereby leave Islam. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:13:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:14:00} In his view only someone who denies something with no interpretive excuse, in open unbelief after being informed, is declared an unbeliever. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:15:00}

## The root of Islam and what lies above it; the legal schools and Sufism
Sami al-Majid, a professor of the principles of jurisprudence at the faculty of sharia of Imam Muhammad ibn Saud University in Riyadh, said that among Islamic sects love and dislike combine. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:15:30} The presenter asked: if Islam is a hundred percent, do we love someone with 60% Islam sixty percent? He answered no: whoever has the root of Islam is a Muslim, and then we disavow whatever violation lies above that foundation. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:16:30} He put the legal schools, Hanbali, Hanafi and Shafi'i, outside the question altogether, since their differences are matters of fiqh and ijtihad, not creed; whoever treats another school as misguided has gone to excess. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:17:00} The Sufis, he said, are of degrees: one who shows what breaks the foundations of Islam is disavowed; one who keeps the root of Islam and falls into a violation has his act disliked while love for him remains, and Muslim unity includes them without doubt. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:17:30}

## The creedal sects: where is the line?
The presenter asked a professor of creed from Jordan about Sunnis, Shia, Ibadis, Zaydis and Isma'ilis: is there wala' and bara' between them? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:18:00} He said there is, between the Sunnis, in his words the great majority of the community, and those who deviated from their way; the line is acknowledging the great foundations of Islam; the Sunnis are the original Islam, and wasatiyya in his account means taking the truth in its whole and its parts. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:19:00} The presenter replied that every sect claims to be the middle and in the right. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:19:00} The guest said the Sunnis agreed that the Isma'ilis and the Druze are outside Islam, and differed over the Shia, Ibadis and Zaydis, without declaring individuals unbelievers; anyone who says something constituting unbelief, such as that the Qur'an is incomplete, is disavowed even if he is a Sunni. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:21:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:21:30} He held that Muslim unity does not include the disputed sects because they are a minority; when the presenter put them at about 20% of the community, the guest said about 7%, according to statistics he mentioned. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:22:00}

Abd Allah Samak, head of the department of schools and religions at al-Azhar University, who gives a near-daily radio talk answering doubts, said the reference in disputes is to return the matter to God and His Messenger. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:23:30} The presenter objected that everyone claims as much and asked for a specific criterion. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:24:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:25:30} Samak said the difference with the Shia lies in the reference and its understanding: Sunnis hold all the Companions upright and take their hadith, while the Shia do not; and he disavows whoever disavows the Companions — that is, the act. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:26:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:26:30} He concluded that disavowal is not of the sect nor of the person but of the wrong act, as the Prophet disavowed what Khalid ibn al-Walid did among the Banu Jadhima when he misread their words. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:27:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:27:30} He added that Shia sects number more than a hundred, and that the Isma'ilis and the Batiniyya in general left the faith. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:27:30}

## Summing up the first part
The presenter framed what the guests agreed: sects that Sunnis agree to be unbelievers, such as the Isma'ilis, the Druze and the Batiniyya, are disavowed; sects still among the people of the qibla are disavowed in their wrong beliefs and acts, not as a whole. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:28:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:28:30} He added that creed has foundations and particular questions: whoever denies the angels, says God became incarnate in a human, or holds there is a Qur'an other than this Qur'an has clearly left the faith; disagreement in branches is something scholars differ over, and a whole sect is not disavowed over branches. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:29:30}

## Second poll: does it turn into takfir?
The presenter asked: if wala' and bara' is applied inside the Muslim fold, Sunnis and others, does it turn into takfir easily, does it do so but not easily, or has it nothing to do with takfir? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:31:30} Twenty-three percent said easily, 35% not easily, and 42% that it does not; the presenter commented that it may remain dislike of some acts and conduct without becoming takfir. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:34:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:35:00}

Abd Allah Samak said a culture of hatred has no place in Islam, that takfir is a declaration of enmity, war and hatred and among the gravest rulings, and that it concerns a foundation of the faith, not a particular question. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:35:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:36:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:37:00} He thought judgements of unbelief should be left to the courts, not to scholars or the public, and referred to the phrase "preachers, not judges", attributing it to al-Hudaybi (needs_source: the line is not fully clear in the recording). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:37:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:37:30} The professor of creed from Jordan said Sunnis do not incline to takfir and that historical aggression came from small sects, citing the seizure of the Black Stone, kept out of its place for 37 years, and the killing of pilgrims who were thrown into Zamzam (needs_source: the duration is as the guest stated it, and the sect's name is unclear in the recording). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:38:30} The presenter answered that takfir also exists among some Sunni scholars and young people, and that what began as excess has become a basic tenet of some schools. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:38:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:39:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:40:00} They agreed on a rule put by the Jordanian guest: declaring a specific person an unbeliever belongs to the judge, because legal rulings follow from it, and declaring a sect so belongs to the consensus of senior scholars; young people should keep away from takfir entirely. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:40:00}

## Specific questions: the example of the Shia in Kuwait
The presenter said there is a proportion of Shia living among the people in Kuwait, as in many countries, and asked quick questions about sects still among the people of the qibla, setting aside those that left the faith, such as the Baha'is and the Isma'ilis: {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:41:00}

- **Love:** the guests answered with a distinction: love them in what is shared, dislike what they do wrong. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:41:30}
- **Support:** yes, without doubt; al-Barr said he backs anyone who fights Israel, even a non-Muslim, let alone a Muslim. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:42:30}
- **Praying behind them:** they differed; the Jordanian guest said the matter needs detail and that behind some Shia sects one does not pray; the presenter asked about someone known to curse Abu Bakr, Umar and Aisha in his prayer; another guest held that prayer behind anyone whose Islam is established is valid in principle, and that questions such as wiping the feet are detailed fiqh matters on which some Sunnis held similar views. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:45:30}
- **Their celebrations:** joining religious celebrations such as Ashura is not permitted in their view because these are innovations, while social occasions pose no problem; they agreed on this. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:46:00}
- **Marriage:** they split: one guest allowed it as long as Islam is established, another withheld judgement and referred it to the fiqh academy, and a third forbade it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:46:30} One guest said he knows Shia who invoke God's pleasure on Abu Bakr and Umar, and heard one recite a poem praising Aisha; the matter depends on the person's religion and character, not the sect (needs_source: the person's name is unclear in the recording). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:48:00}

## The Islamic movements
The presenter asked about Islamic movements active today, such as the Muslim Brotherhood and the Salafis, when some practise wala' and bara' against another group; the guests said this is not permitted since all of them are within the Sunni frame, and called it folly, an intellectual defect and an excess that divides Muslims and must be treated. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:49:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:49:30}

## Third poll: advocates of peace with Israel
The presenter asked: must one disavow everyone who calls for peace with Israel? The options: agree, whether the caller is Muslim or not; disavow him if he is not a Muslim and hesitate if he is; or disagree, because the matter has nothing to do with wala' and bara'. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:50:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:50:30} Sixty-three percent agreed, 13% chose disavowing only the non-Muslim, and 14% rejected the statement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:53:30} A student said she disavows non-Muslims who collude with Israel and boycotts their goods, while with Muslims among them, such as some Arab rulers, she disavows their acts; other students said they agreed because of the Palestinian blood Israel was shedding. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:54:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:54:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:55:30} Al-Barr said he dislikes normalisation with Israel and calls on those who pursue it to stop, and that disavowal is of their acts, not their persons. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:56:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:56:30}

## Secular Muslims
The presenter asked about secular Muslims who call for separating religion and state. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:56:30} Sami al-Majid said that someone who believes Islam cannot govern and is backward and obscurantist is disavowed, while someone ignorant of the matter has it explained to him. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:57:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:57:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:58:00} The presenter said that a month earlier he had been with Recep Tayyip Erdogan, whom he called president of Turkey, and that Erdogan's view is a civil state in which parties compete and the winner applies its programme; he asked whether this form of secularism is to be disavowed. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:58:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:58:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:59:00} Al-Majid said the view is not correct but he does not disavow Erdogan, only the idea. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:58:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 00:59:30} Abd Allah Samak said "secularism" is a poor translation, closer to "worldliness", and distinguished someone with an interpretation who thinks the public good lies in keeping religion out of education, politics and the economy, who needs to reconsider, from someone who holds that religion is unfit altogether; he cited the story of Umar ibn al-Khattab and the woman who corrected him over dowries. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:00:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:01:00} The Jordanian guest said secularism is Europe's problem, not the Muslims'. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:01:30} The presenter concluded that secularism takes several forms, that disavowal is of the idea, not the person, and that the door of takfir is closed here. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:02:30}

## The one who abandons prayer
Asked about someone who abandons prayer entirely, even Friday prayer, a guest said he is an unbeliever; the presenter answered that this is the Hanbali view, not that of all schools, and that a general ruling is applied to a specific person only through a court. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:03:00} The guests distinguished one who abandons it in denial, who is an unbeliever, from one who abandons it out of laziness, a sinning Muslim whose act, not person, is disavowed; one noted that the Hanbalis themselves drew distinctions on it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:04:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:04:30}

## Last poll: the fate of the term
The presenter said the term "al-wala' wa-l-bara'" was not used by the Prophet and the Companions at the start of Islam but came later, as did the term "'aqida", and asked the audience: should we coin terms suited to our time as scholars coined terms in theirs, keep it while defining and regulating it, or abolish it because its problems outweigh its benefits? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:06:30} Twenty-seven percent chose replacing it, 63% keeping and regulating it, and 10% abolishing it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 01:03:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:09:30} The Jordanian guest welcomed the result, mentioned a study of his entitled "Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a between internal fragmentation and external aggression", and saw the concept as a mean between laxity that dissolves the community and excess. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:10:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:10:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:11:00} Another guest was surprised by the proposal to replace the term because its words are precise and Qur'anic, and a third said its origin is in the Book and the Sunna and scholars have defined it, so there is no need for a new term to quarrel over. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:11:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:12:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:13:00}

## The presenter's summing-up (his view)
Tareq al-Suwaidan summarised what the guests had agreed over two episodes: wala' is love and support; love is for the idea and the act, and so is dislike; if a Muslim of his group or household did a wrongful act he would not love that act, and if an unbeliever did a good act he would love it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:17:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:17:30} Disavowal of non-Muslims, for him, is creedal disavowal — he gave the example of disavowing the doctrine of the Trinity while marriage to a Christian woman remains permitted — and the same concept applies inside the Muslim fold. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:17:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep26 01:18:00} Support, for him, goes to justice and truth even from an unbeliever, and injustice is rejected even from a brother;
