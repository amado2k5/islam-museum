# Wala' and bara' among Muslims

## The episode and the question
The 'Al-Wasatiyya' episode debated applying wala' and bara' inside the Muslim rank: among movements, madhhabs and sects, and toward secular Muslims, the religiously lax, and advocates of peace with Israel {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:02:30}. The viewers' question: may it be applied among Muslims, or is it only for non-Muslims? {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:04:00}

## The equation of degrees — disavowal of the act, not the person
The panel distilled the rule through a percentage: if a person carries 60% Islam we love him to that measure and disavow his violation — bara' is from the act, not the person; Islam is degrees, not a single bar {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:14:00}. On unity: ahl al-sunna are the umma's majority (around 80%) while the rest, a minority, remain inside the Muslim rank {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:20:00}.

## The danger of sliding into takfir
The episode warned the concept can slide into takfir — 42% voted it turns easily {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:31:00} — but the boundary is keeping it a dislike of acts and conduct, not a verdict on persons {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:31:30}. And 63% of the audience endorsed disavowal of advocates of peace with Israel {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 00:48:30}.

## The conclusion — regulation, not abolition
The audience concluded the concept needs regulation and research answering its questions: 63% versus only 10% for abolishing it entirely {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 01:03:30} — and one scholar praised the youth for not restricting it to ahl al-sunna {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep09 01:04:00}.
