## Summary
In the third chapter Ibn Sina counts ten genera of signs by which the state of temperament is learned: touch, flesh and fat, hair, body colour, state of the organs, speed of reaction, sleep and waking, functions, expulsion of waste, and the powers of the soul {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.158-163}.

## The list
The first is touch; the second is drawn from flesh and fat; the third from hair; the fourth from body colour; the fifth from the state of the organs; the sixth from the speed with which organs react; the seventh is the state of sleep and waking; the eighth is drawn from the signs of functions; the ninth is the body's expulsion of waste and the quality of what it expels; the tenth is drawn from the soul's powers in their acts and passions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.158-162}.

## What follows
He then separates the signs of temperaments belonging to the original constitution from foreign, incidental temperaments, and lists for the latter the signs of the hot, cold, moist and dry {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.163}.
