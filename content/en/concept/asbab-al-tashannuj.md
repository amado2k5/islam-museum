## Transfer of the matter
I see it as something that occurs often and passes in its time; material convulsion often occurs by way of transfer of the matter, as occurs after the suffocating anginas, after pleurisy and after phrenitis {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}

## The non-material, from heat and dryness
The convulsion not from matter is from heat and prevailing dryness, whereby it shrinks in length and breadth and shrivels, gathering into itself like leather brought near the fire; you know the state of the tendons, that they shorten in winter for moistening and shorten in summer for drying, and so it is with the nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}

## From injury, poisonous quality and cold
Of the convulsion not ascribed to matter is what befalls for a hurtful thing the nerve shrinks from and gathers to repel; that cause is either a pain from a painful cause — often a hot stinging humour — or a poisonous quality reaching the brain and the nerve, as befalls one a scorpion has stung on his nerve, or a non-poisonous quality like the convulsion from severe cold binding nerve and muscle so it contracts toward its head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}

## Its variance by the origins, and from fear
As relaxation differs in the organs by the origins of their organs, so does convulsion, and the measure in both is one for what is below the neck, fore and aft in a direction, and what is above the neck; the repletion-moist convulsion's own cause is the moisture, cold aiding it by congealing and thickening it so it does not extend, while dryness and heat aid its excess by dissolving the moisture; some convulsion befalls for fear, whose cause is that the outspreading spirit plunges inward at once, drawing the muscles moving toward their origins, then they freeze in that shape {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.143}
