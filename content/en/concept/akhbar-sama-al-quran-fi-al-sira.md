## Summary
The page gathers five accounts in Ibn Hisham's Sira about the effect of hearing the Qur'an on masters of speech: those of al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Utba ibn Rabia, the three who listened by night, the conversion of Umar, and the conversion of al-Tufayl ibn Amr; then what is reported of Labid and his giving up poetry. For each account it says how Ibn Ishaq introduces it: with no chain in that place, or with the words "I was told" or "it reached me". These accounts differ in form from what the commentators transmit in [[concept/sama-quraysh-lil-quran|the hearing of the Qur'an by Quraysh]].

## Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira
Under the heading "Al-Walid ibn al-Mughira's bewilderment over what to call the Qur'an", Ibn Hisham reports, within Ibn Ishaq's narrative and with no named narrator at that point, that [[person/al-walid-ibn-al-mughira|al-Walid]] gathered a group of Quraysh at the pilgrimage season and said that Arab delegations would come having heard of their companion, so they should agree on one description. They proposed soothsayer; he refused, having seen soothsayers, and this was not their murmuring or rhymed prose. They proposed madman; he refused, since there was no choking, tremor or whispering. They proposed poet; he said he knew all poetry, in rajaz, hazaj, qarid, maqbud and mabsut, and it was not poetry. They proposed sorcerer; he refused, since it was not their blowing or knots. When they asked what he would say, he said: "By God, his speech has a sweetness; its root is a laden palm and its branch is fruit-bearing." Then he said that whatever they said would be known to be false, and the nearest was sorcerer, since it separates a man from his father, brother, wife and clan. So they sat on the roads warning people. Ibn Hisham adds that the verses from [[passage/quran-74-11|al-Muddaththir 11]] to [[passage/quran-74-25|al-Muddaththir 25]] were revealed concerning al-Walid {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.270} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.271}.

## Utba ibn Rabia
Ibn Ishaq gives it from Yazid ibn Ziyad, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, and in al-Qurazi's words he was told that [[person/utba-ibn-rabia|Utba ibn Rabia]], after Hamza's conversion, offered to go to the Prophet. He offered him wealth, honour, kingship, or treatment if what he had was a visitation. The Prophet recited to him the opening of sura [[passage/quran-41-1|Fussilat 1]] and prostrated at the verse of prostration. Utba returned to Quraysh and said: "I heard speech such as, by God, I have never heard; by God it is neither poetry nor sorcery nor soothsaying," and advised leaving him alone. They said he had bewitched him with his tongue {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.293} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.294}.

## The three who listened by night
Ibn Ishaq reports from al-Zuhri that he was told that Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]] ibn Hisham and al-Akhnas ibn Shariq each went out one night alone to listen to the Prophet praying at night in his house. This happened three nights; each morning the road brought them together and they reproached one another, and on the third they swore not to return. Al-Akhnas visited Abu Sufyan, who said he had heard things whose meaning he knew and things whose meaning he did not know. Abu Jahl said they had competed with Banu Abd Manaf for honour until they said, from us is a prophet to whom revelation comes; when shall we match that? By God, we shall never believe in him {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.315} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.316}.

## The conversion of Umar ibn al-Khattab
Under the heading "Another account of Umar's conversion", Ibn Ishaq says "as it reached me"; this differs from an earlier account by Umm Abd Allah on page 343. [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] set out with his sword at his shoulder to kill the Prophet, and Nu'aym ibn Abd Allah told him that his sister Fatima and her husband Sa'id ibn Zayd had become Muslims. He went to them and found Khabbab ibn al-Aratt reading a sheet containing [[passage/quran-20-1|Ta-Ha 1]], and Khabbab hid. Umar struck Sa'id and wounded Fatima's face; seeing her blood he repented and asked for the sheet, being able to write. She said he was impure through his polytheism and only the pure touch it, so he washed and read its opening and said: "How fine and noble this speech is." Khabbab told him of the Prophet's prayer that God strengthen Islam by Abu al-Hakam or by Umar, and he went to the house at al-Safa and accepted Islam {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.343} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.344} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.345}.

## Al-Tufayl ibn Amr al-Dawsi
Here the narrator is al-Tufayl himself, relating his arrival in Mecca; he was a noble, a poet and shrewd. Quraysh warned him that the man's speech is like sorcery, so he stuffed cotton in his ears, but God willed that he hear some of it at the Ka'ba: "I heard fine speech." Then he told himself that he was a shrewd poet from whom the good is not hidden from the ugly, so he would listen and accept it if it was good. He went to the Prophet's house and asked him to present his message; the Prophet presented Islam and recited the Qur'an, and al-Tufayl said he had never heard speech finer or a command more just; he accepted Islam, asked for a sign, a light appeared on his whip, and he returned to Daws {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.382} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa-1955 1 p.383}.

## Labid ibn Rabia and the giving up of poetry
Ibn Sallam describes [[person/labid-ibn-rabia|Labid]] as a horseman, poet, brave, sweet of speech, a Muslim and truthful. He reports that Umar wrote to his governor to ask Labid and al-Aghlab al-Ijli what they had composed in Islam; al-Aghlab answered with rajaz, and Labid said: "God has given me in place of poetry the sura of al-Baqara and Al Imran", and Umar raised his stipend {cite:tabaqat-fuhul-shakir 1 p.135} {cite:tabaqat-fuhul-shakir 1 p.136}. Ibn Hajar gives the report from al-Marzubani to the same effect {cite:isaba-ilmiyya-1415 5 p.500}.

Ibn Abd al-Barr gives it in another form: Umar asked him one day to recite something of his poetry and he said he would not compose poetry after God had taught him al-Baqara and Al Imran, so Umar added five hundred to his stipend of two thousand. Later Mu'awiya meant to cut the addition, and Labid said he would die soon and they would keep the addition and the base; he died shortly after. Ibn Abd al-Barr adds that it is also said he died in Kufa in the days of al-Walid ibn Uqba in the caliphate of Uthman, which he holds more correct {cite:istiab-bajawi-1960 3 p.1337}. None of the three sources gives a chain for the report on the pages read, and none flags weakness; this silence is not a grading as sound.

## What this page does not reach
Ibn Ishaq's original text was not read here, nor his accounts as given by anyone but Ibn Hisham, and al-Tufayl's story after his conversion was not read in full. The passage of Ibn al-Mulaqqin in al-Tawdih that gives another saying of Labid was not opened. The accounts are not given with a complete chain; they are presented as the books give them, with the formula of reception that Ibn Ishaq states.
