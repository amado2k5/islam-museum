## Summary
Ibn Sina shows that each season suits those with a temperament proper to it, that the meeting of seasons that have strayed in opposite ways can set bodies right, that a year that holds one quality throughout is bad, that the healthiest times are a rainy autumn and a moderate winter, and then describes air good in its substance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.117-118}.

## The rulings of the seasons
Each season suits one with a sound temperament proper to it and goes against one with a bad temperament not proper to it, unless a very great departure from moderation occurs, when it goes against both by weakening strength. Each season also suits the accidental temperament opposed to it. If two seasons depart from their nature in opposite ways without lasting excess, as when winter was southerly and a northerly spring follows, the second following the first suits and moderates bodies, for spring makes good winter's injury; likewise if winter is very dry and spring very moist, so long as the moisture is not excessive and the time not long {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.117}.

## The bad year
A change of weather in one season brings less pestilence than a change over many seasons. The temperament of air most apt to turn to putrefaction is the hot and moist, and changes of air occur most in places of varied position and in hollows, less in level and high ground. Seasons must come as they are due, and when this is broken it is often a cause of bad diseases. A year that holds one quality through all its seasons, wet, dry, hot or cold, is a bad year with many diseases suited to its quality, lasting long; a cold season finding a phlegmatic body stirs epilepsy, palsy, apoplexy, facial paralysis and spasm, and a hot season finding a bilious body stirs madness, acute fevers and hot tumours. If winter comes early its diseases come early, and if summer comes early so do summer's; and when a season lasts long its diseases multiply, especially in summer and autumn {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.117}.

## The healthiest times
The turning of the seasons has an effect not because of time as time but because of the quality that changes with it, and likewise if the air in a single day changes from hot to cold. The healthiest time is when autumn is rainy and winter moderate, not lacking cold but not excessive for the country; and if spring comes rainy and summer is not without rain, that is the healthiest there can be {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.117-118}.

## Good air
Air good in substance is air with no foreign vapour or smoke mixed in it, open to the sky and not shut in by walls and ceilings, except when a general corruption strikes the air and the open is then more susceptible than the closed; otherwise the open is better. This excellent air is clean and clear, with no vapour of marshes, thickets, ditches and vegetable plots, especially cabbage and rocket, nor of trees of bad substance such as walnut, shuhat and fig, nor of putrid winds; and yet the fine winds are not kept from it, since its winds blow from high, level ground, so it is not air held in a hollow that warms quickly at sunrise and cools quickly at sunset, nor shut in walls newly built with cisterns that have not dried, nor stubborn to the breath as if gripping the throat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.118}.

## Changes of air
Changes of air are some natural, some contrary to nature, and some neither natural nor outside it; they may come in cycles or may not keep their cycles, and the healthiest state of the seasons is for them to follow their natures, since their changing brings diseases {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.118}.
