## Overview
The hijab in juristic usage is that which veils the woman; the Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia gives it a dedicated entry distinguishing its material senses — veiling the 'awra, screening during prayer — from its figurative ones {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The jurists detail the rulings of covering {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah}; the encyclopedia's hijab entry names khimar and niqab among related terms {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## Senses of the hijab in jurisprudence
The word has two senses: a material one, a body that stands between two things, and a figurative one, anything that blocks the reaching of an aim. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6819} For the majority, the 'awra of a woman that must be veiled from a stranger is her whole body except the face and hands. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6819} Veiling the 'awra in privacy is recommended out of shame before God, and uncovering it is allowed for need, such as treatment, circumcision or testimony. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6820} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6821} Figuratively, the word appears in the saying that there is no hijab between the prayer of the wronged and God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6823} A judge may not withdraw from people without excuse; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis dislike a judge having a chamberlain, while the Malikis and Hanafis allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6822} On testimony heard from behind a veil, the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis accept hearing alone when the speaker is known, while the Shafi'is require sight as well. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6822} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6823} Veiling as a term of inheritance is a separate headword, hajb. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6832}

## The speaker's view of hijab and its forms
The speaker answers those who call for unveiling in the name of women's freedom, and holds that this exposes women to men's gaze; the sentences are partly unclear (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep01 00:22:00}.
He notes the many varieties of hijab among women and calls some of them fashion; this describes what he sees rather than a general ruling (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep01 00:28:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 9)

The Sheikh says that all bliss lies in looking upon the face of the Generous and all torment lies in being veiled from the Lord of lords, and he holds that whoever is veiled from his Lord has veiled himself {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:40:00}. He relays what some women say, among them older female relatives who have not covered, that the hijab is the hijab of the heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:40:00}. The Sheikh sharply criticizes the statement of a woman who says she will wear the hijab when God permits; he holds that such postponement is itself a veil over the heart from God's light, and he says that God gave the permission and the command some fifteen hundred years ago and that the woman who says this is circling around the matter (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:40:30}.

According to the Sheikh, Aisha related that she admired the women of the Ansar when the verses of the hijab were revealed: each woman tore her wrap in two and gave half to her sister, and they covered themselves with it and came out of the mosque {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:41:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh says, as his own opinion, that he defended the hijab in a lecture in Paris, comparing the turban of a leader with a headscarf to show what he sees as an inconsistency in the French position; he also says that Muslim girls in Paris came first in the national secondary exams (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:52:00}.

He compares dress to uniform: as police and soldiers are known by their uniforms, he says, the Muslim is known only by the hijab {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:26:00}. He advises a woman to keep her lawful dress and not to surrender her religion for a job, since provision comes from God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 01:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 01:14:30}.

The Sheikh holds, as his own opinion, that a husband who wants his wife to wear the hijab should first set a good example in his own conduct {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 01:07:30}. If a wife refuses the hijab, he advises the husband to be patient with her and to pray for her guidance, rather than to divorce her {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep08 01:08:30}. He advises the mother of a daughter who gave up the hijab to pray for her and to look for the gap through which Satan entered the household {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 01:07:00}.

He describes a schoolgirl whose veil was lifted gradually, step by step, and says the story may be symbolic (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 01:17:00}.
