## Overview
The hijab in juristic usage is that which veils the woman; the Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia gives it a dedicated entry distinguishing its material senses — veiling the 'awra, screening during prayer — from its figurative ones {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In the sources
The jurists detail the rulings of covering {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah}; the encyclopedia's hijab entry names khimar and niqab among related terms {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## Senses of the hijab in jurisprudence
The word has two senses: a material one, a body that stands between two things, and a figurative one, anything that blocks the reaching of an aim. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6819} For the majority, the 'awra of a woman that must be veiled from a stranger is her whole body except the face and hands. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6819} Veiling the 'awra in privacy is recommended out of shame before God, and uncovering it is allowed for need, such as treatment, circumcision or testimony. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6820} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6821} Figuratively, the word appears in the saying that there is no hijab between the prayer of the wronged and God. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6823} A judge may not withdraw from people without excuse; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis dislike a judge having a chamberlain, while the Malikis and Hanafis allow it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6822} On testimony heard from behind a veil, the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis accept hearing alone when the speaker is known, while the Shafi'is require sight as well. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6822} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6823} Veiling as a term of inheritance is a separate headword, hajb. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6832}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 9)

The Sheikh says that all bliss lies in looking upon the face of the Generous and all torment lies in being veiled from the Lord of lords, and he holds that whoever is veiled from his Lord has veiled himself {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:40:00}. He relays what some women say, among them older female relatives who have not covered, that the hijab is the hijab of the heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:40:00}. ## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh says, as his own opinion, that he defended the hijab in a lecture in Paris, comparing the turban of a leader with a headscarf to show what he sees as an inconsistency in the French position; he also says that Muslim girls in Paris came first in the national secondary exams (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep01 00:52:00}.

He advises a woman to keep her lawful dress and not to surrender her religion for a job, since provision comes from God {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 01:14:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep07 01:14:30}.

He advises the mother of a daughter who gave up the hijab to pray for her and to look for the gap through which Satan entered the household {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 01:07:00}.

He describes a schoolgirl whose veil was lifted gradually, step by step, and says the story may be symbolic (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep11 01:17:00}.

## From the lecture series Al Naba Al Safi: covering in the sense of faith
The Sheikh explains that hasra, in the language, means scraping off a covering so that the surface is left bare, and says that when the faith which protects a person is uncovered he is exposed to trials, like a child who takes off what protects him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep24 00:08:30}

## In Tareq al-Suwaidan's programme "Al-Wasatiyya": the hijab, worship or custom (episode 29)
The [[work/al-wasatiyya-program|"Al-Wasatiyya" programme]] gave an episode to the question whether the hijab is worship or custom. Its presenter, [[person/tariq-al-suwaidan|Tareq al-Suwaidan]], said people are divided into three views: those who see it as a major matter of religion, those who see it as a secondary one, and those who hold it is not part of religion at all but a social custom. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:01:00} He noted that the dispute had reached thinkers and scholars and that the question now has laws in France and elsewhere. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:01:00} He hosted the writer and journalist Iqbal Baraka, author of a book on the hijab she called "a modern view", as the advocate of the view that it is a social custom. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:27:00}

### The polls
- He asked the audience whether the Muslim woman's hijab is a religious obligation or a social custom: 96% chose obligation and 4% custom. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:04:00}
- He asked whether keeping the hijab helps society towards chastity or arouses desire: 99% saw it as linked to chastity. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:41:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:43:00}
- He asked whether the hijab hinders women's progress and their part in building civilisation: 86% said it does not. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:57:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:59:00}

These are the votes of that episode's studio audience, not representative surveys.

### Terms and ruling according to the sharia guests
Dr Abd al-Majid Abu Diyya, a specialist in language and sharia, defined four terms: hijab originally means screening, appears in the Qur'an in connection with the Mothers of the Believers, and today people use it for the legal dress; the jilbab is the wrap covering the head and body apart from the face and hands; the khimar is the head covering, and he said women in the Jahiliyya covered their heads but left chest and throat bare, so the command came to cover them; and the niqab covers all or part of the face and was an Arab custom. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:07:30} The participants agreed that in the episode "hijab" means the legal dress covering the whole body except the face and hands. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:08:30} Abu Diyya said the hijab is obligatory, rewarded when kept and sinful when left, and that in his view the niqab is recommended, not obligatory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:09:00} Dr Ruhayyil Gharaiba, a specialist in women's rights in the sharia, agreed that the hijab is obligatory but held the niqab to be merely permitted, not recommended. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:09:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:10:00}

### The hadiths and consensus
The presenter asked her why her book did not mention hadiths in the Sahih of [[person/al-bukhari|al-Bukhari]] from [[person/aisha|'A'isha]] on the women of the Muhajirun and Ansar covering their heads when the verse came down, while it did discuss a disputed hadith; she answered that what the Ansari women did does not bind Muslim women until the Day of Judgement. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:23:30} The presenter explained that the disputed hadith is narrated by Khalid ibn Duraik from 'A'isha, about the Prophet's words to [[person/asma-bint-abi-bakr|Asma' bint Abi Bakr]], and that Khalid never met 'A'isha, so the report is mursal; some scholars judged it weak and some accepted it. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:31:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:32:00} Dr Nama, a lecturer in hadith at a sharia college, said the book was not scholarly in method because it issued a fatwa and fatwa belongs to its qualified people; that no Companion is known to have called the hijab a custom; and that the four schools hold it to be obligatory. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:28:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:29:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:30:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:31:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:32:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:34:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:34:30} The presenter said he had not found any scholar or exegete who called the hijab a custom. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 00:30:30} Iqbal Baraka said she could not believe the Ansari women went bareheaded in a desert setting, and the debate grew heated over rejecting al-Bukhari's hadiths; ### The hijab in the West
Dr Abir Faraon, head of a Muslim women's association in Britain, said the hijab in the West is neither a marginal issue nor "a piece of cloth", that the campaign to ban it in France began before 2003 by presenting it as a symbol of terrorism, that a coalition in support of the hijab lobbied European parliamentarians, and that veiled girls marched with the French flag saying they were French, Muslim and veiled. {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 01:15:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 01:16:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 01:16:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 01:17:00} She said the ban had spread to Belgium, where by her account 80% of schools forbid it, even to a mother coming to ask about her child (needs_source). {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep29 01:17:30}

### The presenter's conclusion
