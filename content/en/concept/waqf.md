## Overview
Tying up a principal asset and devoting its revenue to charitable purposes. It is the economic institution through which mosques, schools and hospitals were built in Islamic history. {cite:wikidata}

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the fourteenth episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] speaks of endowments the Muslims set up to care for animals, saying that Damascus had endowments, paid for by the rich, for cats their owners had neglected, so that there was no such thing there as a street cat, and that Damascus was a city resting entirely on endowments. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:19:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:21:00} He says Ibn Battuta passed by these endowments on his journey and described the fat cats he saw there (needs_source: the attribution to Ibn Battuta's travels was not checked here). {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:21:00} He mentions another endowment in Damascus for aged horses no longer able to work, with land, staff, fodder and a veterinarian who treated them until the end of their lives. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:21:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:22:00} These are his accounts.

## The waqf in Mamluk Cairo's architecture, in Yeomans
Yeomans defines the waqf as land or property endowed in perpetuity for charity and, in his own view, traces it to the Byzantine pious foundations known as piae causae. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.201} The founder drew up a deed (waqfiyya) setting out how the property was run and its income; Yeomans cites Michael Rogers that the founder's descendants drew benefits in perpetuity and that the success of this "legalized self-interest" explains the number of such institutions and the excellence of Mamluk architecture. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.201} [[person/barsbay|Barsbay]], for example, had the waqf's conditions carved in the tiraz of his al-Mu'izz street madrasa so that later rulers could not tamper with it. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.210}
