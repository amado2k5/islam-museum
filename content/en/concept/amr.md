## Definition
Amr has two lexical senses: a matter or a state of affairs, and a request to act, the opposite of a prohibition. The first is pluralised as umur and the second as awamir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3381}

## Usul questions
- Is the word shared between the two senses, or is it a true request with the other sense as a figure? {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3381}
- Is a request a command only if it is binding? The Hanafis say yes; al-Baqillani and the Shafi'is do not require it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3381}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3382}
- Must the command be from a higher to a lower? Some Maturidis and al-Amidi require it; al-Ash'ari does not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3382}

## Forms
- Explicit: the imperative verb, the imperative noun form, and the present verb with the command particle. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3382}
- Implicit: a report that states a ruling, a praise of the doer, or something the required act depends on. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3382}

## What the form means
- The imperative for the majority means obligation, for Abu Hashim and many usulis means recommendation, and some hold it shared. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3382}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3383}
- A command after a prohibition means permission for most, and obligation for the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3383}
- A command does not by itself require repetition: the Hanafis say no, others say it does, and some say only once. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3383}
- The preferred Hanafi view is that it does not require speed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3383}
- A command to pass an order to a third party is not a command to that third party, unless a sign shows otherwise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3383}

## Compliance, validity and conflict
- Doing what is commanded, with its conditions and parts, validates it, by agreement when validity means compliance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3384}
- Where a prohibition conflicts with a command, the prohibition prevails, since avoiding harm comes before seeking benefit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3384}
- In homicide and damage the liability falls on the one who kills or destroys, not on the one who commanded, with exceptions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3384}
- Obedience is owed to God and His Messenger, and to others in what is not sinful. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3384}
