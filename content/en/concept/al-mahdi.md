## Summary
The Mahdi is, in the view of most Sunnis, a man of the house of Muhammad who appears before the Hour in a time of intense corruption and injustice, filling the earth with justice as it was filled with injustice, ruling by the law of Islam and guiding people to the right path. Some hold that the Mahdi is Jesus son of Mary, who descends from heaven; others combine the two. This belongs to the unseen, about which nothing beyond revealed text is certain, so the believer hopes for what the texts establish without asserting details they do not.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam explains al-Mahdi as the "divinely guided one", a figure of the last days whom Muslims believe will bring in an age of justice and true belief shortly before time ends. The earliest Muslims, it says, also used the title as an honorific for the four earliest caliphs and for Muhammad, and the Qur'an does not mention it. In the Dictionary's account Shiis and some Sunnis turned the idea into the belief in a messianic deliverer who will return to champion their cause. Themes it finds in both traditions are that he will belong to Muhammad's family, will come when the world is hopelessly corrupt, will reign over a time of natural abundance, and will spread justice and restore the faith. The Dictionary says "some Sunnis", whereas the card above gives the Sunni view as that of most Sunnis; both are reported and neither is settled. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1377}
