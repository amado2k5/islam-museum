## Summary
The books bring together two neighbouring chapters: what the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] left at his death, and his saying that he is not inherited. The gist of the first, as Ibn Kathir's chapter heading has it, is that he left no dinar, no dirham, no male or female slave, but land which he made all charity. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 310} This page sets out the reports one by one as Ibn Kathir, al-Bayhaqi and Ibn Hajar give them. For the rules of an estate in general, see [[concept/tarika-estate|Tarika]].

## The report of 'Amr ibn al-Harith
Al-Bukhari reports from 'Amr ibn al-Harith, brother of Juwayriya, the Mother of the Believers: the Messenger of God left at his death no dinar, no dirham, no male slave, no female slave and nothing at all, except his white mule, his weapons and land he left as charity. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3400} In a wording Ibn Kathir gives, it is the mule he used to ride, and the land he made for the wayfarer. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 310} Ibn Kathir notes that al-Bukhari alone has this wording, not Muslim, that he gave it in several places through Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i, and that al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i report it too. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 310} In Fath al-Bari it stands in the chapter on the maintenance of the Prophet's wives after his death. {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 241}

## What 'A'isha reports
'A'isha, through Masruq, says he left no dinar, no dirham, no sheep, no camel, and made no bequest. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3400} Ibn Kathir cites other reports from her that add the male and female slave, and mentions that Sufyan doubted this addition. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 310} In another report she says that when he died there was nothing in her house that a living creature could eat except half a load of barley on a shelf; she ate from it for a long time, then measured it and it was gone. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3401} {cite:fath-al-bari scan vol. 2 p. 241} 'Ali ibn al-Husayn says the same of dinar, dirham, slave and slave-girl. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3401}

## The pawned armour
'A'isha says he died with his armour pawned to a Jew for thirty sa' of barley, and that he had bought food from him on credit and pawned an iron armour. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 311} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3401} Anas says he heard him say that the family of Muhammad had not a sa' of wheat or dates that morning, though he had nine wives then, and that he had pawned an armour with a Jew in Medina and found nothing to redeem it with until he died. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 3402} The book of his conduct (Zad al-Ma'ad) names this armour Dhat al-Fudul and gives the term of the debt as a year. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} Ibn Kathir also cites a report from Ibn 'Abbas that he looked at Uhud and said it would not please him to have it as gold for the family of Muhammad to spend, and that he would not die with two dinars unless he held them back for a debt. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 311}

## The dinars in his illness
Ahmad's report, as Ibn Kathir and al-Bayhaqi give it, has Umm Salama say he came in looking troubled and said it was the seven dinars that had come to them the day before and which they had not spent by evening, left in a fold of the bedding. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 446} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 517} 'A'isha says she had six dinars, or seven according to the narrator Musa ibn Jubayr, and that in his illness he told her to give them away; his pain kept her from it until God healed him, then he asked and she excused herself. He called for them, laid them in his palm and said: what would the Prophet of God think if he met God with these in his keeping. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 517} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 446} Another report from Anas says he stored nothing for the morrow, which Ibn Kathir takes to mean things that spoil quickly, such as food. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 446} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 447}

## His saying that what he left is charity
Ibn Kathir quotes Abu Hurayra: my heirs shall not divide a dinar; what I leave, after the upkeep of my wives and the cost of my agent, is charity. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 312} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 313} For the lands and their management after him, see [[concept/charitable-lands-of-the-prophet|the Prophet's charitable lands]] and [[event/fatima-inheritance-claim-fadak|Fatima's claim and Fadak]]. The long report of Malik ibn Aws, in which al-'Abbas and 'Ali bring their dispute to 'Umar, is kept as [[passage/bukhari-4033|al-Bukhari 4033]]. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4033}

## Did the Prophet leave a will?
The reports differ. Ibn 'Abbas and Muhammad ibn 'Ali say he left only what is between the two covers, that is, the Qur'anic codex. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 312} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 5019} Talha asked 'Abd Allah ibn Abi Awfa whether the Prophet made a will; he said no, but that he enjoined the Book of God. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 312} By contrast, Ibn Hisham reports from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Shihab, that he made only six bequests: a hundred wasq from Khaybar to each of four groups he names, that Usama's expedition be carried out, and that two religions not be left in the Arabian Peninsula. {cite:ibn-hisham-sira scan p. 521} In the pages I read, Ibn Kathir does not reconcile these two accounts directly, so both stand as reported.

## How Ibn Kathir reads it all
After the reports, Ibn Kathir says many reports will follow on his houses, slaves, horses, camels, sheep, weapons, mule, donkey, clothes, furnishings and ring, and suggests that he perhaps gave much of this away in life, freed the slaves he freed and set aside what he set aside, with the lands of Banu al-Nadir, Khaybar and Fadak serving the Muslims' needs, but left nothing to be inherited. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 312} After the lists of his belongings he returns to say that this implies he had completed the freeing and the charity of everything listed. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 399} For the lists, see [[concept/weapons-mounts-and-belongings-of-the-prophet|the Prophet's weapons, mounts and belongings]], and for what survived him, [[concept/belongings-of-the-prophet-kept-by-the-companions|the belongings the Companions kept]].
