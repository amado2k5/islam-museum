## Treatment by cause
What arises from a blow is treated with relaxing fomentations; what from injury — if from drinking something it is treated by what is known in the chapters of poisons; if from fever, by strong moistening of brain, nerve and muscles with strongly moistening liniments and staying in a cold house; if from pain, the pain is calmed after examining what it is and cutting its cause; if from a sting, treated as said in the chapters of stings; if from a swelling, as said in the treatment of nerve swellings; if from dryness, its treatment is hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.147}

## Treatment of the dry
The fittest treatment of the dry is the immersion bath and anointing with moistening oil after it, repeated many times — provided there is no fever that never remits — all the joints being tended to it; if the strength is not weak the moist patient's food is made bread with honey and the like, while the food of the dry convulsion's owners is all that moistens and softens, the greasy soft gruels (the named juices, oils and waters are not reproduced) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.147} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.148}

## Treatment of the moist and the rule of evacuation
As for treatment: the moist must be treated by the strong evacuations and purgings mentioned where we spoke of evacuating the gross humour from the nerve by purgatives and sharp clysters; if you see the signs of blood's prevalence very clear, venesect first — especially if the cause of the repletion is much wine drinking — without drawing out all the needed blood, and it is dissolved little by little by the convulsion's motions to resist the convulsion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.148}

## Sharing and making haste
Know that when the convulsion is general to the body without the face's organs the physicians aim venesection at the neck vertebrae with plasters and liniments, and if in the face's organs too they aim at the brain as well; if the convulsion is from the stomach's sharing and you see the said sign, hasten to purge that person, for he may once vomit something sharp or a putrid humour and recover on the spot (the named plasters, potions, suppositories and their measures are not reproduced) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.150}
