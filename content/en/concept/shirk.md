## Brief
In the extract, [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya]] holds that shirk is the greatest wrong, and that calling on the dead, seeking their help and asking them for intercession are among its gates {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.8} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.9}. He argues that intercession is not gained by taking intercessors, but only by God's permission and acceptance {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.12}.

## Its origin in his account
The author relies on the people of Noah, and on the idols they worshipped after they had devoted themselves to the graves of their righteous men {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.2} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.3}. He holds that veneration of graves is closer to the heart than veneration of trees and stones, since people worship at graves in their hearts what they would not do in God's houses {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.4} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.5}.

## Intercession in his account
The author states that all intercession belongs to God, that an intercessor intercedes only with God's permission and only for one God approves of, and that the Qur'an limits intercession in this way {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.12} {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.13}. He holds that comparing God to kings, who are approached through their favourites, is a faulty analogy and the root of the shirk of creation {cite:ighathat-lahfan-muntakhab p.12}.

## For the reader
This is the author's view. A general definition of shirk from the wider tradition is not yet included and needs its own sources.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lecture series Al Dinu Al Qayem

The Sheikh relates that the Prophet ordered the tree of Ridwan to be cut down, to uproot the seed of shirk, lest people one day sit beneath it and say that the Messenger of God sat there {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:38:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:39:00}. He recounts Umar's life before Islam and an idol that he worshipped, and a boy who mocked an idol that could neither eat nor answer (needs_source: the details of the story) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:37:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep03 00:38:00}.

He says that Abraham mocked those who buy what neither benefits nor harms (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 01:09:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep04 01:10:00}. He says that God does not forgive shirk, and forgives what lies below it to whomever He wills {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-dinu-al-qayem/ep13 00:17:30}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series Nafahat Ramadaniyya (the faults of the tongue)
In an episode on turning in supplication to other than God, the Sheikh says that some people associate partners with God without realising it, counts calling on other than God as a form of shirk, and urges making one's affirmation of God's oneness and one's creed purely for God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:11:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:12:00} He holds that one who asks the person buried in a shrine for his needs is asking a human being who has no power over anything for himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:03:30} The details are under [[practice/dua|supplication]].
