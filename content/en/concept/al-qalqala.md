## Definition
The qalqala is one of the letter-qualities with no opposite: the sound of the letter trembles when it carries sukun until an echo is heard. Its letters are five, gathered in «قطب جد»: qaf, ta, ba, jim and dal {cite:ar-wikisource}.

## Its ruling
Ibn al-Jazari says in the chapter of the letters' use: "make the muqalqal clear when it is still, and if it is in stopping it is clearest" — so the qalqala must be heard whenever one of its letters carries sukun, and it grows strongest when the sukun comes from stopping {cite:ar-wikisource}. No qalqala appears with a vowel, for the vowel suffices the letter in being heard.

## In teaching
Teachers grade the qalqala as minor when the letter falls still mid-speech and major when it falls still at the end on stopping, and they treat sounding it as a guard that keeps the letter from being swallowed by the still letter before it; it is among the first qualities a learner hears in the teachers' codices {cite:ar-wikisource}.
