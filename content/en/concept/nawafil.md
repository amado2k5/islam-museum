## Overview
Supererogatory acts (*nawafil*) are deeds beyond what is obligatory: prayer, fasting, charity, and remembrance. In the thirty-eighth hadith of the Forty, they are the second rank of drawing near to God after the obligations, as in the words: "My servant does not cease to draw near to Me by supererogatory acts until I love him." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.770}

## Their rank after the obligations
The servant continues with supererogatory acts after establishing the obligation, until God's love reaches him. In [[person/ibn-rajab|Ibn Rajab's]] commentary their rank follows the obligations; it does not precede them. Whoever neglects the obligation and occupies himself with the supererogatory has preferred the lesser over the greater. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.770}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh likens obligatory acts to a house and voluntary acts to the garden around it: a thief who wants a house with no garden is in its yard at once, while in a house surrounded by a garden the family may wake before he reaches the door; likewise Satan comes at the one who does voluntary prayers through the voluntary acts first, while one who does only the obligatory may be overtaken and made lazy about the night or dawn prayer. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep16 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep16 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep16 00:06:30} He holds that one who does not pray dawn and night prayers in the mosque should count himself among those described as hypocrites, and urges two rak'as in the depth of the night. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep16 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep16 00:07:00}
