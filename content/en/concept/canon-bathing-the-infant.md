## Summary
Ibn Sina sets out how to bathe an infant: in balanced water in summer and in water leaning to warm but not stinging in winter, after its longest sleep, two or three times a day, with a particular hold, gentle drying and then sweet oil dropped in the nose {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.

## Water and time
Its bath should be in balanced water in summer and in water leaning to warmth, not stinging, in winter. The best time to wash is after its longest sleep, and it may be washed two or three times a day and moved gradually to water leaning to mildly cool in summer; in winter the water should never leave balanced warmth. It is bathed only long enough to warm its body, then taken out, and its ear passages are guarded from water reaching them {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.

## Holding and drying
During the wash it is held with the right hand along its left arm, resting on its chest not its belly, and the palms touch its back, feet and head gently. Then it is dried with a soft cloth and rubbed gently, laid first on its belly and then on its back while it is rubbed, pressed and shaped, then bound in a cloth and sweet oil dropped in its nose, for that washes the eyes and their coats {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.204}.
