## Summary
Ibn Sina divides the head's movements into its own and those shared with five neck vertebrae, and orders its muscles by direction: bowing, turning back and tilting to the sides {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.64-65}.

## Bowing and throwing back
For bowing there are two muscles coming from behind the ears above and from the sternum bones below; some took them for one muscle or three. When one moves, the head tilts to its side; when both move, it bows straight forward. For bowing the head and neck together there is a pair beneath the gullet joined to the first and second vertebrae. For throwing the head back there are four pairs tucked under the earlier ones, and for turning head and neck back three deep pairs and a covering pair {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.64-65}.

## Tilting and the wisdom of it
For tilting to the sides there are two pairs at the head's joint: a front pair joining the head to the second vertebra and a back pair joining the first to the head. These four are the smallest muscles but make up by the excellence of their position what others reach by size. The head's joint needed two opposed things, firmness and many movements, so the joint was left loose and firmed by the many muscles wound around it, and both aims were met {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.65}.
