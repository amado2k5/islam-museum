## Nature and selection
Dioscorides said the leek has three kinds: the Syrian, with a bulb-like root, very poor in chyme; the Nabataean, sharper than the Syrian with some astringency, which therefore stops blood; and the wild, known as the qurt, worse than the first and more like a drug than a food. The Nabataean goes into treatments {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Temperament
Hot in the third degree and dry in the second; the wild is hotter and drier and so worse {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Actions and properties
The Syrian with sumac removes warts and hives {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Wounds and ulcers
The Syrian with salt helps malignant ulcers, and the wild kind helps ulcers of the breast {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532}.

## Head, ears and teeth
It stops nosebleed, and its seed is fumigated with tar on a tooth with worm, killing and dropping the worm. Eating it gives headache and brings bad dreams; its ash with rose oil and wine vinegar is for a painful ear. It spoils the gums and teeth and makes them scaly, especially the Syrian. The Nabataean, its juice mixed with frankincense or rose oil and dropped into the ear, helps ear pains, ringing and tinnitus {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.532} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Eyes
It brings darkness to the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Chest and breathing
With barley water it is for asthma from a thick matter, especially the Nabataean with honey; it helps swellings of the lung and ripens them. Its seed is given with myrtle berries for spitting blood, and eaten raw it helps the windpipe {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
The wild kind is bad for the stomach, worse than the garden because it is more bitter, sharper and more stinging. All leek causes wind and is boiled in two waters to lessen its wind and harm. Rufus said it cuts sour belching; in sum it is slow to digest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Excretory organs
Nabataean and wild leek hurt an ulcerated bladder and kidney, and help piles, boiled, eaten and applied as a poultice. Its seed is roasted with myrtle berries for dysentery and bleeding of the rectum. A decoction of its roots in safflower or almond oil helps colic. Its dried juice is among the things that loosen blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Excretory organs (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on urine and the menses, especially the Nabataean and the wild, the wild more than the other; it stirs desire, as does its roasted seed; and sitting in a decoction of its leaf is said to help closure of the womb and hardness in it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.

## Poisons and bites
Its juice is for bites {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.533}.
