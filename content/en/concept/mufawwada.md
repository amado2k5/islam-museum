## Summary
A mufawwada is a woman married without a dower named, or on condition that she has none. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14845}

## A marriage without a dower
The jurists agree that a dower is not a pillar of marriage, so the contract is valid without one, citing the Quranic verse on divorce before consummation and Ibn Mas'ud's ruling that she receives the dower of women like her. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14845} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14846}

## Delegation of the dower
When the woman, being sane, adult and of sound judgement, leaves the dower out, most jurists hold the marriage valid. The Malikis, and a Shafi'i view, hold it void when the absence of a dower is made a condition, because it resembles a woman given as a gift. The Malikis say that if such a contract is made it is annulled before consummation and, after it, a dower of like women is owed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14846} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14847}

## Delegation by a guardian
Delegation is not valid for a minor girl, a mad woman or one not of sound judgement. Where a guardian delegates without her consent, the result depends on whether he needs her permission to marry her off: if he does and does not seek it, the marriage is void; if he seeks it for the marriage but not the delegation, the marriage stands and she receives the dower of women like her. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.14847}
