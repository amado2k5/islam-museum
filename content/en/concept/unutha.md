## Definition
Unutha is the opposite of masculinity; the female is the opposite of the male, with the plural inath. Femininity is known by bodily signs, such as menstruation, and by moral traits. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3568}

## Honour of the female in Islam
- A welcome at birth: Islam abolished the pre-Islamic dislike of daughters and their burial, and gave every human the right to life. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3569}
- The aqiqa is one sheep for a girl and two for a boy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3570}
- Good naming, changing of ugly names for girls as for boys, and kunya for a woman as for a man. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3570}
- Inheritance: God gave the female a share of inheritance, after the pre-Islamic practice of not inheriting to women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3570}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3571}
- Care of the girl in childhood, and no preference of a son over a daughter in upbringing or in gifts. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3571}
- Honour of the wife and good conduct toward her; the mother comes first in honour. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3572}

## Shared rights
- Learning: seeking knowledge is an obligation on every Muslim, and women went to learn in the Prophet's time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3572}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3573}
- Obligations: a woman is responsible for the religious duties, and her guardian must teach her prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3574}
- Jihad: when the enemy attacks a land, it becomes obligatory on women as on men, and worship is eased for them in menstruation, pregnancy, childbirth and suckling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3575}
- Her will: a previously married woman is not married until she is consulted and a virgin until she gives leave; her consent is required by the Hanafis, who also allow her to contract her own marriage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3575}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3576}
- Protection: a woman may grant safe conduct to unbelievers, and it applies to the Muslims. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3577}
- Financial personality: a woman has a separate financial personality and may deal with her property by exchange; the jurists differ on whether she may give away all of it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3577}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3578}
- Work: her first duty is her home, but the law does not stop her from trading, selling, buying or acting as agent, within conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3578}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3579}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3580}

## Rules specific to her
- Urine of an infant: the Shafi'is and Hanbalis distinguish between a boy and a girl, the Hanafis and Malikis do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3580}
- Menstruation and pregnancy are signs of puberty; she is excused from prayer during menstruation, and washes when it ends, and she abstains from reciting the Qur'an and entering the mosque. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3581}
- Her milk is clean and creates the bond of suckling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3581}
- Nature: it is recommended to remove facial and pubic hair; her body is awrah in prayer except the face and hands for the majority. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3581}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3582}
- Touch and ablution: the Hanafis hold that touch does not break ablution; the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis have rules tied to desire or to any touch. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3582}
- Bathhouses: women may enter for an excuse such as menstruation or childbirth; the Hanafis and Malikis dislike it when covering is not kept. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3582}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3583}
- Imitation: forbidden to imitate men in dress, speech or conduct; she may adorn herself with jewellery, and silk and gold are permitted to women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3583}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3584}
- Going out: she goes out covered and not displaying herself, and may not be alone with a stranger; a married woman needs her husband's leave to go out. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3584}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3585}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3587}
- Prayer: she does not call the adhan or the iqama and does not lead men; she gathers herself in bowing and prostration, and leaves before the men. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3585}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3586}
- Hajj: without a husband or a mahram it is not obligatory for her, by the Hanafis and Hanbalis; she wears stitched clothes, does not run in the tawaf, and does not veil her face with a niqab. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3586}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3587}
- Voluntary worship: she may not fast while her husband is present without his leave; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis let him stop her once she has started. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3587}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3588}
- Offices: women are preferred in custody, and not in leading prayer, war or judgement; she may be appointed guardian by will and may witness in property matters. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3588}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3589}
- Marriage guardianship: by the majority a woman may not give herself or another in marriage; Abu Hanifa and Zufar allow her to contract her own marriage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3590}
- Retaliation and blood money: retaliation is equal between men and women; there is a view that a woman's blood money is half a man's. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3590}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3591}

## Female animals
- Zakat on camels: what is taken is normally female; the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow a male of the second year when the female is absent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3591}
- Sacrifice: the Shafi'is hold that a male is preferred in the accepted view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3591}
- Blood money: the heavier diya in camels is all female, by the jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3591}
