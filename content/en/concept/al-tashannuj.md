## Its definition
Convulsion is a nervous disorder by which the muscles move toward their origins and refuse extension: of them is what stays in its state and does not extend, and what easily returns to extension like yawning and hiccup {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.142}

## Its cause in sum
Its cause is either a matter or a cause other than matter such as heat or dryness; the matter of the material convulsion is mostly phlegmatic, sometimes melancholic, sometimes sanguine — as in the swellings of the muscles, when the swelling matter resolves and ulcerates the nerve's fibre, adding to its breadth and taking from its length {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.142}

## The material kind and the seat of its matter
Every material convulsion either has its effective matter encompassing all the muscles — when it is convulsion without swelling — or seated in one spot while the rest of the parts run, as in the convulsion of a swelling from matter poured down for a blow, a cut or another cause of swellings; and it is not far that some convulsion arises from a thick blowing wind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.142}
