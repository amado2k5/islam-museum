## Overview
Asceticism (*zuhd*) in its linguistic sense is turning away from a thing because one finds it insignificant and one's aspiration rises above it. Ibn Rajab presents it in his commentary on the hadith "Be ascetic in this world and God will love you." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.643-655}

## Its meaning according to Ibn Rajab
Ibn Rajab notes that asceticism in this world is not forbidding what is lawful, nor squandering wealth. It is that the servant be more reliant on what is in God's hand than on what is in his own. When he suffers a calamity, he is more hopeful of its reward than of its removal. He says that this is the saying of Abu Muslim al-Khawlani in Ahmad's *Musnad*, and that it is the sound view in his opinion. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.643-655}

## Sayings of the early generations
- Al-Zuhri: the ascetic is one whose patience is not overcome by the unlawful, and whose gratitude is not occupied by the lawful.
- Sufyan ibn Uyayna: one who gives thanks when blessed, and is patient when tried.
- Sufyan al-Thawri and Ahmad: asceticism is the shortening of hope, not eating coarse food or wearing a cloak.
- Ibrahim ibn Adham: three kinds, an obligation regarding the unlawful, a virtue regarding the lawful, and safety regarding doubtful matters.
- Abu Sulayman al-Darani: leaving what occupies you from God, which Ibn Rajab regards as comprehensive of its meanings.

{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.643-655}

He notes that people disagreed, in two views, over whether the name of ascetic belongs to one who abstains only from the unlawful. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.643-655}

## In Sahih Muslim
Muslim opens the book of asceticism and tender hearts with the saying of the Prophet: "The world is a prison for the believer and a paradise for the unbeliever." {cite:sahih-muslim 2956}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Sharh Ketab Al Fawaed lectures (episode 5)

The Sheikh says that when hearts renounce the tables of this world, they sit at the tables of the Hereafter {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep05 00:15:30}.

He says that a person should live in this world as a stranger or a traveller to God, with his heart in the Hereafter {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep05 00:37:30}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh holds that renunciation has been misunderstood as poverty, while a person may own billions and be renunciant, or own only a few coins and crave the world; he mentions the hadith that if the son of Adam had a valley of gold he would wish for a second. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep13 00:08:00}

## In Yusuf al-Qaradawi's lessons Ta'ammulat Qur'aniyya
[[person/yusuf-al-qaradawi|Yusuf al-Qaradawi]] says a rich person is not one of "the people of this world" if the world is in his hand and not in his heart, and that the Qur'an ties outcomes to what a person wills ([[passage/quran-17-18|al-Isra' 18]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:04:00} He cites a saying from one of the early generations of giving up the world for its little benefit, much toil and quick end, and a saying attributed to Ali that its start is weeping, its middle toil and its end perishing. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:12:00} He says Yusuf, Dawud and Sulayman were given kingship, and that wealthy Companions such as Uthman and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf owned the world without it owning them. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep08 00:18:00}
