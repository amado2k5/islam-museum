## Summary
Hawa in the technical sense is the inclination of the soul towards pleasures it relishes, without the call of the law. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16565} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16566}

## Hawa and appetite
Hawa concerns opinions and beliefs; shahwa concerns obtaining pleasurable things. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16566}

## The ruling on following it
Hawa that agrees with the law is praised; hawa that opposes it is blamed, and the law forbids following the blamed kind. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16566} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16567}

## Followers of desire
The jurists divide followers of hawa into those whose unbelief is to be declared and those who are not. They differ on the testimony of the first; most accept the testimony of the misguided but not of the Khattabiyya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16569}

## Kinds of hearts and causes
Al-Ghazali divides hearts into three kinds in their steadfastness towards good and evil. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16569} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16570}
The causes of following desire are the strength of its rule and reluctance to think clearly when matters are unclear. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16570}
Scholars count opposing one's own desire among the greatest struggles. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16570} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16571}
