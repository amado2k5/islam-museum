## Summary
Tiryaq is a medicine against poison, of several kinds; some kinds contain viper flesh, and its lawfulness differs by school according to what it contains. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5003} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5004}

## Definition
Tiryaq is an arabized word, usually said with a kasra; it is a medicine used against poison, of several kinds. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5003}

## The Hanbali ruling
The Hanbalis hold that tiryaq is a medicine against poison into which viper flesh is put, so they do not allow eating or drinking it, since viper flesh is forbidden and no forbidden thing may be used for cure, on the hadith of Ibn Mas'ud that God has not put your cure in what He forbade you. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5003}
Al-Khattabi said that drinking tiryaq is not disliked in itself, but because of the viper flesh in it; if none is in it, there is no harm. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5003}

## The Hanafi and Shafi'i views
The Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold viper flesh forbidden. For the Hanafis, the apparent school view forbids viper flesh in a cure; one view allows it when a cure is known and no other remedy exists, and this is the ruling given. The Shafi'is have similar views. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5004}

## The Maliki view
The Malikis allow eating a viper when it is slaughtered at the proper place, its poison is safe, and it is needed for a sickness; so the tiryaq into which its flesh has entered may be used for cure. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.5004}
