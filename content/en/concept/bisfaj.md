## Nature and selection
A fine, dusty stem with knots tending to black, a little red, or green; its branches are like a many-footed worm, and it tastes sweet with astringency. Some say it grows on a tree in thickets, others that it grows on rocks {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.407}. The editor's footnote says the name is Persian.

## Temperament and actions
Hot in the second degree and dry in the third, drying very much {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.407}.

## Uses by organ
Selection: the best is as thick as the little finger, reddish or yellowish, firm and fresh, with a slight bitterness and sweetness with astringency, and a clove-like taste {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.407}.

Actions: a dissolving, ripening agent that dissolves wind and humours {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.407}.

Joints: its poultice helps a twisted nerve {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.407}.

Excretory organs: loosens black bile without colic and loosens phlegm and watery humour; boiled in cock or fish broth it is used for colic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.407}.
