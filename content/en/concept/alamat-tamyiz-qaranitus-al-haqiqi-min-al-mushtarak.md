## The kind by sharing
As for confusion that comes by sharing, it is shown by its falling all at once, following the bad state of another organ, and coming in fits with the fits of intensification, lessening as the other organ's state lessens and growing with its growth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79} That which comes from the cerebral sarsam arises little by little and stays; the signs of the true sarsam come first and then the disease occurs, whereas for the unreal kind the diseases of other organs come first and then its signs appear {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79}

## From the diaphragm and the muscles of the chest
As for that which comes from the diaphragm and the muscles of the chest, the signs of sarsam and of dhat al-janb come before it: a stabbing pain in the side on breathing, tightness of breath, a sawlike pulse and a dry cough, which later becomes moist for the most part, with a continued fever whose heat is mostly in the regions of the chest, whereas in the true kind it is in the regions of the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79} The upward stretching of the hypochondria is frequent in it, and a sense of pain above the skull, not all over, is particular to it; the signs mentioned earlier are not strong or many, and its breath varies, weakening and quickening at times and growing large at others, whereas in true qaranitus the breath is larger, indeed large {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79} The two sarsams share the strength of the confusion, but the sarsam that follows the true one is told apart because its strength follows the strength of the fever and lightens as the fever lightens {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79}

## From the mouth of the stomach and from swellings of organs
That which comes from a humour at the mouth of the stomach is felt with a stinging at the mouth of the stomach, nausea, thirst and bitterness of the mouth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79} That which comes from swellings of other organs is known from what shows of their states, for unless they are plain and clear they do not lead to confusion of mind and plain sarsam {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.79}
