## Summary
Passages of the Qur'an describe its tongue as clear Arabic; what did the commentators understand by the word mubin? This page gathers what a research summary supplied to the museum reports of al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi and Ibn Ashur, attributing each view to its holder and not judging between them. The commentaries named were not opened, and no page numbers are given because they vary by edition.

## The verses
The two principal verses are [[passage/quran-16-103|al-Nahl 103]], which contrasts the tongue of the one alleged to have taught, a foreign one, with this clear Arabic tongue, and [[passage/quran-26-195|al-Shu'ara 195]], which describes the revelation as in a clear Arabic tongue. The summary adds related verses: [[passage/quran-12-2|Yusuf 2]], [[passage/quran-41-3|Fussilat 3]], [[passage/quran-41-44|Fussilat 44]] and [[passage/quran-43-3|al-Zukhruf 3]]. No wording of any verse is typed here; the reader is referred to their pages, taken from the frozen Tanzil text.

## The meaning of mubin
According to the summary the commentators understand mubin as the active participle of abana, that which makes clear and manifest, so it describes eloquence, lucidity and the absence of obscurity. The language is clear and expressive to its Arab audience, which is a description of the medium; it is also a polemical argument against the claim that a foreign human taught the Prophet, since the alleged teacher's tongue is foreign and this Qur'an is in a clear Arabic tongue.

## Al-Tabari
Al-Tabari in Jami' al-bayan, at al-Nahl 103 and al-Shu'ara 195, holds that mubin denotes both the clarity of the language and the lucidity of the message, and that the contrast with a'jami shows the Qur'an to be in the pure, eloquent Arabic known to the Arabs {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section a}.

## Ibn Kathir
Ibn Kathir in Tafsir al-Qur'an al-azim says, at al-Shu'ara 195, that it was sent down in perfect and eloquent Arabic so that it may be quite clear, leaving no room for excuses and establishing clear proof. At al-Nahl 103 he stresses the impossibility that such eloquence could come from a foreigner with limited Arabic {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section a}.

## Al-Qurtubi
Al-Qurtubi in al-Jami' li-ahkam al-Qur'an treats the verses in place and summarises the related linguistic points in the introduction to his book, with a focus on clarity for the audience {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section a}. What he says on non-Arabic words is in [[concept/khilaf-al-ulama-fi-al-alfaz-al-ajamiyya-fi-al-quran|The dispute over non-Arabic words in the Qur'an]].

## Ibn Ashur
[[person/muhammad-al-tahir-ibn-ashur|Ibn Ashur]] in al-Tahrir wa-l-tanwir holds that mubin conveys high articulacy and expressive clarity, that the precedence of Arabic over clear highlights the language's native and purely Arab character, and that mubin underscores the inimitable eloquence {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section a}.

## The four books named
The summary sets the views out in a table of four scholars and four books: Jami' al-bayan of al-Tabari, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-azim of Ibn Kathir, al-Jami' li-ahkam al-Qur'an of al-Qurtubi and al-Tahrir wa-l-tanwir of Ibn Ashur. Each of these treats the verse in place according to its own method, some centring on reports and meaning, some on rulings and language, some on rhetoric. None was opened here, so the commentators' own wording is not given, only the gist of what the summary reports of them.

## What the views share and what marks each
They share that mubin refers to the clarity of Arabic for its people and that it is a proof against those who deny. They differ in emphasis: al-Tabari joins the clarity of language and message; Ibn Kathir centres on the establishing of proof and the impossibility of such eloquence from a foreigner; al-Qurtubi on the relation of the verses to linguistic questions; and Ibn Ashur on the order of the words in the verse and what it signals. The matter ties to [[concept/al-ijaz-al-bayani-inda-al-mufassirin|The inimitability of the Qur'an's language in the commentaries]] and [[concept/al-arabiyya-lughat-al-quran|Arabic, the language of the Qur'an]].
