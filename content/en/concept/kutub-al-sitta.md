## Overview
The Six Books, in the terminology of the later scholars, are: [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]], [[work/sahih-muslim|Sahih Muslim]], [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan Abi Dawud]], [[work/jami-al-tirmidhi|Jami al-Tirmidhi]], [[work/sunan-al-nasai|Sunan al-Nasa'i]], and [[work/sunan-ibn-majah|Sunan Ibn Majah]]. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari} {cite:sahih-muslim} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud} {cite:jami-al-tirmidhi} {cite:sunan-al-nasai} {cite:sunan-ibn-majah}

## Their standing
They are the most prominent collections of hadith with chains of transmission arranged by chapters. They have been the widest reference of the community for the Prophetic hadith since the fifth century AH. A further source is needed to establish this date.

## Differences in counting
Some include the [[work/muwatta-malik|Muwatta' of Malik]] or the Musnad of Ahmad in place of Sunan Ibn Majah. {cite:muwatta-malik}
