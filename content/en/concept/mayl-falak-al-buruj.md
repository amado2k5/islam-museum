## Summary
The obliquity of the ecliptic is the greatest distance between the zodiac and the celestial equator, namely the distance between the solstice point and the celestial equator {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 112}. Al-Farghani gives two values for it: Ptolemy's value and the value from the measurement tested in the days of al-Ma'mun {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 112}.

## The Two Values
According to what Ptolemy found, the obliquity is "twenty-three degrees and fifty-one minutes" when the circle is 360 degrees {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 112}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 150}. According to the measurement tested by order of al-Ma'mun, on which a number of scholars agreed, it is "twenty-three degrees and thirty-five minutes" {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 112}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 150}.

## Its Effect
Al-Farghani notes that the obliquity is what determines the sun's highest altitude above the horizon in the northern signs in polar regions, and what defines the difference between a latitude where the solstice is visible and one where it is not {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 155}{cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 161}.
