## Nature and selection
The best is the Armenian, which is not quickly reached by spoiling and sourness. When apricot is eaten the book says mastic and anise should be taken with it; the stated measure is not carried over here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Temperament
Cold and moist in the second degree; the oil of its kernel is hot and dry in the third {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Actions and properties
Its humour is quick to putrefy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its infusion quiets thirst; apricot suits the stomach better than the peach, and the Armenian does not spoil in the stomach or sour quickly. What prevents its harm is to take anise and mastic after it, and for the chilled, pure honey {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Excretory organs
The oil of its kernel helps haemorrhoids {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.

## Fevers
It breeds fevers through its quick putrefaction, and the book says its infusion helps hot fevers. A word before the infusion is unclear in the copy and is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.572}.
