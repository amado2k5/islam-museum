## The treatment of the spitting of blood
Galen told that a woman was struck by a blood-flow and spat four days and weakened; he fed her a gruel and an astringent fruit since her last food was far back, treated her head with a drug of this kind (a labelled line holding an animal's droppings), allowed her the bath for the drug's sake, did not anoint her head lest it moisten, and gave her to drink what puts to sleep of this kind on his telling, for in it is a sleeping power that bars the cough's tickle and stills the matters' flowing by thickening. On the second day he did not go near stirring her but left her quiet and still, gave her a measure of this kind less than yesterday's — his aim to graduate her to honey so the lung be cleansed by it — then left her an hour, then rubbed her limbs and after that gave her barley-water with a little bread for strength; on the fourth he gave her an old thing of this kind with much honey to cleanse her lung a strong cleansing, and fed her the rest of the days as due and managed her with the convalescents' regimen, and with all that he would set on her head, time after time, a cerate of this kind and forbade her bathing (a report on the book's attribution to Galen; the names excluded per the rule) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.336}
