## Summary
Estimation in Ibn Sina is the power that judges about sensibles by meanings that are not sensible, as an animal judges that the wolf is an enemy; it differs from imagination and the imaginative power in that its acts are judgments, and the physician does not study it because its harms follow harms to the powers before it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.100-101}.

## Its judgment
It is the power by which an animal judges that the wolf is an enemy and that the one who tends it with fodder is a friend, so that it does not flee from him in a non-rational way. Enmity and love are not sensible and the animal's senses do not perceive them, so another power judges and perceives them; though it is not rational perception, it is certainly a perception of some non-rational kind. Humans also use this power in many judgments and in that go the way of the non-speaking animal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.100}.

## How it differs from imagination and the thinking faculty
It differs from imagination, which holds the sensibles fast while this judges about them by meanings that are not sensible; and from the one called thinking or imaginative, whose acts are not followed by any judgment while its own acts are judgments, those acts composing in the sensibles and this act being a judgment on the sensible from a meaning outside the sensible. As sense judges over the forms of sensibles, estimation judges over the meanings of those forms that reach it and do not reach sense. Some speak loosely and call it imagination, and they may, since there is no quarrel over names, but the meanings and the differences must be understood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.100-101}.

## The physician's concern
The physician does not take up the study of this power, because the harms to its acts follow harms to the acts of other powers before it, such as imagination, the imaginative power and memory. He looks only at the powers whose harm in their acts is a disease; if harm to the act of one power comes from harm to the act of an earlier one and follows a bad temperament or a corrupt composition in some organ, it is enough for him to know the harm arises from that organ's bad temperament or corruption, so that he can treat it or guard against it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.101}.
