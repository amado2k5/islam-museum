## Nature and selection
Rods resembling shahsafram; the dry kind has little taste or smell at first, then leaves bitterness and sharpness; when sheep graze it they give blood in their milk. It stands in for pennyroyal and is much stronger; it has two kinds, the true and the false, which is weaker. The editor's note calls it wild pennyroyal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.560}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry toward the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.560}.

## Chest and breathing
It brings sticky moistures out of the chest and lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.560}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its wine is useful against distress and fainting {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.560}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says it brings on the menses and urine strongly, to the point of passing blood in the urine, drives out the foetus drunk, fumigated or as a pessary, and that its wine brings down the blood of childbirth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.560}.
