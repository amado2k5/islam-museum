## Summary
In the lexicon munawala is anything given by hand. In hadith it is a teacher giving his pupil his own book or a copy checked against it, with words such as 'this is my hearing, narrate it on my authority' or 'take it, copy it and return it to me'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15051}

## Related terms
Sama' is hearing, which the handing over may include; ijaza is a licence to narrate, which it may also include. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15051}

## Basis
Some Hijazis cited the Prophet's writing a letter to a commander of a raiding party to be opened at a given place; al-Bayhaqi held the case proves nothing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15052}

## Kinds
The first is handing over with a licence to narrate, the highest kind of licence. The second is handing over without a licence, on which narration is not allowed according to the sounder view of the usulis and the jurists, as Ibn al-Salah and al-Nawawi said. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15052} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15053}

## In sale
In sale, handing over a movable good such as clothes counts as taking possession: one party hands it to the other who takes it, or sets it within reach. The Hanafis hold all movables are taken into possession this way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.15053}
