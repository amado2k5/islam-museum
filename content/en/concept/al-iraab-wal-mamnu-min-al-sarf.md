## What this record is
I'rab in Arabic is the change of the endings of nouns according to their place in the sentence, and it has three cases, nominative, accusative and genitive. This record gives what a research summary supplied to the museum says of these cases and their endings by type of noun, and then of the diptote, the noun that does not take nunation. It serves the science of [[concept/ilm-al-nahw|grammar]] and connects with the marking of the endings in [[concept/naqt-al-irab|the pointing of i'rab]]. The page attributes each ending and each page number to the summary as given.

## Background
The summary rests on three references: Ryding's A Reference Grammar of Modern Standard Arabic, 2005, chapter 7, pages 167 to 184, with the nominative on pages 169 to 171, the genitive on 171 and 172 and the accusative on 172 to 182 {cite:ryding-2005-reference-grammar-msa}; Wright's A Grammar of the Arabic Language, volume 1, 1896, pages 234 to 247 {cite:wright-grammar-of-the-arabic-language-vol1}; and Mustafa al-Ghalayini's [[work/jami-al-durus-al-arabiyya|Jami' al-Durus al-Arabiyya]], volume 2, pages 311 to 319, in a PDF copy {cite:elbassair-jami-al-durus-al-arabiyya-pdf}. The example in the table is from the root k-t-b.

The summary adds that the dual and the sound masculine plural drop their final n (-ni in the dual, -na in the plural) when they are the first term of an annexation, the muḍāf.

## The diptote
The summary says an indefinite, un-annexed diptote takes only -u in the nominative and -a in both the accusative and genitive, and takes no nunation; when it is made definite with al- or annexed, it takes the ordinary triptote endings -u, -a and -i. It gives the main classes: broken plurals on the pattern mafāʿil or mafāʿīl, such as makātib, "offices", and rasāʾil, "letters"; afʿal adjectives and elatives, such as aḥmar and akbar; faʿlān adjectives such as kaslān; feminine proper names and foreign proper names of more than three letters, such as Fāṭima and Ibrāhīm; and nouns ending in -āʾ, such as ṣaḥrāʾ, "desert". It refers for this to Ryding's "declension five", pages 192 to 197, to Wright, volume 1, page 239, and to al-Ghalayini.

## How the references differ
The three references come from different times and languages: Ryding is a reference grammar of contemporary standard Arabic in English of 2005, Wright an English grammar of the Arabic language of 1896, and al-Ghalayini an Arabic teaching text in Arabic. Their terms therefore differ; the summary gives Ryding's "declension five" against al-Ghalayini's al-mamnūʿ min al-ṣarf. The page numbers are as given in the summary and the museum did not check them.

## What this page does not reach
The museum opened none of the three books and did not read what is on these pages. The summary names neither the five nouns nor the defective and shortened nouns nor the secondary marks of i'rab on verbs, so none is given here. No old Arabic text on the causes that bar sarf was quoted, and the two causes known to the grammarians are not discussed here. The museum did not check the vowels written. The record does not decide the differences among the grammarians on the limits of this chapter.
