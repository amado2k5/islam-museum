## Summary
In chapter 26 Ibn Sina treats incision: the direction in which the cut is made, the incisor's knowledge of anatomy, what he must have ready in the way of blood-stopping drugs and instruments, and what to do after an abscess has been opened {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.305-306}.

## The direction of the cut
Whoever wants to make an incision should cut along the creases and folds of that organ. The forehead is an exception: a cut there that follows its creases and folds severs the muscle of the forehead and the brow falls. In organs where the direction of the creases runs against the direction of the muscle fibre, he takes care accordingly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.305-306}.

## Knowledge of anatomy
The incisor must know anatomy, the anatomy of the nerves, the veins and the arteries, so that he does not err and cut any of them and bring about the patient's death {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.

## What he keeps ready
He must have a number of blood-stopping drugs and ointments that ease pain, and instruments of the same sort, such as Galen's remedy, hare's fur or spider's web, since spider's web has a clear benefit for this, and also egg white and all the cauteries, to prevent a flow of blood should an error of his or a necessity bring one on; the relaxing drugs are with him as well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.

## After an abscess is opened
When he has opened an abscess and let out what is in it, he should not bring oil near it, nor watery things, nor an ointment in which fat and oil predominate, such as basilicon, but one like tutty ointment, and put above it a sponge dipped in astringent wine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.306}.
