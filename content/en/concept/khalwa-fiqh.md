## Summary
Seclusion is lexically being alone with a thing in an empty place; it is linked to solitude, withdrawal and concealment. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7789}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7790} Being alone with oneself is lawful and may be recommended for remembrance and worship; with others it is lawful between two men, two women, a man and his mahram or his wife, and between a man and a woman in public where their talk is not heard; it is forbidden with a non-mahram woman. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7790}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7791} Seclusion with a non-mahram woman is forbidden by agreement, on the report "no man is alone with a woman but the devil is the third"; the Hanafis excuse a creditor who sheltered a fleeing debtor; seclusion for treatment is forbidden unless a mahram, a husband or a trustworthy woman is present, on the sounder view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7791}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7792} Seclusion with others present: the Shafiis forbid a man to be alone with several women as with one, but the usual Shafii view allows a man with two trusted women, or men with a woman if collusion is unlikely; the Hanafis lift the prohibition with a trustworthy woman or a mahram present; the Malikis dislike a man praying among women\'s rows; the Hanbalis forbid a man alone with several women. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7791}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7792} The betrothed is a non-mahram to her suitor, so seclusion with her is forbidden by agreement; seclusion with a beardless youth is forbidden if he is handsome and temptation is feared; seclusion with mahrams is lawful, including with a woman divorced by a revocable divorce. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7792}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7793} Seclusion that has effects (the valid seclusion of the Hanafis, the sufficient seclusion of the Malikis): no bar to intercourse, whether physical, legal or natural; physical bars are illness, childhood, closure or horns; legal bars are fasting, pilgrim\'s state and menstruation; natural bar is a third person present; it is not valid in a mosque, the road, the open desert or an uncovered roof. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7793}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7794}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7794} The Malikis require an adult who is not castrated and a woman able to bear intercourse, with a curtain drawn or door closed; the Shafiis in the new view give no effect to seclusion, citing the verse on touching; the Hanbalis give it effect when the man is adult and can have intercourse, even if asleep, blind or under a legal bar. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7794}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7795}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7795}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7796} Effect on the dower: most jurists require the full dower by a valid seclusion, citing the report of uncovering a wife\'s veil and the agreement of the Companions reported by al-Tahawi; the Shafiis in the new view give it no effect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7796}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7797} Effect on the waiting period: the Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis require the waiting period after valid seclusion in a valid marriage; the Shafiis in the new view do not, without intercourse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7797}
