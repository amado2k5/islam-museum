## Summary
An aiwan is an open-fronted hall with a barrel vault. In Iranian architecture the term denotes a vaulted niche or open room, while in Central Asia pillared halls were also called aiwan {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.10}.

## The four-aiwan mosque
In Iran the congregational mosque with four aiwans around a courtyard was perfected by the fourteenth century; the Jami' Masjid at Varamin (1322-26) set the general plan for later Iranian mosques {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.12}. The author notes that the four-aiwan plan appeared in India in the Sultanate period and returned under Jahangir and Shah Jahan, with the Mughals turning the open aiwans into gate-houses with doors {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.12}.

## In India
Quadrangular and octagonal aiwans, inspired by Iranian models, appear in Mughal buildings at Fatehpur Sikri and Agra Fort {cite:rezavi-iranian-influence-2002 p.10}.
