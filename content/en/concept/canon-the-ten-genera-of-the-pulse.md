## Summary
Ibn Sina lists the genera by which physicians learn the state of the pulse: ten according to the physicians, though he thinks they should be nine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.169}.

## The list
The first is taken from the extent of the expansion, the second from how the motion strikes the fingers, the third from the time of each motion, the fourth from the texture of the instrument, the fifth from its emptiness or fullness, the sixth from the heat or cold of its touch, the seventh from the time of rest, the eighth from its evenness or unevenness, the ninth from its regularity within unevenness or lack of it, and the tenth from the weighing, or rhythm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.169}.

## Why nine
He holds that the ninth genus, regularity and irregularity, falls under the uneven and is in effect a species of it, so that it is useful but not right as a division {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.171}.
