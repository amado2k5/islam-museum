## Summary
Ibn Sina says the deep jugular vein follows the gullet and rises to the lambdoid suture, enters the cranial cavity and spreads in the two brain membranes, descends into the brain and gathers in the wide place called the press, from which vessels extend to the front ventricles and weave the choroid net {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.90-91}.

## Toward the skull
The deep jugular follows the gullet and rises straight with it, leaving branches on its way that mix with those from the external jugular, and all divide in the gullet, larynx and all the deep muscles; its end passes to the end of the lambdoid suture, where branches spread in the organs between the first and second vertebrae, a hair-like vessel goes to the joint of head and neck, and branches go to the membrane covering the skull and to the meeting of the two halves of the skull and dive into it there {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.90}.

## In the brain
What remains after these branches passes into the cranial cavity at the end of the lambdoid suture, and branches spread in the two membranes of the brain to feed them and to bind the hard membrane to what surrounds and lies above it; it then descends from the thin membrane into the brain and spreads in it as the arteries do, all of it wrapped in a fold of the thick membrane that brings them to the wide place, the space into which the blood pours and gathers. From there it spreads between the two sides, and the place is called the press; as these branches near the middle ventricle they must become large vessels sucking from the press and its channels, and then extend from the middle ventricle to the two front ventricles, meet the rising arteries there, and weave the membrane known as the choroid net {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.90-91}.
