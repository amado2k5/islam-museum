## The proverb and its meaning
The proverb 'Sinimmar's reward' is one of the Arabic proverbs recorded in the old proverb books, as a research summary supplied to the museum sets out in a choice of ten. Its meaning, in the summary, is repaying kindness with cruelty. It belongs to the matter of the great Arabic proverb collections treated in the page [[concept/kutub-al-amthal-al-arabiyya|The Arabic proverb books]] {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p845}.

## The story as the books tell it
Sinimmar was a Byzantine architect who built the grand palace of [[place/al-khawarnaq|al-Khawarnaq]] for al-Nu'man I, king of al-Hira. When he boasted that he could have built a finer one, or that he knew a single stone whose removal would bring the building down, the king had him thrown from the roof so that he would never build its equal for another ruler. {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p845}

## The old sources
The summary gives it in Majma al-Amthal, volume 1, page 159, number 828, with the KetabOnline page named. Volume, page and serial numbers stand as in the summary, and the museum has not checked them {cite:ketabonline-majma-al-amthal-p845}. Majma al-Amthal is the work of Abu al-Fadl al-Maydani, who died in 518 AH, 1124 CE, and holds according to the summary over 6,000 proverbs; the summary tabulates it with Abu Hilal al-Askari's Jamharat al-Amthal and al-Zamakhshari's al-Mustaqsa {cite:arabictypinglab-arabic-proverbs}.

## When the proverb is used
The proverb is used for what fits its meaning, that is repaying kindness with cruelty, and the hearer calls its tale to mind even when it is not told. The proverb books agree in keeping a proverb together with its story, because the story explains why it was said, and the sense it is applied to may widen with use; but the summary gives no other instances of its use, so the page gives none.

## A note on the nature of the story
The story here is as the books tell it. It is one of the widely repeated traditional reports, and the museum cannot establish that it happened; it is a narrative told to explain the proverb, not confirmed history. The museum has not opened any of the books and pages named.
