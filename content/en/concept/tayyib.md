## Overview
*Tayyib* (good) is a description of what God accepts of deeds, wealth, words, and beliefs, and its opposite is *khabith* (corrupt). {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.239}

## Ibn Rajab's explanation
Ibn Rajab explains *tayyib* in the hadith of Abu Hurayra, "God is good and accepts only what is good," as meaning pure, so that God is holy and free from defects and imperfections. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.239}

## Two readings of acceptance
The first concerns charity: that nothing is accepted from it but what is lawful, as in the hadith "No one gives a charity from good earnings, and God accepts only what is good." The second is more general, making goodness a condition of deeds through their purity from what corrupts them, such as ostentation and self-admiration, and of wealth through its being lawful. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.240}

He notes that deeds, words, and beliefs all divide into good and corrupt. The Qur'an speaks of it in the doubling of the good word, like a good tree, and the corrupt word, like a corrupt tree, and the Messenger is described as one who makes good things lawful and corrupt things unlawful. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.240}

It is connected with [[concept/halal-haram|the lawful and the unlawful]].
