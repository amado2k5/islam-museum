## Summary
Ibn Sina gives three cases by how urine changes: passed thin then thickening, passed thick then clearing and depositing, or a middle state between them, and says water-like urine that stays water-like shows lack of ripening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.190}.

## The three cases
Urine may be passed thin and then thicken, which shows nature struggling and ripening, though the matter has not yet yielded in every way, and sometimes shows the melting of organs. Or it may be passed thick and then clear, the thick part separating as a sediment, which shows that nature has overcome the matter and ripened it; the more the clearing and the more abundant and quick the sediment, the better it shows ripening. If the middle state between the two persists while nature is strong and strength holds, full ripening is to be guessed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.190}.

## Watery and turbid
If the strength does not hold, death may come before ripening is feared. Often urine thickens and turns turbid because strength has collapsed, not because nature is expelling. Urine passed watery and staying watery is certainly a sign of lack of ripening. That which goes on from thinness toward thickness and continues is in many cases better than that which stands still at thickness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.190}.
