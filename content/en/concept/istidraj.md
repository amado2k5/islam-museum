## Summary
Istidraj, as the Sheikh defines it, is God continuing His favour to a servant who falls short in His service, so that the servant goes on without knowing. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep01 00:44:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep01 00:44:30}

## A verse on respite
The Sheikh quotes a verse about respite and the leading on of the wrongdoer, and does not name it in the recording. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-ketab-al-fawaed/ep01 00:44:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy explains Ibn Ata Allah's maxim that a servant should fear that God's kindness to him alongside his own continued wrongdoing may be a leading-on (istidraj); he calls it a grave maxim, and gives the example of a man who prays only on Fridays and whose wealth is doubtful, yet thinks God loves him because He gives to him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:47:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:48:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:48:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep07 00:49:00}
