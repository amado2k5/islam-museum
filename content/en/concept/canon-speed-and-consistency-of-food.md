## Summary
Ibn Sina adds two further distinctions in food beyond amount and quality: how fast or slowly it penetrates, and the consistency of the blood it yields, thick and firm or thin and quickly dissolved, and he shows when each kind is preferred and when avoided {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.268}.

## The kinds
Wine is an instance of quick penetration, roast and fried foods of slow penetration. As to the blood produced, the flesh of pigs and calves gives thick, firm blood, while wine and figs give thin blood that dissolves quickly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.268}.

## When it is preferred and avoided
Quick-penetrating food is needed to make good a collapse of the vital power and revive it, when time or strength will not stand the digestion of slow food. Quick-digesting food is avoided after slow-digesting food, lest the two mix. Thick food is avoided when obstructions are sure to form. Strongly nourishing, slow-digesting food is preferred for someone to be strengthened and readied for hard exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.268}.
