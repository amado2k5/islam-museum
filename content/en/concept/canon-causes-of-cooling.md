## Summary
In the second chapter of the second part Ibn Sina lists the cooling agents: excessive motion and excessive rest, excess and extreme scarcity of food, cold food and drug, excess of retention and of evacuation, excessive passions and more, which are then reduced to six genera {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.144}.

## The causes
Cooling agents come in kinds: excessive motion, through over-dissolving the innate heat; excessive rest, which stifles it; excess of food, eaten and drunk; extreme scarcity of food; cold food; cold drug; contact with what heats to excess among airs, plasters and bath waters; great rarefaction of the body, which scatters the innate heat from it; long contact with what warms in moderation, as a long stay in the bath; great condensation, which confines the innate heat; and contact with what cools in act and what cools in potency, even if it is hot at the moment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.144}.

## Retention, evacuation and the soul
Also excessive retention, because it confines the innate heat; excessive evacuation, because it loses the matter of heat together with the spirit that follows, and with blockages from superfluities; tight and lasting binding of limbs, which cools by shutting the path of heat; and likewise excessive grief, fear, joy and pleasure, and the cooling craft. These are reduced to six genera: excessive motion, excessive rest, contact with what cools or heats so much that it dissolves, cooling matter, scarcity of food to excess and abundance of food to excess {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.144}.
