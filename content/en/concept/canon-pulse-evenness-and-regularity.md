## Summary
Ibn Sina explains the eighth and ninth genera, evenness and unevenness of the pulse and its regularity, and holds that the last falls under the uneven {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.170}.

## Even and uneven
Evenness and unevenness are judged by the likeness of beats, or of parts of a beat, or of one part, in five matters: large and small, strong and weak, quick and slow, frequent and infrequent, hard and soft. A pulse is even without qualification when even in all of them; if even in one alone it is called even in that one, and likewise for the uneven {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.170}.

## Regular and irregular
The uneven are of two kinds: regular, whose unevenness has a kept order on which it turns, and irregular. The regular is either regular without qualification, when its repetition has one difference only, or regular in cycles, when a cycle has two or more differences and then returns in the same order {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.170}.
