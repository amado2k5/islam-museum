## Nature
The fig has a nature of its own, and its leaves and milk have a euphorbia-like power; when its leaves are not found, the broken and bruised branches of the wild kind are used for their juice; and the thickened fig juice resembles honey in its actions {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.692}

## Choice
The best is the white, then the red, then the black; the very ripe is near to doing no harm, and the dry is praised in its actions, though the blood it produces is not good and for that it breeds lice, unless it is with walnut, which improves its chyme, and after walnut almond; the lightest of all is the white {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.692}

## Temperament
The fresh is slightly hot, very watery and little medicinal; the unripe cleansing and tending to cold, except its milk; and the dry is hot in the first degree and subtle at its end {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.692}

## Actions and properties
The dry, especially the sharp, is strongly cleansing, ripening and dissolving; the fleshy ripens more and has gluing, cutting and subtilising; the wild is sharper and stronger; the fig is more nourishing than other fruits, and there is bloating; the sharp and dry may pass from cleansing to causing ulceration, so that the dry, with its leaf, cooked with the root of black madhariyun, was a treatment for the mange of beasts; its juice and leaf strongly heat and cleanse and have a strong softening that drives putrefactions to the skin and brings sweat; its milk coagulates dissolved blood and dissolves the coagulated; the fresh is quick to sink and penetrate in the stomach and the body; its nourishment, though not as compact as that of meat and grains, is more compact than that of all fruits; the juice of its shoots before they leaf is near its milk in power; the repeatedly strained ash-water of its wood is given to drink for milk curdled within, and that of oak wood is near it; the drink of fig is subtle and bad in humour; its shoots are so subtle that they soften meat cooked with them; and in leaven it has a drawing power from the depth, and dissolves what it draws {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.692-693}

## Cosmetics
The unripe is applied and poulticed on moles, warts of all kinds and bahaq, and so is its leaf; eating it mends the bad colour that follows diseases and soft hot swellings; it ripens boils; the milk of the jummayz is of benefit for swellings hard to dissolve, for scrofula and for the swelling printed as al-ʿaḍala; the juice of its leaf cuts the marks of tattooing; it fattens much through its dissolving, and breeds lice through the badness of its humour, or, it is said, because it is quickly driven outward {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.693}

## Swellings and pustules
Hard swellings are poulticed with it, and with the jummayz cooked with barley meal; the unripe is put on bahaq and it ripens boils, and the fresh brings on scabies when used; its decoction benefits swellings of the throat and of the base of the ears as a gargle with pomegranate rind, and the felon with fanidh; the dry harms swellings of the liver and spleen by its sweetness, and if the swelling is hard it neither harms nor helps unless mixed with subtilising, dissolving things, when it benefits greatly; the jummayz strongly dissolves hard-to-dissolve swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.693}

## Wounds and ulcers
Its decoction with the foam of mustard is applied on itch, its leaf benefits ringworm and is put on hives and on ulcers thick in moisture; the repeatedly strained ash-water of its wood is corrosive and cleanses old putrid ulcers, with pomegranate rind it heals the felon, and with qalqand it is for the bad ulcers of the legs; the milk of the jummayz glues wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.693}

## Joints
The unripe is put with poppy leaf on scales of bone, and the strained ash-water of its wood is poured on a painful sinew {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.693}

## Organs of the head
Its fresh and dry are of benefit for epilepsy, its decoction with the foam of mustard is dropped into an ear with ringing, its milk or the juice of its shoots before they leaf is of benefit when put in a decayed tooth, and as a poultice on swellings beneath the ear; the unripe heals ulcers of the head as a powder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.693}

## Ocular organs
Its milk with honey benefits the moist film, the beginning of the water and thickening of the tunics, and the roughness and mange of the lids are rubbed with its leaf {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Organs of the chest
The fresh and dry are of benefit for roughness of the throat and suit the chest and windpipe, its drink benefits chronic cough and pains of the chest, and it benefits swellings of the lung {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It opens obstructions of the liver and spleen; Galen said its fresh is bad for the stomach and its dry not bad, and eaten with muri it cleanses the superfluities of the stomach; it cuts the thirst that comes from salty phlegm while the dry stirs thirst, and it benefits dropsy, especially with wormwood; its drink is good for the stomach and cuts the appetite for food; the fig sinks quickly and penetrates quickly by its cleansing, and the dry harms the swollen liver and spleen by its cleansing alone; taken fasting it has a wonderful benefit in opening the channels of nourishment, especially with almond and walnut, its nourishment with walnut being more than with almond, and eaten with thickening foods its harm becomes great; the jummayz is very bad for the stomach and of little nourishment but benefits hardness of the spleen as a poultice with ammoniacum or with its milk; all kinds of fig are unsuited to a flow of matter to the stomach {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Excretory organs
It benefits the kidneys and bladder, fresh and dry, and helps to bear the holding of urine, and is not suited to a flow of matter to the intestines; the juice of its leaf opens the mouths of the veins of the anus; the fresh softens and loosens a little, especially with pounded almond; the fig, especially its milk, brings gravel out of the kidney when used; the strained ash-water of its wood is given to one with dysenteric diarrhoea and used as an enema, mixed with oil in both cases; and the drink of fig promotes urine and softens, and by its cleansing passes quickly down from the belly {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Womb, menses, foetus and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book says that it benefits hardness of the womb, that mixed with natron and safflower and taken before food, and when its milk is carried with egg yolk, it cleanses the womb and brings on menses, and is made into poultices for the womb with fenugreek; that the drink of fig brings on milk; and that it benefits swellings of the male organ; a report attributed to the book, without recommendation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}

## Poisons and bites
Its milk, as a liniment, benefits the scorpion sting and likewise the spider's; the unripe, or the fresh leaf, is laid on the bite of a rabid dog with benefit, and with kirsanna it is poulticed on the bite of a weasel; the strained ash-water of its wood is of benefit for the spider's sting, rubbed on and drunk; and the jummayz is of benefit for bites, drunk and applied; a report attributed to the book {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.694}
