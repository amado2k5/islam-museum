## Summary
"Ardha" is one turn of the "ard", the presentation: the Prophet presenting the Qur'an to Jibril and Jibril presenting it to him. In the books of Qur'anic sciences the "final review" is the ground two large matters rest on: what remained of the seven ahruf after the Prophet's death, and what the Companions wrote in the Uthmanic codices.

## What is reported
Al-Suyuti relates from Ibn Ashta, through Ibn Sirin: 'Jibril used to review the Qur'an with the Prophet every year in Ramadan; in the year he was taken he reviewed it with him twice, and they hold that this reading of ours is on the final review.' He also reports through a chain from Ubayda al-Salmani that the reading presented to the Prophet in the year he was taken is the reading people recite today. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 335} He quotes al-Baghawi in Sharh al-Sunna that it is said [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]] witnessed the final review in which what was abrogated and what remained was made clear, wrote it for the Prophet and read it back to him, and taught people by it until he died; for that reason [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] and [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] relied on him for the collection and [[person/uthman-ibn-affan|Uthman]] entrusted the writing of the codices to him. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 335}

## Its bearing on the Uthmanic collection
Khalid al-Sabt lists, among the merits that came together in Uthman's codices: limiting them to what was established by memory and writing in the Prophet's presence; leaving out what had its recitation abrogated and was not settled in the final review; the ordering of suras and verses; writing them on the Quraysh idiom that the final review was on; and stripping them of what is not Qur'an, such as the explanations some Companions wrote in their private codices. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 239} Ibn al-Jazari says the codices were written 'on the wording that settled in the final review from the Messenger of God', as more than one imam of the early scholars stated. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 17}

## What the final review rules out
Al-Suyuti records a disagreement: do the Uthmanic codices contain all seven ahruf? Groups of jurists, readers and theologians said yes, building on the claim that the community could not have neglected to carry any of them; most scholars early and late held that they contain only those of the seven ahruf that their script can bear, gathering the final review. He cites Ibn al-Jazari preferring the second, with a reply to the first from Ibn Jarir al-Tabari: reading in the seven ahruf was never obligatory on the community, only permitted and licensed; when the Companions saw that the community would split if it did not unite on one form, they united on it with a lawful agreement, and they are protected from error. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 334} See [[concept/masahif-al-amsar|the Uthmanic codices]] and [[question/what-is-meant-by-the-seven-ahruf|the seven ahruf]].

## What I do not say
I do not fix here which wording was abrogated in the final review: the sources I read state the principle without listing what was abrogated, and I did not read Ibn Ashta's original reports, so they are carried through al-Suyuti.
