## The upright dial
A wide square-and-oblong marble is taken and a circle drawn on its face, the rim being divided by two lines at right angles. The longer line along the marble is the east-west line and the shorter the north-south line, on which the noon line is drawn, and the horizon directions are written at the ends of the lines. The count of azimuth starts from east and west, each quarter divided into thirty parts. A small cone is fixed at the centre and the shadow is read on it by the hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.211-212}

## The limit of the upright dial
The text states that in this upright dial the longest shadow falls at the head of Cancer and the shortest at the head of Capricorn, and that these two instruments are the most exact and sound of those made for knowing the hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.212}
