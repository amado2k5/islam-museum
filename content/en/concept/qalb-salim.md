## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy presents the sound heart as the heart lit by faith, and describes kinds of heart by their states: an active heart, a heedless heart and a lazy heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:33:00}.

## The heart lit by faith
The speaker wants to explain the heart that faith has lit, and says such a heart is known by the light within it {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:31:30}.

## Kinds of heart
The speaker mentions the heedless heart and the lazy heart when describing states of the heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:32:00}.
He says that a Muslim with a sound heart has an active heart, or a heart that is lazy in worship; the statement is his own division of the heart's states {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:33:00}.

## The heart and hypocrisy
The speaker says that the heart with the light of faith does not know hypocrisy, and that only hardened hearts know it; the wording at this point is unclear (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy aadau-al-islam/ep03 00:31:00}.

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

He divides hearts into sick, sound and dead, and holds that the sickness of the heart is spiritual and is treated with religious knowledge rather than medicine {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:01:00}. He presents a person who acts to be seen and to be called righteous as an example of the sick heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:02:30}.

He holds that the hearts of Ibrahim, Hajar and Ismail were sound, and that people's hearts shake at the first disappointment; he cites a verse about the fulfilment of the dream [[passage/quran-37-105|Qur'an 37:105]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:23:00}. He gives examples from ordinary life: a son fails a subject and the house erupts, or a ball team loses and its coach is blamed; he calls for looking for the causes of failure instead of anger {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:23:30}. He holds that the sound heart succeeds in crises, describing children who object to going on an outing and parents who grow angry, and compares this with Ismail's obedience {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:24:00}.

He puts a question to the audience: are their hearts sick, sound or dead? {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:30:00}.

Answering whether Moses' mother's heart was sound from the start, he says the heart is called a heart because it turns, that she was strengthened by God's command, and that she was told to nurse, then to cast the child, then to be at peace; he affirms that God's promise of his return to her is true [[passage/quran-28-7|Qur'an 28:7]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:32:00}. He explains that her heart became empty of everything but Moses, that God steadied her heart so that it filled with faith, and he gives the image of a child crying for a toy whom his mother embraces [[passage/quran-28-10|Qur'an 28:10]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:33:30}. He says her heart became fully sound after having been good, likening this to climbing steps, and he prays for people who are at peace in their hearts {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:35:00}.

He offers an analysis of Hajar's anxiety: women are emotional by nature, and Hajar was the mother of an infant in a desolate place, so her anxiety is natural; he then asks whether this anxiety is weakness or faith {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:36:00}. He warns that some mothers speak ill of the father to the child after separation, and that the sound heart remembers its father well, illustrating this with Ismail's conduct towards his father {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:39:30}.

He describes the sound heart as fearing God, hoping in Him, trusting Him, sincere in certainty, humble at the Qur'an and quick to the mosque, and the dead heart as marked by diseases such as rancour, envy, self-admiration and showing off {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:46:00}.

He criticises the habit of spending the day in front of screens, songs and films, which he says makes prayer heavy and lets the lesson of knowledge pass quickly {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:47:00}.

He calls on each person to examine his heart: if it is sick, he should treat it; if sound, he should guard it; if dead, he should revive it by reciting the Qur'an and attending sessions of knowledge {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep09 00:47:30}.
