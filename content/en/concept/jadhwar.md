## Nature and selection
Pieces like zarawand, finer than it and of the same strength, better than it; it grows with bish and weakens the bish beside it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}. The editor's footnote gives a Sanskrit origin and a Latin name, which are not used here.

## Actions
Ibn Masarjawayh is cited as saying it acts like daronj but is weaker. The author disputes this: if it means that jadhwar is weaker, the claim is wrong; if it means daronj is weaker, that is possible; and no transmitted report from Ibn Masarjawayh supports the comparison {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.

## Uses by organ
Poisons: it is a part of the theriac against all poisons, including those of the viper and bish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.
Substitutes: zarnabad is the book's substitute in the theriac {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.429}.
