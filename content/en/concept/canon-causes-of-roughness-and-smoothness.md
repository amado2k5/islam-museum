## Summary
In chapters 8 and 9 Ibn Sina gives the causes of roughness as something that scours too strongly by cutting or by dissolving, something astringent by its dryness, something cold by its condensing, or earthy particles settling on the organ; and the causes of smoothness as something that glues by viscosity or a gently dissolving agent {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.145}.

## Roughness
Roughness arises either from a cause that cleanses very strongly by cutting, as vinegar and sour superfluities, or by dissolving, as sea foam and sharp superfluities; or from an astringent that roughens by its dryness, as the gall-like things; or from a cold that roughens by condensing; or from earthy particles settling on the organ, such as dust {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.145}.

## Smoothness
The cause of smoothness is either something that glues by its viscosity, or something gently dissolving that thins the matter so that it flows, or removes the condensation from the surface of the organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.145}.
