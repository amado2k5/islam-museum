## Summary
Al-Sallabi attributes to Ali ibn Abi Talib critical views on poetry as well as a number of verses and short poems, while presenting the scholarly debate over the authenticity of many of these attributions {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 251}.

## His Critical Views
The book cites Ali's saying, "Poetry is the measure of speech" {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 251}, explaining it to mean that poetry has artistic qualities by which sound speech is distinguished from flawed speech among specialists in the craft, even if its themes run counter to the values of other people {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 251}.

## Attributed Poems
The book mentions short poems attributed to him on relief after hardship, patience, people's attachment to worldly life, friendship, and humility and contentment {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 252}, noting that poetry was not his primary aim, since his political career and its events left him little opportunity to compose and transmit verse {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 251}.

## Debate Over Attribution
- The book states that Ibn Hisham was the first to cast doubt on the attribution of certain poems to Ali, as he transmitted a rajaz poem said to have been recited by Ali during the building of the Prophet's Mosque, and then remarked that he did not know whether Ali or someone else composed it {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 251}.
- The book quotes Nayif Ma'ruf that the diwan attributed to Ali is a mixture of work by various poets, compiled by some of his admirers in the belief that this would elevate his stature {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 251}.
- Yaqut quotes al-Mazini as saying that only two verses of poetry are authentically established from Ali, to which al-Sallabi responds that transmitters have verified a number of short poems whose attribution they deemed sound {cite:sallabi-ali-vol1 p. 252}.

## Notes for Researchers
- The book quotes the views of Ibn Hisham, Nayif Ma'ruf, and Yaqut from its sources without re-examining them; researchers should consult Sirat Ibn Hisham and the primary sources before relying on these attributions.
