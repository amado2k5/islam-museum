## Definition
Anam is lexically the plural of ni'm, meaning camels, cattle and sheep, and most often camels. For the jurists it is camels, cattle and sheep, so called for the abundance of God's favour in them: growth, birth, milk, wool, hair and general benefit. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3508}

## Rulings
- Zakat: due on livestock once it reaches the nisab, by agreement of the jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3508}
- Hadi, sacrifice and aqiqa: only livestock may be offered; the best hadi is a camel, then a cow, then a sheep. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3508}
- Slaughter and eating: livestock may be slaughtered and eaten in the sanctuary, outside it, and in ihram, unlike wild game. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3509}
- Method: the best slaughter is nahr for camels and dhabh for cattle and sheep. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3509}
- Related zakat matters include branding the camels of charity and the option to return a tasriya animal, held by some jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3509}
