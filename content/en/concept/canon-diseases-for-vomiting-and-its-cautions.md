## Summary
Ibn Sina says vomiting is most fittingly used in hard, long-lasting diseases such as dropsy, melancholia, epilepsy, leprosy, gout and sciatica, that it may bring diseases such as deafness, that venesection should not follow it at once but wait three days, and that a pregnant woman should not be made to vomit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## Where it is used
The best use of vomiting is in the chronic, hard diseases such as dropsy, melancholia, epilepsy, leprosy, gout and sciatica {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## Cautions
With its benefits vomiting may bring diseases such as deafness. Venesection should not be joined to it but put off three days, especially when there is humour in the mouth of the stomach. Vomiting is often hard because the humour is thin, and then it should be thickened by taking pomegranate-seed groats. Vomiting is good for the body and bad for the sight {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.

## The pregnant woman
A pregnant woman should not be made to vomit, for the superfluities of her menses are not driven out by that vomiting and the effort throws her into disturbance; she should be calmed, whereas others who happen to vomit should be helped {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.287}.
