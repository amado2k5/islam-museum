## Summary
Itmam in the lexicon means completion. Jurists gave it no technical definition beyond the lexical one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1504}

## Its specific use
Itmam has one specific use, which concerns number rather than manner: completing a prayer instead of shortening it on a journey. Shortening and completing are both perfection; what is considered in them is the number of units. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1504}

## Completion and perfection
Al-Raghib distinguished tamam, reaching a limit that needs nothing outside it, from kamal, the achievement of a thing's purpose; on this view tamam entails kamal. Dictionaries and commentaries show no clear difference between the two, and no difference emerges in the technical sense. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1504}

## Ruling in general
Completing an obligatory act once begun is obligatory by the agreement of the jurists. Completing a voluntary act once begun is disputed: the Hanafis and Malikis require it, while the Shafi'is and Hanbalis do not, with detail given under each act. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1505} Where completion means performing the essential pillars, the effect of any verbal or physical act depends on performing them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1505}
