## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the fifteenth chapter on other states that follow exercise, moistening, expansion and excessive condensation, begins with the expansion that comes from slight rubbing and from the bath, treats it with slight dry rubbing leaning toward firmness and an astringent oil, and then lists the causes of condensation {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.246}.

## Expansion
One of these states is an expansion that comes upon the body, often from slight rubbing and from the bath. It is treated with slight dry rubbing that leans toward firmness, together with an astringent oil {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.246}.

## Causes of condensation
Another is a condensation that comes from cold or something astringent, or from the abundance, thickness or stickiness of superfluities, which leads to their being held in the pores of the skin; or the condensation is due to exercise that drew it from the depth without earlier causes; or the cause is staying in a dusty place, or strong hard rubbing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.246}.
