## Summary
Hawqala lexically is walking briskly; in common use it is the saying 'there is no might or power except in God'. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7415} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7416} Its meaning: no movement or ability except by God's will, or no turning from sin except by His protection and no power for obedience except by His help. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7416} A listener to the call to prayer should say the hawqala at the two calls 'come to prayer' and 'come to success', according to the Hanafis, Shafiis and Hanbalis; the Malikis have another view. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7416} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7417} It is recommended in other cases too: in danger, in sickness, when fearing the evil eye, at omens, on waking at night, and after prayer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7418} The entry closes at the next headword, hawl (حول), on p.7419. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7418}
