## Nature
Qaranitus is said of the hot swelling in the thin or the thick membrane of the brain, apart from its substance, though its substance too may be swollen {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76} He answers some physicians who said the brain does not swell, because what is soft like the brain or hard like bones does not stretch: the soft viscous thing stretches and bones also swell, and whatever is nourished stretches and grows by its food, so it may stretch and grow by excess, and that is swelling; he notes that Galen admitted this {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76}

## The name
Qaranitus and sarsam are a name particular to the swelling of the membrane of the brain when it is hot, though in some places the name is also applied to swelling of the brain's substance; it is carried over from the name of the sign that goes with it, which is raving and confusion of mind with heat, much as the name of a sign, forgetfulness, was carried to the disease that brings it, the cold sarsam {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76} Some people, not knowing the languages, think birsam is the name of this swelling and that sarsam is lighter than it, which is nothing; birsam is Persian, with al-bar the chest and al-sam the swelling, and sarsam is also Persian, with al-sar the head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76} The common use of sarsam takes in the sarsam of fevers, that from burning humours at the mouth of the stomach, and that from swellings around the head outside or in the outer membrane, and writers describe it differently because the name is shared; the true sarsam in the usage of the craft is the one he has described {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76}

## Places and starting matter
This swelling has different places according to the different parts of the brain; two parts may share in it or it may cover all the places, and its main bulk most often settles toward the front cavity and the middle one {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76} Its beginning is blood, or pure yellow bile, or pure red bile, or burnt bile tending to black, which is very bad; it seems for the most part to arise only from bilious blood rather than pure blood, or from bile {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76}

## Womb (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
Among the kinds of sarsam by sharing, Ibn Sina names the sarsam that comes with swelling of the bladder, the womb and the stomach, beside that which comes by sharing of the diaphragm, its swellings and the other muscles of the chest {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.76}
