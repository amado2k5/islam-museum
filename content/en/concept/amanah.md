## Summary
Sha'rawi comments on the verse that says that among the People of the Book there is one who, if entrusted with a great treasure, will return it, and one who, if entrusted with a single coin, will not return it unless pressed ([[passage/quran-3-75|Al Imran 75]]). He sets out trust in two moments, taking it on and rendering it back, and reads the prepositions used with the verb {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1542}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1545}.

## Taking on and rendering back
Sha'rawi says trust has two moments: taking it, when a person intends to return it and so is sure of himself, and rendering it back, when conditions may change or the self may be moved, so the believer cannot be sure of himself without caution. He offers the trust of obligation, offered to the heavens, the earth and the mountains, as an example {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1545}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1546}.

## The prepositions
Sha'rawi distinguishes the verb with the particle ba', which binds the trust to the thing entrusted so that the two are not separated, from the verb with the particle 'ala', which places the trust above the thing whatever its value {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1546}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1548}.

## Two standards
Sha'rawi reports that some of the People of the Book said there was no way against the unlettered, meaning that they owed nothing in trust to people outside their own community. He holds that this was a law of their own making and not of the scripture, and that God affirmed that trust does not separate people. He reads the answer that follows as affirming that whoever keeps his covenant and is conscious of God, God loves ([[passage/quran-3-76|Al Imran 76]]) {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1548}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.1549}.
