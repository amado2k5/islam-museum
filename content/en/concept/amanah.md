## Summary
On the debt verse, Sha'rawi sets trust between taking on an obligation and discharging it. He holds that faith preserves trust, but cannot guarantee the circumstances of repayment, so documentation helps {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1225} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1226}.

## Pledge and trust
He says a pledge taken during travel is a safeguard like writing and witnessing at home, and that trust does not abolish generosity and giving {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1225}.

## The trust offered
He reads the trust offered to the heavens, the earth and the mountains as the power of choice. They refused to carry it because they are subjected, and the human bore it with his capacity to choose, and is wrongdoing and ignorant if he fails in it {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1226} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1227}.

## From the series Al Fawzu Al Moubeen

The Sheikh says that the Prophet loved the fulfilment of trusts, and that the first of them is keeping secrets; he illustrates this with money left with a friend and returned on time {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:22:00}. He lists the forms of trust in the eye, the ear, the sight and the tongue, and asks whether a Muslim has kept what he was charged with: prayer, zakat, gentleness towards Muslims, kindness to the young and respect for scholars {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:25:00}. He extends trust to action, word, gatherings and lowering the gaze, and mentions a hadith describing the people of Paradise as chaste and gentle, without naming its narrator {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:26:30}. He holds that precision with public money is a form of fulfilling trust, and that word, glance, gathering, honour, homes, religion and the Qur'an are all trusts {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:29:30}.

A caller asked whether trust has become spoil. The Sheikh answered that the authentic collections list among the signs of the Hour that trust becomes spoil and zakat a fine, and he mentioned a number of other signs that the recording does not make clear (needs_source: number of signs) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:42:00}. He holds that the trust of work and office is spoiled when an employee sees his post as a means of enrichment, and that salvation requires treating trust as a great responsibility {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:42:30}.

He lists further forms of trust: word, judgement, office, money, worship, people's secrets, neighbourhood and the manners of the road. He holds that losing them makes a Muslim hostile to non-Muslims because of their religion {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:43:00}. He says that people of the dhimma have rights and duties, and that treating trust as spoil pushes away from faith, while fulfilling it, by keeping secrets and otherwise, is beloved to God (needs_source: figures garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:44:30}. He concludes that trust is a burden and a charge, not spoil {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep02 00:47:30}.

He says that money and self are a trust held by a person; he asks who wastes them on the forbidden, such as drugs and sins, and calls for spending God's bounty in obedience {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep10 00:10:30}. He transmits a saying attributed to Abu Dhar, that people were like a rose with no thorn and then became thorns with no rose, and criticises the disorder and decline of manners he sees in markets and malls (needs_source: saying-attributed-unsourced) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep07 00:47:00}.
