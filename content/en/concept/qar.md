## Nature
The editor's note says that qar' is yaqtin, and refers to the book Madha nakul? of Muhammad Amin al-Dannawi {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Temperament
Cold and moist in the second degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Actions and properties
The boiled gourd gives little nourishment and descends quickly; if it does not spoil before digestion no bad humour arises from it; it spoils in the stomach by mixing with a bad humour or by staying long, like all fruits; the humour it yields is bland; mixed with quince it is praised for the bilious; and its property is that the nourishment arising from it matches what accompanies it, so with mustard an acrid humour arises, with salt a salty one, with an astringent an astringent one; on the whole it is harmful to the black-bilious and the phlegmatic and good for the bilious; the preserved form has no cooling or heating effect, but is perhaps used for pleasure {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Organs of the head
Its juice calms hot earache, especially with rose oil; it benefits swellings of the brain and sirsam, and is beneficial for throat pain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Respiratory organs
Its sawiq benefits cough and chest pain arising from heat {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
Its decoction benefits the hot superfluities in the stomach and makes it slippery, and it quenches thirst; the raw gourd is very harmful to the stomach, even to that of boys and youths, with no remedy for its harm in the stomach but vomiting, and its harm to the colon is great {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Excretory organs
Its water cooked with honey and with natron added softens the belly, but it is very harmful to the intestines and especially the colon {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}

## Fevers
It benefits acute fevers {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.656}
