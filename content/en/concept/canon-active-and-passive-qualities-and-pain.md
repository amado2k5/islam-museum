## Summary
Ibn Sina says that not every bad temperament is of the unequal, painful kind: hot and cold are active qualities, dry and wet are passive ones, dry hurts only by accident because separation of continuity may follow it, and wet does not hurt {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.148}.

## Active and passive
Having said that one of the two genera of pain is unequal bad temperament, Ibn Sina qualifies it: not every bad temperament is unequal in the painful sense. His reason is that hot and cold are active qualities, while dry and wet are passive ones, whose existence does not lie in one body acting on another with them but in one body being acted on by another {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.148}.

## Dryness by accident
Dryness hurts only by accident, because a cause of the other genus may follow it, the separation of continuity: dryness, through intense contraction, may sometimes be the cause of such a separation and of nothing else. The printed sentence at this point adds that wetness does not hurt at all {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.148}.
