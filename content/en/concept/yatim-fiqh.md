Orphan (yatim). Lexically a single or unique thing, and the state of losing one's father; Ibn al-Sikkit says the orphan among people is from the father's side and among beasts from the mother's. Technically it is one whose father has died while still below puberty, on the hadith "there is no orphanhood after a dream of puberty". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17968}

Related terms: the child of fornication, the child of li'an and the foundling are each fatherless in a sense, but the orphan had a father, while the child of fornication had none in law, and the foundling may yet be found a father. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17968} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

Kindness to orphans: they must be cared for and treated with kindness, on the Prophet's saying "I and the one who sponsors an orphan are like these two", and God forbids humiliating or wronging orphans, on the verse in surah al-Duha (verse 9). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

The guardian may teach the orphan and send him to school, since it is part of his good; he may apprentice him to a trade if that is good for him; the hadith of Jabir: "what you would beat your son for, beat him for, without shielding your property with his property". The guardian must feed the orphan lawful food only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

The guardian's dealings with the orphan's property are limited by good judgement and interest; their rules are under the entry on bequest (i'sa). Trading with the orphan's property has three cases: the guardian trades for himself, for the orphan, or gives it to a partner on profit-sharing; the opinions are under i'sa, wisaya and wilaya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

Maintenance: if the orphan has property, the guardian maintains him fairly, without extravagance or stinginess; if not, the duty falls on his relatives; if he has neither property nor relatives, the treasury maintains him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17970}

Recovering expenses from a rich orphan: the Hanafis and Shafi'is treat the guardian as a volunteer, unless he calls the outlay a loan and gives notice of his claim. Zakariyya al-Ansari says a mother who spends on her rich child from her own property may claim back if she witnesses it when unable to reach a judge, otherwise there are two views. The Malikis allow the guardian to recover what he spent in fairness, if he said he spent in order to recover it, whether witnessed or not. The Hanbalis accept the guardian's word on fair spending unless it is shown false or contrary to custom. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17970}

The guardian's fee: all agree that if a fee is fixed for the guardian's work he may take it, rich or poor; the jurists differ on whether he may take one that was not fixed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17971}

Hiring out the orphan or his property: the guardian may hire the orphan out to another, hire him for himself, hire himself to the orphan, or let the orphan hire himself out; he may also let out the orphan's property to another or to himself. See guardianship and hire. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17971}

Pledging the orphan's property: it may be pledged for a debt owed by the child or for a debt owed by the guardian; the jurists' views are under guardianship. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17971}

Gifts of the orphan's property: a gift without return is void by agreement; a gift for return is disputed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17971} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972}

Zakat on the orphan's property: the jurists differ on whether it is due; see zakat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972}

Marrying the orphan: the jurists differ on whether the guardian may marry him or her off, and on whether an orphan may choose to annul a marriage on coming of age; see marriage and puberty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972}

Share of orphans in the spoils: the majority hold that orphans have a share of the fifth of the spoils, on the verse of surah al-Anfal (verse 41). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972}

Share of orphans in the fay: the majority hold the fay is not divided into fifths but belongs to all Muslims for their interests; a group holds that it is divided and its fifth spent as the fifth of spoils is, including on orphans. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972}

Release of an orphan from restriction: his property is handed to him when he comes of age and is found sound in judgement, on the verse of surah al-Nisa (verse 6). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972}

A bequest to orphans: the Hanafis, Malikis and Shafi'is hold that if a bequest is made to "the orphans of such a family", it is valid when they can be counted, since they are then identified; it is valid as with a bequest to the orphans of this street or this house. If they cannot be counted, the bequest is valid and is spent on the poor among them for the Hanafis and Shafi'is, since spending it on the rich would void it for unknown beneficiaries. The Hanafis and Hanbalis and one Shafi'i view treat rich and poor alike, since the word orphan applies to any child whose father has died. The Hanbalis hold the bequest valid for rich and poor alike, since a bequest to the rich is also an act of piety. The Shafi'is and Hanbalis say that a bequest to orphans does not include the child of fornication or of li'an, nor, for the Shafi'is, the foundling. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17972} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17973}
