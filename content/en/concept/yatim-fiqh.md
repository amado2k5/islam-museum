Orphan (yatim). Lexically a single or unique thing, and the state of losing one's father; Ibn al-Sikkit says the orphan among people is from the father's side and among beasts from the mother's. Technically it is one whose father has died while still below puberty, on the hadith "there is no orphanhood after a dream of puberty". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17968}

Related terms: the child of fornication, the child of li'an and the foundling are each fatherless in a sense, but the orphan had a father, while the child of fornication had none in law, and the foundling may yet be found a father. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17968} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

Kindness to orphans: they must be cared for and treated with kindness, on the Prophet's saying "I and the one who sponsors an orphan are like these two", and God forbids humiliating or wronging orphans, on the verse in surah al-Duha (verse 9). {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

The guardian may teach the orphan and send him to school, since it is part of his good; he may apprentice him to a trade if that is good for him; the hadith of Jabir: "what you would beat your son for, beat him for, without shielding your property with his property". The guardian must feed the orphan lawful food only. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

The guardian's dealings with the orphan's property are limited by good judgement and interest; their rules are under the entry on bequest (i'sa). Trading with the orphan's property has three cases: the guardian trades for himself, for the orphan, or gives it to a partner on profit-sharing; the opinions are under i'sa, wisaya and wilaya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17969}

Maintenance: if the orphan has property, the guardian maintains him fairly, without extravagance or stinginess; if not, the duty falls on his relatives; if he has neither property nor relatives, the treasury maintains him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17970}

Recovering expenses from a rich orphan: the Hanafis and Shafi'is treat the guardian as a volunteer, unless he calls the outlay a loan and gives notice of his claim. Zakariyya al-Ansari says a mother who spends on her rich child from her own property may claim back if she witnesses it when unable to reach a judge, otherwise there are two views. The Malikis allow the guardian to recover what he spent in fairness, if he said he spent in order to recover it, whether witnessed or not. The Hanbalis accept the guardian's word on fair spending unless it is shown false or contrary to custom. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17970}
