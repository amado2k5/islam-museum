## Overview
The practice of the people of Cordoba was a proof in the Maghrib because of its weight, so that in rulings they would say: this is something on which the practice of Cordoba has run. Al-Maqqari reports this in *Nafh al-tib*. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.592-593}

## Requiring a school of law of the judge
One example is the ruler's stipulation that the judge must rule according to a specific school of law. Ibn Arafa lists three opinions on its validity:
- It is valid, according to al-Baji, the practice of the people of Cordoba, and the apparent wording of Sahnun's stipulation.
- It is void, according to al-Turtushi, who said of the stipulation of the people of Cordoba: this is a great ignorance.
- The appointment is valid but the stipulation is void, according to al-Mazari.

{cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.592-593}

On this stipulation was built the obligation of judges in al-Andalus to follow a school, and from there it passed to the Maghrib. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.592-593}

## Al-Maqqari's comment
Abu Abd Allah al-Maqqari, chief judge of Fez and grandson of the author, commented that Cordoba and its people have passed away, but its ignorance has not left the people. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.592-593}
