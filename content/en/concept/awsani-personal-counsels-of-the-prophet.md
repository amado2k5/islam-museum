## Summary
The hadith books contain a kind of report in which a Companion says awsani, "he counselled me", and names the one who counselled him, or says to the Prophet awsini, "counsel me", and receives a few words meant for him alone. They are personal counsels, not public sermons: each is for one named person and varies with what [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] knew of him. This page gathers a selection, assigns each to its narrator and book, and notes where the commentators differ. For the final counsels before his death see [[event/prophets-last-counsels-in-final-illness|The Prophet's last counsels in his final illness]]. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 343}

## The wording
Two words appear in the texts the books transmit: awsani, in the mouth of the narrator, as in Abu Dharr's "my friend counselled me", and awsini, in the mouth of the asker, as when a man says to the Prophet "counsel me" and the Prophet answers with a word or a few. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 104}{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 343} In the sources read here no single book gathers these reports under one name; they are a set of passages cited by Ibn Kathir and Ibn Rajab, and what is attributed to the hadith collections in them is attributed according to the editors' references.

## Abu Hurayra: three things
In his entry on [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]], Ibn Kathir quotes from the two Sahihs his words: "My friend counselled me to fast three days of every month, to pray the two rak'as of the forenoon, and to pray witr before I sleep." {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 478} In the same passage he reports from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi that Abu Hurayra shared the night with his wife and daughter in thirds, each one standing for a third. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 4 p. 478}

## Abu Dharr: awe, hearing and obeying
Ibn Rajab reports from Ibrahim al-Hajari from Abu al-Ahwas that [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr]] said: "My friend counselled me to fear God as if I saw Him, for if I do not see Him, He sees me." He says the Prophet gave this counsel to a number of his Companions, naming Ibn Umar in al-Nasa'i's report and Zayd ibn Arqam, and the editor notes that the raised form in Zayd's report is weak. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 104} Ibn Rajab also gives Abu Dharr's words in Muslim: "My friend counselled me to hear and obey, even if it be an Abyssinian slave with cut-off limbs." {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 589} See [[event/farewell-admonition-reported-by-al-irbad|The farewell-like admonition reported by al-Irbad]].

## Ibn Abbas: "Guard God and He will guard you"
[[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] reports that he was behind the Prophet one day when he said: "Boy, I will teach you some words: guard God and He will guard you; guard God and you will find Him before you; when you ask, ask God, and when you seek help, seek help from God", and then that if the whole community gathered to benefit or harm him they could do only what God had written, the pens being lifted and the pages dry. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 432} Ibn Rajab says al-Tirmidhi and Ahmad transmit it through Hanash al-San'ani from Ibn Abbas; the editor's note gives al-Tirmidhi's number as 2516, and Ibn Rajab cites another version with "know God in ease and He will know you in hardship". {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 432}

## "Do not be angry"
Abu Hurayra reports that a man said to the Prophet: "Counsel me", and he said: "Do not be angry", and the man repeated it several times and he said: "Do not be angry"; al-Bukhari transmits it as no. 6116, as the editor's note to Jami' al-Ulum wa-l-Hikam records. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 343} Ibn Rajab notes that al-A'mash is disputed over its chain: some say from Abu Salih from Abu Hurayra, some from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, some from an unnamed Companion, and that for Yahya ibn Ma'in the Abu Sa'id version is the sound one. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 343} The asker is also differently named: Ibn Rajab says it may have been Abu al-Darda, Ahmad gives Jariya ibn Qudama, and in al-Zuhri's report through Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman from a Companion the man says: "I thought about what the Prophet said, and anger gathers all evil"; Malik gives it in the Muwatta as a mursal report. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 344}{cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 345} See [[passage/muwatta-31-2636|Muwatta 2636]], where a man asked for words to live by without being given many and the Prophet said: do not be angry. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.492}

## Mu'adh ibn Jabal: "Fear God wherever you are"
Ibn Kathir reports from Ahmad that [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh]] asked the Prophet to counsel him, and he said: "Fear God wherever you are"; Mu'adh said: "More", and he said: "Follow a bad deed with a good one and it will erase it"; Mu'adh said: "More", and he said: "Treat people with good character"; al-Tirmidhi transmits it and calls it hasan. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 110} Ibn Rajab relates that Shu'ba, when about to travel, used to ask al-Hakam if he had a need, and al-Hakam would say: I counsel you with what the Prophet counselled Mu'adh. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 385} It belongs with his counsels at the farewell to Yemen; see [[event/parting-counsel-to-muadh-ibn-jabal|The Prophet's parting counsel to Mu'adh]].

## Abu Ayyub al-Ansari: a farewell prayer
Ibn Rajab cites the report of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari, in Ahmad, Ibn Majah and al-Tabarani, that a man came to the Prophet and said: "Admonish me, and be brief", and he said: "Pray the prayer of one saying farewell, do not say what you will have to apologise for tomorrow, and put your despair in what is in people's hands." He notes that it comes also from Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Ibn Umar and Anas. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 586} Elsewhere Ibn Rajab gives a similar wording from a man who said "tell me a short hadith", answered: "Pray the prayer of one saying farewell, for if you do not see Him, He sees you"; he attributes it to Anas, and the editor says Ibn Rajab erred in this and that it is Ibn Umar's report in al-Tabarani, with a weak chain. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 104}

## A Companion on civil strife
Ibn Kathir reports from Ahmad, from Abu al-Ash'ath al-San'ani, that a Companion said to him: "My friend Abu al-Qasim counselled me that if I lived to see any of these trials I should go to Uhud, break the edge of my sword on it and sit in my house." Ibn Kathir adds that the report stands in Ahmad's Musnad under Muhammad ibn Maslama, that the Companion's name is left unnamed, and that it is not Muhammad ibn Maslama, who died before the days of Yazid and Ibn al-Zubayr, so it is another Companion. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 599} He quotes Nu'aym ibn Hammad for a similar report from Ahban al-Ghifari, who told Ali that his friend and Ali's cousin had counselled him to break his sword and make a wooden one. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 599}

## A comprehensive counsel to Abu Umama
Ibn Rajab reports from al-Khallal, from Abu Umama, that the Prophet told his Companions after the night prayer to assemble; when he had finished the dawn prayer he asked: "Have you understood?"; they said yes, and he said: worship God and associate nothing with Him, establish prayer, give alms, hear and obey. Abu Umama says they thought he would speak at length, and he had gathered the whole matter for them. The editor notes that its chain is weak. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 588}

## Where the reports differ
- These reports differ in standing: some are in the two Sahihs, some in Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi, and for some the editor weakens the chain, as the notes of Jami' al-Ulum wa-l-Hikam show. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 588}
- The "do not be angry" report differs in its chain and in the name of the asker, as above. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam scan vol. 2 p. 343}
- Other reports of the kind exist that this page does not treat; the selection here is not exhaustive.
