## Summary
The text separates a star that rose by day from one that rose by night: if the part rising with it lies between the Sun's degree and its opposite it rose by day, and if between the opposite and the Sun it rose by night. In both cases the part of the sphere that has turned since sunset up to the time of measurement is found and divided by the times of the night hours, which gives what has passed of the night in seasonal hours, and divided by fifteen it gives equal hours. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.55}.

## The two cases
If it rose by day, the rising times of the degree with which the star rose in the clime are taken from the times against the opposite of the Sun's part and the remainder is taken from what has turned since its rising; if it rose by night, the rising times of the opposite of the Sun's degree are taken from those of the part with which the star rose and the remainder is added to what has turned since its rising. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.55}.
