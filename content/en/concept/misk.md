## Nature and selection
The navel of an animal like a deer, or the animal itself, which has two white hooked tusks like horns. The best, by its origin, is the Tibetan, or as some say the Chinese, then the Jirjiri, then the Indian maritime; by colour and smell the best is the yellow fuqqahi {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry in the second degree, its dryness being greater in the view of some {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Actions and properties
Fine and strengthening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Cosmetic uses
It perfumes when it falls into a cooked dish {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Snuffed with saffron and a little camphor it helps cold headache, and alone as well, for its dissolving power and strength; it strengthens a balanced brain {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Eyes
It strengthens the eye, dries its moistures and clears a thin white {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Chest and breathing
It strengthens the heart and gladdens it, and helps palpitation and desolation of spirit {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.

## Poisons and bites
It is an antidote to poisons, especially aconite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.553}.
