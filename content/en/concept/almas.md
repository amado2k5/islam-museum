## Nature and selection
Almas is a known stone. Some hold that it belongs under the letter mim, but the book treats it here because it is better known {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}.

## Temperament and actions
Some say it is cold and dry; others say it is hot and dry in strength {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. It is a very strong polisher, and according to Dioscorides it burns and rots {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. It is among the deadly poisons {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.373}.

## Uses by organ
Beauty: it polishes the teeth very well {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372}. Head: some say that if it is held in the mouth it breaks the teeth, either by a property of its own or because viper venom gathers in that place. The book rejects this, calling it a risky claim, and notes that viper venom spat out does not act in this way, especially after time has passed {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.372} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.373}. Excretory organs: some say that a grain of it stuck with Roman mastic to the tip of a syringe-like instrument and carried to the bladder breaks up a stone; the book finds this improbable {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.373}.
