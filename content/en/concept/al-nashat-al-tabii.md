## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat divides human activity into natural activity and unnatural activity {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 61}. Natural activity is based on utilizing land, livestock, and the sea, and manufacturing is built upon it, whereas unnatural activity consists of brokerage, fraud, injustice, and the emergence of middlemen.

## Agricultural Activity
The author criticizes the flawed composition of modern food, as foodstuffs arrive from overseas canned, frozen, or wilted, losing their nutritional value {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 63}. He holds that agricultural production is the foundation, and that the generous Earth needs people to work it {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 63}. He presents water, land, and work as the means by which all crops are brought forth, noting that faith and righteous work are the condition for blessing {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 64}.

## Livestock Activity
The author notes that livestock production opens the way for manufacturing activity, such as woolen clothing, and that animals provide shelter, clothing, food, honey, and milk, as well as carrying loads and serving as mounts {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 66}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 70}. He states that the nation that profits from this wealth is the one that exports rather than imports {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 70}.

## Maritime Activity
The author considers working at sea among the noblest of occupations, noting that in ancient times people sought pearls and fish by diving, whereas today oil is extracted from the depths, and that the shores of Arabs and Muslims ought to be bustling with ships and facilities for manufacturing and export {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 71}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 72}.

## Manufacturing
The author views manufacturing as a qualitative leap in the life of society that transforms, develops, and stores the product, resting on the preceding natural activities {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 73}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 74}. He mentions food industries such as canning, salting, and candying, as well as shipbuilding, dam construction, metal industries, and wool and textile manufacturing, which in his view are countless examples {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 75}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 77}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 79}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 80}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 81}. He argues that these industries would have been the first to be undertaken had the nation paid attention to the natural activity that precedes them.

## Unnatural Activity
The author counts among the forms of unnatural activity brokerage, fraud, killing, injustice, and exploitation, attributing them to the emergence of middlemen and to behavioral flaws {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 82}. He states that if the nation follows the natural path in work and production, these forms will disappear {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 81}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 82}.
