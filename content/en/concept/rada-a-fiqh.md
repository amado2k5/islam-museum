## Summary
Rada'a in the lexicon is the opposite of goodness: baseness and defect. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8695} One may not give poor goods in place of good ones due for zakat, and the mean is to be taken. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8695} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8696} Most jurists hold that good and poor goods of the riba kinds are alike, so exchange of equal weight is allowed and excess is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8696} Several schools require the quality or poorness to be stated in a salam contract, and the Malikis, Hanbalis and some Shafi'is require matching quality in a transfer of debt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8696} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8697} The lender need not accept poorer goods in place of good ones in a loan. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8697}
