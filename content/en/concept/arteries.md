## Summary
The fourth group of the anatomy of the simple organs in Ibn Sina is the arteries, the pulsing vessels, in five chapters; they have two coats, with one exception, and spring from the left cavity of the heart {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.83-84}.

## Description
He describes the pulsing vessels, the arteries, as made with two coats, except for one, the inner being the harder since it meets the beating. The strong movement of the spirit's substance is meant to guard that substance, secure it and strengthen its vessel {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.83-84}.

## Origin
The arteries spring from the left of the heart's two cavities, because the right is nearer the liver and so had to be kept busy drawing and using nourishment {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.84}. The chapters that follow treat the venous artery, the ascending artery, the two carotids and the descending artery {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.84-86}.
