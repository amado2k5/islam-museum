## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter thirteen on the signs of the stool, from its amount, consistency, colour, form and time, and refers detail to the particular book. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.198-199}.

## Amount
The stool's amount is judged by whether it is less than, more than or equal to what was eaten; more is from abundant humours, less from their scarcity or from much being held in the caecum, colon and small bowel, a forerunner of colic and a sign of a weak expelling power. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.198}.

## Consistency
Moist stool shows blockages or poor digestion, a weakness of the channels so that moisture is not absorbed, or catarrh from the head or a moistening food. Viscosity in moist stool shows melting, with stench, or many bad viscous humours, or viscous foods taken with strong heat in the temperament. Frothy stool shows boiling from great heat or much wind. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.198}.
