## Overview
In his commentary on *The Present State of the Islamic World*, Shakib Arslan presents the debate among scholars on translating the Qur'an into languages other than Arabic, and on performing prayer in them. He sets out the views as they are transmitted, and inclines to his own view that the Arabic text must be preserved. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

## The views as transmitted
He mentions that the Hanafis and the Hanbalis permitted its translation for teaching and explanation, that al-Shafi'i permitted it in one view, and that Malik forbade it. He mentions that reciting it in a language other than Arabic, writing the codex in another language, or in a script other than the Uthmanic, is forbidden. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

He mentions that the question grew heated in Turkey in 1932, when some people began to pray in Turkish. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

A further source is needed: the attribution of the views to the schools needs checking in the books of jurisprudence, and the report on Turkey needs a second source.
