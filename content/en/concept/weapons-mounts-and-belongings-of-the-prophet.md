## Summary
Authors of the sira have drawn up lists of the weapons, animals and furnishings that were in the hands of the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]]. The lists differ in number and in names, so this page gives each list attributed to its book. Where a report says what survived his death, it is gathered in [[concept/estate-of-the-prophet|What the Prophet left at his death]]; the sword kept in al-Bukhari has its own record: [[object/sayf-al-nabi|the Prophet's sword]].

## Ibn al-Qayyim's list in Zad al-Ma'ad
Ibn al-Qayyim says he had nine swords: Ma'thur, the first sword he owned, inherited from his father; al-'Adb; Dhu l-Fiqar; al-Qal'i; al-Battar; al-Hatf; al-Rasub; al-Mikhdham; and al-Qadib. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} Dhu l-Fiqar was taken as spoil at Badr and hardly ever left him. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} He had seven coats of mail, among them Dhat al-Fudul, which he pawned to a Jew for barley for his family, and Dhat al-Wishah, Dhat al-Hawashi, al-Sa'diyya, Fidda, al-Batra' and al-Khirniq. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} He had six bows, among them al-Zawra', al-Rawha', al-Safra', al-Bayda', al-Katum, which broke at Uhud and was taken by Qatada ibn al-Nu'man, and al-Sadad. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} Then a quiver called al-Kafur, two shields called al-Zaluq and al-Fataq, five lances including al-Muthwi, al-Muthni, al-Naba'a and al-Bayda', and a small spear like a staff, the 'anaza, planted in front of him in the festival prayers as a marker to pray towards. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} He had two iron helmets, al-Mushah and Dhu l-Subugh, a black banner called al-'Uqab and a tent called al-Kinn. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} Among vessels: a cup called al-Rayyan or Mughni, another cup mended with a silver chain, a glass cup, a water-skin called al-Sadira, a great bowl called al-Gharra' with four rings that four men carried, a teak bed given him by As'ad ibn Zurara, and a leather mattress stuffed with palm fibre. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 43} {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44} Ibn al-Qayyim notes that these items were narrated scattered through different reports. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44}

## Horses, mules, donkeys and camels in the same book
Horses: al-Sakb, said to be the first horse he owned and bought from a Bedouin for ten ounces; al-Murtajiz, about which Khuzayma ibn Thabit testified; al-Lahif, al-Lizaz, al-Zarib, Sabha and al-Ward, seven agreed, which Ibn Jama'a put into a line of verse. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44} Mules: Duldul, a grey one given by al-Muqawqis; another called Fidda given by Farwa al-Judhami; one from the lord of Ayla, one from the lord of Dumat al-Jandal, and a report that the Negus gave one. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44} Donkeys: 'Ufayr, given by al-Muqawqis, and another from Farwa al-Judhami. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44} Camels: al-Qaswa', said to be the one he emigrated on, al-'Adba' and al-Jad'a, with a dispute whether the last two are one or two. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44} He also had forty-five milch camels, a hundred sheep and seven milk goats tended by Umm Ayman. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 44}

## 'Ali's list in the report of Ibn Ishaq
Ibn Kathir quotes Ibn Ishaq from 'Ali that the Prophet had a horse called al-Murtajiz, a donkey called 'Ufayr, a mule called Duldul, the sword Dhu l-Fiqar and the armour Dhu l-Fudul. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 398} From al-Bayhaqi he gives the horses kept with the Sa'idis as al-Lizaz, al-Lahif or al-Lukhayf and al-Zarib, the horse he gave Abu Talha to ride as al-Mandub, the she-camels al-Qaswa', al-'Adba' and al-Jad'a, and the mules al-Shahba' and al-Bayda'. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 398}

## The report of Ibn 'Abbas
Ibn Kathir also gives al-Tabarani's report from Ibn 'Abbas, a single list: a sword with silver hilt and pommel he called Dhu l-Fiqar; a bow called al-Saddad; a quiver called al-Jam'; an armour plated with copper, Dhat al-Fudul; a lance; a white shield called al-Mawjaz; a black horse called al-Sakb; a saddle called al-Dajj; a grey mule called Duldul; a she-camel called al-Qaswa'; a donkey called Ya'fur; a carpet called al-Kinn; a water-skin called al-Sadir; and a staff of shawhat wood. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 399} The main names agree with Ibn al-Qayyim's list; the names of the smaller items differ between the two copies I read.

## The mule Duldul
Ibn Kathir holds that the grey mule is also the white one; that it was the gift of al-Muqawqis; and that the Prophet rode it at Hunayn. It is said to have outlived him until it was with 'Ali in his caliphate and then with 'Abd Allah ibn Ja'far, who had barley crushed for it because of its weakness. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 399} Subul al-Huda says there were seven mules, that Duldul was the first and the only one he left, that in one report from Ibn Sa'd it was Farwa al-Judhami who gave it though the well-known giver is al-Muqawqis, that it died at Yanbu', and that Ibn 'Asakir reports 'Ali fought the Khawarij on it. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4780} {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4781}

## The donkey and the she-camel
Of the donkey Ya'fur or 'Ufayr, Ibn Kathir cites reports that he rode it, and rejects the famous story of a donkey named Ziyad ibn Shihab as having no chain of transmission, one the hadith scholars denied. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 399} {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 400} An editor's note in Subul al-Huda, from Sharh al-Mawahib, says that al-Waqidi holds the she-camel the Prophet took from Abu Bakr to be al-Qaswa', that it lived only a little after him and died in Abu Bakr's caliphate, and that Ibn Ishaq calls it al-Jad'a. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 2 p. 337}

## What he left of these
Ibn Kathir comments that the Companions reported that he left no dinar, dirham, male or female slave other than a mule and land he made charity, which implies that he had completed the freeing and charity of everything else. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 399} Al-Bayhaqi, as quoted, says that no report says he died leaving the horses and camels named, only the mule, the weapons and the land. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 398}
