## Summary
Ibn Sina compares boys' drinking of wine to adding fire to fire in weak wood, says the old man bears what he bears, advises the young to take old wine mixed with pomegranate juice or cold water, and says a cold country tolerates drinking while a hot one does not {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.

## Boys, old men and the young
For boys, Ibn Sina says, drinking wine is like adding fire to fire in feeble wood; what the old man can bear he may be given, and the young are to be treated with moderation in it. It is better for the young to drink old wine mixed with pomegranate juice or with cold water, so that harm is kept away and their temper is not scorched {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.

## The country
A cold country can bear drinking, a hot one cannot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.238}.
