## Nature and selection
There is a white and a black kind; the black is stronger and is not used in foods {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}. Its root is close in taste to ishtirghaz, and its temperament is airy. It is slow to digest, though not as ishtirghaz; its decoction or vinegar is preferred to the substance itself {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.358}.

## Temperament and actions
Hot and dry in the third degree {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}. It thins; its root is inflating; rubbed on the body, especially with its milk, it draws humours outward with force {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}.

## Uses by organ
Ornament: it changes the body's odour; if poulticed with oil it cures a bruise under the eye {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}. Swellings: it helps inner cold abscesses, and with ointments it helps scrofula {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}. Joints: mixed with iris oil or henna oil it helps joint pains in particular {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}. Stomach: its root causes belching and binds the bowel; it is slow to digest, warms and strengthens the stomach and sharpens appetite {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}. Excretory organs: boiled with pomegranate peel in vinegar it cures anal piles; it promotes discharge and makes stool and flatus smell foul, and it harms the bladder {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}. Poisons: bitter theriac is taken as a drink against all poisons {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.359}.
