## Overview
The platform in the mosque from which the preacher gives the sermon. It developed from the Prophet's wooden pulpit into decorated artistic pulpits in Islamic architecture. {cite:wikidata}

## The minbar in Yeomans
The minbar is a stepped triangular pulpit from which the Friday sermon is delivered; at first it was used only in congregational mosques where the sermon was given to the whole community at the Friday noon prayer, and the sermon often carried a political as well as a religious message, since it was through it that a ruler's legitimacy was proclaimed. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.20} [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] preached in his [[building/masjid-amr-fustat|mosque]] at Fustat from a stepped wooden minbar given him by a Nubian king, and Umar disapproved because it raised Amr in the mosque against the egalitarian spirit of collective worship. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.21}

Yeomans notes that the minbar's origins are uncertain; Robert Hillenbrand suggests possibilities including the Prophet's raised chair, Coptic ambos (church pulpits) and the portable thrones from which Sassanian generals inspected their troops. Yeomans favours a Coptic origin for the stepped form, pointing to a sixth/seventh-century ambo from the monastery of St Jeremias at Saqqara, now in the Coptic Museum in Cairo. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.21}
