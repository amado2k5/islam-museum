## Summary
Ibn Sina says fatigue arises either from exercise, which is safer, or of itself, which is a prelude to disease, that the kinds combine, and sets down the rule that extra care goes first to what matters most, importance coming from strength, nobility or substance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.

## The two ways it arises
Fatigue arises either from exercise, which is the safer, with a way of treatment of its own, or of itself, when it is a prelude to disease, again with its own way of treatment. The kinds may be compounded one with another according to the compounding of their matters, whether of themselves or through exercise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.

## The rule of what matters most
Once the management of the simple kinds is known, it is carried over to the compound by a rule Ibn Sina states: extra care goes first to what is most pressing, while the lesser is also tended. What matters most does so for three reasons, strength, nobility or substance, and where two or three of these meet in a case it matters more. As his example the inflammatory fatigue is stronger and nobler, but if the substance of the ulcerous is very far from the natural course it rivals it and is put first; if not very far, the inflammatory comes first. The printed text is garbled in this place, so the sense is given as far as it reads {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.243}.
