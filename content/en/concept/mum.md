## Nature and selection
Pure wax is the walls of the bees' cells in which they lay eggs, rear their young and store honey; black wax is the dirt of their hives. The editor's note says wax is the candle-wax {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Temperament
Balanced {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Actions and properties
Softening, it fills ulcers with dirt and moistens incidentally, since it sticks and shuts the pores; it is the base of all cooling and warming ointments. It has a slight ripening and a little dissolving from the much honey in it. Black wax draws strongly from the depth, pulling out splinters and thorns, and has some fineness and slight cleansing with very great softening {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Swellings and pustules
It softens the hardness of swellings {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Wounds and ulcers
It softens scabs and fills ulcers with dirt, and the black draws splinters and thorns {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Joints
It softens the nerves {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Head, ears and teeth
Black wax makes one sneeze by the strength of its smell {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Chest and breathing
It helps roughness of the chest as a paint and a lick, especially beaten with violet oil, and prevents milk from clotting in the breasts of nursing women {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Excretory organs
A small amount is drunk in some broths for ulcers of the intestines; the quantity the book names is not copied here {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.

## Poisons and bites
It is said to draw out poisons, and is laid as a paint on the wounds of poisoned blades without harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.562}.
