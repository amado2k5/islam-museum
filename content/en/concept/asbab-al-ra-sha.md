## On the side of the power
For when the power weakens by the interception of fear, or the reaching of something dreadful and vast — looking from a high place, walking on a wall, addressing an awe-inspiring revered person, or the like of what contracts the psychic powers — or grief, sorrow, or a joy disturbing the order of the powers' motions, tremor occurs; anger may do it too, for it produces disturbance in the spirit's motion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.157}

## On the side of the instrument
What arises from the instrument may be by the nerve's relaxing some relaxation without reaching palsy, so it does not hold firm under motion — as occurs in much drinking, repeated drunkenness, much drinking of cold water or drinking it out of its time; or by obstructions falling in the nerves from a great repletion arising from the known causes of indigestion and leaving off exercise, so the power does not penetrate its full penetration for them; the blocking matter is either displaced from the passages, moving in them, sometimes knocking on the passage, sometimes barring it, or not displaced at all; it may be by the instrument drying a dryness so it does not comply with bending in a flowing compliance {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.157}

## The combined, and the prevalent cause
The combined is that the instrument takes a harm leading to harm of the power, as befalls it from severe cold from without, or an animal's sting, or a humour, or severe heat as occurs in burning and the like, so the power takes its own lesion and the organ takes a lesion of its own and the two harms meet. Hippocrates said: whoever a tremor befalls in the burning fever, delirium of mind — and it is not without reason (a saying transmitted from Hippocrates in the book) {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.157} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.158}

## Coitus on repletion (labelled listing, held for the founder's ruling)
The book counts among the causes weakening the power much coitus on repletion and satiety {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.157}
