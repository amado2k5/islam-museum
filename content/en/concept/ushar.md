## Nature
An Arabian, Yemeni tree, one of the spurges; it is told that there is a kind of ushar in whose shade sitting kills {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Temperament
Hot and dry, its heat reaching the third degree and its dryness the fourth {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Actions and properties
It has a moderate astringency {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Cosmetics
Applied as a coating it benefits scalp scurf and ringworm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Organs of the head
Applied to the head it takes away heat, and applied with honey to thrush in the mouths of children it removes it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Excretory organs
It loosens the bowels and weakens the intestines {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}

## Poisons and bites
There is a kind whose shade harms a man who sits in it and may kill him, so it is to be avoided, and its milky sap is said to be deadly, destroying the lung and the liver. This is a warning that the book gives, not advice {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.620}
