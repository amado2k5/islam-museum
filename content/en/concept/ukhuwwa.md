## Overview
Brotherhood among Muslims, in the thirty-fifth hadith of the Forty: "Be, servants of God, brothers. The Muslim is the brother of the Muslim: he does not wrong him, nor forsake him, nor lie to him, nor belittle him." Then the prohibition of shedding one another's blood, taking one another's property, and violating one another's honour. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

## A brotherhood that protects rights
It is a brotherhood that protects rights, not a mere feeling. It forbids envy, inflating prices, hatred, turning away from one another, and selling over a brother's sale. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.710}

## The hadith of Ibn Umar
In the hadith of Ibn Umar, the thirty-seventh: "Whoever is in his brother's need, God is in his need." And it is enough evil for a Muslim that he belittle his brother. In his commentary Ibn Rajab establishes that brotherhood requires perfection in preferring the brother, loving him, and attending to his condition. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p.711}

Connected to brotherhood are [[concept/hurmat-al-dam|the sanctity of blood]] and [[concept/satr|veiling a Muslim]].

## Brotherhood in Medina (al-Rahiq al-Makhtum, pp. 185–186)
The Prophet made brothers between the Muhajirun and the Ansar on the basis of mutual support, and made it a bond tied by blood and property. The tribal solidarities of the Jahiliyya melted away, and distinctions of lineage, colour, and homeland fell away ([[event/brotherhood-of-muhajirun-and-ansar|the brotherhood between the Muhajirun and the Ansar]]). {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.185} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.186}.

## For Sprouts
Brotherhood among Muslims guards rights, not mere feeling: 'The Muslim is the Muslim's brother — he does not wrong him, forsake him, belie him or despise him', and his life, wealth and honor are inviolable to his brother. Whoever tends his brother's need has God tending his.

## For Explorers
Brotherhood among Muslims, in the thirty-fifth hadith of the Forty: 'Be servants of God, brothers: the Muslim is the Muslim's brother — he does not wrong him, forsake him, belie him or despise him', then the sanctity of life, wealth and honor between them. It is a brotherhood that guards rights, not mere feeling — forbidding envy, false bidding, mutual hatred, turning backs and outbidding a brother; and in Ibn Umar's hadith, the thirty-seventh: 'Whoever is in his brother's need, God is in his need' and 'It is evil enough for a Muslim to despise his brother' — Ibn Rajab establishing that brotherhood's condition is perfection in preferring the brother, loving him and checking on his state. In Medina the Prophet paired the Muhajirun and Ansar in mutual sharing, making it a covenant bound to lives and wealth until the clannishness of the Jahiliyya melted and differences of lineage, color and homeland fell away.

## For Scholars
Brotherhood among Muslims, in the thirty-fifth hadith of the Forty: 'Be servants of God, brothers: the Muslim is the Muslim's brother — he does not wrong him, forsake him, belie him or despise him', then the sanctity of his blood, wealth and honor among them. It is a brotherhood that guards rights, not mere feeling: forbidding envy, false bidding, mutual hatred, turning backs and outbidding a brother. In Ibn Umar's hadith, the thirty-seventh: 'Whoever is in his brother's need, God is in his need' and 'It is evil enough for a Muslim to despise his brother'; Ibn Rajab establishes in his commentary that brotherhood's condition is perfection in preferring the brother, loving him and checking on his state. It connects to the sanctity of blood and concealing the Muslim's faults. In Medina the Prophet paired the Muhajirun and Ansar in mutual sharing, making it a covenant bound to blood and wealth until the clannishness of the Jahiliyya melted and differences of lineage, color and homeland fell away.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh holds that hurt from a brother in God or a relative is harder on the soul, because some people do not grasp the rights of brotherhood, and lists the six rights: greeting him when you meet, congratulating him on good, consoling him in misfortune, visiting him when ill, blessing him when he sneezes and praises God, and following his funeral; he adds rejoicing at meeting one's brother in the hereafter, recalling those who love one another for God's sake among the seven whom God will shade. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:02:30}
He holds that brotherhood in God begins among those who pray the dawn prayer together and on journeys of hajj and umra, and likens two brothers who love each other to two hands that wash each other. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:03:30} He relates that [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]], on his way to Syria, saw two oxen yoked to one plough; one stopped to scratch its hide and the other stopped too, and he wept and said: so are brothers in God; when one stops in obedience the other stops (needs_source: the story is the Sheikh's narration). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:04:30}
Among the rights of brotherhood he counts checking on your brother's condition without waiting to be asked, relating that [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]], when someone knocked, came out with a sword in one hand and a purse in the other; praying for him in his absence; thinking well of him, quoting a righteous man who said that if he saw wine on his brother's beard he would say someone had spilled it on him; overlooking his lapses and accepting his apology; and defending his honour in his absence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:06:30}

## His view of 'brotherhood of the homeland'
He pauses at God's description of Hud, Salih and Shu'ayb as the 'brother' of their peoples, though those peoples did not believe in them, and holds that the Qur'an did not cancel the brotherhood of homeland and nationality; he criticises the distinction drawn in some countries between 'citizen' and others, holding that the earth belongs to God, who gives it to whom He wills; this is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:08:00}
Of the Companions' refined brotherhood he mentions that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] told [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Mu'adh ibn Jabal]] that he had remembered him at night and missed him and wept, and Mu'adh wept too (needs_source: the story is the Sheikh's narration). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep19 00:08:30}
