## Summary
A hiqqa is a camel that has entered its fourth year, and the female is called hiqqa; the name is taken from its fitness to be ridden and loaded and to be covered by the stallion. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7214} Related terms: ibn makhad and bint makhad (second year), ibn labun and bint labun (third year), and jadha'a and jadhaa (fifth year), the last age of camels counted for zakat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7215} The jurists agree that a hiqqa is due for 46 to 60 camels, and two hiqqas for 91 to 120; the mawsu'a grounds this on Anas's narration of the Prophet's charity rulings in Sahih al-Bukhari. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7215} Its rules are treated in the zakat book under camels and in the blood-money articles, where they stiffen or lighten the diya. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7215}
