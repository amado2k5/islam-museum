## Summary
Ibn Sina opens the regimen of travellers, in eight chapters, with a chapter on forestalling symptoms that foretell diseases: each persistent symptom is tied to a disease feared to follow, and the person is told to act before it comes, so that constant palpitation warns of sudden death, nightmare and vertigo of epilepsy and apoplexy, twitching of spasm, and fear or grief without cause of melancholia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.259-260}.

## Heart, head and movement
Whoever has constant palpitation should order his affairs lest he die suddenly; if nightmare and vertigo are frequent, he should evacuate the thick humour lest he fall into epilepsy and apoplexy; if twitching in the body is frequent, he should evacuate phlegm lest he fall into spasm and apoplexy {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.259}.

## Face, eye and mind
If the face twitches much, the brain is purified lest it lead to facial palsy; if face and eye redden greatly, tears flow, light is shunned and there is headache, he should use venesection and purging lest he fall into frenzy; if grief and fear are frequent without cause, he should evacuate the burnt humour lest he fall into melancholia {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.260}.
