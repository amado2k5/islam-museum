## Summary
Hashima is a head wound that breaks the bone. No retaliation is due for it, by agreement, and compensation is due. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16768} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16769}

## Its compensation by school
The Shafi'is and Hanbalis fix it at ten camels on the ruling of Zayd ibn Thabit; the Hanafis at a tenth of the full diya; the Malikis differ according to their definitions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16768} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16769} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16770}

## Hashima with muwadiha
When a hashima accompanies a muwadiha the compensations combine; the victim may take retaliation for the muwadiha, and the Shafi'is and Hanbalis differ on the extra arsh. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16770} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16771}

## A broken bone in the body
The Malikis give discretionary compensation for a broken bone in the body where retaliation is excluded; most jurists do not call a break outside the head or face a hashima. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16771}
