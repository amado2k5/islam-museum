## Summary
Ibn Sina makes the muscles of the eyeball six, and says that only the upper eyelid moves, with one muscle to open it and two to close it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.61-62}.

## The eyeball
Six muscles move the eyeball: four at its four sides, above, below and the two corners, each moving the eye toward its side, and two oblique ones that move it round. Behind the eyeball a muscle supports the hollow nerve, weighing on it, preventing a protruding slackness and holding it firm when the eye fixes its gaze. Anatomists differ on this muscle, whose ligamentous coverings branch: some count one, some two, some three, but in any case it has a single head {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.61}.

## The eyelid
The lower eyelid had no need to move, since the purpose is fulfilled by the upper alone, closing and fixing the gaze; God's care is turned to reducing the instruments where possible. The upper lid is nearer the roots of the nerves. To close it there are two muscles growing from the corners that draw the lid down evenly; were there one, closing would be stronger on the side where the tendon meets first, as in the lid of one with facial paralysis. To open it a single muscle descends straight between the two membranes and joins a cartilage-like substance spread under the roots of the lashes {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.61-62}.
