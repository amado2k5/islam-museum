## Summary
The fifth group of the anatomy of the simple organs in Ibn Sina is the veins, the still vessels, in five chapters; all of them grow from the liver, and the first two to grow from it are the gate vein and the hollow vein {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.87}.

## Their origin
He says that all the still vessels grow from the liver, and that the first two to grow from it are: one from the concave side, whose main use is drawing food to the liver, called the gate; and one from the convex side, whose use is carrying food from the liver to the organs, called the hollow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.87}.

## The chapters
He goes on to treat the gate vein, then the hollow vein and what rises from it, then the veins of the arms, then the descending hollow vein {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.87-92}.
