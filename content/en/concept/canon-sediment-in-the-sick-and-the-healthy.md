## Summary
Ibn Sina says good sediment is looked for in illness, not in health, and that it is scarce in the lean and plentiful in the fat, so a thin patient is not expected to show what a stout one does. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.193}.

## In the sick and the healthy
The sediment is sought in illness because the sick person undoubtedly holds back bad matter in his vessels, and if it is not matured this shows corruption. The healthy person need not have any humour in his vessels breaking down; rather there are leftovers beyond what his food needed, from which some sediment settles, matured or not. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.193}.

## The fat and the lean
In health the settling residue is scant in lean people, especially those who exercise or do tiring crafts, and plentiful in the urine of well-fed stout people. Lean patients are not expected to show what stout ones do: their illnesses often lift with no sediment, or with only a little that floats or hangs, and it is not true that every urine settles except the fully matured, so one must wait a little. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.193}.
