The child of li'an (walad al-li'an) is the child whose lineage the husband denied after mutual cursing (li'an) with his wife. Li'an is four sworn testimonies from each spouse, the husband's bound to a curse and the wife's to wrath, standing in place of the slander hadd for him and the fornication hadd for her. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17939}

The child of fornication and the child of li'an both have lineage from the father cut; the difference is that the latter's lineage was once established and then cut, while the former's never was. He differs from the foundling in that his mother is known. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17939} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17940}

Lineage: once the li'an is completed with its legal conditions and the husband denies the child, the judge cuts the child's lineage from him and attaches it to the mother. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17940}

Restoration of lineage: if the husband, after li'an, acknowledges the child, the acknowledgement is valid and lineage is established for good, since a retraction of an acknowledgement of lineage is void. If he then acknowledges one of twins after their lineage was cut, both attach to him, as they are one pregnancy. An acknowledgement of a denied child by anyone other than the husband is not valid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17940}

Retraction of the husband's own denial: if after li'an the husband, before the judge, retracts his accusation, the judge applies the slander hadd to him and restores the child's lineage to him; a later retraction of that acknowledgement is not heeded. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17940}

Rights that apply or not: all agree that once the child's lineage is cut by li'an, inheritance and maintenance between them are barred. Al-Haskafi, citing the Hanafis, says that for all other purposes lineage remains because the marriage bed stands, except for inheritance and maintenance. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17940} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17941}

Ibn Abidin holds that lineage remains between the child and the husband for testimony, zakat, retaliation, marriage, and the rule against attachment to another, so that neither may testify for the other, the husband's zakat may not go to the child, retaliation is not owed on the father for killing him, and no other person may claim the child. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17941}

Al-Ramli of the Shafi'is holds that a li'an daughter gains no lineage rights except the prohibition of marriage to her, and the sounder view is that nothing else follows, such as the acceptance of testimony, retaliation, or the hadd. Al-Balqini holds that the status of mahram does not follow; the sounder view, as a precaution, is that looking at her and being alone with her are forbidden, and touching her does not break wudu' out of doubt. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.17941}
