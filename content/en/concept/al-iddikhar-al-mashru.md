## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat holds that saving has no place in the Islamic economy if understood as something unacceptable, yet moderation in spending leaves a small portion behind, and this portion is what is called saving {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 125}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 127}. He cites the verse that puts spending, distribution, and consumption in their proper place, arguing that moderation (al-qawam) implies leaving something saved {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 128}.

## Reasons
The author mentions several reasons for legitimate saving: after satisfying daily needs, future needs of the individual, family, and society must be secured; price fluctuations, availability of goods, and consumer behavior make saving necessary; emergencies such as illness, death, and disability require savings; and any enterprise needs reserves for emergencies {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 128}, {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 129}.

## Legitimate and Illegitimate Saving
The author stipulates that saving must come from a lawful source, such as money left over after satisfying needs, that it must enter investment to contribute to production—because if it remained idle it would become hoarding—and that it must take the form of productive working capital not used for monopoly or grave sins {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 129}. Illegitimate saving is that which comes from excessive price increases, exploitation, embezzlement, or usury (riba) {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 129}. If savings are not used in productive investment, they may be spent on building up the family, its education, and its medical care {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 130}.
