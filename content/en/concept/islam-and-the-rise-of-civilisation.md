## Overview
In the two chapters "Islamic civilisation" and "Why Islam rose by its own nature", Shakib Arslan discusses the claim of some European writers and missionaries that Islam was the cause of civilisational backwardness. This is his argument as he sees it. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

## His arguments
He holds that the ancient Eastern civilisations had declined before Islam. He holds that the Arab-Islamic civilisation was built by borrowing from the ancient civilisations and adding to them. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

He mentions that the schools in Basra, Kufa, Baghdad, Cordoba, and Cairo were open to students of different religions. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

He concedes that in later ages Islam took another course, and he attributes that to political and social causes rather than to religion. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

## Note
A note for verification in the data: the source is a single one, and the argument belongs to the defensive literature of the 1920s. It contains quotations from European writers whose originals need checking. The reports about the schools need a reference in the history of Islamic education. It needs a further source.

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the first episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] sets out the differing definitions of civilization: in the dictionaries the Arabic word hadara means settled town life as against the desert, and the English word civilization comes, as he puts it, from civis, the town-dweller; one school makes a person's morals and values the measure of civilization, among them, he says, Malik Bennabi and Sayyid Qutb and, among Westerners, Alexis Carrel and Gustave Le Bon in his book on the civilization of the Arabs; another makes it the fruit of human effort, among them Husayn Mu'nis and Will Durant in The Story of Civilization; and a purely material school is represented for him by Nietzsche, who, he says, held morality to be an invention of the weak. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:16:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:17:30} He then gives his own definition: civilization is a relationship with three dimensions, a person's relationship with the environment he lives in, with the people who share this planet with him, and with his Lord and Creator; whoever reaches the summit in all three is civilized to the highest degree, whoever falls short in all three is the most backward of people, and whoever combines both is civilized in one respect and backward in another. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:20:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:21:00} He makes it a condition of harnessing the environment by invention and building that it does no harm to it, nor to the generations who will come fifty, a hundred or a thousand years later, and mentions global warming and the hole in the ozone layer. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:22:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep01 00:23:00} In the second episode he applies this yardstick and says it was fulfilled completely only by the Muslim community; he separates the method of Islam from the state of Muslims today, who in his view are in a scientific, moral and economic slump, and he makes Islamic civilization the standard by which civilizations are measured, as hospitals are measured against quality standards. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:14:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep02 00:23:00} In the sixth episode he cites Gustave Le Bon's book on the civilization of the Arabs to the effect that Europe owes its civilization, material, moral and scientific, to the Muslims. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep06 00:03:00} These are all his views and readings, which the museum presents as his.
