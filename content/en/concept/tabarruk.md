## Overview

Tabarruk is the seeking of blessing through sanctioned means. The Kuwaiti Fiqh Encyclopedia treats its forms: through the basmala and hamdala, the water of Zamzam, and certain times and places {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}.

## In Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Wahhab's Kitab al-Tawhid
In [[work/kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab|Kitab al-Tawhid]], [[person/muhammad-ibn-abd-al-wahhab|Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Wahhab]] has a chapter titled "whoever seeks blessing from a tree, a stone or the like". It cites [[passage/quran-53-19|al-Najm 19–20]] and Abu Waqid al-Laythi's report. The Companions set out with the Prophet for Hunayn while still new to Islam; the polytheists had a lote tree, called Dhat Anwat, at which they stayed and on which they hung their weapons. When they asked for one like it, the Prophet likened their request to the Israelites asking Moses to make them a god. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 32} Among the points he draws from it: they meant to draw near to God, thinking He loved it; shirk is greater and lesser, since they did not leave Islam by this; the means to it are to be blocked; and imitating the people of the Jahiliyya is forbidden. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-abd-al-wahhab-imam-university p. 33-34}
