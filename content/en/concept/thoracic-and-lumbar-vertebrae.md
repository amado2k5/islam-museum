## Summary
Ibn Sina assigns to the chest twelve vertebrae to which the ribs attach, and to the lower back five lumbar vertebrae that carry the pubic bone and from which the nerves of the leg grow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.51-52}.

## The chest vertebrae
The chest vertebrae are those to which the ribs attach, enclosing the organs of breathing. They are eleven vertebrae with spines and wings and one without wings, twelve in all. Their spines are unequal, since those beside the nobler organs are larger and stronger, and their wings are harder than the others because the ribs join them. The seven upper vertebrae have large spines and thick wings to shield the heart thoroughly, so their articular processes were made short and broad. The tenth has upright, vaulted spines. The twelfth has no wings, since the need for wings that comes from the ribs is smaller there, and the end of the diaphragm attaches to it {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.51}.

## The lumbar vertebrae
The qatan is the lower back. The lumbar vertebrae have spines and broad wings, and their lower articular processes widen to resemble the protecting wings; there are five. With the sacrum the lumbar region is like the base of the whole spine, a prop that carries the pubic bone and the place from which the nerves of the leg grow. They needed extra bone and extra firmness of joints because they have less above them to share the load, so their articular processes were doubled {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.51}.
