## Nature and selection
It is the root of a plant with white and yellow flowers, showing among the first of the blossoms on the slopes of mountains and on hillocks, its leaf lying flat on the ground {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.589}.

The best is the kind white within and hard when broken; the red and the black are bad {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.589}.

## Temperament
Hot and dry to the second degree, with a superfluous moisture; some claimed that the white has a delicate heat; others that if it were hot it would sting ulcers and it has no sting at all; others claimed it very hot {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.589}.

## Actions and properties
It has a purgative power, though it has astringency, as is said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.589}.

## Wounds and ulcers
The white is good for old wounds {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.589}.

## Joints
It helps gout and calms the pain at once as a poultice, but if too much is used as a poultice it hardens the swelling to stone; so also it is the antidote of all joints, especially in times of flux {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Digestion and the organs of nourishment
It is bad for the stomach and weakens it, and the red and black hold purgative drugs in the stomach and bring great harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Excretory organs
It has a purgative power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Poisons and bites
The red and the black of it are poison, which is the book's own warning {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Substitutes
Its substitute in pains of the joints is the leaf of henna and blue muql; the proportions the book gives are left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.

## Womb, menses and desire (labelled book listing, held for the founder's ruling)
It increases desire, especially with ginger, pennyroyal and cumin {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.590}.
