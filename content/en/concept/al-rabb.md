## Summary
"Rabb" in Arabic speech has several senses: the lord who is obeyed among his people, the one who sets a thing right, and the owner of a thing. Al-Tabari reads "Lord of the worlds" as joining all three: God is the sayyid who has no like, the one who sets the affairs of His creation right with the blessings He has poured out, and the owner to whom creation and command belong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 142}

## The senses of rabb among the Arabs
Al-Tabari supports each sense with verse. The lord who is obeyed is called rabb, as in a line of Labid on "the lord of Kinda"; the one who sets a thing right is called rabb, as in verses of al-Farazdaq and of al-Nabigha of Dhubyan; and the owner of a thing is called its rabb. He also uses a line of 'Alqama ibn 'Abda where the word means the one who keeps a matter in order. The editor notes that the attribution of some of these lines is uncertain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 142}

## Lord of the worlds in Ibn Abbas
Al-Tabari gives a report from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak, that the Lord of the worlds is the one to whom all creation belongs: the heavens and all in them, the earths and all in them and between them, what is known and what is not known, and that nothing resembles Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 142} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 143}
