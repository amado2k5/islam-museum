## Summary
Ibn Sina treats autumn as the season of most changeable air, so the best regimen is kept, all drying things are avoided, and one guards against intercourse, much cold water and pouring it on the head, sleeping when full, and seasonal fruits, then evacuates when night and day are equal {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.258}.

## What is avoided
In autumn the best regimen is kept and all drying things are given up; one guards against intercourse, drinking much cold water and pouring it on the head, and sleeping in a cold place where the body shivers; one does not sleep when full, avoids the heat of noon and the chill of early morning, and shields the head from cold at night and at dawn {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.258}.

## Fruit, bath and evacuation
He is wary of seasonal fruits and of eating many of them, bathes only in tepid water, and when night and day are equal evacuates so that residues are not penned up in winter; yet for many bodies the better course in autumn is not to busy themselves with managing and stirring the humours, since quieting them serves them better {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.258}.

## Vomiting, wine and rain
Physicians forbade vomiting in autumn because it brings on fever; of wine, a well-diluted kind is used without excess; and he notes that much rain in autumn is a safeguard against its harm {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.258}.
