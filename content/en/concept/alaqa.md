## Summary
In the lexicon, alaqa is thick coagulated blood, or blood that clots before it dries. It is a stage of the embryo from which the lump of flesh develops, and it follows the drop of semen {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11791}.

## Causing the loss of the clot
The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold that causing the loss of the clot is forbidden. The Hanafis allow the loss of a pregnancy before one hundred and twenty days on conditions they set out; some read this as limited to necessity {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11791} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11792}.

## The ghurra and its effects
The majority do not require a ghurra for a lost clot; the Malikis do. The majority do not count the woman as in post-natal bleeding, while the Malikis do, and a waiting period ends with it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11792}.

## Where to look further
The rules of the ghurra are treated under «ghurra», post-natal bleeding under «nifas», and the waiting period under «idda» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11792}.
