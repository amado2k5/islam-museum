## Summary
In chapter 4 al-Battani states that the obliquity of the zodiac from the equator of the day is known from the Sun's distance at the two solstices in the meridian, reports that Hipparchus is cited by Ptolemy as giving the arc between the solstices as 47 degrees 42 minutes and the obliquity as 23 degrees 51 minutes, and then tells that he observed many times at al-Raqqa and found the Sun's nearest approach to the zenith 12 degrees 26 minutes and its farthest distance 59 degrees 36 minutes, so the arc between the solstices is 47 degrees 10 minutes and the obliquity 23 degrees 35 minutes. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.21-22}.

## Obliquity from the Sun's own measure
He says the obliquity of the zodiac, bounded by the Sun's circuit, from the equator of the day is known by the Sun's distance and the fixing of its path at the two solstices in the meridian circle, the circle of mid-heaven which cuts the poles of the equator, the zenith point and the horizon. So the measure falls on the Sun itself at the two extremes of its altitude, in summer and winter. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion pp.21-22}.

## What he reports of Hipparchus and Ptolemy
Al-Battani says Hipparchus mentioned, and Ptolemy related in his book, that the arc between the winter and summer solstices in the meridian is 47 degrees 42 minutes and the obliquity half of that, 23 degrees 51 minutes. He treats this as a report handed down and calls what he found by observation the better one to work with. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.22}.

## The Raqqa observation and its result
He says that in his lifetime he observed very many times with the long instruments whose making is described in the Almagest, after refining the graduation and setting up the instrument as exactly as he could, and found the Sun's nearest approach to the zenith point at the city of al-Raqqa to be 12 degrees 26 minutes and its farthest distance 59 degrees 36 minutes. So it became clear to him that the arc between the solstices is 47 degrees 10 minutes and the obliquity 23 degrees 35 minutes, the distance between the poles of the two spheres, and he works on it in his book because it is seen with the eye while the first was a report. The name of the instrument is uncertain in the printed text. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.22}.

## The latitude of Raqqa
By this al-Battani knew that the distance of the city of al-Raqqa, where the observation was made, from the equator of the day in the meridian is 36 degrees, which is the altitude of the north pole of the equator there above the horizon and also the distance of the equator from the zenith point to the south. So the latitude of [[place/raqqa|al-Raqqa]] used in the zij is 36 degrees. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.22}.
