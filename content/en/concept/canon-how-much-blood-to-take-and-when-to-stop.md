## Summary
Ibn Sina explains that the stopping of the blood has a set time though it differs, that people differ in how much blood they bear, and gives what is watched, the colour of the blood and the pulse, what warns of faintness, and who is quick to it and who slow {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## Differences in what is borne
Know that for holding the blood and binding the cut there is a set time, though it differs: among people there is one who can bear, even in his fever, the taking of five or six pounds of blood, and one who cannot bear, in health, the taking of a pound {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## Colour and pulse
But in this three states must be watched. The colour of the blood is one; it may thicken much, in that what first comes out of it is thin and white, and when the signs of fullness are there and the case calls for bleeding one must not be deceived by that, and the colour of the blood may thicken in one with swellings. The pulse must never be left; if the colour of the blood changes or the pulse grows small, above all toward weakness, stop {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.

## Warning signs
Likewise, if yawning, stretching, hiccup or nausea comes on, stop. The people quickest to faintness are the hot of temperament, the lean and the porous of body, and the slowest to fall into it are the moderate bodies firm in flesh {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.296}.
