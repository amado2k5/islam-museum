## Summary
In the lexicon, aqr is wounding. The jurists use it of a wound in any part of the body of a wild animal that cannot be caught, and of cutting the legs of an animal {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11764}.

## Its effect on lawfulness of the flesh
A hunted animal wounded in any part of its body is lawful to eat when the conditions of the hunt are met; an animal of domestic kind that runs off and cannot be caught is lawful by wounding, according to the majority {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11765} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11766}.

## Wounding spoils of war
When the Muslims cannot carry away the animals of the enemy, the Shafi'is and Hanbalis forbid wounding them except to eat them; some jurists allow it in case of need {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11767}.

## Liability for a vicious dog
Whoever sets loose a vicious dog that wounds a person is liable according to the Shafi'is and Abu Yusuf; Abu Hanifa does not hold him liable, and the rule varies with whether the owner entered the place, drove the dog and how he kept it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11768} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11769}.

## Where to look further
Slaughter is treated under «zakat al-dhabh», hunting under «sayd», and aggression by an animal under «sayal» {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11765}.
