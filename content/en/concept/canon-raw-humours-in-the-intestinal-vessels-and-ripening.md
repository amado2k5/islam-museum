## Summary
When hardened or raw humours lie in the vessels of the intestine Ibn Sina orders the fatigue treated first and then ripening, with rest, leaving off exercise and holding back venesection before ripening, names pepper, caper, ginger and vinegars for the food, and then thin wine once sediment shows in the urine {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.245-246}.

## Rest and ripening
If hardened or raw humours are in the vessels of the intestine, the fatigue is first treated as it should be, and then the physician turns to what ripens, thins and expels the raw matter. If it is abundant he advises rest and leaving off exercise, for rest digests better, and leaving off venesection, which for the most part removes the pure and leaves the raw; nor should he purge before ripening. Diuresis does no harm, but a heating remedy is not given, lest it spread the raw matter through the body {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.245}.

## Food and wine
His food should contain pepper, caper, ginger, caper vinegar, garlic vinegar and the vinegar of astragalus together with their substance, and the well-known digestive preparations in measure. After ripening, with sediment in the urine and the greater part ripened, wine is used to complete the ripening and promote urine, and it should be the light, thin kind; vomiting is not used {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.245-246}.
