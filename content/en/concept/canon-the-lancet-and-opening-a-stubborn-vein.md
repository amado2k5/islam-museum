## Summary
At the close of the chapter on venesection Ibn Sina gives how the lancet is used and held, how its bite on the skin is tried, how a vein that does not fill is managed, how it is fixed and bound according to the skin and the body, and what shows the need for bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.

## The blunt lancet
Know that the blunt lancet does much harm, for it errs and does not reach, and makes swelling and pain. When you use the lancet do not push it by hand with pressure, but gently by stealth, so that the lancet's tip reaches the filling of the vessels; if you are rough the head of the lancet often breaks invisibly and becomes slippery and wounds the vessel, and if you press on with your bleeding you add to the ill. So the way the lancet takes hold of the skin must be tried before the bleeding and when you wish to strike again {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.

## The stubborn vein
Strive to fill the vein with blood, for then slipping and moving are less. When the vein is stubborn and its filling does not show under the binding, loose it and bind it again and again, rub it, go down in the pressure and up until you rouse it and show it, and try this by gripping with two fingers a place where you know the vessels run, so that with them you hold it, or at times hold it with one and let the blood run with the other until you feel what stands; tighten it at the raising and let it pass at the release {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.

## Holding the lancet
The lancet's tip should have a distance it goes through that is not far, lest it pass beyond to an artery or a nerve, and the filling matters most where the vein is finest. As for taking the lancet, it should be with the thumb and the middle finger, the index finger being left for feeling, and the grip should fall on the middle of the iron, not above that, for then the hold on it would be unsteady {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.

## Binding and fixing
If the vein slips to one side, meet it with binding and holding from the opposite side; if it slips to both sides alike, avoid opening it lengthwise. Know that the binding and pressing must be according to the state of the skin in its hardness and thickness and the amount and fullness of flesh. The fixing must be close, and if the fixing hides the vein, mark it, and take care that your vein does not move from alignment with the mark in the fixing {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.

## A sign of need
Know that one who sweats much because of fullness needs bleeding. It often happened to a feverish man with headache, who was being managed in his case by bleeding, that a natural diarrhoea came on, and he no longer needed the bleeding {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.299}.
