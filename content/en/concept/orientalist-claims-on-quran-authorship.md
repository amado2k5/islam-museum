## The Claim

Zaqzuq states that some Orientalists revived what the polytheists of Mecca said about the Quran, attributing it to the Prophet or to human sources. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 112} He regards this as one of the strongest attempts to shake belief in the Quran's source, because once the Quran is established as revelation, faith in it becomes inescapable. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 112}

## George Sale

The book notes that George Sale asserted in the introduction to his English translation of the Quran—dated by the author to 1736 CE—that the Prophet was the author of the Quran, and that the assistance he received in composing it was no small matter. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 113} It notes that this introduction influenced European writings for a long time. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 113}

## Richard Bell

The book states that Richard Bell argued that the Prophet drew on the Bible, especially in the narrative sections. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 115} Zaqzuq rejects this claim and maintains that the Quran itself refutes it. {cite:al-istishraq-wal-khalfiyya-azhar p. 115}
