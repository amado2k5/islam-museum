## Overview
Zaydan Qa'dat critiques communism as the opposite of capitalism along the same axis: it came to reform the condition of workers, yet confined itself to material reform and gave no consideration to the spiritual side {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 15}.

## Its Foundations as Presented by the Author
The author cites three of its foundations: that its sole preoccupation is workers' rights and wresting them from employers; that it changed the form of authority from the authority of the individual to the authority of the class; and that private property was taken from individuals to belong to the state or the class {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 15}.

## Religion and Matter
The author attributes to Karl Marx the saying "Religion is the opium of the people," and argues that communism denied religion and creed wholesale, so that its attachment was to matter and nothing else {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 15}{cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 16}. He states that it seeks to free human beings from religious restraints only to trap them in material restraints {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 15}.

## Its Place in His Critique
The author groups capitalism and communism together in that both corrupted values, morals, and human rights, the difference being that the former revolved around the individual and the latter around matter {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 16}. See [[concept/al-rasmaliyya|Capitalism in Qa'dat's Critique]].

## Note
This critique is presented as the author's opinion; Marx's text has not been checked against it, and neither the view of Marxists nor the schools of socialist economics are presented here, so it needs to be supplemented.

## From the book of Zaydan Qa'dat: pp. 83–164 of the PDF file (Communism in the Context of the Book)
Qa'dat critiques the communist system because it focused on material rights and the element of materials and land, while forgetting the divine right and private right in the production process, thus yielding the same bad results that came from capitalism {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 99} {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 100}. He argues that this system diminishes the value of the individual, treating him as it treats a machine {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 91}, and gives money a value it does not deserve {cite:qadan-manhaj-iqtisad-quran p. 142}.
