## Summary
Buff ware is a glazed earthenware with a buff body, its decoration painted in black, yellow and green and then covered with a lead glaze; numerically it is one of the largest groups found at [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]]. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 46} According to [[work/nishapur-pottery-of-the-early-islamic-period|Wilkinson]], nothing like it had come to light elsewhere until it was discovered at Nishapur in 1935. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 46}

## Distribution
Three pieces later turned up among the Afrasiyab sherds in the Islamisches Museum, Berlin, probably imported from Nishapur; sherds were found at Gurgan, mostly imported from Nishapur, at Qumis between Semnan and Shahrud, and at [[place/merv|Merv]] in a tenth-century level, where some appears to have been made locally. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 46}

## Origin and style
Wilkinson holds that the ware originated in Khurasan and owed nothing at first to Iraq, to the western Islamic lands or to Chinese centres; a late fifth- or sixth-century vase from Merv with seated figures suggests to him that the animate buff ware of Khurasan was, if not a continuation, at least a rebirth of an existing tradition. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 46} It uses birds, animals and human beings as contemporary lustre ware of Iraq and Egypt did, but its style is its own; the non-representational forms of the later buff ware follow a fashion, from Syria and Egypt to Transoxiana, for a vermicular background behind the main design. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 46} The book divides it into animate and inanimate types. {cite:wilkinson-nishapur-pottery p. 46}
