## Summary
Ibn Sina compares cooling, heating, moistening and drying, holding that cooling and heating last equally long but cooling is the more dangerous because heat is nature's friend, and that heating the cold is easier at the start while cooling the heated is easier at the end, since extreme cold is a death of the innate heat or its companion {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.272}.

## Duration and danger
Cooling and heating last equally long, but cooling is more dangerous because heat is nature's friend. Moistening and drying are equally dangerous but moistening takes longer. Heating a cold state is hard once it is established and very easy at the start; in general heating the cold at the outset is easier than cooling the heated, but at the end cooling the heated, though hard, is easier than heating the cold {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.272}.

## Cooling with drying and moistening, and the old
Cooling may go with drying, with moistening, or with neither; drying fixes cold that has already arisen, and moistening brings on newly arising cold. For moistening there are rest, constant light bathing, the tub and drinking diluted wine. When an old man needs cooling and moistening, what returns him to moderation is not enough; he needs what carries him on to the cold, moist temper that has come upon him, which, though incidental, is for him like the natural {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.272}.
