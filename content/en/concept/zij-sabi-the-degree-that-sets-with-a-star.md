## Summary
To find the degree at which a star sets, one takes the times against the degree opposite to the one that culminates with the star in that clime and adds half the difference of the two days if the star's half arc is the larger or subtracts it if it is the smaller; the result is the rising of the degree opposite to the setting degree, and the opposite of that degree is the one that sets with the star. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.54}.

## A star with no latitude
If the star has no latitude its passage through midheaven does not differ, and it rises and sets with the part of the ecliptic in which it stands, which repeats what was said in the previous chapter about a star without latitude. {cite:zij-sabi-nallino-byblion p.54}.
