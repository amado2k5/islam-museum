## Summary
In the third lesson of his series on rights, Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy takes up a Muslim's rights over a fellow Muslim, after the rights of spouses. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:01:00}

## Six rights
The Sheikh counts six rights of a Muslim over his brother, and names greeting on meeting, visiting the sick, following the funeral, congratulating a brother when good comes to him, and consoling him when misfortune strikes. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:03:00} (needs_source: the full list is not clear in the transcript)

## Greeting and the ties of kinship
The Sheikh speaks of people who stop greeting relatives because of a quarrel, and urges that greetings be spread among people. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:03:30}
He says that keeping the ties of kinship means joining those who have cut you off, not only those who keep in touch with you. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:04:00}

## Standing by a brother
He says that when a brother is in trouble, his brother should stand beside him and visit him, and not wait to be asked. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:05:00}

## Helping a brother
He recounts a saying about helping a brother whether he is wronged or the one who wrongs, and says a questioner asked how to help one who does wrong; the answer is not clear in the transcript (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:06:30}

## Backbiting
The Sheikh warns against backbiting and compares it to fire that consumes wood, saying that all people fall into it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:08:30}
He observes that backbiting has spread through offices, gatherings and neighbourhoods, and that in his time men engage in it more than women. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:09:00}
He defines it as mentioning a brother with what he dislikes: if the thing is in him it is backbiting, and if it is not in him it is slander. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep03 00:09:00}
