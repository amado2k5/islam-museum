## Summary
The Qur'an describes Abraham as a hanif who was a Muslim. Sha'rawi explains the word through its root: in the language, hanf is a bend in the lower legs, and the sense then passed to anything crooked {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1525}.

## Turning from crookedness
Sha'rawi says Abraham was upright in himself, but came to a people in idolatry and open crookedness, and was a hanif by turning away from the crookedness of his time. He holds that prophets come only when belief and law have become corrupt throughout {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1525}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1526}.

## Why not "upright"
Sha'rawi asks why the verse did not simply call Abraham upright. His answer is that "upright" could be taken to mean he followed the ways of his people, who were crooked. The word hanif marks his departure from them. Being muslim, in his reading, points to having handed over one's reins to God, that is, submitting to what the law asks {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1527}.

## Neither Jew nor Christian
Sha'rawi holds that Judaism and Christianity both came after Abraham, so neither can claim him as its own. He presents this as a reply to those among the People of the Book who claimed him {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1524}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-vol-2 p.1525}.
