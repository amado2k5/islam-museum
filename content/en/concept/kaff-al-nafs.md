## Summary
In the lexicon kaff is leaving and restraining. In the usuli sense, restraining the self is ceasing from what is forbidden, and it takes place only after the soul is inclined to the forbidden act {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13406}.

## Leaving and restraint
Leaving is wider than restraint, since it is used for the commanded, the forbidden and rights; restraint is used only of the forbidden {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13406}.

## Restraint as an act addressed by the law
Obligation rests on an act that the person earns. The act demanded by a prohibition is restraint of the self from the forbidden, and there is no obligation before the desire arises; so the prohibition of adultery is taken as conditional on the soul's desire for it {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13406} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13407}. Al-Subki takes two proofs that restraint is an act: the verse "my people have taken this Qur'an as abandoned", and the report of Abu Juhayfa that the best deed is guarding the tongue {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13407}.

## Reward for restraint
Restraint from the forbidden earns reward, since whoever obeys an obligation is obedient, and obedience is good; some require that it be done with the intention of obedience {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13407} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13408}.

## Three elements of a prohibition
Al-Shirbini distinguishes three things in an obligation to avoid: the act required, which is leaving in general and needs no intention of obedience; the act rewarded, which is leaving for the sake of obedience; and the absence of the forbidden act, which is the aim but not itself an obligation {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13408}.
