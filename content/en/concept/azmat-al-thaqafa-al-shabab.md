# The youth cultural crisis

## The episode and the votes
The 'Al-Wasatiyya' episode debated the youth cultural crisis — whether there is a real decline and whether satellites and the internet caused it {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:01:30}. Seventy-one percent voted that the youth suffer a real cultural crisis; {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:06:00} and they split on the tools' effect: 47% saw culture weakened after satellites and the internet, 43% saw it improved {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:35:30}.

## Figures on reading and screens
From the episode's figures: only 4% of girls read books against 96% magazines (a Saudi study); {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:31:30} and the youth spend 3-4 hours before television and another 3-4 before the internet — 6-8 hours daily {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:39:30}.

## The tools are the symptom, not the cause
Panelists held that satellites and the internet are tools that reveal the crisis rather than make it, and the real question is 'which culture do we want?' across the domains of faith, the material, thought and society {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:42:00}. They stressed the entertainment novel is a legitimate gateway to habituating youth to reading {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:31:00}.

## Reform starts with education
Thirty percent voted for reforming the education system and its institutions — teacher, material, the hidden curriculum and the student — against only 7% for the intellectuals and thinkers {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:51:30} {cite:assabile-tareq-al-suwaidan al-wassitiya/ep18 00:59:00}.
