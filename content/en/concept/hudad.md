## Nature and selection
Nature: the book thinks the Indian kind is most likely the sap of a plant it names with a foreign term, and that it is adulterated so that a learner cannot tell, by boiling the sap of a plant called zarshak in water until it sets. The Meccan kind is made by hand. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}
Dioscorides says it comes from a thorny tree with branches three cubits long or more, with a smooth, astringent pepper-like fruit and a yellow bark, with many roots, growing in rough places. The sap is drawn when the leaves are crushed with the tree or steeped for days, then boiled and returned to the fire until it thickens. It is adulterated with the dregs of oil, with the sap of wormwood, or with ox gall. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}
The good kind catches fire, and when put out it foams with a colour like its inside. The Indian is stronger than the Meccan for hair and its strengthening; the Meccan is for swellings. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}

## Temperament
Moderate in heat and cold, dry in the second degree. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}

## Actions
The Indian has a slight dissolving and astringent action that helps every bleeding. Its dissolving is stronger than its binding, it is in the second degree of dissolving, and its binding is less than its drying; it has a fine strength. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it reddens and strengthens the hair, especially the Indian kind, clears freckles, and every kind of hudad helps the scabies-like patch the book calls dahs. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}
Swellings and pustules: it helps soft swellings and nettle-rash. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}
Wounds and ulcers: it helps malignant and cancerous ulcers, and it firms the organs in the joint section. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.473}
Organs of the head: the Indian kind helps the discharge of matter from the ear and from its ulcers, is used as a gargle for thrush and heals it, and is very helpful for gum ulcers and their diseases. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Organs of the eye: it helps ophthalmia, clears the cornea, removes its opacity, and cures the scabies of the eye. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Organs of the chest: the Indian is given to drink for spitting of blood and for the chronic cough. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Organs of digestion: the Indian is drunk and helps black jaundice and the spleen, and its application does the same; it helps diarrhoea. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Excretory organs: it helps fissure of the anus, is drunk and used for chronic diarrhoea and for diarrhoea from weakness of the stomach. Its soft fruit draws off watery phlegm, helps ulcers of the rectum, and helps piles. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Labelled listing, set aside for the founder's ruling: the book says the fruit stops women's bleeding. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Poisons: its fruit helps deadly poisons, and the Indian kind is given to drink for the bite of the mad dog. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
Substitutes: the book gives weights for the substitutes; these are left out as measured quantities. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.474}
