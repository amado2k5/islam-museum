## Summary
Linguistically, a paternal uncle's son is the male child of a paternal brother, by blood or by fostering; without qualification the word means the blood cousin, full, paternal or maternal. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1464}

## General ruling
The full or paternal cousin is an agnate in his own right: he takes the whole estate when alone and no closer agnate exists, and otherwise the remainder after the fixed shares, by agreement. The maternal cousin inherits generally as one with blood-ties through a female, though earlier and later jurists differ on how he inherits. The agnatic cousin may contract the marriage of his cousins when no closer guardian exists, may take retaliation for homicide if he is an heir, and has custody of a male cousin. The Malikis give these rights also to the maternal cousin and, in some of them, put him before the paternal one. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1464} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1465}

## Where it is discussed
The rules are treated under inheritance, marriage, custody, retaliation and guardianship, in the places where the cases arise. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.1465}
