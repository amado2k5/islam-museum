## Overview
The maxim "no harm and no reciprocal harm" is one of the great principles of jurisprudence. It is drawn from the hadith of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, the thirty-second in Ibn Rajab's commentary. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 667-668}

## The hadith and its routes
In a wording transmitted through al-Darawardi and reported by al-Daraqutni, al-Hakim, and al-Bayhaqi: "Whoever harms, God will harm him, and whoever makes hardship, God will make hardship for him." Malik related it in the *Muwatta'* as a *mursal* (without a Companion). {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 667-668}

## Its grade in the view of those Ibn Rajab cites
Ibn Rajab relates from Ibn Abd al-Barr that it is not transmitted through a sound route, and from Khalid ibn Sa'd al-Andalusi that it is not established as a *musnad* (with a connected chain). He relates that al-Hakim authenticated the chain of the route transmitted on the condition of Muslim, and that al-Nawawi said it has routes that strengthen one another. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 667-668}

## Meaning
Its meaning is the negation of harm at the outset and as recompense. A person may not harm himself or others at the outset, and he may not be repaid for harm with the like of it, except by legal retaliation. It is used to lift hardship from obligations and to ward off corruptions. {cite:ibn-rajab-jami-ulum-hikam p. 667-668}
