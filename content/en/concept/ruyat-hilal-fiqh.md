## Summary
The crescent here is the new moon at the start of a lunar month. Sighting it means seeing it with the eye after sunset on the twenty-ninth day of the previous month, by someone whose report is accepted; the sighting proves that the month has begun. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8546} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8547}

## General ruling
Seeking the crescent is prescribed, and it is most urged on the nights of the thirtieth of Sha'ban, Ramadan and Dhu al-Qa'da, because fasting, the pilgrimage and both Eids depend on them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8547} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8548}
If the crescent is hidden, the month is completed as thirty days. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8554}
The Hanafis make the search a collective duty. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8548}

## Ways of proof
Sighting is proved by a mass report that cannot be agreed upon to lie, which the Hanafis and Malikis accept under conditions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8549}
It is also proved by two just witnesses, the view of most jurists. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8549} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8550}
For the start of Ramadan, the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis accept one just witness; the Malikis do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8550} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8551}
For Shawwal and other months most jurists require two witnesses, and each school states its own details. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8551} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8552}

## Sighting in daylight
The reports from the Companions differ on a crescent seen in the day: whether it belongs to the night before or to the coming night. Some split the answer at midday, and others treat it as belonging to the coming night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8552} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8553}

## Astronomy and differences of horizon
Most jurists did not rely on astronomical reckoning to prove the month, including the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis; some allowed it for someone who knows it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8556} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8557} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8558}
The majority held that differences of horizon do not matter, while the Hanafis and Shafi'is take them into account for distant lands. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8558} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8560} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8561}

## Effects of error
Error can occur at the start of Ramadan, Shawwal or Dhu al-Hijja through a long cloud or lack of care in the search. The gravest case is standing at Arafat on the wrong day, and some jurists held that the standing remains valid. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8561} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8562}

## Announcing the sighting
When the crescent is proved, the people must be informed; the report follows the rules of transmission, requiring justice and accuracy. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8562} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8563}
Some supplications are transmitted for seeing the crescent. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8563} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8564}

## Where to look further
The rules of fasting, Eid and pilgrimage are given in their own entries, and the doubtful day is treated under fasting. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8549}

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] explains the hadith on fasting when the new moon is seen, breaking the fast when it is seen and completing thirty days of Sha'ban if it is hidden, and says the month for him begins only when the crescent is seen just after sunset, since the day begins at sunset, while astronomical tables compute the crescent's birth precisely but count the month from whatever moment it is born. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:05:30} His own view on the question is that a sighting anywhere begins the month for the whole earth, allowing for time differences, and that Muslims' disagreement over fasting and breaking the fast comes from their division into more than 62 states and the lack of a single authority. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:08:00} The lunar month, he says, lasts between 29 days 5 hours and 29 days 19 hours, so never more than thirty days. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep12 00:09:30}
