## Overview
Shaykh 'Ali al-Khafif holds acting on the solitary report (khabar al-ahad) obligatory when probability outweighs that the Prophet said, did, or approved it — legal obligation hangs on likelihood, not certainty {cite:makanat-al-sunna-azhar p. 41}. Denying its authority was the doctrine of some people of passions — the Rafida, the philosophers, some Mu'tazila — and it would annul the whole Sunna, for apart from the mutawatir the entire practical Sunna of prayer, zakat, fasting and hajj rests on solitary reports {cite:makanat-al-sunna-azhar p. 84}. The Prophet himself relied on single informants in his missions.

## The single report in Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's introduction to al-Tamhid
In the introduction to [[work/al-tamhid-ibn-abd-al-barr|al-Tamhid]], [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Ibn 'Abd al-Barr]] (d. 463 AH) reports that the scholars of fiqh and athar in all regions, as far as he knew, agreed on accepting the report of a single upright transmitter and on the obligation to act on it once established and not abrogated by another report or consensus — the position of the jurists of every age since the Companions, except the Kharijites and some groups of innovators. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 192} He argues from the Muslims' agreement that a questioner may accept the answer of a single scholar, and says he devoted a separate book to the subject. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 193} He sums up Malik's position as requiring action on both the musnad and the mursal report unless the established practice of his city stood against it, giving examples of reports Malik acted on against others' opinion, and of reports he left because practice stood against them, such as the hadith on the option of the two parties to a sale. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 193-194}

He then sets out the disagreement over whether it yields knowledge:

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Shafi'i and most jurists and theorists, Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's own choice**: it obliges action but not knowledge, like the testimony of two or four witnesses, and only what comes in a way that removes all excuse yields knowledge; yet all of them hold to the single upright report in matters of belief and treat it as law and religion — the position of the body of Ahl al-Sunna, Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 198-199}
- **Sunni: many traditionists and some theorists, among them al-Husayn al-Karabisi**: it yields outward knowledge and action together; Ibn Khuwayzmandad said this view can be derived from Malik's school. {cite:tamhid-furqan-2017-v1 p. 199}
:::

## Al-Shafi'i's rule in Kitab al-Umm

At the start of [[work/ikhtilaf-malik-wal-shafii|the Disagreement of Malik and al-Shafi'i]] in [[work/kitab-al-umm|al-Umm]], al-Rabi' asks [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] how a report from the Messenger of God is established; he refers to his book Jima' al-Ilm and says: when a trustworthy narrator relates from a trustworthy one back to the Messenger of God, it is established, and no hadith of his is ever left except for another hadith from him that contradicts it. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.513} When hadiths conflict, either one abrogates the other and the abrogating one is followed, or the abrogating one is unknown and the better-attested report is taken, and if they are equal, the one closer to the Book of God and the sunna of His Prophet. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.513} An established hadith from the Prophet gains no strength from agreement by anyone below him, and anyone below him who contradicts it is disregarded; had the one reported to contradict the sunna known it, he would have followed it. {cite:kitab-al-umm-wafa-v8 p.514}
