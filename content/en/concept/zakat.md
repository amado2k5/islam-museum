## Overview
The jurists define zakat as the transfer of a fixed portion of a defined wealth to a defined recipient — the third pillar after the two testimonies and the prayer {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 43} {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 45}. Abu'l-Wafa' al-Maraghi lists the thresholds in his *Qadaya*: five camels, thirty cattle, forty sheep; twenty mithqals of gold and two hundred dirhams of silver, at a quarter-tenth {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 46}; a tenth on rain-fed crops and half on irrigated, and a fifth on minerals and buried treasure {cite:min-qadaya-al-mal-wal-amal-azhar p. 47}. Its eight Qur'anic categories of recipients bind the worship of wealth to the service of society.

## Sha'rawi on the meaning of zakat
Sha'rawi says zakat is so named because it appears to be a loss but is in truth a gain, and that riba is the reverse: it appears to be an increase but is in truth a loss. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.527} He holds that the believer who pays zakat can be sure that the act is recorded with God as a reward that is multiplied, and that zakat, charity and the other acts of God's way are repaid many times over. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.527} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan-pp-501-1000 p.528}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 2)

The Sheikh works an arithmetic example: on a thousand, twenty-five is due, and nine hundred and seventy-five remains with its owner (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:07:30}.

He contrasts what stays with the owner and what goes to his poor brothers, and counts withholding zakat as clinging to this world {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:08:00}. He says that the surahs of Juz' Amma come to awaken the servant, as do the five prayers with the call to come to prayer and come to success {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 00:08:00}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 7)

The Sheikh holds that once a person has the four basics, whatever is beyond them is surplus, and that zakat may be given to someone whom we want to feed, give drink, clothe, house, treat or marry {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:36:30}. He counts these as six uses (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:36:30}.

The Sheikh says that every Muslim living in a camp today makes all the wealthy Muslims sinful, and calls for providing him with a source of clean water and a home {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:37:00}.

He says that any Muslim to whom food, drink and medicine do not reach with dignity, because of oppression, coercion or occupation, makes every capable person who says there is no god but God sinful {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:37:30}.

He says that the zakat of about five countries last year came to roughly 582 billion dollars, and asks whether, had that sum been paid out, there would still be camp dwellers (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:38:00}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy says that if the rich of the Muslim world paid the zakat on their wealth no one would remain poor, and explains that zakat is the poor man's right in the wealth, not a favour from the rich; he works it out with an example, twenty-five in every thousand dirhams, one part in forty. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 00:41:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 00:43:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 00:44:00} (needs_source: the arithmetic example in the recording is confused.)

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme "Liqa' al-Iman"
[[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] answers many questions on zakat in [[work/likaa-al-iman|Liqa' al-Iman]]. On money a young man saves for his marriage or a home, he says zakat is due on it as long as it exceeds his basic needs, and that one should not save for a future need while leaving a poor man who needs food today. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep02 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep02 00:04:00} He says zakat has a minimum threshold, that on cash it is two and a half per cent every Hijri year, that crops and livestock have their own system, such as a tenth or a twentieth depending on irrigation, and that the owner of wealth asks those who know as he would ask a doctor. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep02 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep02 00:05:00}

On a father's zakat he says he does not give it to his married daughter but to her husband if he is poor, because a woman's maintenance falls on her husband. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep11 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep11 00:02:30} He holds that donations to the Ramadan "tables of the Merciful" do not count as zakat, because zakat is handed to the recipient himself to use as he wishes; the giver is not his guardian choosing meat or anything else for him, and the recipient may have a more pressing need. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep21 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep21 00:08:30} He says taxes and customs duties do not count as zakat, because they pay for services rendered to the person with a business, such as security, roads and transport, and fund the subsidy on bread and tea. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep24 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep24 00:01:30} He says there is no zakat on household furniture, tools of work or a car used for getting about, however expensive. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep25 00:06:30} On a woman's jewellery he distinguishes what she acquired as savings, which is subject to zakat, from what she acquired for adornment, which is not. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep26 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep26 00:04:30} He permits paying zakat on wealth before it falls due, and zakat al-fitr in mid-Ramadan, with the intention of zakat at the moment of giving. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep18 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep18 00:00:30}
