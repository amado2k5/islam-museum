## The cerebral
If the epilepsy is cerebral, the first thing for it is the evacuation of its matter by evacuants of that kind (their names are not carried), purge after purge within the year; if venesection is required for whatever humour, it must not be stinted; cupping on the nape may draw the matter from the brain unless the brain's temperament and weakness bar it; if venesection has to be done often, rest a week, then purge, then rest, then cup, then rest, then purge, and keep alternating with rests until the matter is cleansed; afterwards use what purifies the head alone (the drugs' details and sites are not carried). The fit is to be met with a clean stomach — vomiting before food if possible — then the brain's temperament is tended with warming strengtheners, without loading him with heaters and temperament-changers all at once but gradually; if any harm to his functions appears, he is rested {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.129}

## The phlegmatic, melancholic and bilious
For that caused by phlegm, the best evacuants are those of the phlegm-evacuating kind (not carried); if general fullness attends the phlegm, venesection as described profits them. The melancholic is purged by what purges black bile (not carried). For bilious epilepsy, cooling and moistening must be attended to, especially clysters; if it is burnt, it is ruled as the melancholic, or between the bilious and the melancholic {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.129} {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.130}

## Umm al-sibyan
What is called 'umm al-sibyan' may be of the bilious kind according to some, and so its treatment orders baths, cold moist snuffs and strong moistening of the body; if the sufferer is a child, his nurse is given what cools her milk and is made to live in a cool, cellar-like place; it is likely this is in his view the sabir or mani epilepsy — this name not being in common use among the verifying physicians {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.130}

## The gastric, the rising-from-an-organ, and the whole-body
If the epilepsy is gastric, the gentlest evacuants are used, several times a year; after purifying, the stomach is tended and strengthened, only quickly digested foods of good chyme given to it, good digestion secured, and the stomach is left empty a long time; whatever of it flares on hunger is met by what was said under headache and elsewhere. For that which comes with something rising from an organ, a ligature is tied above the organ at the fit — perhaps preventing the fit — and the humour in the organ is evacuated by the known evacuations if they reach it, or by what ulcerates, suppurates and burns the matter (its means are not carried). For that which rises from the whole body, some said that but for the danger of cutting the two arteries of sleep — the interruption of pneuma and the apoplexy that would follow — there would be a complete cure in it for one whose epilepsy shares the whole body; Ibn Sina says: if that cannot be done, then among the ascending arteries those whose cutting carries no such danger, it is not far that severing them would profit greatly — know all that we have said {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v2 p.130}
