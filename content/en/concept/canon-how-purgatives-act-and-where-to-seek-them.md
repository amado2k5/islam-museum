## Summary
Ibn Sina divides the ways a drug purges into dissolving, as turbith does, squeezing, as myrobalan does, softening, as shir khusk does, and slipping, as psyllium mucilage and the plum do, notes that bitterness, pungency and astringency help each, and refers the many purgative and softening preparations to the pharmacopoeia and the correction of single drugs to the book on single drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}.

## The ways
A purgative drug may purge by dissolving, together with a special property, as turbith does; by squeezing, with a special property, as myrobalan does; by softening, as shir khusk does; and by making slippery, as the mucilage of psyllium and the plum do. Most strong drugs have some poison in them and purge by forcing nature, so they must be put right with something that has the property of a bezoar. Bitterness, pungency and astringency may help when they agree with the drug's property: bitterness and pungency help dissolving, astringency helps squeezing, and sourness helps the cutting that prepares slipping. A slipping drug and a squeezing one should not be joined so that their powers are equal, but it suits that one be slower than the other. Purgative and softening drugs to drink or smear, and others by age, are to be sought in the pharmacopoeia, and the correction of each drug and how it is given in the book on single drugs {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.285}.
