## Summary
Scholars differed on whether the Qur'an contains words of non-Arabic origin, the question of the mu'arrab. This page sets out what a research summary supplied to the museum reports, in three positions, each assigned to its holders, with no judgement between them. The question is already covered in the record [[concept/al-muarrab-fi-al-quran|Foreign words in the Qur'an]] in detail drawn from al-Itqan, al-Risala and the commentaries of al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir, so this page does not repeat it; it adds only what the summary adds: three classified positions, the books where they stand, the number of words al-Suyuti compiled, and al-Qurtubi's summing up.

## The three positions
The first position denies non-Arabic words: the Qur'an is purely Arabic, and what looks similar to other languages is a coincidence of languages or words arabised before revelation. The second accepts a limited number of foreign words that were arabised and became part of Arabic usage. The third is mediating: the origins of words may be foreign, but by the time of revelation they function as fully Arabic {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section b}.

## Al-Shafi'i in al-Risala
Al-Shafi'i in al-Risala strongly rejects any non-Arabic elements in the Qur'an, saying that rare or obscure words remain Arabic and that whoever says otherwise speaks from ignorance {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section b}. His words are given in detail in [[concept/al-muarrab-fi-al-quran|Foreign words in the Qur'an]].

## Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an
Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an states that the Qur'an was revealed in a clear Arabic tongue, and that whoever claims otherwise, as one who says Ta-Ha is a Nabataean word, makes a grave error; the similarities seen between languages are only a coincidence of languages {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section b}.

## Al-Tabari in Jami' al-bayan
[[person/al-tabari|Al-Tabari]], in the introduction to Jami' al-bayan and at verse 103 of al-Nahl, holds that the Qur'an is purely Arabic, that no word in it is unknown to the Arabs, and that the reports attributing some words to Persian, Abyssinian or other tongues express a coincidence of languages {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section b}.

## Al-Suyuti and his lists
In his books, al-Mutawakkili, al-Muhadhdhab and al-Itqan according to the summary, [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] compiles lists of what is claimed to be non-Arabic, some 100 to 125 words from Abyssinian, Persian, Syriac, Hebrew, Greek, Nabataean, Coptic and others. He leans toward accepting the mu'arrab after its arabisation {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section b}.

## Al-Qurtubi's summing up
Al-Qurtubi in the introduction to al-Jami' summarises the consensus that there are no non-Arabic sentences or phrases in the Qur'an and that proper names may be foreign, then sets out both camps and the mediating view that makes the arabised words Arabic {cite:research-summary-quran-arabic-classical-tafsir-2026 on section b}. Its bearing on the description of the tongue as clear is in [[concept/lisan-arabi-mubin-inda-al-mufassirin|The clear Arabic tongue in the commentators]].

## How the positions part
The positions part over the origin of the words, not over the tongue in which the Qur'an came: the deniers hold there is no non-Arabic origin, the accepters acknowledge one, and the mediating view joins the two descriptions by assigning them to different times. All positions agree, according to the summary, that the Qur'an contains foreign proper names and no non-Arabic sentences.

## What this page does not reach
al-Risala, Majaz al-Qur'an, Jami' al-bayan, al-Itqan, al-Mutawakkili, al-Muhadhdhab and al-Qurtubi's introduction were not opened; the views come from the research summary the founder approved using on 21 November 2026 without re-verification. No page numbers are given, al-Suyuti's lists are not set out, and no attribution of a word to a given language was checked.
