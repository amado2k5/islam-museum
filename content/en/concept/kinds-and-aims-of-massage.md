## Summary
Ibn Sina opens chapter 4, on massage, by dividing it into hard, which firms; soft, which relaxes; much, which thins; and moderate, which fattens, whose combinations give nine pairings; then into rough, with coarse cloths drawing blood quickly to the surface, and smooth, with the palm collecting it in the organ; its aim is to condense the loose, harden the soft, loosen the dense and soften the hard {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## Hard, soft, much and moderate
Massage may be hard, which firms; soft, which relaxes; much, which thins; or moderate, which fattens. When these are combined nine pairings arise {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## Rough and smooth
Some massage is rough, with coarse cloths, and draws the blood quickly to the surface; some is smooth, with the palm or a soft cloth, and gathers the blood and holds it in the organ {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.

## The aim
The aim of massage is to condense loose bodies, harden soft ones, loosen dense ones and soften hard ones {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.225}.
