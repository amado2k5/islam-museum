## Summary
The pillars of faith are six: belief in God, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and the decree, its good and its ill. They are the Prophet's answer when Jibril asked him about faith, as Ibn Abi al-'Izz al-Hanafi quotes it in his commentary on the Tahawi creed {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz p.235}. They are not the [[concept/five-pillars|five pillars of Islam]]: those are outward acts on which Islam is built, and these are foundations the heart affirms. The Pillars and Branches of Faith Browser gathers every place these foundations are mentioned in the museum's sources.

## In the hadith of Jibril
Al-Bukhari reports from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet was out among the people one day when Jibril came and asked him: what is faith? He answered: "Faith is that you believe in God, His angels, the meeting with Him and His messengers, and that you believe in the resurrection" {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}; he then asked about Islam and about ihsan. In Sahih Muslim's report of the same hadith, when the man had gone the Prophet said: "This is Jibril; he came to teach the people their religion" {cite:sahih-muslim 0008}. Ibn Abi al-'Izz quotes the answer on faith in the wording that counts all six: "that you believe in God, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day, and that you believe in the decree, its good and its ill", and describes the hadith as agreed to be sound {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz p.235}.

## In the Qur'an
Commenting on al-Tahawi's words "we believe in the angels, the prophets and the books sent down to the messengers", Ibn Abi al-'Izz says these matters are among the pillars of faith, and cites the close of Surat al-Baqara, [[passage/quran-2-285|al-Baqara 285]], and the verse of righteousness, [[passage/quran-2-177|al-Baqara 177]]. He says God made faith to be belief in this set and named those who believe in it believers, as He named those who deny it disbelievers in [[passage/quran-4-136|al-Nisa' 136]] {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz p.235}. Beside these verses, the browser's Qur'an table lists the verses on belief in the unseen and in what was sent down at the opening of al-Baqara, [[passage/quran-2-3|al-Baqara 3]] and [[passage/quran-2-4|al-Baqara 4]]; the verses on belief in what the prophets were given, [[passage/quran-2-136|al-Baqara 136]] and [[passage/quran-3-84|Al 'Imran 84]]; and the verses on the decree, [[passage/quran-54-49|al-Qamar 49]], [[passage/quran-57-22|al-Hadid 22]] and [[passage/quran-64-11|al-Taghabun 11]].

## Five, six or seven
The two verses Ibn Abi al-'Izz cites name five: God, the angels, the books, the messengers and the Last Day. The hadith of Jibril adds the decree, making six. Ibn Abi al-'Izz reports from Abu Talib al-Makki that the pillars of faith are seven: these five, belief in the decree, and belief in Paradise and the Fire; he then says: "this is true, and the proofs for it are established, firm and decisive" {cite:sharh-tahawiyya-ibn-abi-al-izz p.237}. The difference is in the counting, not in what is counted: whoever counts seven sets Paradise and the Fire apart from the Last Day.

## The pillars among the branches of faith
When Badr al-Din al-'Ayni counted the branches of faith in 'Umdat al-Qari, he placed these pillars at the head of the group of beliefs: belief in God is the first branch, belief in His angels the third, in His books the fourth, in His messengers the fifth, in the decree, its good and its ill, the sixth, and in the Last Day the seventh; under the Last Day he includes the questioning in the grave, the resurrection, the reckoning, the scale and the bridge {cite:umdat-qari-ilmiyya-2001-v1 p.212}. For him the pillars are part of the [[concept/shuab-al-iman|branches of faith]] and their root.
