## Nature and selection
The black is the worst and most harmful, then the red, and the white is the safest and the one used. The black flower is purple, the red yellow, and the white white or tending to yellow, and the kind in use has an oily moisture {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}. The best is the white; if it is not found, the red is used, and the black is always avoided, though the juice of its stems is sometimes used in place of opium {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}. The editor's footnote gives the plant as shawkaran, hemlock.

## Temperament and actions
The black is cold and dry at the end of the third degree, the white at its start {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}. A narcotic that stops bleeding and, by its numbing, calms throbbing pains {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}.

## Uses by organ
Adornment: it is used in a thickening compound for its binding and setting power {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}.

Swellings and pustules: it eases their pain and dissolves hardness of the testicles {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}.

Joints: it eases gout when applied and when drunk; the dose is left out {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}.

Head: juice of either kind, taken, eases earache; with vinegar and rose oil it eases toothache {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}.

Head: it causes drowsiness, and a sizeable amount of its leaf confuses the mind {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.401}.

Eyes: juice of the leaf or seed applied to the eye eases stubborn eye pain, and its flower, leaf or seed applied to the forehead keeps rheum from descending {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.402}.

Breath organs: its seed, drunk with a compound whose name the scan does not read clearly, helps excessive spitting of blood {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.402}.

Excretory organs: its juice eases pain of the womb and stops bleeding; its leaf is applied as a poultice to testicular swelling {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.402}.

Poisons: a poison that confuses the mind, abolishes male sexual function, and causes quinsy and madness {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.402}.
