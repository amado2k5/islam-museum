## Summary
Halif is lexically an oath, from a root meaning firm resolve; technically it is the affirmation of a ruling by naming something revered in a fixed form. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7245} Swearing in court makes one litigant take the oath to settle the dispute; the practice rests on the Prophet's instruction to the defendant to swear by God, and his word to al-Ash'ath ibn Qays: your proof, or else his oath. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7245} A binding oath is sworn by God or His attributes, and swearing by other than God is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7245} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7246} Oaths lie over financial rights without dispute; a claim for a legal penalty of God is not sworn to, while non-financial private rights such as slander, retaliation, marriage, divorce, revocation and manumission are disputed between the schools. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7246} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7247} The majority hold that the oath ends the dispute at once but does not discharge the right; the Malikis treat it as barring later proof except for a valid excuse. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7247} The oath is sworn to the act itself, or to another's act by denial of knowledge; the Hanafis divide oaths on the cause from those on the outcome. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7248} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7249} The judge administers the oath only when the claimant asks, and the Hanafis list five cases where it applies without that request. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7249} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7250} For intention, the oath follows the swearer's intention when he is the wronged party and the examiner's intention when he is the wrongdoer. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7250} The entry refers the reader to the headword on oaths (ayman) for detail, and closes at the next heading حِلْف on p.7251.
