## Definition
Dhimma is lexically safety and covenant, and the dhimmi is one under covenant. In law it is the state of a non-Muslim who remains in the land of Islam in his unbelief, under a covenant with the imam that secures his life and property in return for the poll tax and his submission to the rules of Islam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3616}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3617}

## Related terms
- The People of the Book: Jews and Christians and those of their faith; the protected people may be of them or of others, such as the Magians. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3617}
- Musta'manun (those under safe-conduct): those who enter the land of Islam under a temporary safe-conduct, while the dhimma is perpetual. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3617}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3618}
- Ahl al-harb: unbelievers for whom there is neither dhimma nor safe-conduct. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3618}

## How the dhimma is acquired
- By contract: the unbeliever is allowed to remain in his unbelief on paying the poll tax and accepting the worldly rules of Islam; it is concluded by the imam or his deputy, though the Hanafis allow any Muslim. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3618}
- By circumstance: a year's residence in the land of Islam, by the Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis; marriage of a combatant woman under safe-conduct to a Muslim or protected man, by the Hanafis; and buying land liable to the land tax, by the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3620}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3621}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3622}
- By following another: minor children and a wife follow the father or husband; a foundling found in a place of the protected people is a protected person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3622}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3623}
- By conquest, where the ruler leaves the people of the land under the dhimma. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3623}

## Who may be granted a dhimma
- The jurists agree that it may be granted to the People of the Book and the Magians, and not to an apostate. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3618}
- The Hanafis and some Malikis and Hanbalis allow it to all unbelievers except the idolaters of the Arabs. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3619}
- The Shafi'is and, in the prevailing view, the Hanbalis allow it only to the People of the Book and the Magians. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3618}
- The prevailing Maliki view allows it to every kind of unbeliever. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3619}

## Conditions of the covenant
It must be perpetual by the majority; the protected person must accept the rules of Islam in dealings; and the men must accept paying the poll tax each year. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3619}
Al-Mawardi, a Shafi'i, lists six conditions: that they not disparage the Qur'an, not disparage the Prophet, not revile Islam, not violate a Muslim woman, not tempt a Muslim from his religion, and not aid the enemy or shelter spies. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3619}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3620}

## Rights
- Protection and safe residence in the land of Islam; they may not settle permanently in Mecca or Medina, by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3623}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3624}
- Places of worship depend on the city: a city founded by the Muslims admits no new church by consensus; a city conquered by force admits none, though existing ones are not destroyed in some views; a city taken by treaty follows its terms. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3625}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3626}
- Their worship and beliefs are left to them when practised among themselves and not openly. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3626}
- They may choose their work and trade as Muslims do, except offices that require Islam, such as the caliphate and command in war. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3627}
- They deal in property as Muslims do, subject to the prohibition of usury and invalid contracts; they may deal in wine and pork among themselves privately, and the jurists differ on compensating their destruction. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3627}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3628}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3629}
- Testimony: their testimony is not accepted against Muslims by agreement, and the Hanafis accept it among themselves, while the majority do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3632}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3633}
- Marriage: a Muslim man may marry a Jewish or Christian woman, and a Muslim woman may not marry a non-Muslim. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3633}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3634}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3630}
- Judgement: by the majority they may not be appointed judges over their own people; the Hanafis allow it in matters open to arbitration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3636}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3637}

## Duties
- The poll tax: due from a free, adult male; not from children, women or the insane. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3630}
- The land tax on land, either a fixed tax by area or a share of produce. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3631}
- The tithe on goods in trade carried from one city to another within the land of Islam, at half the tenth. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3631}

## Prohibitions
- To speak ill of God, His book, His Prophet or His religion; the majority hold that failure to refrain breaks the covenant, the Hanafis do not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3631}
- To display the sale of wine and pork in Muslim cities, or to display what they hold forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3631}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3632}
- To be distinguished in dress, mounts and clothing, as a mark of their lesser standing. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3631}

## Crimes and penalties
- The hadd penalties apply to them as to Muslims, except that they are not punished for drinking wine unless they display it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3631}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3632}
- Stoning: the Shafi'is, Hanbalis and Abu Yusuf treat them equally with Muslims; Abu Hanifa and Malik do not stone a married protected person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3632}
- Retaliation: a protected person is put to death for killing a Muslim by the majority, and a Muslim is not put to death for killing one by the Shafi'is and Hanbalis; the Hanafis allow retaliation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3633}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3634}
- Blood money: no difference between a Muslim and a protected person in blood money for accidental killing, paid by the killer's group. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3633}
- Discretionary penalties: fixed by the ruler for both, according to the offence. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3634}

## What breaks the covenant
- Joining the land of war or taking a fortress from which the Muslims are attacked, by agreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3634}
- Refusal to pay the poll tax, which breaks the covenant by the majority and not by the Hanafis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3634}
- Other acts named by the schools: defiance of the rules, adultery with a Muslim woman, and reviling Islam; the Hanafis hold that a secret insult to the Prophet does not break the covenant. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3634}{cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3635}
