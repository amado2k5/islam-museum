## Summary
In chapters eleven and twelve Ibn Sina mentions animal urine for testing, and liquids that resemble urine and how to tell them apart, and then ends his treatment of urine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.197-198}.

## Animals' urine
A physician may profit from knowing animals' urine when testing, though that is hard. They said camels' urine in the flask is like melted butter with turbidity and thickness outside; pack animals' urine is like it but clearer, the upper half of the flask clear and the lower half turbid. Sheep's urine is white with yellow, close to human urine, but without body, its residue like fat. The gazelle's resembles sheep's and human urine but has no body or residue and is clearer than sheep's. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 pp.197-198}.

## Look-alike liquids
Oxymel and all liquids such as honey water, fig water and saffron water grow clearer as one nears them, whereas urine does the opposite. Honey water has yellow foam, and fig water's residue settles to one side, not in the middle, and without motion. He concludes that this much suffices and that the particular books give further detail on urine. {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1 p.198}.
