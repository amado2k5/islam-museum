## Summary
In [[work/al-hisba-fi-al-islam|al-Hisba fi al-Islam]], after discussing compelling craftsmen to supply what people need at the fair wage, [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] digresses to sharecropping and holds it lawful in the sounder of the scholars' two views. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 26}

## The view that it is lawful
Ibn Taymiyya says sharecropping was the practice of Muslims under their Prophet and the Rightly Guided Caliphs, of the households of Abu Bakr, Uthman, Ali and other Emigrant families, the view of Ibn Mas'ud, and the school of the hadith jurists such as [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]], [[person/ishaq-ibn-rahwayh|Ishaq ibn Rahawayh]], [[person/dawud-al-zahiri|Dawud]], al-Bukhari, Ibn Khuzayma and Ibn al-Mundhir, and of al-Layth, Ibn Abi Layla, [[person/abu-yusuf-al-qadi|Abu Yusuf]] and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 26} He argues from the Prophet dealing with the people of Khaybar for part of the fruit and crops until his death, an arrangement that lasted until Umar expelled them, with the seed provided by them; hence he holds it sound that the seed may come from the worker. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 26} {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 27} He reads the prohibition of mukhabara and land rental in the light of the explanation that they used to stipulate for the landowner the crop of a particular plot - a condition void by text and consensus. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 27}

## The views that forbid it, or allow it as a dependent
Ibn Taymiyya reports that some scholars took this partnership to be hire for an unknown wage and said analogy requires forbidding it; some forbade orchard-share and sharecropping while allowing mudaraba out of need, as [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] says; others allowed orchard-share generally, as [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] and [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]'s old view, or on palms and vines, as al-Shafi'i's new view, and allowed sharecropping as its dependent - where the land is the larger part, for al-Shafi'i, or limited to a third, for Malik. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 27}

## Partnership, not hire
Ibn Taymiyya holds that most early scholars and the jurists of the cities treated it as partnership, since each party seeks the fruit and crop, one by his labour and the other by his property, as in mudaraba; therefore he holds that if such partnerships are invalid, a fair share, not a fair wage, is due. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 27} He considers sharecropping more fundamental than rental and closer to justice, as both parties share gain and loss, and holds that renting out granted land (iqta') is lawful, adding that no scholar of the four schools forbade it before some of his contemporaries. {cite:hisba-ibn-taymiyya-ilmiyya p. 28}
