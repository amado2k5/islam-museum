## Summary
The question starts from a hadith that, al-Suyuti says, twenty-one Companions narrated, and Abu Ubayd held mass-transmitted: 'this Qur'an was revealed in seven ahruf'. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 308} Ibn Hibban, as al-Suyuti relays, says the dispute over its meaning reached thirty-five views, which the qadi did not name; then he gives the best known. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 328} This museum sets out the main ones attributed to their holders, without choosing. See [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|the seven ahruf]] and [[event/umar-and-hisham-ibn-hakim-surat-al-furqan|Umar and Hisham]].

## What scholars agree on
Ibn al-Jazari says scholars differed on what the seven means 'while agreeing that it does not mean that one harf is read in seven ways, since that occurs only in a few words', and that it cannot mean these seven famous readers, who had not yet been created or existed. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 34} Al-Sabt says it does not mean every word is read seven ways, or the hadith would say 'seven ahruf' without 'in'; rather the Qur'an came down on this condition and this latitude, so that the kinds of variation do not exceed seven however much the multiplicity. The first word of [[passage/quran-1-4|al-Fatiha 4]] is read in ways reaching seven or ten; a word in al-Ma'ida in twenty-two readings; the exclamation of impatience at [[passage/quran-17-23|al-Isra' 17:23]] al-Rummani brought to thirty-seven dialect forms; and the difference in all of them does not go beyond seven kinds. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 173}

## The wisdom ascribed
Al-Sabt says the wisdom in the revelation in the seven ahruf is ease for the whole Muslim community, above all the Arabs who first heard it; he argues from Ubayy ibn Ka'b's hadith that Jibril said 'God commands that your community read the Qur'an in one harf', and the Prophet asked God for pardon and forgiveness and said his community could not bear it until it reached seven ahruf, and from the hadith that the Prophet told Jibril he was sent to an unlettered community, among them the aged man, the old woman and the boy. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 159} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 157} Ibn al-Jazari likewise says God gave them 'room in dialects and play in vowels, as He eased them in religion'. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 33}

## Do the ahruf remain?
Another question is bound up with it: did the seven ahruf remain in the codices? A group of jurists, readers and theologians held all of them to be in the Uthmanic codices because the community could not neglect carrying any; most scholars held that the codices hold only what their script bears, gathering the final review; and Ibn Jarir al-Tabari and those who agree held that the community limited itself to one harf to unite its word. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 183} {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 184} Al-Sabt chooses that the codices contained some of the seven ahruf, not all. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 184} See [[concept/al-arda-al-akhira|the final review]].

## What I do not say
I relay these views from al-Sabt, Ibn al-Jazari and al-Suyuti; I copy no word of the Qur'an, so the examples are described in words, and the list does not count all forty views or every dissenter. I relied on Tahdhib Manahil al-Irfan, not the original Manahil al-Irfan of al-Zurqani, so what I attribute to al-Zurqani I attribute through al-Sabt.
