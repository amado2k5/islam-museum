## Summary

Hadith critics differed over [[person/al-bazzar-abu-bakr-ahmad-ibn-amr|al-Hafiz Abu Bakr al-Bazzar]], author of the [[work/musnad-al-bazzar|Musnad]]: most called him trustworthy and praised his memory, while criticism of errors in chains and texts is reported from some. A university study of a section of the Musnad of Anas from his work sets out the views and how they may be read. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.28} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.30}

## Praise

The study quotes Ibn Abi Khaythama, a contemporary (d. 279 AH), that he was a pillar of Islam, likened to Ibn Hanbal in his asceticism and scrupulousness; Ya'qub ibn al-Mubarak: "I have seen no one nobler than al-Bazzar, nor a better memoriser"; and his student [[person/abu-al-shaykh-al-isfahani|Abu al-Shaykh al-Isfahani]] (d. 369 AH), that he was one of the world's foremost memorisers, with the report that after Ali ibn al-Madini no one knew hadith better. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.28} [[person/al-khatib-al-baghdadi-ahmad|Al-Khatib al-Baghdadi]] (d. 463 AH) and al-Sam'ani (d. 563 AH) said he was trustworthy and a memoriser who wrote the Musnad, discussed its hadiths and showed their defects; Ibn al-Jawzi called him a memoriser of hadith, Ibn al-Qattan al-Fasi the best memoriser of hadith, and [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] the learned memoriser, author of the large annotated Musnad. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.28}

## Criticism

[[person/al-daraqutni|Al-Daraqutni]] answered a question about him: he errs in chains and texts; he related the Musnad in Egypt from memory, looking into other people's books and relating from memory without his own books, and so erred in many hadiths; people speak against him; Abu Abd al-Rahman [[person/al-nasai|al-Nasa'i]] criticised him. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29} Abu Ahmad al-Hakim (d. 378 AH) said: he errs in chains and texts. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29} Elsewhere al-Daraqutni said: trustworthy, often mistaken, relying on his memory. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.30}

## The modern study's reading

The thesis author holds that the criticism originates with al-Nasa'i, a foremost and strict critic, al-Bazzar's contemporary and companion in Egypt when he related his Musnad from memory, who perhaps heard some of his slips; that no detailed statement about him has reached her in al-Nasa'i's books; that Abu Ahmad al-Hakim and al-Daraqutni explained that general criticism as errors in chains and texts; and that Ibn Hajar in al-Lisan pointed to some of his errors while showing they came from others. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.30} She compares this with the thousand hadiths in which Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi was faulted although Ibn al-Madini testified to his memory, holds that errors are unsurprising in one who related such a Musnad from memory, and notes that those who called him trustworthy are more numerous. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.30}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (most critics): trustworthy, a memoriser.** Ibn Abi Khaythama, Abu al-Shaykh, al-Khatib, al-Sam'ani, Ibn al-Qattan and al-Dhahabi called him reliable and praised his memory. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.28}

**Sunni (al-Nasa'i as reported by al-Daraqutni, and Abu Ahmad al-Hakim): he errs in chains and texts.** Because he related the Musnad in Egypt from memory without books. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29}

**A modern academic study.** The criticism is general, explained as errors of memory; those who called him reliable are more; it does not lessen his worth. {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.29} {cite:musnad-bazzar-anas-thesis-1413 p.30}
:::
