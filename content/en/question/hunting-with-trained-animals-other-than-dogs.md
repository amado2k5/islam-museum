## Summary
In the book of hunting in [[work/bidayat-al-mujtahid|Bidayat al-Mujtahid]], [[person/ibn-rushd|Ibn Rushd]] discusses the hunting animal, saying that agreement and disagreement about it concern partly the kind of animal and partly the conditions. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 12} The kind agreed upon is the dog; disputed are trained beasts and birds of prey other than the dog. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 12} {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 13}

## The jet-black dog
Ibn Rushd notes that some disliked hunting with the black dog, among them [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan al-Basri]], [[person/ibrahim-al-nakhai|Ibrahim al-Nakha'i]] and [[person/qatada-ibn-diama|Qatada]], and that [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said he knew no one who allowed it when it was entirely black, as did Ishaq; the majority allow its game if it is trained. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 12} {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 13} He traces this disagreement to analogy conflicting with general wording: the general wording of the verse on trained hunting animals puts all dogs on a par, while the command to kill the jet-black dog implies by analogy that hunting with it is not allowed, for those who hold that a prohibition renders the prohibited act invalid. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 13}

## Hunting animals other than the dog
- **Whatever accepts training**: some allowed all of them once trained, even the cat, as Ibn Sha'ban said; this is the school of [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] and his followers, the view of the jurists of the great cities, and is reported from [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]]: whatever hunting animal accepts training is an instrument for making game lawful. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 13}
- **The dog alone**: some said there is no hunting with any animal but the dog - neither goshawk nor falcon nor any other - except what is caught alive and slaughtered; this is the view of [[person/mujahid-ibn-jabr|Mujahid]]. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 13}
- **The dog and the goshawk**: some excepted, among birds of prey, the goshawk alone and allowed its game. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 13}

## From the edition's commentary
The commentator of the Dar al-Salam edition notes in the margin that those who allowed hunting with these animals required that the animal go when sent and come when called; that the majority do not require birds of prey to refrain from eating the game, citing Ibn Abbas: if the dog eats, do not eat, but if the falcon eats, eat, since you can beat a dog but cannot beat a falcon; and that the Shafi'is require of them what they require of the dog. {cite:bidaya-abbadi-dar-al-salam-v3 p. 14}

## Not covered here
Ibn Rushd's other conditions for the hunting animal (training, sending, naming God) and hunting with weapons are not given here.
