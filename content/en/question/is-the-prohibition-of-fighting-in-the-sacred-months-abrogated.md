## Summary
Scholars differ on whether the prohibition of fighting in the sacred months was abrogated or still stands. [[person/muhammad-al-amin-al-shinqiti-al-jakani|Muhammad al-Amin al-Shinqiti]] held both views in his lifetime: he argued for abrogation for a time and then abandoned it. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.38} This entry sets out the two views as he gives them, without a verdict.

## The two views
His biographer quotes what he said on the fifth verse of Surat Bara'a: that he had held the prohibition abrogated and for a long time argued for this view as the sounder one, and that it then became clear to him that the sounder and more correct of the two views is that the prohibition of the sacred months remains and was not abrogated; and on the thirty-fourth verse, that what he later verified and came to hold firmly is that they remain prohibited to this day. See [[passage/quran-9-5|al-Tawba 5]]. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.38} The biographer gives this as an example of the shaykh's returning to what he saw as right even after defending another view for years. {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.38}

## Disagreement and views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Shinqiti's later view**: the prohibition of fighting in the sacred months remains and was not abrogated {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.38}.
- **Sunni: al-Shinqiti's earlier view**: the prohibition was abrogated {cite:athar-shinqiti-adwa-ataat-2019-v1 p.38}.
- **Not yet given here**: the views of other exegetes and jurists on the question, and academic studies, have not yet been added; later books will supply them.
:::
