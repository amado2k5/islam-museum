## Summary
[[passage/quran-2-30|al-Baqara 30]] says God told the angels He would place a vicegerent on earth, and they asked about one who would corrupt it and shed blood. Neither Adam nor his offspring yet existed, so the exegetes asked how the angels knew, and whether anything lived on earth before him. Al-Tabari sets out the views at that verse, and Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi relay and add to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF pp. 454-475} {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-alexandria-024188 pp. 70-77} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari pp. 274-275}

## First voice: the jinn dwelt on earth before Adam
Al-Tabari relates from al-Dahhak on Ibn Abbas that the first to dwell on earth were the jinn, who corrupted it and shed blood; God sent Iblis with a force of angels who drove them to the islands of the seas and the edges of the mountains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 454} From al-Rabi' ibn Anas on Abu al-Aliya: a group of the jinn disbelieved and the angels fought them. Ibn Kathir has from Mujahid on Abd Allah ibn Amr that they preceded Adam by two thousand years. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-alexandria-024188 p. 70} Shakir's note on al-Tabari weakens al-Tabari's chain here and says al-Hakim has a similar report from Mujahid on Ibn Abbas, called sound in its chain, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; this is relayed from the note, as I did not read the Mustadrak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 454}

## Second voice: the angels knew from God, or inferred
In al-Suddi's account from Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas and other Companions, God told the angels that the vicegerent would have offspring who corrupt, envy one another and kill. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 456} Qatada said God had told them that if He placed a creature on earth it would corrupt. Al-Qurtubi has that the angels understood from the word vicegerent that some of Adam's kind would corrupt, and generalised; he calls Qatada's view good and the first view very good. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-alam-kutub-bukhari pp. 274-275} Al-Tabari read the angels' words as a question for information, not an objection; he thought prior notice possible while criticising inconsistencies in some wording of al-Suddi's report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF pp. 465-475} Ibn Kathir cautions about Israelite material in al-Suddi's well-known chain. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir-alexandria-024188 pp. 76-77}

## Third voice: no conclusive report before Adam
Al-Tabari holds that knowledge of the past comes only by a report that excludes excuse and rules out falsehood and error, and that al-Dahhak's report and Ibn Zayd's statement are not of that kind; he chose what the plain sense of the text shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 475} In Ibn Zayd's report in his book, God then had no creation but the angels and the earth held no creature. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 PDF p. 455}

:::perspectives
- **Sunni, secondary view:** the jinn dwelt on earth before Adam. Ibn Abbas, al-Dahhak, Mujahid, al-Rabi' ibn Anas.
- **Sunni, principal view in al-Tabari and al-Qurtubi:** the angels knew because God told them, or inferred from the sense of vicegerency.
- **Sunni, al-Tabari's methodological stance:** no conclusive report on what came before Adam; in one report the earth held no creature.
:::

## What is not said
In what I read I found no statement that the angels' knowledge came from the Preserved Tablet, though one can imagine such an explanation, so I attribute it to no one. This record does not decide among the views; the museum presents them.
