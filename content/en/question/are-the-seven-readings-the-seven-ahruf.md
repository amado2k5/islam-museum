## Summary
When the seven readings gathered by Ibn Mujahid became famous, some people thought they were the 'seven ahruf' of the hadith. Scholars have treated this thought from early on, and Ibn al-Jazari gave it a place in al-Nashr headed, as far as I read the scanned text, 'the error of one who thinks the seven ahruf are what is in al-Shatibiyya and al-Taysir'. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 46}

## What al-Mahdawi, Ibn Ammar and Abu Shama said
Ibn al-Jazari quotes al-Mahdawi that the cities' mostly limiting themselves to the seven was an abridgement and selection that some later men favoured, which the common people made a binding duty, so that on hearing anything else they called it error or unbelief; and that whoever made them seven, had he fallen short of seven or exceeded it, would have removed the confusion. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 46} Abu Shama says the notion of some people that the seven readings found now are those intended in the hadith is against the consensus of all the people of knowledge, and only some of the ignorant think so; he quotes Ibn al-Arabi that these seven are not the only ones allowed, as with the readings of Abu Jafar, Shayba and al-A'mash. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 525}

## Makki and the readers before Ibn Mujahid
Makki says, as al-Suyuti has it, that the earlier imams who wrote on the readings, such as Abu Ubayd, Abu Hatim al-Sijistani, al-Tabari and Ismail al-Qadi, listed many times these men; and that around the year two hundred people followed the reading of Abu Amr and Yaqub in Basra, Hamza and Asim in Kufa, Ibn Amir in Syria, Ibn Kathir in Mecca and Nafi' in Medina; by the year three hundred Ibn Mujahid put in al-Kisa'i and dropped Yaqub. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 526} He relates that Ibn Jubayr of Mecca, before Ibn Mujahid, wrote a book of readings limited to five, choosing an imam from each city because the codices Uthman sent were five. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 527} See [[concept/tasbi-al-qiraat-ibn-mujahid|Ibn Mujahid's limiting to seven]].

## Al-Kawashi and al-Qarrab
Al-Suyuti quotes al-Qarrab in al-Shafi that holding to the reading of seven readers and not others has no report or sunna behind it; it is the gathering of some later men that spread and gave the impression that nothing may be added to it, which no one said; and al-Kawashi that anything whose chain is sound, whose form is right in Arabic and which agrees with the Imam codex's script is one of the seven stated, and where one condition is lacking it is irregular; and that the masters of the field strongly objected to those who thought the famous readings confined to what is in al-Taysir and al-Shatibiyya. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 528} See [[concept/shurut-al-qiraa-al-sahiha|the conditions of an accepted reading]].

## The remaining question
A question remains: do the readings established today, the seven and the ten, take in all seven ahruf or some? Ibn al-Jazari's answer is that they are 'a little of a lot'; he does not claim the continued mass transmission of all the ahruf, and points out that to claim so would make the whole community disobedient and mistaken in leaving what it left, which it, being protected, is not. {cite:nashr-dahman-damascus-1345-v1 p. 43} Al-Sabt holds that the Uthmanic codices held some of the ahruf, not all. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 184} [[question/what-is-meant-by-the-seven-ahruf|What is meant by the seven ahruf?]] takes it up from the side of what a harf is.

## What I leave aside
In what I read I find no one saying the seven readings are the seven ahruf as a position held among scholars; it is reported of 'some of the common people' and 'some people', whose conduct al-Suyuti and Ibn al-Jazari describe. If a named holder appears in other sources, I do not attribute it here.
