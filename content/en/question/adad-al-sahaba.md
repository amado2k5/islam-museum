## The question
People often ask how many [[concept/companionship-suhba|Companions]] there were, and the figures handed down vary widely. [[person/al-dhahabi|Al-Dhahabi]] gathered the best known of them in the preface to [[work/tajrid-asma-al-sahaba|*Tajrid Asma' al-Sahaba*]] and commented on them. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67}

## The positions
- **[[person/al-shafii|Al-Shafi'i]]:** the Prophet died when the Muslims numbered sixty thousand — thirty thousand in Medina and thirty thousand elsewhere. The editor notes that al-Saji reports it in his *Manaqib al-Shafi'i*, as cited in *al-Taqyid wa-al-Idah*, with a good chain according to al-'Iraqi. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67}
- **[[person/abu-zura-al-razi|Abu Zur'a al-Razi]]:** when the Messenger of God died, more than a hundred thousand people had seen him and heard from him; al-Khatib reports it in *al-Jami' li-Akhlaq al-Rawi*. A gloss in one manuscript says that the hadith master 'Abd al-Qadir specified the excess in *Tabaqat al-Hanafiyya*: one hundred and fourteen thousand Companions who narrated from him and heard him. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67}
- **[[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim]]:** four thousand people narrated from the Prophet. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67}
- **Al-Dhahabi:** "Rather, those who narrated from him were perhaps about fifteen hundred souls, never reaching two thousand; and I think those named in this book of mine come to eight thousand persons, most of them unknown." {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67}
- **[[person/abu-musa-al-madini|Abu Musa al-Madini]]:** Ibn Manda's book on the Companions comes close to three thousand eight hundred entries. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67-68}

## How they fit together
These statements measure different things: al-Shafi'i's concerns all Muslims at the Prophet's death; Abu Zur'a's those who saw and heard him; al-Hakim's and al-Dhahabi's those who narrated from him; and the estimates of al-Dhahabi and Abu Musa those whose names were recorded in books on the Companions. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67-68} That is why al-Dhahabi distinguishes the number of narrators from the number named in his book, and describes most of the latter as little known. {cite:tajrid-darbirr-v1 p. 67}
