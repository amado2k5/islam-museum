## Summary
The "exception in faith" (*al-istithna' fi al-iman*) is a Muslim saying of himself, "I am a believer, God willing." [[person/ibn-batta-al-ukbari-ubayd-allah|Ibn Batta al-Ukbari]] gives it a chapter in *[[work/ibana-al-kubra-ibn-batta|al-Ibana al-Kubra]]*, and the book's editor notes that scholars hold three views on it. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 862}

## Ibn Batta's position
Ibn Batta opens the chapter by saying that believers are marked by constant apprehension for their faith and great caution for their religion. Their hearts fear that faith may be taken from them; they do not know what God will do with them in the rest of their lives, and they are wary of praising themselves. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 862} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 863} He narrates with his chain the report that [[person/aisha|Aisha]] asked the Prophet whether those who "give what they give with hearts afraid" are people who steal, commit adultery and drink wine. He answered no: it is the one who fasts, prays and gives charity and fears it will not be accepted. The editor traces the report to al-Tirmidhi and others. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 864} Ibn Batta then says that because this apprehension stayed in their hearts, they kept to the exception in their speech. It is a mark of people of reason and knowledge, he says, to say "I am a believer, God willing", "not by way of doubt", since faith is acknowledgement of God's lordship, submission in servanthood and belief in Him. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 864}

## The editor's explanation
In his study the editor sets out what the exception means to those who use it. It refers the matter to God's will. It avoids being certain about what one cannot know of one's future deeds and their acceptance. It expresses hope of remaining steadfast, and it avoids praising oneself. In these scholars' view, he says, refusing the exception is linked to the position of the [[group/murjia|Murji'a]], who exclude action from faith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 137} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 138} He quotes Ibn Taymiyya as saying that the early hadith scholars, such as Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Uyayna and Ahmad ibn Hanbal, made the exception in faith. According to this quotation, this is mass-transmitted from them, and they made it because faith includes doing what is obligatory, so they would not testify to that for themselves. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 139}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Hanbali): Ibn Batta in al-Ibana.** The believer makes the exception out of apprehension and to avoid praising himself, not from doubt in his faith. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 864}

**Sunni: the view that distinguishes, as reported by the editor.** [[person/al-ajurri|Al-Ajurri]] places the exception in deeds, not in belief or speech: one makes the exception about the completeness of one's faith, not its validity. [[person/ibn-abi-al-izz-al-hanafi|The commentator on the Tahawiyya]] holds that those who allow it have the better evidence. In his view it is forbidden if one means doubt about the root of faith, and allowed if one means the complete faith described in the Qur'an or simply refers the matter to God's will. The editor counts Ibn Batta and al-Baghawi among the holders of this view. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 862} {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 863}

**The view that forbids it, as reported by the editor.** Some forbade the exception because it implies doubt in faith; the editor does not name them here. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 862}

**The view that requires it, as reported by the editor.** Some made it obligatory and counted anyone who did not make it an innovator. {cite:ibana-kubra-muti-iman p. 862}
:::
