## Summary
At the end of "the proof that faith increases and decreases" [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] gathers reports that deny faith to one who commits certain grave sins or call an act unbelief, and [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|al-Khattabi]] explains how scholars read them, noting that [[group/khawarij|the Kharijites]] cite them wrongly. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44-45}

## The reports
- [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn 'Umar]]: "Do not turn after me into unbelievers, striking one another's necks." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44}
- Ibn 'Umar: "Whatever Muslim calls another Muslim an unbeliever: if he is one, so be it; otherwise he himself is the unbeliever." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44}
- 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr: "Four traits make whoever has them a pure hypocrite… when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, when he makes a pact he betrays it, and when he quarrels he is wicked." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44}
- [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]: "The adulterer is not a believer while he commits adultery, the thief is not a believer while he steals, the drinker is not a believer while he drinks wine; and repentance remains open afterwards." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44-45}
- Abu Hurayra: "When a man commits adultery, faith leaves him and is over him like a canopy; when he stops, faith returns to him." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 45}

## Al-Khattabi's reading
- **"The adulterer is not a believer"**: scholars read it in two ways: as a prohibition in the form of a statement — let him not commit adultery or steal, these being acts unbecoming to believers — or as a threat meant to deter rather than to take effect, like "there is no faith in one who has no trustworthiness" and "he is no Muslim whose neighbour is not safe from his harm", all of it in the sense of deterrence or denial of excellence and perfection, not of the reality of faith. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 45} He adds a third reading from the "canopy" report that Abu Dawud gives in the same chapter. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 45}
- **"Do not turn after me into unbelievers"**: he gives two readings: "men covered in weapons", from *takaffara* "to cover oneself with one's weapon", the root sense of *kufr* being covering; or "do not become factions striking one another's necks", like unbelievers who are enemies of one another. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44} He relays from Ibrahim ibn Firas that he asked Musa ibn Harun about it and was told: these are the apostates whom [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] fought. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 44}

:::perspectives
**Sunni: al-Khattabi and the scholars he cites.** One who commits a grave sin does not leave faith; the denial of faith in these reports is a prohibition in the form of a statement, or a deterring threat that denies perfection and excellence, not the reality of faith; and repentance remains open. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 45}

**Another view, in its critic's words: the Kharijites as al-Khattabi reports them.** The Kharijites and those who follow them in declaring Muslims unbelievers for sins cite "the adulterer is not a believer…" and, in al-Khattabi's view, interpret it wrongly. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v5 p. 45}
:::
