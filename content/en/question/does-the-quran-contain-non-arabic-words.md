## Summary

In the introduction to his commentary [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]] has a chapter titled 'Whether there are words in the Qur'an outside the languages of the Arabs, or not'. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.110} He states that the community does not disagree that the Qur'an contains no speech composed in non-Arab styles, and that it contains proper names of people whose tongue was not Arabic, such as Isra'il, Jibril, Imran, Nuh and Lut; the disagreement concerns single words that are not names. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.110}

## Ibn Atiyya's argument

Ibn Atiyya argues, as al-Qurtubi quotes him, that an Arab might not know one of these words just as he might not know plain words of another dialect — as Ibn Abbas did not know the meaning of 'fatir' until he heard two Bedouins use it. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.111}

## Disagreement and views

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Baqillani and al-Tabari**: no foreign words; the languages coincided {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.110}.
- **Sunni: some scholars**: a few words from other languages occur {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.110} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.111}.
- **Sunni: Ibn Atiyya, as al-Qurtubi quotes him**: foreign in origin and Arabized; coincidence is rare {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.111}.
:::

## Notes for researchers

- The chapter's examples of words ascribed to other languages come from Qur'anic verses; their wording is not reproduced here.
