## Short answer
The obligatory prayer is five times a day and night, and it is one of the [[concept/five-pillars|five pillars of Islam]] {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 8}. The Qur'an calls it "a decreed book", meaning an obligation with set times, and [[passage/quran-4-103|An-Nisa 103]] is the verse that establishes this.

## Detailed answer
The root of the obligation is in the Qur'an, while the number of prayers and their times were explained by the Sunna. The narration of Jibril in al-Bukhari reports that he led the Prophet in prayer at the beginning and the end of the time on two days, explaining the times {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 50}.

## Common questions
- The five daily prayers are the obligatory ones, and anything beyond them is supererogatory.
- The schools of law differ on the details of the times and the rulings, and this is set out in the entry [[practice/salah|Prayer]].

## Not covered here
The rulings on prayer, its conditions and its pillars, and the details of disagreement between the schools, will be added later from the books of jurisprudence.
