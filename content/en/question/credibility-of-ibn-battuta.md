## Summary
After his return to Fez, [[person/ibn-battuta-muhammad|Ibn Battuta]] told stories of the kings and marvels of the world, most of them about the realm of the ruler of India, and some listeners found them hard to believe. This question gathers three voices from within the Rihla and its printed edition: Ibn Battuta himself, his redactor [[person/ibn-juzayy-muhammad|Ibn Juzayy]], and [[person/ibn-khaldun|Ibn Khaldun]] as quoted by the corrector of the Cairo edition. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.313}

:::perspectives
**Ibn Battuta (Maliki, the traveller):** he says most of what he reports of Sultan [[person/muhammad-ibn-tughluq|Muhammad Shah]] he witnessed during his stay in the sultan's lands, and swears by God, His angels and His messengers that what he relates of the sultan's extraordinary generosity is true, even if some of it is beyond many people's belief, adding that most of it is established by widespread report in the East. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.354} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.355}

**Ibn Juzayy (Maliki, the redactor):** he says he included every story and report the shaykh told, "and I did not undertake to investigate or test their truth", adding that the shaykh vouched for the sound ones in the soundest way and freed himself of responsibility for the rest by wording that signals it. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.14} {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.15}

**Ibn Khaldun (Maliki, in the Muqaddima):** he says that a man of the elders of Tangier known as Ibn Battuta came to the Maghrib in the reign of Sultan [[person/abu-inan-faris|Abu Inan]] after twenty years in the East, telling of the marvels of the king of India, such as counting the people of his city when he travelled and paying them six months' provisions, and setting up catapults on his return to throw purses of dirhams and dinars to the crowd, "so people at court whispered that he was lying". {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.313} He relates that he met the vizier Faris ibn Wadrar and showed his disbelief, and the vizier warned him not to deny what he had not seen, telling of a vizier's son raised in prison who knew no meat but mouse and so imagined sheep, cattle and camels as mice. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.313} Ibn Khaldun then lays down that one should distinguish the possible from the impossible by sound reason, meaning possibility according to a thing's matter, kind, size and strength, not absolute logical possibility. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.314}
:::
