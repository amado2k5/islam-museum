## Summary

In the sixth aim of [[work/irshad-al-fuhul|Irshad al-Fuhul]], [[person/al-shawkani|al-Shawkani]] takes up the question whether an age may be empty of independent reasoners (mujtahids). He reports two views and then answers those who held that the age was empty. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255}

## Ijtihad as a collective duty

Al-Shawkani notes that a group held that no time may be without a mujtahid upholding God's proofs and explaining to people what was revealed to them, and that some required one in every region sufficient for the need, since ijtihad is a collective obligation; he quotes a division of ijtihad into an individual duty, a collective duty and a recommended act. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} He holds that calling ijtihad an obligation entails that no time is without a mujtahid, and cites the hadith "a group of my community will remain manifest upon the truth until the Hour comes". {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255}

## The view that it cannot

Al-Shawkani reports that for the Hanbalis no age may be without [[concept/ijtihad|ijtihad]]; Abu Ishaq and al-Zubayri affirmed this, and Abu Ishaq attributed it to the jurists, reasoning that if God left a time without anyone upholding the proof, legal obligation would lapse, since obligation is established only by a manifest proof. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} He quotes al-Zubayri that the earth will never be without someone upholding God's proof, however few. [[person/ibn-daqiq-al-id|Ibn Daqiq al-Id]] chose this view, but only up to the point where the order of things breaks down as the world ends. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255}

## The view that it can

He reports from [[person/badr-al-din-al-zarkashi|al-Zarkashi]]'s al-Bahr that most allowed an age to be without a mujtahid, and that the author of al-Mahsul, [[person/fakhr-al-din-al-razi|al-Razi]], affirmed it. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} He quotes [[person/al-rafii-al-qazwini|al-Rafi'i]] that people were all but agreed there was no mujtahid in his day, and [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|al-Ghazali]] in al-Wasit that the age had become empty of the independent mujtahid; al-Zarkashi remarked that claiming agreement was strange, since the question was disputed between the Shafi'is and the Hanbalis. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} Al-Shawkani adds that al-Qaffal preceded al-Ghazali in saying the age was empty, but contradicted himself by saying he did not imitate al-Shafi'i, his opinion merely agreeing with his. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255}

## Al-Shawkani's answer

Al-Shawkani finds the claim astonishing: if it refers to the claimants' own contemporaries, al-Qaffal, al-Ghazali, al-Razi and al-Rafi'i lived alongside scholars who fully mastered the sciences of ijtihad, and others came after them who knew still more. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255} {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256} If it means that God withdrew from later generations the understanding he gave earlier ones, or that knowledge was easier for the earlier ones, he calls that an empty claim: Qur'an commentaries and the Sunna had been written down, the imams had discussed authentication and preference beyond what a mujtahid needs, and the early generations used to travel from land to land for a single hadith; so ijtihad, he argues, is easier for later scholars. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256} He names, among Shafi'is after those who claimed the age empty, a chain in which each was the student of the one before: [[person/izz-al-din-ibn-abd-al-salam|Ibn Abd al-Salam]], [[person/ibn-daqiq-al-id|Ibn Daqiq al-Id]], [[person/ibn-sayyid-al-nas|Ibn Sayyid al-Nas]], [[person/al-iraqi-zayn-al-din|Zayn al-Din al-Iraqi]], [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani]] and [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]]. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (the Hanbalis and those who agreed): it cannot.** No age may be without a mujtahid upholding the proof, since obligation rests on a proof; affirmed by Abu Ishaq and al-Zubayri and chosen by Ibn Daqiq al-Id up to the great signs of the Hour. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255}

**Sunni (Shafi'is, as al-Zarkashi reports): it can.** An age may be without a mujtahid; this is the view of most according to al-Zarkashi and was affirmed by the author of al-Mahsul, while al-Ghazali, al-Qaffal and al-Rafi'i spoke of the independent mujtahid as having in fact disappeared. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.255}

**Al-Shawkani in Irshad al-Fuhul.** The claim of an empty age is unfounded; with the sciences written down, ijtihad is easier for later scholars than for earlier ones. {cite:irshad-al-fuhul-dar-al-fikr p.256}
:::
