## The reports
In the notice of [[person/muhammad-ibn-shihab-al-zuhri|al-Zuhri]] in [[work/al-tabaqat-al-kubra-ibn-sad|al-Tabaqat al-Kubra]], [[person/muhammad-ibn-sad|Ibn Sa'd]] gives two reports on his writing down of knowledge:
- From 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Salih ibn Kaysan: "Al-Zuhri and I met while seeking knowledge and wrote down what came from the Prophet. Then al-Zuhri said: let us write what came from his Companions, for it is sunna. I said: it is not sunna. He wrote and I did not; he succeeded and I lost." {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 168}
- From 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri: "We used to dislike writing down knowledge until these rulers compelled us to it; then we decided not to withhold it from any Muslim." {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 169}

Ibn Sa'd also relates that al-Zuhri attached himself to the Umayyad caliphs from 'Abd al-Malik to Hisham, that Yazid ibn 'Abd al-Malik made him a judge, and that Hisham placed him with his sons to teach them law and hadith and go on pilgrimage with them. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 162}

## The readings
:::perspectives
- **Sunni — the reading of the editor of the completing section**: al-Zuhri did not want hadith written down lest people rely on books instead of memory. He began writing when Hisham ibn 'Abd al-Malik pressed him to dictate for his sons, and then went out to people with his well-known saying, which ends with their decision not to withhold knowledge from any Muslim. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 168-169}
- **Academic — Goldziher's reading as the editor describes it**: the editor says [[person/ignaz-goldziher|Ignaz Goldziher]] quoted the saying as "these rulers compelled us to write hadiths", altered and cut short, to cast doubt on al-Zuhri and suggest he was ready to serve the rulers' wishes. This reading is given here in its critic's words and has not yet been taken from Goldziher's own work. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 169}
:::

## A point to notice
The disagreement turns on the wording. In Ibn Sa'd's report the saying speaks of "writing down knowledge" and ends "then we decided not to withhold it from any Muslim"; the wording the editor ascribes to Goldziher speaks of "writing hadiths" and lacks the ending. {cite:tabaqat-ibn-sad-qism-mutammim-mansur-1987 p. 169}
