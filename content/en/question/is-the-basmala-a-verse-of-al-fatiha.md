## Summary
[[concept/al-basmala|The basmala]] is written at the head of al-Fatiha in the copies of the Qur'an, but readers and jurists disagree whether it counts as one of its verses. This entry sets out the disagreement as [[person/al-baydawi-abd-allah-ibn-umar|al-Baydawi]] reports it in [[work/anwar-al-tanzil|Anwar al-Tanzil]]. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6}

## The positions
Al-Baydawi reports that it is part of al-Fatiha for the readers and jurists of Mecca and Kufa, and for Ibn al-Mubarak and [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]]; that the readers and jurists of Medina, Basra and Syria, [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]] and al-Awza'i disagreed; and that [[person/abu-hanifa|Abu Hanifa]] said nothing explicit, so it was thought that for him it is not part of the sura, while [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-hasan-al-shaybani|Muhammad ibn al-Hasan]], asked about it, said: what lies between the two covers is God's speech. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6}

## Al-Baydawi's argument
Al-Baydawi argues for the first view from hadith, among them Abu Hurayra's report that al-Fatiha is seven verses, the first of them the basmala, and Umm Salama's report that the Messenger of God recited al-Fatiha and counted the basmala as a verse; he says these are why it was debated whether it is a verse by itself or together with what follows, and notes the consensus that what lies between the covers is God's speech and the agreement to write it in the copies. {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6} The basmala at the head of Surat al-Tawba is a separate question; see [[question/why-no-basmala-in-surat-al-tawba|Why does Surat al-Tawba not open with the basmala?]].

## Disagreement and views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni (Shafi'i): al-Shafi'i and the readers of Mecca and Kufa, argued for by al-Baydawi**: the basmala is a verse of al-Fatiha {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6}.
- **Sunni (Maliki): Malik, al-Awza'i and the readers of Medina, Basra and Syria**: it is not part of al-Fatiha {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6}.
- **Sunni (Hanafi): Abu Hanifa and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan**: Abu Hanifa said nothing explicit; Muhammad ibn al-Hasan said what lies between the covers is God's speech {cite:anwar-al-tanzil-fleischer-v1 p.6}.
- **Not yet given here**: the Hanbali position and academic studies of verse counting have not yet been added; later books will supply them.
:::
