## The question
Did the Prophet see his Lord in this world, on the night of [[question/what-is-isra-miraj|the Night Journey and Ascension]]? Sunni scholars agree that the believers will see their Lord in the hereafter ([[concept/ruyat-allah|the vision of God in the hereafter]]). The Prophet's vision of his Lord in this world, says the editor of [[work/tawhid-ibn-khuzayma|Kitab al-Tawhid]], is a question disputed among Sunnis, on which the Companions themselves differed: affirmation is reported from [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn 'Abbas]] and his circle, Ka'b al-Ahbar and [[person/abu-dharr-al-ghifari|Abu Dharr]], denial from [[person/aisha|'A'isha]] and [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]], and both views from Abu Hurayra. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 477}

## How Ibn Khuzayma presents it
[[person/ibn-khuzayma-muhammad|Ibn Khuzayma]] devotes chapter 47 to "the affirmation of the Prophet's vision of his Creator", saying God singled him out with the vision as He singled out Abraham with close friendship, since God raised some messengers above others. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 477} The first report in it is Ibn 'Abbas's saying: "Do you wonder that close friendship should be Abraham's, speech Moses' and the vision Muhammad's?" {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 479} A following chapter gathers the reports from 'A'isha denying it. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 548}

On the verses of al-Najm, he takes Ibn Mas'ud's reports to show that [[passage/quran-53-18|al-Najm 18]] is the vision of Gabriel, and finds it "not objectionable" that [[passage/quran-53-13|al-Najm 13]] means what Ibn 'Abbas said, that the Prophet saw his Lord twice. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 508} He then grants that it is established that the Prophet, asked about this verse, said he had seen Gabriel in his true form, but adds: "It is possible that the Prophet saw his Lord, as Ibn 'Abbas reported." {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 519} Abu Dharr's report, he says, was interpreted differently by their scholars because its wording can bear both denial and affirmation. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 508}

## The views
:::perspectives
- **Ibn Khuzayma (Sunni, Shafi'i): affirmation, following Ibn 'Abbas.** Here, as his editor says, he leans towards the view that the Prophet saw his Lord, arguing at length from Ibn 'Abbas's unqualified reports. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 479} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 508}
- **A vision of the heart (Sunni): he saw Him with his heart, not his eyes.** The editor quotes [[person/ibn-hajar-al-asqalani|Ibn Hajar]] reconciling Ibn 'Abbas's affirmation and 'A'isha's denial by taking the denial as of eyesight and the affirmation as of the heart, and reading Ibn 'Abbas's unqualified reports by the qualified ones; and [[person/ibn-qayyim-al-jawziyya|Ibn al-Qayyim]] that [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]] said once "Muhammad saw his Lord" and once "with his heart", and that whoever reported him as saying "with the eyes of his head" altered the report. The editor prefers this view and reads the verse of al-Najm as the vision of Gabriel. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 478-479} {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 508}
- **Denial (Sunni): he did not see Him in this world.** This is reported from 'A'isha and Ibn Mas'ud; 'A'isha argued from [[passage/quran-42-51|al-Shura 51]] and [[passage/quran-6-103|al-An'am 103]]. The editor says her argument was not accepted and that Ibn Khuzayma discusses it, that her denial does not imply the vision is impossible, and quotes al-Qadi 'Iyad that the vision is rationally possible and that there is no decisive text that the Prophet saw Him with his eyes. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 478}
- **Suspension (Sunni, Maliki).** A group suspended judgement because there is no decisive proof: each side relies on apparent meanings that conflict and admit interpretation, while a question of creed needs decisive proof; al-Qurtubi preferred this in al-Mufhim. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 477-478}
:::

## A note on the evidence
The editor observes that the reports from the Prophet adduced for affirmation come from only two Companions, Ibn 'Abbas and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'A'ish al-Hadrami, and that al-Hadrami's report has defects — his status as a Companion is disputed and its chain varies in three forms, though al-Bukhari and al-Tirmidhi judged one of them sound — while the evidence for a vision of the heart comes from more Companions. {cite:kitab-al-tawhid-ibn-khuzayma-shahwan-1988 p. 478}
