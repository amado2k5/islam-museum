## Summary

A modern question: money placed in bank and post-office savings funds, or used to buy investment certificates, earns its owners a profit fixed in advance; is that profit forbidden [[concept/riba|riba]] or lawful? [[person/ahmad-shalabi-historian|Ahmad Shalabi]] treats it in [[work/al-iqtisad-fi-al-fikr-al-islami|al-Iqtisad fi al-Fikr al-Islami]], noting that Muslims dealing in this way number in the hundreds of millions, so the subject must be studied carefully and repeatedly. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.123}

## The views

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: the prohibiting view, as Shalabi reports it**: Shalabi notes that some researchers hold this dealing forbidden, and quotes Shaykh Abd Allah al-Mashadd that some researchers consider it forbidden and others lawful; among the reasons of those inclined to prohibition he cites that the money earned profit without work, or without risk. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.140} {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.141} This view is not given here from its holders' own books or from the resolutions of fiqh academies that adopted it (needs_source).
- **Sunni: the permitting view, Shalabi's and those he cites**: Shalabi holds that the Qur'an, the commentators, the hadiths and the jurists tie riba to loans, that contracts and conditions are permissible in principle unless a text forbids them, compares the return with partnership profit and with the rent of houses and land, and suggests it may compensate for the collapse of the currency. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.13} {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.140} {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.142} He reports the permissive view from Abd al-Rahman Isa; from Ali al-Khafif, who held dealing with the savings fund not usurious because the depositor can withdraw his money at will; from a study presented by Ali al-Khafif and Yasin Suwaylim at the Islamic Research Academy conference in Cairo in 1972; from Abd al-Mun'im al-Nimr; and from the Academy's committee for juristic research at al-Azhar, where in meetings of April and May 1976 most representatives of the four schools decided that the profits of the National Bank's investment certificates are not riba, since exploitation and the chance of loss are absent, some of them holding them a lawful partnership. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.144} {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.145} He cites passages of Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim and Muhammad Abduh on removing hardship and tying riba to its purpose. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.142}
- **Academic view: not yet sourced (needs_source)**.
:::

## Shalabi on the disagreement

Shalabi holds that disagreement where there is no text is natural, citing Zayd ibn Thabit's reasoning on the mother's inheritance, that the plurality of schools is a blessing that should not turn into conflict, and that asking everyone to agree on one ruling is asking the impossible. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.124} {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.125} He says Islam "is not run by academies" as some religions are; Islamic thought welcomes consensus where possible but gives the deeply learned Muslim the right to independent reasoning, for which he cites the hadith of Mu'adh ibn Jabal. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.125} He describes categorical prohibition as disrespectful of others' views and quotes Shaykh Abd Allah al-Mashadd that a Muslim may choose either view without blame. {cite:iqtisad-fikr-islami-shalabi-1993 p.141} These are the author's views; the museum reports them as his and does not choose between the positions.
