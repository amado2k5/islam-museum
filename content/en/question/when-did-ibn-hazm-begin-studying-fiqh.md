## Summary

Biographies circulate a report that [[person/ibn-hazm|Ibn Hazm]] came late to the study of law, at twenty-six; the editors of [[work/al-muhalla-ibn-hazm|al-Muhalla]] discuss it in their introduction, quoting modern scholars who reject it. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.25} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.26} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.27}

## The well-known report

Historians relate that Ibn Hazm attended a funeral and, on returning, went into the mosque and began to pray, after the afternoon prayer; he was told: "Sit down, this is not a time for prayer." He was then twenty-six. He left upset and asked the teacher who had raised him to direct him to the house of the jurist Abu Abd Allah ibn Dahhun, who set him to the Muwatta'; he read it and other books with him and others for about three years, then began to debate. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.25} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.26} Another story has him objecting in a class to an answer on a legal question, being told "this science is not your trade", devoting himself to it, and returning months later to debate, saying: "I follow the truth and reason for myself, bound to no school." {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.25}

## Objections to the age

Shaykh Muhammad Abu Zahra holds that this age does not fit the historical setting: he is known to have learned hadith from Ahmad ibn al-Jasur, and it is impossible that one who knew how to relate hadith did not know the greeting prayer of the mosque; he was also taken to the gatherings of great scholars, and no one would speak of a guide raising a man of twenty-six who had reached the rank of vizier. The reasonable reading, he says, is sixteen, with a copyist's slip. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.26} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.27} Muhammad al-Manuni, in Shuyukh Ibn Hazm, took the same view with five arguments, and the editors note that al-Dhahabi in al-Ibar dates his first hearing to 399 AH, which would make him fifteen or sixteen. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.27} The editors add that even if the report were true it would not detract from his standing. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.28}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (the report as historians give it): twenty-six.** He began studying law after being corrected for praying after the afternoon prayer, and read the Muwatta' with Ibn Dahhun. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.25} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.26}

**Academic view (Muhammad Abu Zahra and Muhammad al-Manuni): the age is wrong.** Sixteen is likelier, given his early hearing from Ibn al-Jasur, his attendance at scholars' gatherings, and al-Ibar's date of 399 AH for his first hearing. {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.26} {cite:muhalla-dar-ibn-hazm-2016-v1 p.27}
:::
