## Summary
If we say the Companions wrote the codex in a particular rasm, did they take it from the Prophet or write as they were used to writing? This is what, for the scholars, divides those who make the rasm a legal foundation from those who make it a respected convention. Al-Sabt takes it under 'Is the rasm of the codex tawqifi?', and Ibn Khaldun gives his view in 'Section thirty: that writing is among the human crafts'. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 317} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 416}

## Ibn Khaldun and the origin of writing
Ibn Khaldun begins by saying that writing is forms and letter-shapes indicating spoken words, a noble craft among human distinctions, and that excellence of writing in a city follows its civilisation. He then follows Arabic script: it was firm under the Tubba's, the Himyari script, and passed to al-Hira, from where, as reported, the people of al-Ta'if and Quraysh were taught it; it is said Sufyan ibn Umayya or Harb ibn Umayya learned writing from al-Hira. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 416} He cites what he saw in Ibn al-Abbar's al-Takmila, a report from Abdallah ibn Abbas on the origin of Arabic writing, passing through Harb ibn Umayya, Abdallah ibn Jud'an and the people of al-Anbar to al-Khulajan ibn Qasim, scribe of revelation for Hud, in which Quraysh wrote the alif, lam, mim and nun joined and separate. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 417}

## His view of the rasm of the codex
He says: look at what resulted in their writing of the codex, which the Companions wrote in their own hands, not yet perfected in skill, so that much of their spelling departed from what the analogies of the writer's craft require among its practitioners. Then the Successors followed their writing for blessing from what the Messenger's Companions wrote, as in our day the hand of a saint or scholar is followed for blessing and its spelling followed, right or wrong; and how does that compare with the Companions in what they wrote? So it was followed and fixed as rasm, and the scholars of the rasm noted its places. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 417} Then he warns: 'do not turn to what some simpletons claim, that they were masters of the craft of writing', counts the explanations given for adding or dropping a letter as arbitrary, and says being unlettered was a perfection for the Prophet but not for us, and that perfection in crafts is relative. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 418}

## What al-Sabt answers
Al-Sabt answers those who treat the rasm as conventional so as to allow changing it: the proofs for keeping the Uthmanic rasm are the consensus of the Companions and those after them, so al-Baqillani's 'for this reason the scripts of the codices differed' cannot be granted once consensus is formed. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 322} He separates two questions: is it tawqifi, and must it be kept? He says those who made keeping it obligatory did not require tawqif. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 319} See [[question/may-the-mushaf-be-written-in-imlai-spelling|May the mushaf be written in modern spelling?]]

## On scribes' errors
Al-Suyuti records reports from A'isha and Ibn Abbas saying that in some places the scribes erred in spelling, and the edition's editors comment that some are weak in chain, that Ibn Kathir called one of the Ibn Abbas reports 'very strange' because it opposes the mass-transmitted reading and written rasm on which the Companions and those after them agreed, and that al-Tabari says of the reading the cities follow that it is by the agreement of the authorities among readers and its accord with the script of the Muslims' codices. {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 1250} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 1251} These reports raise the matter from another side; I give no verdict on them here and do not copy the Qur'anic wording they touch, only record their existence and the editors' comment.

## What I leave aside
I give Ibn Khaldun's view from the text of his Muqaddima itself, and the views of al-Baqillani, al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam and al-Marrakushi through al-Sabt and al-Suyuti; I set out no proofs of those who say tawqif because I found no names in what I read. The museum sets out the views and does not choose.
