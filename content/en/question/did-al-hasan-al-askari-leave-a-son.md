## Summary
At the end of his account of [[concept/twelve-imams|the twelve imams]], [[person/al-allama-al-hilli|Ibn al-Mutahhar al-Hilli]] named the twelfth, Muhammad ibn al-Hasan, the Mahdi of the [[group/imamiyya|Imamiyya]]. [[person/ibn-taymiyya|Ibn Taymiyya]] devoted a chapter of [[work/minhaj-al-sunnah|Minhaj al-Sunna]] to this, discussing his existence, his [[concept/ghayba|occultation]] and the meaning of his imamate. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1078}

## What Ibn Taymiyya quotes from al-Hilli
In Ibn Taymiyya's quotation al-Hilli says: "and his son, our master the Mahdi Muhammad", and relates from Ibn al-Jawzi, with his chain to Ibn Umar, that the Prophet said: "A man of my descendants will come forth at the end of time; his name is my name and his kunya my kunya; he will fill the earth with justice as it was filled with injustice – that is the Mahdi." {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1078} Ibn Taymiyya relates that those Imamis who hold that [[person/al-hasan-al-askari|al-Hasan al-Askari]] had a son say he entered the cellar at Samarra as a small child, and differ about his age: some say two years, some three, some five. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1079} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1080}

## Ibn Taymiyya's answer
**Lineage.** Ibn Taymiyya reports that Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari, Abd al-Baqi ibn Qani' and other experts in genealogy and history recorded that al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Askari had no offspring or descendants. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1078} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1079}

**The orphaned child.** He holds that if such a child existed and were known, the ruling of the Qur'an, Sunna and consensus would be that he be placed in the custody of a guardian, and that his property be held by his father's executor, a relative or the ruler's deputy, since he was an orphan; he cites the verse on handing orphans their property once they show maturity. How then could one who must be restricted in person and property be the infallible imam of all Muslims? {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1080}

**The purposes of the imamate.** He says that the Imamiyya agree – whether his existence is supposed or not – that they gain nothing from him in religion or worldly life and that he has taught no one anything, so none of the purposes of the imamate is achieved through him; he sees this as contradicting their doctrine of [[question/must-the-imam-be-infallible|the divine favour of an infallible imam]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1080} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1081} To the reply that he hid because of people's injustice, he answers that there was injustice in his forefathers' time and they did not hide; that his believers have filled the earth, so why has he not met them or sent someone to teach them; and that he could have taken refuge in places where his partisans lived. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1081}

**Long life.** He says they have prayed for his coming forth for more than four hundred and fifty years, and that a Muslim surviving so long is known to be false by the constant course of nature: no one is known to have been born in Islam and lived a hundred and twenty years. He cites the hadith of Ibn Umar in the Sahih that the Prophet said near the end of his life that at the head of a hundred years no one then on the face of the earth would remain, and holds that lifespans grow shorter as time goes on. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1081} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1082}

## The editor's footnote
In a footnote the editor gives the report of al-Qurtubi in his continuation of al-Tabari's history that in the time of al-Muqtadir a man claimed to be Muhammad ibn al-Hasan and Ibn Tumar, the marshal of the Talibids, told him that al-Hasan had left no son; and he quotes Ahmad Subhi's *Nazariyyat al-Imama* that the Shi'a split into many sects after al-Hasan al-Askari's death, some holding that he left no son, and that Ja'far ibn Ali, al-Hasan's brother, claimed his inheritance. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1079} He also gives from al-Kulayni's [[work/al-kafi|al-Kafi]] the account of Ahmad ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Khaqan on the search of al-Hasan al-Askari's house at his death and the division of his estate between his mother and his brother [[person/jafar-ibn-ali-al-hadi|Ja'far]]. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1079} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1080}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Hanbali): Ibn Taymiyya in Minhaj al-Sunna.** According to the genealogists al-Hasan al-Askari left no son; an orphaned child cannot be an infallible imam; an absent one fulfils no purpose of the imamate; and his surviving so long contradicts the course of nature. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1079} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1080} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1081}

**Imami: Ibn al-Mutahhar al-Hilli, as Ibn Taymiyya quotes him.** Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Askari is the Mahdi promised in the hadith, who went into occultation as a child at Samarra. {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1078} {cite:minhaj-sunna-shamela p. 1079}
:::
