## Summary

Dar al-Ma'rifa prints al-Zamakhshari's [[work/al-kashshaf|al-Kashshaf]] with Ibn al-Munayyir's al-Intisaf in its footnotes, so the debate between them appears on the same page. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} On Moses' request to see God in [[passage/quran-7-143|al-A'raf 143]], al-Zamakhshari sets out his argument in his 'if you say… I say' manner, and Ibn al-Munayyir answers him note by note. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.385}

## God's speech to Moses

At the same place al-Zamakhshari says that God's speaking to Moses was His creating the speech, uttered, in some body, as He created it written on the Tablet; Ibn al-Munayyir sees in this an open statement of the createdness of God's speech, the Mu'tazili doctrine, and argues that the verse is framed as a favour to Moses in singling him out for speech — if he had heard letters created in a body, everyone who heard speech would equal him — so Moses heard the eternal speech subsisting in God's essence without intermediary; and as God's essence may be seen though He is not a body, His speech may be heard though it is neither letter nor sound. {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384}

## Disagreement and views

:::perspectives
- **Sunni (Maliki): Ibn al-Munayyir in al-Intisaf**: the vision is possible, Moses asked for himself, and 'lan' emphasises the denial without making the thing impossible {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.385}.
- **Other view (Mu'tazili): al-Zamakhshari in al-Kashshaf**: the vision is incompatible with God's attributes; Moses' request was to silence his people {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.384} {cite:kashshaf-dar-al-marifa-shiha-2009 p.385}.
:::
