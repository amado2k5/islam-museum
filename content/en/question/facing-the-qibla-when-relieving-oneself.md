## Summary
In the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan]], [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] gives a chapter on the dislike of facing [[concept/qibla|the qibla]] when relieving oneself, followed by a chapter on the permission to do so; in his commentary [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|al-Khattabi]] brings the two chapters together and relates the two views on them. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 19} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 21-22}

## The reports of prohibition
- [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman]]: he was told, "Your Prophet has taught you everything, even how to relieve yourselves!" He said, "Yes: he forbade us to face the qibla when defecating or urinating, to cleanse ourselves with the right hand, or to cleanse ourselves with fewer than three stones." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 19-20}
- [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]: "I am to you like a father teaching you: when one of you goes to relieve himself, let him neither face the qibla nor turn his back to it." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20} Al-Khattabi calls this a word of ease and reassurance, so that they would not be shy of asking him, as a child is not shy of asking his father. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20}
- [[person/abu-ayyub-al-ansari|Abu Ayyub]]: "When you go to relieve yourselves, do not face the qibla… but face east or west." He said: we came to [[place/al-sham|Syria]] and found latrines built facing the qibla, and we would turn away from it and ask God's forgiveness. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20-21} Al-Khattabi says "face east or west" is addressed to the people of Medina and those whose qibla lies in the same direction; those whose qibla lies east or west do not turn east or west. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20}
- The report of Ma'qil al-Asadi mentions "the two qiblas", which al-Khattabi explains as [[building/kaaba|the Ka'ba]] and [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]; the prohibition may be because whoever faces Jerusalem from Medina turns his back on the Ka'ba, or out of respect for Jerusalem as a former qibla. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 21} (The text of this report is garbled in the machine-read scan; the footnote has been relied on.)

## The reports of permission
- Marwan al-Asfar: he saw [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Ibn 'Umar]] make his camel kneel facing the qibla and then sit urinating towards it; asked about it, he said, "That was forbidden only in open country; if there is something between you and the qibla that screens you, there is no harm." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 21-22}
- Ibn 'Umar: he climbed onto the roof of the house and saw the Prophet on two bricks facing Jerusalem while relieving himself. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 22}
- [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah]]: the Prophet forbade us to face the qibla when urinating, then I saw him facing it a year before he died; the annotator quotes al-Tirmidhi calling it *hasan gharib*. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 22}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Maliki and Shafi'i): Ibn 'Umar, al-Sha'bi, Malik and al-Shafi'i, preferred by al-Khattabi.** The prohibition concerns open country; within buildings there is no harm. For al-Khattabi this view "is preferable, because it reconciles the differing reports and uses each of them". The reason given is that open ground is a place of prayer and worship for angels, humans and jinn, where one who sits is exposed to view, which does not apply within buildings. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 21}

**Sunni: Abu Ayyub al-Ansari and Sufyan al-Thawri.** The prohibition is general and makes no distinction between open country and buildings; hence Abu Ayyub turned away from the latrines built in Syria. Al-Khattabi considers that this view leaves some of the reports unused and set aside. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20-21}
:::

## Not covered here
Al-Khattabi does not give here the views of Abu Hanifa and Ahmad or their distinction between facing and turning one's back; these are to be added from their schools' books.
