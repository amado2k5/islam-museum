## Summary
The question is a practical one: may the mushaf today be printed in the spelling rules people use, instead of the rasm in which the Companions wrote it by Uthman's order? Khalid al-Sabt sets out three views in Tahdhib al-Manahil, and al-Suyuti relays the sayings of Malik, Ahmad and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 317} {cite:itqan-suyuti p. 2199}

## First view: it must be kept
Al-Sabt gathers the majority's evidence: that the Companions wrote the codices under Uthman in this rasm and agreed on it, that opening the door to departing from it is a means to tamper with the Qur'an, and that some rules of the rasm differ from land to land, which leads to confusion and dispute. He relays that Mus'ab ibn Sa'd said: I met the people when Uthman had the codices copied and it pleased them. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 317} Al-Sakhawi reports from Malik and judges his view right because it preserves the first state of things. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 318} Al-Sabt cites Nizam al-Din al-Naysaburi that readers, scholars and writers must follow this rasm because it is Zayd ibn Thabit's, who was the Messenger of God's confidant and writer of his revelation. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 319}
He notes that those who hold it obligatory differ on the grounds: some see it as fixed by the Prophet, which al-Sabt says their proofs do not establish; he judges it more likely not to be so fixed but to be kept for the reasons above. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 319} See [[question/is-the-rasm-tawqifi|Is the rasm tawqifi?]]

## Al-Sabt's reply to those who want ease
Al-Sabt answers the objection that many learners cannot read the codex because they do not know the rasm, with six points: the Uthmanic rasm has merits; the majority's view rests on ample evidence; writing conventions are open to change; submitting the codex to modern conventions may draw a trial like that of Uthman's time, as people or peoples might say to one another 'my rasm is better than yours'; the Uthmanic rasm is like a common script that unites the community in writing its Lord's Book across ages and lands, as Arabic does; and reading can be made easier by broadcasting the Qur'an precisely and teaching tajwid. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 320} He adds that al-Azhar magazine suggested noting at the foot of each page the words that depart from familiar spelling, and that the Uthmanic rasm departs from our rules of writing and spelling only a little, in a few words. {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 321}

## The other two views
The second view, which he ascribes to Ibn Khaldun and which al-Baqillani held eagerly: that the rasm of the codex is conventional. In the Muqaddima itself Ibn Khaldun says the Companions wrote the codex in their own hands, which were not perfected in craft, so much of their writing departed from the analogies of the writer's art, and then the Successors followed their writing for blessing from what the Messenger's Companions had written, so it was followed and fixed as the rasm, and the scholars of the rasm noted its places. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-khaldun p. 417} The third view, ascribed to al-Izz ibn Abd al-Salam as al-Zarkashi relays in al-Burhan, is given in the perspectives above. Al-Zarkashi said: 'this was so in the first age, when knowledge was alive and fresh; now confusion may be feared.' {cite:manahil-irfan-sabt p. 322}

## What I do not say
I relay these views from al-Sabt and al-Suyuti; I did not read al-Dani's al-Muqni', al-Baqillani's al-Intisar or al-Zarkashi's al-Burhan themselves, so I attribute to these men only what is quoted; the museum sets the views out and does not choose between them.
