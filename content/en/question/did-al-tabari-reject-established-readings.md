## Summary
In [[work/jami-al-bayan|Jami' al-Bayan]], [[person/al-tabari|al-Tabari]] stops at a plurality of [[concept/qiraat|readings]] in 854 places, explaining them and giving his view, at times agreeing with the readers and at times differing from some of them. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1365} Scholars differ on how to describe his practice: was he applying a standard accepted among the scholars of the readings, or did he reject readings the community held to be sound?

## Al-Tabari's principle in his book on the readings
[[person/makki-ibn-abi-talib|Makki ibn Abi Talib]] quotes in al-Ibana from al-Tabari's lost book on the readings: whatever is soundly known to have been taught by the Prophet to his community among the [[concept/al-ahruf-al-sabaa|seven ahruf]], no one may fault the one who reads it if it agrees with the script of the codex, and what departs from the script is not read, and one refrains from it and from discussing it. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1364} In the introduction to his commentary al-Tabari relates that when someone recited before [[person/abu-al-aliya-al-riyahi|Abu al-Aliya]], he would not say "it is not as he reads" but "as for me, I read thus", and that Ibrahim al-Nakha'i said: I think your companion had heard that whoever denies one harf of it denies all of it. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1364}

## His practice in Jami' al-Bayan
Ahmad Khalid Babakr concludes that al-Tabari's judgements on readings rest on three pillars: agreement with the script of the codex, the agreement of most readers, and conformity with the most eloquent speech of the Arabs. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1381} He agrees with the readers when two readings share a meaning, are equally well known or equally sound in the language — as when he says of [[passage/quran-3-48|Al Imran 48]] that the two readings do not differ in meaning, so whoever reads either is right. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1365} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1366} He rejects what one, two or three readers — sometimes four — read alone, because most readers read otherwise, whether or not that reader was later counted among the Seven or the Ten. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1382} The thesis gives examples at [[passage/quran-6-137|al-An'am 137]], [[passage/quran-3-181|Al Imran 181]], [[passage/quran-6-105|al-An'am 105]], [[passage/quran-2-140|al-Baqara 140]] and [[passage/quran-7-44|al-A'raf 44]]. {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1381} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1382}

## Disagreement and views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Tabari himself**: a sound reading agreeing with the script is not to be faulted, but a reading departing from what was transmitted openly and widely is a view contrary to the truth, and the preferred reading agrees with the script, most readers and the most eloquent Arabic {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1364} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1376} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1377} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1382}.
- **Sunni: Ibn al-Jazari in al-Nashr, as quoted in the thesis**: the imams of the readings act on what is best established in transmission, not on what is most regular in grammar; agreement with Arabic even in one respect is one condition of acceptance, and some made sound transmission the only condition {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.92} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1376}.
- **Academic: Ahmad Khalid Babakr (Umm al-Qura University)**: al-Tabari did not keep the principle of his book on the readings; he made language the judge and was at times harsh in rejecting readings sound in transmission and in Arabic and agreeing with the script, though he may be excused as trying to show that the variants agree with Arabic {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1365} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1376} {cite:qiraat-inda-al-tabari-thesis-umm-al-qura p.1377}.
:::
