## The question
In type forty of [[work/tadrib-al-rawi|Tadrib al-Rawi]], on knowing [[group/tabiun|the Successors]], [[person/al-nawawi|al-Nawawi]] reports views on who was the best of them; [[person/al-suyuti|al-Suyuti]] adds the discussion of al-Iraqi and al-Bulqini, and the editor Muhammad Awwama comments. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 257} It is a question of ranking in which the answers vary with the kind of excellence in view.

## The views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab (Ahmad, and the people of Medina)**: [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] is reported to have said that the best Successor is [[person/said-ibn-al-musayyab|Ibn al-Musayyab]]; asked "what of Alqama and al-Aswad?", he said: he and they. [[person/ibn-khafif|Ibn Khafif al-Shirazi]] reported that the people of Medina say the best is Ibn al-Musayyab. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 257} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 258}
- **Sunni: Uways al-Qarani (the people of Kufa, and al-Iraqi)**: Ibn Khafif reported that the people of Kufa say [[person/uwais-al-qarni|Uways]]. [[person/abd-al-rahim-al-iraqi|Al-Iraqi]] said the correct, indeed the right, view is the Kufans', because [[person/muslim-ibn-al-hajjaj|Muslim]] narrates in his Sahih from [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar]] that he heard the Prophet say: "The best of the Successors is a man called Uways"; this, he said, settles the dispute. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 258} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260}
- **Sunni: al-Hasan al-Basri (the people of Basra)**: Ibn Khafif reported that the people of Basra say [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]]; [[person/ibn-al-salah|Ibn al-Salah]] approved this division by city. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 258}
- **Sunni: other reports from Ahmad**: he is also reported to have said: I know of none among them like [[person/abu-uthman-al-nahdi|Abu Uthman al-Nahdi]] and [[person/qays-ibn-abi-hazim|Qays]]; and: the best of them are Qays, Abu Uthman, [[person/alqama-ibn-qays-al-nakhai|Alqama]] and [[person/masruq-ibn-al-ajda|Masruq]] — men of merit among the most eminent Successors. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 257}
- **Sunni: reconciliation (al-Bulqini)**: [[person/siraj-al-din-al-bulqini|Al-Bulqini]] said it is best to say: in asceticism and scrupulousness the best is Uways, and in preserving reports and traditions it is Sa'id. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260}
:::

## How Ahmad's preference was understood
Al-Iraqi offered three explanations for Ahmad's preferring Ibn al-Musayyab and others: perhaps the hadith had not reached him, or it was not sound in his view, or he meant superiority in knowledge rather than in goodness. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260} The editor Awwama finds the first two untenable, because Ahmad himself narrates the hadith in his Musnad, in the story of Umar searching among the Yemeni pilgrims until he met Uways, with the same chain as Muslim's; so only the last explanation, distinguishing superiority from goodness, remains. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 259} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260} He adds that what was reported from Malik casting doubt on Uways is what led al-Iraqi to these explanations, as a way of excusing both imams. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 259}

## Al-Dhahabi's view and the editor's comment
The editor notes that [[person/al-dhahabi|al-Dhahabi]] opens his entry on Uways in Tarikh al-Islam with "the master of the Successors" and closes it by saying that no Successor is better than he, though another may equal him in merit, such as Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, and such men are few. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 259} {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260} Awwama comments: in virtue, yes; but in knowledge and in benefiting the community through learning, preaching and standing firm for the truth before an unjust ruler, no — and he leaves the matter to God's knowledge. {cite:tadrib-rawi-awwama-v5 p. 260}
