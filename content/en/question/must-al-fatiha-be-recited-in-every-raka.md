## Summary

In the second chapter of his commentary on al-Fatiha, 'on its revelation and its rulings', [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]] sets out twenty questions, among them whether reciting it in the prayer is required. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.176} He quotes the jurists' views from [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Ibn Abd al-Barr]]'s al-Tamhid and al-Istidhkar and other works, then gives his preference. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.181} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.182}

## What is agreed

Al-Qurtubi reports the scholars' agreement that there is no prayer without recitation {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.191}, and rejects the report that Umar prayed the sunset prayer without reciting and asked 'how were the bowing and prostration?' as a report of objectionable wording and broken chain. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.191} He also reports a narration from Malik that recitation is required in no part of the prayer — which some of Malik's followers call objectionable — and that al-Shafi'i held something similar in Iraq about one who forgot it, then withdrew it in Egypt. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.190} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.191}

## Al-Qurtubi's argument

He cites Ibn al-Arabi: the negation in 'there is no prayer for one who does not recite al-Fatiha' can be read as denying completeness or validity, and the stronger reading is the general one, so the stronger narration from Malik is that the prayer of one who does not recite it is void; and whoever reads the words 'do this in all your prayer' as applying to it must repeat the recitation in each rak'a as he repeats the bowing and prostration. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.189}

## Disagreement and views

:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Malik, al-Shafi'i and Ahmad; al-Qurtubi's choice**: it is required in every rak'a for everyone praying {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.180} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.183}.
- **Sunni: al-Hasan al-Basri and most Basrans**: once in the prayer suffices {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.181}.
- **Sunni: Abu Hanifa, al-Thawri, al-Awza'i and Abu Hanifa's two companions**: it is not fixed and other Qur'an suffices; for the two companions the minimum is three verses or one long verse {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.181}.
- **Sunni: al-Tabari, with Ibn Abd al-Barr's reply**: recited in every rak'a, and if omitted only an equal amount suffices; Ibn Abd al-Barr rejects this {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 p.182}.
:::
