## The question
In the second point of the first type, Ibn al-Salah writes: when we find, in the hadith booklets or elsewhere, a hadith whose chain is sound, which we do not find in either of the two Sahihs and which no recognised, widely known work of the hadith imams declares sound, we do not venture to affirm that it is sound. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 16} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}

His reason is that in these ages every chain includes men who relied on what was in their books, without the memory, precision and mastery the sound requires. Knowledge of the sound and the good has therefore come to rest on the imams' works, whose fame keeps them safe from alteration and corruption. The main purpose of the chains still in circulation has become to keep alive the chain of transmission, a distinction of this community. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}

## The views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Ibn al-Salah**: one may not affirm soundness; one relies on what the imams stated. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 16} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}
- **Sunni: al-Nawawi and al-Iraqi, as the editor Itr quotes them**: al-Nawawi said, to me it is more evident that it is allowed to one who is capable and of strong knowledge; al-Iraqi said that al-Nawawi's preference is what hadith scholars act on. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}
- **Sunni: al-Suyuti, as Itr summarises him**: in al-Tanqih li-Mas'alat al-Tashih he took the forbidden judgement to be "sound in itself" and the permitted one "sound through other support", reconciling the two views. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}
:::

The editor, Nur al-Din Itr, considers it more cautious to say "sound in chain" rather than declare a hadith sound outright, since it may have a defect hidden from the one examining it. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 17}
