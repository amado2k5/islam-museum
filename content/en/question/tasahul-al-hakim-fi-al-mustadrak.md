## The question
[[person/al-hakim-al-nisaburi|Al-Hakim]] described his [[work/al-mustadrak-ala-al-sahihayn|*al-Mustadrak 'ala al-Sahihayn*]] as a collection of hadiths whose narrators are trustworthy, of the kind the two masters or one of them relied on, and he followed each hadith with a verdict: on their condition, on the condition of one of them, or sound in chain. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 40}

Hadith critics after him agreed that he was lax in these verdicts; Muqbil al-Wadi'i goes so far as to say that scholars objected to nothing in him but his laxity in the *Mustadrak*. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 8} The question remained: when al-Hakim alone declares a hadith sound, what is to be done with it?

## The positions
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: al-Hakim himself**: these are hadiths with chains of the same kind as the two masters' chains; additions by trustworthy narrators are accepted; and the two masters never confined the sound to their two books. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 39-40}
- **Sunni: Ibn al-Salah, as Ibn Kathir quotes him**: a middle course; what al-Hakim alone authenticated is, if not sound, then hasan and usable as proof, unless a defect appears in it that makes it weak. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 15}
- **Sunni: al-Dhahabi, Ibn Kathir and Ibn Hajar**: each hadith must be examined on its own. Al-Dhahabi put what meets their condition at about a third in one version and about half in another, and noted some hundred fabricated hadiths in it; Ibn Kathir listed the sound, the good, the weak and the fabricated in it; Ibn Hajar classified it by whether the two masters relied on its narrators, and placed its mainstay in narrators they used for corroboration. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 9-12, 15}
- **Sunni: Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim**: al-Hakim's authentication on its own is not relied on. For Ibn Taymiyya he is like a trustworthy narrator who errs often, even though most of what he declares sound is sound; for Ibn al-Qayyim his authentication does not even show a hadith to be hasan. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 16}
:::

## What the critics point to
The critics cite examples that al-Wadi'i gathers. Al-Hakim listed 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn Aslam in his book of weak narrators, stating that he transmitted fabricated hadiths from his father, and then authenticated a hadith of his in the *Mustadrak*. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 12} He added hadiths that are in fact in the two Sahihs or one of them; al-Wadi'i reports that Salih ibn Qa'id al-Wadi'i collected more than three hundred and fifty of them. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 27} And, as al-Zayla'i notes, he graded on the two masters' condition chains in which some men were al-Bukhari's and others Muslim's. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 17-18}

## What is said in his defence
Ibn Hajar writes in *Lisan al-Mizan* that al-Hakim stands too high to be listed among weak narrators, and passes on the excuse that he compiled the *Mustadrak* late in life, when some decline and inattention had come over him. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 14} Al-Wadi'i adds that his errors outside the *Mustadrak* do not lessen his standing. {cite:mustadrak-haramayn-1997-v1 p. 29}
