## Short answer
Revelation began for [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] with true dreams for six months. Then the angel came to him in [[place/ghar-hira|the Cave of Hira]] in Ramadan, when he was forty years old, with the opening verses of Surat al-Alaq. Revelation then paused for some days, then the opening verses of al-Muddaththir were revealed, with the command to rise and warn, and revelation followed in succession {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.66} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.67} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.70}.

## Stages
Seclusion in the Cave of Hira before the mission lasted one month each year for three years {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.65}. Then came true dreams for six months after the completion of forty years {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.66}. Then came [[event/first-revelation|the beginning of revelation]] with the opening verses of al-Alaq, in the report of Aisha, and his return to [[person/khadija|Khadija]] and [[person/waraqa-ibn-nawfal|Waraqa]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.67} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.68}. Then came [[event/fatrat-al-wahy|the pause in revelation]], and then [[event/second-revelation-al-muddaththir|the revelation of al-Muddaththir]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.70}.

## Where scholars differ
The month of the mission is given as Rabi al-Awwal, Ramadan or Rajab. Its day in Ramadan is given as the seventh, the seventeenth or the eighteenth, and the author, by his own judgement, favours the twenty-first {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.66}. The length of the pause is, in the author's view, a few days, and in the well-known account three years or two and a half years {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.69}.

## Notes for researchers
- The answer is taken from the presentation in Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum and its hadith sources. Academic readings of the beginning of revelation are not presented here.

## In Ibn al-Jawzi's Zad al-Masir
[[person/ibn-al-jawzi|Ibn al-Jawzi]] says the transmitted view is that the first revealed was the opening of Surat al-Alaq, as Urwa relates from [[person/aisha|A'isha]], held also by Qatada and Abu Salih; that it is reported from [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir ibn Abd Allah]] that it was the opening of al-Muddaththir; and he holds that when the opening of al-Alaq came down the Prophet went home and wrapped himself, and then the opening of al-Muddaththir came down, citing Jabir's hadith in the two Sahihs on the pause in revelation; it is also reported from [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]] and [[person/ikrima-mawla-ibn-abbas|Ikrima]] that the first revealed was the basmala. See [[passage/quran-96-1|al-Alaq 1]] and [[passage/quran-74-1|al-Muddaththir 1]]. {cite:zad-al-masir-maktab-islami-v1 p.5}
