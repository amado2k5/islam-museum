## The question
Ibn al-Salah divides the sound hadith into seven classes:
1. what al-Bukhari and Muslim both report;
2. what al-Bukhari alone reports;
3. what Muslim alone reports;
4. what meets the conditions of both but neither reports;
5. what meets al-Bukhari's conditions;
6. what meets Muslim's conditions;
7. what others hold sound but meets the conditions of neither.

The highest is the first, of which hadith scholars say "sound, agreed upon". They mean the agreement of al-Bukhari and Muslim, not of the community. Yet the community's agreement follows from it, because the community received what the two agreed on with acceptance. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 27} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 28}

## The views
:::perspectives
- **Sunni: Ibn al-Salah**: this whole category is certainly sound and yields reasoned certain knowledge, because the probable judgement of one protected from error does not err, and the community in its consensus is protected; what only one of the two reports belongs with it, except a few places criticised by critics such as al-Daraqutni. He says he had inclined to the opposite view and thought it strong, then saw that his first choice was right. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 28} {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 29}
- **Sunni: al-Nawawi, as the editor Itr quotes him**: in al-Taqrib he wrote that the verifiers and the majority disagreed and said it yields probability unless mass-transmitted; in his commentary on Muslim, that their hadith are single-chain reports, that the two books differ from others only in that what they contain is sound without need of examination, and that consensus on acting on them does not entail consensus that their attribution is certain. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 28}
:::

Ibn al-Salah describes the view he rejects as that of those who argued as follows: a hadith in itself yields only probability; the community accepted these reports only because it must act on probability; and probability can err. {cite:muqaddima-ibn-salah-itr p. 28}
