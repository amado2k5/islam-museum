## Summary
The Jabal Usays inscription, also written Jabal Says, is a four-line text in Old Arabic engraved on a cut stone near the summit of a volcano in the basalt steppe south-east of Damascus, dated to 528 or 529 CE. In it Ruqaym son of Ma'arrif the Awsite records that "al-Harith the king" sent him to Usays as a frontier guard {cite:wikipedia-jebel-usays-inscription-en}.

## Date and calendar
The inscription is dated to year 423 of the Bostran era, the era of Bostra, which the summary gives as 528 or 529 CE, adding that an alternative reading gives 532 or 533 CE {cite:wikipedia-jebel-usays-inscription-en}. The gap between the two dates is four years and concerns the reading of the number, not a difference of calendar; the museum does not choose between them, and the date stays derived from a local calendar and in dispute.

## Find-place and where it is now
The text is engraved on ashlar, dressed square stone, near the summit of Jabal Says, also known as Jabal Usays, a volcano in the basalt steppe south-east of Damascus in southern Syria {cite:wikipedia-jebel-usays-inscription-en}. The summary does not say the stone was moved and gives no holding place for it, so it is described by its location.

## Language and script
The whole text is in Old Arabic, in four lines, with no accompanying Greek or Syriac text, unlike the Zabad and Harran inscriptions {cite:wikipedia-jebel-usays-inscription-en}. This makes it a witness to Arabic written alone, in what the summary calls Paleo-Arabic script, on rock on a frontier route and not in a religious building.

## What it says
Its writer, Ruqaym son of Ma'arrif the Awsite, says that "al-Harith the king" sent him to Usays in year 423 and that he came there as a frontier guard {cite:wikipedia-jebel-usays-inscription-en}. The summary explains "al-Harith" as al-Harith ibn Jabalah, the Jafnid phylarch, that is, the chief of the tribes allied to Byzantium, whose house appears in the [[polity/ghassanid-kingdom|Ghassanid kingdom]]; so the text points to an Arab authority and to a man of the Aws serving him.

## Why it matters for the history of Arabic
The inscription falls about seventeen years after the [[object/zabad-inscription|Zabad inscription]], and the summary ties its Arabic to the web of alliances on the Levantine frontier some eighty years before Islam. Its value to the history of Arabic is that it gives a complete text in Arabic alone, in consecutive lines, naming a person, a descent and a duty. It is one link in [[concept/aqdam-al-nuqush-al-arabiyya|the earliest Arabic inscriptions]], and the summary says no more than this.

## Basis and what is uncertain
This entry rests on a research summary supplied to the museum. The museum did not open the pages the summary cites; the founder approved using the summary as it stands on 21 November 2026, so every fact here is the summary's report of those pages, not something the museum checked itself. The account of the text is a paraphrase, not a transcription, and the reservations and alternative readings the summary states are kept as they are. What stays uncertain, as the summary reports it, is the year of the inscription between 528/529 and 532/533 CE, the identity of "al-Harith the king", whom the summary explains as al-Harith ibn Jabalah, and the exact reading of some names. The founder's second file names the Western Michigan University ScholarWorks dissertation "The Qur'anic Jesus: A Study of Parallels with Non-Biblical Texts" as the support for the three Syrian inscriptions, Zabad, Jabal Usays and Harran {cite:wmich-quranic-jesus-dissertation}. The museum has not opened it; its title is about the Qur'anic Jesus, and the file gives the dates 512, 528 and 568 CE for these inscriptions. Wikipedia is used here only for the entity facts.
