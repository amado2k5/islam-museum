## Overview
An ivory casket carved in Cordoba in 968 CE for the Umayyad prince al-Mughira ibn Abd al-Rahman III, with hunting scenes, musicians and carved monastic motifs on very thin Andalusian ivory. {cite:wikidata}

## Impact
Held in the Louvre; it is regarded as the height of Islamic ivory carving and one of the most famous rare survivals of al-Andalus. {cite:wikidata}

## Collection record
The Louvre holds this ivory box in its Department of Islamic Arts under the number OA 4068. Its Kufic dedication runs round the lower rim of the lid: blessing from God, favour, joy and happiness to al-Mughira, son of the commander of the faithful, with a prayer for him, among what was made in the year 357 AH (967-968 CE), as read by Ernst Kuhnel in 1971. The Louvre gives Madinat al-Zahra as its place of making. It came from the collection of Juan Facundo Riano and was bought by the Louvre in 1898, from the dealer Stanislas Baron, after committee meetings on 24 October and 10 November of that year. The record also notes that Riano's 1879 publication places it in the same private collection as the pyxis now numbered 368-1880 in the Victoria and Albert Museum. At the time of the record the box was shown at the Louvre Abu Dhabi on exceptional loan. {cite:louvre-collections cl010329109}
