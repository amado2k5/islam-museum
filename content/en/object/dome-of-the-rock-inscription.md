## Summary
The Dome of the Rock inscription of 72 AH, which is 691 to 692 CE, is a mosaic band about 240 metres long in early Kufic running along the inner and outer octagonal arcades inside the [[building/dome-of-the-rock|Dome of the Rock]] in Jerusalem. It is the last of the six in the research summary, and it carries some of the earliest surviving Qur'anic passages beside pious formulas and a dedicatory text {cite:britannica-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:wikipedia-dome-of-the-rock-en} {cite:smarthistory-dome-of-the-rock}.

## Date and calendar
The inscription is dated to the year 72 of the Hijra, 691 to 692 CE {cite:britannica-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:wikipedia-dome-of-the-rock-en}. The Hijri year is the date stated in the dedicatory text; the Gregorian equivalent is derived, following the summary. It is the first inscription in this file dated by the Hijri calendar rather than by an old local era, and so it is recorded with both dates. It belongs to the building story told in [[event/dome-of-the-rock-construction|the construction of the Dome of the Rock]].

## Find-place and where it is now
The inscription is inside the Dome of the Rock on the Temple Mount, in the Old City of [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]], running in a mosaic band about 240 metres long in early Kufic along the inner and outer octagonal arcades {cite:smarthistory-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:wikipedia-dome-of-the-rock-en}. It stands where it was made, in a building that still exists, and is not an object moved to a museum. The building has its own record, so this entry does not repeat its description, and the esplanade has its record at [[building/al-haram-al-sharif|al-Haram al-Sharif]].

## Language and script
The text is in early Kufic {cite:smarthistory-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:wikipedia-dome-of-the-rock-en}. The museum's own record on [[concept/kufic-script|Kufic script]] names the arcade inscription here of 72 AH among its dated architectural examples {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 23}. It is the only one of the six written on a monumental building of the new faith, and in one long continuous band, not on a tomb, a lintel or a frontier stone.

## What it says
According to the summary the inscription contains some of the earliest surviving Qur'anic passages, beside pious formulas such as the basmala and the shahada and verses affirming that Jesus is a prophet and messenger and not divine, and a dedicatory inscription dated 72 AH whose original patron, the Umayyad caliph [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]], was later replaced by the Abbasid caliph [[person/abd-allah-al-mamun|al-Ma'mun]] {cite:met-byzantium-and-islam-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:smarthistory-dome-of-the-rock} {cite:wikipedia-dome-of-the-rock-en}. The museum does not reproduce the Qur'anic text here and does not identify which verses they are; the source pages are the place to look.

## Why it matters for the history of Arabic
Its value to the history of Arabic is that it is a dated and ordered witness to official Arabic writing on a public building about 124 years after the Harran inscription in the Lajah and only some seventy years after the Hijra, so it is the natural close of the line of [[concept/aqdam-al-nuqush-al-arabiyya|the earliest Arabic inscriptions]]. It links Arabic to Kufic and to the writing of the Umayyad coins in [[concept/quranic-inscriptions-on-coins|Qur'anic inscriptions on coins]]. The summary says no more than this, and the museum does not rule on the order of age between its inscriptions and the codices.

## Basis and what is uncertain
This entry rests on a research summary supplied to the museum. The museum did not open the pages the summary cites; the founder approved using the summary as it stands on 21 November 2026, so every fact here is the summary's report of those pages, not something the museum checked itself. The account of the text is a paraphrase, not a transcription, and the reservations and alternative readings the summary states are kept as they are. What the summary reports with a plurality is the replacement of the original patron's name in the dedication by that of the caliph al-Ma'mun, and whether this changes the dating of the text; the summary reports it without settling it, and the museum does not guess.
