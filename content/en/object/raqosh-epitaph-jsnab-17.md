## Summary
The epitaph of Raqosh, catalogued as JSNab 17, is a funerary text carved on a tomb facade at [[place/al-hijr|al-Hijr]] (Hegra, today Mada'in Salih) in north-west Saudi Arabia, dated to June or July 267 CE. It is written in a mixture of Nabataean Aramaic and Old Arabic in the Nabataeo-Arabic script and records that Ka'bu son of Hrtt built the tomb for his mother Raqosh {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}. It is the first of six dated inscriptions in the research summary on which this entry rests.

## Date and calendar
The text is dated to the month of Tammuz in year 162 of the era of the Province of Arabia, which the summary converts to June or July 267 CE {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}. The conversion from the provincial era to the common calendar is the summary's own, and the museum has not recomputed it, so the date is recorded as derived and as accurate to the month, not the day, with the doubt between two months left standing.

## Find-place and where it is now
The inscription is carved on the sandstone facade of a tomb at Qasr al-Bint in al-Hijr, ancient Hegra, known today as Mada'in Salih, in north-west Saudi Arabia {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}. The summary does not say it was moved, so it is described by its find-place and no other holding place is given.

## Language and script
The text is written in a mixture of Nabataean Aramaic and Old Arabic, in what the summary calls the Nabataeo-Arabic script {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}. The Nabataeans are the people of the [[polity/nabataean-kingdom|Nabataean kingdom]], and the Encyclopaedia Britannica says the Arabic alphabet probably developed in the 4th century CE as a direct descendant of the Nabataean alphabet, its origins and early history being vague {B}. This third-century inscription is therefore a dated example of Nabataean letters used to write a text that contains Old Arabic.

## What it says
The text records that Ka'bu son of Hrtt built the tomb for his mother Raqosh, who died at Hegra in the month of Tammuz in year 162, and it calls down a curse in the name of Mry 'lm', the "Lord of the World", on anyone who alters the tomb, or buries or exhumes anyone else in it {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}. The names are given as written in consonants, and the museum offers no vocalised reading beyond the summary's.

## Why it matters for the history of Arabic
The summary treats it as the first dated inscription in a line that begins with the Nabataean script and ends with the [[object/dome-of-the-rock-inscription|Dome of the Rock inscription]] of 72 AH. It matters because it carries an explicit date and contains Old Arabic phrasing, showing that text with Arabic in it was being written in Nabataean letters more than three centuries before Islam; about sixty years later comes the [[object/namara-inscription|Namara inscription]], in Old Arabic in Nabataean letters. The line is laid out in [[concept/aqdam-al-nuqush-al-arabiyya|the earliest Arabic inscriptions]]. How Arabic its language is, and where it stands in the descent of the script, are questions for specialists, and the summary says no more than the above.

## Basis and what is uncertain
This entry rests on a research summary supplied to the museum. The museum did not open the pages the summary cites; the founder approved using the summary as it stands on 21 November 2026, so every fact here is the summary's report of those pages, not something the museum checked itself. The account of the text is a paraphrase, not a transcription, and the reservations and alternative readings the summary states are kept as they are. What stays uncertain, as the summary reports it, is the choice between June and July of 267, the reading of the names, and the proportion of Aramaic to Old Arabic in the text {cite:diconab-inscription-196} {cite:wikipedia-jsnab-17-en}.
