## Summary
The Iskandar Sultan miscellany is a small-format manuscript made for him at [[place/shiraz|Shiraz]] around 1410–11, when his academy was at its height; it is in the British Library as Add. 27261. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 222} {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 233} Norah Titley says Iskandar Sultan's illuminators were then unsurpassed in inventive design and in the use of colour and gold. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 233}

## Its borders
The borders of some pages hold small, lightly painted drawings of subjects from the Shahnama and Nizami — warriors fighting, Khusraw watching Shirin — far outnumbered by varied geometric and arabesque motifs. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 224} Among them is what is probably the earliest example of the [[concept/waqwaq-arabesque|waqwaq arabesque]], with human and animal heads on its tendrils. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 224} {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 233} Every folio has a triangular "thumbpiece" of different design, and a deep maroon favoured by Iskandar Sultan's illuminators runs through its decoration. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 233}

## Its place in a chain
Compositions from the [[object/khamsa-khvaju-kirmani-bl-add-18113|Khvaju Kirmani manuscript]] (1396) recur in this miscellany, and its own compositions in turn served as models throughout the fifteenth century, in a chain running from Baghdad to Shiraz and Herat. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 222} {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 223}
