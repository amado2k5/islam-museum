## Summary
Al-Uzza is the idol that the Arabs took at [[place/wadi-nakhla|Wadi Nakhla]] after Manat and al-Lat. Together with [[object/manat|Manat]] and [[object/al-lat|al-Lat]], it was among their greatest idols in the account of al-Raheeq al-Makhtum {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul p.12}.

## On the order of the idols
Among their oldest idols was Manat. Then they took al-Lat in Ta'if, and then they took al-Uzza at Wadi Nakhla. These three were their greatest idols. Then shirk multiplied, and idols multiplied in every part of the Hijaz {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35}. The rites of its worship are covered in [[concept/ibadat-al-asnam-jahiliyya|Idol worship in the Jahiliyya]].

## Not covered here
Quraysh's veneration of al-Uzza, and the dispatch of Khalid ibn al-Walid after the conquest to demolish it at its entrances.

## For young visitors
Al-Uzza is an idol in a valley between Mecca and Ta'if. Quraysh revered it until it was demolished after the conquest of Mecca.
