## Summary
This manuscript of the poems of Khvaju Kirmani was completed in 1396 in the academy maintained by [[person/ahmad-jalayir|Sultan Ahmad Jalayir]], himself a poet, calligrapher and artist; Norah Titley sees it as that academy's peak; it is in the British Library as Add. 18113. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 26} Its poems were written in nasta'liq by [[person/mir-ali-tabrizi|Mir Ali Tabrizi]], who perfected the script under Sultan Ahmad's patronage, after which it became the predominant script of illustrated Persian manuscripts. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 27}

## Its paintings
Its paintings take up the whole page and spill into the borders, the text reduced to a couplet or two in a panel above or below; for Titley they mark a stage between Mongol-Chinese influence and the development of Persian art at Shiraz and Herat. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 27} Titley compares its Sultan Sanjar and the old woman with the same subject in the Khamsa of Nizami of 1386–88 to show the development of painting at Baghdad. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 26} The paintings are probably all by [[person/junayd-al-baghdadi-painter|Junayd of Baghdad]], to whom (and to Sultan Ahmad) an inscription on one painting (folio 45b) refers. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 27}

## Contents
The manuscript contains the romance Humay u Humayun, finished by Khvaju Kirmani at Baghdad in 1331 under Abu Sa'id, on the Persian prince Humay's adventures and courtship of the Chinese princess Humayun, and the Sufi poem Rawdat al-anwar. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 29} Compositions from it recur in the [[object/iskandar-sultan-miscellany-bl-add-27261|Iskandar Sultan miscellany]] at Shiraz and served as models throughout the fifteenth century. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 222} {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 223}
