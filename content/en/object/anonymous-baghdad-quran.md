## Summary
The "Anonymous Baghdad Qur'an" is the name given to a large thirty-part Qur'an produced at [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]] in 707 AH/1307–8 CE; The Met holds its colophon folio, written in [[concept/muhaqqaq-script|muhaqqaq]]. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 37}

## Its makers
The calligrapher was [[person/ahmad-al-suhrawardi|Ahmad ibn al-Suhrawardi al-Bakri]], pupil of [[person/yaqut-al-mustasimi|Yaqut al-Musta'simi]], known for many luxurious Qur'ans and for designing inscriptions for buildings in Baghdad; this is his best-known work. The illuminator was Muhammad ibn Aybak ibn Abdallah. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 37} Maryam Ekhtiar names Ahmad ibn al-Suhrawardi as the most notable of Yaqut's pupils and the colophon, which carries his name, as a fine example of his hand. {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 26}

## Patron
Made under the [[polity/ilkhanate|Ilkhanids]], the lavish Qur'an was, the author suggests, possibly commissioned by the ruler or a statesman of the time — perhaps the sultan (she names [[person/ghazan-khan|Ghazan]] and Uljaitu) or one of his viziers, possibly [[person/rashid-al-din-hamadani|Rashid al-Din]]; hence it remains "anonymous". {cite:ekhtiar-how-to-read-islamic-calligraphy-2018 p. 37}
