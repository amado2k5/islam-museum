## Summary
Hubal is the idol that [[person/amr-ibn-luhayy|Amr ibn Luhayy]], chief of Khuza'a, brought from Syria. He placed it inside the [[building/kaaba|Ka'ba]] and called the people of [[place/mecca|Mecca]] to associate partners with God, and they answered him. Soon the people of the Hijaz followed the people of Mecca, because they were the custodians of the House and the people of the sacred precinct {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul p.12}.

## Its origin
Amr ibn Luhayy travelled to Syria and saw its people worshipping idols. He found this good and thought it right, since Syria was the land of messengers and scriptures, so he brought Hubal back with him {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35}. In the narration of al-Bukhari that the entry on Amr ibn Luhayy presents, he saw the people of al-Balqa venerating idols, and brought an idol to Mecca.

## The arrows at Hubal
When they were in doubt about a man's lineage, they took him to Hubal with a hundred camels, and gave them to the keeper of the arrows. They cast lots with [[concept/azlam|the arrows]] on which were written "from you", "from others", or "attached" {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.37} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/152-153}.

## The end
The polytheists filled the Sacred Mosque with idols. When the Prophet conquered Mecca, he found three hundred and sixty idols around the House. He began poking them until they fell, then ordered them removed from the mosque and burned {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul p.50-54}. See [[event/fath-mecca-cleansing-kaaba|the cleansing of the Ka'ba on the day of the conquest]].

## Not covered here
According to al-Azraqi, the origin of Hubal is Hit in Iraq; this is in the data of this entry. Also, Abu Sufyan's cry "Exalt Hubal!" on the day of Uhud is in the entry on that battle.

## For young visitors
Hubal is an idol that Amr ibn Luhayy brought from Syria and placed in the Ka'ba. It was the greatest idol of Quraysh until the Prophet broke it on the day of the conquest of Mecca.
