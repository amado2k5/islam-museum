## Summary
The Harran inscription is a bilingual Christian dedicatory text in Greek and Old Arabic on a stone at a martyrium in Harran, in as-Suwayda Governorate on the Lajah plateau south of Damascus in Syria, dated 567 or 568 CE. It records that the phylarch Sharahil son of Zalim built a martyrium of Saint John in the year 463 {cite:wikipedia-harran-inscription-en}.

## Date and calendar
The inscription is dated to year 463 of the Bostran era, the era of Bostra, which the summary gives as 567 or 568 CE {cite:wikipedia-harran-inscription-en}. The Arabic text notes that this year came one year after the "rebellion of Khaybar", as the summary reports it; the museum does not interpret the reference or tie it to any other event. The date is derived from a local calendar and is accurate to the year.

## Find-place and where it is now
The text is on a stone at a martyr shrine in Harran in as-Suwayda Governorate, on the Lajah plateau south of [[place/damascus|Damascus]] in [[place/syria|Syria]] {cite:wikipedia-harran-inscription-en}. This is not the Harran of the Upper Mesopotamian region whose record the museum keeps at [[place/harran|Harran]]; that is another town. The summary gives no holding place for the stone, so it is described by its location.

## Language and script
The inscription is bilingual, Greek and Old Arabic, and is a Christian dedicatory text {cite:wikipedia-harran-inscription-en}. The summary calls the Arabic script Paleo-Arabic and places the text among the inscriptions that precede Islam. It is close to the Zabad inscription, which joins Arabic, Greek and Syriac, and differs from it in having two languages only.

## What it says
The text records that the phylarch Sharahil son of Zalim built the martyrium of Saint John in year 463, a year that the Arabic part sets one year after the rebellion of Khaybar {cite:wikipedia-harran-inscription-en}. Phylarch is a Byzantine title for the chief of allied tribes, so the text is a religious foundation in the name of an Arab man of authority, written in Arabic alongside Greek. The summary gives no more than this on the content.

## Why it matters for the history of Arabic
The inscription comes about forty years after the [[object/jabal-usays-inscription|Jabal Usays inscription]], about fifty-five after the [[object/zabad-inscription|Zabad inscription]], and about fifty-five years before the Hijra. Its value to the history of Arabic is that it gives a precise date for an Arabic text paired with Greek and names an Arab man with his name, title and office, showing Arabic writing as a means of religious and official foundation in this region before Islam. It is one link in [[concept/aqdam-al-nuqush-al-arabiyya|the earliest Arabic inscriptions]], and the summary says no more than this.
