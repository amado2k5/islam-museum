## Overview
The Table of Solomon, according to al-Maqqari, is among the marvels taken as spoils in al-Andalus at its conquest. [[person/tariq-ibn-ziyad|Tariq ibn Ziyad]] found it in the church of Toledo. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.171}

## Its origin
Some historians say that it, together with a few pearls that [[person/musa-ibn-nusayr|Musa ibn Nusayr]] found in the church of Merida, were among the spoils from Jerusalem that came to the lord of al-Andalus. The name of that king was Bryan, and he had taken part in the conquest with Nebuchadnezzar, and this fell to his share. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.171}

## Description
It is said to be of green zabarjad, with its edges and legs made of it. It has three hundred and sixty-five legs, and it used to be placed in the church of Toledo. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.325-326}

## Another account
Some non-Arab sources claim that it did not belong to Solomon. They say it is one of the objects that churches make from the heritage of the pious in their religion, for display at festivals, and that the Gothic kings embellished it until it surpassed what had been made before. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.325-326}

Al-Maqqari presents both accounts here without preferring either.

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Ibn Abd al-Hakam says that when Tariq reached Toledo his only concern was the table, the table of Solomon son of David "as the People of the Book claim". {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 279} Al-Layth reports that al-Andalus was conquered for Musa ibn Nusayr and that Solomon's table and the crown were taken from it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 279} Tariq was told the table was in a fortress called Firas, two days from Toledo, held by a nephew of Roderic; Tariq gave him safe-conduct and he handed it over, with more gold and jewels on it than had ever been seen. Tariq broke off one of its legs and put another in its place, and the table was valued at two hundred thousand dinars. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 279} Ibn Abd al-Hakam reports that Musa later claimed the table before the caliph, and Tariq produced the missing leg to show that he was the one who had found it. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 285}
