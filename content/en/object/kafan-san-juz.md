## Overview
A piece of embroidered Khurasani silk in which the relics of a saint in tenth-century France were wrapped; evidence of Islamic silk trade with Europe. {cite:wikidata}

## Collection record
The Louvre holds this silk in its Department of Islamic Arts under the number OA 7502. It is a samite (a twill-woven silk) with a Kufic band of good wishes: blessing and happiness to the commander Abu Mansur Bukhtegin, with a prayer for a long life. For centuries it lay in the former abbey of Saint-Josse in the Pas-de-Calais, France; the Louvre bought it from the commune of Saint-Josse after the national museums' art council met on 7 November 1921, and entered it in the inventory in 1922. The Louvre is careful about where it was made: it lists the Iranian world and the Caucasus (formerly given as Khurasan), with Asia Minor, eastern Europe and the Arab Near East as possibilities marked with a question mark. It dates the cloth to the second half of the tenth century, where an older catalogue dating said before 961. {cite:louvre-collections cl010329960}
