## Summary
This page is about one particular mantle: the burda which, according to the books on the Companions, the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] gave to [[person/kab-ibn-zuhayr|Ka'b ibn Zuhayr]] when he became Muslim and recited his ode, which [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya]] later bought from his sons and which came to the caliphs. It has nothing to do with the manuscript of al-Busiri's poem in a London museum (see [[object/makhutat-burda|the Burdah manuscript]]), nor with another cloak among the Prophet's relics (see [[object/kisa-al-nabi|the Prophet's cloak]]).

## Ka'b's coming
Al-Bayhaqi reports, from Ka'b's grandson, that Ka'b and his brother Bujayr, sons of Zuhayr, set out and reached Abraq al-'Azzaf; Bujayr went on to the Prophet and became Muslim. When Ka'b heard, he composed verses against his brother; they reached the Prophet, who declared his blood forfeit, and Bujayr wrote to his brother to flee, then wrote that the Prophet accepted anyone who came bearing witness to the two testimonies. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 2256} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 2257} Ka'b became Muslim, came, knelt his camel at the mosque door, went in, sat before the Prophet and asked for protection; then he recited his ode that begins Banat Su'ad. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 2257} {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 2258} Ibn Kathir cites 'Asim ibn 'Umar ibn Qatada that Ka'b praised the Emigrants in the poem and the Ansar were angry, so after his conversion he praised them. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 843}

## The gift
In Usd al-Ghaba Ibn al-Athir writes, after giving his verse, that the Messenger of God had given him a mantle of his, the one the caliphs have to this day. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 1037} In al-Isaba Ibn Hajar says the Prophet clothed him in a mantle of his, that Mu'awiya bought it from his son, and that it is the one the caliphs wear at the festivals. {cite:isaba scan vol. 2 p. 1276} A note on the Dala'il al-Nubuwwa text says the Prophet pardoned him and put his mantle on him. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 2256} The text of the Dala'il itself, in the pages I read, does not mention the mantle.

## Mu'awiya's purchase
In al-Kamil Ibn al-Athir reports that Ka'b was asked to sell the Messenger's burda, and said he would not prefer anyone over the Messenger of God's garment; when Ka'b died Mu'awiya bought it from his children for twenty thousand dirhams, and it is the burda the caliphs have today. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 145} The page I read begins mid-sentence, so it does not say who asked while Ka'b was alive.

## Ibn Kathir's position
Ibn Kathir says some reports have it that the Prophet gave him his mantle when he recited the ode, that al-Sarsari put this into verse in his praise poems, that Ibn al-Athir mentions it in Usd al-Ghaba, and that it is the burda the caliphs have. He then says this is very well known, but that he did not see it in any of the famous books with a chain he accepts. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 843} He quotes from al-Suhayli a line by Ka'b describing the Prophet wrapped in the burd. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 6 p. 844}

## The mantle of Ayla and the Abbasid caliphs
Elsewhere in al-Bidaya Ibn Kathir quotes al-Bayhaqi, from Ibn Ishaq on the Tabuk story: the Prophet gave the people of Ayla a mantle with his letter, which Abu al-'Abbas 'Abd Allah ibn Muhammad, al-Saffah, the first Abbasid caliph, bought for three hundred dinars; the Abbasids inherited it, the caliph wearing it on the festival over his shoulders and taking in one hand the staff attributed to the Prophet. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 397} So the books give two accounts: Usd al-Ghaba, al-Isaba and al-Kamil make the caliphs' mantle that of Ka'b, while Ibn Kathir, in the place where he gives al-Bayhaqi's report, makes it the mantle of Ayla. The pages I read do not reconcile them.

## What became of it
Ibn Kathir does not say that any particular mantle survived, nor when it was lost, in the pages I read. The modern editor of al-Kamil says in a note that the mantle passed from the Abbasids to Egypt and that Sultan Selim, on conquering Egypt, moved it to Topkapi in Istanbul, citing two modern works. {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir scan vol. 1 p. 145} That is the editor's statement; I did not find an early chain for it in the pages I read.
