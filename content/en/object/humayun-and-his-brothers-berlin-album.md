## Overview
An opaque watercolour on paper from the Berlin Album (folio 15r), folio 41 x 25.5 cm, in the Staatsbibliothek Preussischer Kulturbesitz, Berlin, attributed to [[person/dust-muhammad-haravi|Dust Muhammad]] and dated about 1550. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 144}

## Subject
In the foreground of a craggy, somewhat ominous landscape [[person/humayun-mughal|Humayun]] sits on a rustic stone throne receiving his brothers; the ladies of the household entertain one another in a rustic zenana, musicians play, attendants wait for orders, and three small boys romp beneath the trees, one of whom must be Prince [[person/jalal-al-din-akbar|Akbar]], near a life-sized elephant concealed in the rocks, prophetic of Indian days ahead. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 144} Such hidden faces in the rocks were admired by Shah Tahmasp, the painter's former patron. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 144}

## The painter and the picture's history
The catalogue describes Dust Muhammad as a master artist, calligrapher and man of letters who had studied at Herat with Bihzad and whose wandering career brought him to Humayun's court, where he painted this and other pictures. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 144} {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 146} Welch counts it among his liveliest and best, and thinks that before it was mounted in Jahangir's album it was probably kept at Agra, where Humayun's palace included a three-building complex called the *Khana-i tilism*, or Magic House, one of whose upper rooms held his books and gilded specimens of calligraphy. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 146} Dust Muhammad did not stay long at the Mughal court, which was at Kabul when this was painted; a decade or so later, at about seventy, he apparently returned to Iran and spent his last years piously copying texts. {cite:welch-india-art-and-culture-1985 p. 146}
