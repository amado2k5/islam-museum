## Summary
The hair of the [[person/muhammad|Prophet]] is among the most often mentioned relics in the books, because its origin is known: he shaved his head at Mina on the [[event/farewell-pilgrimage|Farewell Pilgrimage]] and it was shared among those present. Ibn Kathir notes that he shaved his head on that pilgrimage and died eighty-one days later. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 410} This page gives the reports as they stand, each tied to its book. For blessing-seeking from relics in general, see [[concept/tabarruk|Tabarruk]].

## The shaving at Mina and the sharing of the hair
Ibn al-Qayyim says that when he had finished the sacrifice he called the barber and had his head shaved; the barber was Ma'mar ibn 'Abd Allah, and the Prophet told him, in effect, that he had been given power over his ear-lobe with a razor in his hand, to which Ma'mar answered that this was a grace of God to him. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 269} He then pointed to his right side and divided that hair among those near him, then had the left side shaved and said: is Abu Talha here? and gave it to him, as in Sahih Muslim. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 269} Ibn al-Qayyim holds that al-Bukhari's report from Ibn Sirin, from Anas, that Abu Talha was the first to take his hair does not contradict Muslim's. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 269} Muslim's wordings differ over which side Abu Talha took: in one the right side and then the left went to him with the words to share it among the people; in another he gave to Umm Sulaym. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4682} {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 5480} Ahmad reports from 'Abd Allah ibn Zayd ibn 'Abd Rabbih that the Prophet shaved his head into his garment and gave it to him, who divided it among men, and that he pared his nails and gave them to his companion; the narrator says that hair is with them, dyed with henna and katam. {cite:zad-al-maad scan vol. 1 p. 270} Al-Salihi cites Ibn Sa'd that he ordered his hair and nails to be buried. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 5479}

## Umm Salama's strands
Al-Bukhari reports from 'Uthman ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Mawhab that his family sent him with a cup of water to Umm Salama, who brought out a silver vessel holding some of the Prophet's hair; when a person was struck by the evil eye or an illness he would send her a vessel, she would stir the hair in it and the sick man drank, and looking into it he saw red hairs. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4669} {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4670} The editor of Subul al-Huda notes that the word for the vessel is garbled in the manuscripts and may be a corruption. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4669} In al-Tirmidhi's Shama'il Umm Salama brought out a pouch with hair dyed with henna, in another wording with henna and katam. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 4670} Ibn Kathir gives the same: we went in to Umm Salama and she brought out some of the Messenger of God's hair, red, dyed with henna and katam. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 411} Al-Bayhaqi's version says the vessel was large and of silver, and that for a person with fever it was sprinkled on his face. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 407} In his list of descriptions of the Prophet's hair Ibn Kathir has Anas saying there were only seventeen or eighteen white hairs on his head. {cite:al-bidaya-wal-nihaya scan vol. 3 p. 411}

## Khalid ibn al-Walid's cap
Al-Bayhaqi reports that Khalid ibn al-Walid lost a cap at Yarmuk; they searched and found a worn old one. He said that the Messenger of God performed 'umra and shaved his head, the people rushed for the sides of his hair, and he got to the forelock first and put it in this cap; he never went to a fight with it that he was not granted victory. {cite:dalail-al-nubuwwa-bayhaqi scan vol. 1 p. 2805} Al-Salihi adds that Khalid spoke to him about his forelock as he shaved and he gave it to him, and that Khalid kept it in the front of his cap and met no host that he did not rout; he attributes the report to al-Tabarani and Abu Ya'la with men of the Sahih. {cite:subul-huda-salihi scan vol. 4 p. 5480} So al-Bayhaqi places the shaving in an 'umra and al-Salihi in the Farewell Pilgrimage, and the occasion stays disputed between the two readings. See [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]] and [[event/battle-of-yarmuk|the Battle of Yarmuk]].

## Anas's request
Ibn Hajar cites Ibn al-Sakan: Thabit al-Bunani said Anas told him, this is a hair of the Messenger of God, put it under my tongue; he put it under his tongue and he was buried with it there. {cite:isaba scan vol. 1 p. 277} See [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]].

## Mu'awiya's request
Ibn al-Athir reports that when death came to Mu'awiya he asked to be shrouded in a shirt the Messenger of God had given him, placed next to his body; he also had the parings of the Messenger of God's nails and asked that they be ground and put in his eyes and mouth, saying: do that, and leave me with the Most Merciful of the merciful. {cite:usd-al-ghaba scan vol. 1 p. 1147} See [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]].

## Notes
These reports concern what individual Companions and later people kept; they say nothing of where any of this hair is today. The narrators differ over which side Abu Talha received and over the occasion of Khalid's cap, and I have kept the difference as it stands.
