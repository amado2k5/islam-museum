## Summary
The Muraqqa'-e-Gulshan is one of two large volumes of [[person/jahangir-mughal|Jahangir]]'s [[practice/mughal-muraqqa-album|albums]] that remain substantially intact; it is now in the Imperial Library, Gulistan Palace, Tehran, and its few dated pages fall between 1599, when Jahangir was still a prince, and 1609. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 23} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 40}

## Contents
It holds two pages among the earliest known works of [[person/aqa-riza-jahangiri|Aqa Riza]], almost purely Iranian, and margins signed by him and dated 1008 AH/1599–1600 CE that use European motifs — proof for Milo Cleveland Beach that he was then copying European prints. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 20} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 91} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 92} Among its treasures is a series of marginal portraits by [[person/daulat-painter|Daulat]], about 1605–9, of fellow artists at work — [[person/abu-al-hasan-nadir-al-zaman|Abu'l Hasan]], [[person/bishan-das|Bishan Das]], [[person/manohar-painter|Manohar]], [[person/govardhan-painter|Govardhan]] and Daulat himself — which make up for the lack of written notices of these men; from one we learn that Abu'l Hasan was left-handed. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 43} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 84} {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 112} An inscription in its borders states that Bishan Das was a nephew of [[person/nanha-painter|Nanha]]. {cite:beach-grand-mogul-1978 p. 107}
