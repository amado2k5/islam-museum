## Summary
The verses of Surat Yusuf from [[passage/quran-12-93|Qur'an 12:93]] to [[passage/quran-12-96|12:96]] tell that [[person/yusuf|Yusuf]] sent his shirt with his brothers to be thrown over the face of his father [[person/yaqub|Yaqub]] so that his sight would return. In Stories of the Prophets Ibn Kathir says it was the shirt next to his body, and that this is "among the extraordinary things and signs of prophethood and the greatest miracles". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 326} Al-Qurtubi relays from Mujahid that this shirt was the shirt of [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]], which God put on him in the fire, of Paradise silk, and which passed to Ishaq and then to Yaqub. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3670} In that account, then, the shirt is a trace passed down among the prophets before it reached Yaqub.

## Three shirts in the story
The commentaries mention three shirts in the story of Yusuf. The first is the shirt his brothers brought to their father with false blood on it; Ibn Kathir says they slaughtered a kid and put some of its blood on the shirt to make him think a wolf had eaten Yusuf, and some say they forgot to tear it, "and the bane of lying is forgetfulness". {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 300} The second is the shirt that was torn from behind in the story of the wife of the governor, where a witness from her household made the place of the tear a sign of Yusuf's truthfulness or falsehood. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 305} The third is the shirt sent to Yaqub, which is the subject of this record.

## What Ibn Kathir reports
Ibn Kathir says that when Yusuf made himself known to his brothers he told them to take his shirt and put it over their father's eyes so that his sight would return by God's leave, and then to bring all their families to Egypt. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 326} He reports from Ibn Abbas that when the caravan left Egypt Yaqub said he could smell Yusuf, and he smelled him from three days' journey away; and from al-Hasan and Ibn Jurayj that the distance between them was eighty farsakhs and that it was eighty years since he had parted from him. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 326} {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 327} When the bearer of good news came he threw the shirt over Yaqub's face and he at once regained his sight after being blind, and told his sons that he knew from God what they did not know. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 327}

## What al-Qurtubi reports: a shirt from Paradise
Al-Qurtubi quotes, from Ibn al-Suddi from his father from Mujahid, that Yusuf told his brothers to take his shirt, and that Mujahid said Yusuf knew God too well to think his shirt would restore Yaqub's sight, but that it was the shirt of Ibrahim which God had put on him in the fire, of Paradise silk; Ibrahim clothed Ishaq in it and Ishaq clothed Yaqub, and Yaqub put the shirt into a silver tube and hung it on Yusuf's neck, fearing the evil eye for him; and Jibril told him to send his shirt, for in it was the scent of Paradise, and the scent of Paradise falls on no one afflicted without his being healed. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3670} {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3671}

Al-Qurtubi quotes al-Hasan that unless God had told Yusuf that the shirt would return his father's sight he would not have known it, and that the one who carried the shirt was Yahuda, who said to Yusuf that he was the one who had carried his shirt to Yaqub with false blood and grieved him, and now he would carry it to gladden him and restore his sight, as al-Suddi relates. He quotes Masruq that they numbered ninety-three, men and women. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3671} He also gives another view, that the shirt he sent was the one torn from behind, so that Yaqub would know Yusuf had been protected from sin; al-Qurtubi says the first view is sounder, and that it is reported as raised to the Prophet in a hadith from Anas, which al-Qushayri mentions, and closes with "God knows best". {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3671}

## Ibrahim's shirt in the report from Wahb ibn Munabbih
At the place where Yusuf is thrown into the pit, al-Qurtubi quotes al-Suddi and others that when his brothers were lowering him into the well he clung to its rim, and they tied his hands and took off his shirt, and he asked them to give him back his shirt to cover himself. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3555} He then gives an account, linked to the transmission of Wahb ibn Munabbih, that when Ibrahim was thrown into the fire unclothed Jibril brought him a shirt of Paradise silk and put it on him; it remained with Ibrahim, then Ishaq inherited it, then Yaqub; when Yusuf grew up Yaqub put it into an amulet around his neck and he never parted from it; and when he was thrown into the pit unclothed Jibril took out that shirt and put it on him. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3555}

In the same account, when Yusuf stood on the rock in the pit he said to his brothers that every dying person has a testament, so they should hear his: when you are together and keep each other company, remember my loneliness; when you eat, remember my hunger; when you drink, remember my thirst; when you see a stranger, remember my exile; when you see a young man, remember my youth. Jibril told him to stop this and busy himself with prayer. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3555}

## The Station of Ibrahim, another trace
The museum already has a record for another trace connected to Ibrahim, [[object/maqam-ibrahim|the Station of Ibrahim]], named in the verse of al-Baqara ([[passage/quran-2-125|Qur'an 2:125]]), and a record on the verse and its commentary, [[concept/maqam-ibrahim-ayat-tafsir|the Station of Ibrahim in the verse and its tafsir]]; so no new record is written for it here.

## What these books do not say
The books on which this record is built do not say what became of the shirt after it restored Yaqub's sight. The descriptions of the shirt, its descent from Ibrahim and its coming from Paradise are reports of Successors and those who took from others, passed on by the commentators, and the museum states none of them in its own voice. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 3671}
