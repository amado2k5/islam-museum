## Overview
The black kiswa of the [[building/kaaba|Kaaba]], embroidered with gold and silver; it is replaced each year in the season of the hajj, and its old cloth is distributed as gifts. {cite:wikidata}

## Impact
Its traditions run from the pre-Islamic period to the modern Mecca workshop; it is made today at the Kiswa Factory in Umm al-Jud by two hundred craftsmen. {cite:wikidata}

## In al-Umari's Masalik al-absar
[[person/ibn-fadl-allah-al-umari|Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari]] gathers reports on the covering of the Ka'ba: the historians' disagreement over who first covered it with brocade, al-Hajjaj according to Ibn Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr according to Ibn Bakkar, Khalid ibn Ja'far ibn Kilab according to al-Mawardi, and the vow of Nutayla, mother of al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, reported by several including al-Daraqutni. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.137} Then al-Azraqi's report that Mu'awiya covered it with brocade, and that al-Ma'mun covered it three times a year: red brocade on the day of Tarwiya, Coptic linen at the new moon of Rajab and white brocade on 27 Ramadan, beginning the white in 206 AH. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.138} He quotes al-Azraqi from Ibn Jurayj that Tubba' was the first to give it a full covering. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.139}

He describes the covering in his own day: it was put on once a year in the pilgrimage season, brought from the sultan's treasury in Egypt with the caravan; the Sharifs and the Banu Shayba took the old one and divided it, and its pieces were carried to every land for blessing; the ruler of Yemen used to send a covering that was put on beneath the Egyptian one, both of black silk with white writing. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.138} The Ka'ba was washed with Zamzam water on 27 Dhu al-Qa'da, its hangings were tucked up, and it was dressed on the day of the Adha. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.139} He says that on his pilgrimage of 738 AH he took off the old covering and put on the new with his own hands. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.138}
