## Summary
This copy of Nizami's Khamsa was made for [[person/tahmasp-i-safavi|Shah Tahmasp]] in the Tabriz studios between 1539 and 1543; it is in the British Library as Or. 2265. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 225}

## Its margins
Norah Titley notes that the border paintings at the start of the manuscript are clearly original, while towards the end designs repeat and stencils must have been used. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 217} Nearly every page has a different motif, most margins filled with paintings in two tones of gold of bears, monkeys, tigers, lions, gazelles and snow leopards and of mythical creatures — dragon, simurgh, qilin — among trees, streams, birds and ribbon clouds. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 225} Titley sees in them the Iranian love of nature and the complete absorption of earlier Chinese elements. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 225}

## Influence
Titley compares it with the Khamsa of Nizami completed for the Mughal emperor [[person/jalal-al-din-akbar|Akbar]] in 1595 (Or. 12208), whose borders, though much influenced by Tabriz work, introduce Indian animals and birds. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 225} She suggests that the circular "rolling" arabesque of Ottoman manuscripts may have been inspired by a "whirling" arabesque in this manuscript. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 237}
