## Summary
The tabut, or Ark, was a chest of the Israelites. The verse of al-Baqara ([[passage/quran-2-248|Qur'an 2:248]]) says that it held a "sakina" from their Lord and a remnant of what the house of [[person/musa|Musa]] and the house of [[person/harun|Harun]] left, and that the sign of Talut's kingship was that it would come to them carried by angels. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 585} The Qur'an does not list its contents, so the commentators give different accounts of the "sakina" and the "remnant"; this record gives each, attributed to the one who says it.

## The Ark as the People of the Book describe it
Ibn Kathir relays from the People of the Book that God told Musa to make the tabernacle ("qubbat al-zaman") and an ark of acacia wood two and a half cubits long, two cubits wide and a cubit and a half high, overlaid with pure gold inside and out, with four rings at its corners and on its edges two gold cherubim in the form of two winged angels, made by a man named Bezalel; he then says that this ark is, "God knows best", the one named in the verse. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 509} He adds that Musa used to stand by the ark and God spoke to him from the pillar of cloud. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 510} The description is the People of the Book's, and linking it to the ark of the verse is Ibn Kathir's view.

## What was in the Ark: the sakina
In the Stories of the Prophets Ibn Kathir says it was said that the sakina was a gold basin in which the breasts of the prophets were washed, or that it was like a violent wind, or that its form was like a cat which, when it cried out in battle, made the Israelites certain of victory. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 585} In his Tafsir he gives, from Ali, that the sakina has a face like a human face and is a gentle wind, and from him also that it is a violent wind with two heads; from Mujahid, that it has two wings and a tail; from Wahb ibn Munabbih, in Ibn Ishaq's report, that it was the head of a dead cat, which when it cried out in the ark with a cat's cry made them certain of victory, and in 'Abd al-Razzaq's report from Wahb, a spirit from God which spoke when they disagreed about anything and told them plainly what they wanted. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 888}

Al-Qurtubi gives the same views and adds that Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi said it was a gold basin from Paradise in which the hearts of the prophets were washed, and that Mujahid said it was an animal like a cat with two wings and a tail, its eyes giving off a ray, which put an army to flight when it looked at it. He quotes Ibn Atiyya that the sound view is that the ark held excellent things left by the prophets and their traces, so that souls found calm in it and drew confidence. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 1173} Al-Qurtubi cites the hadith of al-Bara' in Muslim, about a man reciting Surat al-Kahf over whom a cloud settled and of which the Prophet said it was the "sakina" that descended for the Qur'an, and the hadith of Usayd ibn Hudayr about the angels listening to his recitation; he concludes that the sakina descends with the angels, and that this supports those who say it is a spirit or something that has a spirit. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 1173}

## What was in the Ark: the remnant
Ibn Jarir reports from Ibn Abbas, by a chain the editor calls sound, that the remnant is his staff and the fragments of the tablets, and Qatada, al-Suddi, al-Rabi' ibn Anas and Ikrima said the same, Ikrima adding the Torah. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 888} Abu Salih said it was the staff of Musa, the staff of Harun, two tablets of the Torah and the manna; Atiyya ibn Sa'd said the staff of Musa, the staff of Harun, the clothes of Musa, the clothes of Harun and the fragments of the tablets. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 888} Abd al-Razzaq reports from al-Thawri that some say a measure of manna and the fragments of the tablets, and some say the staff and the two sandals. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 888}

Al-Qurtubi gives among the views a measure of manna in a gold basin, the staff of Musa, the turban of Harun and the fragments of the tablets, and explains "fragments" thus: when Musa came to his people with the tablets and found that they had worshipped the calf, he threw the tablets down in anger and they broke; he removed those that were whole, took the fragments of what had broken and put them in the ark. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 1174} He quotes al-Dahhak that the remnant is jihad and fighting enemies, which Ibn Atiyya explains as the command to that being in the ark, either written in it or the very act of bringing it being like the command. {cite:jami-ahkam-quran-resala-v1 scan vol. 3 p. 1174}

## How the Ark came back
Ibn Kathir says in the Stories of the Prophets that when the Amalekites seized the ark and put it under an idol of theirs, in the morning it was on the idol's head; they put it beneath again and the next day it was above again, so they knew it was God's doing, took it out and left it in one of their villages, where a disease struck the villagers in their necks; they put it on a cart yoked to two cows and sent them off, and it is said that angels drove them until they reached the Israelite elders; Ibn Kathir thinks the verse most naturally means that the angels carried it themselves. {cite:qisas-al-anbiya-ibn-kathir-iqra-ebook scan p. 585} In the Tafsir, Ibn Abbas says the angels came carrying the ark between heaven and earth until they set it down in front of Talut, al-Suddi that the ark was in Talut's house in the morning so that they believed in the prophethood of Sham'un and obeyed Talut, and Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, that the angels drove it on a cart on a cow or two cows. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 889} Another account says that the ark had been at Ariha and that a girl among the captives from the Israelites told them to return it to be free of the disease; they put it on two cows, which went with it, and no one who came near it did not die, until the cows came close to the Israelite land; and it is said it was in a village in Palestine called Azdard. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 889}

## What these books do not say
The books on which this record is built do not give the fate of the Ark after its return, and the record says nothing about where it is now. The descriptions of the sakina and the remnant above are statements of Companions, Successors and those who took from the People of the Book, each attributed to the one who made it. {cite:tafsir-ibn-kathir scan vol. 1 p. 888}
