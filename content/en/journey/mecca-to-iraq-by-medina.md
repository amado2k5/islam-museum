## Overview
Ibn Battuta's route from [[place/mecca|Mecca]] to Iraq after his pilgrimage, by way of [[place/medina|Medina]], with the Iraqi caravan, as he recounts it in his journey {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.106–109}.

## The route
He left Mecca on the twentieth of Dhu al-Hijja after the farewell circumambulation, by way of Batn Marr, then 'Asfan, Khulays and the valley of al-Samak, and camped at [[place/badr|Badr]] {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.106–107}.

From Badr to al-Safra, then three days to Medina, where he stayed six days {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.107}.

From Medina to the valley of al-Maruws and then the land of Najd, with the fortress of Fayd among the stations, then al-Tha'laba and the pool of al-Marjum, then Zumala, the Pass of the Devil and Waqisa, then the minaret of al-Qurun and al-Udhayb, until [[place/al-qadisiyya|al-Qadisiyya]] {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.107–109}.

After al-Qadisiyya he alighted at the shrine of Ali at al-Najaf. He noted that Kufa and Baghdad lay ahead on the road, but did not say that he entered them at this point {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.109}.
