## Overview
The pilgrimage road from Kufa to Mecca, recorded by Qudama ibn Ja'far in the postal-station system, with its stations, its distances in miles, and the pools, wells and pulpits at each station {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp.193-194}.

## From Baghdad to Kufa
It begins at the City of Peace ([[place/baghdad|Baghdad]]) and goes to [[place/kufa|Kufa]], passing the bridge of Kuthaa, the palace of Ibn Hubayra, and the markets of Asad and Sayy. From Kufa to al-Qadisiyya is fifteen miles {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp.193-194}.

## The stations
Then al-Udhayb, which was a guard post between the Arabs and Persia at the edge of the desert, with two walls. Then al-Mughaytha, al-Qar'a, Waqisa, al-Aqaba, al-Qa', Zubala the inhabited, al-Shuqqa, the grave of al-Abbadi, and al-Tha'laba {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp.193-194}.

Al-Khuzayminya is walled, with a pulpit and a bath; it was called Zarud, and its sand is red. At Fayd there is a station for the agent, a canal, fields and a pulpit. Then come Tawz, al-Hajir and the mine of al-Nuqra. Al-Rabadha is rich in water and has a pulpit, and it is the mine of Banu Sulaym {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp.193-194}.

Then the Hijaz road to [[place/mecca|Mecca]] {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih pp.193-194}.
