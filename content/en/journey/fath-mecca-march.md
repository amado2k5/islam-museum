## Summary
The march of the conquest army — ten thousand fighters — from [[place/medina|Medina]] to [[place/mecca|Mecca]] in Ramadan 8 AH (January 630 CE) under the Prophet's command; he left Abu Ruhm Kulthum ibn Husayn al-Ghifari in charge of Medina {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/42}.

## The route
The army set out ten days into the month of Ramadan, fasting, until they reached the water of [[place/al-kudayd|al-Kudayd]] between [[place/asfan|Asfan]] and Amaj; there the Prophet broke his fast and the people broke theirs with him, to ease them {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/42}.

The army marched on until it camped at [[place/marr-al-zahran|Marr al-Zahran]] — Wadi Fatima — where the Prophet ordered fires kindled: ten thousand fires lit the valley and awed Quraysh {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/42}. That night al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib brought [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]], who became Muslim on the morrow's morning {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/44-46}.

Abu Sufyan was held at the valley's narrows by the mountain's ridge while the tribal battalions paraded in arms — among them the Ansar's and Muhajirun's battalion in iron — then the army moved on and camped at [[place/dhu-tuwa|Dhu Tuwa]], where the entry plan was laid and the axes divided {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/46-49}.

## For young visitors
The Muslim army travelled a long distance through the desert while fasting, then broke their fast at a water-well. As they neared Mecca they kindled ten thousand fires in the valley, so Mecca's people saw the army's size and surrendered peacefully without a great fight.
