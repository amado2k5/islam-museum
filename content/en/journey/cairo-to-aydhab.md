## Overview
Ibn Battuta's route from [[place/cairo|Cairo]] to [[place/aydhab|Aydhab]] on the Red Sea, across Upper Egypt on the way to the Hijaz, as he recounts it in his journey {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.26–31}.

## The route
He left Cairo by the Upper Egypt road, and on the night of his departure he stayed at the rubat that the Sahib Taj al-Din ibn Hanna built at Dayr al-Tin. He then passed Munyat al-Qa'id and Bush {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.26–27}.

Then the towns along the Nile bank followed one another: Manluwi, Manfalut and Akhmim {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.28–29}, then Luxor, Armant, Asna and Edfu {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.30}.

From al-Mutawani he hired camels and travelled with a group of Arabs across a desert with no settlements, and reached Humaytha, where the tomb of Abu al-Hasan al-Shadhili is {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.30}. From Humaytha it was fifteen days to Aydhab, a town abundant in fish and milk, to which grain and dates were carried from Upper Egypt {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 pp.30–31}.
