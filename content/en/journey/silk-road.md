## Overview
The Silk Road is the modern name for history's oldest network of overland and sea trade: from Xi'an in China through Kashgar and Ferghana to Samarkand, Bukhara, Merv and Nishapur, then on to Iraq, al-Sham and the Mediterranean, with branches to India and Arabia {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}.

## The cities on the road
Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan describes the road's cities one by one: Merv, capital of Khurasan and the gate to Transoxiana; Bukhara the seat; Samarkand, in his words the greatest of the cities beyond the Oxus; then Kashgar on the edge of China {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995}. Khans and ribats followed one another at every stage, guarding the caravans and easing exchange and barter {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}.

## What travelled the road
The road carried more than silk: spices, paper, porcelain and silver moved along it, and with them religions, sciences and crafts. Islam entered Central Asia with the caravans and the Sufis, and papermaking left Samarkand along it to the Islamic world and then to Europe {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}.
