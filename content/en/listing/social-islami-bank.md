## Summary
Social Islami Bank PLC is a Shari'ah-based Islamic bank in Bangladesh, operating from 22 November 1995 as Social Investment Bank. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en} Up to October 2021 it had 181 branches. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en}

## Origins
The article names its founders as M. A. Mannan, Hamid Al Gabid, a former OIC secretary-general, Abdullah Omar Nasseef and Ahmed M. Salah Jamjoom; it is an Islamic bank based on Sharia law forbidding usury. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en} In 2009 it took the name Social Islami Bank. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en}

## Allegations and responses
In July 2012 a US Senate subcommittee said it had found evidence of terrorism financing in the bank's documents and linked two of its shareholders to extremist groups. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en} The bank denied all the allegations and went to a local court to remove the two shareholders. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en}

## Ownership change and crisis
In November 2017 nine directors resigned after S. Alam Group bought half its shares, and a new board with ties to the group was appointed. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en} In 2022 bdnews24.com reported that US$1.64 billion had vanished through letters of credit; the bank protested the report and the site defended it on the basis of the Financial Intelligence Unit's findings; on 4 December 2022 the High Court ordered an investigation. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en} In January 2023 it took emergency loans from the central bank. {cite:wikipedia-social-islami-bank-en}
