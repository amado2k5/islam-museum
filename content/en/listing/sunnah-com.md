# Sunnah.com

## Summary

Sunnah.com is a website publishing the books of hadith in their Arabic texts and translations; it says its aim is to bring them to as many people in the world as possible, for research and for understanding Islam. {cite:wikipedia-sunnah-com-fi}

## What it publishes

At its core are the Six Books: [[work/sahih-al-bukhari|Sahih al-Bukhari]], Sahih Muslim, the Sunan of Abu Dawud and of al-Nasa'i, the Jami' of al-Tirmidhi and the Sunan of Ibn Majah, with the Muwatta of Malik, the Musnad of Ahmad, al-Nawawi's Riyad al-Salihin and others; its collections keep growing. {cite:wikipedia-sunnah-com-fi} In 2021 it offered English, Arabic and Urdu. {cite:wikipedia-sunnah-com-fi}

## Its sources and method, as it states them

The site says its Arabic text comes from al-eman.com and other now-defunct sources, that its English is by several translators and has been through two rounds of cleaning. {cite:sunnah-com-about} It explains that its English numbering differs from the Arabic because translators split or combined some books and hadith, that it is moving gradually to unified numbering following well-known scholars and editions, such as that of Muhammad Fuad Abd al-Baqi, and that a number shown in bold has been checked. {cite:sunnah-com-about} It shows the grades of Shaykh al-Albani and of Darussalam (Hafiz Zubair Ali Zai) for hadith outside the two Sahihs, and hopes to add those of other muhaddiths such as al-Arna'ut, Ahmad Shakir and Abu Ghudda. {cite:sunnah-com-about} It states four values: authenticity and referencing, comprehensiveness, ease of use, and openness. {cite:wikipedia-sunnah-com-fi}

## Today

The site is active at sunnah.com; by the Finnish article, its only Wikipedia article, it gives no information on who funds it. {cite:wikipedia-sunnah-com-fi} {cite:wikidata-sunnah-com}
