# AlMaghrib Institute

## Summary

The AlMaghrib Institute is an American non-profit Islamic studies organisation founded by Muhammad AlShareef in Houston, Texas, in 2002 (its infobox says 2001). It teaches in an intensive six-day format over two weekends, and in shorter three-day courses over a single weekend. {cite:wikipedia-almaghrib-en}

## Instructors and orientation

Most of its instructors are graduates of the Islamic University of Madinah, among them [[person/yasir-qadhi|Yasir Qadhi]], [[person/omar-suleiman|Omar Suleiman]] and Abu Eesa Niamatullah; scholars therefore describe it as Salafi in orientation, although its founder committed himself to no label other than "Islam" and "Muslim". Its founders aim at a seminary granting M.A. and Ph.D. degrees, with a permanent campus in the United States. {cite:wikipedia-almaghrib-en}

## Scrutiny and responses

The institute faced public scrutiny because recordings of Anwar al-Awlaki continued to be sold at its events until it banned them in 2009, and because analysts traced former students later involved in violent acts or plots, among them the man who tried to bomb a Detroit-bound airliner in December 2009 after attending a course in Houston. {cite:wikipedia-almaghrib-en} Yasir Qadhi told CNN that "we did not convince him of the validity of our views", which was "cause for regret", and its vice-president [[person/waleed-basyouni|Waleed Basyouni]] said its stance against extremism and violence had always been clear and that it condemned them in all forms. {cite:wikipedia-almaghrib-en} Other American Salafi groups, by contrast, have accused it of being "liberal" and "apolitical", and it has since avoided calling itself Salafi in public. {cite:wikipedia-almaghrib-en} The museum gives these views attributed to their authors and does not judge between them.

## Today

The institute is active at almaghrib.org. {cite:wikipedia-almaghrib-en}
