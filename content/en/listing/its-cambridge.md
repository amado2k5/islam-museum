# Islamic Texts Society

## Summary
The Islamic Texts Society is a peer-reviewed British publishing house in Cambridge concentrating on academic and general titles on Islam; it is registered as an educational charity in the UK. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-texts-society-en}

## Founding
It was founded in Cambridge in 1981 by a group of scholars including Aisha Gray Henry, and has worked with scholars such as Prince Ghazi bin Muhammad, David Burrell, Mohammad Hashim Kamali, Martin Lings, [[person/seyed-hossein-nasr|Seyyed Hossein Nasr]], Eric Ormsby and Timothy Winter. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-texts-society-en}

## What it publishes
It has published over sixty titles, among them Martin Lings's biography of the Prophet based on the earliest sources and works on hadith studies, jurisprudence and Sufism; its long-term aim is a comprehensive English library on Islam and its disciplines. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-texts-society-en} Its Ghazali Series brings the works of [[person/al-ghazali-abu-hamid|Abu Hamid al-Ghazali]] into English, two of its volumes winning the British Book Design and Production Award for best general paperback in 1991 and 1993; its Fundamental Rights and Liberties in Islam series includes several studies by Mohammad Hashim Kamali. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-texts-society-en}

## Imprints
Its imprint Quinta Essentia, devoted to symbolism, the arts and universal spirituality, was later transferred to the American publisher Fons Vitae. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-texts-society-en}
