# Yaqeen Institute for Islamic Research

## Summary

The Yaqeen Institute for Islamic Research is an independent American non-profit research institute founded in 2016, mainly interested in the study of modernity, Islam and Muslim societies, and in presenting complex Islamic topics to audiences outside academia. It was founded by the American scholar [[person/omar-suleiman|Omar Suleiman]] and is based in Irving, Texas. {cite:wikipedia-yaqeen-institute-en}

## What it publishes

The institute publishes research papers, infographics and videos, among them khutbas, interviews, Ramadan series and Qur'anic exegesis; its YouTube channel has more than 1.7 million subscribers and over 270 million views, by InfluenceWatch. {cite:wikipedia-yaqeen-institute-en} In 2021 it launched Yaqeen Curriculum for North American Islamic schools, and it holds an annual academic conference in Chicago with the Muslim American Society. {cite:wikipedia-yaqeen-institute-en} Its revenue was about $8.76 million in 2023, by its tax filing. {cite:wikipedia-yaqeen-institute-en}

## Its positions, as it publishes them

A prominent part of its work is what it calls combating "the Islamophobia network". It has published articles stating that Islam's prohibition of homosexual acts is "categorical" and calling for advocacy of the Islamic paradigm of gender and sexuality while supporting Muslims who struggle with same-sex attraction or gender dysphoria, and it criticised a Canadian poster for the International Day Against Homophobia; English Wikipedia accordingly lists it among organisations opposing LGBTQ rights. {cite:wikipedia-yaqeen-institute-en} After the Gaza war began it published resources on Islam, Palestine and international politics "from a Qur'anic lens", has written on the persecution of the Uyghurs, and its Canadian branch launched IMIRT, an app for reporting Islamophobic incidents, in 2024. {cite:wikipedia-yaqeen-institute-en} The museum gives these positions attributed to the institute as it publishes them, and does not judge them.

## Beginnings and today

The earliest capture of its site in the Internet Archive is dated 7 August 2016. {cite:wayback-yaqeen-2016} The institute is active at yaqeeninstitute.org.
