## Summary
Faisal Islamic Bank (Sudan) is an Islamic bank in Sudan that describes itself as having "Islamic orientation" and "Sudanese features". {cite:wikipedia-faisal-islamic-bank-sudan-en} The Indian site Siasat Daily described it in 2016 as Sudan's largest lender, favouring low-risk, short-term loans. {cite:wikipedia-faisal-islamic-bank-sudan-en}

## Origins
According to the bank, it was founded in May 1977 by 86 Sudanese and Saudi founders and nationals of other Islamic states, who subscribed and paid up half of the authorised capital. {cite:wikipedia-faisal-islamic-bank-sudan-en} It is headquartered in Khartoum, with 65 branches mostly in Khartoum, Khartoum North and Omdurman, and 1,288 employees. {cite:wikipedia-faisal-islamic-bank-sudan-en}

## Ownership
The Chicago Tribune reported in 2001 that the Dar Al-Maal Al-Islami Trust, founded in 1981 to spread Islamic banking, owned 19% of it, and the article notes that it is partly owned by Saudis. {cite:wikipedia-faisal-islamic-bank-sudan-en}
