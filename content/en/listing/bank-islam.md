## Summary
Bank Islam Malaysia Berhad began operating on 1 July 1983 as Malaysia's first Islamic bank; it was set up to give the country's Muslims a Sharia-based alternative in banking. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms}

## Founding and capital
It was established with authorised capital of RM500 million and paid-up capital of RM80 million, and its shareholders' funds reached RM2.5 billion in December 2010. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms} Lembaga Tabung Haji, Malaysia's pilgrims' fund, was its main holding company, and the bank aligned its financial year with the fund's. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms}

## Losses and recovery
After heavy losses in 2005 and 2006 the bank launched a recovery plan in October 2006 under its new managing director Zukri Samat, covering recapitalisation, IT renewal, a transformation programme, cost rationalisation and staff development. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms} It was recapitalised with RM1.02 billion in October 2006 and RM540 million by October 2009, and its net non-performing financing ratio fell from 13.6% in September 2006 to 4.9% in June 2009. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms} It recorded profit before zakat and tax of RM503.4 million for the eighteen months to 31 December 2010. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms}

## Products and recognition
In May 2009 it received the Reader's Digest Platinum Trusted Brand award for Islamic financial services. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms} It bought a 20% stake in Amana Bank of Sri Lanka, that country's first Islamic commercial bank. {cite:wikipedia-bank-islam-malaysia-ms}
