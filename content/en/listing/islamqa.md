## Summary
IslamQA (Islam Question & Answer, islamqa.info) is a website of fatwas and questions and answers, begun by [[person/muhammad-salih-al-munajjid|Muhammad Salih al-Munajjid]] in 1996 and still run under his general supervision. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en} {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-ar} It is counted among the first online fatwa services, and one writer suggests it may have been the very first. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en}

## What it is
The site describes itself as an academic, educational and da'wa website whose vision is to be "an encyclopaedia about Islam", and it takes questions from Muslims and others on religious, psychological and social matters. {cite:islamqa-about-us} Its answers are published numbered and dated with a summary of the reply; the museum holds several of them, such as [[work/fatwa-islamqa-salawat-on-friday|its answer on invoking blessings on the Prophet on Friday]]. {cite:islamqa}

## Beginnings
The English and Arabic Wikipedia articles give 1996 as the start; the Wikidata item and the founding line of the Arabic infobox give 1997. The museum records both. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en} {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-ar} {cite:wikidata-islamqa} The site first ran at www.islam-qa.com, and its earliest capture in the Internet Archive is dated 19 January 1998, under the title "Islam Questions and Answers". {cite:wayback-islam-qa-com-1998} In the capture of 25 June 1998, dated on the page 30 Safar 1419, the site presents itself as answering questions from Muslims and non-Muslims, states that the answers are composed by al-Munajjid, and sorts the questions under creed, principles of jurisprudence, jurisprudence, da'wa, manners, character, history and the Prophet's biography, and upbringing. {cite:wayback-islam-qa-com-1998}

## Its method, as the site states it
The site says it promotes the creed of Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jama'a and the way of the salaf, and that it seeks to base its answers on the Qur'an, the sound Sunna and the writings of scholars, including the imams of the four schools, and on the resolutions of fiqh councils. {cite:islamqa-about-us} The Arabic article adds that it also draws on the fatwas of Abd al-Aziz ibn Baz and Muhammad ibn Salih al-Uthaymin, the Permanent Committee for Fatwas and the resolutions of the Council of Senior Scholars in Saudi Arabia. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-ar} The site states that every question and answer on it was prepared, revised or edited under al-Munajjid's supervision. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en}

## Languages
The site appears in sixteen languages: Arabic (its original language), English, French, Turkish, Russian, Urdu, Persian, Chinese, Uyghur, Spanish, Tajik, Indonesian, Hindi, Bengali, German and Portuguese. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-ar} The English article gives the same count and lists the fields it covers: the basic tenets of faith, manners and morals, Islamic history and Islamic politics. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en} It also has an app on Apple's App Store. {cite:wikidata-islamqa}

## Reach
The measurement service Similarweb estimated about 13.66 million visits in March 2021 and about 10.1 million in January 2022, when Islamweb was ahead at about 17.2 million. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en} The Arabic article reports about 19 million visits in October 2023 by Similarweb, with a global rank of 4,302. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-ar} These are dated figures from one measuring service, not a count of readers. The site has Wikipedia articles in at least eleven languages, Hausa, Albanian and Ukrainian among them. {cite:wikidata-islamqa}

## The 2010 block in Saudi Arabia
In August 2010 a Saudi royal decree reserved the issuing of public fatwas to the Council of Senior Scholars; restrictions of this kind had existed since 2005 but were seldom enforced. In September that year the site was blocked in the kingdom because it issued independent fatwas, as Al Arabiya reported. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en} Christopher Boucek of the Carnegie Endowment described the decree as the latest example of the state working to assert its primacy over the religious establishment. {cite:wikipedia-islamqa-en}

## Today
The site is active at islamqa.info, and the Internet Archive holds more than 1,200 captures of its first domain between 1998 and 2026. {cite:wikidata-islamqa} {cite:wayback-islam-qa-com-1998}
