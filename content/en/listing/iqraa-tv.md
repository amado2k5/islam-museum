## Summary
Iqraa is a Saudi Islamic religious television channel, launched on 1 Rajab 1419 AH, 21 October 1998, within the Arab Radio and Television network (ART), and founded by the businessman Saleh Abdullah Kamel. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar} {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-en} The channel calls itself Islam's first ambassador in visual media, under the slogan "for all people". {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar} Its name is the first word of the Qur'an revealed to [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] in Muslim belief, [[passage/quran-96-1|"Read"]]. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-en}

## What it offers
The channel addresses the whole family, with programmes set aside for children, women, young people and adults on matters of faith and daily life. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar} It says it seeks to meet viewers' everyday problems from an Islamic perspective with a modern vision, and to present the moderate face of Islam to the West. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-en} Its programmes, many filmed at the Misk studios in Cairo, include Muhammad al-Arifi's *Da' Basmatak*, Saad al-Buraik's *Khudhu 'Anni Manasikakum*, Jasem al-Mutawa's *The Happy Family*, Mahmoud al-Masri's *A Night in the Prophet's House*, [[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]]'s *Khada'uka fa-Qalu* and Abla al-Kahlawi's *Khayr Nisa' al-Ard*. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar} Its international output includes *The Market*, on finance from an Islamic perspective, featuring the owner Saleh Kamel, and programmes for Muslims in the West such as *Muslim Minority* and *The Role of the Masjid in the West*. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-en}

## Languages and broadcasting
The channel began in Arabic and aired its first English-language programmes in August 2011 through Iqraa International, which says its mission is to teach non-Arab Muslims. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-en} It was carried on Arabsat, Nilesat and Hot Bird, and from 2012 became available in the Americas, Australia and New Zealand through the internet service myTV. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar} {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-en}

## Today
The channel announced that it would end its satellite broadcasts, which stopped from 1 January 2025; it continues online and on social media. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar} It has Wikipedia articles in four languages. {cite:wikipedia-iqraa-ar}
