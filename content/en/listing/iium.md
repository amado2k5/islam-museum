## Summary
The International Islamic University Malaysia (IIUM) is a public university headquartered at Gombak in Selangor, near Kuala Lumpur, with six other campuses: two medical campuses and a foundation-studies centre in Pahang, two city campuses in Kuala Lumpur, and a languages and tourism campus at Pagoh in Johor. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} It is sponsored by eight governments and the [[group/organisation-of-islamic-cooperation|Organisation of Islamic Cooperation]], and was established on 23 May 1983 on Islamic principles. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}

## Origins
Prime Minister [[person/mahathir-mohamad|Mahathir Mohamad]] conceived the idea in 1982 at a meeting of OIC leaders on the Islamisation of knowledge, and Mohd Kamal Hassan of the National University of Malaysia wrote the first working paper on the concept. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} Ismail al-Faruqi played an advisory role in its foundation, as in that of the International Islamic University in Islamabad in 1980. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} The university took up the concept of "integration of knowledge with morals" adopted at the International Convention of Islamic Education in Mecca in 1976. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}

It was first incorporated as a company under the Companies Act 1965, because its languages of instruction were to be English and Arabic rather than Malay; it became a public university on 23 May 1983 by an establishment order of the king, Ahmad Shah of Pahang, after an exchange of diplomatic notes between Malaysia, seven other governments and the OIC. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} The first academic session, from 8 July 1983, enrolled 153 students in economics and law, who graduated at the first convocation on 10 October 1987. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}

## Studying there
The university awards bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees in fourteen faculties, among them the Ahmad Ibrahim Kulliyyah of Laws, the AbdulHamid AbuSulayman Kulliyyah of Islamic Revealed Knowledge and Human Sciences, and engineering, architecture, medicine, dentistry and pharmacy. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} Its language of instruction is English, with Arabic in the Islamic sciences; it is one of only two public universities in Malaysia with English as its official language. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} It requires its students to attend *usrah*, a weekly Islamic study circle, and a community-service programme, Usrah in Action. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} In 2019 it had about 26,000 students and 1,900 academic staff. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} By 2020 it had produced more than 70,000 graduates from over a hundred countries since 1987, most of them Malaysian. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}

## Achievements
The university helped prepare the Malaysian Shariah Index, launched by the Prime Minister on 10 February 2015; its team won the 2012 Proton Green Mobility Challenge with an electric car; and its debating teams placed well between 2014 and 2017. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} It says it has won more International Green Gown sustainability awards than any other institution, six in all, including Sustainability Institution of the Year in 2020 and 2024. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}

## People
Its alumni include [[person/omar-suleiman|Omar Suleiman]], president of the Yaqeen Institute, and its former teachers include [[person/ahmet-davutoglu|Ahmet Davutoğlu]], who taught there from 1993 to 1996 before becoming prime minister of Turkey. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}

## Today
Its constitutional head since 2019 is Tunku Azizah Aminah Maimunah Iskandariah, its tenth president Abdul Rashid Hussain since 1 July 2025, and its motto since 2000 "Garden of Knowledge and Virtue". {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} On 2 September 2025 it set up the Institute of International and ASEAN Studies. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en} It has Wikipedia articles in twenty-five languages. {cite:wikipedia-iium-en}
