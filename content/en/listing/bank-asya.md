## Summary
Bank Asya was a Turkish participation bank in Istanbul that operated from 1996 to 2016. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en} The sources the article cites describe it as strongly tied to the Gülen movement, led by the preacher Fethullah Gülen, and widely considered founded and run by his followers. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en}

## Opening
Its first branch opened in Altunizade, Istanbul, on 24 October 1996, at a ceremony attended by Deputy Prime Minister Tansu Çiller, State Minister Abdullah Gül and Istanbul's mayor Recep Tayyip Erdoğan. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en}

## Closure
On 18 September 2014 President Erdoğan told a meeting of the Turkish Industry and Business Association, without naming the bank, that "that bank is already bankrupt." {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en} The article quotes the banking regulator's Decision No. 6318 of May 2015 transferring the bank's management to the Savings Deposit Insurance Fund. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en} On 22 July 2016 the regulator cancelled its licence as part of the measures after the coup attempt, and on 16 November 2017 an Istanbul commercial court declared it bankrupt. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en}

## After 2016
After the 2016 coup attempt the Turkish government accused the Gülen movement of plotting it and designated it a terrorist organisation. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en} The UK Home Office's country note on the movement (October 2023), as the article reports it, says that being a partner, employee or customer of the bank became a factor in criminal investigations for membership. {cite:wikipedia-bank-asya-en}
