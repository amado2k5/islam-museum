## Summary
The Islamic Bank of Thailand (iBank) is a state enterprise established in 2002 that operates according to the principles of Sharia as practised in Islamic banking, and serves all customers regardless of religion. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-bank-of-thailand-en}

## Ownership
The Ministry of Finance holds 48.54%, the Government Savings Bank 39.81% and Krung Thai Bank 9.83%; the ministry's direct stake is capped at 49%, but with the other state banks the government controls over 98% of its shares. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-bank-of-thailand-en}

## Course
It was set up under the Islamic Bank of Thailand Act 2002 and began operating in 2003; in November 2005 it acquired Krung Thai Bank's Shariah banking services, raising its branches from 18 to 27. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-bank-of-thailand-en}

## Losses and bailout
In 2014 it reported a net loss of 9.5 billion baht. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-bank-of-thailand-en} The Finance Ministry injected almost 20 billion baht to raise its capital after its non-performing loans reached 50 billion baht, half its portfolio; critics saw this as evidence of weak management at state-run financial institutions. {cite:wikipedia-islamic-bank-of-thailand-en}
