## Overview
The lava field to which the people of [[person/rifaa-ibn-zayd-al-judhami|Rifa'a ibn Zayd al-Judhami]] went, and where they settled after they answered and became Muslims. See [[event/islam-of-rifaa-ibn-zayd-al-judhami|the Islam of Rifa'a and his people]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/243}

## What is not covered here
Its location and coordinates will be added later from Yaqut's dictionary of places or from the dictionary of what is hard to read.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Harra al-Rajla': he cites Ibn al-A'rabi that it is the hard, solid harra; others say it is the one whose top is black and base white; al-Asma'i says a rough track is called rajil and a harra rajla' is a coarse, rugged one — and for Yaqut it is the proper name of a harra in the lands of the Banu al-Qayn ibn Jasr between Medina and Syria, with a cross-reference to his "al-Rajla'" entry. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.246} He quotes verses of al-Akhnas ibn Shihab and of al-Ra'i naming the Harrat al-Rajla'. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.246} Under "al-Rajla'" he makes it a water beside a mountain called al-Mirda belonging to the Banu Sa'id ibn Qart, called Salb al-'Ilm; he quotes Abu Mansur that Harrat Rajla' is flat land full of stones, and Abu al-Haytham that a rajla' is a hard, rough harra that neither horse nor camel can work and only a foot-traveller crosses. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.28}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's harra in the country of the Banu al-Qayn ibn Jasr between Medina and Syria is the same northern-Hejazi harra belt the record names from Ibn Hisham; the settlement there of Rifa'a ibn Zayd al-Judhami's people is Ibn Hisham's report, not Yaqut's, and Yaqut does not name the Judham in his entry. The museum's points for Harrat al-Rajla' and Medina stand about 808.9 km apart (haversine), consistent with a northern Hejazi site between Medina and Syria.
