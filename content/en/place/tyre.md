## Overview
Tyre is described by Pleiades as the ancient city of Tyre, today es-Sur on the coast of Lebanon, a UNESCO World Heritage Site in part for its important archaeological remains, mainly from Roman times. {cite:pleiades 678437}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Tyrus/Col. Septimia Severa» (Pleiades no. 678437), classed as a settlement, a modern settlement and a findspot, with a time span from 10000 BCE to 2100 CE. Among the names it records are «صور» in Arabic, «Τύρος» in Greek and «Ṣurru» in Akkadian. It describes the place as the ancient city of Tyre, modern es-Sur on the coast of Lebanon. {cite:pleiades 678437}

## In Nomisma
The Nomisma numismatic vocabulary links this entry to the concept «tyre», and its linked collections record 737 coins or coin types that name it as issuer, authority or mint. {cite:nomisma tyre}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Sur, with a damma on the sad and a sukun on the waw, places it in the fourth clime, and says that in the language it means a horn, as the commentators take it in the verse about the blowing of the horn; he calls it a famous city where many ascetics and scholars lived, and one of the frontier ports of the Muslims. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.433} He describes it as overlooking the Syrian sea and thrusting into it like a palm on a forearm, surrounded by sea on every side but the fourth, where its gate lies, and very strongly fortified. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.433} He says the Muslims took it in the days of Umar ibn al-Khattab and held it until 518, when the Franks besieged it until its provisions ran out; the ruler of Egypt, al-Amir, had sent supplies by sea but the wind drove the fleet back to Egypt, and the people surrendered on safe-conduct less than ten days before it arrived. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.433} He adds that it was still in Frankish hands in his day, that it is counted in the district of al-Urdunn, and that it lies six farsakhs from Acre, to its east as he puts it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.433} He lists scholars attributed to it, among them the hafiz Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah al-Suri, who heard hadith at an advanced age until he became a leading authority, moved to Baghdad in 418 and died there in 441. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.433} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.434}
