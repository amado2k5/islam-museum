## Overview
Al-Hilla is a city in Iraq on the Euphrates, near Babylon. Ibn Jubayr passed through it on his way to Syria. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907}

The coordinates match a modern town of the same name; an additional source is needed to confirm the identification.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says al-Hilla is a proper name for several places, the best known being Hillat Bani Mazyad, a large city between Kufa and Baghdad that used to be called al-Jami'ayn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.294} He gives its astronomical coordinates and says the first to build it up and settle there was Sayf al-Dawla Sadaqa ibn Mansur ibn Dubays ibn Ali ibn Mazyad al-Asadi, whose forefathers had lived at al-Dur on the Nil canal. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.294} As his power grew and the Seljuq rulers were busy with wars among themselves, he moved in Muharram 495 to al-Jami'ayn, on the west of the Euphrates, to be out of reach of the Sultan's pursuer; it was then a thicket where lions sheltered. He built houses there and his companions followed, merchants came, and it became among the finest places of Iraq in his lifetime; it stayed prosperous after he was killed and in Yaqut's day was the chief town of that district. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.294} He quotes verses of Ibrahim ibn Uthman al-Ghazzi, who visited it and did not praise it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.294} On the next page he lists other places called al-Hilla, which are different from this one. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.295}
