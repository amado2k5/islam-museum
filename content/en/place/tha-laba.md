## Overview
Al-Tha'labiyya is a town of the desert lands, where Arabs gather and where there is a busy market, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 381} In the same passage he names Zubala, once a town, of which only a trace and a place where travellers stop now remain. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 381} Al-Qadisiyya, he notes, lies on the edge of the desert two stages from Kufa, and fodder for camels is gathered there on the road to the Hijaz. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 381}

Yaqut describes Batan as a station on the road to Kufa from the Mecca side, after al-Shuqq and before al-Tha'labiyya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 619}

Yaqut places al-Tanahi between Batan and al-Tha'labiyya on the Mecca road, and gives a mosque of al-Tha'labiyya eight miles beyond it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 817}

Yaqut also reports al-Waqidi's account that Khalid ibn al-Walid, after Yamama and Bahrain, went from Medina to Iraq by the road of Fayd and al-Tha'labiyya, and that al-Waqidi denied he passed through Basra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 598}
