## Overview
Oman is a land in the Arabian Peninsula. Al-Idrisi describes it as self-governing and well populated, with bananas, pomegranates, figs, grapes and other fruits. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 155}

## Details
Al-Idrisi places Oman to the north of Mahra, the land that borders it. He describes Oman as a self-governing country with a large population, rich in palms and in the fruits he calls the Jarumi fruits: bananas, pomegranates, figs, grapes and others. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 155}

Its two best-known towns are Sur and Qalhat, both on the shore of the Persian Sea. They are small but thriving, they drink from wells, and a little pearl is fished off them. The land road between them is one long stage; by sea the distance is less. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 155}

Mahra, beside it, is mostly empty land that only its camel-riding people cross. Al-Idrisi says their speech is hard to follow and that it keeps the old Himyaritic tongue. Their livelihood is camels and goats; they eat fish and dates and drink milk, and they do not know wheat. One who eats wheat for the first time suffers for it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 155}

He gives the length of their land as nine hundred miles, its breadth between fifteen and twenty-five miles, and the whole ground as loose sand that the wind shifts from place to place. From the last town of Shihr to Aden is three hundred miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 155}
