## Overview
Suways are two cities which were on the Red Sea, most of them now in ruin. Al-Idrisi mentions that their people used to drink from the spring of Suways, a brackish spring which the drinker cannot swallow. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.348}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Suways: a small town on the shore of the Sea of Qulzum in the district of Egypt, in his day the Egyptians' port of embarkation for Mecca and Medina; between it and al-Fustat are seven days through waterless waste, and provisions are carried to it from Egypt on pack animals, then loaded onto the ships that take them to the two sanctuaries. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.286}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Suways is a single working port of his own time (the seventh/thirteenth century), while the record's al-Idrisi passage describes two towns at Qulzum, mostly ruined, whose people drank from the brackish Suways spring — two states of one place on the Qulzum shore.
