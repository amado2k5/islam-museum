## Overview
Sirt is a city in sandy land two miles from the sea. It has remnants of palms but no olives, and many figs. Its wells are few, so its people depend on rainwater stored in cisterns (mawajil), according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that Sirt lies two miles from the sea. It has a wall of earth, and sand lies all round it. It has remnants of palm trees but no olives, a great deal of mulberry, and many remnants of fig trees. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}

He adds that the Arabs have ruined most of this by their damage, and that the grapes and fruit the people once had have perished. Only what remains in the valley bottoms and on the mountain tops survives. The grass is not as rich as at Awjila, and the dates are not as plentiful as at Wadan. Their palms once gave more than they needed. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}

The water comes from rain stored in cisterns, the wells are few, and Berber tribes live on the land. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}

The road from Tripoli to Sirt, eastward, is two hundred and thirty miles in eleven stages. The traveller leaves Tripoli for al-Mujtana, twenty miles; then Wardasa, twenty-two; Rughugha, twenty-five; Tawargha, twenty-two; then al-Mansaf, the palaces of Hassan ibn al-Nu'man al-Ghassani, al-Asnam, and Sirt. The whole route lies in the land of two tribes, ʿAwf and Dabbab. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}
