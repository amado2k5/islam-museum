## Summary
A broad valley one stage north of [[place/mecca|Mecca]] (known today as Wadi Fatima); the conquest army of ten thousand fighters camped in it, kindling ten thousand fires in a scene that struck terror into Quraysh; and in it [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] declared his Islam {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/42-46}.

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
Ibn Ishaq relates that the Prophet camped at Marr al-Zahran at evening and ordered his companions, and every man kindled a fire — ten thousand fires lighting the mountains and horizons {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/42}. There al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib brought Abu Sufyan on the Prophet's white mule, and the tribes paraded their arms at the valley's narrows along the mountain's ridge {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/44-47}.

## For young visitors
A great valley near Mecca where the army kindled ten thousand fires at night — a sight dazzling and frightening to anyone watching from afar — making Mecca's leaders realise resistance was useless.
