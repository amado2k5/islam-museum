## Summary
Bazla was a town on the western side of the Rhine river in the land of the Germans — a fine town with an earthen wall, many villages and farms, broad canals and ample resources; al-Idrisi counts it among the German lands he sets out to describe in his book. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.745}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that adjoining the Burgundy of the Germans is a narrow strip of the Germans' land containing Bazla, and that the town of Bazla stands on the west side of the Rhine river. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.745} From the town of Alma to the town of Bazla in the land of Burgundy was one hundred and seventy miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.746}
