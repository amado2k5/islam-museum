## Overview
Jordan, in the record's data, is a Levantine kingdom whose capital is [[place/amman|Amman]]. The medieval source read for this page, Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan, does not describe a state of that name. It describes al-Urdunn as a province of [[place/al-sham|Syria]] and as a river, and this page reports only that, kept apart from the modern kingdom {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.147}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut opens the entry with the vocalisation: a damma on the first letter, a sukun, a damma on the dal and a shadda on the nun. He gives the opinion of the grammarian Abu Ali that the initial hamza is an added letter, and quotes lines of Abu Dahlab and of Adi ibn al-Riqa' that use the name. Lexicographers, he says, hold that al-urdunn also means drowsiness in the speech of the Arabs, and he quotes a line of rajaz for this; to him the sense seems rather to be force and overpowering, since the line makes little sense with drowsiness. He adds that Ibn al-Sikkit recorded no verb from the word and said that the name of the district is taken from it {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.147}.

The chroniclers, as Yaqut relays them, say that al-Urdunn and Filastin were sons of Sam, son of Aram, son of Sam, son of Nuh. He counts al-Urdunn among the five ajnad of Syria and calls it a wide district that takes in the Ghawr, [[place/tiberias|Tiberias]], [[place/sur|Tyre]], [[place/akka|Acre]] and the lands between {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.147}.

## Two rivers called al-Urdunn
Yaqut quotes the philosopher Ahmad ibn al-Tayyib al-Sarakhsi for a distinction between a greater and a lesser Urdunn. The greater is a river that runs into the lake of Tiberias, gathering water from mountains and springs and irrigating most of the estates of the jund on the coastal side. The lesser leaves the lake, runs south through the middle of the Ghawr and waters its estates, whose main crop, he says, is sugar, shipped to the lands of the east. Villages on it include Baysan, Qarawa, Ariha and al-Awja, and near Tiberias it is crossed by a great bridge of more than twenty arches. Joined by the Yarmuk, it flows on to empty into the stinking lake at the western edge of the Ghawr {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.147} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.148}. For the modern river see [[place/jordan-river|the Jordan River]].

## The conquest and later references
Yaqut names districts of the jund, among them Tiberias, Baysan, Bayt Ras, Jadar, Saffuriya, Tyre and Acre {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.148}. Telling the conquest from the books of the futuh, he says that [[person/shurahbil-ibn-hasana|Shurahbil ibn Hasana]] took al-Urdunn by force except Tiberias, whose people made terms keeping half of their houses and churches and a site for the Muslims' mosque. When they broke the agreement under Umar, Abu Ubayda sent [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] with four thousand men, who took it on the same terms as Shurahbil's. The other cities and fortresses of the jund, he says, fell on the same terms with little or no fighting, among them Baysan, Afiq, Jarash, Bayt Ras, Qadas, the Golan, Acre, Tyre and Saffuriya. When Amr reached the coast the Byzantine forces there were many and he asked for help. Abu Ubayda sent [[person/yazid-ibn-abi-sufyan|Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan]], with his brother [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Muawiya]] in the vanguard, and Yazid and Amr took the coast, in which Yaqut says Muawiya's part was a good one {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.148}.

He records that the shipyard of al-Urdunn stayed at Acre until [[person/hisham-ibn-abd-al-malik|Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik]] moved it to Tyre, where it remained into the Abbasid period. He quotes [[person/al-mutanabbi|al-Mutanabbi]]'s verses praising Badr ibn Ammar, who held the frontier posts of al-Urdunn and the coast for Muhammad ibn Ra'iq, and a tale from al-Yazidi of a young woman in a howdah on al-Ma'mun's Byzantine campaign who asked him for a poem, and he recited verses mentioning lightning "from the side of al-Urdunn or Damascus". He ends with the nisba al-Urduni: [[person/hassan-ibn-malik-ibn-bahdal|Hassan ibn Malik ibn Bahdal]] al-Kalbi is called by it because he governed al-Urdunn and Palestine, and through him, Yaqut says, the affair of Marwan ibn al-Hakam was set in order against the Zubayrids. The account continues on the next page of the source, which is not read here {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.148}.

## A note on scope
The sources read for this page say nothing about the modern kingdom, its borders or its history, so none is stated; its identity here rests only on the record's data (capital Amman). Yaqut's province is described through Tiberias, Acre, Tyre and the Ghawr, and his entry should not be read as a description of the modern state.
