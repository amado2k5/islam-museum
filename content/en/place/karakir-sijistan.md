## Summary
Karakir was a fire temple in Sijistan, founded — in al-Mas'udi's account of the fire temples of the Persians — by Bahman ibn Isfandiyar ibn Gustasp. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 549}

## Details
In the chapter on the reports of the Persian fire temples in Muruj al-Dhahab, al-Mas'udi enumerates the places of the fires and their histories: among them a fire temple in Sijistan called Karakir, founded by Bahman ibn Isfandiyar ibn Gustasp; another in the land of China toward al-Barkand; and a fire temple in the city of Arjan in Fars, founded in the days of Bahrasf. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 549}

Al-Mas'udi also mentions the fire temple of Istakhr in Fars, venerated by the Magians: Humaya daughter of Bahman ibn Isfandiyar had taken it out and made it a fire temple before the fire was removed and it fell into ruin. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 549} Karakir, in this account, is thus one of the ancient Zoroastrian fire temples of Sijistan, ascribed by al-Mas'udi to one of the early Persian kings. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 549}
