## Overview
Multan is a city in southern Punjab, Pakistan. Al-Mas'udi mentions that there, on the river Mihran in the land of Sind, is the temple in which stands the idol famous among its people. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.557} Ibn al-Nadim relates that there is the house said to be one of the seven houses, in which an iron idol is held up in the middle of the dome by magnetic stones, and that India makes pilgrimage to it from the far reaches of its lands. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.485}

As for Muhammad ibn al-Qasim's conquest of it in the year 712 CE, and its being a centre of Sufism, these need an additional source.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Multan as a town in India on the same line as Ghazna. He quotes al-Istakhri that it is about half the size of al-Mansura, and that it was called the "gold-house of Multan", with an idol that the Indians revere and visit from the farthest parts of their land. Each year a great sum is offered to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.227}

He describes the idol as a seated figure, its body covered in red leather, with a gold crown and eyes of gems, and says that the ruler of Multan took most of the offerings. When Indian armies came to attack, they brought the idol out and showed it broken and burned, and then withdrew. Yaqut says the city took its name from this idol. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.227}

He gives the name "gold-house" as the result of a conquest in early Islam: the place had been poor and short of water until the Muslims found much gold there. Its ruler, he says, was a Qurashi of the line of Samma ibn Lu'ayy, who did not obey the ruler of al-Mansura. He reports that Muhammad ibn al-Qasim ibn Abi Aqil killed Dahir and took Multan in the caliphate of al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik. Yaqut gives no Christian-era date for the conquest. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.227} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.228}
