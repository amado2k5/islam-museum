## Overview
Beirut: a city on the bank of the salt sea. Near it is a mountain with good iron ore that is carried to the land of al-Sham, and there is a thicket of trees that extends to Mount Lebanon. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.371}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] vocalizes the name Bayrut and describes it as a famous city on the coast of the Syrian sea, counted among the dependencies of Damascus, three parasangs from Sidon, in the fourth clime. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.732} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.733} He quotes verses by al-Walid ibn Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik that mention it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.733}

He records that it remained in Muslim hands in good condition until [[person/baldwin-i-jerusalem|Baldwin the Frank]], who had taken Jerusalem, besieged it and took it by force on Friday 21 Shawwal 503 AH; that [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Saladin]] recovered it from them in 583 AH; and that it was "in their hands to this day", that is, back with the Franks in his own time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.733} Among scholars named after it is al-Walid ibn Mazyad al-Udhri al-Bayruti, who transmitted from al-Awza'i. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.733}
