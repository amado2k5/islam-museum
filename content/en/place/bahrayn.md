## Overview
Bahrayn: the old name for the peninsula's eastern coast region; home of Abd al-Qays and clans of Bakr and Tamim, and one of the seats of Zoroastrianism.

## The place
Abd al-Qays, clans of Bakr ibn Wa'il and clans of Tamim emigrated to Bahrayn and settled there {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya}. Kinda had settled it first, then were forced to leave and settled in Hadramawt {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

Zoroastrianism was mostly among the Arabs neighbouring Persia: in the Arabs' Iraq and in Bahrayn — al-Ahsa — and Hajar and the adjoining shores of the Arabian Gulf {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran}.

## For young visitors
Bahrayn is an old name for the peninsula's eastern coast — not only today's small island; many tribes lived in it.
