## Overview
Ronda is one of the fortified cities west of Malaga, in [[place/al-andalus|al-Andalus]]. Al-Maqqari mentions that the lord of Castile besieged it in Jumada I of the year 890 AH, when its people had gone out to support Dhakwan. He demolished its walls, and its people came out under safe conduct. All that land obeyed him, and nothing remained west of [[place/malaga|Malaga]] except whoever had entered his obedience. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.520}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Runda", with a damma on the first letter and a sukun on the second, and calls it a strong stronghold (ma'qil) in al-Andalus among the districts of Takurna, an ancient city on a flowing river, with wide tillage and full udders. He reports from al-Silafi that Abu al-Hasan Saqi ibn Khalaf ibn Sulayman al-Asadi al-Rundi used to visit him after returning from the Hijaz in 530 and told him that Ronda is a fortress between Seville and Malaga; al-Silafi calls him a man of evident goodness who heard in al-Andalus and went back to his own country. He also names Abu Ali Umar ibn Muhammad al-Rundi, the littérateur, from Malaga, who narrated from Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Fakhari and Abu Zayd al-Suhayli and was an excellent elder. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.73-74} His headword for the kura is "Takurna" in the spelling Takurni, with a fatha on the kaf and a sukun on the ra, which al-Sam'ani vowelled with a damma on kaf and ra and a doubled nun; it is a large kura of al-Andalus with strong mountains, from which several rivers rise without entering it, and it holds the stronghold of Ronda; Abu Amir Muhammad ibn Sa'd al-Takurni, the secretary and eloquent poet, is named from it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.6-7} In a separate entry he lists "Shirs", another fortress of the districts of Takurna, which he does not link with Ronda. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.382}

## The museum's identification
The spelling of the kura differs between the Ronda entry (Takurna) and its own headword (Takurni); the museum reproduces both as the text has them. Yaqut does not mention the siege of Jumada I 890 that the record takes from al-Maqqari: he wrote centuries before it, and the two sources do not conflict but complete each other. Al-Maqqari describes a city west of Malaga; Yaqut, on al-Silafi's report, puts it between Seville and Malaga. That agrees with the record's point (36.7462, -5.1612), about 66 km from the museum's Malaga point and about 101 km from its Seville point; but Yaqut gives no coordinates and "between" does not fix a place exactly, so the museum counts this as agreement in direction only. Yaqut does not say that the stronghold he names is the present town of Ronda: that is the museum's identification, on the name and the setting. The museum holds no record of the kura of Takurni and links nothing to it.
