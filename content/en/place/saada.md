## Overview
Sa'da is a small but civilised city in Yemen, with tanneries where fine leather is cured. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 146} Ibn Khurdadhbih calls it a great city where leather and sandals are tanned, although al-Idrisi calls it small. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p. 143} {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p. 144} Al-Idrisi says that the leather prepared for trade comes from Sa'da, because the workshop for it is there, except what is made in Sana'a. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 55} According to Ibn Khurdadhbih most of its merchants came from Basra, and the road passes through it from 'Arqa, then to al-A'mashiyya and Khayawan. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p. 197} Al-Idrisi gives the distance from Hayran to Sa'da as forty-eight miles, and from Sa'da to Sana'a as a hundred and eighty miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 55} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 146} Ibn Khurdadhbih puts the distance from Khayawan to Sa'da at sixteen farsakhs, and from Sana'a to Khayawan at twenty-four. {cite:masalik-mamalik-ibn-khurdadhbih p. 144} Yaqut mentions the market of al-Bar, a place in Yemen between Sa'da and 'Annar, and places Bawsan in the land of Huwlan on the Sa'da side. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 436} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 709}
