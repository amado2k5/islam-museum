## Summary
Ṭulaymaytha is a good fortress with a wall of stone, ten miles from the castle of al-Unayn, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.315}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Ṭulaymaytha in an account of a road that moves from one castle to the next. From Qamanas, a castle ten miles on, the road goes to Awṭalīṭ, a castle full of people that a traveller crosses in half a day; then to al-Arbaʿa Burūj, a castle a day away; then ten miles to the castle of al-Unayn. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.315}

From the castle of al-Unayn the traveller reaches the castle of Ṭulaymaytha, which he calls "a good fortress with a wall of stone." It lies ten miles from al-Unayn. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.315}

From Ṭulaymaytha the road continues towards Uwayrār; the text does not give that distance clearly here. The passage gives no account of the people of Ṭulaymaytha, its farming, or what the inside of the fortress held. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.315}

The entry therefore records its position on the road and the description of its walls. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.315}
