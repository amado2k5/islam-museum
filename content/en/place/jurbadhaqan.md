## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Jarbadhaqan is a large and well-known town near Hamadan between al-Karaj and Isfahan; Yaqut also lists a second Jarbadhaqan in Tabaristan, between Astarabad and Jurjan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.118}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a fatha on the jim and notes that the Persians say Karbadhakan. He describes it as a town near Hamadan, between al-Karaj and Isfahan, large and well known. He quotes lines of Abu Ya'la Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Hashimi that speak ill of the town and spare only Ibn Salih; they are lines of blame, not praise, given to show what was said of it. He names among its people the judge of the town, Abu Ahmad Ubayd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Isma'il ibn Abd Allah al-Attar al-Jarbadhaqani, from whom the hafiz Abu Bakr ibn Mardawayh transmitted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.118}

## Another Jarbadhaqan
Yaqut adds that Jarbadhaqan is also the name of a town between Astarabad and Jurjan in the region of Tabaristan, to which belongs Nasr al-Jarbadhaqani, a Hanafi jurist skilled in law. On Yaqut's text this is a different town from the one near Hamadan, and it is not part of this place page. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.118}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record identifies Jurbadhaqan with Gulpayegan in present-day Iran. That identification is later than Yaqut and is not in his text. The position of the town near Hamadan between al-Karaj and Isfahan is consistent with it in general, but Yaqut does not mention Gulpayegan.
