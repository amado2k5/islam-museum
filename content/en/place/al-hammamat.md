## Summary
Al-Hammamat was a cape on the coast of Ifriqiya east of Tunis, a long stage from the city, with a built fortress on a point jutting into the sea. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.293}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that between Tunis and al-Hammamat lies a long stage which is the width of the island called Jazirat Bashu, a blessed, pleasant land of olive trees, continuous settlements, ponds, goods and crops {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.293}. Tracing the coast, he notes that from Qasr al-Khayyat to Qasr al-Takhil is six miles, then seven miles to Taraf al-Hammamat, a built fortress on a point entering the sea about a mile {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.302}. From al-Hammamat to al-Manar, also a fortress, is five miles set back from the sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.302}.
