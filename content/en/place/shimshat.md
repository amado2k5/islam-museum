## Overview
Shimshat is one of the frontier posts of [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]], and according to al-Baladhuri it is counted as the fourth Armenia. Habib ibn Maslama al-Fihri and Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal al-Sulami conquered it under the command of Mu'awiya in the days of Uthman, on terms like the peace of al-Ruha. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.259}

Safwan stayed in it and died there. Constantine besieged it in the year 133 AH and achieved nothing against it. It remained subject to kharaj until al-Mutawakkil made it a tithe district (ushriyya), like the other frontier posts. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.269}

From it came Malik ibn Abd Allah al-Khath'ami, known as Malik al-Sawa'if (of the summer raids), campaigning in the year 46 AH. Around it revolve the conquests of Kamakh, Yaghra and Khilat. The details of the conquest are at [[event/conquest-of-shimshat|The conquest of Shimshat]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.269}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Shimshat", spelled with a kasra on the first letter, a sukun on the second, a shin like the first, and an undotted ta at the end, and says it is a city in Rum on the bank of the Euphrates, with a place he writes "Baluya" (as the Shamela text prints it) on its east and Kharput on its west, and in his day counted as one of the districts of Kharput. He passes on Ptolemy's figures, longitude 71 degrees 30 minutes and latitude 37 degrees 50 minutes, in the fifth clime, and those of the author of a zij, longitude 62 and two thirds and latitude 38 and three quarters. He says it is in ruins in his time with only a few people living there, and that it is not Sumaysat: the one is spelled with two undotted sins and the other with two dotted shins, both are on the Euphrates, but Sumaysat belongs to the districts of Syria and Shimshat to the edge of Armenia. He reports a saying that it was named after Shimshat ibn al-Yafaz ibn Sam ibn Nuh, who first founded it, and gives as its nisba Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad al-Shimshati, a poet and author on literature in the time of Sayf al-Dawla ibn Hamdan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.362-363} Elsewhere he says that one view counts four Armenias and that the fourth, which holds the grave of Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal near Hisn Ziyad, includes Shimshat, Qaliqala, Arjish and Bajnis, which matches al-Baladhuri in the record. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.160} In his entry on the river Nahr al-Kilab he says the first river to flow into the Tigris rises above Shimshat in the land of Rum. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.323}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut agrees with the record that Shimshat belongs to the fourth Armenia: he lists it there with Qaliqala, Arjish and Bajnis, as al-Baladhuri does, and he puts Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal's grave in that same fourth Armenia, which agrees with the record's saying that Safwan settled in Shimshat and died there, though Yaqut does not say he died in Shimshat itself. Yaqut does not mention the conquest, Safwan, Constantine or al-Mutawakkil, all of which the record has from al-Baladhuri, so he adds a geographical description to it: the Euphrates, Kharput, the old coordinates, and the distinction from Sumaysat. The museum keeps a separate record for [[place/sumaysat|Sumaysat]], which stays apart from this one. On coordinates there is a caution. Yaqut puts Kharput on Shimshat's western side, on the Euphrates, but the point on the record (38.68, 39.22) is an approximate placing made by the museum, not taken from Yaqut's description and not compared with it; the point lies only about 4 km, by the museum's arithmetic, from the museum's own [[place/kharpoot|Kharpoot]] record (38.7039, 39.25), so it sits at Kharput rather than east of it and does not agree with Yaqut's placing of Kharput to the west of Shimshat; the museum does not claim to know where Shimshat lay, and the question goes to outside research. This pass adds Yaqut's text only and leaves the coordinates unchanged.
