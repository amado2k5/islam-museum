## Summary
Baghlan was a fine, prosperous city with rivers, trees, buildings, many villages, shops and wide-reaching benefits, two stages from Andaraba and six stages from Balkh, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.485}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from Andaraba to Baghlan is two stages, from Baghlan to Samangan two stages, and from Baghlan to Balkh six stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.485}. The city of Baghlan he describes as prosperous, fine and urbanized, possessing rivers, trees, buildings, many villages, shops and ample goods {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.485}. From Baghlan westward to al-Bamiyan is three stages; Bamiyan stands at the head of its mountain, from which many rivers and waters descend to join the river of Andawab {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.485}. Baghlan thus lay in his scheme at a crossroads of eastern Khurasan, linking Andaraba with Samangan and Balkh with Bamiyan, and combined abundant water, a peopled countryside and extensive trade.
