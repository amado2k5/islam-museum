## Overview
Jayy is the city of [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]]. Al-Baladhuri reports that Abd Allah ibn Budayl conquered it by a peace agreement after fighting, on tax and poll tax, with safety for lives and property, except for the weapons in people's hands. Fadusfan handed it to him after a duel with him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises Jayy with a fatha and a doubled ya, and says it is a city in the district of ancient Isfahan, by his day almost a ruin standing alone, called Shahrastan by the Persians and al-Madina by the hadith scholars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.202} He says it lies about two miles from the city of Isfahan, which was long called al-Yahudiyya, with ruins between them; that the shrine of al-Rashid ibn al-Mustarshid is in it and is visited; and that it stands on the bank of the Zindarud. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.202} He quotes verses mocking the stinginess of its people by al-Badi' Hibat Allah ibn al-Husayn al-Astrulabi and verses of Abu Tahir al-Isbahani and A'sha Hamdan that mention it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.203} In a separate entry he lists Jiyy, with a kasra, a valley near al-Ruwaytha between Mecca and Medina, which is a different place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.203}
