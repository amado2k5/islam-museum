## Overview
Marw al-Rudh is a land in Tukharistan, one of the districts of Khurasan. [[person/al-ahnaf-ibn-qays|al-Ahnaf ibn Qays]] besieged it after a fierce battle, and its people took refuge in their fortress. Its marzban made peace with him for sixty thousand, or, it is said, six hundred thousand. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Among its fortresses is the palace of al-Ahnaf. He made peace with its people for three hundred thousand, on condition that a man of the Muslims enter the palace, call the adhan within it, and stay with them until he leaves. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut explains the name Marw al-Rudh: marw is the white stone from which fire is struck, and rudh is Persian for river, so it is as if it meant the river's Marw. He describes it as a city near Marw al-Shahijan, five days from it, on a great river, small compared with the other Marw; many people of merit came from it, with the names of origin Marwarrudhi and Marwudhi. It lies in the fifth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.112}

He records that al-Muhallab ibn Abi Sufra died at Marw al-Rudh, and quotes verses by Nahar ibn Tawsi'a mourning him, saying that the raiding that brought one near to wealth had gone, that generosity and kindness had died after al-Muhallab, and that the two stayed at Marw al-Rudh as pledges of his stay there, shut out from every east and west. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.112}

Among those named for it he lists Abu Bakr Khalaf ibn Ahmad ibn Abi Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Mattuya al-Marwarrudhi and his brother Abu Amr al-Fadl, men of merit and hadith, Khalaf dying in Rajab 506; the qadi Abu Hamid Ahmad ibn Amir ibn Yasr al-Marwarrudhi, a leading scholar of the Shafi'i school (Yaqut's words are "among the great companions of al-Shafi'i"), who settled in Basra, taught there, commented on al-Muzani's book and died in 362; and Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Salih ibn Hajjaj al-Marrudhi, the companion of Ahmad ibn Hanbal, who held a leading place among his companions and died in Baghdad in 275, buried near Ahmad ibn Hanbal's tomb. Yaqut tells that when he left for the frontier, people saw him off as far as Samarra, and at Samarra the crowd, not counting those who had turned back earlier, was estimated at about fifty thousand; someone told him to thank God that this knowledge had been spread for him, and he wept and said that this knowledge was not his but Ahmad ibn Hanbal's. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.112}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Marw al-Rudh, the city on the river near Marw al-Shahijan. What the record says of al-Ahnaf ibn Qays besieging it in Tukharistan comes from al-Baladhuri and not from Yaqut, who in this entry neither names its province nor mentions al-Ahnaf's siege. The record's coordinates are approximate.
