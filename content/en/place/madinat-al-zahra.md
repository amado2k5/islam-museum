## Overview
Madinat al-Zahra is among the great buildings of [[place/al-andalus|al-Andalus]]. Al-Nasir Abd al-Rahman founded it near [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]] at the beginning of the year 325 AH, four and two-thirds miles from the city. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.560-561}

## Construction
Al-Maqqari quotes Ibn Bashkuwal, who cites Ibn Khallikan, that it was one of the wonders of the world's buildings. Its length from east to west was two thousand seven hundred cubits, and its width one thousand five hundred cubits. It had four thousand three hundred columns, and its doors numbered more than fifteen thousand. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.560-561}

Al-Nasir used to divide the revenue of the land into thirds: one third for the army, one third held in reserve, and one third spent on its building. Al-Maqqari mentions that the revenue of al-Andalus was 5,480,000 dinars. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.560-561}

## Building
Al-Maqqari quotes Ibn Hayyan that ten thousand men and one thousand five hundred beasts worked on it every day, and that six thousand hewn stones were spent on it each day. Al-Nasir rewarded each marble slab with ten dinars. The marble was brought from Almeria, Ifriqiya and al-Sham, and it was said that some of it came from Constantinople. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.563}

## The Caliphal hall
Al-Nasir built in it the hall called the Caliphal Palace. Al-Maqqari describes its ceiling as gold and marble, and says that in its centre was a basin filled with mercury. He recounts that al-Nasir would order the mercury to be set in motion, and a glint like lightning would appear in the hall. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.563}

## Ruin
Al-Maqqari mentions that it was destroyed in the civil strife (al-fitna). Later, al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad and his viziers sat one day among its ruined remains, when his messenger came to them calling them to the Palace of al-Bustan. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.660}

These descriptions and figures are historical reports transmitted by al-Maqqari, and they need an additional source.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Zahra', the lengthened feminine of al-Azhar, the shining white: a small city near Cordoba in al-Andalus founded by the Umayyad Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir ibn Muhammad ibn Abdallah, then sovereign of that land, in year 325 as his resort. He spent on its building beyond the bounds of extravagance, had marble brought from the land's far quarters, and received from the kings of his realm furnishings beyond price; al-Nasir had divided the revenue of his realm in thirds — a third for his troops, a third for his treasury, a third for the upkeep and building of al-Zahra'. From one transmitter, the sum spent on it in Qasimi dirhams — named for the director of their mint, pure silver by the Cordovan measure — came to eighty muds and six aqfaẓa and more kayls; Yaqut fixes the measures: the mud weighs eight qintars, the qintar a hundred and twenty-eight ratls, the ratl twelve uqiyyas, the six aqfaẓa half a mud. The distance between al-Zahra' and Cordoba is six miles and five-sixths of a mile. The Cordovans, he says, wrote much in its description, on the greatness of the spending and in verse, compiling whole works, and he quotes Abu'l-Walid ibn Zaydun's two openings on it: "Is there no return to al-Zahra'" and "I remembered you at al-Zahra', yearning". Al-Zahra' is also, for him, another place in a verse of Mus'ab ibn al-Tufayl al-Qushayri. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.161-162}

## The museum's identification
The record's Zahra is al-Nasir's city by Cordoba, founded in 325 as the text itself says; the other Zahra of the verse is not it. The nisba al-Zahri attaches to it in his account of Abu Ali al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Ghassani al-Zahri the hafiz, imam of the Andalusians in hadith, who died in 498 — "of the sultan's city by Cordoba", in his words. The printed distance is six and five-sixths miles; the museum's own calculation from the record's point (37.886, -4.867) to Cordoba's (37.8916, -4.7728) gives 8.3 km, close to the printed figure on the statute mile — each number on its own basis.
