## Summary
Dahrut was a locality in Upper Egypt on the western side of the Nile, mentioned by al-Idrisi between the village of Tunis and the city of al-Qays on the roads of Egypt. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.329}

## Details
In the itineraries of Upper Egypt al-Idrisi reports that from the village of Tunis to Dahrut is half a day, Dahrut lying on the western side of the Nile, and from it to the city of al-Qays in the western direction is about twenty miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.329}. Al-Qays is an ancient primeval city already mentioned in the account of the land of Egypt in the second climate, and the road from it to the city of Aswan runs along the Nile {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.329}. Elsewhere he notes that al-Qays on the bank of the Nile, to its west, is an old city of fine building and lovely surroundings, with abundant sugar cane, kinds of dates and many good things, and between it and Dahrut to the north is a crossing of eighteen miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.124}. Dahrut is thus for him a stage on the crossing road west of the Nile between al-Qays and Tunis, linked southward with the route of the Fayyum, its monastery and villages rich in gardens, vines and sugar-cane farms {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.329}.
