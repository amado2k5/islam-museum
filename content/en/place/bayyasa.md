## Summary
Bayyasa is a city on an earthen knoll overlooking a great river which al-Idrisi places twenty miles from the city of Jiyan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from the city of Jiyan to the city of Bayyasa is twenty miles, that Bayyasa can be seen from Jiyan and Jiyan can be seen from Bayyasa, and that Bayyasa stands on a knoll of earth overlooking the great descending river {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}. In the same passage he notes that east of Jiyan and opposite Bayyasa is a great fortress called Shudhar from which the Shudhari pitch takes its name, and that Jiyan has orchards, gardens, farms and crops of wheat, barley, beans and all grains, with the great river Balun a mile away, and a congregational mosque and scholars {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}. Bayyasa is thus one of the mutually visible landmarks of that Andalusi region facing Jiyan.
