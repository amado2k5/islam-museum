## Summary
Bayyasa is a city on an earthen knoll overlooking a great river which al-Idrisi places twenty miles from the city of Jiyan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from the city of Jiyan to the city of Bayyasa is twenty miles, that Bayyasa can be seen from Jiyan and Jiyan can be seen from Bayyasa, and that Bayyasa stands on a knoll of earth overlooking the great descending river {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}. In the same passage he notes that east of Jiyan and opposite Bayyasa is a great fortress called Shudhar from which the Shudhari pitch takes its name, and that Jiyan has orchards, gardens, farms and crops of wheat, barley, beans and all grains, with the great river Balun a mile away, and a congregational mosque and scholars {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}. Bayyasa is thus one of the mutually visible landmarks of that Andalusi region facing Jiyan.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut writes the name with a doubled ya and calls Bayyasa a large city in al-Andalus, counted in the district of Jayyan, two farsakhs from Abbada, whose saffron was the best known in the western lands. He says the Christians entered it in 542 AH and were driven out in 552 AH. He gives a man of the nisba al-Bayyasi, Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Yusuf ibn Tamam al-Ya'muri, quoting the hafiz Abu Tahir that he was an outstanding poet and a careful man of letters who knew much of the verse of the later Andalusian poets by heart and turned to asceticism late in life. Yaqut also passes on a story of Abd al-Jalil ibn Wahbun of Murcia, called al-Dam'a: he praised al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad in a poem of ninety verses and was rewarded with ninety dinars, one of them clipped; he understood why only after studying his poem and finding that in one verse it had slipped from the metre tawil into the metre kamil. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.518}
