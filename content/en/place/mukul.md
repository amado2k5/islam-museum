## Summary
Mukul is a village populous with Berbers, like a great fortress on a gravel plain; it has a brisk market, much cultivation, flocks and cattle, and lies one stage from Anqal. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says: from Anqal to the village of Mukul is one stage; Mukul stands on a gravel flat, and adjoining it is a waterless tract called Fahs Kharaz, twelve miles long {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}. The village of Mukul is like a great fortress, populous with Berbers; it has a brisk market for all the goods and wares brought to it that necessity requires, and in it are many crops, flocks and cattle {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}. From Mukul to the village of Aksis is a small stage, the road running over Fahs Kharaz; at the end of the tract is a wadi with ever-flowing water and fruit groves, in which lions appear openly to the people by night and day, familiar with them, never hiding in their thickets {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}. In this village stands a house made for hunting lions, so that on a single Friday three or four might be taken, or more or fewer {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}.
