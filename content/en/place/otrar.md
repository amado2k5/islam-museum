## Overview
Farab is the Arabic name of the Otrar region on the Syr Darya. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan as a fortified province beyond the river, and names al-Jawhari and al-Farabi among those from it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.225}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Farab is a province beyond the river Sayhun on the edge of the Turkic lands, farther than al-Shash and near Balasaghun. Its length and width are less than a day's travel, but it has strength and martial vigour. It is a marshy district with thickets, and its people have fields on the west of the valley that draw water from the river of al-Shash. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.225}

He names those from it: Isma'il ibn Hammad al-Jawhari, author of the Sihah in lexicography; his maternal uncle Abu Ibrahim Ishaq ibn Ibrahim, author of the Diwan al-adab; and the philosopher Abu Nasr Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Farabi, author of works on the branches of philosophy, who died in Damascus in 339 and was a pupil of Yuhanna ibn Jilan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.225}

He adds Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Salama al-Maqdisi al-Farabi, who heard in Damascus from Hisham ibn Ammar and others, was narrated from by several, and was praised by Abu Hatim ibn Hibban and others. The Mu'jam also has a separate entry, "Utrar" (some say "Atrar"), which Yaqut describes as a fortified city and wide province at the start of the Turkic frontier beyond the river on the Sayhun, near Farab; so he gives the two names as neighbouring entries, not as one. The museum records this place as Otrar, an equation of the Farab province with the Otrar site that later scholarship makes and that Yaqut does not state in the Farab entry. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.225}
