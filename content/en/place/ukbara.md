## Overview
Ukbara is a town on the land bank of the Tigris. Al-Baladhuri relates that al-Nusayr came to it and granted its people safety, and they brought out food and fodder for those with him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut notes that the name Ukbara appears not to be Arabic, and quotes Hamza al-Isfahani that Buzurj Sabur is the arabised form of Wazrak Shapur, the place called Ukbara in Syriac. He describes it as a small town in the district of Dujayl near Sarifin and Awana, ten farsakhs from Baghdad, with the names of origin Ukbari and Ukbarawi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142}

Among its natives he names his own teacher, Muhibb al-Din Abu al-Baqa Abd Allah ibn al-Husayn al-Nahwi al-Ukbari, who died in Rabi I of 616. He quotes verses in praise of the town that were read on a pillar of the mosque of Ukbara, and a line of al-Buhturi on alighting there, saying that they had no nabidh and that wine was not lawful for them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Ukbara, a town on the Tigris in the district of Dujayl near Baghdad, so it is the place al-Nusayr reaches in al-Baladhuri's account. The record's coordinates are approximate. Yaqut gives a distance of ten farsakhs to Baghdad and, quoting Hamza al-Isfahani, a latitude of thirty-three and a half degrees and a longitude on an older reckoning that cannot be compared with modern meridians; the latitude is close to the record's (about 33.78 north).
