## Overview
Bursa is an Anatolian city on the northern foothills of Mysian Olympus, on the site of ancient Prusa; the Ottomans took it on 2 Jumada I 726/6 April 1326 and it was their main capital until 805/1402. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1397-1398}

## City of silk and learning
Pachymeres names it with Nicaea and Philadelphia among the three principal cities still in Byzantine hands when the Turkish borderers overran western Anatolia about 699/1300. Bursa long remained one of the empire's three capitals, and from about 802/1400 it was an international market and a centre of the silk trade and industry, reached by Iranian silk caravans through Tabriz, Erzurum and Tokat. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 1397-1398}

## In Evliya Çelebi's travels
[[person/evliya-celebi|Evliya Çelebi]] reached Brussa on his second journey; in Hammer's translation he calls it the emporium of silk and the ancient capital of the country, built towards the north on natural rock at the foot of Mount Olympus, its houses looking out over the plain of Filehdar. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v2 p.17} The fortress, he says, had a circumference of eleven thousand paces, six thousand battlements, sixty-seven towers and four gates: the gate of the head fountain, the prison gate, the gate of the hot baths to the west, and the gate of the fish market. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v2 p.17}

Of its conquest he relates that Osman besieged it three times, then sent his son Orkhan with Shaykh Haji Bektash, who built two towers and besieged it for a year until it surrendered in 722 AH (1322), and that Osman died the moment he received the news; Orkhan entered it, made it his residence, buried his father in the castle, and Muslim settlers came from all quarters. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v2 p.17} {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v2 p.18} Inside the fortress he counts two thousand houses, seven quarters and seven mosques, among them the mosque of Sultan Orkhan, a hundred and ten feet square, with his tomb and "Orkhan's drum"; the palace in the castle was the residence of the early Ottoman sultans until Mehmed II moved to Constantinople. {cite:evliya-narrative-hammer-v2 p.18} All of this is his account as Hammer translated it.
