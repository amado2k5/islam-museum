## Overview
Sayda is a city on the shore of the salt sea, cheap in price and surrounded by gardens and trees, according to al-Idrisi. It is attributed to a woman who lived in the pre-Islamic era. A kind of salted fish is made there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.371}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Sayda, Sidon, as a town on the coast of the Syrian sea, in the districts of Damascus, six farsakhs east of Tyre. He gives the traditional origin of its name: Saydun son of Sadqa, a descendant of Canaan son of Ham son of Noah, reported by Hisham on his father's authority. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.437}

He records that Ali ibn Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ati passed by the region of Sayda when it was in the hands of the Franks and saw meadows full of narcissus, and that a prisoner who escaped from Sayda was recaptured by horsemen who pursued him. He notes that the word sayda also means a white stone from which cooking pots are made, and a stony plain. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.437}
