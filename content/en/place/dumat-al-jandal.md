## Summary
A town that, according to Ibn Ishaq, was ruled by Ukaydir ibn Abd al-Malik al-Kindi, a Christian. The Prophet sent Khalid ibn al-Walid to it from Tabuk.

## Location
Its approximate coordinates are 29.8114 north and 39.8683 east. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name Dumat al-Jandal with the first letter vowelled either with a damma or a fatha, noting that Ibn Duraid rejected the fatha as a slip of the hadith transmitters, that al-Waqidi's report has Dawma al-Jandal, and that Ibn al-Faqih counted it among the districts of Medina. For the name he reports that it was called after Duma, Duman or Dawma, a son of Ishmael, who settled there and built a fortress, and that it lies seven stages from Damascus. From Abu Sa'd he gives that it lies in a hollow five farsakhs across, with a spring on its western side that waters its palms and crops, and a fortress called Marid built of jandal stone. From Abu Ubayd al-Sakuni he gives that it is a fortress and villages between Syria and Medina near the two mountains of Tayyi, one of the Qurayyat with Sakaka and Dhu al-Qara, with a wall around it and within it the strong fortress of Marid, the stronghold of Ukaydir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.487}

He relates that the Prophet sent Khalid ibn al-Walid to it from Tabuk, that Khalid took Ukaydir captive while he was hunting, killed his brother Hassan and took the town by force in the year 9 of the Hijra, and that the Prophet then made peace with Ukaydir over Dumat, imposing the jizya on him and his people. Ukaydir was a Christian; his brother Hurayth became Muslim and the Prophet confirmed him in what he held. He adds that Ukaydir broke the treaty after the Prophet's time and Umar expelled him to al-Hira, where he settled near Ayn al-Tamr and built houses he called Duma. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.487}

On the next page Yaqut gives the text of the letter the Prophet wrote to Ukaydir and the people of Dumat, with an explanation of its terms. He then says that Ukaydir withheld the alms after the Prophet's death and went to the region of al-Hira, building near Ayn al-Tamr a place he called Duma, and that his brother Hurayth, who had become Muslim, kept what he held. He notes that Ukaydir moved between Dumat al-Jandal and Dumat al-Hira, and that one account has the Kalb finding a ruined town built of jandal stone, rebuilding it and planting olives, so that it was called Dumat al-Jandal to tell it from Dumat al-Hira, which Yaqut says resolves the disagreement. He also says that some narrators placed the arbitration between Ali and Muawiya at Dumat al-Jandal, though most say it was at Adhruh, and that he knows of no verse on Dumat except one line by al-A'war al-Shanni. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.488}
