## Overview
al-Khurayba: a place that Khalid passed after the fighting at al-Ubulla. He conquered it and took captive those in it. al-Baladhuri reports that he appointed as its governor, according to al-Kalbi, Shurayh ibn Amir ibn Qayn of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr ibn Hawazin. It was a garrison post against the Persians. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives al-Khurayba, a diminutive of khariba, "a ruin", as a place in Basra. He quotes al-Zajjaji that the name comes from a palace a marzban had built there, which fell into ruin; when the Muslims settled Basra they built beside it and called the place al-Khurayba. He quotes Hamza that Basra was founded in 14 AH at the edge of the open land beside an old Persian city called Wahishtabad Ardashir, which al-Muthanna ibn Haritha al-Shaybani had ruined by raiding it, and that when the Arabs came to Basra they called it al-Khurayba. The entry then says that the Battle of the Camel between 'Ali and 'A'isha took place near it, and for this gives a verse by a speaker it does not name about the day of al-Khurayba; it does not show where Hamza's words end. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.363} He reports that al-'Umrani heard the name with a ra' from his teacher al-Zamakhshari, and that al-Ghuri said "Khuzayba" with a zay, a place in Basra called al-Basira al-Sughra, and then the entry calls this an undoubted error, since the place is still known in Basra with the ra'; it does not show whether that judgment is al-'Umrani's or Yaqut's. He names 'Abd Allah ibn Dawud al-Khuraybi, a Kufan by origin who settled in al-Khurayba in Basra and who, in the entry's words, died in 211. He lists two other places of the name: Khurayba al-Ghar, a fort on the Syrian sea coast, and a water near al-Qadisiyya where some of Sa'd's armies halted in the days of the Qawadis. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.363-364} In the entry on Basra, in the account of its founding, he reports from Nafi' ibn al-Harith and others that a man of Banu Sadus named Thabit told 'Umar ibn al-Khattab of a place short of the Tigris with a palace and Persian frontier posts called al-Khurayba and also al-Basira, four farsakhs from the Tigris; then he says, "it is said", that Khalid ibn al-Walid did not leave Basra until he had opened al-Khurayba, which was a frontier post of the Persians, killed and took captives, and left there a man of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr ibn Hawazin called Shurayh ibn 'Amir; he adds that al-Waqidi denied that Khalid passed through Basra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.430} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.431}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Baladhuri, is a site Khalid passed after the fighting at al-Ubulla, opened and took captive, leaving Shurayh ibn 'Amir ibn Qayn there; it had been a Persian frontier post. Yaqut's entry on al-Khurayba names none of Khalid, al-Ubulla or Shurayh, but his entry on Basra says, with "it is said", that Khalid opened al-Khurayba, a Persian frontier post, and left Shurayh ibn 'Amir of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr ibn Hawazin there, which matches the record on the opening, the frontier post and the man left behind; the museum reads his al-Khurayba of Basra as the record's site, a reading Yaqut does not state. He does not set the opening after fighting at al-Ubulla as the record does: al-Ubulla comes later in that entry, in the account of 'Utba ibn Ghazwan, and the name al-Khurayba is explained by al-Zajjaji and Hamza as the one the Arabs gave when they settled Basra; Yaqut does not reconcile the accounts. The record's point is 3.1 km from the museum's Basra point and 31.4 km from its Ubulla point, that is nearer Basra, and the museum leaves it unchanged. The water near al-Qadisiyya is another place and the museum does not connect it. See [[place/basra|Basra]], [[place/ubulla|al-Ubulla]] and [[event/battle-of-the-camel|the Battle of the Camel]].
