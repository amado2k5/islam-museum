## Overview
Takrur is a city south of the Nile in the land of the Sudan. It is larger than Sally and busier in trade, and people of the far Maghrib travel to it with wool, copper and beads, and take gold out of it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 18}

## Location
Al-Idrisi places the city of Takrur south of the Nile. Between it and Sally, which lies on the northern bank of the river, the distance is two days by river and by land. Sally belongs to the district of the Takruri ruler, a sultan with slaves and troops, known for firmness, vigour and a well-known justice, and his lands are safe and peaceful. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 18}

## Trade and livelihood
Al-Idrisi says that the people of the far Maghrib travel to Takrur with wool, copper and beads, and leave with gold. The food of the people of Sally and Takrur is millet, fish and milk, and most of their livestock are camels and goats. The common people wear coarse woollen cloaks, and their elite wear cotton garments and wraps. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 18}

## Distances
From Takrur to Barisi on the Nile to the east is twelve stages. Barisi is a small town without a wall, like a settled village, and its people are travelling merchants obedient to the Takruri ruler. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 19}
