## Overview
Mystras is described by Pleiades as a fortified town in Laconia, in the Peloponnese, Greece, named a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1989. Its notes add that it was founded in 1249 by William of Villehardouin, prince of Achaia, and was later held by the Byzantines, the Turks and the Venetians. In the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries it was the capital of the Byzantine Despotate of the Morea, and it was abandoned in 1832. {cite:pleiades 410249370}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Mystras» (Pleiades no. 410249370), classed as a settlement and an archaeological site, with a time span from 1200 CE to 2100 CE. It records the name «Mystras» in English. It describes the place as a fortified town in Laconia, Peloponnese, Greece. {cite:pleiades 410249370}
