## Summary
Bisha was a small urbanized town, excellent in dwellings and fair in site, with visible water and a few palms, on the southern road beyond Tabala, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

## Details
On the southward road al-Idrisi reports that from the town of Tabala — small, set in the hollow of a hill, with many springs, palms and farms — one comes to Bisha, a small urbanized town of excellent dwellings and a fair site, having visible water and a few palm trees {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}. From it the road runs to the village of Hasda', which has a well of little water and few inhabitants, then to Bayat, a great village with many people, many palms and a spring of sweet water {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}. Bisha thus lay in his scheme on a continuous route from Tabala toward Najran and Jarash, distinguished by the fineness of its houses, the beauty of its site and its visible water, though its palms were few.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes Bisha as a lush village in a densely settled valley in the lands of Yemen. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.529} He reports al-Qasim ibn Ma'n al-Hudhali that "Bi'sha" and "Zi'na", spelled with a hamza, are two lands, and 'Uqayl that they are two valleys where the Banu Khafaja gather; Bisha drains from the Yemen side and Zina from the Sarat of Tihama, with twenty-four miles between Bisha and Tabala. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.529} From Abu Ziyad he relates that the best of the abodes of Banu Salul is Bisha, a valley whose flood runs from the Hijaz of al-Ta'if and then into Najd until it ends in the lands of 'Uqayl, with many clans living in it, among them Khath'am, Hilal, Salul and 'Uqayl. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.529} He adds that it belongs to the province of Mecca on the Yemen side, five stages from Mecca, with many palms and offshoots, and that its valley holds a wooded spot full of lions, for which he cites a verse of al-Samhari. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.529}
