## Summary
Julfar was a village on the coast of the land of Oman where, together with al-Khayl, were the pearl fisheries; opposite them in the sea lies the tip of a great submerged mountain that ships pass with difficulty. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from Oman the road runs along the coast to the lands of Bahrayn, namely from Suhar and Dama to Masqat, to al-Khayl, to Julfar; these last are two villages in which are the pearl fisheries {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. Opposite them in the sea lies the tip of a great mountain submerged in the sea, of which a little appears in some places and vanishes in others; when the ships ascending from Basra to Oman reach this point they unload their cargoes on the shore until the ship grows light and passes that tip, then they are loaded again and proceed to Oman {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. From Julfar, as one descends to Bahrayn, one comes to the anchorage of al-Sabkha, an anchorage with a gushing fresh spring {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. He notes that the river al-Falh, a great river on which are villages and continuous buildings, pours into the sea near the village of Julfara {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.158}. The author counts Julfar among the known chief towns of this part together with Masqat, Suhar, Hajr, Birman and al-Jayl {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.150}.
