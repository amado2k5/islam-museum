## Overview
Samarkand, the chief city of Transoxiana in present-day Uzbekistan, stood at the junction of the caravan roads between Khurasan and China; Yaqut calls it the greatest, most flourishing and finest of the cities beyond the Oxus {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995}.

## City and trade
Standing on the Silk Road's main branch between Bukhara and Ferghana, Samarkand gathered the silk, spice and silver caravans of China, India and Persia, and served as a capital of successive Transoxanian states {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}.

## The city of paper
In the second Hijri century Muslim captives carried papermaking from China to Samarkand; its paper became proverbial, and the craft travelled from it to Baghdad and then to the West {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2}.
