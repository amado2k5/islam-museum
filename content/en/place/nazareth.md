## Overview
Nazareth is a town in the north of Israel, recorded by Pleiades as a settlement of the ancient world. The first two volumes of Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan hold no entry for it. {cite:pleiades 678299}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Nazareth» (Pleiades no. 678299), classed as a settlement, with its recorded location dated from 330 BCE to 640 CE. It records one name, «Nazareth», attested in the Hellenistic and Republican, Roman and Late Antique periods. Its description cites the Barrington Atlas of the Greek and Roman World, map 69, grid B4. {cite:pleiades 678299}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Nazareth as a village thirteen miles from Tiberias, where Jesus, son of Mary, was born according to Christian tradition, and from which the name of the Christians is derived. He reports a dispute between the people of Nazareth and those of Jerusalem over the place of the birth: the Jerusalem community holds that Christ was born in Bethlehem and that the traces of that birth are visible there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.251}

Yaqut gives his own reading of the Gospel account: Jesus was born in Bethlehem, Joseph took the child to Egypt until the death of Herod, and on return he was directed toward the land of Israel, came to Jerusalem, and then settled in a town called Nazareth, which is why Jesus is called the Nazarene. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.251}
