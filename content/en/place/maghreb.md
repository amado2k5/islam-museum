## Overview
Al-Maghrib, "the West", is the name Muslim geographers gave to the western lands of the Islamic world; today the Maghreb is taken to mean North Africa west of Egypt. Yaqut al-Hamawi devotes an entry of the Mu'jam al-Buldan to the term to fix its limits, and related entries treat the Berbers and the western cities. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.161}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says al-Maghrib, with a fatha on the first letter, is the opposite of the east, and is a wide land of great extent and rough going. He quotes some authorities that its limit runs from the city of Miliana, the last border of Ifriqiya, to the end of the Sus mountains, behind which lies the Encircling Ocean; that the peninsula of al-Andalus is counted in it though it lies nearer the north; and that its length overland is a journey of two months. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.161} See [[place/ifriqiya|Ifriqiya]].

In his entry on the Berbers he says they are a name covering many tribes in the mountains of the Maghrib, beginning with Barqa and continuing to the end of the Maghrib and the Ocean, and south as far as the Bilad al-Sudan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.368} In his entry on Tadlis he calls it a city in the far Maghrib on the Ocean. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.17} See also [[place/tadlis|Tadlis]], [[place/marrakesh|Marrakesh]] and [[place/morocco|Morocco]].

Taken together these entries show that for Yaqut the Maghrib is a large region running from Ifriqiya in the east to the Atlantic in the west and meeting the Sudan belt to the south, which agrees with the place card's description of the lands of North Africa west of Egypt.
