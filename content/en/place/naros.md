## Summary
Nārūs is a small, civilised town with markets for buying and selling, one hundred miles from Nūshā, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}

## Details
Al-Idrisi places Nārūs in the land of Qumaniya, which belongs to the Qumanians. Among its towns he names Fīra, Nārūs, Nūshā and Qīnyū. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.916}

Nūshā lies fifty miles north of Qumaniya al-Bayḍ. He calls it a civilised town of middling size, with crops and produce. From Nūshā to Nārūs, on a line between north and west, the distance is one hundred miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}

He describes Nārūs as a small civilised town with markets for buying and selling. From Nārūs eastwards to Ṣalaw is one hundred and thirty-five miles; westwards to Fīra, fifty miles; and from Fīra westwards to Nābī, twenty-five miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}

The passage says nothing about Nārūs's farming, its population or its produce. It records the town as a market stop linked by distances to its neighbours. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}
