## Overview
Qudayd is a place in the Hijaz where episodes of the Prophet's biography occurred, according to al-Mubarakfuri. The water of al-Muraysi', which belonged to the Banu al-Mustaliq, lay in the district of Qudayd toward the coast. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 217}

The battle of the Banu al-Mustaliq took place in Sha'ban of the sixth year after the Hijra. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 216} When the Prophet reached al-Muraysi', the two sides prepared for battle. The Muslims then charged as one man and the enemy fled. Women, children, herds and flocks were taken as captives, and only one Muslim was killed, by a man of the Ansar who mistook him for an enemy. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 217}

In the seventh year after the Hijra the Prophet sent the expedition of Ghalib ibn 'Abd Allah al-Laythi to the Banu al-Mulawwah at Qudayd, in Safar or Rabi' I, to avenge the companions of Bashir ibn Suwayd whom the Banu al-Mulawwah had killed. The Muslims raided by night and drove off the camels, but a large enemy force pursued them, and a heavy rain and flood came between the two sides, so the Muslims withdrew successfully. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 254}

Among the expeditions after the conquest of Mecca, Sa'd ibn Zayd al-Ashhali was sent with twenty horsemen to Manat, which stood at al-Mushallal near Qudayd and was held by the Aws, the Khazraj, Ghassan and others. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 274}

The geographer al-Bakri describes the mountain of Jumdan, in the Hijaz between Qudayd and 'Usfan, among the dwelling places of the Banu Aslam. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p. 256}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Qudayd as a diminutive — of al-qadd ("you shredded the hide"), or of al-qidad, the skin of a kid, or of the qidad named in the Qur'an meaning "separate ways". Kathir, asked why Qudayd was so named, thought a while and answered: "its torrent went shredding". Qudayd is a place near Mecca; Ibn al-Kalbi says that when Tubba' returned from Medina after warring its people he camped at Qudayd, a wind rose and shredded (qaddat) his companions' tents, and it was named accordingly — as Ubaydallah ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat said in his verses, "setting out from Qudayd at evening, watering at 'Usfan by morning". To Qudayd is ascribed Hizam ibn Hisham ibn Hubaysh ibn Khalid ibn al-Ash'ar al-Khuza'i al-Qudaydi of the people of al-Raqm in the Hijazi badia, who related from his father, his brother Abdallah ibn Hisham and Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, and was related from by Abdallah ibn Idris, al-Qa'nabi and others; he was trustworthy. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.313-314} Elsewhere he notes that the idol Manat was set up on the seashore at al-Mushallal by Qudayd between Medina and Mecca {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.204}, and that al-Muraysi' is a water in the Qudayd direction toward the coast, to which the Prophet marched in year five — Ibn Ishaq says year six — against the Banu al-Mustaliq of Khuza'a. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.118}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Qudayd is the record's place between 'Usfan and Rabigh on the Mecca road (by the two records' points about 36.8 km from 'Usfan and 66.8 km from Rabigh); Suraqa's catching up there comes from the record's al-Raheeq and Yaqut does not mention it, giving instead the naming accounts, the hadith men ascribed to it, and its neighbourhood to Manat's site and to al-Muraysi' of the Banu al-Mustaliq raid.
