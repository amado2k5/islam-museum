## Overview
Tifash is an ancient city that al-Idrisi places in the central Maghrib, where, he says, the Berbers are the main people. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 222}

It is built of stone and lime and has an old wall. It has a running spring, gardens and orchards, and its main crop is barley. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 295}

It lies one stage from al-Arbus. From Tifash to the Qasr al-Ifriqi is another stage; the Qasr has no wall and has farms. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 295}

Idrisi's road routes pass through it: six stages from Bejaia, two long days from Qal'at Bishr, and two long days from there to Qalma. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 260} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 267}

These distances show that Tifash lay on a route linking Bejaia, Qalma and Qal'at Bishr. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 260}

Yaqut al-Hamawi writes the name with a shin and describes it as an ancient city in Ifriqiya with tall, solid buildings. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 846}
