## Summary
A district in the land of Basra in Iraq. According to Ibn Ishaq, Umar ibn al-Khattab appointed al-Nu'man ibn Adi over it. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/9}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Maysan (with a fatha): the name of a wide kura rich in villages and palms between Basra and Wasit, whose capital is Maysan; in this kura is a village holding the famous, tended tomb of the prophet 'Uzayr, which the Jews serve and on which they have endowments and vows — Yaqut says he saw it himself; the nisba is given maysani and maysnani with two nuns. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.242} He records that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, when Maysan was conquered in his days, appointed over it al-Nu'man ibn 'Adi ibn Nadla, who was one of the emigrants to Abyssinia and the only man of Banu 'Adi whom 'Umar ever appointed, for the piety he saw in him; he then transmits the story of his wife and the verses he sent her from Maysan about drink, which reached 'Umar and led to his dismissal, as is well known in the siyar books. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.242; 5 p.243}

## The museum's identification
The record's Maysan is the kura of the Basra land, and it is Yaqut's kura "between Basra and Wasit"; his naming of al-Nu'man ibn 'Adi as 'Umar's appointee agrees with the record's Ibn Ishaq source, and the verses-and-dismissal story is the museum's event [[event/umar-dismisses-al-numan-ibn-adi|Umar dismisses al-Nu'man ibn 'Adi from Maysan]]. The 'Uzayr tomb in one of its villages is added report Yaqut says he saw himself.
