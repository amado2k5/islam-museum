## Overview
Abbadan: the island of Basra and its seaward fortress, as the conquest accounts describe it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Name and ownership
It was a fief of Humran ibn Aban, a freedman of Uthman, granted by Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. The source reports that al-Hajjaj threatened Humran through the words of Abbad ibn al-Husayn al-Habati, so Humran gave him the western bank of the river, and it came to be named after Abbad. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The garrison
Abbad ibn al-Husayn was the first to keep watch there. Later al-Rabi ibn Subayh, the jurist and freedman of Banu Sa'd, fortified it and kept watch there. He set out on a naval raid to India, died, and was buried on an island among the isles in the year 160 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} The place is also connected with [[place/basra|Basra]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Abbadan, with a doubled b and a vowelled first letter, and reports from Ptolemy that it lies in the third climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74} Citing al-Baladhuri, he says it was a land grant to Humran ibn Aban, the freedman of Uthman ibn Affan, from Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan (some said part of it came from Ziyad); Humran was one of the captives of Ayn al-Tamr and claimed descent from Namir ibn Qasit. When al-Hajjaj threatened him over the claim and Abbad ibn al-Husayn al-Habati carried word of it, Humran gave Abbad the western side of the river and kept the eastern, and that side took Abbad's name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74} From Ibn al-Kalbi he reports that Abbad ibn al-Husayn was the first to keep a frontier watch there, and that the jurist al-Rabi' ibn Sabh gathered money from the people of Basra, fortified the place and kept watch in it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74}

On the name, Yaqut says that abbad are men of much worship, and that adding alif and nun to a place-name is Basran usage, as in Ziyadan and Bilalan; he adds that people lived there in devotion and retreat, and that it once stood at the front of a frontier post. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74} He places it below Basra near the salt sea, where the Tigris divides near the village of al-Muhrizi; Abbadan lies on the triangular island between the two branches, with shrines and ribats. He calls the ground poor salt marsh with brackish water, says its devotees live on endowments and mostly on vows, that they eat chiefly fish, that visitors come in the seasons to make visits, and that the shrine of Ali ibn Abi Talib is there. He adds that the traditions told about its merits are not firmly established, and that the Persians call it Miyan Rudhan, "between the rivers". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74}

He lists ascetics and hadith transmitters named after it, among them Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Sulayman al-Abbadani, who settled in Baghdad and was born on the first of Rajab 248, and the Shafi'i judge Abu Shuja Ahmad ibn al-Hasan al-Abbadani, who by al-Silafi's account taught in Basra for over forty years. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74}
