## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Ishbiliya is a great city, the capital of the ruler of al-Andalus in Yaqut's time, on a large river called the Wadi al-Kabir, and known for growing cotton. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.195}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling with a kasra on the hamza and the ba, and describes a great city than which al-Andalus then had none greater; he adds that it is also called Hims. It was the seat and throne of the ruler of al-Andalus and the home of the Banu Abbad, whose residence there, he says, caused the ruin of Cordoba. Its district joins that of Labla, and it lies west of Cordoba at thirty farsakhs. He reports, on the word of some, that it was in earlier times the seat of the Byzantine ruler and their chief see, and that this has now passed to Toledo. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.195}

## Land and river
Yaqut says it is near the sea and is overlooked by Jabal al-Sharaf, a mountain with many trees, olives and fruit of every kind. What sets it above other parts of al-Andalus is cotton farming, since cotton is carried from it to all the lands of al-Andalus and the Maghrib. It stands on the bank of a great river, in size close to the Tigris or the Nile, on which loaded boats sail, called Wadi al-Kabir. In its district are towns and regions he lists under their own names. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.195}

## People
Yaqut notes that a great many scholars are attributed to it and names first Abd Allah ibn Umar ibn al-Khattab al-Ishbili, its judge, who died, as he gives it, in 276. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.195}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record uses the name Ishbiliya for the city now called Seville in Spain during Muslim rule of the Iberian Peninsula, between 713 and 1248 CE; that dating comes from Wikidata, not from Yaqut. What Yaqut says of the old Byzantine see and of the name Hims is what he reports from others.
