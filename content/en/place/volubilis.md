## Overview
Walila is a city in the Maghrib as Yaqut al-Hamawi records it, which Idris ibn Abd Allah entered in 172 AH and where he stayed until he died in 174; the museum reads it as Volubilis, the archaeological site near Meknes in Morocco. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.384}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Walila" and says it is a city in the Maghrib near Tangier. When Idris ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib entered the Maghrib, escaping from the battle of Fakhkh, he came to it in the year 172, in the days of al-Rashid, and stayed there until he died poisoned, in a long story, in 174. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.384} In his entry on Fas, quoting Abu Ubayd al-Bakri, he says the Andalusian quarter of Fas was founded in 192 and the Qarawiyyin quarter in 193 in the rule of Idris ibn Idris, and that Idris died in the city of Walila, "of the land of Fas, a day's distance to the west", in the year 213. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.230}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut describes Walila's position in two ways: "near Tangier" in its own entry and "of the land of Fas, a day's distance to the west" in the entry on Fas. The point on the record (34.04, -5.56) lies west of [[place/fez|Fez]] and, by the museum's arithmetic from the two records' coordinates, about 190 km south of [[place/tanja|Tangier]], so it sits closer to the Fas description than to "near Tangier"; the museum does not choose between the two and does not call either a mistake. Yaqut describes no Roman remains and gives no Latin name; the link to the site of Volubilis rests on the Arabic name in the record's label and the Wikidata identifier Q391215 recorded for it. His statement that Idris ibn Abd Allah died poisoned in 174 is Yaqut's account; the museum's records for [[person/idris-ibn-abdallah|Idris ibn Abd Allah]] and [[person/idris-ibn-idris|Idris ibn Idris]] give other accounts from other sources, and the reader who wants more detail should turn to them. The description "site in Morocco" comes from Wikidata.
