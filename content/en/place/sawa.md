## Overview
Sawa is a city in the mountains. Al-Baladhuri mentions that Layla al-Akhyaliyya came to al-Hajjaj, and he gave her a gift, and she asked him to write to his governor in al-Rayy. When she arrived at Sawa she died, and she was buried there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name Sawa with, after the alif, an open waw then a quiescent ha': a fine city between al-Rayy and Hamadhan, midway, thirty farsakhs from each; near it is a city called Awa — Sawa's people Sunni Shafi'i, Awa's Imami Shi'i, about two farsakhs apart, partisanship ever flaring between them. Both stood inhabited until year 617, when the Tatars came; word reached Yaqut that they ruined it and killed everyone in it, and that they burned its dar al-kutub, the like of which the world had not held. Its longitude is seventy-seven and a half and a third degrees and its latitude thirty-five. In Satih's hadith on the signs of prophecy the quenching of Persia's fire, the drying of Sawa's lake and the flooding of Wadi al-Samawa are named. The poet Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Khalifa al-Sinbisi, poet of Sayf al-Dawla ibn Mazyad, mentions it in an ode; the nisba is Sawi and Sawaji, and scholars are ascribed to it, among them Abu Ya'qub Yusuf ibn Isma'il ibn Yusuf al-Sawi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.179}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Sawa is the record's Sawa, the Jibal city between al-Rayy and Hamadhan (present-day Saveh); he describes it in the seventh century before its reported ruin by the Tatars in 617, while Layla al-Akhyaliyya's death there, which the record tells from al-Baladhuri, lies centuries earlier and is not mentioned. By the two records' points Sawa stands about 117.1 km from al-Rayy and 169.8 km from Hamadhan, where Yaqut gives thirty farsakhs to each.
