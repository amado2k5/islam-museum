## Summary
Al-Samman was a populated village in the desert of al-Yamama inhabited by poor Arab tribesmen, lying on the desert road from al-Yamama to Basra. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.161}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes the route for one travelling from al-Yamama to Basra: from Hadrama one stage to al-Sal, then one stage to Salmiyya, then through stretches of barren land three stages to al-Mar'ab, a small village of Arab folk where one halts at well waters in desolate spots, then three further stages to al-Samman, a populated village whose people are Arabs, hungry and naked, for whom poverty has been written as a safeguard {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.161}. In his geography al-Samman is a Bedouin halt on a long road through empty country.
