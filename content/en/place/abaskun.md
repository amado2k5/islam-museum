## Summary
Abaskun was a fine, well-settled town on the border of [[place/tabaristan|Tabaristan]], serving as Jurjan's port on the Caspian Sea, from which travellers embarked for the land of the Khazars, Bab al-Abwab, al-Jil and Daylam. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.688}

## Details
Al-Idrisi writes that Jurjan had a port on the sea called Abaskun, a fine and prosperous town, and that one sails from it to the land of the Khazars, Bab al-Abwab, al-Jil and Daylam {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.688}. Describing the Caspian Sea, he notes an island facing Abaskun called Siyah Kuh, uninhabited but having springs, trees, thickets, livestock and game {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.832}. He also reckoned about nine hundred miles from Abaskun to the mouth of the Khazar river, the length of that sea, and six stages from Abaskun to Dihistan {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.835}. In his map of the Islamic east, Abaskun thus stood as a seaport linking western Persia with the Turkish and Khazar frontier lands.
