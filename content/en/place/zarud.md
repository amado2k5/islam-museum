## Overview
Zarud and Ramal Zarud are two places on the Iraqi road, where the lava-field (al-harra) ends, on the left side of the road. Al-Hamdani mentions them in his description of the stations on this road. {cite:hamdani-akwa-hawali p. 265}

An additional source is needed: the location of the two names is taken from a single sentence, and no coordinates have been recorded.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says the name may come from the phrase "a zarud camel", a gulping one, since zarad is swallowing, and that the place may have been named for swallowing the water the clouds shed on it, because it is sand. He places it between al-Tha'labiyya and al-Khuzayma on the pilgrim road from Kufa. He quotes Ibn al-Kalbi, from al-Sharqi, that Zarud, al-Shuqra and al-Rabadha were daughters of Yathrib ibn Qaniya, a descendant of Iram, son of Sam, son of Noah. He adds that it is also called Old Zarud, that it lies a mile short of al-Khuzayma, and that it has a pool, a palace and a trough; then he reports, on the word of "they said", with no speakers named, that the first of the sands is al-Shayha and then Raml al-Shaqiq, and that it is five hills, the two hills of Zarud, Jabal al-Ghurr, Marbakh (the hardest) and Jabal al-Tarida (the easiest), until the mountains of the Hijaz are reached. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.139} He gives the Day of Zarud as a well-known day of the Arabs between Banu Taghlib and Banu Yarbu', an unattributed report that al-Rashid, on pilgrimage and coming in sight of the Hijaz, quoted a verse by a poet he does not name that speaks of having passed Zarud, and verses by Mihyar longing for Zarud. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.139} In the entry al-Hubayr he says the Hubayr is the sand of Zarud on the road to Mecca. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.392}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Hamdani, names Zarud and Raml Zarud as two places on the Iraqi road where al-Harra ends, on the left of the road. Yaqut's Zarud lies on the Kufa pilgrim road between al-Tha'labiyya and al-Khuzayma, which fits "the Iraqi road"; his entry does not mention al-Harra or the left side of the road, and his Old Zarud a mile short of al-Khuzayma has no counterpart in the record, which has Raml Zarud instead. Yaqut gives no distance apart from the mile short of al-Khuzayma, so he cannot be set against the record's point.
