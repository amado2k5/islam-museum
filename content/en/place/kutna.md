## Summary
Kutna is a large village in the south of the Arabian Peninsula with springs, vineyards, tall palms and vegetables, on the road toward Najran. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

## Details
Al-Idrisi lists it among the halts of that road: after Sabkha, an uninhabited stopping place, comes Kutna, a large village with springs, vineyards, tall palms and garden produce, then al-Najm, a populous village with a well, then Sadum, a big village of settled people with connected buildings, many springs and vineyards {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}. The town of Jarash lies eight miles from it, and Jarash and Najran are close in size, rich in palms, with tanneries whose hides are their merchandise and trade and whose people are famous for them {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}.
