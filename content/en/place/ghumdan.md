## Overview
Ghumdan is a palace in the city of [[place/sanaa|Sanaa]]. Al-Mas'udi reports that al-Dahhak built it in the name of Venus, that it was ruined in the caliphate of Uthman ibn Affan until it became a great mound, and that the vizier Ali ibn Isa ibn al-Jarrah built a water fountain there and dug a well when he was exiled to Yemen. Al-Mas'udi saw it as rubble and a mound, as if it had never been, in 332 AH. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.539-540}

## Accounts of its building
Al-Mas'udi reports that As'ad ibn Ya'fur wanted to build it, and Yahya ibn al-Husayn al-Hasani advised him not to touch it, because it would be built at the hands of a boy who would come out of the land of Saba and Ma'rib. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.539-540}

In the accounts of the people of Yemen that al-Hamdani records, Ghumdan and al-Qalis were built by Bilqis. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.221}

## In al-Umari's description
Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari counts it among the most famous monuments and most conspicuous landmarks, and says that it was the dwelling of the Tubba' kings of Himyar. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.232-233}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the vowels of the name (Ghumdan) and notes that al-Layth misread it as «Umdan» with the letter ayn. He suggests it may be a plural of «ghimd», the sheath of a thing, so that the palace would be a sheath over the lesser halls and buildings around it. He then relays Hisham ibn Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ib al-Kalbi: Lisharh ibn Yahsub wished to build a palace between Sanaa and Tayyuh; the builders stretched the measuring cord, a kite snatched it and dropped it where Ghumdan stands, and he ordered the palace built there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.210} Yaqut describes it with four faces, white, red, yellow and green, and inside it a palace of seven ceilings, forty cubits between each pair; on top a hall of coloured marble with a ceiling of a single slab, and at each corner a bronze lion through which the wind entered at the rear and left by the mouth with a roar; lamps were lit at night so that it flashed like lightning. He quotes verses by Dhu Jadan al-Hamdani about it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.210} He also records the view of some that Solomon son of David ordered the spirits to build three palaces for Bilqis at Sanaa: Ghumdan, Salhin and Baynun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.210} Finally he reports that Ghumdan was pulled down in the days of Uthman ibn Affan; the soothsayers of Yemen were said to hold that whoever demolished it would be killed, so rebuilding was ordered, but he was told the land tax could not restore it as it had been and left it. Another account says a beam was found inscribed that its destroyer would be killed. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.211}
