## Overview
al-Musayla: a newly founded, populous town on a level plain. al-Idrisi reports that it was founded in the time of the rule of Idris ibn Abd Allah, and that it has extensive farms, horses, sheep and cattle. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.254}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "al-Masila", with a fatha, then a kasra, a silent ya and a lam, and says it is a city in the Maghrib called al-Muhammadiyya, laid out by Abu al-Qasim Muhammad ibn al-Mahdi in the year 315 AH, when he was heir to his father; this is the one nicknamed al-Qa'im after al-Mahdi, of those who trace themselves to the Alids who were in Egypt, in Yaqut's words. He gives as its nisba Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Harb al-Muqri in Egypt, who read the Qur'an, travelled to Badajoz and met Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Muzahim al-Khazraji there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.130} In his entry "al-Muhammadiyya" he gives more: al-Masila is also called al-Muhammadiyya, Muhammad was sent by his father with an army as far as Tahart, where he killed and took power; passing the site of al-Masila he liked it, drew the plan of a city on the ground with his spear as he sat on his horse, ordered Ali ibn Hamdun al-Andalusi to build it, and named it al-Muhammadiyya after himself. The ground had been the holding of the Banu Kumlan, a Berber tribe, whom he ordered moved to the plain of Qayrawan, and they later became the followers of the Kharijite Abu Yazid against him. He fortified the city and moved stores to it, in 315. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.64-65} Elsewhere he says Tamukida lies two stages from al-Masila and Tahart six stages from it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.7}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut and al-Idrisi agree that it is a newly founded city in the Maghrib, but they name different founders. Al-Idrisi, in the record, says it was founded in the time of Idris ibn Abd Allah; Yaqut has Abu al-Qasim Muhammad ibn al-Mahdi lay it out in 315, and the museum reads him as the [[person/al-qaim-fatimid|al-Qa'im bi-Amr Allah Muhammad ibn al-Mahdi]] of its own record. The museum does not choose between the two accounts. The museum also has a record called [[place/al-muhammadiyya|al-Muhammadiyya]], which is the city at Rayy built by al-Mahdi; it is not the Algerian Masila, and Yaqut's entry "al-Muhammadiyya" gathers many places of that name, so the name alone does not fix one place. The point on the record (35.7058, 4.5419) agrees with Yaqut's placing of a city in the Maghrib six stages from Tahart; the museum ties the record to modern M'sila through the Wikidata identifier recorded for it, and Yaqut gives no coordinates.
