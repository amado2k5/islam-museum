## Overview
Qashmir is a famous city among the lands of India, which was under the king of Qanuj, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 193}

He distinguishes between Inner Qashmir (al-Dakhila), which lies north of Samandar at seven stages, and Outer Qashmir (al-Kharija), which is four stages from Asnand. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 193}

From Inner Qashmir to Qanuj is about seven stages; Qanuj he describes as a large, fine city with much trade. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 193} From Qashmir to [[place/qarmut|Karmut]] is four stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 193}

Samandar itself is a city of wide trade, thirty miles from Kanja, and part of the lands of Qanuj. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 192}

In a list of Indian places he names Lower Qashmir and Upper Qashmir beside Midara, Karmut, Qanuj and Asnand, without describing them in that place. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 186}

He says the people of Qashmir fought the Turks, whom he calls unbelievers, and that the harm of the Turks sometimes reached them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 193}
