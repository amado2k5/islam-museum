## Summary
Haran al-Qarin is a small but populous village in the land of Tihama on the road to Sanaa, with running waters and a few date palms. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from coastal Hay to Wadi Dhankan, which joins the city of Dhankan, is one stage; thence to Bisha Yaqzan, which he mentioned on the road to Sanaa, one stage; then to Haran al-Qarin, a small but populous village with running waters and a few date palms, one stage; and from it to Khawlan Dhu Suhaym, a fortified stronghold whose people enjoy protection and might {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}. He explains that all these lands he has mentioned lie in Tihama, and that Tihama is a part of Yemen: a chain of interlocking mountains beginning at the Sea of Qulzum and overlooking it, with tracts extending eastward {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}. Haran al-Qarin is thus one of Tihama's little villages combining a running spring with a few palms, a halt between Bisha Yaqzan and Khawlan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}
