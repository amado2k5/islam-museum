## Overview
Suakin is a port on the Red Sea coast. Yaqut records it briefly in the Mu'jam al-buldan as a well-known town where ships arriving from Jidda put in. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Suakin is a well-known town on the shore of the Bahr al-Jar, near 'Aydhab, where the ships of those arriving from Jidda come to port, and that its people are black Beja Christians. The entry gives the town's place on the sea and its link with the Hijaz by shipping, and it assigns the inhabitants to the Beja. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276}

The mention of Jidda and 'Aydhab shows that, for Yaqut, Suakin was a landing on the sea road between the Hijaz and the coast of Sudan. The entry is a few lines long, and what it says about the inhabitants' religion is Yaqut's report of what had reached him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276}
