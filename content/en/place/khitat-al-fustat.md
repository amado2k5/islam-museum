## Overview
For Ibn Abd al-Hakam a khitta is the quarter laid out by a man or a tribe; his editor glosses it as a quarter or district. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 132} Once [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr]] had moved to [[place/al-fustat|al-Fustat]] and built [[building/masjid-amr-fustat|the mosque]], the people laid out their plots. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 134} Ibn Abd al-Hakam admits he does not know the plots of every conqueror: "God knows best." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 139}

## Around the mosque
Around Amr and the mosque settled Quraysh, the Ansar, Aslam, Ghifar and Juhayna, and the people of the Banner, men whose clans had no numbers. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 141} He reports from Ibn Lahi'a that the Banner were Quraysh, who carried Amr's banner; others say it was a banner Amr created for small groups unwilling to stand under anyone else's. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 162}

## The tribes
Bali settled behind Kharija ibn Hudhafa as far as Suq Wardan, and stood on the right of Amr's banner because the mother of al-As ibn Wa'il was of Bali. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 161} Ghafiq were a third of the people when Amr entered Egypt, in Yazid ibn Abi Habib's report, and their plot lay between Mahra and Lakhm. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 166} Mahra first settled at Dar al-Khayl on the slope of Jabal Yashkur, with a black dome for their mosque, and Amr later moved them near him. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 164}

## The Lane of Lamps
The Lane of Lamps was once called the Lane of the Nobles, because Amr lived at its end by the mosque and [[person/kab-ibn-dinna-al-absi|Ka'b ibn Dinna]] at its other end by Suq Barbar, with houses of several Companions between them, among them Abu Dharr al-Ghifari. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 156}

## Notable houses
Among the houses he names are [[building/dar-al-baida-fustat|the White House]], [[building/dar-al-raml-fustat|Dar al-Raml]] and [[building/dar-al-fulful-fustat|the Pepper House]]. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 142} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 143} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 152}
