## Overview
al-Hijr: a place where the Prophet and the Muslims stopped on their way to Tabuk. They drew water from its well, and then they were forbidden to drink from it.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name al-Hijr with a kasra and a sukun, and makes it the name of the dwellings of Thamud in Wadi al-Qura between Medina and Syria; from al-Istakhri he says it is a small village of few people, a day's journey from Wadi al-Qura among mountains, where the dwellings of Thamud were, that he saw houses like the houses of his own time amid masses of mountains called al-Athalith, each mass standing alone with sand around it that can hardly be climbed, and that the well of Thamud is there; and he quotes two verses of Surat al-Shu'ara which the museum does not reproduce. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.220-221} The name has other senses for him: the Hijr of the Ka'ba, the city of al-Yamama, and a village belonging to Banu Sulaym beside which is a hill called Qinnat al-Hijr, on 'Arram's authority. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.221}

## The museum's identification
The record makes al-Hijr the place where the Prophet and the Muslims halted on their way to Tabuk, drawing from its well and then being forbidden to drink from it. Yaqut's entry (vol 2 p.220-221) makes it the dwellings of Thamud in Wadi al-Qura between Medina and Syria and transmits al-Istakhri's description of its dwellings and its well, which fits the record's place; the entries read do not mention the Prophet's halt or the prohibition of the water, which stays with the record's texts of Ibn Hisham and al-Bukhari. The record's point (26.8142, 37.9475) is 309.7 km from the museum's Medina point. See [[place/medina|Medina]] and [[place/tabuk|Tabuk]].
