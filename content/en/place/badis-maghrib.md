## Summary
Badis was an urbanized town with markets and a few crafts; its people were of Ghumara, who resorted to it for their needs, and it lay about half a day from the fortress of Karkal, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.532}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the fortress of Karkal to the city of Badis is about half a day, and that Badis is an urbanized city containing markets and a few crafts, to which the Ghumara resort for their needs {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.532}. It is the last of the lands of Ghumara, and adjoining it is the end of a mountain whose other extremity reaches southward until it stands four miles from the city of Tawda {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.533}. From Badis to the anchorage of Budhakur is twenty miles; Budhakur had been a city in former times but was ruined with no trace left, and between it and Badis runs a continuous mountain called al-Ajraf that has no anchorage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.533}. Badis thus stood in his account as an inland centre for the Ghumara country, their place of resort for their affairs at the far end of their land.
