## Summary
Achir is a town in the mountains of the Berbers in the Maghrib, at the western edge of Ifriqiya, facing Bejaia on the land side. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.202} Ziri ibn Manad al-Sanhaji founded it; he was the leader of his people in his time and the grandfather of al-Mu'izz ibn Badis and of the kings of Ifriqiya after al-Mu'izz left the country. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.202}

As Yaqut tells it, Ziri's courage gathered a band of his kin, who raided the Zanata and the Berbers around them. When the place grew too narrow for them, he sought a site to settle and found Achir, an empty place with many springs and wide open ground. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.202} He brought builders from the neighbouring towns and began the building in 324 AH; it was completed well, and he made a strong fortress on its mountain that could be reached by one road only. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.203}

After him the Banu Hammad, cousins of the Banu Badis, held the town and took the districts around it, and they opposed the Badisid rulers of Ifriqiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.203} Yaqut names among its people the shaykh Abu Muhammad Abdallah ibn Muhammad al-Ashiri, whom he describes as an imam of the people of hadith and jurisprudence. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.203}

## Identification
The record places Achir in modern Algeria from its Wikidata item. Yaqut's text does not give the modern name; the identification rests on the mountain town and its position opposite Bejaia.
