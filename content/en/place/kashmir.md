## Overview
Kashmir is a mountainous region of the northern Indian subcontinent. The pages read for this page come from Lothrop Stoddard's Hadir al-Alam al-Islami in the Arabic of Ajjaj Nuwayhid with the commentary of Shakib Arslan, a survey of the Muslim world as it stood in the 1920s; its statements are attributed here to its authors and read as a picture of that time.

## In Hadir al-Alam al-Islami
The book lists Kashmir among the large Indian principalities and gives its people, by the latest census, as about four million, of whom three and a half million were Muslims and half a million Hindus, with a Hindu maharaja at the head {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 1 scan p.353 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.01)}. It reports that serious unrest broke out the previous year between Muslims and Hindus after Muslim complaints about how the government treated their religious feelings, that calm returned only when a British army entered, and that Muslims kept asking that a Muslim prince rule Kashmir since most of its people were Muslim {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 1 scan p.353 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.01)}. This is the authors' account of those events, not a judgement by the museum.

In the historical part of the book, Mahmud, son of Sebuktigin, took Kashmir in 1021 CE among his Indian campaigns {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.293 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}, and Akbar annexed it in 1586 among the realms he brought into his sultanate {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.306 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}. The dates are as the book gives them and have not been checked against another source.
