## Overview
Adhruh: a place mentioned by Ibn Ishaq in the account of the expedition to [[place/tabuk|Tabuk]]. Its people came to the Prophet there and gave him the poll-tax. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

The place data gives its approximate location as the present-day town of Adhruh in southern Jordan near Ma'an. This identification is at the level of the district, not the exact site.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.), signed H. Lammens and L. Veccia Vaglieri, describes Adhruh (also Udhruh) as a place between Ma'an and Petra, with a magnificent Roman camp whose remains were described by Brunnow and Domaszewski, and a gushing spring. In pre-Islamic times it lay in the Judham country and was visited by the Quraysh caravans. It submitted to Muhammad on payment of tribute during the expedition to Tabuk in 9/630; the treaty of capitulation transmitted by the sources is probably authentic. Mu'awiya is said to have received there the homage of al-Hasan b. 'Ali. Some Arab geographers made it the chief town of the district of al-Sharat in the province of al-Balqa'. It is not mentioned after the Crusaders, who held places such as Wadi Musa. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 213}

Adhruh became famous in Islamic history for the conference held there after the battle of Siffin, to settle the conflict between 'Ali and Mu'awiya. The article notes that al-Hamdani's statement in the Hudud al-'Alam that its people were Kharijites comes from a confusion between al-Sharat and al-Shurat, which means the Kharijites. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 213}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a fatha on the alif, a sukun on the dhal, a damma on the ra and the plain ha. He offers two origins: a plural of «dharih», meaning red hills spread flat on the ground, or a plural of «al-dharh», a tree from which saddles are made, and says that on either reading the form is irregular. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.129} He calls it a town on the edge of Syria, in the district of al-Shara and then of al-Balqa, with Amman lying next to the land of the Hijaz. He reports Ibn al-Wadda' as saying it belongs to Palestine and judges that a mistake: it lies south of Palestine, on the side of al-Shara. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.129} He cites a line in the book of Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj giving three days between Adhruh and al-Jarba', then relays the account of the amir Sharaf al-Din Ya'qub ibn al-Hasan al-Hudhbani, who said he had seen the two several times and that they stood a mile or less apart, which other local people confirmed to Yaqut; he adds that some have wrongly read the name with a jim. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.129}

He says the arbitration of the two arbiters, 'Amr ibn al-'As and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, took place at Adhruh and al-Jarba'; some said Dumat al-Jandal, but he holds Adhruh and al-Jarba' to be correct, and he cites verses by Dhu al-Rumma and al-Aswad ibn al-Haytham in support. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.130} He adds that Adhruh and al-Jarba' were taken in the Prophet's lifetime, in the year 9, and the people of Adhruh made peace for a hundred dinars of tribute. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.130}
