## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan the Khabur is a great river of al-Jazira between Ras al-Ayn and the Euphrates, giving its name to many towns, and it empties into the Euphrates at Qarqisiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.334}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut begins with the word. He gives its spelling and then several linguistic derivations: that it is a form like fa'ul from khabra', a flat ground where lote trees grow; or from khabar, soft stony land; or from khabartu al-ard, I ploughed the land. He adds from Ibn Bazraj that words of the pattern fa'ulaa are few, and from Ibn al-A'rabi that al-Khaburaa is the name of a place, then says he does not know whether that is this river or another. He therefore keeps al-Khabur and al-Khaburaa apart. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.334}

## The river and its towns
Yaqut describes the Khabur as a great river between Ras al-Ayn and the Euphrates in al-Jazira, a wide province whose many towns took their name from it. He lists Qarqisiya, Makisin, al-Majdal and Arban. The river rises from the springs at Ras al-Ayn and receives the overflow of the Hirmas, which is the river of Nasibin, so that it becomes a large river that waters those lands, then reaches Qarqisiya and enters the Euphrates there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.334}

## Verse
Yaqut quotes lines by the sister of al-Walid ibn Tarif mourning her brother and addressing the trees of the Khabur; she says he was a youth who wanted provision only from piety and wealth only from spears and swords. The lines are described here, not quoted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.334}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls the Khabur a river in Syria and Turkey. That is consistent with Yaqut's river between Ras al-Ayn and the Euphrates; that the entry describes this river is the museum's reading.
