## Summary
Dijon was a city of the land of Burgundy, standing in the midst of an open plain, fine of site and blessed of ground, with abundant livelihoods and provisions. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.743}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Mashkun to the city of Dijon is sixty miles; Dijon stands in the midst of an open expanse of land, fine of site and blessed of ground, possessing many livelihoods and provisions {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.743}. From the city of Dijon to the city of Lanka is seventy miles; Lanka is a lofty city of wide extent, with crops, vineyards, running waters and plentiful goods {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.743}. Dijon is mentioned in the description of the lands of that part which includes the land of the Frankish Burgundians, where the author describes the climate of Ray bordered on the south by the land of Iklrmant, on the north by the climate of Tarsh, on the west by the land of Bitarsh and on the east by the land of Burgundy {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.742}. Dijon thus appears in his scheme among the towns of the land of Burgundy, spacious of court and wide of confines, neighbouring Mashkun and Lanka on the road and adjoining the lands of the German Burgundians toward the river Rudnu {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.743}.
