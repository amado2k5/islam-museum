## Overview
Amarkot, in the Tharparkar district of West Sind (Pakistan), is the town where the Mughal emperor Akbar was born. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 453}

## Early history
Tradition says it was founded by a branch of the Sumra Rajputs who embraced Islam in the reign of 'Ala' al-Din Khalji. The Sumras lost it in 624/1226 to the Soda Rajputs, who were expelled in 731/1330 and returned in 843/1439. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 453}

## Humayun and Akbar
In 949/1542, after his defeat by Shir Shah, Humayun sought refuge in Amarkot with the Soda prince. Akbar was born there on 5 Rajab 949/23 November 1542. The old fort was demolished by Nur Muhammad in 1746, and a stone slab marks the birthplace about half a mile north-west of the town. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 453}

## Mughal and later rule
In 999/1590, when 'Abd al-Rahim Khan-i Khanan conquered Sind, Amarkot became part of the Mughal empire, though in 1008/1599 an Arghun prince drove out the Mughal commander. Nur Muhammad Kalhora took it in 1149/1736, and Nadir Shah forced him into submission in 1152/1739. The Talpurs captured the fort in 1228/1813, and it passed to the British with the conquest of Sind in 1843. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 453-454}
