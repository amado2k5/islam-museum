## Overview
Cordoba: a Spanish city, which was the capital of the emirate and then of the Umayyad caliphate in al-Andalus, and in which is the congregational mosque. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari}

## In the books of biography and routes
al-Maqqari quotes al-Hajari in al-Mashhab that Cordoba, in the Marwanid state, was the dome of Islam, the seat of the generous and the mine of scholars, and that in al-Andalus it stood as the head stands to the body. He mentions the famous mosque and the bridge over the greatest river. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.189-190}

According to Ibn Sa'id in al-Maghrib, the sultans of al-Andalus turned between three poles: Cordoba, al-Zahra and al-Zahira. He reports, on the authority of al-Shaqundi, that Cordoba was the greatest of the kingdoms of al-Andalus in knowledge and the most abundant in merit. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.491-492}

Ibn Bashkuwal, as transmitted by al-Maqqari, mentions that it had seven gates and that its suburbs reached twenty-one suburbs. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.501-502}

According to al-Baladhuri, Musa ibn Nusayr ordered Tariq ibn Ziyad not to go beyond it in his raid. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}
