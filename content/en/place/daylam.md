## Overview
A mountainous land south of the Caspian Sea and west of [[place/tabaristan|Tabaristan]]. al-Baladhuri reports that Urwa ibn Zayd broke the Daylam together with the people of [[place/rayy|al-Rayy]] on the day of the conquest, and that Kathir ibn Shihab raided them from al-Rayy and struck them. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas also raided them in his second governorship, and the people of al-Rayy continued to raid them from Dastabi. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut notes that the word daylam has several senses in Arabic: death, enemies and the small black ant. He says that the Daylam are a people named after their land, in the view of some transmitters, and not after a forefather. The astronomers place their country in the fourth climate, at 75 degrees of longitude and 36 degrees and ten minutes of latitude. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.544}

He also records a water called al-Daylam belonging to Banu Abs, and cites a line of Antara that mentions it, together with a report about a debate between philologists over the word. The Bedouin in that debate said it was a set of pools in the lowland that his camels had watered at more than once. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 pp.544-545}
