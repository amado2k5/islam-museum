## Summary
Ashkala was a small town of the land of al-Laniyya, near the sea, self-sufficient for its people and standing on its own livelihood, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of [[place/ashkshiya|Ashkshiya]] to the city of Ashkala in the land of al-Laniyya is twenty miles, and that between Ashkala and the sea are about six miles; it is small but urbanized, sufficient to its people and standing upon its own means of living {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}. From it along the coast to the city of Istabriya is twenty miles, and Istabriya on the seashore is a flourishing town with wide markets whose people are mostly merchants {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}. By this ordering Ashkala stood in his account on a coastal road linking Ashkshiya with Istabriya and then with the city of al-Laniyya, after which the Lani peoples were named {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}. Small as it was, he described it as a settled place adequate to its inhabitants' needs, one of the lesser self-standing towns on the coast of al-Laniyya.
