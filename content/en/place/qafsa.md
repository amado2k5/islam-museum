## Overview
Qafsa is a fine walled city with a flowing river whose water is finer than that of Qastiliya. It is the centre of the country around it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 277}

## Description
Al-Idrisi says that in the middle of Qafsa is a spring called al-Tarmadh, and that it has busy markets and shops. Around it are orchards and gardens, where henna, cotton and cumin are grown, and palm groves surround it with many kinds of wonderful dates. Its people are Berber, and most of them speak the African Latin tongue. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 277} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 278}

## Routes and distances
From Qafsa to al-Qayrawan, northward and a little east, is four stages. To the south-west lies the town of Bilqan, five stages away; it is now a ruin that the Arabs destroyed, and it is four stages back to Qafsa. From Qafsa to Nafta are two small stages. Nafta is a civilised town with markets, trade, palms, crops and running water. From Qafsa southward to Nafzawa is two days and a half. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 278}

## Sfax
From Qafsa to Sfax is three days. Al-Idrisi describes Tiqyus, a fine town near it, which lies one stage from Qafsa and has tender, good vegetables. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 278} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 279}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Capsa (Iustiniana)» (Pleiades no. 324684), classed as settlement and modern settlement, with activity attested from 330 BCE to 2099 CE. Its recorded names include Capsa Iustiniana, Capsa and Gafsa. It describes an ancient city of Byzacium in North Africa. {cite:pleiades 324684}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
