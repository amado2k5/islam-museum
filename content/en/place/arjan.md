## Overview
Arjan: a city in the land of Persia, according to al-Idrisi. From Arjan to the village of [[place/asak-khuzistan|Asak]] are two stages, to al-Dawraq one stage, and then to al-Basyan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.401}

An additional source is needed to fix its relation to [[place/arrajan|Arrajan]], which al-Baladhuri mentions.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Arrajan, with a doubled r, and says that most Persians call it Arghan and that al-Mutanabbi lightened the r. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.142} Quoting al-Istakhri, he describes a large, prosperous city with many palms, olives and fruits of both warm and cold climates, lying between plain and mountain, one stage from the sea, sixty farsakhs from Shiraz and sixty from Suq al-Ahwaz. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.143} By the Persians' own account, as he reports it, it was first founded by Qubadh son of Firuz, father of Anushirwan, who after recovering the kingdom from his brother Jamasp and campaigning against the Byzantines took Mayyafariqin and Amid, built a city between the borders of Fars and al-Ahwaz called Abaz Qubadh, which is Arjan, settled the captives of those two cities in it and made it a district. Yaqut adds that Arjan is mentioned in the conquest reports and that he does not know whether that is the same place or one of the accounts is mistaken; he also notes a report that parts of its district belonged to Isfahan, Istakhr and Ramhurmuz before it became a single district of Fars in Islamic times. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.143} He relays from Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Faqih the story of a cave in its mountain from whose rock a sweat-like water yields the white mummiya, closed with an iron door, guards and the sultan's seal and opened once a year before the qadi and the town elders; he then notes that al-Bashshari and al-Istakhri place that cave in the district of Darabjird. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.143} He gives the distance to Nawbandajan, toward Shiraz, as twenty-six farsakhs, with the valley of Buwwan between them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.143} Among those named after it he lists Abu Sahl Ahmad ibn Sahl al-Arrajani; Abu Sa'd Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Darir al-Arrajani of Isfahan, who died in Rabi' al-Awwal 606 AH; and the judge and well-known poet Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Arrajani, judge of Tustar, born about 460 AH and died 544 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.143} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.144}
