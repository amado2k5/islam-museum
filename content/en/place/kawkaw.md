## Overview
Kawkaw is a large, famous city on the bank of a river that flows from the north, according to al-Idrisi. People differ about its site: it is said to be on a gulf, and it is said that the river passes through it for days and then sinks into the sand. Its king is independent, proclaims the sermon for himself, and has many servants and troops. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.28}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives Kawkaw as the name of a nation and a land of the Sudan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.495} He quotes al-Muhallabi that it lies in the first climate at a latitude of ten degrees, that its king professes Islam before his subjects and most of them do the same, and that the king has a city on the east bank of the Nile called Sarnah, with markets and trade, and another on the west bank where he lives with his men and trusted companions, with a mosque in which he prays and a congregational prayer ground between the two cities. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.495} He adds from the same report that the king and his chiefs wear shirts and turbans and ride horses without saddles, that the kingdom is more populous than that of Zaghawa although the land of Zaghawa is wider, that their wealth is livestock, and that the king's treasuries are large and consist mostly of salt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.495}
