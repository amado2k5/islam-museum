## Overview
Two villages, one of them populous, lie at the foot of Mount al-Muqattam, and most of them are now in ruins, according to al-Idrisi. He mentions a place known as the Oven of Pharaoh, where there is a mirror in which a person's image can be seen, and a talisman associated with it is related. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.326}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Manf", with a fatha, a sukun and a fa, and says it is the name of Pharaoh's city in Egypt; he reports from al-Quda'i that its origin is the Coptic "Mafa", which was arabicised to "Manf". He then relates from Ibn Abd al-Hakam, with a chain, that the first to settle Egypt after the flood was Baysar ibn Ham ibn Nuh, who settled at Manf with his offspring, thirty persons, so that it was called Mafa, which in Coptic means thirty, and that it was the first city to be built after the flood. He says it is the city meant in the verse [[passage/quran-28-15|Qur'an 28:15]]. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.213-214} From al-Hamadhani he repeats the word of a trustworthy elder who saw Pharaoh's house at Manf, all of it one hewn stone; the city's remains and the stones of its palaces, he says, were still to be seen in his time, three farsakhs from al-Fustat and six from Ayn Shams. He passes on a report of four rivers there and Ibn Zulaq's report that some said that from Misr to Manf there were thirty miles of continuous houses, among them Pharaoh's house in one piece of green stone; he asked some of the wise men of Egypt, who confirmed it but gave its size as five cubits by five. He relates, from one of the wise men of Egypt, that Uthman ibn Salih, the scholar of Egypt, told him of an inscription on a church door at Manf saying that its small size was due to the price of land, each cubit having been bought for two hundred dinars because of how closely it was built, and that the Bishop's church there is roofed with one stone and that Joseph and those before him lived at Manf, as did the Pharaoh of Moses. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.213-214} He explains the name "Tannur Fir'awn" thus: when Pharaoh wished to ride from Ayn Shams to Manf, the keeper of the lookout at Manf lit a fire, which the keeper of the lookout on a corner of Muqattam saw and answered with another, and when the man at Ayn Shams saw the blaze he made ready for his coming, and the same in the other direction; he adds that al-Fustat in his day lies between Manf and Ayn Shams at the end of the Muqattam range. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.214} The next headword is Manfalut, another town, in Upper Egypt west of the Nile. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.214}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut explains the name "Tannur Fir'awn" differently from the al-Idrisi passage in the record: al-Idrisi speaks of a mirror in which a person's image is seen and of a talisman, while Yaqut ties the name to fire signals between the lookout at Manf, the one on the Muqattam and Ayn Shams, and mentions neither mirror nor talisman. The museum measured the distances between the points of its own records: the Manf point (29.8447, 31.2508) is about 17 km from its al-Fustat point and about 32 km from its Ayn Shams point, close to Yaqut's three and six farsakhs at about six kilometres to the farsakh; Yaqut gives no coordinates, and the thirty miles of continuous houses between Misr and Manf that Ibn Zulaq reports from others is another measure that Yaqut does not reconcile with the three farsakhs, and the museum does not attempt to. The record, following al-Idrisi, puts the place at the foot of the Muqattam; Yaqut puts al-Fustat between Manf and Ayn Shams at the end of the Muqattam and does not say that Manf lies at the foot of the range. The reading of Manf as Memphis rests on the name and on Yaqut's description of it as Pharaoh's city with stone remains: a fit, not proof. Manfalut is a different town and has its own record, so it is not confused with Manf.
