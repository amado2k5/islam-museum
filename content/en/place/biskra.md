## Overview
Biskra is recorded by the German Archaeological Institute as a populated place, within Algeria. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2110421}

Yaqut al-Hamawi describes Biskra as a town in the Maghrib, in the districts of al-Zab, two stages from the fortress of the Banu Hammad and one stage from Tubna. It has palms, trees and good dates of the qasb kind. The entry adds that it is a walled town with markets and baths, whose people are scholars following the school of the people of Medina, and that a mountain of salt nearby is cut into great blocks like rock. It is also known as Biskrat al-Nakhil, and the entry names a scholar of the nisba al-Biskari who travelled east and studied with teachers there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut 2 p.586}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer of the German Archaeological Institute lists this place under no. 2110421 with the preferred name «Biskra», classed as a populated place; its parent in the hierarchy is Algeria. Among the names it records are «ولاية بسكرة», «بسكرة», «Briskra», and it links the place to GeoNames no. 2503826. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2110421}
