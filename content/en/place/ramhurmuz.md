## Overview
Ramhurmuz is a city in the districts of al-Ahwaz. The book Futuh al-Buldan mentions that Abu Musa made a truce with it, then its people broke the covenant, and Abu Maryam al-Hanafi made peace with it for eight hundred thousand dirhams. Then its people betrayed again, and Abu Musa conquered it by force at the end of his days. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut explains the name. In Persian, ram means the desired or the sought, and Hurmuz is one of the Sasanian kings, so the word is compounded to mean the wish or aim of Hurmuz. Hamza says it is a shortened form of Ramhurmuz Ardashir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.17}

Yaqut calls it a well-known city in the lands of Khuzistan, which the common people shorten to Ramz. It is unusual among the towns of Khuzistan in producing date palms, walnuts and citrons together, which Yaqut says no other town of the province gathers. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.17}

Yaqut quotes Warad ibn al-Ward al-Ja'di, who asks whether he has become a stranger in Ramhurmuz, and a poem by Ka'b al-Ashqari that records the news of the death of Bishr ibn Marwan reaching the company at Ramhurmuz after they had left al-Ahwaz. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.17}
