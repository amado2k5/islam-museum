## Overview
Wasit is a city built by [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj]] in the year 83 or 84 AH. According to al-Baladhuri, he built its mosque, its palace and the green dome in it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.407}

It was a land of reeds, and so it was called Wasit of the Reeds (qasab). It is at an equal distance from al-Ahwaz, [[place/basra|Basra]] and [[place/kufa|Kufa]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.407}

Al-Hajjaj wrote to [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik]] that he had built a city in a hollow (karsh) of the land between the mountain and the two garrison cities, and so the people of Wasit were called the Karshiyyun. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.407}

The founding date is given in two versions (83 or 84 AH); an additional source is needed.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the reason for the name: Wasit lies midway between Basra and Kufa, fifty farsakhs from each. He reports, on the authority of al-Kalbi, that before al-Hajjaj built the city a place called Wasit al-Qasab existed there, and that al-Hajjaj named his city after it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.347}

The astronomers' table gives Wasit a longitude of 71 and two-thirds degrees and a latitude of 32 and a third degrees, in the third climate. On grammar, Abu Hatim held that the city is treated as masculine and declined, because the word was taken to mean a town called Wasit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.347}

Quoting Abu al-Nada, Yaqut says the Arabs knew seven places called Wasit, among them Wasit of Najd, Wasit of the Hijaz and Wasit of al-Jazira, each illustrated with lines of verse. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.348}
