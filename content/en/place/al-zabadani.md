## Overview
Al-Zabadani is a well-known district between Damascus and Baalbek as Yaqut al-Hamawi records it, from which the river of Damascus rises; the museum reads it as the town of al-Zabadani in Syria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.130}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "al-Zabadani", with a fatha on the first two letters, an undotted dal, then after the alif a nun and a doubled ya like the ya of a nisba, and says it is a well-known kura (district) known to lie between Damascus and Baalbek, from which the river of Damascus rises. He says that "al-Adl al-Zabadani", who used to carry messages between Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub and the Franks, is named after it, remarks that the name of the place and the nisba to it are the same, as one says a man is "Shafii" for the school of al-Shafii, and adds that the man was not praised in his conduct, so that the Damascene al-Shihab al-Shaghuri satirised him in two lines that play on the word "adl", which is both the man's title and the word for justice. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.130} In his entry "Barada" he reports from Niftawayh that Barada rises in a village called Qanawa in the kura of al-Zabadani, five farsakhs from Damascus on the side toward Baalbek, and then flows to the village of al-Fija, two farsakhs from Damascus. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.378} In his entry "al-Fija" he calls it a village between Damascus and al-Zabadani where the river of Damascus, the Barada, and a lake have their source. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's entry is about a kura, a district, not a city, while the museum's record calls it a city on the strength of Wikidata; the museum reads the district's name as the name that stayed with the town, which is its reading and not Yaqut's. The point on the record (33.7247, 36.1003) fits Yaqut's saying that it lies between Damascus and Baalbek and at the source of the Barada; Yaqut gives no coordinates. The museum holds records for [[place/damascus|Damascus]], [[place/baalbek|Baalbek]] and the [[place/nahr-barada|Barada river]], to which the reader can turn. Yaqut's remark on al-Adl al-Zabadani is his own judgment of the man, not one the museum adopts; it passes it on as his and adds nothing, and Yaqut says nothing else about him. The two lines of verse are only described here and are not quoted.
