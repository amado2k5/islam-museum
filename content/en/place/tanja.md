## Overview
Tangier is a city of the far Maghrib on the sea. Musa ibn Nusayr conquered it in his governorship of Ifriqiya. He settled in it, and was the first to settle there and lay out the Muslims' plots of land in it, according to al-Baladhuri. The authority of the Byzantine sultan in Ifriqiya extended from Tripoli to it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.318} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.322}

See [[event/conquest-of-tanja|The conquest of Tangier]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut places Tangier in the fourth climate, on the Maghrib sea coast facing al-Jazira al-Khadra, on the mainland among the Berber lands. He cites Ibn Hawqal, who calls it an ancient city whose stone buildings still show, while the inhabited town in his day lay a mile from the sea, without a wall, on the back of a mountain, with its water brought in a conduit from a source no one knew for certain. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.43}

He says it was a day's journey from Ceuta, and on al-Sukkari's authority from Abu Ubayda it was the last boundary of Ifriqiya. Its distance from Qayrawan is two thousand miles. Among those born there he names Abu Abd al-Malik Marwan ibn Sanjun al-Lawati al-Tanji, who travelled to the East, stayed seventeen years and studied hadith. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.43}
