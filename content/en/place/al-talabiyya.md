## Overview
al-Tha'labiyya: a place on the road to Iraq. al-Baladhuri reports that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas stayed there for three months until the people caught up with him, and then came to al-Mudhayyib in the year 15 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.353}

It is also said that Jarir ibn Abd Allah took the road by Fayd and al-Tha'labiyya to al-Mudhayyib. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.356}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Tha'labiyya with a fatha: one of the stations of the Mecca road from al-Kufa after al-Shuquq and before al-Khuzaymiyya, two-thirds of the way; below it, a mile off and overlooking it, is a water called al-Duwayja, then come pools called Bark Hamad al-Sabil, then unbroken sand reaching to al-Khuzaymiyya. On its naming he gives three accounts: it was named for Tha'laba ibn Amr Muzayqiya' ibn Amir Ma' al-Sama', who settled there when the Azd scattered from Ma'rib — his sons grew strong, returned to the Yathrib district and drove the Jews from it, and they are the Ansar (as he says he will tell under Ma'rib); al-Zajjaji says it was named for Tha'laba ibn Dudan ibn Asad ibn Khuzayma ibn Mudrika ibn Ilyas ibn Mudar, the first to dig and settle it; and Ibn al-Kalbi says it was named for a man of the Banu Dudan ibn Asad called Tha'laba, who was overtaken by sleep there, heard the murmur of water in his dream, woke saying it was surely a place of water, dug it and built. He also relates, from Ishaq al-Mawsili by way of al-Zubayr ibn Mus'ab and Salama the blind al-Asadi, verses of Salama ibn al-Harith ibn Yusuf ibn al-Hakam ibn Abi al-Asi ibn Umayya, who used to stay among them at al-Tha'labiyya and loved a mawla's wife named — her husband called Mansur. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.78} To al-Tha'labiyya is ascribed Abd al-A'la ibn Amir al-Tha'labi, counted among the Kufans, who related from Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya, Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn and Sa'id ibn Jubayr; al-'Uqayli lists him among the weak and says his hadith from Ibn al-Hanafiyya is a written leaf containing weakness. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.79}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's al-Tha'labiyya (with tha') is the Kufa–Mecca road station of the record's "al-Ta'labiyya" (with ta'): between al-Shuquq and al-Khuzaymiyya at two-thirds of the road. The record's Sa'd encampment of three months comes from al-Baladhuri and Yaqut does not mention it; his three naming accounts are transmitted to their authors without a verdict.
