## Overview
Mahra is a sandy, barren land which only the camels named after it inhabit, famous for their speed. Al-Idrisi says its length is about nine hundred miles, and that its people live on dried fish, dates and milk, and know little wheat. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.155}

## In al-Hamdani
Al-Hamdani named them "the people of the wastelands", and mentioned that they used to venerate the sun and prostrate to it. He also related of their camels on the shores of the Yemeni sea that when a camel smells ambergris it kneels over it and does not leave until its owner comes to pick it up, and that the camel sometimes dies of hunger. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab pp.20-21}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says most people pronounce the name Mahra, with a sukun on the second letter, but holds the correct form to be Mahara, with a fatha on the second letter, as he found it written by several early authorities. He cites al-Umrani for the claim that Mahra is a land to which the camels are attributed, and corrects him: Mahra is a tribe, Mahra ibn Haydan ibn Amr ibn al-Haf ibn Quda'a, to whom the Mahri camels are attributed. He adds that they have a district in Yemen named for them, about a month from Oman and, according to Abu Zayd, also about a month from Hadramawt, with a length of 64 degrees and a width of 17 degrees 30 minutes in the first climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.234}
