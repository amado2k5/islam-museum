## Overview
Amorium is a city of Phrygia in Anatolia. Ptolemy and Strabo knew it as one of the great cities of Phrygia. Al-Mu'tasim took it in 223 AH, and the Arabs had earlier taken it in 92 AH under Yazid ibn Mu'awiya before the Byzantines regained it. The sources disagree on its latitude, which the commentator gives as between 38 and 43 degrees according to the authority each cites. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 540}

Golius says that Amorium was one of the largest cities of Phrygia and that Ptolemy and Strabo both recorded it. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 541}

Golius, citing al-Makin (the text has "Almasinus"), relates that al-Mu'tasim gathered his army at Tarsus and then marched on Amorium in 223 AH, after a captive woman appealed to him for help and he answered her. He adds that the Byzantine emperor Theophilus had gone out to the city with his army, was defeated and fled. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 540}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes Amorium as a town in the lands of Rum. Al-Mu'tasim campaigned against it when news reached him of the captivity of the 'Alawiyya. The tellers say it was named after Amoriya, daughter of Rum, son of Ifraz, son of Sam, son of Noah. He quotes Abu Tammam: "O day of the battle of Amorium, your hopes turned away from you, each of them sweet milk in its pail." {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.158}

Yaqut reports Ptolemy's latitude and longitude for Amorium and the positions of the planets, placing it in the fifth climate. The Zij of Abu Awn places it in the fourth. Yaqut then says that al-Mu'tasim took it in 223 AH and took Ankara with it, in the story of the 'Alawiyya captives, and that it was one of the greatest conquests of Islam. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.158}

Yaqut also records a second Amorium: a small town on the bank of the Orontes between Famiya and Shayzar. It had ruins from an earlier time, a good income, and a mill that brought in much money. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.158}
