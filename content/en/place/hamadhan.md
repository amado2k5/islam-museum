## Overview
Hamadhan is a city of the mountain region. Al-Baladhuri reports that Jarir ibn Abd Allah conquered it in 23 AH, or 24 AH by another account, on the same terms as Nihawand, after an arrow struck his eye and he said he offered it to God. Its people then broke faith, and al-Ala' ibn Wahb fought them until they came down to his judgement, for a land tax, a poll tax on heads, and a hundred thousand dirhams for the Muslims. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.430-433}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut places Hamadhan in the fourth climate and gives the astronomers' figures of 73 degrees of longitude and 36 of latitude. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.410}

Quoting Hisham ibn al-Kalbi, he says the city took its name from Hamadhan son of Fallij son of Sam son of Noah. He also cites Shu'ba's saying that the mountains are an army and Hamadhan is their fighting front, and that its water is the sweetest and its air the finest. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.410}

The sources differ on the date of its conquest. One account has Rabi'a ibn Uthman dating it to Jumada al-Ula, six months after the death of Umar; another has al-Mughira send Jarir ibn Abdallah al-Bajali in year 23 AH, when Jarir lost an eye in the fighting, after which the city was joined to Kathir ibn Shihab, governor of Dinawar. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.410}

A Persian scholar, quoted by Yaqut, described Hamadhan as the largest city of the mountain region, four farsakhs by four, with its merchants in the city, its money-changers at Sanjabad and its cloth-dealers at Barshiqan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.410}
