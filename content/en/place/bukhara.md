## Overview
Bukhara: a city on the Silk Road in present-day Uzbekistan, which was a centre of learning in the time of the Samanids. al-Maqdisi reports that no meat like its mutton is to be seen, and that they have a kind of melon called the saff. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p.247}

In the book Futuh al-Buldan there is an account of the khatun of Bukhara and her peace with Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad, and then her breaking of it in the days of Sa'id ibn Uthman, and the restoration of peace by a pledge. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} What is said of the upbringing of Ibn Sina there needs an additional source.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut al-Hamawi]] describes Bukhara as one of the greatest and most splendid cities of Transoxiana, reached by crossing from Amul al-Shatt, two days from the Oxus, and the seat of the Samanid kingdom. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.485} He says he looked for the derivation of its name and found none, that in his day its fruit was carried to Marw, twelve stages away, and that it lay seven days or thirty-seven parasangs from Samarqand. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.486}

### The city and its walls
Yaqut quotes an author on the regions who had seen no town in Islam with finer surroundings than Bukhara: from the top of its citadel you see nothing but unbroken green. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.486} Its old name was Bumijkath; it was built of latticed timber; a wall twelve parasangs by twelve enclosed its palaces, gardens and villages, a second wall about a parasang square ran inside it, then the strong wall of the city itself, with a citadel outside housing the Samanid governors of Khurasan and the congregational mosque at the citadel gate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.486} The Sughd river, at its last reach, runs through its suburb. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.487}

### Coinage
Under the Samanids the people of Bukhara dealt in dirhams, not dinars, treating gold as a commodity; they had dirhams called *ghitrifi*, alloyed of iron, brass, lead and other metals, current only in Bukhara and its districts, and others called *musayyabi* and *muhammadi*. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.487}

### The conquest
Yaqut relates from al-Baladhuri that when Ziyad died in 53 AH Mu'awiya gave Khurasan to his son Ubayd Allah, who crossed the river with twenty-four thousand men; Bukhara was then ruled by a woman called the *Khatun*, who called on the Turks, who were routed, and she then made peace for a million. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.488} Sa'id ibn Uthman ibn Affan crossed the river in 55 AH; the Khatun broke the treaty and then restored it after the Sughdians and Turks were beaten. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.489} Yaqut says he found nothing more on the city until 87 AH, when [[person/qutayba-ibn-muslim|Qutayba ibn Muslim]] crossed to it, was besieged for four months by the forces of Sughd, Farghana, al-Shash and Bukhara, defeated them and took it, after which "Bukhara was clear for the Muslims". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.489}

### People named after it
Among those named after the city Yaqut lists [[person/al-bukhari|Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari]], author of the *Sahih*, born in 194 AH and died on the night of Eid al-Fitr 256 AH at Khartank after he had been forced out of Bukhara, {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.489} and [[person/ibn-sina|Abu Ali ibn Sina]], vizier to the Buyid Shams al-Dawla of Hamadhan, who died on Saturday 6 Sha'ban 428 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.490} He also cites, without grading it, a report with a chain going back to Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman on the merit of a city beyond the Oxus called Bukhara, {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.487} and verses by poets who disparaged the filth of its lanes. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.487} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.488}
