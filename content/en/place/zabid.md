## Overview
Zabid is a Yemeni coastal city, which was a centre of Islamic learning in the late Middle Ages, and has been inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List since 1993. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

The cited source is a reference for coordinates; the description needs an additional source.

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The German Archaeological Institute's iDAI.gazetteer (no. 2043303) records this place under the preferred name «Zabid», typed as a populated place. It sits under «Al Ḥudaida (muḥāfaẓāt)» in the gazetteer's hierarchy, and among the names it records are «Zabīd», «Zebid». {cite:idai-gazetteer 2043303}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the vowels of the name (Zabid) and says it is the name of a valley holding a city called al-Husayb, whose name the valley's name came to overshadow, so that it is known only by it. He calls it a famous city of Yemen, founded in the days of al-Ma'mun, facing the coast of Ghalafiqa and the coast of al-Mandab, and takes the name to be one coined for this place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.131} Among scholars linked to it he names Abu Qurra Musa ibn Tariq al-Zabidi, its judge, who narrated from al-Thawri, Ibn Jurayj and Rabi'a and was praised by Ahmad ibn Hanbal. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.131} He then relates that al-Ma'mun sent Muhammad ibn Ziyad to Yemen as governor, with Ibn Hisham as vizier and al-Taghlibi as judge, and that al-Ziyadi made the pilgrimage in 203 AH, went on to Yemen, conquered Tihama and laid out Zabid in 204. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.132}
