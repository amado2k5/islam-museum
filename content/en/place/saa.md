## Summary
Ṣāʿ is a small, pleasant town at the foot of a hill, overlooking a great river that cuts through its suburbs and runs through its houses. He says it is now ruined. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.247}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Ṣāʿ on a road that passes Karmaṭa, which has a market, fields and cattle, then Marāwiz and the valley of Masūn. From Karmaṭa to Marāwiz is one stage; from Marāwiz to the valley of Masūn is another; and the road to Masūn goes by Tābarnda, an impregnable fortress on a hill overlooking the valley of the Mulūya. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.247}

The Mulūya valley meets the valley of Ṣāʿ, and the two join and flow into the sea between Jarāwa ibn Qays and Melilla. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.247}

From the valley of Masūn to Ṣāʿ is one stage. He calls it a small, pleasant city at the foot of a hill, overlooking a great river that cuts through its suburbs and runs through its houses. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.247}

He adds that it is now ruined and in decline, so the entry records it as it was described before its ruin. The passage does not name its people or its crops. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.247}
