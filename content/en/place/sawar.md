## Summary
Sawar is a town near Bulghar, with wooden buildings where its people shelter in winter, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Sawar in his account of the lands of the Rus and the Bulghars. After the city of Bulghar, which has Christians and Muslims and a congregational mosque for the Muslims, lies the town of Sawar near it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}

Its buildings are of wood, and its people take shelter in them in winter; in summer they move to tents, which he calls khargahat. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}

Al-Idrisi says nothing more about Sawar in this passage: no account of its people, markets or farming. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}

In the same section he notes the climate of the region: among the Rus and the Bulghars the winter day lasts about three and a half hours, which he says al-Hawqali observed himself in winter, when the day was about as long as four prayers said one after another. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}
