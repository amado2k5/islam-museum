## Overview
al-Muhammadiyya: a city that al-Mahdi built at al-Rayy in the year 158 AH, and surrounded with a trench. He made for it a suburb (faṣīl) built of fired brick, and named it al-Muhammadiyya. The people of al-Rayy came to call the inner city and its suburb the inner city and the outer city. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Its surroundings
The fortress of al-Zunbadi lies within it, and after it was built the family of Lahrish settled there. The old al-Rayy was called Araziy in the pre-Islamic period. It is said that it was swallowed by the earth; it lies six farsakhs from al-Muhammadiyya, and al-Rayy is named after it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut treats al-Muhammadiyya as the name of several places and lists them: a village in the environs of Baghdad in the Khurasan-road district whose crop is mostly rice; a Muhammadiyya at Baghdad among the villages between the rivers, home of the man of letters Abu Ali Muhammad ibn al-Husayn; a Muhammadiyya in the workings of Barqa toward Alexandria; a city in the district of the Zab in the land of the Maghreb; al-Masila in the Maghreb, which Muhammad ibn al-Mahdi called al-Qa'im marked out with his spear, named al-Muhammadiyya and had Ali ibn Hamdun al-Andalusi build in 315; a city in Kirman in the third clime; and al-Itakhiyya, which al-Mutawakkil renamed al-Muhammadiyya after his son Muhammad al-Muntasir, near Samarra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.64-65} He then relates that at Marw he came upon Ibn Faris's book Tamam al-Fasih in the author's own hand, dated Ramadan 390 "at al-Muhammadiyya," and asked for years about a place of that name in the mountain districts until he found in Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn al-Faqih's book, from Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Razi, that when al-Mahdi came to Rayy in al-Mansur's caliphate he built the city of Rayy where the people now are, dug a moat around it, built its congregational mosque through Ammar ibn Abi al-Khasib, and the work was finished in 158; he gave it a fired-brick suburb girding it and named it al-Muhammadiyya, so the people of Rayy call the inner town the City and the suburb the Outer City; the fortress known as al-Zubaydiyya inside the city was al-Mahdi's lodging, then became a prison, fell to ruin, was rebuilt by Rafi' ibn Harthama in 278 and ruined again by Rayy's people after he left. Yaqut says the report's wording is defective but its point stands — it is a quarter of Rayy — and adds from Abu Sa'd al-Abi's history the tale of the ancient foundations and the quarter of Mahdi-Abadh, and that Rayy was called al-Muhammadiyya after al-Mahdi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.65}

## The museum's identification
The record's Muhammadiyya is exactly Yaqut's Muhammadiyya of Rayy: his report (from Ibn al-Faqih through al-Razi, and from al-Abi's history) agrees with the record on the year 158, the moat, the fired-brick suburb and the naming of the inner and outer cities, while Yaqut prints the fortress as "al-Zubaydiyya" where the record has "al-Zanbadi" — a wording difference left as it stands. The other Muhammadiyyas he lists in the same entry (Baghdad, Barqa, the Maghreb, Kirman, Samarra) are different towns.
