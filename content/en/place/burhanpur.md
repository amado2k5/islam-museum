## Overview
Burhanpur lies in Khandesh, a region of west-central India in the upper valley of the Tapti river, north-west of the Deccan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1061} The Encyclopaedia of Islam describes it as the capital of the Faruqi dynasty that ruled Khandesh, and notes that the city was named after Shaykh Burhan al-Din Gharib, who settled at Dawlatabad and introduced the Chishti order into the Deccan. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 844} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 65}

## Trade and cloth
Much of the region's prosperity, the article on Khandesh says, came from the trade route from the north through the Burhanpur gap, along Khandesh and on to Surat. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1062} In Mughal times Khandesh was famous for sugar-cane, indigo, fine cotton and the cloth trade, especially the gold and silver brocaded cloth produced at Burhanpur; the article cites European travellers such as Thevenot and Tavernier, and notes Bernier's remark that the labour was exacted almost by force and that the labourers were wretchedly poor. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1061}

## Capital of the Faruqis
The Faruqis became independent of Delhi about 784/1382, and their neighbours in Gujarat, the Bahmani sultanate and Ahmadnagar usually called the ruler of Asir and Burhanpur a hakim or wali rather than a sultan, which the article links to the lesser title Khan from which Khandesh takes its name. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 844} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1061} The capital was plundered in 839/1435 by the Bahmani general Malik al-Tujjar, and in 914/1508 Mahmud Begra of Gujarat captured Thalner and Burhanpur from the forces of the Nizam Shah of Ahmadnagar. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 844} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 845} The article adds that Burhanpur was a favourite burial place for Sufis, and that the Faruqis provided land grants for the disciples of Shaykh Burhan al-Din Gharib, who is said to have foretold the founding of the city and the later Faruqi rule there. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 846} The article takes the Faruqi period down to Akbar's capture of Asir in 1009/1601. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1062}

## Mughals, Marathas and British
Under the Mughals the Deccan operations were conducted from Burhanpur, and the Maratha mercenary bands trained by Malik Ambar harassed the Mughal armies from the region. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1062} Burhanpur fell to the Marathas in 1096/1695; Nizam al-Mulk gained Asirgarh and Burhanpur in 1132/1720; and the British, who first appeared in 1779, took Burhanpur and Asir in 1803, and in 1864 the Burhanpur-Asirgarh region became part of the Nimar district of the Central Provinces. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 1062}
