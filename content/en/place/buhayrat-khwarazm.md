## Overview
Lake Khwarazm: a lake surrounded by wastelands. al-Idrisi reports that its circuit is about three hundred miles, that rivers flow into it, and that it lies about eighteen stages from the sea. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.699}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Buhayrat Khwarazm" and says that the water of the Jayhun (the Oxus) flows into it at a place where fishermen live, with no village or building, called Khalajan, and that opposite Khalajan on its shore lies the land of the Ghuzziya of the Turks. He puts its circuit, "as it has reached me", at about a hundred farsakhs; its water is salt and it has no visible outlet. He says that both the Jayhun and the Sayhun (the Jaxartes) run into it, that a journey of several days in the lake separates the place where one enters from the place where the other does, and that many other rivers also flow in, yet its water stays salt and the lake does not grow, small as it is. He supposes, adding "and God knows", that there are cracks and seepages between it and the Khazar Sea feeding it, and that about ten stages lie between the two seas, with broad sands in between that do not prevent seepage. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.351}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut and al-Idrisi agree that it is a salt lake fed by many rivers, but their figures differ. Yaqut gives its circuit as about a hundred farsakhs and al-Idrisi as about three hundred miles; the two agree only if a farsakh is counted as three miles, the usual reckoning, which the museum has not checked against Yaqut's own text. On the distance to the sea, Yaqut gives about ten stages to the Khazar Sea and al-Idrisi about eighteen stages to "the sea"; the museum does not choose, and does not assume that the two mean the same sea. Right after this entry Yaqut gives a separate "Buhayrat Zarah" in the land of Sijistan, a lake of sweet water about thirty farsakhs long, which is a different lake. The point on the record is approximate and Yaqut gives no coordinates. The museum reads this lake as the Aral Sea; that is the museum's reading, not Yaqut's wording, and the museum already holds a separate record for the [[place/aral|Aral Sea]], so whether the two records should be merged, or one made an alias of the other, is left to the editors.
