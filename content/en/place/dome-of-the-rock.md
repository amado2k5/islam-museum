## Overview
The Dome of the Rock mosque, in the middle of the Haram al-Sharif in Jerusalem above the sacred rock; 'Abd al-Malik b. Marwan built it on an octagon of stone — begun 66 AH (685) and finished 72 AH (691) {cite:bayt-al-maqdis-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 43}. The book records its restoration with its dome in 1964 under King Husayn's patronage {cite:bayt-al-maqdis-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 93}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam calls the Dome of the Rock (Qubbat al-Sakhra) a Muslim shrine built around 692 in Jerusalem's Haram al-Sharif. It says the caliph Abd al-Malik of the Umayyad dynasty raised the domed building over the rock (sakhra) from which, in Muslim tradition, the Prophet Muhammad set out on his mystical journey (miraj) toward heaven and hell. The ring-shaped plan, which it links closely to Roman and Byzantine precedents, has two ambulatories encircling the domed space at the centre, and Arabic mosaic inscriptions and intricate vegetal ornament decorate the interior. The Dictionary's date of about 692 differs from the record above, which gives 66 AH (685) to 72 AH (691) for the building; both are reported and neither is settled. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1939}
