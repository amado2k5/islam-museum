## Overview
Al-Hadr is a known fortress in the district of Ayajir, in the land of [[place/mosul|Mosul]]. It belonged to al-Satirun, son of Asitirun, king of the Syrians. Al-Mas'udi reports that poets mentioned it for the greatness of its kingdom and the fine quality of its building, among them Abu Dawud Jariya ibn Hajjaj al-Iyadi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.552-554}

## Al-Dayzan and Shapur
Al-Mas'udi reports that al-Dayzan ibn Jabhala ibn Mu'awiya of Tanukh ruled it after that. Shapur came down upon it with his troops, and it held out for a month, and he could find no way to take it, until the daughter of al-Dayzan, al-Nadiriyya, showed him its water source from the river al-Tharthar. He broke it down after killing al-Dayzan and those with him. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.552-554}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says that al-Hadr is the name of a city opposite Tikrit in the open desert, between Tikrit, Mosul and the Euphrates, with houses, roofs and doors built of dressed stone. It is said to have had sixty great towers, nine small towers between each pair, with a palace facing each tower and a bath beside it. The Tharthar river passed by it, a great river with villages and gardens along it, fed from the Hirmas, the river of Nisibin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.267} In his own day, he says, nothing remained of al-Hadr but the outline of the wall and traces pointing to its former greatness; he cites a man of Tikrit who went hunting, reached it and saw remains and carved figures in what was left of the walls, and says its king was called al-Satirun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.268}

He cites al-Sharqi ibn al-Qutami that al-Dayzan, a king of the Quda'a, settled in al-Hadr and raided the lands of Persia; the city was said to be under a talisman that only the blood of a grey dove together with the menstrual blood of a blue-eyed woman could break. Al-Dayzan raided the Sawad and took the sister of Shapur al-Junud, who then besieged him for two years without success, until al-Dayzan's daughter al-Nadira began her period and her father sent her out to the place set aside for that. She told Shapur the means, on his promise to marry her, and the wall fell; he entered the city and killed about a hundred thousand of the Quda'a. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.268} Yaqut goes on to say that Shapur then had al-Nadira put to death at Ayn al-Tamr by having her braids tied to two unruly horses, which became a proverb among the Arabs. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.269}

He also gives another account, that al-Satirun ibn Asturun al-Jurmuqi built al-Hadr and marched with four hundred thousand against the Children of Israel, and that the prophet Jeremiah called down a curse on him so that he and his men perished. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.269}
