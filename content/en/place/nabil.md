## Overview
Nabil is a palace on the sea that stood near a populous Roman city, which was later ruined. A small, well-kept palace remains on the site. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 293}

## Location
Al-Idrisi describes the palace of Nabil as lying on the sea, near a large city that flourished in the days of the Romans and then fell into ruin. The site of the city is still known, and the small palace still stands on it. Close by is the palace of Tawsihan, where traces of another city that flourished under the Romans and then fell remain, and its ruins still show what it was. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 293}

## Ruin
Al-Idrisi says Nabil was a very large and flourishing city of the Romans. When the island was conquered at the beginning of Islam, its revenues and fine places were plundered, until nothing remained of it but the site of the palace, and the remnants of its ruin still bear witness to it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 302}

## Distances
From Tawsihan to the palace of Nabil is eight miles. From Nabil to the palace of al-Khayyat is eight miles, and that palace lies about two miles from the sea. The island of Shiklah Mur lies thirty miles from Nabil, and it is near the coastal city of Nabil. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 302} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 585}

Note: the text places Nabil in the section on the island that al-Idrisi describes; the modern identification of the place is not settled in this entry.
