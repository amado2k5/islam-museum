## Overview
Tikrit is a city on a river. The Arabic text in our data says that the river is the Euphrates, while the English version says it is the Tigris; an additional source is needed. According to al-Baladhuri, al-Nusayr and Hudhayfa ibn Mihsan raided it after the Day of the Bridge and took plunder of cattle and sheep. It is related from Abu Mas'ud that the two gave its people safe conduct and wrote them a letter, which Utba ibn Farqad al-Sulami carried out when he conquered Tirhan and [[place/mosul|Mosul]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.348}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] gives Takrit with *a* on the first letter, saying common people say Tikrit; for him it is a famous town between Baghdad and Mosul, nearer Baghdad, thirty parasangs from it, with a strong citadel at its upper end overhanging the Tigris, on the west bank. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.803} He says the first to build its citadel was Shapur son of Ardashir son of Babak, and that some said it was named after Tikrit daughter of Wa'il. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.803} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.804}

He relates from al-Abbas ibn Yahya al-Takriti, known for his learning in Mosul, a story he calls "widespread among the well-informed at Tikrit": a Persian king built the citadel on a great rock standing out in the middle of the Tigris and garrisoned it against the Byzantines; its marzban saw a Christian Arab clan in the desert and asked for the hand of their chief's daughter; they required him to join their religion, which he did; he took her to the citadel, her clan settled round it and built houses, and since her name was Tikrit the suburb was named after her. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.804}
