## Overview
Siraf is a land on the coast of Fars. According to one report, its fort named Suryanj is the one from which Abd Muslim wrote a safe conduct to its people and threw it to them on an arrow, and Umar ordered it carried out. It is also said that the fort was Shahriyaj, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «[Siraf]» (Pleiades no. 29762), classed as a settlement, with activity attested from 30 BCE to 1599 CE. Names recorded there include Siraf, سيراف. {cite:pleiades 29762}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut records that the Persians, in their book called the Abastak, say that Kay Kavus tried to ascend to heaven and fell at Siraf, asking for water and milk; the name is from shir (milk) and ab (water), later Arabised as Siraf. It is a great city on the Persian Gulf coast, once the harbour of India, and some say the capital of the Kura of Ardashir Khurra. It had no harbour of its own, so ships anchored about two farsakhs away at a bay called Nabad, between two mountains, safe from every wind. From Siraf to Basra was seven days when the weather was fine. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 pp.294-295}
