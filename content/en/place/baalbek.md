## Overview
Baalbek: a city among the districts of Damascus, in the province of Sanir. al-Mas'udi reports that in it is the house of Ba'l, the idol mentioned in the Qur'an. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.554}

## The temple
al-Mas'udi reports that the Greeks chose for this temple a piece of land between Mount Lebanon and Mount Sanir, and made it a place for idols. There are two great buildings, one older than the other. In them are wonderful carvings cut in the stone, together with the height of their structure, the greatness of their stones and the length of their columns. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.554}

## In the books of antiquities
Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari counted "the playground of Baalbek" among the famous monuments, from the remains of the buildings of the ancients in al-Sham. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.231}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Heliopolis/Col. Iulia Augusta Felix» (Pleiades no. 678179), classed as settlement, archaeological site, temple and architectural complex, with activity attested from 1200 BCE to 2100 CE. Recorded names include «Heliopolis», «Baalbek» and «Balbec». It describes the place as an ancient settlement in Lebanon's Beqaa Valley, named a UNESCO World Heritage Site for its colossal structures, among the finest examples of Imperial Roman architecture. {cite:pleiades 678179}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says that Baalbek is an ancient city with wonderful buildings, great remains and palaces on marble columns, three days from Damascus, or twelve farsakhs on the coastal side according to another report. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.453} He explains the name as a compound of Ba'l, the name of an idol, and Bakk, and sets out how it is declined, diminished and relativized. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.453} He says its molasses, cheese, olive oil and milk are proverbial, and that Imru' al-Qays mentions it in verse. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.454} He reports a view that it was the dowry of Bilqis and holds the palace of Sulayman ibn Dawud on marble columns, and lists tombs its people ascribe to al-Ashtar al-Nakha'i and to Hafsa and a station of Ibrahim in its citadel; he rejects some of these, saying al-Ashtar died at al-Qulzum and that the tomb of Hafsa, wife of the Prophet, is in Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.454} He relates that Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, after the conquest of Damascus in the year 14 AH, passed through and made a treaty with its people securing their persons, property and churches, those who stayed paying the jizya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.454} Under the entry Ba'l he says that Ba'l was an idol of the people of Ilyas, that Baalbek is named for it, and that the Greeks made two great temples there with carvings in stone. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.455}
