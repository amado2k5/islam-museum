## Overview
Tabarqa is a harbour for ships on the coast. It lies seventy miles from Banzart, and Andalusian ships reach it, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.289}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Thabraca» (Pleiades no. 315192), classed as a settlement, with activity attested from 330 BCE to 640 CE. Recorded names include Thabraca, Thabraka and Cabraca. It describes Thabraca as a maritime city of Numidia at the mouth of the river Tusca. {cite:pleiades 315192}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Tabarqa", with vowels on the first letters and a qaf after the silent ra, and says it is a city in the Maghrib on the Berber mainland side, on the seashore near Baja, with remains of the ancients and wondrous buildings, populated because merchants come to it; it has a large river which large ships enter and leave into the sea of Tabarqa, and to the east of the city are fortresses called the fortresses of Banzart. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.16} In another entry he quotes Abu Ubayd al-Bakri that to the east of Tabarqa, a day's journey and somewhat more, there are fortresses called the fortresses of Banzart. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.500}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut and al-Idrisi both tie Tabarqa to the coast and to Banzart: al-Idrisi puts it seventy miles from [[place/banzart|Banzart]] and calls it an anchorage for ships, and Yaqut puts the fortresses of Banzart to its east and says large ships enter its river. Yaqut puts Tabarqa near Baja, for which the museum holds a record, [[place/baja|Baja]], but the museum has not checked the distance between them. The point on the record (36.955, 8.755) is on Tunisia's north coast and fits Yaqut's placing of it near Baja and west of the fortresses of Banzart; Yaqut gives no coordinates. The link to modern Tabarka rests on the Wikidata identifier and the Pleiades entry already on the record, not on Yaqut's wording.
