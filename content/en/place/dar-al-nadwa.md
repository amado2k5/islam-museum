## Overview
The council of [[group/quraysh|Quraysh]] in [[place/mecca|Mecca]] before and after Islam. Qusayy ibn Kilab built it near the Kaaba, and the notables of Quraysh met there to decide matters of war, peace and marriage alliances.

## Significance
From it came the consultations of Quraysh about the Prophet and the war against him; in Arabic memory it became the name of any aristocratic consultative council. {cite:wikidata}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut treats Dar al-Nadwa in two entries. In the first he says it is in Mecca, that Qusayy ibn Kilab ibn Murra made it when he became master of Mecca, a house where they met for consultation, and that after his death he gave it to his son 'Abd al-Dar; he derives the word from al-nadi and al-muntada, the gathering place of a people. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.423} He then gives two accounts of what became of it: that it passed to Hakim ibn Hizam, who sold it to Mu'awiya for a hundred thousand dirhams and, when Mu'awiya reproached him, answered that honours had gone but piety remained, that he had bought it in the Jahiliyya for a skin of wine and that he put the price in God's cause; and, from Ibn al-Kalbi, that it was the first house Quraysh built in Mecca, passed to 'Abd al-Dar and his sons until 'Ikrima ibn 'Amir, of that line, sold it to Mu'awiya, who made it the house of government. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.423} The two accounts name different sellers. In his entry on Dar al-'Ajala, relayed by Ahmad ibn Jabir from al-'Abbas ibn Hisham al-Kalbi, a reply of the latter's father says that Banu Sa'd claim that house was built before Dar al-Nadwa and was the first house Quraysh built in Mecca, a claim that differs from what Yaqut quotes from Ibn al-Kalbi about Dar al-Nadwa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.422}

In the entry on al-Nadwa he explains the word, says the house was so named because people, when something happened, gathered there to consult, quotes al-Kharazanji that it is the house of invitation, where people were invited to food and counsel, and also called the house of boasting; the words "Dar al-Nadwa is from the Sacred Mosque" then follow with no mark of where al-Kharazanji's quotation ends, so the speaker is open, and the phrase can mean part of it, belonging to it or near it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.279} In the entry on Mecca he says Qusayy built it there, and that no woman was married, no banner tied, no boy "excused" (yu'dhar, a word usually explained as circumcision or marking coming of age) and no girl put on the dir' (a word for a shift or a coat of mail) except in it, and that it was called al-Nadwa because they gathered there for good and ill. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.186}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Raheeq al-Makhtum, has the house of Qusayy ibn Kilab in Mecca, the council chamber of Quraysh, where Quraysh held the assembly that planned to kill the Prophet before the emigration; the page adds, from Wikidata, that he built it near the Kaaba. Yaqut agrees that it is Qusayy's house in Mecca and a place of consultation, and adds its passing to 'Abd al-Dar, its sale to Mu'awiya and use as the house of government, two conflicting accounts of the seller, and a claim by Banu Sa'd, in his Dar al-'Ajala entry, about which house was first. He says nothing in either entry of the assembly against the Prophet or of the Kaaba, so those rest on the other sources. The record's point (21.423, 39.826) is marked as a site and lies 0.1 km from the museum's Mecca point; Yaqut gives no coordinates, and his phrase "from the Sacred Mosque" is open, so the museum draws no conclusion on the exact position from it.
