## Overview
Ayn Shams: one of the villages of Egypt. After the conquest of Fustat, Amr ibn al-As sent Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa al-Sahmi to it. He settled on its land, and made peace with the people of its villages on the same terms as the judgement of Fustat, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.304}

It is a site in the accounts of [[event/four-lieutenants-egypt|the four commanders of Amr in Egypt]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes Ayn Shams as the city of Pharaoh of Moses in Egypt, about three farsakhs from Fustat, between it and Bilbays on the Syrian side, near al-Matariyya, and not on the bank of the Nile. It was a large city and the capital of the Itrib district. It lies in ruins today, with old remains and tall columns that common people call the pillars of Pharaoh. He reports from al-Hasan ibn Ibrahim al-Misri that two columns were used to mark the sun's turning points: at the start of Capricorn the sun reached the southern column, and at the start of Cancer the northern one, and their tops were moist when the Nile rose. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.178}
