## Overview
Al-Baqi', known in the old sources as Baqi' al-Gharqad, is the cemetery of the people of Medina. Yaqut says it lies inside the city {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.473}; Ibn Battuta places it east of the city and says that one reaches it through a gate called the Baqi' gate {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.75}. The museum has a second record of the cemetery, [[place/baqi-al-gharqad|Baqi' al-Gharqad]], which carries al-Samhudi's account of its merits; this page gathers what Yaqut and Ibn Battuta say.

## The name
Yaqut explains that in the language a baqi' is a place where the roots of trees of various kinds grow, and that Baqi' al-Gharqad takes its name from the gharqad, the large boxthorn; he supports both words with a line of rajaz and a line of verse {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.473}. Under the headword gharqad he repeats that it is a plant, the large boxthorn, from which Baqi' al-Gharqad, the cemetery of the people of Medina, was named {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.194}.

## In early poetry and report
Yaqut gives verses by Amr ibn al-Nu'man al-Bayadi mourning a people who entered one of their orchards during a war, shut its gate behind them and then fought one another there; the poet asks where are those he knew in comfort "between al-Aqiq and Baqi' al-Gharqad". Yaqut adds that in the Hamasa the same verses are ascribed to a man of Khath'am {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.473}. He also quotes al-Zubayr that the upper part of the wadis of al-Aqiq is al-Baqi' {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.473}.

## Other places called Baqi'
Yaqut separates Baqi' al-Gharqad from other places with the name: Baqi' al-Zubayr in Medina, with houses and dwellings; Baqi' al-Khayl in Medina, near the house of Zayd ibn Thabit; and Baqi' al-Khabjaba, which occurs in the Sunan of Abu Dawud. He reports from al-Suhayli, in his commentary on the Sira, that khabjaba is a shrub after which the place was known, a rare word Yaqut found nowhere else, and that the transmitters fix it with two jims {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.474}. A diminutive Buqay' is a different place, among the lands of Banu Uqayl beyond al-Yamama, and must not be confused with the cemetery of Medina {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.474}.

## In Ibn Battuta's Rihla
Ibn Battuta lists al-Baqi' among the venerated sites outside Medina and describes it from the gate outward. The first tomb on the left of one leaving is that of Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib, the aunt of the Prophet and mother of al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.75}. He then names the burials he mentions: in front of it the tomb of Imam Malik ibn Anas under a small dome; in front of that the tomb of Ibrahim, son of the Prophet, under a white dome; to its right the grave of Abd al-Rahman ibn Umar ibn al-Khattab, known as Abu Shahma; opposite them the tombs of Aqil ibn Abi Talib and of Abd Allah, son of Ja'far ibn Abi Talib (Dhu al-Janahayn) {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.76}. He describes an enclosure with the graves of the Mothers of the Believers, and next to it another holding the tombs of al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib and al-Hasan ibn Ali under one lofty, well-built dome on the right of one leaving by the gate; al-Hasan's head lies by al-Abbas's feet, and both tombs are raised above the ground, broad and clad in finely joined panels set with brass plates {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.76}.

He adds that al-Baqi' holds the graves of the Emigrants, the Ansar and other Companions, though most are not identified, and that at its far end is the tomb of Uthman ibn Affan under a large dome, with the tomb of Fatima bint Asad, mother of Ali ibn Abi Talib, near it {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.76}.

Elsewhere in the Rihla he tells that when the emir Chupan was killed, his body and his son's were brought to Arafat and then carried to Medina to be buried in a tomb Chupan had built near the Prophet's Mosque; this was refused and he was buried in al-Baqi' {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p.145}.

## A note on dates
The sources read here give no date for the founding of the cemetery or of its monuments, so none is stated on this page.
