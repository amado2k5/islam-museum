## Overview
Dhi Qar is a site near al-Kufa which Ali made the rallying point of his army in 36 AH. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 47}

## The mustering from there
Ali camped at Dhi Qar eight nights after leaving Medina, sent Ibn Abbas ahead to the Kufans, who were slow to respond, then sent Ammar and al-Hasan, and removed Abu Musa al-Ash'ari from the governorship of al-Kufa. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 47} {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 48}

## al-Qa'qa''s address
Al-Qa'qa' ibn Amr stood up among the people of al-Kufa saying that people must have an authority to order their affairs, that Ali had been fair, and that he was only calling to reconciliation. {cite:sallabi-ali-vol2 p. 48}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Dhu Qar: a water of Bakr ibn Wa'il near al-Kufa between it and Wasit; "hanu Dhi Qar" lies a night away; and there took place the famous battle between Bakr ibn Wa'il and the Persians. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.293} He tells its story at length: Kisra grew angry with al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir over Adi ibn Zayd and his son Zayd, in a long tale; al-Nu'man went to Tayyi', who refused to let him into their mountain; the Banu Rawaha offered him help, which he thanked them for, saying "I have no hands for you against Kisra"; he placed deposits with the Arab clans and entrusted his family and arms to Hani' ibn Qubaysa ibn Hani' ibn Mas'ud of the Banu Rabi'a ibn Dhu'l ibn Shayban, and the Arabs gathered — Banu Abs, Shayban and others. Then al-Nu'man was taken on a safe-conduct, imprisoned at Sabat and died — some said of plague, some that he was thrown under the elephants' feet. Kisra sent to Hani' demanding the property, and Hani' answered: "a trust is with me and I will never hand it to you." Kisra sent against him al-Hamaruz, his great marzban, with a thousand Persian horse, and Khunayr and Iyas ibn Qubaysa, whom he had made king of al-Hira in al-Nu'man's place, in squadrons of Shahba', Dawsar, Bahra', Iyad, Taghlib and al-Nimr ibn Qasit. Hani' shared out al-Nu'man's coats of mail — seven thousand of them — among his people and the Arabs, saying they were a trust; the Banu Shayban camped in the land of Dhi Qar between the two julha's and battle was joined; al-Hamaruz came forth and Yazid ibn Haritha al-Yashkuri killed him; the Persians had the upper hand the first day, then on the second they suffered thirst, moved to the batha' of Dhi Qar where the fighting grew hard, and were routed. The battle — as is commonly told — fell on the day of the Prophet's birth, or, it is also said, as he returned from Badr; it was the first day the Arabs took their due of the Persians, and it is among the glories of Bakr ibn Wa'il; Yaqut quotes Abu Tammam's verses on it in praise of Abu Dulaf al-Ijli and Khalid ibn Yazid al-Shaybani. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.293-294}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Dhu Qar is the record's site near al-Kufa between it and Wasit; the record's account of Ali's mustering there in 36 AH comes from another source, and Yaqut does not mention that camp — he dwells instead on the older battle between Bakr ibn Wa'il and the Persians that was named after the place.
