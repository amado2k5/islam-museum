## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Sahyun is a name for a quarter of Jerusalem and also for a strong fortress of the Syrian coast that Salah al-Din took back from the Franks in 584 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.436} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.437}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling, a kasra on the sad, a sukun on the ha, an open ya, a quiescent waw and a final nun. He reports from al-Azhari, on the authority of Abu Amr, that Sahyun is al-Rum, the Byzantine lands, or according to others Bayt al-Maqdis, and cites verses of al-Asha that he says praise Yazid and Abd al-Masih, sons of al-Dayyan, or, according to another report, the two bishops of Najran, al-Sayyid and al-Aqib; the verses are described here, not quoted. He then says it is a known place in Jerusalem, a quarter that holds the church of Sahyun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.436}

## The fortress
Yaqut gives a second sense: a strong fortress of the coastal districts of the Syrian sea, under Hims, but not overlooking the sea. It is a sturdy castle on the edge of a mountain; its ditches are wide, deep natural valleys, and only on one side is there a dug ditch, about sixty cubits long, cut in the rock. It has three walls, two below its enclosure and one below the castle itself. He says it was in Frankish hands for a long time until al-Malik al-Nasir Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub recovered it in 584, and that it was in Muslim hands up to his own day. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.436} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.437}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record describes Sahyun as a Crusader-era castle of the Syrian coastal mountains that became an Ayyubid and Mamluk seat. That is Yaqut's second sense, not the quarter of Jerusalem. Yaqut assigns it to the district of Hims and says it does not overlook the sea; that is his administrative and geographical description as written, and the museum ties its record to his entry because it is the fortress Salah al-Din recovered. Salah al-Din has a record, [[person/salah-al-din-yusuf-ibn-ayyub|Salah al-Din Yusuf ibn Ayyub]].
