## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Harim is a strong fortress and a notable district facing Antioch, attached to Aleppo in Yaqut's day, with many trees and waters. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.205}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling with a kasra on the ra and describes it as a strong fortress and a notable district facing Antioch, among the districts of Aleppo in his time. He says it has many trees and waters and is therefore unhealthy, prone to disease. He explains the name in two ways: either from hirman, deprivation, because its strength denies it to an enemy, or from harim, a sanctuary, because it is a sanctuary for those inside. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.205}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Harem a town in Idlib, Syria, with coordinates west of Aleppo near Antioch. Yaqut does not mention Idlib, which is a modern administrative name; the link between Yaqut's Harim and the museum's Harem rests on the name and on the position facing Antioch.
