## Overview
Kirkuk is recorded in Pleiades under the name of the ancient city of Arrapha and in the German Archaeological Institute's iDAI.gazetteer as a populated place, at about 35.47° N, 44.40° E. {cite:pleiades 893941} {cite:idai-gazetteer 2771787}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Arrapha/Karkha de Beth Seluk» (Pleiades no. 893941), classed as a settlement, an archaeological site and a citadel, with a location attested from 2000 BCE to 1000 CE. {cite:pleiades 893941} Recorded names include «Arrapha» [Akkadian, 750 BCE – 330 BCE]; «Karkha de Beth Seluk» [330 BCE – 640 CE]; «Kerkük» [Ottoman Turkish, 1500 CE – 1599 CE]. {cite:pleiades 893941} Pleiades notes that the name of the ancient city of Arrapha is first attested in the time of the Neo-Sumerian empire, that the city later belonged to the Akkadian empire, and that its site is thought to lie at modern Kirkuk in north-eastern Iraq. {cite:pleiades 893941}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The German Archaeological Institute's iDAI.gazetteer (no. 2771787) records this place under the preferred name «Kirkuk», typed as a populated place, with the further names «كركوك» [Arabic]. Its parent unit is «Kirkuk», an administrative unit. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2771787}
