## Summary
A coastal mountain region of northern Morocco, where, according to The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, the Rif Rebellion of 1921 to 1926 took place. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2016}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes the Rif as a coastal mountain region of northern Morocco stretching from the Straits of Gibraltar to the mouth of the Moulouya River. It was the scene of the Rif Rebellion (1921 to 1926), an important anticolonial movement fighting the protectorate forces of Spain and France, led by Muhammad ibn Abd el-Krim al-Khattabi (died 1963), whom it calls an Islamic reformer; see [[person/abd-el-krim]]. In 1921, it says, Rifian forces caused up to nineteen thousand casualties among the Spanish, the greatest single defeat of any European power in North African colonial history. The Rifian Republic (1923 to 1926), according to the Dictionary, put weight on modern self-determination and on applying Islamic law, and the idea of such a republic, though inspiring, has been viewed as a possible source of division in Morocco today. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2016}
