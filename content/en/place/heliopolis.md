## Summary
In Yaqut's account, Ayn Shams is the city of Pharaoh of Moses in Egypt, three farsakhs from Fustat, between Belbeis and near al-Matariyya, and not on the bank of the Nile. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.178} It was the chief town of the district of Atrib, and by Yaqut's time it was in ruins. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.178}

Ancient remains stood there, and tall columns that the common people called the obelisks of Pharaoh. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.178} Yaqut reports the words of al-Hasan ibn Ibrahim al-Misri, who called it the temple of the sun and described two columns of about fifty cubits built on the ground without foundations, with the figure of a man on an animal, from which water dripped when the Nile rose. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.179}

## Identification
The record places the entry at Heliopolis (Masr al-Jadida) from its Wikidata item. Yaqut describes the ancient city of Ayn Shams, not the modern district.
