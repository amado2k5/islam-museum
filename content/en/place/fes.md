## Overview
Fez is one of the cities and capitals of the Maghrib. Uthman ibn Abd al-Haqq al-Marini imposed a set tax on it in return for an end to raids against it. It then became the seat of the Banu Marin, and Sultan Abu Yusuf laid out the new city beside it as a residence for himself and his army, according to Ibn Khaldun. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes Fes as a famous large city on the mainland of the Maghrib, in the Berber lands, the grandest of its cities before Marrakesh was laid out. It lies between two great hills with buildings climbing both flanks; its springs run down to a valley with a middling river that rises from springs west of the city, two thirds of a farsakh away, at Duwi island. On reaching the city the river splits into eight channels that cut through it and turn some six hundred mills inside it, day and night, and a water channel enters every house; he says no city of the Maghrib has water running through it except Granada. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.230} He adds that purple and crimson cloaks are dyed there, that its citadel stands on the highest ground, and that it has three mosques where the Friday sermon is given. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.230} Citing Abu Ubayd al-Bakri, he says it is two separate walled towns, the bank of the Qarawiyyin and the bank of the Andalusians, each with its own mosque, holding over three hundred mills and about twenty baths and many Jews. He records a Maghribi saying, "Fes, a town without people", and says the Andalusian bank was founded in 192 AH and the Qarawiyyin bank in 193 AH under Idris ibn Idris, who died at Walila in 213 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.230} Among those named after it he gives Abu Umar Imran ibn Musa ibn Isa ibn Najh al-Fasi, the jurist of Qayrawan in his day. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.231}
