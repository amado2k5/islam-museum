## Summary
Mashki is a city adjoining the districts of Kirman, populous with people whose inhabitants possess defence and great valour; it has palms, crops, camels and a wealth of fruits. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says: adjoining the districts of Kirman is the city of Mashki, populous with people, in whose inhabitants there is defence and great valour; it has palms, crops, camels and a wealth of fruits {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}. He counts it among the cities of the land of Makran together with Kayz, Dizak, Rasak, Bah, Band, Qasr Qand, Asqafa, Falfahra, al-Tiz and al-Balin {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.172}. Makran is a contiguous land of broad, wide districts over which wastelands, drought and hardship prevail {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.172}. He notes that the tongue of the Makranis is Persian and Makrani, both of which they speak, and that the dress of their common folk is the qaratiq {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}. From the city of Fayrbuz to Dizak are three stages; Dizak is a great, large, populous city with much trade and briskly selling goods {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}.
