## Summary
Gharkhan was a large populous city of the land of the Turkish Isqutiya on the north of the river Itil, where the king of Isqutiya resided and where saddles and arms were made. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.924}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Numanjan to the city of Gharkhan is eight stages; it is a city of the land of the Turkish Isqutiya, large and populous, on the north of the river Itil which reaches the sea of Tabaristan {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.924}. It is a city with estates and continuous buildings; merchants and travellers reach it by that river, and there resides the king of the Turkish Isqutiya, a king possessing men, numbers, weapons and many fortresses, the settlements around him being far apart {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.924}. In this country the crafts and manufactures exceed what is needed, and there are made saddles and arms such as are not made in any town of the land of the Arak for goodness and perfection {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.924}. From the city of Gharkhan to the city of Qaruqiya downstream on the river is eight days, and overland sixteen stages westward {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.924}. The author counts it among the lands of outer Basjirt together with Qaruqiya and Numanjan, lands populous and self-sufficient, with such trade and crafts as suffice them, fertile and rich in herbage and flocks {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.922}.
