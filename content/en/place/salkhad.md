## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Sarkhad is a land adjoining Hawran, under Damascus, with a strong fortress and a wide, pleasant governorate, and wine is attributed to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.401}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a fatha on the sad, a sukun on the ra, a dotted kha and an undotted dal, and says it is a land adjoining the country of Hawran, among the districts of Damascus, a strong fortress and a fine, wide governorate, from which wine is named. He cites a line by a poet who compares the sweetness of something he left in enemy territory to the taste of Sarkhad wine, and explains that sweetness here means sleep. The line is described here, not quoted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.401}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record spells the town Salkhad, the modern form with a lam, while Yaqut writes Sarkhad with a ra. The museum treats them as one place because Yaqut puts it next to Hawran in the districts of Damascus, where Salkhad stands today; Yaqut himself does not give the modern name. The entry that follows in Yaqut, Surkhiyan, a village of Balkh, is another place and has no part in this record.
