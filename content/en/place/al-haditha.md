## Overview
al-Haditha: a town near [[place/mosul|Mosul]]. It was an ancient village with two churches and monasteries for Christians. Harthama ibn Arfaja made it a garrison town and settled some Arab people in it. It was called al-Haditha ("the new") because it came after Mosul, and he built a fortress near it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

It is also said that it was named so when people from al-Anbar moved to it during the governorship of Ibn al-Rufayl in the days of al-Hajjaj, and they built a mosque there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed to decide between the two accounts.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut explains that al-Haditha, meaning "the new one", is a name borne by several places; each was so called because it was newly built, and the name then stuck as a proper name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230} He lists among them Hadithat al-Furat, also called Hadithat al-Nura, some farsakhs from al-Anbar, with a strong fortress in the middle of the Euphrates surrounded by water. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230} Citing Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir, he says that Ammar ibn Yasir, while governing Kufa, sent an army under Abu Mudlaj al-Tamimi to take the lands above the Euphrates, and that Abu Mudlaj built al-Haditha and Hit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230} He reports from al-Sam'ani that its people were Nusayris, and tells an anecdote from al-Sam'ani's teacher, the grammarian Abu al-Baraka Umar ibn Ibrahim, about passing through it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230} Among those named after it he gives Suwayd ibn Sa'id al-Harawi al-Hadathani; quoting al-Khatib, he says Suwayd lived at Hadithat al-Nura, a farsakh from al-Anbar, heard from Malik ibn Anas and Sufyan ibn Uyayna among others, and was cited by Muslim in his Sahih, then records the critical remarks of al-Bukhari and Abu Zur'a and says Suwayd died in Shawwal 240 AH aged a hundred and blind. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.231} Another is Abu Talib Ruh ibn Ahmad al-Hadithi, chief judge of Baghdad, who by Yaqut's account was given that office in 566 AH and died in Muharram 570 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.231}
