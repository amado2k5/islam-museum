## Overview
Kashgar is a city at the far west of China on the Silk Road. It became an Islamic centre after the Qarakhanid khans embraced Islam in the tenth century CE. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Kashghar with the meeting of two still letters, shin and ghayn both dotted; it is a city with villages and rustaqs to which people travel from Samarkand and those parts, lying in the midst of the Turks' lands, its people Muslims. Later scholars are named after it: Abu l-Ma'ali Tughrilshah Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Kashghari the preacher, born in the year 490 and living past 550, and Abu Abdallah al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Alma'i al-Kashghari, shaykh and preacher of many works, who composed more than a hundred and twenty hadith compilations and died in Baghdad in the year 484. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.430-431}

## The museum's identification
The record's Kashgar is the same mid-Turkland city to which travellers came from Samarkand in Yaqut's day. Nearby in the section Kajghar is pointed as a dialect form of the name Kashghar, not another place, and under kha stands Khutan, a town and district short of Kashgar; the record situates the city in Alti-Shahr as its other material does.
