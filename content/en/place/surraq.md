## Overview
Surraq is a city in the districts of al-Ahwaz. Al-Baladhuri mentions that Abu Musa conquered it on terms like the peace of Ramhurmuz. Then its people betrayed, and Haritha ibn Badr did not conquer it. Abd Allah ibn Amir then conquered it by force, and afterwards appointed Haritha over it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut records the name Surraq with a damma on the first letter, a fatha and shadda on the second and a qaf at the end, says it is a non-Arabic word, and describes it as one of the districts of al-Ahwaz: a river with settlements along it, dug by Ardashir ibn Bahman ibn Isfandiyar of old, with Dawraq as its town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.214}

He relays, from Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Mawsili, an account of Haritha ibn Badr al-Ghudani, who stood high with Ziyad ibn Abihi, and who found himself shunned by Ubaydallah ibn Ziyad after Ziyad's death. When Haritha complained, Ubaydallah explained that his own youth and Haritha's drinking made the closeness awkward and asked him to give it up. Haritha replied that he would not, and asked instead to be given some post, so Ubaydallah appointed him over Surraq in the Ahwaz. Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali joined the crowd that saw him off and addressed advisory verses to him, and Yaqut gives Haritha's reply. He also records a second place of the same name, outside the city of Sinjar, which in his day was called Zarraq with a za, and which is therefore a different place from the one in the Ahwaz. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.214}
