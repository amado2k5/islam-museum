## Overview
Kadhimiya is a quarter of Baghdad built around the shrine of Musa al-Kazim. Yaqut does not use the name Kadhimiya. He describes the same ground as Bab al-Tibn, a large quarter of Baghdad beside the moat, opposite the Qati'at Umm Ja'far, and the Quraysh cemeteries beside it, where the grave of Musa al-Kazim lies. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.306} The identification of Bab al-Tibn with the modern quarter is the record's, not Yaqut's.

## Bab al-Tibn in Yaqut's account
Yaqut writes that Bab al-Tibn was in his time a ruined, open tract that was sown, and that Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Hanbal, who had left instructions to that effect, was buried there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.306} Beside it lie the Quraysh cemeteries, which Yaqut says contain the grave of Musa al-Kazim, known as the shrine of Bab al-Tibn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.306} In the Baghdad entry he quotes Ahmad ibn Hanbal that Baghdad runs from al-Sarat to Bab al-Tibn, "which is the shrine of Musa al-Kazim". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.457} In the entry on the Quraysh he repeats that the Quraysh cemeteries of Baghdad are the cemeteries of Bab al-Tibn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.337}
