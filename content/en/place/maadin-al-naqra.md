## Summary
Ma'din al-Naqra is a large populous village near Bahrayn where the pilgrims of Basra and Kufa gather on their road to the Hijaz. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.163}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that Ma'din al-Naqra is a large populous village where the pilgrim caravans of Basra and Kufa assemble; whoever wishes to travel to Medina bears right to al-Ghasila, a halt with Arabs and six salt wells, forty-six miles, then to Batn Nakhl, a village rich in water and palms, thirty-six miles, then to al-Taraf, an empty halt with ponds that gather rainwater, twenty-two miles, then to Medina, fifteen miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.163}. It is thus a pilgrims' station on this route.
