## Overview
Hulay is a small town on the coast of the sea. It is a port that ships coming from Yemen and sailing up from Qulzum make for. Goods are brought to it and carried from it, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.138}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Hali, with a fatha and a sukun, on the pattern of zaby, and then quotes 'Umara al-Yamani that Hali is a city in Yemen on the sea shore, one day from al-Sirrayn and eight days from Mecca. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.297} The figures here are 'Umara's, not Yaqut's. The sentence "it is the Hilya mentioned before" follows, with no mark of whether it is 'Umara's or Yaqut's. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.297} He gives a verse by an unnamed Bedouin on the lote tree of Hali. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.297}

In the entry on Hilya just before, he says it is a lion haunt in the Yemen region and lists views on its place without choosing: a wadi between A'yar and 'Ulayb that empties into al-Sirrayn, a place in Yemen, a place in the districts of al-Ta'if; al-Zamakhshari has a wadi of Tihama whose upper part is Hudhayl's and lower part Kinana's; he also names a fortress of that name among the fortresses of Ta'izz in Mount Sabir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.297} From Abu al-Mundhir he gives the account that Qasr ibn 'Abqar settled the mountains of Hilya and Aslam after Bajila and Khath'am had gone up to the Sarat mountains and drove out their earlier people, and he cites a poem of Suwayd ibn Jud'a on it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.297} The name "Hali ibn Ya'qub" does not occur anywhere in the book; the whole text was searched with vowel marks removed and ya' and alif maqsura merged.

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi, has a small city on the coast, a port which ships coming from Yemen and sailing up from Qulzum make for, with goods brought in and carried out. Yaqut, quoting 'Umara al-Yamani, agrees that it is a city on the shore of the sea, but puts it in Yemen, one day from al-Sirrayn and eight from Mecca, and says nothing of the port, the ships or Qulzum. The museum's al-Sirin record, from al-Idrisi, has it about five days from Hulay, against 'Umara's one day, and the museum does not settle the difference. The record's point (18.6667, 41.35) is marked as a locality, 129.7 km from the museum's al-Sirin point and 345.3 km from its Mecca point; these are straight-line figures and are not set against the days. The record's coordinate note names "Hulay ibn Ya'qub", which is not in Yaqut's text. The museum does not follow the identification with Hilya, because Yaqut's Hilya entry gathers several places.
