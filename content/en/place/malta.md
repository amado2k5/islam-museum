## Summary
Malta is a large island in the western Mediterranean east of Sicily; al-Idrisi notes it has a safe harbour opening to the east and contains a town. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.588}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Malta as rich in pasture, sheep, fruit and abundant honey, and says that between it and the nearest mainland point of Sicily, a place called Akranta, lay eighty miles, and that beyond this Malta to east and west there is only the island of Crete. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.588} He lists it among the islands of that part of the sea together with Qawsara (Pantelleria region), Kumuna and Namusha and other islands near the coast of Ifriqiya. As for the island of Linbadhusha, between it and the nearest mainland of Ifriqiya, at Qabudiyya, lay two crossings; it had a safe harbour sheltered from every wind and able to hold great fleets, yet it had no fruit or animals at all, and to its north lay a small island called the Island of the Book, five miles away. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.588}
