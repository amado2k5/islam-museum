## Overview
al-Sarir: a land in the high Caucasus, whose king was titled "Lord of the Throne". Marwan ibn Muhammad entered it and took its fortresses, and then its king submitted to him and obeyed him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.292-293}

## The agreement
The king made peace with him for a thousand heads each year: five hundred boys and five hundred girls, with black hair and eyebrows and long eyelashes. He also made peace with him for a hundred thousand measures to be poured into the granary of the gate, and a pledge was taken from him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.292-293}

The place is connected with [[event/marwan-caucasus-expedition|the expedition of Marwan to the Caucasus]], which belongs to the Umayyad period. An additional source is needed for the date.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes al-Sarir as a wide kingdom between the Alans and Bab al-Abwab, reached by only two roads, one into the land of the Khazars and one into Armenia, and counting eighteen thousand villages in the mountains. He cites al-Istakhri that the name belongs to the kingdom, not to a city, and that its people are Christians. He reports a saying that the name comes from a golden throne once belonging to a Persian king, carried off when that dynasty fell, and that the kingship stayed with its holders to his own day. He also notes a truce between its province and the city of Samandar, about two farsakhs away, and a truce with the Muslims, each side wary of the other. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.219}
