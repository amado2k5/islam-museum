## Summary
Ghazza was a town small in size in the lands of the Maghrib, with a famous market held on a known day, a bath, fine dwellings and farms. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.251}

## Details
On the road from Tahart to Tanas, al-Idrisi says that from the city of Yillal a stage leads to the city of Ghazza; Yillal has springs and abundant waters, fruits and crops, its lands good for farming and its crops flourishing {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.251}. Then comes the city of Ghazza, a city small in size with a famous market having a known day; it has a bath, fine dwellings and farms {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.251}. From it to the city of Suq Ibrahim is a stage; it is of the size of Ghazza and its site is on the bank of the river Shalif {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.251}. From Suq Ibrahim to Baja is a stage; it is a fine small city with a district where fig trees are very many, and there dried fig slices are made in the manner of bricks, named accordingly and carried from it to many regions {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.251}. Ghazza thus appears in his scheme as a small town on the central Maghrib road between Yillal and Suq Ibrahim, known for its market day, its bath and its farms, on the traveller's way from Tahart to the sea coast at Tanas.
