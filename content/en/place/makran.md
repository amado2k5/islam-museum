## Overview
Makran is a coastal land between Fars and Sind. Al-Baladhuri mentions that Sinan ibn Salama ibn al-Muhabbaq al-Hudhali conquered it by force and made it a garrison city (misr). He quotes Ibn al-Kalbi that its conqueror was Hakim ibn Jabala. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Makran is mentioned in a poem by al-A'sha of Hamadan, which al-Baladhuri quotes. An additional source is needed to identify the pages where the citation appears.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut treats the name under the entry Mukran, with a damma on the m, and says it most often occurs in Arab verse with a doubled k and that its origin is foreign; he adds a possible Arabic derivation, and reports Hamza as saying that Mah Kirman was shortened to Makran and that the name is also used for the sea coast. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.179}

On the conquest he quotes two lines by al-Hakam ibn Amr al-Taghlibi, who, he says, took it in the days of Umar. He reports from Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir that Ziyad ibn Abi Sufyan, under Mu'awiya, appointed Sinan ibn Salama ibn al-Muhabbiq al-Hudhali, who was the first to make troops swear on the divorce of their wives not to flee; Sinan came to the frontier, took Makran by force, made it a garrison town and stayed there. From Ibn al-Kalbi he reports that the conqueror was Hakim ibn Jabala al-Abdi, and that Ziyad then put Rashid ibn Amr al-Judaydi al-Azdi over the frontier, who raided Qiqan and then Sind, where he was killed, after which Sinan ibn Salama took charge for two years. He gives verses of A'sha Hamdan about Makran. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.179}

He relates that Uthman ibn Affan told Abdallah ibn Amir to send a man to the frontier of Sind, and that Hakim ibn Jabala, sent, returned and described the country to Uthman as one whose water is scant, whose dates are poor and whose robbers are bold, where a small army is lost and a large one goes hungry; so nobody raided it in Uthman's reign, and it was first raided in the days of Ali ibn Abi Talib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.180}

He reports that the biographers say it was named after Makran ibn Farik ibn Sam ibn Nuh, brother of Kirman, who settled there when the tongues were confused at Babil. He describes it as a wide province of towns and villages, the source of faniz (a sugar sweet) sent to all countries, the best being that of Maskan, bounded by Kirman to the west, Sijistan to the north, the sea to the south and India to the east. He quotes al-Istakhri that it is broad, mostly desert and given to hardship and dearth, that around the year 340 it was held by a man called Isa ibn Ma'dan at the city of Kiz, its port, that its largest town was al-Qayrabun, and that its length from al-Tiz to Qusdar was about twelve stages. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.180}
