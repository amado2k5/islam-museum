## Overview
Sayhan is a river of Basra. In al-Baladhuri's list of Basra's canals and estates, he records that it belonged to the Barmakids, and that they were the ones who named it Sayhan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 506}

The governors of Basra granted land and dug canals, and canals were often named after their owners, as the Canal of Muqatil and the Canal of Umayran show. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 506}

Al-Baladhuri also mentions the Canal of the Amir in Basra, which al-Mansur dug and then gave to his son Ja'far; it was first called the Canal of the Commander of the Faithful. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 505}

Al-Idrisi describes another river of the same name in the lands of Rum, near Adhana. Its source lies in those lands, and in size it is smaller than the Jayhan, with a long and wonderfully built bridge. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 647}

From Adhana to al-Massisa is one stage, and both towns stand on this river. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 647}

From Adhana northward to Tarsus is one stage; Tarsus is a large city with two walls of stone. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 647}
