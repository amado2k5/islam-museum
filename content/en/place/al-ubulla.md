## Overview
al-Ubulla: a town near Basra. al-Baladhuri reports that its people gathered against Suwayd ibn Qutba, and Khalid ibn al-Walid deceived them by withdrawing by day and returning by night. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.338}

They were broken, a number of them were killed, and a group was drowned in the Tigris of Basra. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.338}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes al-Ubulla as a town on the bank of the Tigris, in the great Basra district, at the bend of the creek that runs into the city of Basra. It is older than Basra, which was founded as a garrison city in the days of Umar ibn al-Khattab, when al-Ubulla already had a town with Persian guard posts and a commander. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.77}

Yaqut quotes Khalid ibn Safwan, who said he had seen no land like al-Ubulla for the length of its distances, no place more nourishing for a drop of water, no easier road for a mount, no better profit for a merchant, and no place more concealing for one who wants cover. He also quotes al-Asma'i that the gardens of the world are three: the Ghuta of Damascus, the river of Balkh, and the river of al-Ubulla. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.77}

Yaqut notes that the canal of al-Ubulla, which runs toward Basra, was dug by Ziyad. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.77}

Yaqut sets out the grammarians' disagreement over the word's pattern, whether it is fa'lala or af'ala. He also gives an account from al-Asma'i of the name's origin: a tavern keeper called Hawb in the Nabatean period, whose name the Persians rendered in a form the Arabs then turned into al-Ubulla. Yaqut presents it as a linguistic report and does not vouch for it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.76}

Several transmitters of knowledge are named after al-Ubulla: Shayban ibn Farrukh al-Ubulli; Hafs ibn Umar ibn Isma'il al-Ubulli, who narrated from al-Thawri, Mis'ar ibn Kidam and Malik ibn Anas; and his son Isma'il ibn Hafs. Yaqut also names Kathir ibn Sulaym, Abu Hashim al-Ubulli, and records that he fabricated hadith on Anas, so that his narrations may not be transmitted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.78}
