## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut explains the al-Nu'man of the name Ma'arrat al-Nu'man first as the Companion al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, who passed through, lost a son there, buried him and stayed by the grave, so that the town took his name. He adds that beside the wall on the town side there is a tomb said to be that of Yusha ibn Nun, though he holds that Yusha lies in the land of Nablus, and that the town also has the tomb of the Companion Abdallah ibn Ammar ibn Yasir, which al-Baladhuri mentions in Futuh al-Buldan. Yaqut judges this reason weak, no basis for naming a city, and thinks it was named instead for al-Nu'man called al-Sati', of the Tanukh line of Quda'a. He describes it as a large, old and famous city in the Homs district between Aleppo and Hama, drinking from wells and rich in olives and figs. He names Abu al-Ala Ahmad ibn Abdallah ibn Sulayman al-Ma'arri as its son and quotes two verses of his, and also the Hanafi judge Abu al-Qasim al-Hasan ibn Abdallah al-Tanukhi al-Ma'arri, born in Rabi' I 349 AH, who taught, went on pilgrimage in 419 AH, died at Wadi Marr in Dhu al-Qa'da of that year, and was carried to Medina and buried in al-Baqi; Yaqut says he left works, testaments and poems. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.156}
