## Overview
Barbastro: the capital of a district of the land of Britain, near Saragossa, according to al-Maqqari. The army of the Ardumaniyyin overcame it in the year 456 AH, after a siege of forty days and the collapse of its water channel. al-Maqqari gives, on the authority of Ibn Hayyan, a description of the killing and captivity that took place in it, and his saying that the like of it had not been witnessed by the Muslims. Then Ahmad al-Muqtadir ibn Hud took it back in Jumada al-Ula in the year 457 AH. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p.449-454}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Barbashtar", with a damma on the second ba, a sukun on the shin and a fatha on the ta, and calls it a great city in the east of al-Andalus, of the districts (a'mal) of Barbataniya. He says that it passed to the Rum at the start of the year 452 AH, that seven thousand chosen virgins were sent from it among the gifts to the lord of Constantinople, and that the Muslims took it back under the rule of Ahmad ibn Sulayman ibn Hud in the year 457, five years later, taking ten thousand women among their spoil, after which it returned to the Franks. He says it has many fortresses and names three: Hisn al-Qasr, Hisn al-Baka and Hisn Qasr Minuqish. He gives two men with the nisba al-Barbashtari: Abu al-Qasim Khalaf ibn Yusuf al-Muqri, a man of the Qur'an and hadith who died in Ramadan 451, and Abu Amr Yusuf ibn Umar al-Tujibi al-Thaghri, who travelled, heard hadith in Egypt, lived in Alexandria and taught there, on the authority of al-Silafi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.370-371} Elsewhere he says that Hisn al-Baka is of the districts of Barbashtar and was in Frankish hands in his day. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.328}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut agrees with the record that Barbastro was a great city of eastern al-Andalus that fell and was retaken, but the two accounts differ on dates. Yaqut places the fall at the start of 452 AH and the recovery five years later, in 457 (the printed text reads only "57" for the recovery, and the hundreds follow from his "five years after"); al-Maqqari, reporting Ibn Hayyan, in the record puts the fall in 456 and the recovery in 457, less than a year apart. The museum keeps both and does not choose. It reads Yaqut's "Ahmad ibn Sulayman ibn Hud" as the [[person/al-muqtadir-ibn-hud|Ahmad al-Muqtadir ibn Hud]] of the record, a match of name and period and not something Yaqut says. Yaqut ends with the city going back to the Franks, where the record stops at the recovery. He also has a separate entry for Barbataniya, a great city whose district adjoins that of Lerida, which is the name he uses for Barbashtar's province; the museum does not equate the two. Yaqut gives no coordinates, so nothing in him disputes the point on the record.
