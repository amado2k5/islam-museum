## Overview
Balkh: the city of Tukharistan in Khurasan. al-Ahnaf ibn Qays marched to it, and its people made peace with him for four hundred thousand, or it is said seven hundred thousand; the latter is more firmly established in al-Baladhuri. He appointed over it Usayd ibn al-Mutashammis, or it is said his nephew. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] calls Balkh a famous city of Khurasan, among the most splendid, renowned, prosperous and productive of its cities, whose harvest was carried to all Khurasan and to Khwarazm. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.667} He gives differing coordinates: the "Malhama" attributed to Ptolemy puts it in the fifth clime, and Abu Awn's tables also in the fifth with a latitude of 38°40', although in his own chapter on the climes he had placed it in the fourth. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.667}

He reports two accounts of its founding: that it was first built by King "Uhrasf" (so in the copy read; Luhrasp is meant) when his commander Nebuchadnezzar destroyed Jerusalem, or that Alexander built it and it was anciently called Alexandria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.667} It lay twelve parasangs from Tirmidh; the Oxus was called "the river of Balkh", about ten parasangs away. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.667} It was taken by al-Ahnaf ibn Qays for Abd Allah ibn Amir ibn Kurayz in the days of Uthman ibn Affan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.667}

Among its people he names the hadith master al-Hasan ibn Shuja' al-Balkhi, whom, Yaqut reports, [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad ibn Hanbal]] counted with al-Bukhari, Abu Zur'a al-Razi and Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Samarqandi among the hadith masters of Khurasan, calling him "the one who gathers the most chapters"; he died in mid-Shawwal 244 AH aged forty-nine. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.668}
