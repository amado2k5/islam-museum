## Summary
A water of Khuza'a in lower [[place/mecca|Mecca]]; at it [[group/banu-al-dil|Banu al-Dil]] of [[group/banu-bakr|Banu Bakr]] ambushed Khuza'a by night during the Hudaybiyya truce — the [[event/khuzaa-bakr-clash-al-watir|breach]] upon which the conquest of Mecca followed {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31-32}.

## The location
Ibn Ishaq describes it as "at a water called al-Watir in lower Mecca" — in the lower part of Mecca's valley {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31}. Its exact spot is not fixed in the source {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/31}.

## For young visitors
A water-well in lower Mecca near which safe people were attacked by night in truce-time — and from that night the road to the conquest began.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes al-Watir as a water below Mecca belonging to Khuza'a, which some later writers spelled with a final nun. He cites verses by Amr ibn Salim al-Khuza'i describing the attack on his people there, paraphrased here and not quoted. He dates the clash between Kinana and Khuza'a to the seventh year after the Hijra, and reports that Kinana acted treacherously against Khuza'a with Quraysh's support, which he gives as the cause of the broken truce and the opening of Mecca. He places the Watir between Arafat and Adam. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 pp.360-361}
