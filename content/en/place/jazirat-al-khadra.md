## Overview
Al-Jazira al-Khadra is an island joined to the mainland, facing the city of [[place/ceuta|Ceuta]] across the strait. Al-Idrisi says that the sea between it and Ceuta is eight miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.528}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut defines al-Jazira al-Khadra as a famous city of al-Andalus, with Ceuta facing it on the Berber shore, its district adjoining that of Sidona, east of Sidona and south of Cordoba. He says the sea washes against its wall, yet it is not surrounded by water like an island but is joined to the mainland of al-Andalus, as several who had seen it told him. He adds that its anchorage is among the best for crossing and the nearest to the open sea, eighteen miles from it, and that it lies fifty-five farsakhs from Cordoba. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.136} The relational adjective is {jaziri}, distinguished from {jazari} for an earlier place of similar name; he names Abu Zayd Abd Allah ibn Umar al-Tamimi al-Jaziri, who died in 365. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.136} He then notes that al-Jazira al-Khadra is also the name of a large island in the land of the Zanj in the Indian Ocean, which is a different place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.136}
