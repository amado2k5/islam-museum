## Summary
The Hirmand (Helmand) is, in al-Idrisi's account, the river of Sijistan, emptying into the lake near Farah and crossing that country toward Zaranj and Kas. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.446}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says Farah is a large village near the lake into which the Hirmand river, the river of Sijistan, falls, and that the branch leaving the Hirmand toward Nishak never reaches this lake. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.446} Between Kas and Zaranj lay ninety miles; Kas stood on the desert's edge, the Hirmand crossed over to it, and it had crops and produce. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.458} The town of Tal, the town of the Dawar country, and the town of Darghash stand on the two banks of the Hirmand. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.467} On the road from Sijistan to Herat the surplus waters of the Hirmand are crossed at the bridge of Bishtar, and the road then reaches the fortress of Dazza on the shore of the lake into which the Hirmand pours. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.458}
