## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Mayurqa is an island in eastern al-Andalus that was the seat of the rule of Mujahid al-Amiri, and jurists and hadith scholars who travelled east are attributed to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.246}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling, a fatha on the mim and a damma on the ya, a silent waw and ra, which he says brings two silent letters together, and a qaf at the end. He describes an island in eastern al-Andalus, near which lies another called Manurqa, spelled with a nun, and says it was the seat of the rule of Mujahid al-Amiri. The rest of the entry concerns those attributed to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.246}

## People
Yaqut names Abu al-Hajjaj Yusuf ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Lakhmi al-Mayurqi, a Maliki jurist who went to Baghdad, studied law there for a time, reached Damascus in 505, and returned to Alexandria where he taught. He names Abu Ali al-Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Ghafiqi al-Mayurqi, a Maliki jurist known as Ibn al-Unsuri, born in Mayurqa in 449, who heard in his own country and in Jerusalem, Mecca, Baghdad and Damascus, and went home in Dhu al-Hijja 471. And he names the hafiz Abu Amir Muhammad ibn Sa'dun al-Qurashi al-Abdari al-Mayurqi, who followed the school of Dawud ibn Ali al-Zahiri, entered Damascus and then settled in Baghdad. Yaqut quotes the hafiz Abu al-Qasim's account of him, which includes severe judgements on him and his views; these are not part of this place page. The years are Hijri, as Yaqut gives them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.246}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Mallorca an island in the Mediterranean, the island of Majorca. That link is the museum's: Yaqut places the island in eastern al-Andalus near Manurqa, and that Manurqa is Minorca is the museum's reading, not Yaqut's text.
