## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam puts the age of Islam in China at more than thirteen centuries and the number of Chinese Muslims today above thirty million. Traders and wandering teachers were the first to arrive. As China's reach grew into Central Asia, Muslim peoples such as the Uighurs were brought into the state, and so the Muslims of China are both the Hui, the Chinese Muslims, and members of other Muslim ethnic groups. The Dictionary states that Muslims have held high imperial office and have also stood against imperial expansion. It says that Communist rule brought them suppression, though they won backing from other Muslim countries as an Islamic resurgence spread internationally toward the close of the 1900s. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e427}
