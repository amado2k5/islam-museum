## Overview
The Arabian Peninsula. Al-Hamdani, in the introduction to "The Description of the Arabian Peninsula", sets its bounds: its west is the gulf of Aqaba and what the coasts drive to the Red Sea and Fustat in Egypt; its east is Oman to Bahrain, Kazima and Basra; its head is the Hijaz, Najd and al-Arud; and its south is [[place/aden|Aden]]. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab pp. 4-5}

Al-Hamdani says it was named so because the Arabic tongue is common throughout it, though with differences. He estimated its length at one thousand five hundred and thirty-three miles, that is seventy-six and a third stages. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab pp. 4-5}

Among its old kingdoms and great monuments he counted Namat, Ghumdan, Hakar, Raydan, Baynun, Qayman, the Pool of the Pillars, and Iram of the Pillars. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.6}

## Its limits according to al-Bakri
In the introduction to the [[work/mujam-ma-istaajam|Mu'jam ma sta'jam]], [[person/al-bakri-abu-ubayd|Abu Ubayd al-Bakri]] gives the scholars' definitions of the Arabian peninsula: from Malik ibn Anas, that it is Medina, Mecca, al-Yamama and Yemen; from al-Mughira ibn Abd al-Rahman, Mecca, Medina, Yemen and its villages; and from al-Asma'i, what the kings of Persia never reached, from the far end of Aden Abyan to the edges of Syria in length and from Jidda to the farmland of Iraq in breadth. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.17} From al-Sha'bi, that it lies between Qadisiyyat al-Kufa and Hadramawt; and from Abu Ubayd, between Hafar Abi Musa and the far end of Yemen in length, and between the sands of Yabrin and the edge of al-Samawa in breadth. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.18} He says it is called an "island" because the Persian Gulf, the Abyssinian Sea, the Euphrates and the Tigris surround it, so that it is the land and origin of the Arabs, and traces the waters round it from the Euphrates to Basra and Abbadan, then along the coast to al-Qatif, Hajar, Oman, al-Shihr and Aden, and on to Jidda, al-Jar, Ayla and al-Qulzum of Egypt. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p.18}
