## Summary
Batash was a small but flourishing town with extensive districts and estates, standing on one of the two rivers on which Aqra also stands, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.884}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the land of Bu'amiyya issue two rivers running westward and tending south, which pour into the river Danu near Buzana; they rise in a mountain called Balwak forming the barrier between the lands of Bu'amiyya and Baluniyya {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.884}. On these two rivers stand the towns of Aqra and Batash; Batash is a small city but prosperous, possessing many districts and country estates {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.884}. From Aqra to the city of Batash is forty miles between east and north {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.884}. Batash thus appeared in his scheme among the small inhabited towns of the land of Bu'amiyya, sharing with Aqra the banks of the two mountain rivers that reach the river Danu.
