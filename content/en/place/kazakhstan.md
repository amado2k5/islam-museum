## Summary
A Central Asian republic that The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes as 47 percent Muslim. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1247}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam presents Kazakhstan as a Central Asian republic with China, Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, the Caspian Sea and Russia on its borders. Russians (34.7 percent) and Kazakhs (46 percent) are the main ethnic groups, alongside minorities of Ukrainians, Germans, Uzbeks and Tatars. By religion it counts 47 percent of the population as Muslim, 44 percent as Russian Orthodox and 9 percent as other. Since independence from the Soviet Union in 1991, it says, the Naqshbandi and similar Sufi orders have grown. According to the Dictionary, Sunni Islam entered the Kazakh territories under Catherine the Great, who aimed to pacify the pastoral nomads. Most religious activity was severely repressed under Soviet rule, though rural practices were little affected. Numerous mosques and religious schools were opened after independence, with money coming in part from local believers, Turks and Saudis. The 1993 constitution, the Dictionary adds, expressly protects freedom to worship and bars the adoption of a state religion, and no Islamic holy day is a state holiday. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1247}
