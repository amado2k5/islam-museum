## Summary
Buzana was a medium-sized, populous and civilised city on the river Danu, the last town of the land of Qarantara in al-Idrisi's account, lying between Balaghrata and Badhwara. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.883}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Balaghrata to the city of Buzana on the river Danu is thirty-five miles; it is a medium-sized, populous and civilised city with facilities and continuous building {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.883}. From Buzana to the city of Badhwara is sixty miles, Badhwara also standing on the Danu, and between Buzana and Badhwara the river Darwa flows into the Danu; Buzana is the last of the land of Qarantara {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.883}. The author counts Buzana, Badhwara, Balaghrata, Nitram, Siq Law and Buza among the chief towns of the land of Qarantara, which lies between the river Danu and the wadi Dharwa, a land neighbouring the country of the Venetians with whom there were at times severe wars {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878}. Buzana thus appears in his description as a river town on the edge of Qarantara, thickly built and well provided, on the road from Balaghrata in the north toward Badhwara and the lands beyond.
