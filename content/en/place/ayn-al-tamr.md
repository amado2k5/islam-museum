## Overview
Ayn al-Tamr: a place in Iraq, whose reports are found in two sources.

## In Ibn Ishaq
Umayr ibn Ri'ab al-Sahmi was killed there with Khalid ibn al-Walid, on his return from al-Yamama in the caliphate of Abu Bakr. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## In al-Baladhuri
It is a fortress with a great garrison of the Persians. Khalid besieged it until its people asked for safe-conduct, but he refused, and he conquered it by force. He took captive a group from its church, among them the fathers and clients of Ibn Sirin, Ibn Ishaq, Musa ibn Nusayr and the family of Abu Farwa. Bashir ibn Sa'd al-Ansari and Umayr ibn Ri'ab were buried in it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Ayn al-Tamr as a town near al-Anbar, west of Kufa, close to a place called Shafatha; from the two of them qasb dates and ordinary dates are carried to every other land, and dates are very plentiful there. It lies on the edge of the desert and is ancient. The Muslims took it in the days of Abu Bakr at the hand of Khalid ibn al-Walid in the year 12 of the Hijra, by force, so that its men were killed and its women taken captive. Among the captives he names the mother of Muhammad ibn Sirin and Humran ibn Aban, the client of Uthman ibn Affan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.176}

He quotes verses by Ubayd Allah ibn al-Hurr al-Ju'fi, composed on a clash between him and the followers of Mus'ab, in which the poet says that he took a noble man captive at Ayn al-Tamr. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.176} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.177}

In another entry he reports that in a church in a village of Ayn al-Tamr called al-Naqira they found boys learning to write, among them Humran, client of Uthman. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.301}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Ayn al-Tamr; what the record takes from Ibn Ishaq and al-Baladhuri is consistent with Yaqut's report of Khalid's conquest. Yaqut does not mention here the killing of Umayr ibn Ri'ab al-Sahmi, which is Ibn Ishaq's report in the record. The record's coordinates lie in Karbala governorate, which agrees with Yaqut's placing it west of Kufa near al-Anbar on the edge of the desert.
