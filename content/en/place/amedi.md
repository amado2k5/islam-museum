## Overview
Al-Imadiyya is a strong fortress north of Mosul as Yaqut al-Hamawi describes it, with neighbouring places of the Hakkariyya country in his account; the museum reads it as the town of Amedi in Iraqi Kurdistan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.149}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "al-Imadiyya" and says it is a strong, firm and great fortress in the north of Mosul and of its districts, built by Imad al-Din Zangi ibn Aq Sunqur in the year 537 AH. Before that it was a fortress of the Kurds, who, because of its size, ruined it; Zangi rebuilt it and named it after himself, attributing it to him, and the name of the first fortress was Ashib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.149} In his entry "Ashib" he gives a different account: it was one of the grandest fortresses of the Hakkariyya in the land of Mosul, which Zangi ibn Aq Sunqur ruined, building al-Imadiyya near it in its place, so that it was named after him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.54} In two other entries he names places around it: Dayr Kum is near it, in the Hakkariyya country of the districts of Mosul, and the fortress of Harur, of the works of the Hakkariyya, lies three miles from it and has a mine of mumiya and a mine of iron. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.530} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.403}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's two entries agree that Zangi built al-Imadiyya, named it after himself, and that the older fortress was Ashib, but they differ on who ruined the old one: the first blames the Kurds, for its size, and says Zangi restored it; the second blames Zangi and says he built al-Imadiyya "near it" and not on the very site. The museum reads Imad al-Din Zangi here as the [[person/imad-al-din-zangi|Imad al-Din Zangi]] of its own record, a match of name and description. Yaqut gives no coordinates and names no modern town. The link to Amedi in Iraqi Kurdistan rests on the Arabic name in the record's label, on the Wikidata identifier Q460736, and on his placing it north of Mosul with neighbours in the Hakkariyya country. The record's coordinates (37.0919, 43.487) come from Wikidata. The record's description as a "town" comes from Wikidata, not from Yaqut.
