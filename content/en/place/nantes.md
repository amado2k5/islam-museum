## Overview
Nantes stands on the shore of the sea at the bottom of a gulf that runs in from it, and it is the first of the lands of Britain (Brittany). It is a large, populous city with farmland, voyages, ships and traffic in and out, strongly built and pleasant, and from it the sea bends to the north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 856}

## Distances
From the city of Belfir on the sea to Nantes is seventy miles. From Nantes to the city of Shant Mijal, the last of the lands of Britain, is a hundred and ten miles by land and eight hundred and thirty miles by sea, because the sea bends and hollows there so much that the land is like a purse, narrow at the mouth and wide in the middle. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 856}

## By sea
For a voyage by sea one goes from Nantes to Bats, fifty miles along the hollow of the gulf; Bats is a fine, populous city with shipbuilding, sailing and anchoring, many markets and trade. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 857} Nantes is the first name listed among the lands of Britain in this part of the sixth clime. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 855}
