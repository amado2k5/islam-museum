## Overview
Rafah is a sound town on the road from [[place/gaza|Gaza]] to al-Arish. It lies one stage from Gaza, al-Arish is another stage beyond it, and from al-Arish to al-Warada is a third stage, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 357}

Al-Idrisi describes Gaza as the last city of Palestine on the side facing al-Jifar and the road to Egypt. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 356}

Al-Idrisi also describes al-Arish, the town beyond Rafah, as a city that once had two congregational mosques. Most of its land is sand, it has fruit and fruit trees, and it lies close to the sea. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 357}

In his description of the boundaries of Palestine, al-Idrisi says that its length, from Rafah to Lajjun, is about four days, and that its width, from Jaffa to Riha, is two days' journey. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 354}

In his description of Syria, al-Idrisi says that its length begins at Malatya and runs to Rafah, and that the road from Ramla to Rafah takes two days. The whole route, he states, comes to thirty-five stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 378}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Rafah, with a fatha on the ra' and the fa' and an undotted ha, and describes it as a station on the road to Egypt after al-Darum, the first of the sand, and now in ruins; he adds that dogs are ascribed to it. He says that between "it" and Ascalon there are two days for one going to Egypt, and the pronoun there could mean Rafah or al-Darum. He then quotes al-Muhallabi that Rafah is a flourishing city with a market, a congregational mosque, a minbar and inns, whose people are from Lakhm and Judham and raid and steal travellers' goods, with a governor of the ma'una (a term left untranslated here) and a number of soldiers. The passage goes on to the distance between Rafah and Gaza, a line of sycamores and the end of the sand of al-Jifar, and the entry does not show where al-Muhallabi's words end. In the text the museum holds, the Rafah-to-Gaza figure reads eighteen, then a page break, then "days"; the unit looks doubtful and the museum does not use the figure. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.54-55} In the entry on 'Arish he quotes al-Muhallabi that from al-'Arish it is six miles to the two wells of Abu Ishaq, six to al-Shajaratayn (the first of the districts of Syria), six to al-Barmakiyya and then six to Rafah, which makes twenty-four miles named between al-'Arish and Rafah. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.114} In the entry on al-Jifar he makes Rafah the first of the Jifar on the Syrian side and counts it among inhabited places on the road to Egypt where traders settle (Rafah, al-Qas, al-Za'qa, al-'Arish, al-Warrada and Qatya), then quotes al-Muhallabi that the chief towns of the Jifar are al-'Arish, Rafah and al-Warrada; this does not agree with his "in ruins now" in the Rafah entry, and he does not reconcile the two in either entry. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.145}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi, is a sound town one stage from Gaza on the road to al-Arish. Yaqut agrees that it lies on the road to Egypt at the start of the sand, but says in the Rafah entry that it is ruined, while al-Muhallabi, as he quotes him, describes a flourishing city with a market and a mosque, and the entry on al-Jifar counts it among inhabited places; his words describe different conditions, and the museum records both without choosing. The record's point is 31.9 km from the museum's Gaza point and 46.1 km from its al-Arish point; Yaqut gives no reliable distance to Gaza, and the twenty-four miles that he quotes from al-Muhallabi between al-'Arish and Rafah are not converted here to kilometres, because the length of the mile is not fixed in the text.
