## Overview
Caesarea is a coastal city in the district (jund) of Palestine, which according to the source belongs administratively to [[place/jerusalem|Jerusalem]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} See [[event/conquest-of-caesarea|The conquest of Caesarea]].

## The conquest
Al-Baladhuri relates that [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] was the first to besiege it, in the year 13 AH, and that [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan]] carried out its conquest in Shawwal of the year 19 AH, though some say the beginning of the year 20. This came after a siege of about seven years and the guidance of Yusuf the Jew to the tunnel (sarb). {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

He relays from al-Waqidi large figures for those found in it, the number of its markets, and the guard of its wall. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} These are figures each of which needs an additional source.

## After the conquest
The source mentions that the Byzantines raided it in the days of Ibn al-Zubayr, ravaged it and demolished its mosque. When matters were settled for [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]], he restored it, rebuilt its mosque and filled it with men. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Al-Muqaddasi's description
Al-Muqaddasi said around the year 375 AH that there is no city on the Roman Sea more glorious or more abundant in goods than it. It has an impregnable fortress, and around it a thriving suburb. Its people drink from wells and cisterns. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p.142}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut defines Qaysariyya as a town on the coast of the Syrian sea, counted in the district of Filastin and three days from Tiberias; in the past it was one of the chief mother cities, broad, pleasant and rich in goods and people, but in his day it resembled villages more than a city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.421} He then lists a second, separate Qaysariyya in the lands of Rum, the seat of the Seljuk rulers, which is not the Palestinian town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.421} Quoting the book on Damascus, on the authority of Yazid ibn Samura, he says Mu'awiya besieged Qaysariyya for almost seven years and that its stipended Byzantine fighters numbered a hundred thousand, its Samaritans eighty thousand and its Jews a hundred thousand. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.421} He then relays that a man called Lantaq showed the Muslims a weak point and led them into a conduit wide enough for a camel with its litter to walk through; it was a Sunday, and the people, in the church, knew nothing until the takbir sounded at the church door. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.422} The news of the conquest was sent to Umar ibn Tamim ibn Warqa, the Khath'am chief, who climbed the minaret and proclaimed that Qaysariyya had been taken by force. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.422} Of the scholars attributed to Palestinian Qaysariyya he names Ibrahim ibn Abi Sufyan al-Qaysarani, who died in 278, Amr ibn Thawr al-Qaysarani, who died in 279, and the devout Fudayk ibn Sulayman Abu Isa al-Uqayli al-Qaysarani. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.422}
