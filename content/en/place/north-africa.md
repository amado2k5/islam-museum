## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
According to The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, Islam reached the Maghreb soon after Muhammad died. Arab Muslim armies carried Islamic government westward, from Egypt into Tunisia by 647 and as far as the Atlantic by 710. Most of the population converted fairly quickly, but revolts against central control were many, and by the tenth century the Muslim northwest of Africa was developing on its own. The al-Murabitun and al-Muwahhidun, whose states controlled most of the region, established significant models during the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. After that, the Dictionary says, most of the region's Muslims were Sunnis who followed the Maliki school of jurisprudence, and pious leaders and Sufi teachers had great influence. In modern times, it adds, Islamic identity supported nationalism, and the Islamic revival of the late twentieth century showed itself in movements including Algeria's Islamic Salvation Front. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1771}
