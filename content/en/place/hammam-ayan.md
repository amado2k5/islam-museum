## Overview
The Bath of Ayan is a bath in Kufa, named after Ayan, a client of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. It is the one that al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf sent to Abd Allah ibn al-Jarud al-Abdi when he rebelled. The bath had belonged before to a worshipper called Jabir, and Ayan bought it from his heirs. According to al-Baladhuri, Uthman granted Khalid ibn Arfata land beside the bath. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.382} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.395-396}

The place appears in [[place/kufa|Kufa]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Hammam A'yan, with a doubled mim, as a bath in Kufa, well known and mentioned in the reports, named for A'yan, the freedman of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas; the entry says no more. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.299} Among the entries that follow are Hammam Sa'd, a place on the pilgrim road at Kufa, and baths of Basra named for Balj, Fil and Minjab, which are other places. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.299}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Baladhuri, tells that al-Hajjaj sent A'yan to 'Abd Allah ibn al-Jarud al-'Abdi, that the bath had belonged before to Jabir and was bought from his heirs, and that 'Uthman granted Khalid ibn 'Urfuta land beside it. None of this is in Yaqut, whose entry confirms only the bath, Kufa and the naming for A'yan, freedman of Sa'd. That the bath's A'yan is the A'yan whom al-Hajjaj sent is the record's statement, not Yaqut's. The record's point is 6.8 km from the museum's Kufa point; Yaqut gives no position within Kufa.
