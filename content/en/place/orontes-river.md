## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan al-Asi is the river of Hama and Homs, known as al-Maymas, rising from the lake of Qudus and reaching the sea near Antioch. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.67} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.68}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name with an undotted sad, notes that the word is the opposite of obedient, and describes it as the river of Hama and Homs, known as al-Maymas, rising from the lake of Qudus and flowing into the sea near Antioch, where it is called al-Arind. He mentions an explanation of the name, that most rivers run toward the south while this one runs toward the north, and adds that this is not a regular rule. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.67} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.68}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls the Orontes a river in Western Asia, which does not conflict with Yaqut, who makes it the river of Hama and Homs with its mouth near Antioch. The name al-Arind that Yaqut gives for it near Antioch is his own wording and the museum adds nothing to it. Hama, Homs and Antioch have records, [[place/hama|Hama]], [[place/homs|Homs]] and [[place/antioch|Antioch]].
