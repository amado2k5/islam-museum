## Summary
The coast of Hajr was the first of the lands of Bahrayn on the coastal road from Oman to Basra, mentioned by al-Idrisi after the anchorage of al-Mafqud. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.163}

## Details
On the coastal route from Oman to Bahrayn, al-Idrisi says that from Julfar one comes to the anchorage of al-Sabkha, an anchorage with a gushing fresh spring, then to Shaqab and Bawar and the sea of Uways, hard to traverse; these places are called the sea of Qatar, and in this sea are several empty islands uninhabited, to which kinds of sea and land birds resort {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. He notes that the droppings the birds heap in those islands are loaded by ships when the sea is calm and carried to Basra and elsewhere, where they are sold for a great price and used in the planting of vines, palms, gardens and orchards {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.163}. No one dwells on the sea of Qatar nor does anyone resort to it; it is a fearsome place by land and sea. Thence one comes to the anchorage of al-Mafqud, a noble anchorage sheltered from many winds, with a copious fresh spring, then to the coast of Hajr, which is the first of the lands of Bahrayn; and from the coast of Hajr to Basra runs an uninhabited road along the shore {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.163}. The coast of Hajr is thus for him the threshold of the lands of Bahrayn on the empty coastal road from Oman to Basra.
