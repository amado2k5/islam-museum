## Overview
Sindan is a district on the coast of India. Al-Fadl ibn Mahan, a client of Banu Sama, conquered it and took control. There he built a congregational mosque and invoked the caliph al-Ma'mun in it, and sent him an elephant and his secretary. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.626}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri says al-Fadl ibn Mahan prolonged his stay there, then sailed with seventy barges to the Indian coast, killed many of its people, took a town, and returned to Sindan. By then a brother named Mahan ibn al-Fadl had seized the district and written to the caliph al-Mu'tasim. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.626}

The Indians who had sided with that brother turned against him, killed him and crucified him. Afterwards the Indians took Sindan for themselves, but they left its mosque to the Muslims, who gathered there and prayed for the caliph. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.626}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives Sindan with a fatha on the first letter and a final nun, and quotes Nasr that it is the capital of the land of India, then adds that he does not know what Nasr meant, since a capital in common usage is the foremost city of a district. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.265} He says Sindan is a city adjoining Sind, about ten stages from al-Daybul and al-Mansura, that it does not deserve to be called the capital of India, that it lies about half a farsakh from the sea and about fifteen stages from Saymur. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.266} He cites a line of al-Buhturi that mentions Sindan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.266}
