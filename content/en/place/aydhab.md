## Overview
Aydhab: a coast in the land of the Beja. There the supply ships came to it, which Muhammad al-Qummi carried from al-Qulzum in his campaign against the land of the Beja in the time of al-Mutawakkil, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.333}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Aydhab", with a fatha, a sukun, a dotted dhal and a ba at the end, and calls it a small town (bulayda) on the bank of the Qulzum sea, the landing place of the ships that come from Aden for Upper Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.171} In his account of the Qulzum sea he says that it curves in a half-circle to Aydhab and the land of the Buja and then joins the lands of the Habasha. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.344} Under Suakin he calls it a well-known town on the shore of the sea of al-Jar near Aydhab, where the ships of those coming from Jidda put in, whose people are black Christian Buja. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276} He names "al-Dayqa" as a halt ten farsakhs from Aydhab, from which Abu al-Hasan Tahir ibn al-Atiq al-Sakkak al-Dayqi is named. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.465} And he mentions al-Allaqi, a fortress in the Buja country south of Egypt with a gold mine, with open plains between it and Aswan and eight stages between it and Aydhab. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.145}

## The museum's identification
In Yaqut Aydhab is a small town and a harbour of his own day on the Qulzum sea; in al-Baladhuri, as the record has it, it is a coast in the Buja lands where the provision ships joined Muhammad al-Qummi's campaign in al-Mutawakkil's time. The two are one place by name, sea and neighbourhood, which is the museum's identification; Yaqut says nothing of al-Qummi's campaign. Yaqut gives no coordinates, so the record's point (22.33, 36.47), marked as an identified site, still needs checking against a gazetteer. The museum measured that its Suakin record lies about 370 km from the Aydhab point, so Yaqut's "near Aydhab" for Suakin is a loose description and the museum does not use it as a measure. The short entry for Aydhab itself carries no story and no history.
