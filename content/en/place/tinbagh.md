## Overview
Tinbagh is the chief city of the Taghazghaz, a group of Turks. Al-Idrisi also calls it the city of the Khaqan, and says that the ruler of the Taghazghaz, the Khaqan, lived there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 510}
It was a great city with a strong wall, on a large river flowing eastward, and al-Idrisi says it had twelve gates of iron. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 511}
He describes its people as zanadiqa, and separately notes that among the Taghazghaz Turks there was a group of Magians who worshipped fire. These are his descriptions, reported as his; the museum does not adopt them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 511}
From Tinbagh it is two months' journey to the upper Barsakhan, in the land bordering Farghana. Bakhwan, a city of the same region with a local ruler who was one of the Khaqan's men, lies twelve days away, between west and north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 511}
