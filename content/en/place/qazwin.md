## Overview
Qazwin is a city in the mountains of al-Daylam. Al-Baladhuri mentions that the Asawira residing opposite al-Daylam embraced Islam when the Muslims came upon them at Qazwin, in the same way as the Asawira of [[place/basra|Basra]] did. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.394}

An additional source is needed: the data does not give the date of the event.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes Qazwin as a famous city twenty-seven farsakhs from Rayy and twelve from Abhar, in the fourth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.342} Citing Ibn al-Faqih, he says it was first built by Shapur Dhu al-Aktaf, that its fortress was called Kashrin in Persian, and that between it and the Daylam lay a mountain where the kings of the earth kept a frontier guard of asawira against the Daylam. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.342} He reports that Uthman ibn Affan made al-Bara ibn Azib governor of Rayy in 24 AH; al-Bara conquered Abhar and then besieged Qazwin, offered its people the terms given to Abhar, and they accepted all except the jizya, but when told it was required they embraced Islam and stayed, so that their land became ushr land; he stationed five hundred Muslims there and granted them land, and they were called its tunna'. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.342} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} He says that when Sa'id ibn al-As became governor of Kufa after al-Walid ibn Uqba he raided the Daylam, came to Qazwin, made it a garrison town and a base for the men of Kufa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} Al-Hadi ordered a city built opposite it, known as Madinat Musa, and Harun al-Rashid entered the city, built its congregational mosque, endowed it, and eased its people's burden by fixing their tax at ten thousand dirhams a year. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} Al-Hajjaj sent his son Muhammad against the Daylam, who stayed in Qazwin and built a mosque known as Masjid al-Thawr. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} Yaqut notes that hadith scholars reported accounts in praise of Qazwin that the critical hafiz scholars do not hold sound, and that he left them out. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} He says countless people are named after it, among them al-Khalil ibn Abdallah ibn al-Khalil Abu Ya'la al-Qazwini, and Ibn Majah, Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Qazwini, the hafiz and author of the Sunan, who according to Ja'far ibn Idris died on a Monday, eight nights before the end of Ramadan 273 AH, and who said he was born in 209. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.344}
