## Summary
A place in the land of al-Sham where the army of Mu'ta halted for two nights, when news reached them of the size of Heraclius's forces. There they debated whether to ask for reinforcements, until Abd Allah ibn Rawaha encouraged them to press on.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name Ma'an with a fatha and a final nun, notes that the hadith scholars say it with a damma and that al-ma'an means a dwelling place, and from al-Azhari makes it a city at the edge of the desert of Syria facing the Hijaz in the region of al-Balqa'. He relates that the Prophet sent an army to Mu'ta with Zayd ibn Haritha, Ja'far ibn Abi Talib and 'Abd Allah ibn Rawaha, who marched until they reached Ma'an and stayed in it, intending to write to the Prophet about the armies gathering; it is said that some two hundred thousand of the Byzantines and the Arabs had assembled, and he records 'Abd Allah ibn Rawaha's words urging them forward, and quotes verses containing "they stayed two nights at Ma'an". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.153}

## The museum's identification
The record makes Ma'an a place in the land of Syria where the Mu'ta army halted two nights when the size of Heraclius's forces reached them, and where the debate over asking for reinforcements ran until 'Abd Allah ibn Rawaha urged them on. Yaqut's entry (vol 5 p.153) agrees on the place, the halt and the Ibn Rawaha story, makes it a city at the edge of the Syrian desert in the Balqa' region, adds the two nights in the quoted verse, and gives the size of the gathered army with "it is said", which the museum keeps as a reported figure. The record's point (30.1933, 35.7333) is 100.4 km from the museum's Mu'ta point. See [[place/mutah|Mu'ta]].
