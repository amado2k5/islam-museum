## Overview
Nicaea (Iznik) is an ancient city whose founder is not known. It lies on a fresh-water lake twelve miles long and is three miles from the sea. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 805}

## The lake and the fortress
Al-Idrisi says the lake is twelve miles long and seven miles wide. From the city a small gate leads to the lake, and when fear or sudden danger came upon the people, they brought the families out of the fortress into boats. The fortress of al-Abra is a large, strong fort near Nicaea, three miles away. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 805}

## Routes
From Nicaea, greens are carried to Constantinople, thirty miles away. From Nicaea to Nicomedia is four days. Nicomedia was an old and famous seat of the Roman kingdom before Constantinople was built; when Constantinople was built, the kingdom moved there, and the distance between Nicomedia and Constantinople is a hundred and sixty miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 805}

## Nearby towns
From Nicaea to Dhamiliya is four stages. Dhamiliya is a small, civilised town with all its resources and amenities. From there to Azla, a small town on the shore of the sea, is four days, and from Azla to Abdas is three days. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 805}
