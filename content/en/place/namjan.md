## Summary
Namjān is a small, civilised town ruled by a Turkish man, on the bank of a river called Sūqān, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.923}

## Details
Al-Idrisi places Namjān in the account of Outer Basjart, which he describes as populous lands, self-sufficient, with Qarawqiya, Namjān and Gharkhān among them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.922}

From Outer Basjart eastwards to Namjān is eight stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.923} He calls Namjān a small civilised town ruled by a man from the Turks, with the rule passing to his sons and grandsons, who are not displaced because of their good conduct and their kindness to the common people. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.923}

The town lies on the bank of a river called Sūqān. To its east is the mountain of Arjīqā, which has copper mines worked by more than a thousand men. Much of the ore is taken to the land of Khwārazm and the other lands of al-Shāsh and its neighbours. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.923}

Al-Idrisi does not give Namjān's population, farming or markets. The entry therefore covers its rulers, its river and the nearby mine. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.923}
