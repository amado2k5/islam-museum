## Summary
Raghulda is a large, populous city on the sea-river, attributed to the land of Tabst. Al-Idrisi gives its distance as one hundred miles from the second arm of the river Qatulu, and two hundred miles from Anhu. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.954}

## Details
Raghulda lies in the fourth section of the seventh climate, which al-Idrisi describes, and this section covers most of the land of the Russians, Finmark, Tabst, Astlandat and the land of the Majus. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953} He describes these lands as mostly empty and wild, with villages and permanent snow, and with few inhabited regions. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953}

He describes Finmark as a land with many villages, buildings and sheep, with no settled land except the cities of Abura and Qalmar, which he calls two large cities. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953} From Qalmar to the second arm of the river Qatulu is eighty miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953}

From the river Qatulu to Raghulda is one hundred miles, and he calls Raghulda a large and populous city on the sea-river, attributed to the land of Tabst. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953} The one hundred miles are counted from the second arm of the river, not from Qalmar itself.

From Anhu to Raghulda is two hundred miles, and he calls Anhu a fine and noble city, populous, in the land of Astlandat. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.954}

What he records about Raghulda's buildings, markets or produce is slight. He gives its site, its attribution to Tabst and its distances to Qalmar and Anhu. The surviving text gives no foundation date and no single event at Raghulda.
