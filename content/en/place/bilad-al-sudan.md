## Overview
Bilad al-Sudan, "the land of the blacks", is the name Muslim geographers gave to the belt south of the Sahara, from West Africa to the Nile basin. Yaqut al-Hamawi's Mu'jam al-Buldan has no single entry under this name but mentions the land in many related entries, on which this page is built: they describe its edges next to the Maghrib, Fezzan and Barqa, nearby cities and kingdoms, and the trade routes into it.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut marks several points on its borders. Under Zawila, he says it is an unwalled city in the middle of the desert, the first limit of Bilad al-Sudan, with a congregational mosque, a bath and markets where caravans gather from every side before their roads part; and that Uqba ibn Nafi reached it when Amr conquered Barqa, so that the land between Barqa and Zawila belonged to the Muslims. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160} See [[place/zawila|Zawila]]. Under Kuwar he names a region of Bilad al-Sudan south of Fezzan, which, he says, Uqba ibn Amir took entirely, seizing its king and imposing three hundred and sixty slaves on him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.486} Under Kakudam and Ghana he names two cities on the southern edge of the Maghrib: Kakudam, in the far Maghrib, bordering Bilad al-Sudan, from which the veiled kings came before Abd al-Mu'min; {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.431} and Ghana, a large city south of the Maghrib joined to the Sudan lands, where merchants gather and from which they enter the wastes toward the land of gold, and without which they could not reach those people. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.184} See [[place/ghana|Ghana]].

Under al-Tibr he says it is a land of Bilad al-Sudan known as the land of gold dust, to which pure gold is attributed, lying south of the Maghrib. He describes the merchants' route: they travel from Sijilmasa to a town on the Sudan frontier called Ghata, carrying salt, a kind of pine wood, glass beads and copper bracelets and rings; they take water from the land of Lamtuna, the veiled people; and they cross hard desert with scorching winds until they reach Ghana, and from there go on to the gold-dust people. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.12} See [[place/sijilmasa|Sijilmasa]]. Under Zafun he describes a wide province in the Sudan lands neighbouring the Maghrib and joined to the land of the Mulaththamun, with a strong king whom the Mulaththamun acknowledge as their superior and appeal to in great judgments. He came to the Maghrib one year on pilgrimage and visited the Amir al-Muslimin of the Lamtuna, who received him on foot, as one who saw him in Marrakesh reported. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.127} Under Kanim he calls it a land of the Berbers in the far Maghrib within Bilad al-Sudan, and it is said to be a kind of Sudan people; he quotes al-Bakri that forty stages lie between Zawila and Kanim and that its people are pagans who claim that a group of the Banu Umayya went there during their trial at the hands of the Banu al-Abbas. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.432}

Under Jazirat al-Arab Yaqut also notes that the Nile comes from the upper Bilad al-Sudan, running west of the Red Sea coast before reaching the sea of Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.137} In some of these entries Yaqut says he is quoting others, such as al-Bakri, and he gives no single boundary for the whole region in any one entry.
