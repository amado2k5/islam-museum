## Overview
Modern Arabic histories tie the name Karachi to the site of al-Daybul, the Sindhi port whose story recurs in the Umayyad conquest of Sind. Hasan Ibrahim Hasan says that "Khawr al-Daybul" is now known as Karachi, and Muhammad Qabbani says that al-Daybul lay near present-day Karachi. The books read for this page have no entry for Karachi itself and do not tell the history of the modern city, so the page sets out what they say about al-Daybul and its link to this site, and leaves the description of the city today to the place card. See [[place/daybul|al-Daybul]], [[place/sind|Sind]] and [[place/pakistan|Pakistan]].

## Al-Daybul in the first accounts of the conquests
Hasan Ibrahim Hasan, relaying al-Baladhuri, says that Umar ibn al-Khattab appointed Uthman ibn Abi al-As al-Thaqafi over Bahrain and Oman in the year 15 AH. Uthman sent his brother al-Hakam to Bahrain and himself went to Oman, from where he sent an army to Thana. When the army came back and he wrote to Umar, Umar answered with a reproach and swore that had the force been lost he would have taken the like number from Uthman's people. Uthman then sent al-Hakam to Barus and sent his other brother, [[person/al-mughira-ibn-abi-al-as|al-Mughira ibn Abi al-As]], to Khawr al-Daybul on the western coast of the land of India, which the author says is now known as Karachi; al-Mughira met the enemy and prevailed. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.250} See [[place/tana-india|Tana]].

The author adds that nobody raided these lands until 39 AH, when Ali ibn Abi Talib sent an expedition under al-Harith ibn Murra, and that in the time of Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan [[person/al-muhallab-ibn-abi-sufra|al-Muhallab ibn Abi Sufra]] raided them in 44 AH, with the conquests extending to take in al-Qawqan, al-Qiqan and al-Daybul. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.250} {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.251}

## The taking of al-Daybul in 89 AH
In the caliphate of al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik, which Hasan Ibrahim Hasan dates 86 to 96 AH, [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi]] entrusted [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-qasim|Muhammad ibn al-Qasim]] with the invasion of India. He set out in 89 AH, besieged the port of al-Daybul and took it by force, building a mosque there; he then went to Birun, whose people received him well, let him into their town and kept to the peace. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.251} He reached the Indus, then called the Mihran, and met [[person/dahir|Dahir]], king of Sind, there; the fight ended in Dahir's death and the rout of his men. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.251} See [[event/conquest-of-sind-89|The Conquest of Sind]].

Muhammad Qabbani, in his booklet on the Umayyad state, gives the plan of the campaign in more detail. Muhammad ibn al-Qasim took the Mihran district in southern Fars as his base; al-Hajjaj had reinforced him with six thousand Syrian soldiers, bringing his force to about twenty thousand by some historians' estimate. He divided the army in two, one part by land and one by sea, and made for the city of al-Daybul, which the author says lies near present-day Karachi in Pakistan, taking Fanzabul and Armael on the way. The ships carrying men and equipment then joined him; he besieged al-Daybul and took it after three days of fighting, left a garrison of four thousand and built them a mosque. {cite:umayyad-birth-to-fall-2006 p.51} The author says the fall of al-Daybul made a great impression on the people of Sind, who hurried to ask for terms; he made peace with them, treated them gently and went on to al-Birun, which he says is today Hyderabad in Sind. {cite:umayyad-birth-to-fall-2006 p.51} See [[place/hyderabad-sindh|Hyderabad]].

## Al-Daybul in Yaqut and al-Qazwini
Yaqut al-Hamawi has an entry for al-Daybul in the Mu'jam al-Buldan. He calls it a famous city on the shore of the Indian sea, in the second climate, gives its longitude and latitude in degrees and minutes, and says it is a fursa, a harbour, into which the waters of Lahore and Multan run to reach the salt sea. He lists transmitters named after it, among them Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ibrahim al-Daybuli, who took up residence at Mecca and transmitted from Abu Abd Allah Sa'id ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Makhzumi and from Husayn ibn Hasan al-Marwazi, and his son Ibrahim ibn Muhammad al-Daybuli, who transmits from Musa ibn Harun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.495} See [[place/multan|Multan]] and [[place/lahore|Lahore]].

Al-Qazwini, in his entry on Sind in Athar al-Bilad, quotes al-Istakhri that the Mihran river falls into the sea east of al-Daybul. {cite:athar-al-bilad-qazwini p.94}

## What these sources do not say
Neither Yaqut nor al-Qazwini has an entry called Karachi or connects al-Daybul with it. The link between the two names on this page is the word of Hasan Ibrahim Hasan and of Muhammad Qabbani alone, both twentieth-century writers, and they do not say the same thing: the first makes Khawr al-Daybul the place now called Karachi, the second says al-Daybul is near it. The history and population of the modern city and its standing in Pakistan are described by the place card, which rests on two other sources; they were not read from the books named here.
