## Overview
Fadak is a Jewish oasis in the north of the Hijaz, near [[place/khaybar|Khaybar]]. On the Prophet's return from Khaybar, its people made a settlement with him to give up half the land and its produce, so the pure half became a share of the Prophet's, which he spent on wayfarers. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.41-42}

Its people remained there until [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]], in his caliphate, expelled them to Syria, along with the Jews of Khaybar, after valuing their share of the palms and land at a fair price. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.41-42}

In Sahih al-Bukhari, within Umar's account of the Prophet's properties, he says that Fadak was for wayfarers. {cite:sahih-al-bukhari 4033}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says Fadak is a village in the Hijaz, two days from Medina by one account and three by another, which God granted to His Messenger as fay' in the year 7 AH by treaty: its people sent word, after he had come down on Khaybar, asking peace for half of their fruit and goods, and he agreed. It was land taken without horses or camels, so it was his alone, and it had a gushing spring and many palms. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.238}

He presents the disagreement over it after the Prophet's death. He reports that Fatima said the Messenger had given it to her and that Abu Bakr asked for witnesses. He says Umar ibn al-Khattab's own judgement led him to return it to the heirs of the Messenger, and that Ali and al-Abbas then disputed over it. He then follows its later history: Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz returned it to Fatima's descendants, Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik took it back, and it stayed with the Umayyads until al-Saffah gave it to al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan; al-Mansur seized it, al-Mahdi restored it and al-Hadi took it again; in 210 al-Ma'mun ordered it handed to Fatima's descendants, and al-Mutawakkil returned it to its earlier arrangement. He adds that the poet Di'bil recited a verse on the occasion. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.238}

Yaqut says there is much disagreement about Fadak and that some narrators told its story according to their leanings and in heated dispute; the soundest account he found, he says, is the one al-Baladhuri gives in his Futuh. In summary he reports from it that, after returning from Khaybar, the Prophet sent Muhayyisa ibn Mas'ud to the people of Fadak, whose chief was Yusha' ibn Nun the Jew; they made peace for half of the land with its soil, and he spent what came from it on wayfarers. Later Umar evicted the Jews, having had half the soil valued and paid to them, and sent them to Syria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.238}

He then relates an account that Fatima asked Abu Bakr for Fadak, that Ali testified for her and Abu Bakr asked for a second witness, that Umm Ayman testified, and that Abu Bakr answered that testimony is valid only from two men, or one man and two women, so she left. He gives a report from Umm Hani of a conversation between Fatima and Abu Bakr, in which Abu Bakr says he heard the Messenger of God say that it was a provision God fed him in his lifetime and that after his death it belongs to the Muslims. He also gives a report from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr that the Prophet's wives sent Uthman to ask for their inheritance and that Abu Bakr cited the saying that prophets leave no inheritance. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.239}

He gives the speech of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz on Fadak, in which he says Mu'awiya granted it to Marwan ibn al-Hakam, that it passed to Marwan's sons and then to himself, and that he returned it to the state it had in the days of the Prophet, Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali. He then says al-Zajjaji held that it was named for Fadak ibn Ham, the first to settle it. He names Muhammad ibn Sadaqa al-Fadaki, who heard Malik ibn Anas, as a man attributed to it, and closes with a verse of Zuhayr that mentions it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.240}
