## Summary
Lawqin is a handsome city on the sea and on the bank of a freshwater inlet that ships enter, three coastal stages from the island of Awrisin. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.202} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.203}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says: from Awrisin to Lawqin are three stages along the coast, and it is a handsome city on the sea and on the bank of a freshwater khawr which ships enter {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.202} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.203}. From it to the city of Tarighyuqn are four stages; that city is populous on the shore of the Salt Sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.203}. The island of Awrisin is one of the islands of the land of India, rich in elephants which are bred there and exported to all the lands of India, and it has iron mines {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.202}. Rhubarb grows on most of its mountains, though the rhubarb brought from the lands of China is better, being firmer of body and deeper of colour {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.202}. On the island also grows the thorny tree called al-Shahkir with black roots, which the kings of China and India cultivate because they prepare from it a renowned poison {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.202}.
