## Summary
Mount Carmel is a headland running out into the sea by the city of Haifa, with a fine anchorage for the mooring of fleets and other ships. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Caesarea to the city of Haifa on the coast is two days, that Haifa lies beneath the point of Carmel — a headland running out into the sea in which there is a fine anchorage for the mooring of fleets and other vessels — that Haifa is the port of Tiberias with three light stages between them, and that from Haifa to the city of Acre overland is one stage, reckoned at thirty miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}. In al-Idrisi's description Carmel is thus a mountainous cape extending into the waters of the Salt Sea on the Syrian coast, with the city of Haifa clinging to its foot; he places in it an anchorage fit for ships and fleets, and it stands as a prominent landmark of that coast between Caesarea to the south and Acre to the north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}
