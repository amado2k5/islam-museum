## Overview
Baku is the capital of Azerbaijan on the Caspian Sea. The pages read here come from Lothrop Stoddard's Hadir al-Alam al-Islami in the Arabic of Ajjaj Nuwayhid with the commentary of Shakib Arslan, a survey of the Muslim world in the 1920s, so its statements are attributed to its authors and do not cover the city before that time.

## In Hadir al-Alam al-Islami
The book says of trans-Caucasian Azerbaijan that its capital is Baku, famous for its petroleum springs, that the city's industry grew until it became an industrial centre of the western type, and that its inhabitants and workers were of mixed Asian and Russian origin {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.278 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}. It adds that the Bolsheviks found in those classes of owners and workers a field for their plan {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.278 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}.

It describes a conference called by the Soviet government at Baku in the autumn of 1920 as the Congress of the Peoples of the East, which, its author says, aimed not only at freeing the East from Western bondage but also at making it Bolshevik {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.278 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}. It gives the text of the invitation, and says that the delegations numbered about 1,900 members, about 1,300 of them Communists, and that the largest came from Turkey, Persia, Armenia and the Caucasian lands {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.281 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}. This is the authors' view of the congress, not a neutral report of it.

Elsewhere the book says that Enver Pasha led a campaign in Azerbaijan in 1918 that drove the British out of Baku and founded an independent Azeri state that lasted some years {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 4 scan p.121 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.04)}; this is Arslan's account of it. It also mentions a congress of representatives of the Turkic peoples held at Baku to examine their history and languages and the reform of their letters, where the philologist Alimjan Sharaf Bey defended the Arabic script; the page read does not date it {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 vol. 3 scan p.394 (archive.org item hadir.al.aalm.al.islami.03)}.
