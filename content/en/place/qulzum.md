## Overview
Al-Qulzum is a gulf and a road. The gulf branches off the Indian Ocean at Bab al-Mandab and runs past western Yemen, Tihama and the Hijaz to Madyan, Aylah and Faran, until it reaches the city of al-Qulzum, which gives the gulf its name. It then turns south past the eastern side of the Sa'id toward Jun al-Malik and on to Aydhab. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.10}

Al-Baladhuri records that al-Ashtar fell ill at al-Qulzum on his way to Egypt, and that Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr was appointed over Egypt again, until Muawiya ibn Hudayj killed him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.319} Under al-Mutawakkil, Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Qummi was given charge of the mine in Egypt and the road to the Hijaz, along with the escort of Egyptian pilgrims. Grain was carried in ships from al-Qulzum to the lands of the Beja, and he reached a coast called Aydhab. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.333}

## Dhanab al-Timsah (the Crocodile's Tail)
A mile from it is a site known as Dhanab al-Timsah. A king of the Romans dug a channel there toward the Roman sea, but it did not join, because of the height of the Qulzum. On it is a great bridge which the pilgrim caravans of Egypt cross. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.558}
