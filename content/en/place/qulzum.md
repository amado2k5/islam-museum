## Overview
Al-Qulzum is a gulf and a road. The gulf branches off the Indian Ocean at Bab al-Mandab and runs past western Yemen, Tihama and the Hijaz to Madyan, Aylah and Faran, until it reaches the city of al-Qulzum, which gives the gulf its name. It then turns south past the eastern side of the Sa'id toward Jun al-Malik and on to Aydhab. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.10}

Al-Baladhuri records that al-Ashtar fell ill at al-Qulzum on his way to Egypt, and that Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr was appointed over Egypt again, until Muawiya ibn Hudayj killed him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.319} Under al-Mutawakkil, Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Qummi was given charge of the mine in Egypt and the road to the Hijaz, along with the escort of Egyptian pilgrims. Grain was carried in ships from al-Qulzum to the lands of the Beja, and he reached a coast called Aydhab. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.333}

## Dhanab al-Timsah (the Crocodile's Tail)
A mile from it is a site known as Dhanab al-Timsah. A king of the Romans dug a channel there toward the Roman sea, but it did not join, because of the height of the Qulzum. On it is a great bridge which the pilgrim caravans of Egypt cross. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.558}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut opens the entry by explaining that qalzama means swallowing a thing, and that the sea was called the Sea of al-Qulzum for its engulfing those who sail it; he names it as the place where Pharaoh and his people drowned. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.387} Citing Ibn al-Kalbi, he says a neck of the Indian Ocean ran up past the Tihama of Yemen, Jidda and the coasts of Mecca and Medina, al-Tur, Tayma and the Gulf of Ayla until it reached the Qulzum of Egypt; he adds that some say al-Qulzum is a town on the coast of the Yemen sea near Ayla, al-Tur and Madyan, four days from al-Farama, and that al-Quda'i counted it with Ra'ya among the southern districts of Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.387} He relays al-Balkhi's description of the sea as about thirty stages long and, at its widest, three nights across, and al-Muhallabi's remark that a mountain near al-Qulzum holds magnetic stone. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.387} Of the city he reports, from an earlier description he quotes, that it lies three days from Egypt on the very edge of the sea, with no crops, trees or water of its own, its water carried from distant wells, and that it was the port of Egypt and Syria from which loads went on to the Hijaz and Yemen. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.388} He comments that this was how it was long ago, whereas in his day it lay in ruins and the port had moved to a nearby place called Suways, itself almost deserted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.388} Among those attributed to it he names al-Hasan ibn Yahya al-Qulzumi, and he quotes Ibn al-Banna calling it an ancient city on the edge of the China Sea with no water or pasture, water being brought from Suways, yet a treasury of Egypt, the port of the Hijaz and a refuge of the pilgrims. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.388} He closes by noting that al-Qulzum was also the old name of the river of Granada in al-Andalus, called Hadarra in his day. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.388}
