## Overview
Abiward: a town in Khurasan. The conquest accounts report that its chief, Bahmatah, came to [[person/abd-allah-ibn-amir|Ibn Amir]] and made peace with him for four hundred thousand. Another report, introduced with "it is said", states that Abd Allah ibn Khazim was sent to it and made peace with its people for four hundred thousand dirhams. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The two reports agree on the sum and differ on the name of the commander; the matter needs an additional source.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The unsigned Encyclopaedia of Islam (2nd ed.) entry on Abiward places the town and district on the northern slopes of the mountains of Khurasan, in the region that became part of the Turkmen republic of the USSR. In ancient times it was the first line of defence of Khurasan against the nomads. Under the Sasanians it was split among small principalities, and in the twelfth to fourteenth centuries it belonged to the Ghurbani princes. Nadir Shah came from this region, and after the Russo-Persian frontier was fixed in 1885, Atak was incorporated into Russia. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 118}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] spells out the vowels of Abiward and repeats the Persian tradition that King Kay Kawus granted "Bawurd son of Gudarz" land in Khurasan, where he built a city and named it after himself. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.106} For him it is a city of Khurasan between Sarakhs and Nasa, with poor water and an unhealthy climate in which the guinea-worm was common. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.106}

To it is traced the man of letters [[person/al-abiwardi|Abu al-Muzaffar Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Umawi al-Mu'awi]], the poet, originally from Kufan, a village of Abiward, whom Yaqut calls a master of every field, grammar, language, genealogy and history; he died in Isfahan on 20 Rabi' al-Awwal 507 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.106} Abiward, he says, was taken by [[person/abd-allah-ibn-amir|Abd Allah ibn Amir ibn Kurayz]] in 31 AH, or earlier, by some accounts, by [[person/al-ahnaf-ibn-qays|al-Ahnaf ibn Qays]]. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.106}
