## Overview
Al-Shadhrawan is a great weir that, the account says, King Shapur ordered built in front of [[place/tustar|Tustar]] across the river's course, and al-Idrisi counts it among the famous wonders. Strong high masonry was raised on both banks, and at the head of the channel a dam was fixed with huge stones and Hijazi columns until it stood level with the two banks; the water was held back until it stood opposite Tustar. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 393}

## The river and its use
Tustar stands on a high rise of ground with the water held before it. The largest river of Khuzistan is the river of Tustar, called Dujayl of al-Ahwaz, which rises in mountains on the side of the Lur country. It runs behind [[place/askar-mukram|Askar Mukram]], passes al-Ahwaz and reaches the river of al-Sidra and the fort of Mahdi, where it falls into the sea. From it comes off the river Masruqan, going west to Askar Mukram, where a great bridge of about twenty boats crosses it; from Askar Mukram to al-Ahwaz is thirty miles by water. None of its water is wasted, and all of it is spent irrigating cane, grain, palms, orchards and fields. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 393}

## A tradition attached to it
Al-Idrisi says the water rises in the weir as far as the gate of Tustar. It is told that the coffin of Daniel lies in the river of Tustar; he reports from al-Hawqali that Abu Musa al-Ash'ari found the tomb, that the People of the Book used to carry it about in their gatherings and pray with it for rain in a drought, and that Abu Musa took it, made three brick-lined graves and buried the coffin in one of them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 395}
