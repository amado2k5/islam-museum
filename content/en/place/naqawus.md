## Overview
Naqawus is a small town near the Aurès mountain, with many trees and gardens. Its commonest fruit is the walnut, and the neighbouring regions take supplies from it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264}

## Setting and buildings
The town has busy markets and many farms and crops. A fortress stands above it with a lookout from which one can see the country of the Arabs and look far beyond. Its people drink from flowing springs. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264} Dar Mulul lies three stages away, and the Aurès mountain is one stage and a little more from the town. Al-Idrisi describes the mountain as a long range with continuous settlements and many waters, whose people are fierce and dominate those around them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264}

## Distances
From Tabna to Naqawus is two stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264} From Naqawus to al-Msila is four stages, though some say three. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264} To the fortress of Biskra it is two stages; that fort is strong, built on a high mound of earth, and it has a market, a population and dates of every rare and fine kind. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264}

## Neighbouring forts
To the fortress of Badis it is three stages. Badis is a fine town, well populated, at the foot of the Aurès. The Arabs own its land and do not let its people leave without an escort from among them. From Naqawus to the city of al-Msila is four miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 264}
