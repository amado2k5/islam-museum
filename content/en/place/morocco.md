## Overview
Al-Maghrib, in the sense of the far Maghrib (al-Maghrib al-Aqsa), is the land at the western edge of the Islamic world on the Atlantic, where the Almoravid, Almohad and other states arose; it is today a kingdom. This page records what Yaqut al-Hamawi's Mu'jam al-Buldan says of the cities and rulers of the far Maghrib at the turn of the sixth and seventh centuries AH; it does not narrate the history of the modern state.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut bounds the Maghrib generally from Miliana to the end of the Sus mountains, behind which lies the Ocean. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.161} Under Sala he calls it a city in the far Maghrib, beyond which only a small city called Gharnitof lies before the Ocean; he adds that it is of middling size, set where the sea and a river meet, with the sea to its north and the river to its west. Abd al-Mu'min laid out a city west of the river and named it al-Mahdiyya; he lodged there when he wished to settle a matter or prepare an army, and it lay ten stages from Marrakesh. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.231} See [[place/sala|Sala]] and [[person/abd-al-mumin-ibn-ali|Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali]].

Under Marrakesh he calls it the greatest and most majestic city of the Maghrib, the seat of the throne of the Banu Abd al-Mu'min, in the middle of Berber country ten days from the sea. Its founder, he says, was Yusuf ibn Tashfin of the veiled people, styled Amir al-Muslimin, around 470 AH. He puts it three farsakhs from the Daran mountain, which lies to its south and from which Ibn Tumart, called al-Mahdi, appeared; says the site had been a place where thieves attacked caravans, that the name means "walk fast" in Berber, and that Abd al-Mu'min was the first to lay out gardens there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.94} See [[place/marrakesh|Marrakesh]], [[person/yusuf-ibn-tashfin|Yusuf ibn Tashfin]] and [[person/ibn-tumart|Ibn Tumart]].

Under Kakudam he describes a city at the far Maghrib, south of the sea and bordering the Sudan lands, from which came the veiled Arab kings who preceded Abd al-Mu'min. Their tribes include Lamtuna, Massufa and Kudala; the rule lay among Lamtuna, and from them came Yusuf ibn Tashfin, Amir of the Mulaththamun, who ruled the whole West. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.431} Under Tadlis he names it a city in the far Maghrib on the Ocean. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.17} See [[place/tadlis|Tadlis]].
