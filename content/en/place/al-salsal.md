## Overview
al-Salsal: a water in the land of [[group/judham|Judham]]. Ibn Ishaq says that when [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] was in charge of it he was afraid, so he sent to the Prophet asking for reinforcements. The expedition of [[event/expedition-of-dhat-al-salasil|Dhat al-Salasil]] was named after this water. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/272}

## Markers of weakness and doubt
- The position of the water is not specified in the text, and it has no coordinates; the naming of the expedition by sources other than Ibn Ishaq is explained differently (needs_source).

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut treats the place in two entries. In al-Salasil he says it is a water in the land of Judham from which the campaign of Dhat al-Salasil took its name, and that Ibn Ishaq said the water's name is Salsal and that Dhat al-Salasil was named for it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.233} He cites verses of Jiran al-'Awd and of al-Ra'i that name a Dhat al-Salasil without his fixing where, then a hadith of 'Asim ibn Sufyan al-Thaqafi about a campaign of al-Salasil, and Ibn Hibban's remark that the campaign of al-Salasil belonged to Mu'awiya's days and the campaign of Dhat al-Salasil to the Prophet's; he then says himself, "I said", that he does not know what these chains are, without saying whether he means those of both campaigns or of one. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.233} In the entry on Salsal, with a fatha on the sin and the lam, he says the word means sweet clear water, gives Abu Mansur that it is a mountain in the Dahna in the land of Tamim, and quotes Ibn Ishaq on the campaign: the Prophet sent 'Amr ibn al-'As to the land of Judham until he was at a water in Judham's land called al-Salsal, from which the campaign was named. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.236}

The next entry, Silsil, with a kasra on both letters, is a river in the Sawad of Iraq, attached to a district on the Khurasan road in the province of Shadhqubadh on the east side; it is not the water of Judham. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.236} Yaqut gives no more exact place for the water than the land of Judham and no other explanation of the name.

## The museum's identification
The record, from Ibn Hisham, has a water in the land of Judham called al-Salsal, where 'Amr ibn al-'As, afraid, sent to the Prophet for help, and from which the campaign of Dhat al-Salasil took its name; the page says its position is not fixed in the text, that it has no coordinates, and that the campaign's name is explained otherwise by sources other than Ibn Ishaq. Yaqut agrees, from Ibn Ishaq, on a water of Judham and on the reason for the name, says plainly that he does not know what the chains are, and has no other explanation of the name and no place more exact than the land of Judham. The data record, however, carries coordinates (32.25, 46.5) whose note names an Abbasid canal in the Wasit district of Iraq; they lie 1,041.1 km from the museum's Wadi al-Qura point, 1,045.2 km from its Tabuk point and 1,096.0 km from its Medina point. Yaqut has two entries that differ in spelling, Salsal, the water of Judham, and Silsil, a river in the Sawad of Iraq on the Khurasan road, so his text does not join them and does not show that the record's point belongs to this water. The museum does not change the coordinates here and sends the question to outside research.
