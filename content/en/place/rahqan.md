## Overview
Rahqan is a valley between al-Nazia and the pass of al-Safra, on the road to Badr. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.205}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Ruhqan, with a damma on the ra', a sukun on the ha', a qaf and a final nun, says that of this form only rahiq, "wine", occurs in their speech, and says the Prophet took this way on the Badr expedition, sending the reader to the entry al-Naziya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.37} Under al-Naziya he describes a flowing spring on the road from Mecca to Medina near al-Safra', nearer Medina, and gives Ibn Ishaq's account that, on the way to Badr, the Prophet left al-Rawha' as far as al-Munsarif, left the Mecca road on his left, took the right-hand way by al-Naziya, and then crossed a valley called Rahqan between al-Naziya and the narrow pass of al-Safra'. He goes on to say that Ibn al-Furat vocalised it so in several places, adds "as if from naza yanzu", "to leap", and gives, as reported from Ibn al-Furat, that al-Naziya is a wide open tract with 'idah trees and marukh (left untranslated here); it is not clear whether the vocalisation remark is about the name Rahqan or about al-Naziya, or whether the derivation is Yaqut's or Ibn al-Furat's. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.251}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Mubarakfuri, is a valley between al-Nazia and the Safra' defile on the road to Badr. This is what Yaqut gives from Ibn Ishaq, with the order of the stations added: al-Rawha', al-Munsarif, al-Naziya, Rahqan, the pass of al-Safra'. The record writes Rahqan and Yaqut writes Ruhqan, so the vocalisation is open. Yaqut gives no distances. The record's point is 5.4 km from the museum's al-Rawha' point and 26.3 km from its al-Safra' point. See [[place/al-rawha|al-Rawha']], [[place/al-safra|al-Safra']] and [[place/badr|Badr]].
