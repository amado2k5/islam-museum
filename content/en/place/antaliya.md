## Summary
Antaliya was a pair of cities on the Anatolian coast: Antaliya the Burned and New Antaliya standing on a high mountain, two days apart. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.647}

## Details
In the coastal itineraries of the land of Rum al-Idrisi states that from Awlash to Seluqiyya is two days, from Seluqiyya to Antaliya the Burned four miles, and to New Antaliya, between the two cities being two days, the new one set on a high mountain, and from it to a harbour {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.647}. He thus distinguishes the old, ruined Antaliya from the newer city standing on the commanding hill, separated by a two days' journey along that shore.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name Antaliya in the same pattern and letters as the Antakiya before it, with a lam in place of the kaf, and calls it a large and well-known town of the Byzantine lands. He reports that the first to settle it was Antaliya, daughter of Rum ibn al-Yaqin ibn Sam ibn Nuh and sister of Antakiya, and that it was named after her. Quoting al-Balkhi, he says that past Qalamiyya and al-Lamis one comes to Antaliya, a strong Byzantine fortress on the sea shore, with a wide district and many inhabitants, after which the route reaches the Gulf of Constantinople. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.270}
