## Overview
Bayhaq: a rustaq of Nishapur. al-Aswad ibn Kulthum al-Adawi, the rival of the Rabab, entered it through a breach in its walls, and he fought until he and those with him were killed. Then Adham ibn Kathum took charge of the people's affair, was victorious, and conquered Bayhaq. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] says that Bayhaq comes from the Persian "bayhah", meaning "the best", and that it is a large district of Nishapur's territory with three hundred and twenty-one villages, between Nishapur, Qumis and Juwayn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.750} Its chief town was first Khusrawjird and then Sabzawar ("Sabzwar" in common speech), and it stretched twenty-five parasangs from the edge of Nishapur's lands to near Damghan, with a similar breadth. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.750}

He says this district produced countless men of learning, law and letters, then states that most of its people in his time were Shi'is, describing them in his own words as "of the school of the extreme Rafida"; the label is Yaqut's. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.750} The most famous of its imams, for him, is [[person/al-bayhaqi-abu-bakr-ahmad-ibn-al-husayn|Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn al-Husayn al-Bayhaqi]] of Khusrawjird, the prolific author, one of the foremost pupils of al-Hakim Abu Abd Allah who then surpassed him in several fields; he wrote close to a thousand parts, was called to Nishapur to teach his *Ma'rifa* and went there in 441 AH, then returned home and died in Jumada al-Ula 454 AH; his works include the *Mabsut* and the *Sunan*. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.750}
