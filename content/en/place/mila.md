## Summary
Mila is a fine city rich in trees and fruitful, its waters abundant, its people of mixed Berber stock; it lies east of Qal'at Bani Hammad, four stages away. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.265}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says: east of the city of Qal'at Bani Hammad is the city of Mila, at four stages from it {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.265}. Mila is a fine city rich in trees, productive of fruit, its fruits many, its beauties evident and its waters abundant; its people are for the most part of mixed Berber stock, the Arabs rule its outskirts, and it was under the authority of Yahya ibn al-Aziz, lord of Bejaia {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.265}. From it eastward to the city of Constantine the Airy are eighteen miles, joined by a mountain along which the road runs {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.265}. Constantine is a populous city with markets and merchants; its people are wealthy, possessed of broad means, having dealings with the Arabs and sharing with them in ploughing and storing grain {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.265}.
