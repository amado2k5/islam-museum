## Overview
Azerbaijan: a region whose capital is Ardabil, where its governor is and to which its land-tax is collected. Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman conquered it under a governorship from Umar, which al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba later transferred to him. Its governor made peace with him for eight hundred thousand dirhams, of the weight of eight. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut quotes Ibn al-Muqaffa that the name is derived from Adharbadh son of Iran son of al-Aswad son of Sam son of Noah. He himself prefers the view that azar is the Pahlavi word for fire and that the second element means guardian or keeper, so the name means the house of fire or the keeper of fire, because fire-temples were very numerous in this region. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.128}

He bounds Azerbaijan from Barda'a in the east to Arzanjan in the west, and to the north it joins the lands of the Daylam, the Jil and the Tarm. Among its famous cities is Tabriz, which in his time was its seat and its largest city; earlier its seat was Maragha. Other cities include Khoy, Salmas, Armiya, Ardabil and Marand. He describes it as a great realm, mostly mountain, with many fortresses and wide resources, and Abu Awn Ishaq ibn Ali places it in the fifth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.128}
