## Overview
Shikla is a fortress which al-Idrisi describes as among the finest fortresses, populous and thriving. In it are gardens bearing every kind of fruit, and a spring known as the Spring of Times (ayn al-awqat). {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.598}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that Shikla is a fortress on the top of a mountain. Its site is the best of sites and its condition the most prosperous, for it is inhabited and thriving, both a town and a settled place with desert country around it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.598}

It has markets to which goods are brought from every region. Its people are in good circumstances. Its gardens bear every fruit, its farms are the finest and its houses the most pleasant. Traders come to it by sea from every region, including Calabria, Africa, Malta and other lands. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.598}

Great rivers flow through it, with many mills on them. Its most unusual feature is the Spring of Times: it runs at the hours of prayer and dries up at other times. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.598}

Al-Idrisi places it about three miles from the sea. The route from Nutas is one stage along the coast. From Shikla to Rughus is thirteen miles, and Rughus is a strong fortress and a noble town. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.598}
