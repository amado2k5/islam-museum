## Overview
The capital of the Abbasid caliphate, founded by al-Mansur in the year 762 CE, and a great centre of learning.

## What this page covers

## For Sprouts
Baghdad is Iraq's historic capital of learning, built by the Abbasids on the Tigris River, famous for the House of Wisdom.

## For Explorers
Baghdad is the capital of Iraq and the historic center of the Abbasid Caliphate for centuries, founded by Caliph al-Mansur as the City of Peace. Famous for its round design on the Tigris River, it became the preeminent metropolis of science and arts, home to the House of Wisdom, the Nizamiyya academy, and hospitals.

## For Scholars
The imperial capital of the Abbasid Caliphate and the cosmopolitan epicenter of medieval Islamic civilization, founded in 145 AH (762 CE) by Caliph Abu Jafar al-Mansur on the Tigris River at a vital crossroads of Afro-Eurasian trade. For five centuries, it stood as the undisputed global capital of sciences, philosophy, translation, and literature, nurturing the House of Wisdom, the Nizamiyya, and early teaching hospitals. Home to founding jurists like Abu Hanifa and Ahmad ibn Hanbal and master scientists like al-Khwarizmi, Baghdad remains the enduring symbol of the Islamic Golden Age.

## The round nucleus is not the whole city
[[invention/round-city-baghdad|Baghdad's round-city nucleus plan]] concerns a seat connecting palace,mosque,four entrances and rings for defence,circulation and housing,not every area inhabited over centuries. Settlements,canals,routes and circular-planning precedents predated al-Mansur's commission {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Foundation and plan}. Le Strange gives145AH/762foundation,146AH/763treasury/bureau transfer and149AH/766completion,not one opening day or new conversions {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.15–19}. Two ramparts differ from the central enclosure; every drawn ring does not share one function.

## Housing,markets,water and access
Radial routes,rings and neighbourhoods coexist,and street/arcade uses changed. Le Strange describes market removal,guard accommodation,water carriers,Matbak prison and riding restrictions into the centre {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–29}. Duri dates market relocation to773and describes expansion/changing centres {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets,Development}. Geometric order establishes neither equal access nor absence of coercion,and does not place every later scholarly institution inside the circle. Water networks helped make habitation possible; their complete operation cannot be recovered from a wall outline.

## Map,measurements and retained accounts
Le Strange's1900map is reconstruction,not aerial photography or foundation survey; not every marginal feature necessarily belongs to762 {cite:commons-round-city-lestrange}. Duri/Le Strange differ on Kufa/Basra compass labels and diameter estimates. Existing city coordinates have not been replaced by an inferred circle centre {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Measurements} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.17–18}. The retained Muqaddasi-based Arabic account gives4,000,833dirhams,whereas Duri gives4,000,883. Retain each attribution rather than silently correct one tradition {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p.107–109} {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Costs}. Masudi's gate/prison narrative also remains an attributed account,not modern engineering measurement {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.276–278}.

## Site of covered-conduit arrangement
Baghdad is also linked to [[invention/mansur-covered-water-conduits|the covered water-conduit arrangement]] as place of implementation,not as the first city ever to know water management. Reports connect the urban core with outside supplies such as Dujayl/Karkhaya through lined/covered channels {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.28–29}. This adds a service-history dimension to place description beyond geometric form alone.

## From fixed nucleus to changing city
The site's historical value includes movement from a defined political nucleus to a broader city with shifting functions. Water is part of that movement: sustaining habitation,enabling market relocation,and supporting reorganization of circulation/work {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets,Development}. So the round nucleus should be read as a beginning of an evolving urban process,not a static final map.

## Limits of spatial certainty
Even with strong textual/cartographic evidence,this page cannot by itself deliver a complete operational map of every historical water line. We preserve the distinction between interpretive witness (the1900reconstruction map) and field-execution evidence,and between historical narrative and modern engineering measurement. That keeps Baghdad's record precise: a site with documented organized water service,but not fully recoverable technical geometry from one source layer alone.

## Site of reported brick reckoning
Baghdad in this record is not only a round-plan and conduit site; it is also the setting of the reported [[invention/abu-hanifa-brick-stack-reckoning|measurement-based brick-stack reckoning]] episode in construction supervision {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.17}. This adds materials-management history to place memory.

## Material estimation and city organization
Reading brick-estimation reports together with water,market and access reports highlights Baghdad as a web of operational decisions,not architecture alone. Estimation affects scheduling,spending and pace of service completion in a growing capital {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Development}. The place therefore carries administrative-technique history as well as political symbolism.

## Markets inside and outside the walls
In [[invention/karkh-market-relocation-zoning|the reported Karkh market relocation]], Duri describes four inner markets along arcaded roads and four other market places outside the walls before the relocation order {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Le Strange says shops were removed from the inner roads and the cleared arcades were then used as barracks for city police and horse-guard {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Duri dates the transfer toward Karkh to773CE, while Le Strange also describes suburbs outside several gates; the accounts do not establish that every market moved to one street {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. In his account of Baghdad's growth, Duri describes separate bazaars for crafts and trades and quarters for merchants arriving from different cities, without dating every later arrangement to the relocation itself {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}.

## What this report does not prove alone
From this report alone we do not derive daily production tables,precise efficiency percentages or a full modern engineering model. What it does establish is a transmitted estimation logic inside city-building practice. Keeping that line clear preserves spatial and historical accuracy.
