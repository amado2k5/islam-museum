## Summary
Daybul was the seaport of [[place/sind|Sind]] at its creek. In year fifteen 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As al-Thaqafi sent his brother al-Mughira to Khawr Daybul, where he met the enemy and prevailed — the first raid on that frontier in al-Baladhuri's account. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 607}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri reports that 'Umar appointed 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As over Bahrayn and 'Uman in year fifteen; 'Uthman sent his brother al-Hakam to Bahrayn and to Barus, and sent his brother al-Mughira ibn Abi al-'As to Khawr Daybul, who met the enemy and was victorious {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 607}. In al-Hajjaj's day 'Ubaydallah ibn Nabhan raided Daybul and was killed, so al-Hajjaj wrote to Budayl ibn Tahfa al-Bajali at 'Uman ordering him to march on Daybul; when he met them his horse shied, the enemy surrounded him and killed him {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 612}. Muhammad ibn al-Qasim then conquered it: at Daybul stood a great budd-temple with a tall mast bearing a red banner that circled the city when the wind blew, and he set up for the siege a mangonel called "the Bride" worked by five hundred men {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 613}. He also records that 'Anbasa ibn Ishaq al-Dabbi, governor of Sind under al-Mu'tasim, demolished the top of that tower and turned it into a prison {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 614}.
