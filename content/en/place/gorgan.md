## Overview
Gorgan (Jurjan) is a large old city of the eastern Islamic lands; the museum's record places it at about 36.8 degrees north and 54.4 degrees east. Yaqut al-Hamawi, in his Mu'jam al-Buldan, calls it a famous, great city between Tabaristan and Khurasan and notes that some count it with one and some with the other {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.119}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the vocalisation of the name and then the astronomers' figures: according to the astronomical table he cites, a longitude of about eighty and three quarter degrees and a latitude of thirty-eight degrees fifteen minutes in the fifth climate, though some placed it in the fourth {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.119}. He reports that the first to build it, it was said, was Yazid ibn al-Muhallab ibn Abi Sufra {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.119}, that many literary men, scholars, jurists and hadith transmitters came from it, and that it has a history written by Hamza al-Sahmi {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.119}.

He quotes al-Istakhri that Gorgan is the largest city of its district, with less dew and rain than Tabaristan, its people more dignified and more generous and better off. It lay in two parts, the city and Bakrabad, with a large navigable river between them; its silk and silk cloth went to every quarter, and silkworm seed went from it to Tabaristan. It had snow and palm trees together and the fruits of both cold and hot lands {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.119}.

He also gives Mis'ar ibn Muhalhil's description of a fine city on a great wadi, with olives, dates, walnuts, pomegranates, sugar cane and citron, good silk and stones said to have wonderful properties {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.120}. He adds verses in praise of the city by Abu al-Ghamr, verses against it by the vizier al-Sahib Kafi al-Kufat, and lines by Abu Mansur al-Nisaburi on how its weather changes within a single day {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.120}. He mentions that al-Fadl ibn Sahl put the poet Muslim ibn al-Walid in charge of the estates of Gorgan and that the poet stayed there until his death {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.120}.

## The conquest and its scholars
Yaqut relates, from the biographers, that after Suwayd ibn Muqarrin finished taking Bistam in the year 18 AH he wrote to the king of Gorgan and then marched there; the local ruler Ruzban Sul hurried to make peace on payment of the jizya, and Suwayd entered and wrote them a treaty {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.121}.

Among the imams he lists is Abu Nu'aym Abd al-Malik ibn Muhammad ibn Adi al-Jurjani al-Astarabadhi, a jurist whom he describes, on the authority of those he cites, as an imam of the Muslims and a memoriser of the religious rulings; on Hamza al-Sahmi's report he died at Astarabadh in Dhu al-Hijja 323 {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.121}. The museum's record [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-adi-al-istarabadhi|Abd al-Malik ibn Adi al-Istarabadhi]] concerns this scholar. Another is Abu Ahmad Abd Allah ibn Adi al-Jurjani, the hadith master known as Ibn al-Qattan, who travelled to Damascus, Egypt and elsewhere to collect hadith {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.121}.

## A note on scope
The sources read for this page say nothing about Gorgan after the medieval period, so nothing is stated for the modern city.
