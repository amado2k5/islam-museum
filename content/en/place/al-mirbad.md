## Overview
al-Mirbad: the square and market of [[place/basra|Basra]], at its western gate. Sulayman ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Abbas, governor of Basra for Abu al-Abbas, moved to it and settled there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

According to Ibn al-Jawzi, Abu Tahir al-Janabi al-Qarmati burned it in the year 311 AH on the day he entered Basra. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p.466}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name as al-Mirbad, with a kasra on the mim, a sukun on the ra and a fatha on the ba, and says it is the name of a place and does not follow the pattern of a verb; he cites Qadi Iyad's view that it derives from rabada, to stay in a place. From al-Asma'i he reports that a mirbad is anything in which camels are kept, as in the Mirbad al-Na'am at Medina, and that Basra's Mirbad took its name from this because it was where camels were sold. He describes it as one of the best-known quarters of Basra that grew into a large settled district, the venue of the poets' boasting contests and the orators' assemblies, and says that in his own day it lay about three miles from Basra with ruins in between, so that it stood like a separate town in open country. Among those named for it he lists Simak ibn Atiyya al-Mirbadi al-Basri, whose hadith, he says, is in the two Sahih collections. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.97} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.98}
