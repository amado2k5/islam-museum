## Summary
Hintuna was a populous city in the south of the island of England, on a cape jutting into the sea where the river Awnastra flows in east of it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.945}

## Details
Among the chief towns of the southern side of the island of England al-Idrisi names the cities of Sahstar, Ghurham, Hintuna, Sharham, Hastinkash, Dabars, Jarnmuda, Targhiq, Aghrims, Lundras and others {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.880}. From the city of Ghurham to the headland of Hintuna, a cape entering the sea, is twenty-five miles; on its end toward the east stands the city of Hintuna, a populous city, into which the river Awnastra flows on its eastern side {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.945}. Awnastra is an inland town; between Hintuna and Awnastra are eighty miles, and from Awnastra to Salabars forty miles westward; the river Awnastra issues from a mountain lying across the middle of the island {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.945}. From Hintuna to the city of Sharham is sixty miles, it lying on the neck of the sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.945}. Hintuna thus appears in his scheme as a populous coastal city in the south-east of the island of England, at the mouth of the river Awnastra on a cape projecting into the sea between Ghurham and Sharham.
