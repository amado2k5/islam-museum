## Overview
Homs is a city in Syria on the Orontes river, one of the districts of [[place/damascus|Damascus]] according to the entity data. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

After the conquest of Damascus, its people made a peace agreement with Abu Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah for safety for their persons, property, city wall, churches and mills. A quarter of the Church of John was excepted for the mosque, in return for one hundred and seventy thousand dinars. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

When Heraclius gathered his army for Yarmuk, the Muslims returned to its people the tribute they had taken from them. Its people chose to be governed by the Muslims, and they shut its gates against the army of Heraclius; its Jews did the same. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Then it and Qinnasrin became one military district. Marwan ibn Muhammad demolished its wall, because they had disagreed with him. Al-Mu'tasim ordered its stone paving removed in the days of al-Fadl ibn Qarin, then its people rebelled, killed him and crucified him. Musa ibn Bugha al-Kabir took it by force in the year 250 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Homs as an old, large and walled city, with a strong fortress on a high hill at its southern end, halfway between Damascus and Aleppo; he notes that the name is used with both feminine and masculine forms. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.302}

He gives the builder as a man called Homs ibn al-Mahr according to some, and Homs ibn Mukannaf al-Amaliqi according to others. Philologists, he reports, derive the name from «the wound was swollen» (hamasa), that is, when the swelling goes down. For latitude he cites two tables: one gives 33 degrees and two-thirds, the other 34 degrees and 45 minutes, both in the fourth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.302}

In his account of the conquest, Khalid ibn al-Walid and Malhan ibn Zayyar al-Ta'i go ahead of Abu Ubayda after Damascus; the people of Homs fight them, then take refuge in the city and ask for safe-conduct. Yaqut also gives al-Waqidi's report that a large enemy cavalry came to the gates of Damascus and was met by Muslims between Bayt Lahya and al-Thaniyya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.302}
