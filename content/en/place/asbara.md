## Summary
Asbara was a city partly mountainous and partly plain, among the most pleasant of the lands of Farghana, with lofty walls, fine districts, many merchants, and a large prosperous suburb holding its markets, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.507}

## Details
Among the districts of Farghana al-Idrisi lists two plains of meadow without mountains, and among them Asbara, mountainous and level together; its towns include Tamakhsh, Bamkakhsh and Qaba, and it is among the most agreeable of Farghana's lands {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.507}. He estimates Asbara as the equal of Akhsikath: a city of lofty walls and fine quarters, with many merchants, builders, travellers and envoys, rich in blessings and gathering every kind of good; it has a large flourishing suburb in which its markets stand, and a fine wall surrounds both suburb and city {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.507}. It has many running waters on which stand gardens, orchards, adorned parks, buildings and pleasure grounds, and a large populated rustaq of many villages reaching to the river of Shash, about one stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.507}. He thus drew it as a complete commercial city on the edge of Farghana.
