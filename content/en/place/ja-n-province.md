## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Jayyan is a city with a wide district in al-Andalus, about seventeen farsakhs east of Cordoba, with major hadith scholars attributed to it; Yaqut also lists a second Jayyan, a village near Isfahan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.195}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling, a fatha on the jim, a doubled ya and a final nun, and calls it a city with a wide district in al-Andalus that joins the district of Elvira, lying off from it toward the north, east of Cordoba, seventeen farsakhs from that city. He calls it a large district that gathers many villages and towns which he lists in their own places, and says it joins the districts of Tudmir and Toledo. He thus describes the city and its region together. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.195}

## People
Yaqut attributes many scholars to it. Among them is al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Ghassani, called al-Jayyani, who he says was not from there; his father settled there in the time of the civil strife and the family came from al-Zahra. He was the leading hadith scholar of Cordoba and wrote a book on the narrators of the two Sahihs titled Taqyid al-muhmal wa tamyiz al-mushkil; Yaqut, on the authority of Ibn Bashkuwal, gives his birth as Muharram 427 and his death as Sha'ban 498. Among the later ones is Abu al-Hajjaj Yusuf ibn Muhammad ibn Faruwa al-Jayyani al-Andalusi, who travelled east, reached Khurasan and settled in Balkh; he was born at Jayyan in 499 and died at Balkh in 545. The years are Hijri. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.195}

## Jayyan near Isfahan
Yaqut notes that Jayyan is also the name of a village of the Isfahan region in the district of Qahab. He quotes Ibn al-Najjar that near it stands a well-known shrine called the Mashhad of Salman al-Farisi, which people visit, and that he entered the village and visited it. From Hibat Allah ibn Abd al-Warith al-Shirazi he reports that Salman returned to Isfahan when it was conquered and built a mosque in his village of Jayyan, which is still known. He attributes to Jayyan of Isfahan Abu al-Haytham Talha ibn al-A'lam al-Hanafi, who narrated from al-Sha'bi and from whom al-Thawri narrated. This is a different place from Jayyan in al-Andalus and is not part of this page. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.195} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.196}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record lists Jaén as a province of Spain. That is the museum's reading: Yaqut describes a city and its district, not a province within modern boundaries, and the link between the two is the museum's.
