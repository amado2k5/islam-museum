## Summary
Dhu Suhaym of Khawlan is a fortified stronghold in the land of Tihama, which al-Idrisi makes the end point of the road from Mecca to Khawlan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the road from Mecca to Dhu Suhaym of Khawlan leaves Mecca for Malkan, a watering-place where travellers halt {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}. He names the stages of this road: Yalamlam, a mountain barring the way from east to west where the miqat of the people of Tihama lies; then a halting-place in the desert with a spring; then Qayna, a small village with two wells; then Dawqa and Alib, two populated villages; then al-Hasba {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}. After Haran al-Qarin he states that from it one reaches Khawlan Dhu Suhaym, which is a fortified stronghold whose people possess protection and might {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}. He explains that all these lands he has mentioned lie in Tihama, and that Tihama is a part of Yemen: a range of interlocking mountains beginning at the Sea of Qulzum and overlooking it {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}.
