## Summary
Kuyaba is named by al-Idrisi as the city of the Turks called Rus; it lay eight stages from the city of Slawa in the direction of the land of Bulgar. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says the Rus were of three kinds: one tribe called the Braws, whose king resided in Kuyaba; another called the Slawiyya, whose king lived in Slawa, a town on a mountaintop; and a third called the Arthaniyya, whose king stayed at Artha. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917} Artha was a fine town on a fortified mountain, situated between Slawa and Kuyaba; from Kuyaba to Artha was four stages and from Artha to Slawa four days. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918} Muslim merchants, he adds, reached Kuyaba from Armenia, showing its link to trade routes. Al-Shaykh al-Hawqali relates of Artha that no stranger enters it, for its people kill every outsider who reaches them, and none dares enter their land. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}
