## Summary
Baja was the city holding the palace of the king called the Baghbugh, his treasuries and the safeguarded residence of his family; it stands on the bank of the river Khamdan, four stages from Isnakhwa, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.212}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of Isnakhwa to Baja is four stages, and that it is the city of the king called the Baghbugh, containing his royal palace, the quarters of his men, the treasuries of his wealth and the guarded residence of his womenfolk and family {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.212}. This city stands on the bank of the river Khamdan, up which boats ascend to it from Khanfu, Khanku and other famous ports of China, and from Baja to the city of Shadhkhu is four stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.213}. From the city of Qasha to Baja is ten stages, and likewise from the city of Sharkhiya to Baja ten stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.213}. He thus depicted it as the seat of the Chinese king and his treasuries on a great river navigable from China's harbours.
