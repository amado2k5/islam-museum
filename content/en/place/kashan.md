## Overview
Kashan is an Iranian city on the desert's edge. Yaqut al-Hamawi writes it Qashan in his Mu'jam al-buldan and calls it a city near [[place/isfahan|Isfahan]], mentioned together with Qum, from which the Qashani glazed pottery (al-ghada'ir) is exported, adding that the common people say al-Qashi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 p.296} He states that all its people in his time were Imami Shi'a, and he quotes the littérateur Abu al-'Abbas Ahmad ibn 'Ali ibn Baba al-Qashi, who came to Merv, settled there and died after 500 AH, from a book he wrote on the Shi'a sects, which describes a group of 'Alids in his homeland who await the Qa'im every morning; Ibn Baba calls this and similar things the dreams of those whose minds are spoiled. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 p.296} That is Yaqut's report of Ibn Baba's description and judgement of a group, given in his voice and not the museum's; Sunni and other views of the sects are treated on the pages for those sects, not here.

Yaqut also records a satirical poem by Ibn al-Habbariyya against Qashan and several other cities of the Jibal, which is literature and not a description of the town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 pp.296-297} He gives the distances: twelve farsakhs between Qum and Qashan, three stages between Qashan and Isfahan and four stages from Qashan to Ardistan, and says it has large, fearsome black scorpions. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 p.297} He lists a group of scholars named after it, among them Abu Muhammad Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Qashani al-Razi, from whom Abu Sahl Harun ibn Ahmad al-Astarabadhi narrated and whose words a number of Isfahan people wrote down. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 p.297}
