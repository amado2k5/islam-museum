## Overview
Qarmasin is a city in the mountains. Al-Baladhuri relates that Jarir ibn Abd Allah conquered it in the same manner as he conquered Hulwan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed: the date is not established, and the page recorded in the extraction data is 423.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut has two entries close in spelling, and they must be kept apart. The first is "Qarmasin", with a fatha, a sukun, an alif and then a kasra on the sin, a silent ya and nun; in it he quotes al-Umrani that it is a place eight farsakhs from al-Zubaydiyya, says he thinks it is on the road to Mecca, and states plainly that it is not Qirmisin near Hamadan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.330} The second is "Qirmisin", with a fatha, a sukun, a kasra on the mim, a ya, a kasra on the sin, a silent ya and nun, which he says is the arabicisation of "Kirman Shahan", a known city thirty farsakhs from Hamadan near al-Dinawar, between Hamadan and Hulwan on the pilgrims' high road. He quotes Ibn al-Faqih that Qubadh ibn Fayruz surveyed his lands and found no spot on the road between al-Mada'in and Balkh more pleasant, nor with sweeter water and air, than from Qirmisin to the ascent of Hamadan, so he founded it and built himself a residence set on a thousand vines; that it holds Qasr Shirin and the arch carved with the figures of Shabdiz, Parviz's horse, and of Shirin, his concubine; and that it holds the platform around which the kings of the earth gathered before Khusraw Parviz, among them Faghfur king of China, Khaqan king of the Turks, Dahir king of India and Caesar king of Rome, a square platform a hundred cubits each way of dressed stone fastened with iron nails. He names Abu Bakr Umar ibn Sahl ibn Isma'il ibn Ja'd, the hafiz al-Qirmisini al-Dinawari, nicknamed Kaddu, who came to Hamadan in 317 and again in 329 on the authority of Shirawayh whom Yaqut quotes, who is reported to have been reliable, truthful and a good memoriser, and who died in 330. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.330-331}

## The museum's identification
The record's spelling "Qarmasin" is the spelling of Yaqut's first entry, which in his hands is a different place on the road to Mecca and not Qirmisin near Hamadan, so nothing in that first entry may be attached to it. The museum reads what the record has from al-Baladhuri, Jarir ibn Abdallah's taking of a city of the Jibal on the same terms as Hulwan, as Qirmisin; that is the museum's reading, resting on a city of the Jibal not being a halt on the Mecca road; Yaqut does not mention the conquest in the two entries, but he does under Hulwan: Jarir took Hulwan by treaty, went on towards al-Dinawar without taking it, and 'took Qirmisin on the same terms on which he took Hulwan' (vol 2 p.291). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.291} That supports the museum's reading, which then rests on Yaqut's own text; he does not say that this is the record's Qarmasin. The museum measured that the record's point (34.3142, 47.065) is about 143 km from its Hamadan point, whereas the thirty farsakhs Yaqut gives come to about 180 km at six kilometres to the farsakh, a larger figure, as is to be expected when a straight line is set against a road count, and about 46 km from its Dinawar point (34.5892, 47.4394), which fits "near al-Dinawar". The record itself says the date is not fixed, and what Yaqut has on Qubadh and Ibn al-Faqih is his report, not a documented date.
