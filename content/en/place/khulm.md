## Summary
Khulm is a handsome town of abundant good things, full of blessings, with farms, running waters and many advantages; it lies two days from Balkh and two days from Warwaliz. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.484}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says: whoever wishes goes from Balkh to Khulm in two days, and Khulm lies west of Warwaliz with two days between them {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.484}. It is a handsome town of abundant good things, full of blessings, with farms, running waters and many advantages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.484}. From Khulm to Samangan is two days; Samangan lies west of al-Tabaqan, two stages from it, and is itself a handsome, inhabited town populous with merchants, folk and notables, with an earthen wall {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.484}. From Samangan to Andaraba is five days; it is a town on a mountain slope from which is gathered the silver of Jarbaya and Panjhir {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.484}. As for al-Tabaqan, it is a populous, inhabited town a quarter the size of Balkh, with a mud wall, buildings of clay and gypsum, a great river, vineyards, markets, brisk trade and many crafts {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.484}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name Khulm with a damma and a quiet lam, and notes that in Arabic {khulm} means the fat of a sheep's omentum and also friends. For the place he gives a town in the district of Balkh, ten farsakhs from it, a land of Arabs where the Asad, Banu Tamim and Qays settled in the days of the conquests. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.385} He calls it a small city with villages, orchards, rural districts and ravines, rich in crops, and says the wind hardly ever drops there, night or day, in summer. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.385} Among those named for it he lists Abu al-Awja Sa'id ibn Sa'id al-Khulmi, known as Sa'idan, who transmitted from Sulayman al-Taymi, and Uthman ibn Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Khalili al-Khulmi, Abu Amr, a jurist, mufti and debater who served as preacher in Balkh and became its Shaykh al-Islam; he gave a licence to Abu Sa'd in Dhu al-Qa'da 529. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.385}
