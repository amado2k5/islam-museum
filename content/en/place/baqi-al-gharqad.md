## Overview
al-Baqi al-Gharqad: the great cemetery of the city, east of the Prophet's mosque, in [[place/medina|Medina]]. Asad ibn Zurara was the first of the Muslims buried in it, according to the report of Abu Hatim transmitted by Razin, and Uthman ibn Maz'un was the first of the emigrants. Al-Samhudi mentions among its virtues that seventy thousand of its dead are sent to Paradise without reckoning, and that prayers are answered at the corner of the house of Aqil beside it. Inside it there was a mosque of Banu Hudayla on the right of whoever enters by its gate. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.91} {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.271}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut explains that al-baqi' in the language is a place where roots of many kinds of trees grow, and that the Baqi' al-Gharqad takes its name from the gharqad, a large thorny shrub. He identifies it as the cemetery of the people of Medina, inside the city. He records an elegy in which a member of his tribe describes a group that shut the door of their own garden during a feud and killed one another behind it. He also lists other places called baqi' in Medina: the Baqi' of al-Zubayr, with houses and dwellings, and the Baqi' of the horses beside the house of Zayd ibn Thabit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.473}

In his fourth volume the entry is shorter and states the same identification: the cemetery of the people of Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.194}
