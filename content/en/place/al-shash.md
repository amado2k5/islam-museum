## Overview
al-Shash: a land beyond the Oxus river, whose ruler was titled Tarbend. Qutayba conquered most of it and reached Asbijab. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Asbijab
The fortress of Asbijab was conquered in ancient times; then the Turks prevailed over it, with a group of the people of al-Shash alongside them. Then Nuh ibn Asad conquered it in the caliphate of al-Mu'tasim, and built around it a wall that enclosed its people's vineyards and fields. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut quotes Ptolemy's coordinates for al-Shash: longitude one hundred and twenty-four degrees, latitude forty-five, in the sixth climate. He then cites al-Istakhri, who says that al-Shash and Iylaq form one connected district with no difference between them, and that its breadth is a journey of two or three days. He adds that no district of Khurasan or Transoxiana has more pulpits or more villages and settlements of that size. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.308-309}

Al-Shash is the greatest frontier post against the Turks. Its border reaches the Shash valley, which flows into the lake of Khwarazm, and the Turks' houses there are wide and built of clay, most with water running through them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.309}

Its chief city is Binkath, and it has many towns. Yaqut reports that all of them lay in ruins in his own day: the Khwarazm Shah Muhammad ibn Tukush destroyed them, killed their kings and drove out their people, leaving the houses, trees and rivers empty, and Yaqut counts it a breach in the Muslim lands that could never be mended. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.309}
