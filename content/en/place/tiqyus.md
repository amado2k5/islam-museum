## Summary
Tiqyus is a fine town about twenty miles from al-Hamma, with tender vegetables and crops of henna, cumin and coriander, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.277}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Tiqyus after al-Hamma. Between al-Hamma and Tiqyus is a small stage. He says the water of al-Hamma is not good, but it is drinkable, and its people are content with it; there are many palms and abundant dates there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.277}

From al-Hamma to Tiqyus is about twenty miles. He calls it a fine town lying between al-Hamma and Qafsa, a populous place with crops of henna, cumin and coriander, good palms and dates, and very fine tender vegetables. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.277}

From Tiqyus to the city of Qafsa is one stage. He calls Qafsa a fine town with a wall. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.277}

Al-Idrisi does not give the population or the markets of Tiqyus here. The entry records its crops, its palms and its place on the road. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.277}
