## Summary
Kalkisar was a populous Indian coastal town, close to Danji, both rich in rice and grain, and a landfall for the ships making for Sarandib from the east. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.192}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Findarina to the city of Jarbat is five stages; it is a populous city on a small inlet, a land of much rice and many grains, and it is said that from them comes the provision of Sarandib, and in its mountains the pepper tree grows abundantly {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.192}. From Jarbat to Danji and Kalkisar is a journey of two days; they are two cities on the sea, populous and close together, with rice and many grains {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.192}. From them to Kalkiyan is a day, and from Kalkiyan to al-Lulwa and Kanja a journey of two days, in which are rice and wheat, and in whose land the baqam plant grows abundantly, with coconut palms and much fruit {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.192}. He notes that one leaving the island of Sarandib who wishes makes for her nearest mainland, the land of the city of Jarbat, while if one takes the eastward incline then one's landfall is the city of Kalkisar or one reaches the end of Mount al-Amra {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.198}. Kalkisar thus appears in his scheme as a populous sea city on the coast of India between Jarbat and Kalkiyan, a source of rice and grain and a destination of the Sarandib ships.
