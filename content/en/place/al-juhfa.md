## Overview
al-Juhfa: a land between Mecca and Medina. al-Mas'udi reports that Ubayl ibn Awas ibn Aram ibn Sam ibn Nuh settled there with his children and those who followed him, and that they perished in a flood. The place was named al-Juhfa because the flood swept them away. Later people of Banu Hanifa from Yamama settled there and made it their home. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.457-458}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as al-Juhfa, with a damma on the jim, a sukun and a fa'. He calls it a large village with a pulpit on the road from Mecca to Medina, at four stages in his own words, a phrase that does not say where the count starts (the museum reads it as from Mecca); he then quotes al-Sukkari for three stages from Mecca and for its being the first of the Ghawr on the way to Mecca and, in a phrase hard to pin down, likewise from the other side toward Dhat 'Irq. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} It is the miqat of the people of Egypt and Syria who do not pass through Medina; those who do have Dhu al-Hulayfa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} Its name was Mahyaʿa, and it was called al-Juhfa because a flood swept (ijtahafa) it and carried off its people in some year; in his time it was in ruins. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} He gives distances: about three stages to the coast at al-Jar, six stages to Medina, and two miles to Ghadir Khumm; of Aqran he says six miles, but his wording, "Aqran, a place from the sea", leaves the meaning open, and his own entry for Aqran holds only a line of verse and no position. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.236} In the Khumm entry he gives a view that the pool is three miles from al-Juhfa and quotes 'Arram for one mile; the word he uses, here rendered "short of", could also mean "below" or "beyond". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.389} His Mahyaʿa entry says it is al-Juhfa or, by another report, a place near it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.235}

He reports from al-Kalbi that the Amalekites drove out Banu 'Aqil, whom he calls brothers of 'Ad ibn Rabb, and that they settled al-Juhfa, then called Mahyaʿa, until a flood took them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} He then gives two reports about the Prophet and the fever of Medina: a prayer, on arriving there, that its fever be moved to al-Juhfa, which runs on from al-Kalbi's passage with no mark of where his words end, so its reporter is open, and a night when he woke from a doze and said the fever had passed him in the form of a dishevelled woman going to al-Juhfa, which Yaqut introduces with "it is reported"; neither is given with a chain. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} He describes a verse of Jarir who drops the final ha' and counts the place among the Ghawr. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.111} Other entries place Rabigh between al-Juhfa and Waddan (Ibn al-Sikkit) and, for the valley of Rabigh, ten miles from it (al-Waqidi), al-Abwa' twenty-three miles from it, and Waddan a stage from it (Abu Zayd). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.11} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.79} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.365}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Mas'udi, has a land between Mecca and Medina named for the flood that swept away those who first settled it, with people of Banu Hanifa living there afterwards. Yaqut agrees on the reason for the name and on the position between the two cities, and adds that it was a village with a pulpit, later in ruins, that its old name was Mahyaʿa and that it is a miqat. He differs on who came first: in al-Kalbi's account, as Yaqut reports it, Banu 'Aqil after the Amalekites drove them out, which is not what the record says, and he has nothing on Banu Hanifa. The record's point (22.7156, 39.1464) is marked as a site and lies 14.7 km from the museum's Rabigh point, 14.0 km from its Ghadir Khumm point, 43.9 km from al-Abwa', 159.9 km from Mecca and 200.7 km from Medina; these are straight-line figures from the museum's own points and are not set against Yaqut's stages and miles, whose units vary. The museum's Ghadir Khumm point is marked approximate, so the museum does not settle the gap between it and Yaqut's two miles.
