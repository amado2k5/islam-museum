## Overview
Arafat: a wide plain outside [[place/mecca|Mecca]], where pilgrims stand on the day of Arafat. al-Azraqi reports that it was so named because Gabriel said to [[person/ibrahim|Ibrahim]]: "Have you come to know your rites?" In its middle is the mount of al-Mashash, and behind it is the mount of Kabkab. {cite:azraqi-akhbar-makka}

## For Sprouts
Arafat is a plain outside Mecca where the pilgrims stand on the ninth day of Dhu al-Hijja; there they hear the sermon and pray to God until sunset. The Prophet said that standing at Arafat is the Hajj itself, so whoever does not stand there has no Hajj.

## For Explorers
Arafat is a plain about twenty kilometres east of Mecca, outside the sacred boundary, where the pilgrims stand on the ninth of Dhu al-Hijja from noon to sunset; they hear the sermon, pray and supplicate there. The Prophet said 'Hajj is Arafat' — standing there is a pillar without which the Hajj is invalid. Among its landmarks is the small Mount of Mercy, linked in people's telling to Adam and Eve; its plain holds millions of pilgrims, who move on by night to Muzdalifa then Mina. White boundary markers separate it from the sacred zone.

## For Scholars
Arafat is a plain some twenty kilometres east of Mecca outside the sacred boundary, the site of the standing on the ninth of Dhu al-Hijja. The hadiths establish that 'Hajj is Arafat' — the standing is the supreme pillar without which there is no Hajj — and the Prophet stood there in the Farewell Pilgrimage and delivered the famous sermon; the sira books record that the verse 'Today I have perfected your religion' came down there. The schools fix the standing's time from noon of Arafa to dawn of sacrifice day, most allowing a moment of presence at night for the weak. Among its landmarks is the Mount of Mercy at its eastern edge, tied in tradition to Adam and Eve's reunion, and white boundary signs marking that it lies outside the sanctuary so pilgrims and non-pilgrims may both stand there. After sunset the crowds move to Muzdalifa then Mina, and state services steer millions within hours.

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
Episode 20 of [[work/fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah|Fi rihab al-hadith al-qudsi]] explains the hadith of God boasting to the angels of the people of 'Arafat, who have come weary and covered in dust. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:00:00} The presenter says that three things come together at 'Arafat: one dress, in ihram; a place without which the hajj is not complete; and a time fixed by God. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:04:30} He mentions al-Tirmidhi's report that the best supplication is that of the day of 'Arafa, and Muslim's that there is no day on which God frees more people from the Fire than the day of 'Arafa. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:12:30} He says 'Arafat is the mountain beside which God created Adam, relying on the hadith of the covenant at Na'man. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:23:30} (needs_source: the presenter's statement.)

[[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] says that boasting happens when the one boasted to is made to witness the greatness of what is boasted of, as if he had objected, and it was the angels who asked whether God would place on earth one who would corrupt it. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:15:00} He holds that the pilgrim is the only one called by the title of his worship: no one says "O one who prays" or "O Muslim", but people say "O Hajj", because the title shows he has completed the elements of Islam, being able in body and wealth to make the pilgrimage and so able to pay zakat and to pray. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:17:00} He says the pilgrim takes off the suit that marked his rank and wears what others wear, and learns courtesy with flowers, trees and everything in existence. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:19:30} He tells of a village headman who tested his son on caring for people, to say that whoever carries the burdens of a group is its master. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:20:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:20:30} For him tawaf and sa'y may be done outside ihram, but at 'Arafat ihram is required, to complete one's stripping oneself before God. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep20 00:23:00}
