## Summary
Amman was a city in the Balqa country east of the Jordan, which al-Idrisi places at two days and part of a day from Jerusalem. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.363}

## Details
In his table of Syrian distances al-Idrisi states that from Jerusalem to Amman and the Balqa is two days and part of a day {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.363}. The city appears in his survey of the Jordan district and the measure of the roads from the Holy City to the towns east of the river; for him Amman is the city of the Balqa reached in roughly two days' march from Jerusalem {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.363}.  Its place in the gazetteer thus rests on its role as the Balqa chief town, fixed in the geographical tradition by distances rather than by monuments.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives Amman with a fatha, then a doubled mim and a final nun, and offers two readings of its form: from amma, so that it is not declined as a proper name but is as a common noun, or from amana, so that it is declined in both cases. He calls it a town at the edge of Syria that was the chief town of the land of al-Balqa, says the most common reading of the Hadith of the Pool is this Amman, as al-Khattabi fixed it, and notes that al-Tirmidhi has the wording from Aden to Amman al-Balqa; he takes this Syrian al-Balqa to be meant because it is named alongside Adhruh, al-Jarba and Ayla. He relays a view that it is the city of Daqyanus, with the Cave and al-Raqim near it, known to the people of that land, and refers to other views. He also gives a report, which he says he read of a Jew reading in some scripture, that Lot's daughters bore a son named Amman and another named Moab, each of whom built a city in Syria after himself, the two lying close together in the Syrian steppe. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.151}
