## Overview
Qasiyun is the mountain that overlooks Damascus, with the cemetery of the righteous at its foot. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan along with what people say about its caves. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.295}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a fatha on the first letter, an unpointed sin, a ya with damma and a final nun, and says it is the mountain overlooking the city of Damascus, with a number of caves and grottoes in which are traces of the prophets, and at its foot a cemetery of righteous people. He calls it a revered and sacred mountain about which traditions are told and pious people have reports. He quotes verses by Muhy al-Din Abu Hamid al-Shahrazuri, mourning Kamal al-Din, chief judge of Syria, who died in Damascus in 572, in which the foot of Qasiyun is mentioned. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.295}

He then gives what people claim: a cave called the Cave of Blood, where Cain is said to have killed his brother Abel, with a bloodlike mark that they say is his blood, still dry there, and a stone lying about that they say is the one used to strike his head; and a Cave of Hunger in which they say forty prophets died. These are reports Yaqut passes on from the people and does not confirm. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.296}
