## Overview
Rhodes is an island which, according to al-Baladhuri, is among the most fertile of the islands, about sixty miles in extent, with olives, vines, fruits and fresh water. He mentions that Junada ibn Abi Umayya conquered it in the year 53 AH, and that the Muslims stayed in it for seven years in a fortress, until Yazid ordered it demolished and they returned. Mujahid ibn Jabr was residing there, teaching the recitation of the Qur'an. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.330}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Rudis and vowels it on al-Qadi 'Iyad's authority with a damma on the first letter and a kasra on the dal, noting the transmitters' variants on the fatha of its ra', the vowel of its dal, and its sin — given by one chain as a shin. It is an island in the lands of the Byzantines, and in the hadith Mu'awiya raided Cyprus and Rudis; it lies in the fourth clime, longitude 50 degrees from the west, latitude 35 and a half degrees; it is an island opposite Alexandria, a night's sail away, and it is the first of the lands of the Franks. Al-Mas'udi said — in his time, year 332 — that the island was the Byzantines' dockyard where seagoing ships were built and where many Byzantines lived, whose ships used to draw near Alexandria and the other coasts of Egypt to raid and take captives. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.78}

## The museum's identification
The record's Rudis is the island of Rhodes, and it is Yaqut's island opposite Alexandria "a night away at sea" (about 602.8 km between the museum's Rhodes and Alexandria points); his raid of Mu'awiya on it with Cyprus agrees with the record's al-Baladhuri strand, and al-Mas'udi's description of it as a Byzantine dockyard is added report with a stated date (332 AH).
