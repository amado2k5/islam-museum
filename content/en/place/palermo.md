## Overview
Palermo is a city on the island of Sicily. Ibn Jubayr's Rihla calls it "the seat of Sicily" and describes it as the seat of the island's king in his time. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p.93}

## The conquest
Ibn Khaldun, in his account of the conquest of Sicily, says that the Muslim army "besieged Balarm", and then that "the Muslims opened the city of Balarm". The lines read here do not state the year of that conquest. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1026}

## The city in Ibn Jubayr's eyes
Ibn Jubayr describes the city's building as resembling Cordoba's, says that its open squares and plains are all orchards, and mentions the palaces the king adorned there. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p.93}

## Muslims in the city
Ibn Jubayr reports that the Muslims had quarters of their own, apart from the Christians, and that most of their mosques were kept in use for prayer with the call to prayer sounded aloud. They had a qadi to whom they referred their cases, and a congregational mosque, and most of their mosques were used for teaching the Qur'an. They had no Friday prayer, because the sermon was forbidden to them, so they prayed the feasts with a sermon in which the Abbasid caliph was prayed for. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p.92}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Pan(h)ormus» (Pleiades no. 462410), classed as settlement and modern settlement, with activity attested from 750 BCE to 2100 CE. Its recorded names include Panhormus, Panormus and Palermo. It says the city was founded by the Phoenicians in 734 BCE as Ziz and was an important city in Greek, Roman and Arabic times. {cite:pleiades 462410}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer of the German Archaeological Institute records this place as no. 2359599, of type populated place, with the preferred name «Palermo», placed in its hierarchy under «Palermo (Città metropolitana)». Recorded names include Palermo. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2359599}
