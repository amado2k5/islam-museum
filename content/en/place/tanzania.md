## Summary
Country in East Africa; about a third of its mainland people are Muslim. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2331}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
In the Dictionary's figures, mainland Tanzania is Christian 45 percent, Muslim 35 percent, and a fifth attached to indigenous religions. Trade carried Islam inland from the coast, and the Sufi brotherhoods spread it broadly in the twentieth century: it reports some 70 percent of the country's Sunnis affiliated with orders, the Qadiriyya alone holding 75 percent of all brotherhood membership, with the Shadhili, Askari, Ahmadi-Dandarawi and Rifai orders also active. The union with Zanzibar followed independence in 1961, and the island keeps limited self-rule today. The entry ends by noting friction between a pro-capitalist, West-leaning government and the African Muslim community amid economic strain. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2331}
