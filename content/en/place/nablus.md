## Overview
Nablus is the city of the Samaritans. In it is the well at which, according to the people of Jerusalem, Isa sat and asked the Samaritan woman for water. It lies one day from [[place/ramla|Ramla]], according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.356}

Al-Maqdisi describes it as called "Lesser Damascus", abundant in olives, in a valley pressed between two mountains. Its market runs from gate to gate, its congregational mosque in the middle is paved and clean, and its people have wondrous underground chambers. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p.142}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name a folk explanation told by a learned man of Nablus. A great snake lived in a valley there, which the people called lis; they killed it, took out its fang and hung it on the city gate, and the phrase naab lis, the snake's fang, became the name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.248}

He describes Nablus as a well-known city in Palestine between two long mountains, with many waters and a stony soil, ten farsakhs from Jerusalem. Near it is a mountain where Adam is said to have prostrated, and a mountain that the Jews believe was the place of the sacrifice; Yaqut says the Samaritans pray toward it, and that a spring under a cave there is revered. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.248}
