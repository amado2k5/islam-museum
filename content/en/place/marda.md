## Overview
Marda was the seat of a kingdom. It has remarkable ruins of building, an arch and a water channel, and a palace called the House of Cooking. Al-Idrisi describes it as speaking of kingship and power. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.545}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Marida" and says its form is the feminine of the headword before it, "Marid". He calls it a wide kura of al-Andalus adjoining the hawz of Farish, "between the west and the north", among the districts of Cordoba, and one of the capitals that the Caesars and the Romans chose to live in. In his words it is a fine city with much marble and high buildings, with handsome ancient remains that people go to see and marvel at; it is six days from Cordoba, and it has fortresses and villages he describes under their own names. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.38} Several men of learning and transmission are named from it, and he lists Sulayman ibn Quraysh ibn Sulayman, Abu Abdallah, whose family came from Marida and who settled in Cordoba; he heard from Ibn Waddah and others, travelled and heard in Mecca from Ali ibn Abd al-Aziz the books of Abu Ubayd, went into Yemen, and was made judge at Batalyus by a Marwan whom the entry does not identify further; he then returned to Cordoba, where people heard from him, Yaqut calls him reliable and says he died there in Muharram 329. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.38-39} Under Batalyus he calls that a large city of the districts of Marida, on the river Ana, west of Cordoba. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.447} The next headword in the book is Mardin, another fortress, on a peak of the Jazira overlooking Dunaysir, Dara and Nusaybin; he draws no link between it and Marida of al-Andalus. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.39}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut does not say that Marida is the modern city of Mérida and gives no coordinates. The museum's link rests on the Arabic name, on al-Idrisi's description of a city of the same name with ruins, a bridge and a palace, on the Wikidata identifier Q14323 recorded for the place, and on the record's point (38.916, -6.344). Yaqut's account of a capital chosen by the Caesars and the Romans, rich in marble and ancient remains, fits a Roman city, but the museum reads that as a fit and does not count it as independent proof. Yaqut and al-Idrisi agree that it is a city with ancient remains worth a visit; al-Idrisi adds the bridge and the palace called the House of Cooking, which Yaqut does not mention, and Yaqut adds the scholars named from it and the bounds he gives its kura, which al-Idrisi does not. The six days to Cordoba have not been used to test the coordinates: the museum's two points are about 178 km apart in a straight line, and Yaqut does not say what rate of travel he means. The hawz of Farish is not placed by the museum.
