## Overview
al-Qatul: a place on a river that draws from the Tigris. There was a village in it inhabited by a people of the Jaramiqa and some people of the Nabat. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.444-445}

## Al-Mu'tasim at al-Qatul
al-Mas'udi reports that al-Mu'tasim first settled at al-Baradhan, but did not find its air healthy, and so he moved until he reached al-Qatul. He built a palace there, and people moved to it from Madinat al-Salam. Then he was harmed by it and resolved on Samarra. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.444-445}

al-Mas'udi quotes the verse of Ayyar in satire of that move: "O dweller of al-Qatul among the Jaramiqa, you left the patriarchs of Baghdad the rams." {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.444-445}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Qatul, fa'ul from qatl (cutting): the name of a river as though cut off from the Tigris — a river that lay on the site of Samarra before it was built. Al-Rashid, or whoever dug this river, built at its mouth a palace called Abu al-Jund for the many lands it watered, and made it rations for his troops; it is also said that at Samarra he erected a building on it which he gave to Ashnas the Turk, his client, then moved to Samarra and brought the people over — as told in the Samarra entry. Above this Qatul is the "Kisrawi Qatul" dug by Kisra Anushirwan the Just, drawing from the eastern side of the Tigris, with a shadhrawan on it watering the rastaq between the rivers of the Buzurjsabur districts; and after it al-Rashid dug another Qatul below it on the Baghdad side, emptying into the Nahrawan below the shadhrawan. He quotes verses of Jahza al-Barmaki naming the Qatul and the neighbouring Qadisiyya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.297} In his Dujayl entry he says Dujayl is a river between the Qatul and Baghdad from which Baghdad floods whenever it floods, and that when Kisra dug the Qatul he harmed the people of the lowlands by cutting their water until they grew poor, so a channel was made for them below the Qatul toward al-Quraj and their lands flourished. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.412}

## The museum's identification
For Yaqut al-Qatul is first the river, then the site on it; the record's account of al-Mu'tasim's stay and palace comes from al-Mas'udi, while Yaqut credits the building at its mouth to al-Rashid in one saying and to Ashnas the Turk in another, and does not name al-Mu'tasim in this entry — two reports each under its own transmitter. The museum's al-Qatul point lies about 9.2 km from its Samarra point, close to Yaqut's "on the site of Samarra before it was built."
