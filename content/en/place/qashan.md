## Overview
Qashan is a city in the mountains. Al-Baladhuri relates that [[person/al-ahnaf-ibn-qays|al-Ahnaf ibn Qays]], who is al-Dahhak ibn Qays al-Tamimi, conquered it by force as an envoy of Abu Musa. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed: the date is not established.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a dotted shin and a final nun, and describes Qashan as a city near Isfahan, mentioned together with Qum, from which the clay vessels called al-Qashani ghada'ir are brought. He notes that ordinary people say al-Qashi, and says that, as he read, all its people are Imami Shi'a. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.296}

He gives the distances: twelve farsakhs between Qum and Qashan, three stages between Qashan and Isfahan, and four stages from Qashan to Ardistan. He says that Qashan has large black scorpions. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.297} Among its learned men he names Abu Muhammad Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Qashani al-Razi, from whom Abu Sahl Harun ibn Ahmad al-Astarabadhi narrated and a number of people of Isfahan wrote. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.297} He also quotes satirical verses by Ibn al-Habbariyya against it and several other towns of the Jibal. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.297}
