## Overview
Al-Arj: a stopping place on the migration road between [[place/quba|Quba]] and Mecca, mentioned by Ibn Ishaq in describing the migration route, according to the author of The Sealed Nectar. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

An additional source is needed: the exact site.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as al-'Arj, with a fatha on the 'ayn, a sukun and a jim, and lists views on the word: a large herd of camels (Abu Zayd), more than two hundred and nearing a thousand (Abu Hatim), about eighty (Ibn al-Sikkit). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.98} From Ibn al-Kalbi he gives a story that Tubba', returning from fighting the people of Medina toward Mecca, saw animals limping and named the place from that, and from Kuthayyir that one turns aside from the road to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.98} He then describes it as a large village in a valley in the districts of al-Ta'if, the home of the poet al-'Arji ('Abd Allah ibn 'Umar ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn 'Uthman ibn 'Affan), the first of Tihama, seventy-eight miles from Medina, in the land of Hudhayl, which is why a verse of Abu Dhu'ayb names it beside Hawazin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.98} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.99} He retells, on the authority of Ishaq, a story of an orchard of al-'Arji called al-'Arj among Banu Nasr ibn Mu'awiya, and refers to the Kitab al-Aghani. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.99} He quotes al-Asma'i in the Jazirat al-'Arab that a wadi called al-'Arj lies in the Ta'if districts and is not the al-'Arj between Mecca and Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.99}

He then adds, from al-Hazimi, that al-'Arj is also a pass between Mecca and Medina on the pilgrim road, named with al-Suqya, whose mountain joins the mountain of Lebanon, and that Yemen has an al-'Arj between al-Mahalib and al-Mahjam; he says he does not know which of these al-Qattal al-Kilabi meant in a verse. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.99} The entry on Rukuba calls it a hard pass between Mecca and Medina at al-'Arj which the Prophet took on his emigration, with Dhu al-Bijadayn with him; the entry on al-Ruwaytha says it is an evening halt between al-'Arj and al-Rawha'. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.64} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.105}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Mubarakfuri, has a halting place on the Hijra road between [[place/quba|Quba']] and Mecca, mentioned by Ibn Ishaq, and asks for a further source for the exact site. Yaqut folds several meanings into one entry that need separating: the "large village" in the Ta'if districts in Hudhayl's land, then the al-'Arj that he gives from al-Hazimi as a pass between Mecca and Medina on the pilgrim road, then the Yemeni one. Al-Asma'i, whom he quotes, says the Ta'if wadi is not the one between Mecca and Medina. The phrase "between it and Medina seventy-eight miles" stands in the description of the first, so it is open whether it belongs to the Ta'if al-'Arj or to the road one. The sense that fits the record is the road al-'Arj near Rukuba, and Yaqut's link of that pass to the Prophet's emigration supports the record's tie to the Hijra, though this is the museum's reading, not Yaqut's ruling. The record's point (23.8644, 39.1244) is marked as a locality, 83.6 km from the museum's Medina point, 81.4 km from its Quba' point and 280.9 km from its Mecca point; it is not set directly against the seventy-eight miles because the mile varies.
