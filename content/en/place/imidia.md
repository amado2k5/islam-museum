## Summary
Imidia is a fine populous city near the sea on the coastal road from Constantinople to the mouth of the Danube. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.896}

## Details
Al-Idrisi, describing the road from the city of Constantina along the coast to the mouth of the river Danube where the city of Maris stands, says: whoever takes it leaves Constantina for the city of Ayllughis, twenty-five miles, a city on a mountain rising twelve miles over the sea, where the king takes his pleasure once every year and stays many days hunting wild asses {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.896}. From Ayllughis to the city of Imidia is twenty-five miles — a fine populous city near the sea; thence to the city of Aghathublis twenty-five miles; thence to Basiliko on the seashore twenty-five miles; and from Basiliko to the city of Suzubli on the sea twenty-five miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.896}. Imidia is thus a thriving coastal halt on this road connected to Constantinople. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.896}
