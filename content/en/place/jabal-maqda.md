## Summary
Jabal Maqda is a mountain overlooking the city of Tripoli in the lands of the Maghrib, about three stages from the city. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}

## Details
Al-Idrisi, describing that district, says that its people's dwellings are in the beds of the valleys and on the tops of the mountains, that its water comes from rain collected in pools, that its wells are few, and that tribes of Berbers live upon it {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}. He states that above the city of Tripoli lies Jabal Maqda, three stages from it {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}. Jabal Maqda is thus a landmark of the interior in the Maghrib behind the city of Tripoli, in a tract where wells are scarce, the inhabitants depend on rainwater gathered in basins, and Berber tribes dwell — three days' journey from the city over which it stands, as al-Idrisi reports of the regions and routes of that land. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.298}
