## Summary
Rhodes is an island in the Mediterranean opposite Alexandria, about a night's sail from it, according to Yaqut al-Hamawi, who relays al-Mas'udi. This page sets out what the *Mu'jam al-buldan* says about the island's position and its place in early Muslim geography, and what it reports about the island in the fourth century AH. It is a geographical note, not a history of the island, and it reports Yaqut's words as he gives them.

## Its position in the geographers
Yaqut calls Rhodes "an island in the land of the Rum", places it in the fourth clime, and gives its longitude as fifty degrees from the west and its latitude as thirty-five and a half degrees. He says it lies opposite Alexandria, one night's sail away. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.78} In another entry he says that before Islam the first of the Byzantine lands on the Muslim side was the island of Rhodes, opposite Alexandria in the middle of the Syrian sea. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.228} Among the islands of the Mediterranean he lists it with Cyprus, Sicily and others. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.22}

## What al-Mas'udi reports
Yaqut quotes al-Mas'udi, who writes in 332 AH, that the island was then a shipbuilding yard of the Rum, where naval vessels were made and where many Byzantines lived; their ships came close to Alexandria and other Egyptian coasts and raided, took captives and seized goods. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.78} This is a fourth-century account of a naval base facing the Egyptian coast, copied in Yaqut and not checked against another source on these pages.

## A hadith
Yaqut, quoting the judge Iyad on how the name is vowelled, gives a hadith that says Muawiya raided Cyprus and Rhodes. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.78} The passage is about the spelling of the name; Yaqut gives no date or account of the raid, so this page draws nothing from it about the raid itself.

## Note on the source
The *Mu'jam al-buldan* is a seventh-century AH geographical dictionary that gathers the statements of geographers, traditionists and poets. Its information on Rhodes from the fourth century comes from al-Mas'udi as Yaqut gives it. No Byzantine or European source on the island itself was read for these pages, so the page stops at what Muslim geographical writing says about its position and role at sea.
