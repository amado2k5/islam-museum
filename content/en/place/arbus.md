## Overview
Arbus: a city on level land, with running springs that never dry up, the best known of which is the Spring of Rabah. al-Idrisi reports that its saffron rivals the Andalusian, and that it lies three stages from Qayrawan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.292}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives al-Arbus as a city and a district of Ifriqiya, its district wide, with saffron as most of its produce and an iron mine, three days from al-Qayrawan toward the west. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.136} A passage under al-Bakri's name, whose end the entry does not mark, calls it a walled city with a large suburb, known by a name with 'anbar in it, a word the museum leaves untranslated because it can mean ambergris or a storehouse. It says Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab went there on leaving al-Qayrawan in a year the printed text gives as 196; that Abu 'Abd Allah al-Shi'i advanced on it and besieged it; that Ibrahim fled to Tripoli with commanders and soldiers; that al-Shi'i took it by force and killed those who had taken refuge in its mosque; and that Aghlabid rule ended after that. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.136} Of its people he names the poet Abu Tahir al-Arbusi and Ya'la ibn Ibrahim al-Arbusi, who died in Egypt in 418, on Ibn Rashiq's word. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.136}

Other entries: Abba, in the district of al-Arbus, is known for fruit and saffron {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.85}; a passage in the Zaghwan entry, begun under al-Bakri's name and not marked at its end, puts the city west of Jabal Zaghwan {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.144}; and al-Muhallabi gives one stage between Marmajanna and al-Arbus {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.109}.

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, p.292) calls Arbus a city on level land with never-failing springs, among them the spring of Rabah, whose saffron rivals the Andalusian, three stages from Qayrawan. Yaqut agrees on saffron and on three days from al-Qayrawan; his entry (vol 1 p.136) does not mention the springs, Rabah or level land. The year 196 in the printed text does not fit the Aghlabid end and the arrival of al-Shi'i that the passage describes, which belong to the next century in the museum's reading (a slip in the text or the print is possible); the museum does not use the figure. The record's point is 124.6 km from the museum's Qayrawan point, and is not tied to the day-count. See [[place/qayrawan|Qayrawan]].
