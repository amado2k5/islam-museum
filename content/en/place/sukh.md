## Summary
Sukh is a town set apart by itself, with sixty villages in a single district, and mercury is extracted from it, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.508}

## Details
Al-Idrisi calls Sukh a town set apart by itself, with sixty villages in one district. He describes it as settled and rich in produce, and says mercury rises from it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.508}

From Sukh the road runs one stage to Rishtan, from Rishtan two light stages to Qaba, which he calls a fine town, and from Qaba one large stage, thirty miles, to Osh. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.508}

Al-Idrisi describes Osh as well sited on the bank of a river that bears its name, with a large outer quarter, a strong wall joined to the city wall, a fortified citadel, markets that draw trade, and three gates of iron. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.508} It lies against the mountain next to the land of the Tibetan Turks, where its people keep a lookout on the heights to watch over the Turks' pastures. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.508}

From Osh the road goes to Uzkand, which he calls the last city of Fargana on the side of the Turks' land towards the east. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.508}
