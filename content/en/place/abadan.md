## Overview
Abadan: a small, populated place on the seashore, reached by the waters that come down from [[place/basra|Basra]], two stages away from it, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.385}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Abbadan, with a fatha on the first letter and a doubled ba, and cites Ptolemy for its place in the third climate. He reports from al-Baladhuri that it was a land grant to Humran ibn Aban, freedman of Uthman ibn Affan, and that it came to be linked with Abbad ibn al-Husayn; from Ibn al-Kalbi that Abbad ibn al-Husayn was the first to keep a frontier garrison there and that al-Rabi ibn Sabh fortified it with money collected from the people of Basra. On the name he says abbad means a person of much worship and that people in Basra and its districts add an alif and nun to names of places, as in Ziyadan and Bilalan. He places it below Basra near the salt sea, on an island between two arms of the Tigris, a salty, marshy and poor spot with shrines and ribats, where devotees live on endowments and vows, eat mostly fish, and where there is a shrine of Ali ibn Abi Talib. He adds that the traditions told about its merits are not firmly established, that the Persians call it Miyan Rudan, meaning between the rivers, and that a number of ascetics and hadith transmitters carry the nisba al-Abbadani. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.74} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.75}
