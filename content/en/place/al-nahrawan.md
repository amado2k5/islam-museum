## Overview
al-Nahrawan: a small town, cut through its middle by the river al-Nahrawan. It lies on the road from Baghdad to the east. al-Idrisi mentions it among the places of al-Sawad. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.668}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Nahrawan — commonly pronounced with a kasra on the nun — as three Nahrawans, upper, middle and lower: a broad district between Baghdad and Wasit on the eastern side, its upper limit adjoining Baghdad, holding several middling towns including Iskaf, Jarjaraya, al-Safiyya and Dayr Qunna; the famous battle of the Commander of the Faithful Ali ibn Abi Talib with the Khawarij took place there, and a group of scholars and literati came out of it. Its river rises near Tamarra or Hulwan — Yaqut says he cannot confirm it and has seen no one state it — and in his day it lies ruined, its towns and villages mounds with walls still standing; the ruin came from the sultans' quarrels and wars in the Seljuk period and from its lying in the armies' path, and kings held it ill-omened to re-dig its river, claiming that whoever began the work died before finishing it; it had been among the fairest of Baghdad's districts and the richest in revenue. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.324-325} Ibn al-Kalbi said the Persians dug the Nahrawan, whose name was nahr-wana: if its water ran low its people thirsted, if high they drowned; Hamza al-Isfahani said the Jarrar valley comes down from the Azerbaijan side, waters many villages and pours into the Tigris below al-Mada'in, bearing a Persian name (Jurwan) and a Syriac one (Tamarra), the Persian name Arabicised as nahrawan — though common folk say nahrawan with a kasra in error. And he read in Ibn al-Kalbi's book on the genealogies of towns that Tamarra and Nahrawan were sons of Jukhi who dug the two rivers and lent them their names; he then quotes at length, from Abu Ali al-Tanukhi, a tale of the name's derivation which he himself says does not fit the word, yet he set it down in full. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.325}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Nahrawan is the broad canal-district east of Baghdad toward Wasit, and the record's al-Idrisi town — a small place on Baghdad's eastern road crossed by the canal — is the same water and region; Yaqut gives the district, its ruin in the seventh century and Ali's battle with the Khawarij, while the record's own text cites only town and river from al-Idrisi. By the two records' points al-Nahrawan lies about 28.5 km from Baghdad.
