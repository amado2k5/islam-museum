## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan, Turkestan is the collective name for the lands of the Turks. Yaqut describes its limits and peoples and passes on reports from travellers and envoys. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.23}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Turkestan is a collective name for all the lands of the Turks. At the start of the entry he sets down traditions and reports attributed to Companions and to the Prophet about the Turks, giving them as they reached him without comment of his own. He then turns to geography and ethnography. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.23}

He says the widest of the Turkic lands is that of the Taghazghaz, bounded by China, Tibet, the Kharluq, the Kimak, the Ghuzz, the Jafar, the Bajanak, the Badhakish, the Khifshaq and the Kyrgyz, and that its first boundary on the Muslim side is Farab, with sixteen famous cities. He describes the Taghazghaz as like the nomads, people of tent-poles who move and settle, and the Badhakishiyya as people of towns and villages. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.23}

He relays the account of an envoy whom Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik sent to the Turkic king to invite him to Islam; the king showed him a great army that appeared when banners were unfurled and asked how his men would earn a living if they became Muslims, since they had no cupping-physician, cobbler or tailor among them. He also relays what Tamim ibn Bahr al-Mutawwi'i said of his journey to the land of the Taghazghazi khaqan, where, he says, most of the people were fire-worshippers of the Magian way, with zindiqs of the Manichaean way among them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.24}
