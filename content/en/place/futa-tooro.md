## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes Futa Toro as the state that followed the medieval kingdom of Takrur, in the basin of the Senegal River in West Africa. There, in the 1660s, Nasir al-Din began a reform movement with popular backing, based on Islam. He demanded that rulers accept him as imam, the Dictionary says, and was met with armed force; he fell in a battle in 1673. Abdul Kader took the office of almaami, the religious leader or imam, in the 1780s, though his forces could not carry the revolution into neighbouring states. In 1806 he died, and with him the original aims of the reform revolution, defeated by the non-Muslim kingdom of Kaarta, the state of Bundu and a subversive element inside Futa Toro itself. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e688}
