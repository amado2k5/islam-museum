## Summary
For al-Hamdani the Hijaz is "what screens between Yemen and al-Sham" — in the Yemenis' division of Arabia one of its named belts alongside Yemen, al-Sham, Najd, Tihama, the Sarat, the 'Arud, 'Iraq and Shahr {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.33}. It faces [[place/najd|Najd]]: what rises east of the Sarat mountains is Najd, and what slopes down west of them is Tihama and the Hijaz {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.87-88}.

## In the Arabs' poetry and landmarks
The Arabs spoke of the Hijaz, al-Jals, Tihama and Najd in abundant verse; among its landmarks al-Hamdani notes "the heart of the Hijaz" guarded by Hudhayl, Dhat Irq which stands as the boundary between Tihama, Najd and the Hijaz, and — among the mountains on the right of the road from Mecca to Medina — al-'Arj, Qudus, Ara, al-Ash'ar and al-Ajrad {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.32} {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.33}.

## For young visitors
The Hijaz is the western strip of Arabia which — as the geographer al-Hamdani put it — lies between Yemen and Syria; it holds Mecca and Medina and faces the highlands of Najd.
