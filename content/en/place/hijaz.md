## Summary
For al-Hamdani the Hijaz is "what screens between Yemen and al-Sham" — in the Yemenis' division of Arabia one of its named belts alongside Yemen, al-Sham, Najd, Tihama, the Sarat, the 'Arud, 'Iraq and Shahr {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.33}. It faces [[place/najd|Najd]]: what rises east of the Sarat mountains is Najd, and what slopes down west of them is Tihama and the Hijaz {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.87-88}.

## In the Arabs' poetry and landmarks
The Arabs spoke of the Hijaz, al-Jals, Tihama and Najd in abundant verse; among its landmarks al-Hamdani notes "the heart of the Hijaz" guarded by Hudhayl, Dhat Irq which stands as the boundary between Tihama, Najd and the Hijaz, and — among the mountains on the right of the road from Mecca to Medina — al-'Arj, Qudus, Ara, al-Ash'ar and al-Ajrad {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.32} {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.33}.

## For young visitors
The Hijaz is the western strip of Arabia which — as the geographer al-Hamdani put it — lies between Yemen and Syria; it holds Mecca and Medina and faces the highlands of Najd.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises al-Hijaz with a kasra on the first letter and a final zay, and passes on two derivations from Ibn al-Anbari: from the Arabic verb for hobbling a camel, or because the land is girdled by mountains. He himself says the scholars agree on a derivation from the verb for holding back, since the Hijaz is a mountain range running between the lowland of Tihama and Najd, a barrier between the two. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.218} For its limits he gives attributed views: al-Asma'i counts twelve abodes, among them Medina, Khaybar, Fadak and Dhu al-Marwa, and holds that Mecca is Tihami while Medina and al-Ta'if are Hijazi; Hisham ibn al-Kalbi divides Arab lands into five, Tihama, the Hijaz, Najd, al-'Arud and Yemen, and takes the Hijaz to be the Sarat range itself with what it encloses to the east toward Fayd and Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.219} He also reports that Tasm ibn Iram settled here when the Arabs dispersed and that the land was called the Hijaz because it held them back from following their brothers, and that Ibrahim al-Harbi counted Tabuk and Palestine within it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.219} He then relays from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab that God steadied the shaking earth with this mountain, and closes the entry with verses of longing for the Hijaz by bedouin and poets. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.220}
