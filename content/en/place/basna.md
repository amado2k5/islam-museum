## Summary
Basna is a small city near al-Sus where the famous curtains bearing its name are made, and it contains imperial embroidery workshops. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.397}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the city of Basna is small in bulk but great in renown, and contains tiraz workshops for the sultan in which are made the celebrated curtains named after it, famous in all regions, with 'made at Basna' written in their embroidery; yet curtains are also made at Bardhun, Kalyawan and other cities and marked Basna, being passed off among the Basna curtains {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.397}. He notes that between Basna and al-Sus is one stage, and that at al-Sus is the town of Bardhun, small in size but populous, with farms and buildings {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.397}. Basna's fame is thus a craft fame resting on its embroidered curtains, which were imitated elsewhere.
