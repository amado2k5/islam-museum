## Summary
Istija (Ecija) was a town of the Kanbaniyya district of al-Andalus, on the river of Granada called Shanil — a fine town with a remarkable bridge built of dressed stone. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.572}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes it as having thriving markets and established trade, with orchards, luxuriant gardens and blooming parks, and says that from Istija to Cordoba was thirty-five miles, while from the fortress of Shant Yala to Istija westward was fifteen miles and from Shant Yala to Cordoba twenty-three miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.572} From Istija southward to the fortress of Ashuna was half a day, and from it to the city of Qarmuna forty-five miles; the fortress of Ashuna was populous and thickly inhabited, and from it to Balshana twenty miles — a large, well-settled fort of great strength, ringed by olive trees. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.573} He lists it among the towns of the Kanbaniyya district together with Cordoba, al-Zahra', Bayana and Qabra. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.537}
