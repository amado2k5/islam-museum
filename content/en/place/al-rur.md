## Overview
al-Rur: one of the cities of Sind, on a mountain. al-Baladhuri reports that Muhammad ibn al-Qasim besieged it for months and then conquered it by treaty, on condition that he would not kill them or interfere with their temples. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

According to the report, he said that the temples are like the churches of the Christians and Jews and the fire-houses of the Magians. He imposed the kharaj on them and built a mosque. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut writes the name with two unpointed ra letters and first gives a district in or near al-Ahwaz. He then gives al-Rur in Sind, a district about as large as Multan with two walls, on the bank of the Mihran river near the sea, on the borders of al-Mansura and Daybul, a trading town and port of that country. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.79} He describes its fields as poorly fertile, with few trees and no palms, a harsh land where people stay for trade; it lies four stages from Multan, and near it is a town called Baghrur, mentioned in the conquests of Sind. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.79}
