## Summary
Artha was a fine city on a fortified mountain, situated between Slawa and Kuyaba, and the residence of the king of the Arthaniyya among the Rus, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}

## Details
Al-Idrisi counts three divisions of the Rus: the Baraus, whose king dwells in Kuyaba; the Slawiyya, whose king dwells in Slawa; and the Arthaniyya, whose king resides in the city of Artha {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}. Artha is a fine city on a fortified mountain between Slawa and Kuyaba: four stages from Kuyaba and four days from Slawa, and Muslim merchants reach Kuyaba from Armenia {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}. He relates from al-Shaykh al-Hawqali that no stranger enters Artha, for its people kill every foreigner who reaches them and none dares enter their land; from them come black sable and black fox pelts and lead, carried out by the merchants of Kuyaba {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}. In his account it was thus a closed, well-defended city whose goods reached trade without admitting traders.
