## Overview
Mashhad is the name of the place of the burial of Ali ibn Musa al-Rida, near Tus in Khurasan. Yaqut al-Hamawi's Mu'jam al-Buldan has no entry for the city under its name, but it records the spot around which the shrine grew in the entries "Sanabadh" and "Tus", which are the basis of this page.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Under Sanabadh, Yaqut says it is a village of Tus holding the tomb of the Imam Ali ibn Musa al-Rida and the tomb of the Commander of the Faithful al-Rashid, about a mile from the city of Tus. He names a man from it, Muhammad ibn Ismail ibn al-Fadl Abu al-Baraka al-Husayni al-Alawi, described as one of the people of "al-Mashhad al-Radawi" at Sanabadh, among the villages of Nawqan of Tus. This man heard from al-Hasan ibn Ismail ibn al-Fadl and al-Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Samarqandi; Abu Sa'd and Abu al-Qasim heard from him; he was born in 457 and died at the end of Dhu al-Hijja 541. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.259} See [[person/ali-al-rida|Ali al-Rida]] and [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]].

Under Tus, Yaqut says it is a city of Khurasan about ten farsakhs from Nishapur, made up of two towns, Taberan and Nawqan, with more than a thousand villages, taken in the days of Uthman, and that it holds the tomb of Ali ibn Musa al-Rida and the tomb of Harun al-Rashid. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.49} He quotes Mis'ar ibn al-Muhalhil that Tus is four cities, two large and two small, and that in one of its gardens lie the tombs of al-Rida and al-Rashid. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.49} See [[place/tus|Tus]] and [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]].

These two entries show that in Yaqut's day, the seventh century AH, the spot was a "mashhad" (shrine) attached to the village of Sanabadh in the district of Tus, not a city called Mashhad. The place card describes the city today as a major Shi'i pilgrimage centre; Yaqut does not state this in either entry, and that description rests on a source other than this book.
