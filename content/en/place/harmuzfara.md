## Overview
Harmuzfara is a town one stage west of Marw and a farsakh from Kashmihan. It has buildings, markets, and coming and going, and it has a pulpit, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.476}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name Harmuzfara, with a fatha on the fa and a doubled ra, and calls it a village on the edge of the districts of Marw, beside the desert on the road to Khwarazm, now called Masfara, which he says he saw himself. He gives the origin of the name: when the Muslim army came to Marw on campaign, the place was the seat of a ruler named Hurmuz, who fled, and the Arabs said Hurmuz farra, 'Hurmuz fled', so the name stuck. He names two men with the nisba al-Harmuzfarahi: Abu Hashim Bukayr ibn Mahan, one of those who worked to establish the Abbasid state and one of its leading commanders, and Ibrahim ibn Ahmad ibn Ibrahim, who heard Ali ibn Khashram, Sulayman ibn Ma'bad al-Sinji and others. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.403}
