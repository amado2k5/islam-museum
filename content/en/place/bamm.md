## Summary
Bamm is a town in Kirman whose people made terms with al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad al-Harithi, then broke faith and were retaken by Mujashi' ibn Mas'ud, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri relates in Futuh al-Buldan that Abu Musa al-Ash'ari dispatched al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad, who conquered the country around al-Shirjan and made terms with the people of Bamm and al-Andaghar; its people then renounced and broke the pact, so Mujashi' ibn Mas'ud reconquered it, took Jirraft by force, and went through Kirman subduing it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} In his account Bamm is thus one of the Kirman towns that fell to al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad's expedition by treaty within Abu Musa's conquests, then shared the fate of revolt and renewed conquest under Mujashi' ibn Mas'ud, as befell Jirraft and the rest of Kirman, where he records conquest and subjugation. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with fatha on the first letter and a doubled mim, and calls Bamm a noble, important city among the leading cities of Kirman, one stage from Jiruft. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.495} He says its people are skilled, most of them weavers, and that its cloth is famous in every land. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.495} They drink from underground channels, he says, with some salinity in the water, and the city has a running river, gardens and busy markets. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.495} He cites a line of verse by al-Tirimmah that mentions it, and names among those with the nisba al-Bammi Isma'il ibn Ibrahim, vizier of Sunkuri, ruler of Fars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.495}
