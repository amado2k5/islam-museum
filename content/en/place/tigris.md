## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut defines the Tigris as the river of Baghdad, a name that takes no definite article, and cites Hamza that it is an Arabisation of a Persian name and that it has two further Persian names, one meaning the little sea. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.440} He then relays, through al-Marzubani, a text ascribed to al-Kisrawi that traces the river from its source at 'Ayn Dijla near Amid and lists the streams and valleys that join it, among them the Greater and Lesser Zab, as far as Samarra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.441} He gives a second account that it rises from a mountain near Amid by the fortress of Dhu al-Qarnayn and runs past Mayyafariqin, Hisn Kayfa, Jazirat Ibn 'Umar, Mosul, Tikrit, Baghdad, Wasit, Basra and 'Abbadan into the Indian Ocean, splitting below Wasit into five great channels. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.441} He reports from Ibn 'Abbas that God told the prophet Daniel to dig two rivers, says al-Dijla al-'Awra' is the name of the Tigris at Basra, quotes verses by al-Ma'arri, al-Tanukhi and others praising it, and notes that Dijla is also the name of a place in the Arabian steppe. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.442}
