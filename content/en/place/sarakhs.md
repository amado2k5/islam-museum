## Overview
Sarakhs is a city in Khurasan. Abd Allah ibn Khazim fought it, then its marzban Zadhwayh asked for peace in exchange for the safety of a hundred men and the surrender of the women. His daughter fell to the share of Ibn Khazim, and he named her Maysha'. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Two accounts
It is said that Zadhwayh named the hundred and did not name himself, so he was killed, the city was entered by force, and Ibn Khazim prevailed over its land. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Sarakhs as an old, large city in the Khurasan region, midway on the road between Nishapur and Marw, six stages from each. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.208}

He gives two accounts of its name: that a man of the Dhi'ar lived there in the time of Kay Kawus, and Alexander the Two-Horned completed its building; and that the Persians said Kay Kawus granted land to Sarakhs ibn Khudhurz, who built the city and named it after himself. He describes it as a thirsty place, with only well water in summer and no permanent river, and its pasture land is better than its crops. Many imams came out of it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 pp.208-209}
