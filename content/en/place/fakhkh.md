## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Fakhkh is a valley at Mecca, and the day of Fakhkh is the engagement in 169 AH in which al-Husayn ibn Ali, of the Alid line, was met by Abbasid armies. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.237} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.238}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling, a fatha on the fa and a doubled kha, and notes that fakhkh in the sense of a trap for catching birds is a borrowed word, not Arabic, whose Arabic name is tarq. He then says Fakhkh is a valley at Mecca and reports on the authority of al-Sayyid Ali that it is the valley of al-Zahir. He describes a line of Bilal in which the poet wishes to spend a night at Fakhkh with idhkhir and jalil grass at hand. He also records a second sense of the name, a water that the Prophet granted to Azim ibn al-Harith al-Muharibi, a report he attributes to al-Hazimi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.237} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.238}

## The day of Fakhkh
Yaqut relates that al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib rose in Dhu al-Qada of 169 calling people to himself, that a group of Alids pledged allegiance to him for the caliphate at Medina, and that he set out for Mecca. At Fakhkh the Abbasid armies under al-Abbas ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn Abbas and others met him; they fought on the day of al-Tarwiya, after offering him safe conduct. Yaqut adds that it is said a Turk named Mubarak shot him with an arrow and that his head was carried to al-Hadi, that a number of his men and household were killed, and that their bodies lay three days until beasts ate them, so that it was said no calamity after Karbala was worse. He gives two laments for the men of Fakhkh, one by Isa ibn Abd Allah and one that Musa ibn Dawud ibn Salm recited from his father. The verse is described here, not quoted. Yaqut also says that Abd Allah ibn Umar and some Companions were buried at this place; that is his statement. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.238}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Fakhkh a site near Mecca and the scene of the Alid battle of 169 AH, which agrees with the Mu'jam. The coordinates in the record are approximate, taken from classical itineraries, and Yaqut gives no distance. The battle has its own record, [[event/battle-of-fakhkh|the battle of Fakhkh]], and its leader has [[person/husayn-ibn-ali-sahib-fakhkh|al-Husayn ibn Ali, sahib Fakhkh]]. Yaqut gives the day of Fakhkh as the narrators of reports tell it; this is not a judgement on either side.
