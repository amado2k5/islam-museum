## Overview
Tunkat is the capital of Ilaq, a large city with a populous suburb and two strong walls, with several gates in its wall, thriving markets and ample revenues. Water runs through its lanes, irrigates its districts and keeps it built up. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 704}

## Place and size
It is less than half the size of Binkath, and has a strong citadel. The city and its markets, with its suburb, lie on the bank of the Burk river, and its land joins the district of Isbijab; the text then describes Isbijab as a populous city on level ground with Badhkhakath and Sabanikat among its towns. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 704}

## The neighbouring land of al-Shash
Before this al-Idrisi describes the land of al-Shash as level and easy, with no hills, full of gardens, greenery and pleasure grounds, among the frontier posts against the Turks, whose people have force and strength. Its greatest city is Binkath, large, populous, built up and busy with trade, and surrounded by a strong wall. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 703}
