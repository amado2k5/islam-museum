## Overview
Latakia is a coastal city on the coasts of Syria, and it was among the districts of the military region of [[place/homs|Homs]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Al-Baladhuri reports that [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]] conquered it by force, by the ruse of the pits, since it had a great gate that the group could not open. The Muslims built a congregational mosque in it by his command. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Then the Romans destroyed it in a sea raid in the year 100 AH and took its people captive. Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz ordered it rebuilt and fortified, and Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik completed it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Laodicea» (Pleiades no. 668290), classed as settlement, port and modern settlement, with activity attested from 550 BCE to 2100 CE. Recorded names include «Laodicea», «al-Lādhiqīyah» and «Laodikeia». Its description calls it an ancient city, also called Laodicea ad Mare and today Latakia in Syria, with notable Punic, Greek and Roman phases. {cite:pleiades 668290}
