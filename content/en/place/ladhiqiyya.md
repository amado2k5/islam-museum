## Overview
Latakia is a coastal city on the coasts of Syria, and it was among the districts of the military region of [[place/homs|Homs]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Al-Baladhuri reports that [[person/ubada-ibn-al-samit|Ubada ibn al-Samit]] conquered it by force, by the ruse of the pits, since it had a great gate that the group could not open. The Muslims built a congregational mosque in it by his command. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Then the Romans destroyed it in a sea raid in the year 100 AH and took its people captive. Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz ordered it rebuilt and fortified, and Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik completed it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Laodicea» (Pleiades no. 668290), classed as settlement, port and modern settlement, with activity attested from 550 BCE to 2100 CE. Recorded names include «Laodicea», «al-Lādhiqīyah» and «Laodikeia». Its description calls it an ancient city, also called Laodicea ad Mare and today Latakia in Syria, with notable Punic, Greek and Roman phases. {cite:pleiades 668290}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name with a dotted dhal and a kasra, a kasra on the qaf and a doubled ya, and says it is a city on the coast of the Syrian sea, once counted among the districts of Homs, six farsakhs west of Jabala and, in his own day, part of the district of Aleppo. He quotes Ptolemy's Malhama for its longitude, 68 degrees 20 minutes, and latitude, 35 degrees 6 minutes, in the fourth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.5}

He calls it an old Roman city with solid ancient buildings, pleasant and set on level ground, with a good, well-built harbour and two joined citadels on a hill above the suburb, the sea lying to its west. He cites verses by al-Mutanabbi and a poem by al-Ma'arri composed while the city was in Byzantine hands, when it had a judge, a preacher and a mosque for the Muslims, and the Byzantines struck their bells whenever the call to prayer sounded. From Ibn Fadlan he reports that it is an ancient city named after its founder. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.6}

He notes that two philosophers came from the city, and that among its scholars was the Shafi'i jurist Nasr Allah ibn Muhammad al-Misisi, later al-Ladhiqi, who was born there in 448 and died in 542, and taught in Damascus. He says the Franks took it around 500 AH among the coastal lands they seized, that it was in Muslim hands again in his time, and that in Dhu al-Qa'da of 620 an army from Aleppo went out to it and levelled its citadel, fearing the Franks would camp there and cut the Muslims off from it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.6}
