## Overview
Jarash is a town in the land of Hawran. The Hawl reports wonders of its remains. In the eighth century AH, Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari described it as ruined on its foundations, empty of its people and inhabitants. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.231}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives Jarash, pronounced with a fatha on both the first and second letters, as the name of a great city that once existed and is now in ruins; he reports from someone who had seen it that ancient wells there show its size and that a river running through its middle turned several working mills up to his own time. He places it east of Jabal al-Sawad in the land of al-Balqa and Hawran, in the district of Damascus, on a mountain holding estates and villages that are all called Jabal Jarash, named for a man, Jarash ibn Abd Allah of the Kalb line; Jabal Awf adjoins it, and the Hima of Jarash is attributed to it. He says it was among the conquests of Shurahbil ibn Hasana in the days of Umar, and that the poet al-Mutanabbi went there to praise Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ahmad al-Marri al-Khurasani. {C(2,127)}
