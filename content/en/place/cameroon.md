## Summary
Cameroon is a Central African state. The Oxford Dictionary of Islam has a short entry, Islam in Cameroon, which ties the spread of Islam there to the Fulani and the Sufi brotherhoods. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e406}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The entry says that Muslims are about sixteen percent of Cameroon's 13.1 million people, Christians thirty-three percent and followers of indigenous African religions fifty-one percent. It credits the Fulani, a pastoral nomadic people, with spreading Islam in West Africa in the early nineteenth century through trade and through the Sufi brotherhoods, naming the Qadiri and Tijani. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e406}

It adds that the country was colonised by Germany from 1884 to 1916 and by Britain and France from 1916 to 1960, that northerners strongly resisted westernisation, and that at independence in 1960 the difference in development between a Muslim north and a south dominated by indigenous religions and Christianity was plain. It reports that the Cameroon People's Democratic Movement won the last elections it mentions, in 1997, that the party is under government control and had no rival until opposition parties were legalised in 1990, and that there are no active Islamic political parties. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e406}

The entry names the Qadiri order ([[group/qadiriyya|Qadiriyya]]) and lists the Sokoto caliphate among its related topics. The figures are its own wording and have not been compared with a census.
