## Overview
Constantinople, on the Bosporus, was made the capital of the Eastern Roman Empire by the emperor Constantine on 11 May 330 and named after him; the Arabs knew it as al-Kustantiniyya and also knew its older name Byzantion. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 570}

## Facing the Arab campaigns
Under the Umayyads, Muslim campaigns were directed at the city: a land army appeared before it about 47/667 and a fleet blockaded it in the Sea of Marmora until about 53/673, and in that siege Abu Ayyub al-Ansari fell and was buried before its walls. Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik then led a great expedition whose siege began on 25 August 716 and lasted a whole year until he was forced to withdraw by Bulgar attacks and lack of provisions. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 5.1 p. 571}

## From the Franks to the Ottomans
The Franks seized Constantinople in 601/1204 and expelled the Romans, holding it until 660 when the Romans recovered it. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 696}

The Ottomans then renewed the attempts: Sultan Bayezid I drew a tight blockade around the city from about 796/1393 until he was forced to lift it to face Timur in 1402, and Sultan Murad II besieged it in 825/1422 before raising the siege. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p. 68, 89-90} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 7 p. 648-649}

Sultan Mehmed II finally took it on 20 Jumada al-Akhira 857, corresponding to 29 May 1453, after a siege of about fifty days; it became the Ottoman capital and remained so until 1342/1923, under the name Istanbul. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 345} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 4 p. 237}
