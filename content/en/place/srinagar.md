## Overview
Srinagar is the heart of the Vale of Kashmir with its saffron fields, on Lake Dal, round which the Mughal emperors built their gardens; under [[person/jahangir-mughal|Jahangir]] there were, [[person/john-brookes|Brookes]] writes, no fewer than seven hundred. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.136}

## The Mughals and Kashmir
[[person/jalal-al-din-akbar|Akbar]] first visited Kashmir in 1586 after its conquest and called it his private garden; his first building there was the fortress of Hari Parbat over the lake, followed by the garden palace of Nasim Bagh. Jahangir and [[person/nur-jahan|Nur Jahan]] loved it in turn, spending whole summers there, and the gardens multiplied in their time. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.135} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.136} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.139}

## The lake gardens
On the lake and its slopes lie the [[building/shalimar-bagh-srinagar|Shalimar Bagh]], the [[building/nishat-bagh|Nishat Bagh]], the [[building/chashma-shahi|Chashma Shahi]] and the [[building/pari-mahal-srinagar|Pari Mahal]]; further off in the valley are [[building/verinag-garden|Verinag]] and [[building/achabal-garden|Achabal]]. The emperors reached the lake gardens by boat, and the tradition of floating gardens survives there. {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.136} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.141} {cite:brookes-gardens-of-paradise-1987 p.147}
