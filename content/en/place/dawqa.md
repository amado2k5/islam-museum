## Summary
Dawqa is a village that al-Idrisi mentions together with the village of Alib on the road from Mecca to Dhu Suhaym of Khawlan; the two are villages populated by their inhabitants. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the road from Mecca to Dhu Suhaym of Khawlan leaves Mecca for Malkan, a watering-place where travellers halt; thence to Yalamlam, one stage, a mountain barring the way from east to west, where the miqat of the people of Tihama lies; then to a halting-place in the desert with a spring of water, one stage; thence to Qayna, a small village with two wells, one stage; thence to Dawqa and Alib, two villages populated by their inhabitants, one stage; and from them to al-Hasba {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}. Dawqa is thus one of the halts of this road which the caravans take from Mecca toward Khawlan and Tihama, adjoining Alib and sharing its mention and its stage; it was inhabited by its people when al-Idrisi recorded it among the places of that region he described {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}.
