## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Qaysariyya is a city on the coast of the Syrian sea in the districts of Palestine, three days from Tiberias, once among the great mother cities, with an account of Muawiya's siege and capture. The same entry covers a second Qaysariyya in the Byzantine lands. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.421} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.422}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says it is a town on the coast of the Syrian sea reckoned to Palestine, three days from Tiberias, once among the notable mother cities, spacious, pleasant and rich in goods and people, though in his day more like a village than a city. He then gives the second sense, Qaysariyya in the lands of the Rum, a great city, seat of the Seljuk kings, with places said to be in it and astronomical positions that he does not explain. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.421}

## The capture of Qaysariyya
From the Book of Damascus, on the authority of Yazid ibn Samura, Yaqut reports that Muawiya besieged it for seven years less some months, that the Byzantine fighters there numbered a hundred thousand, its Samaritans eighty thousand and its Jews a hundred thousand; these are the report's figures, not ones Yaqut confirms. A man showed the besiegers a weak point and led them into a channel through which a camel with its litter could walk, on a Sunday, and the people knew nothing, being in church, until they heard the takbir at its door, which was their ruin. The news of the conquest was sent to Umar ibn Tamim ibn Warqa, chief of Khath'am, who stood on the minaret and proclaimed that Qaysariyya had been taken by force. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.421} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.422}

## People of Qaysariyya
Among those attributed to Qaysariyya of Palestine, Yaqut names Ibrahim ibn Abi Sufyan al-Qaysarani, who died in 278; Amr ibn Thawr al-Qaysarani, who died in 279; Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahim al-Qaysarani; and Fudayk ibn Salman, Abu Isa al-Uqayli al-Qaysarani, who transmitted from al-Awzai and is described as devout. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.422}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Caesarea Maritima an ancient city of the Levant, which is the coastal Qaysariyya of Palestine in Yaqut's first sense, not the Qaysariyya of the Rum, which is today's Kayseri and has its own record, [[place/kayseri|Kayseri]]. The museum also holds a separate record for the same coastal city, [[place/qaysariyya|Caesarea]]; that duplication is a matter for the museum, not decided by this record.
