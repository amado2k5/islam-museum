## Overview
Tiberias is the great city of Jordan, and its capital, as al-Idrisi describes it. It lies ninety miles from Jerusalem, three stages from Ramla and two easy days from Acre. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 363} Its harbour is Haifa, which al-Idrisi calls the port of Tiberias; it lies three stages away, below the foot of Mount Carmel. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 365} Yaqut counts Jordan as one of the five military districts of Syria, with a sub-district of Tiberias, and the city stands on a mountain slope overlooking the lake that bears its name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 190} He describes the lake as a basin ringed by mountains. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 484} The Great Jordan flows into it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 190} The city's water is fresh and drinkable, though not very sweet. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 484} Yaqut relates that Shurahbil ibn Hasana took the Jordan by force except Tiberias. Its people made terms after a siege of several days, granting safety for persons, property and churches, with a site kept back for a Muslim mosque. When they broke the terms in the caliphate of Umar, Abu Ubayda sent four thousand men and took it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 190} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 191}
