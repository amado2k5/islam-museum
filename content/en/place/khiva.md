## Overview
Khiva is a town in Khwarazm in present-day Uzbekistan, and it was the capital of the Khanate of Khiva. {cite:geonames-gazetteer} What is said here about its history needs an additional source.

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The German Archaeological Institute's gazetteer, iDAI.gazetteer, records this place as no. 2733465, typed as populated place, with the preferred name «Chiwa» under «Usbekistan» in its hierarchy, and recorded names including Chiva, Хива, Jiva. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2733465}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Khiwaq", with a fatha on the first letter, which may also take a kasra, a sukun on the second, a fatha on the waw and a qaf at the end, and says it is a town in the districts of Khwarazm and a fortress, "about fifteen farsakhs between them"; the text does not make clear which two he means by the pronoun. He says that the people of Khwarazm say "Khiwa" and form the nisba al-Khiwaqi from it, and that its people are Shafi'is, unlike all the rest of Khwarazm, who are Hanafis. He counts the form of the name as irregular, because the waw is kept after a silent ya, where the rule is to change and merge it, and gives "Hayawa", a man's name, as a parallel. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.415}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut says that the people of Khwarazm say "Khiwa", which is what links his Khiwaq to Khiva, but he does not say that it is the city of the Khanate of Khiva, so what the record says of the khanate comes from outside Yaqut. The museum's identification with the present city of Khiva in Uzbekistan rests on the name Yaqut gives and on the province, and the museum counts it as no more than a reading. The fifteen farsakhs do not make clear which two places are meant; the museum measured that the record's point (41.3856, 60.3641) is about 145 km from its Jurjaniyya point (42.3278, 59.1544), for long the political capital of Khwarazm, and about 27 km from its record for present Urgench, while fifteen farsakhs come to about 90 km at six kilometres to the farsakh, which matches neither, and the museum has not used it. That its people were Shafi'is unlike the rest of Khwarazm is Yaqut's statement alone, for his own time.
