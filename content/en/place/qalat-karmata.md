## Overview
Qal'at Karmata is a fortress in the Maghrib on a road that al-Idrisi describes starting from Fez. It lies one stage from Bab Zanata and has a market, crops and livestock, and it overlooks the Inawn river. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 247}

## The road
In this passage the road runs from Fez to the Sebu river, and then to Tamalta, one stage, a village with farms on a river that comes from the south and is called the wadi Inawn. From there it is one stage to Karanta, which in earlier times was a city with many vineyards, fruit and irrigated farms. From Karanta it is about ten miles to Bab Zanata, a wadi with ploughed land watered from it, with sheep, cattle and many crops, close to the Inawn river. From there to Qal'at Karmata is one stage. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 247}

## Beyond it
From Karmata, at the foot of the mountain, it is one stage to Marawiz, a small fortress that is mostly empty, with plenty of wheat and barley. From there it is one stage to the wadi Masun, by way of Taberenda, a strong fort on a hill overlooking the wadi Muluya. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 247}
