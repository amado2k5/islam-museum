## Overview
Al-Idrisi names Rikka together with Aghmat, writing of "the city of Aghmat and Rikka". It lies at the foot of the Darn (High Atlas) mountain on its northern side, in a wide plain of good soil, rich in plants and grasses, where water runs through the open spaces by night and day. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 231}

## Setting
Gardens, orchards and tangled trees surround the city. Al-Idrisi calls its site one of the finest places on earth, with fertile ground, sweet water and healthy air. A river, not a large one, divides the city; it enters from the south and leaves by the north, and the people's mills for grinding wheat stand on it. The river flows into the city on Thursday, Friday, Saturday and Sunday; on the other days the inhabitants divert it to water their gardens and fields, so none of it reaches the town. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 231}

## Roads and people
From Sijilmasa to Aghmat and Rikka is about eight stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 226} The road from Tarudant in the Sus runs to Aghmat and Rikka along the foot of the great Darn mountain. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 229} Al-Idrisi describes the people of Aghmat as Hawwara, a Berber tribe, wealthy merchants who take camel caravans into the land of the Sudan laden with copper, cloaks, woollen cloth and other goods. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 232} The text describes the two towns together and does not separate what belongs to Rikka alone.
