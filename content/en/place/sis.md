## Overview
Sis is a city of the northern Syrian frontier towns in Cilicia. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan as one of the greatest cities of the frontier in his day and the seat of the Armenian ruler. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.297}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Sis is a town that is now the greatest of the Syrian frontier cities, between Antioch and Tarsus, on the spring of Zarba, and that it is the residence of Ibn Layun, ruler of that region among the Armenians. This describes the town as Yaqut knew it, around the late sixth and early seventh centuries after the Hijra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.297}

Yaqut's headword is "Sisiyya", and he says the common people call it "Sis", so one entry covers both names. On the following page, after the description, he carries a report from al-Waqidi that the people of Sisiyya withdrew and joined the highlands of the Byzantine lands in the year 94 or 93. This is a report attributed to al-Waqidi and goes no further than what Yaqut files under this entry. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.298}
