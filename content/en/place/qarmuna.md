## Overview
A fortified city on the top of an impregnable mountain, overlooking a plain abundant in wheat and barley. It lies two stages from [[place/seville|Seville]], according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.572}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes Qarmuniyya as a district (kura) of al-Andalus, whose territory adjoins the districts of Seville, lying west of Cordoba and east of Seville. Its buildings are old. The district rebelled against Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad al-Umawi, who came against it with his troops, took it, and laid it waste; afterwards it returned to something of what it had been. It is seven farsakhs from Seville and twenty-two from Cordoba, and most people say Qarmuna. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.330}

The nisba al-Qarmuni belongs to Khattab ibn Maslama ibn Muhammad ibn Sa'id, Abu al-Mughira al-Iyadi, a man of Cordoba. He heard from Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Lubaba, Aslam ibn Abd al-Aziz, Ahmad ibn Khalid and Qasim ibn Asbagh, travelled to the East and performed the pilgrimage in 332 AH, then returned to al-Andalus. Ibn al-Faradi heard from him and mentioned him in his history, saying that he died on the twelfth night of Shawwal 372 AH and was skilled in grammar and language. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.330}

Yaqut quotes Ibn Sara al-Andalusi on a king of the Arabs who had taken Qarmuna. The verses praise the ruler who held the town by the sword and then returned its fire to the smiths, and they are quoted as a praise poem, not as history. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.330}
