## Overview
Al-Ta'if is a town in Arabia, south-east of Mecca. In the early days of Islam it belonged to the [[group/thaqif|Thaqif]] tribe. The Encyclopaedia of Islam says that today it is the fourth largest town in Saudi Arabia, standing at a road junction on the way from Mecca to [[place/riyadh|Riyadh]]. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 130}

## Setting and climate
In earlier times the journey from [[place/mecca|Mecca]] took two or three days, depending on the route. The town lies in the Sarat mountains, 1,680 metres (5,500 feet) above sea level. Writers disagree over its region: some place it in [[place/najd|Najd]], others in the [[place/hijaz|Hijaz]]. Its pleasant summer climate has made it the summer capital of western Arabia. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 130}

## Valleys and the sacred valley
Al-Ta'if is surrounded by valleys. The most important is the one in which it stands, Wadjdj, which gave the town its pre-Islamic name. A clause in the agreement between the Prophet and the Thaqif declared this valley a haram, a sacred territory. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 130}

## Walls and fortresses
Shortly before Islam a brick wall was built around the town. Reports credit the initiative and the funding to a merchant who had migrated to al-Ta'if from [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]]. The town also had fortresses before Islam, and their origin is disputed. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 130}

One account links the first fortress to a joint Thaqafi and Qurashi trading expedition to Persia, in which [[person/ghaylan-ibn-salama|Ghaylan ibn Salama al-Thaqafi]] and [[person/abu-sufyan-ibn-harb|Abu Sufyan]] took part. Khusraw sent a skilled builder back with Ghaylan, and this man built the first fortress, called in the sources an utum or a hisn. Both men later became Companions of the Prophet. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 130}

A rival claim to the first fortress points to an earlier generation and to [[person/masud-ibn-mutib|Mas'ud ibn Mu'attib]], father of the Companion [[person/urwa-ibn-masud|Urwa ibn Mas'ud]]. Ghaylan and Mas'ud both belonged to the Ahlaf branch of the Thaqif and to the same clan, the Mu'attib. The Ahlaf fought the Malik, a rival branch. According to the article, Mas'ud sought military help from a friend in Yathrib, [[person/uhayha-ibn-al-julah|Uhayha ibn al-Julah]], who instead sent a slave with him, a skilled builder of utums, and this man built the first utum of al-Ta'if. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 10 p. 130}

## Summary
A mountain city south-east of Mecca, which the Prophet visited in 619 CE and which was besieged in 630 CE.

Taif is a mountain city southeast of Mecca, at roughly 21.27 N, 40.42 E. The Prophet visited it in 619 CE on his journey seeking support; it was besieged in 630 CE after Hunayn, and the idol al-Lat was demolished the year it opened. ## For Scholars
Taif is a mountain city southeast of Mecca, at roughly 21.27 N, 40.42 E. The Prophet visited it in 619 CE on his journey seeking support and refuge; it was besieged in 630 CE in the wake of Hunayn, and the idol al-Lat was demolished the year it opened. The museum's holdings on it gather the four events that took place there — the Prophet's journey to Taif, the battle of Hunayn, the siege of Taif and the demolition of al-Lat — together with those born in it, those who died in it, those who sought it and the community that settled it.
