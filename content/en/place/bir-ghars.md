## Overview
The well of Ghars: a well in [[place/medina|Medina]] from whose water the Prophet liked to drink sweet water. Rabah, the freedman of the Messenger of God, once drew water from it. al-Baladhuri reports that the Prophet said it is one of the springs of Paradise. {cite:ansab-al-ashraf p.536}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut has two entries. Under Bi'r Ghars he says only that it is a well in Medina and sends the reader to the entry al-Ghars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.301} Under al-Ghars he explains the word as a sapling or a tree planted so that it grows, and says that the well of Ghars is in Medina, at Quba', and appears in more than one hadith. He says the Prophet found its water pleasant and blessed it. Without a chain of transmitters he gives a report that the Prophet told 'Ali to wash him after his death with its water in seven skins, a report that he spat in it and said it holds one of the springs of Paradise, and the hadith of Ibn 'Umar in which the Prophet, sitting at its rim, said he had seen himself that night sitting at a spring of Paradise, meaning the well of Ghars. He then quotes al-Waqidi that the homes of Banu al-Nadir lay on the side of al-Ghars and what adjoins it; the cemetery of Banu Hanzala follows in the same sentence. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.193} He adds a Wadi al-Ghars between Ma'din al-Naqra and Fadak, and the entry does not connect it with the well. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.193}

## The museum's identification
The record, from Ansab al-ashraf, is a well in Medina whose water the Prophet found sweet, from which his freedman Rabah drew, and which al-Baladhuri reports to be one of the springs of Paradise. Yaqut agrees on the well, on the sweet water and on the saying that it holds a spring of Paradise; neither of his entries names Rabah. The record's point, which its note places north-east of the Quba' mosque, is 0.8 km from the museum's Quba' point, which fits Yaqut's "at Quba'"; the museum does not use his mention of Banu al-Nadir to adjust the point.
