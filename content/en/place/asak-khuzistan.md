## Summary
Asak was a large, prosperous village of the Khuzistan districts bordering the land of Fars; it had no congregational minbar, but many palm groves and crops, and from it were carried the mats (adyas) woven into rush matting for Iraq, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.400}

## Details
Al-Idrisi places Asak among the villages of Khuzistan adjoining the land of Fars: a large, flourishing village without a minbar, possessing many palm groves, crops and abundant yields {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.400}. He reckons two light stages from Asak to [[place/arjan|Arjan]] and likewise two stages to al-Dawraq, so that it stood connected with both roads {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.400}. He also notes that the reeds known as adyas, named after Asak, are carried from the village and worked into matting in Iraq, and that this variety of reed is superior to every other kind {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.400}. The village thus combined ample cultivation with a craft renown that carried its name into the markets of Iraq through the matting made from its reeds.
