## Summary
Jabal Ikjan was a mountain near Satif in the lands of the Maghrib, inhabited by the tribes of Kitama, bearing a strong fortress and an impregnable refuge that had belonged to the governorship of the Banu Hammad. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.269}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that near Satif is a mountain called Ikjan where the tribes of Kitama dwell; on it is a strong fortress and an impregnable refuge, and it had formerly belonged to the governorship of the Banu Hammad {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.269}. Joining its end on the west is a mountain called Jilawa, and between it and Bijaya is a stage and a half {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.269}. The tribe of Kitama extends its settlement until it passes the land of al-Qal and Buna; among them are generosity and the offering of food to whoever seeks them out or alights with one of them, for they are the most generous of men {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.269}. The fortress of Satif is great in extent and many in people like a city; it has abundant waters and fruit trees of kinds of fruits, from which walnuts are carried, because of their plenty, to all regions; they are of surpassing goodness and are sold cheap there, and between Satif and Qantina are four stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.269}. Jabal Ikjan thus appears in his scheme as an impregnable mountain stronghold of the Kitama tribes neighbouring Satif and Bijaya, once subject to the Banu Hammad, and joined westward to Jabal Jilawa.
