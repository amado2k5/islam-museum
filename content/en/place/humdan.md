## Summary
Humdan is the name Abu Dulaf al-Yanbu'i gives to the capital of China's Great King, in the account of the land of China transmitted by Ibn al-Nadim in the Fihrist. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 491-492}

## Details
Among the reports Ibn al-Nadim transmits about China is a "story from one other than the monk": "Abu Dulaf al-Yanbu'i said: the city of the Great King is called Humdan, and the city of merchants and wealth is Khanqu, forty parasangs long" — though the monk's account differed, and another source told him China had three hundred cities, all thriving. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 491-492}

The description adds that every fifty cities have a king under the Baghbur, that among their cities are Warshanu, Bansu and a city called Armabil, and that a traveller in China, native or foreign, has his lineage, appearance, age, wealth, servants and retinue written down until he reaches his safe destination — lest something happen to him there and it be a disgrace upon the king. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p. 492}
