## Overview
Sind is the land of India on the coast. Al-Baladhuri relates that Hakim ibn Jabala described it to Uthman: its water is a trickle, its fruit is inferior dates, and its thief is a champion. If the army in it is small they are lost, and if it is large they go hungry. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Sind as a land between India, Kirman and Sistan, whose people are called Sindi, plural sind. Some include Makran in it, and others count five districts, beginning on the Kirman side with Makran, then Turan, then Sind, then India, and then Multan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.267}

Its capital is al-Mansura, and among its towns is Debal on the shore of the Indian Ocean, which he says was opened in the days of al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf. Most of its people follow the school of Abu Hanifa, and Yaqut mentions a jurist there of the Dawudi school who was judge of al-Mansura. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.267}
