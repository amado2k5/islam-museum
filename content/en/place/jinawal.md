## Summary
Jinawal was an inland Indian town between Buruj and Nahrwara, close in size to Dulqa with less than a stage between them, at the foot of the mountain of Shamutima. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that between Buruj and Nahrwara are two cities, one named Jinawal and the second Dulqa, close in size with less than a stage between one and the other {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. The city of Dulqa stands on a river that reaches the sea forming an inlet, and on its west is the city of Buruj {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. Jinawal and Dulqa lie at the foot of a mountain stretching across from the side of Shamutima {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. The author counts Jinawal among the inland towns of the Indian lands in the eighth part of the second climate together with Dulqa, Nahrwara, al-Qandahar, Zuwayla, Lata, Udhghust, Kabul, Khwash and Hask {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.186}. In enumerating the cities of India in the seventh part he names Mamhil, Kanbaya, Subara, Asawil, Jinawal, Sindan, Saymur, al-Jundur, al-Sundur, Zuwayla in the desert, Lumta, Udhghust, Nahrwara and Lahawur {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.180}. Jinawal is thus for him an inland Indian town at the edge of the desert, adjoining Dulqa beneath the mountain of Shamutima between Buruj and Nahrwara.
