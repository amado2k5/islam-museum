## Overview
Bamiyan: one of the first lands of India on the side of Sijistan. In it is a great house of the Budd temples, where ascetics and worshippers dwell, and in it are golden idols set with jewels, according to Ibn al-Nadim. India makes pilgrimage to it from the farthest parts of its lands, by land and sea. Ya'qub ibn al-Layth reached this place, and from it images were sent to the City of Peace. {cite:fihrist-ibn-al-nadim p.486}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
For [[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]], Bamiyan is a town and district in the mountains between Balkh, Herat and Ghazna, with a strong citadel, a small chief town and a wide realm, ten stages from Balkh and eight from Ghazna. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.453} He records there a tall hall on raised columns painted with every bird God created, and two enormous idols carved in the mountain from its foot to its top, one called "Surkhbud" and the other "Khinkbud", said to have no equal in the world. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.453} Among scholars named after it he lists Abu Muhammad Ahyad ibn al-Husayn al-Sulami al-Bamiyani, who transmitted from Makki ibn Ibrahim, and Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Ali al-Bamiyani, a prolific hadith transmitter. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.453}
