## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name with a kasra and then a fatha, and says it is a city in the land of the Byzantines, named after Hiraqla, daughter of al-Rum ibn al-Yafaz ibn Sam ibn Nuh. He reports that Harun al-Rashid campaigned against it in person and took it by force after a siege and hard fighting, with fire and naphtha thrown until its people were overcome. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.398} He then tells that the caliph came to al-Raqqa in Ramadan, and that when he sat for the poets at the festival Ashja al-Sulami recited a poem on the conquest; al-Rashid ordered him ten thousand dinars and said no one should recite after him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.398} Among the captives, he adds, was the daughter of the city's patrician, whom a companion of al-Rashid bought and took to al-Raqqa; he built her a fort between al-Rafiqa and Balis on the Euphrates and named it Heraclea after the one in Byzantine territory, a different fort whose remains were still visible in Yaqut's day. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.399}
