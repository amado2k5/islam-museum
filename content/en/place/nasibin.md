## Overview
Nasibin is one of the cities of [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]]. The book Futuh al-Buldan mentions that Iyad ibn Ghanm conquered it after fighting, on terms like the peace of al-Ruha. Then after it he conquered Tur Abdin, the fortress of Mardin, Dara, Qurra and Badhbadhi on similar terms. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Scorpions
In the same book, its governor complained to Mu'awiya, in the days of Uthman, that a group of Muslims had been stung by scorpions. Mu'awiya ordered him to assign to the people of each quarter of the city a set number of scorpions each night, which they would bring to him, and he would order them killed. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut makes Nasibin "a thriving city of the [[place/jazira|Jazira]] on the caravan road from [[place/mosul|Mosul]] to Syria"; in it and its villages, as its people tell, forty thousand gardens; between it and [[place/sinjar|Sinjar]] nine farsakhs, between it and Mosul six days, and two days — ten farsakhs — to Dunaysir. Its wall the Romans built and King Anushirwan finished at his capture of it; the reason given for that capture is that he besieged it without success, so scorpions were gathered for him from a village called Tiyaranshah and cast upon the defenders in jars and mangonels until they opened the city — all of it related by Ahmad ibn al-Tayyib al-Sarakhsi in one of his books and carried on his name {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.288}. He gives its longitude as seventy-five degrees twenty minutes and its latitude thirty-six degrees twelve minutes in the fourth clime — while the author of the Zij gives twenty-seven and a half — and calls it "an unhealthy city for its many gardens and waters"; he relates in some reports the isra' report concerning it, carried as a transmitted report {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.288-289}. Iyad ibn Ghanm marched on it, it resisted, he besieged it and took it on the like of al-Ruha's peace; he records the administrator's letter to Mu'awiya — Uthman's governor over Syria and the Jazira — complaining of Muslims stung by scorpions, and the order that each quarter of the city bring a named quota of scorpions nightly, and Abd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Utban's verses on the peace {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.289}.

## The museum's identification
The record's Nasibin is the Jazira city of the same entry; Yaqut names no other of the name here. The museum's own measurement — not Yaqut's words — between the records' points Nasibin (37.0758, 41.2146) and Sinjar (36.3209, 41.8766) gives 102.6 km by the haversine formula on a sphere of R = 6371.0088 km against the printed "nine farsakhs", and between Nasibin and Mosul (36.335, 43.1189) gives 188.7 km against the printed "six days".
