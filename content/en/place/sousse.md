## Overview
Sousse is a city on the coast of Tunisia. Ibn Battuta passed through it on his journey. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958}

## Note
The coordinates are taken from a modern settlement of the same name in the GeoNames gazetteer, and matching it to the ancient site needs review. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says that al-Sam'ani's account of Susa is confused, and that in his own judgement Sousse is a small city in the districts of Ifriqiya, two days from Sfax and three from al-Mahdiyya, whose people are mostly weavers of the fine cloth called Sousi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.282} He quotes Ibn Tahir that it is a town of the Maghrib that produced hadith scholars, jurists and men of letters, among them Yahya ibn Khalid al-Sousi, who related from Abd Allah ibn Wahb. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.282} He describes the sea as surrounding it on three sides, its wall of strong rock, a lighthouse known as the Manar of Khalaf al-Fata, eight gates and a great old amphitheatre. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.282} He tells how Mu'awiya ibn Hudayj sent Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr there when word came that the king of the Romans had dispatched a patrician, Nicephorus, with thirty thousand fighters; Abd Allah prayed the afternoon prayer at the city gate while the Romans looked on in surprise, then charged and routed them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.282} He adds that the Kharijite Abu Yazid Makhlad ibn Kaydad besieged it for months and then withdrew in defeat. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.282} The wall is credited to Ziyadat Allah ibn al-Aghlab, and Yaqut notes watch-posts and ribats for the devout inside and outside the city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.283}
