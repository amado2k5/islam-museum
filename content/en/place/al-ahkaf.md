## Summary
Al-Ahkaf is the title of Sura 46 of the Qur'an and the name of a region in southern Arabia. Dictionaries usually read the word as "curved sand dunes", but the regional name points to the mountains behind the coast of southern Arabia.

## Encyclopaedia of Islam account
The title of Sura 46 comes from the passage in which 'Ad are said to have warned their people in al-Ahkaf. Dictionaries, works of tafsir and translations usually render ahkaf as "curved sand dunes". Medieval Arab geographers placed al-Ahkaf as a sand desert of southern Arabia between Hadramawt and Oman, in the eastern part of al-Ramla or al-Rub' al-Khali, and some modern writers identify it with all of al-Ramla or its western half. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 279}

C. Landberg showed that al-Ahkaf as a regional name is used in southern Arabia roughly as a synonym of Hadramawt in the broadest sense, and is not applied to the sands farther north. For the southern bedouin, Barr al-Ahkaf is the mountainous area behind the coast from Zufar west to Aden, whose central valley is Wadi Hadramawt. To them akkaf means mountains, not dunes, or caves. A report of a statement made to 'Ali b. Abi Talib by a man of Hadramawt suggests that the word was used this way in ancient times. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 1 p. 279}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says al-Ahqaf is the plural of hiqf, a sand dune, the Arabs calling curved sand hiqaf and ahqaf, and that this is the obvious sense in their language. He then lists views on the Ahqaf named in the Qur'an: a valley between Oman and the land of Mahra, from Ibn Abbas; a sand tract between Oman and Hadramawt, for Ibn Ishaq; sands overlooking the sea at al-Shihr in Yemen, for Qatada; a mountain in Syria, for al-Dahhak; and, in the Kitab al-Ayn, a mountain encircling the world, which he takes as a description of Mount Qaf. He favours the first three, and holds that the sound view is that they are sands in Yemen where Ad lived. He then gives a transmitted report, through Hisham ibn Muhammad, in which Ali ibn Abi Talib, in the caliphate of Abu Bakr, asks a Hadrami man about the Ahqaf; the man says that in his youth he went with boys of his clan to a red dune with many caves, looking for the tomb of Hud, and describes seeing a dead man on a bier in one cave with a writing at his head naming Hud, and Ali says he heard the same from the Prophet. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.115} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.116}
