## Overview
Dania: a city with a populous suburb and a strong wall, whose wall is built on the sea with skill. Ships are built in it and the fleet sails out from it, and to its south lies a great mountain, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.557}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Dania as a city in al-Andalus, in the districts of Valencia, on the sea shore to the east, with a remarkable anchorage that he calls al-Samman, and wide rural districts rich in figs, grapes and almonds. He says it was the capital of the realm of Abu al-Jaysh Mujahid al-Amiri, and that its people were the best reciters of the Qur'an in al-Andalus, because Mujahid used to bring reciters, favour them and spend money on them, so that they came to him, stayed with him and multiplied in his lands. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.434}

Among its natives he names the master of the reciters Abu Amr Uthman ibn Sa'id al-Dani, author of works on the readings. He also quotes verses by Ali ibn Abd al-Ghani al-Husri, mourning his two sons, which name Dania in their opening. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.434}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Dania, a city on the sea in eastern al-Andalus, which agrees with al-Idrisi's description in the record of its wall on the sea and its shipbuilding. The step from Yaqut's name to the modern Denia rests on the museum's reading and the Wikidata match recorded for the place, and a source that equates the two outright would settle it.
