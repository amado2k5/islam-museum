## Overview
Shabwa is an ancient city of Himyar, between Bayhan and Hadramawt. In it is the Mountain of Salt, and its second mountain is shared with the people of Ma'rib, according to al-Hamdani. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.70}

Al-Hamdani relates that when Himyar and Madhhij fought each other, the people of Shabwa left it and settled in Hadramawt, and by them [[place/shibam|Shibam]] was named. He says that the original form was "shabah", and the mim was substituted for the ha'. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.70}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name Shabwa with a fatha on the first letter, a sukun on the second and a fatha on the waw — a name also given to the scorpion — and makes it the name of a place, quoting verses for it from a man of Banu 'Amir ibn 'Awbashan and from Bishr ibn Abi Khazim. He counts it among the fortresses of Yemen at Jabal Rayma; from al-Azdi it lies at the edge of Iraq in a verse of Ibn Muqbil; and from Nasr it is a town of Yemen on the highway from Hadramawt to Mecca. From Ibn al-Ha'ik, writing on the districts of Hadramawt, he relates that Shabwa is a city of Himyar and holds one of the two Mountains of Snow, the other belonging to the people of Ma'rib, and that when Madhhij and Himyar fought one another its people left it and settled in Hadramawt, and by them Shibam was named, the original form being Shabah with the mim substituted for the ha' — these are Ibn al-Ha'ik's words as he transmits them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.323} The book also has the mikhlaf of Shabwa, inhabited by the Ashba', the Abirun and those of its environs. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.67}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Hamdani (p.70), makes Shabwa an ancient city of Himyar between Bayhan and Hadramawt, holding the Mountain of Salt and sharing its second mountain with the people of Ma'rib, and relates its people's departure to Hadramawt, the naming of Shibam by them and the original form Shabah. Yaqut's entry (vol 3 p.323) carries the same account from Ibn al-Ha'ik on the districts of Hadramawt, so the two texts meet in the Himyari city, the two mountains and the migration to Shibam; Yaqut adds other places of the name — a fortress at Jabal Rayma, a Shabwa at the edge of Iraq and a mikhlaf (vol 5 p.67) — which are not to be conflated with it.
