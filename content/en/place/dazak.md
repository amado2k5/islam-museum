## Summary
Dazak was a town of Makran, described by al-Idrisi as a distinguished, large and flourishing city with much trade, marketable goods and adjoining districts. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}

## Details
Al-Idrisi lists Dazak among the towns of Makran together with Kiz, Rask, Band, Qasr Qand, Asqafa, Falfahra, Mashki, al-Tiz and al-Balin; Makran is a land of wide connected tracts, mostly desert, drought and hardship. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.173} The people of Dazak drank from springs and wells, and to its west, inclining southward, rose a great impregnable mountain called the Salt Mountain because most of its waters were brackish; it contained buildings and villages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174} From the city of Fayrbuz to Dazak was three stages, and from Dazak to Rask three stages; the people of Rask were Kharijites, and it had two districts, one called al-Khuruj and the other Kirkayan, with much sugar-cane and plenty of fanidh sugar made there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}
