## Overview
al-Ushmunayn: a small, populous, well-kept town with gardens, palm trees and crops, known for the cloths that are made in it. al-Idrisi reports that it lies about half a day or a little more from Munyat Ibn al-Khasib. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 124} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 125}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut calls al-Ushmunayn an ancient city, still inhabited, the chief town of a kura of lower Upper Egypt, west of the Nile (the wording does not make clear whether this describes the city or the kura), with gardens and many palms. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.200} They said, with no speaker named, that it was named for its builder Ashmun, a son of Misr, who received from his father the districts from Ashmun and below it to Memphis, east and west, and settled at Ashmun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.200} He lists scholars with the name, among them Abu Isma'il Dammam ibn Isma'il al-Ashmuni, who died in Alexandria in 185, and Hajna' ibn Qays al-Harithi, and records Ibn Yunus's remark on Hajna' and two slips that al-Sam'ani made over him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.200} The kura of al-Ushmunayn is also named in the list of the kuras of Upper Egypt taken from al-Quda'i. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.139}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, pp.124-125) calls it a small, populous, well-kept town with gardens, palms and crops, known for its cloths, about half a day from Munyat Ibn al-Khasib. Yaqut agrees on gardens and palms; his entry (vol 1 p.200) says it is old and the chief town of a kura, not small, and does not mention cloths or Munyat Ibn al-Khasib. The two accounts are of different kinds and the museum does not set one against the other. That al-Idrisi's town is the one in Yaqut's entry is the museum's reading. The derivation from Ashmun belongs to the unnamed speakers and is not the museum's.
