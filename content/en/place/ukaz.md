## Summary
Ukaz was one of the famous markets of the Arabs. It was held in the sacred months alongside [[place/dhu-al-majaz|Dhu al-Majaz]], [[place/majanna|Majanna]] and others, as The Sealed Nectar presents it. Trade was the greatest means of obtaining the necessities of life, and it could only be carried on in safety, which was missing across the peninsula except in those months {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.46}. See [[concept/economic-condition-jahiliyya|the economic condition]].

## What is not covered here
The site of Ukaz near al-Ta'if, the poetic boasting that took place there, and the Prophet's going out there to present himself to the tribes belong at their own places in the book. They will be added when we reach them.

## For young visitors
Ukaz was a great market where Arabs gathered in ancient times for trade and poetry contests, in the months when there was no fighting.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with damma on the first letter and a final dotted za. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142} He quotes al-Layth that it was so named because the Arabs gathered there and pressed one another with boasting, adds other views on the root, and reports from al-Suhayli that they vied in glory in its market when they met. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142} He calls it a market of the Arabs in the Jahiliyya where the tribes met every year to boast, their poets attended and recited their new verse, and then all dispersed; the leather called 'Ukazi is named for it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142} He reports from al-Asma'i that it is a palm grove in a valley one night from al-Ta'if and three nights from Mecca, that the market stood at a spot in it called al-Uthayda, and that the days of the Fijar were held there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142} From al-Waqidi he reports that it lies between Nakhla and al-Ta'if, with Dhu al-Majaz behind Arafa and Majanna at Marr al-Zahran, 'Ukaz being the greatest of them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142} Others, he says, held that the Arabs stayed at 'Ukaz through Shawwal, moved to Majanna for twenty days of Dhu al-Qa'da, then to Dhu al-Majaz until the days of the pilgrimage. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.142}
