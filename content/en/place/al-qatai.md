## Overview
Al-Qata'i was the government suburb to which [[person/ahmad-ibn-tulun|Ahmad ibn Tulun]] moved the seat of government from al-Askar after he came to Egypt, at the foot of the present Citadel below the "Dome of the Air". {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.29} He laid it out on the model of Samarra, the Abbasid capital where he had grown up: it covered over 1.6 square kilometres and held a mosque, government buildings and a palace complex beside a hippodrome. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.28} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.29}

## Before it: al-Askar
When the caliphate passed to the Abbasids in 750 the governors left Fustat and set up a new centre in the suburb of al-Askar to the north-east, formerly the district of al-Hamra al-Quswa; it became their army's camp, and there for the first time they built a palace as the official seat of government. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.27} They also built a palace known as the Dome of the Air on a spur of the Muqattam hills, where the Citadel now stands. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.27}

## The Maydan and its gates
The palace comprised several buildings, among them a separate harem, extensive gardens and a menagerie; palace and hippodrome together were called al-Maydan, the city's recreational centre for military parades, horse races and polo. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.29} Gates served different classes: a triple gate, the Bab al-Maydan, was reserved for the army, who rode through its outer arches leaving the central arch for Ibn Tulun; others were the Gate of the Nobles, the Gate of the Harem, the Gate of al-Darmun, the Saj Gate and the Gate of the Lions, crowned by a gallery and two stucco lions. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.29}

The city had a hospital, many markets and bath-houses supplied by the Aqueduct of Basatin, which brought water from a spring in the southern desert and was built by a Copt. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.29} Its greatest building was the [[building/masjid-ibn-tulun|Mosque of Ibn Tulun]]. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.32}

## The palaces of Khumarawayh
[[person/khumarawayh|Khumarawayh]] son of Ahmad enlarged the palace and turned the Maydan into a garden; Yeomans quotes al-Maqrizi's description of its trees sheathed in gilt copper and of the lake of quicksilver laid out before the palace. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.40} {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.41}

## The destruction
After the Tulunids weakened, the caliph sent his army to Syria and then against Egypt by land and sea; it took Fustat in 905, and the commander Muhammad ibn Sulayman let his troops loose, so that al-Qata'i with all its palaces was destroyed and only the Mosque of Ibn Tulun was left. {cite:yeomans-islamic-cairo-2006 p.41}
