## Summary
Jenin is a town in the northern West Bank at the foot of the Nablus hills on the edge of the Jezreel valley (Marj Ibn Amir), between its plain and Mount Gilboa. The ancient sources know it as Gina of the Amarna letters and biblical Ein-Ganim; the Crusaders called it "Le Grand Gerin," and Saladin retook it in 1187. {cite:marom-lajjun-2023} {cite:wafa-news}

## Postal station and Turabay capital
Under the Mamluks Jenin was a subdistrict of the province of Safad and a key station on the Cairo–Damascus postal route. In Ottoman times it became a nahiya centre in the sanjak of Lajjun; Emir Ahmad ibn Turabay moved his emirate's capital there in 1601, and the Ottomans built the Saraya opposite the [[building/great-mosque-of-jenin|Great Mosque]] in 1882. Yaqut described it around 1225 as "a small and beautiful town" rich in water and springs. {cite:marom-lajjun-2023} {cite:travel-palestine}
