## Overview
Yaqut describes al-Mizza as a large, fertile village in the middle of the orchards of Damascus, half a farsakh from the city, also called Mizzat Kalb. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.122}

## Yaqut's entry
Yaqut says he suspects the name is foreign, since he knows of no Arabic meaning for it with a kasra on the m. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.122} He reports that the grave of Dihya al-Kalbi, a companion of the Prophet, is said to be there. The entry gives this as what is said, not as settled fact. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.122}

Yaqut also quotes two lines by Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat about nights spent at Mizzat Kalb, in which the poet speaks of drink. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.122}
