## Summary
Mardin is a great city on a mountaintop — among the most beautiful and finely situated cities of Islam, in Ibn Battuta's judgment — crowned by a soaring citadel called al-Shahba'. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p. 153}

## Details
Ibn Battuta reached the city of Mardin and described it as great, set on the flat top of a mountain, among the most beautiful of the cities of Islam and the most elegantly situated. It has a towering citadel, one of the most renowned of fortresses, on the summit of its mountain; Ibn Juzay notes that this citadel of Mardin is called al-Shahba'. Its lord was al-Malik al-Mansur Najm al-Din, a generous man of wide fame who held its rule for about fifty years and lived to see the days of Qazan, khan of the Tatars. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p. 153}

In 281 AH al-Mu'tadid marched on Mardin, fought those in its citadel all day, climbed until he came near the gate and called out the name of Hamdan ibn Hamdun, who answered. He told him to open the gate; it was opened, he sat in it, its contents were removed, and he ordered the citadel demolished, and it was demolished. {cite:muntazam-ibn-al-jawzi p. 147} The coordinates of the modern city bearing the name come from GeoNames. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}
