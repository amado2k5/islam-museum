## Summary
Qawun is a large, populous city on the river Danube, with markets and crafts. Al-Idrisi says that it and the city of Baqsin are the two chief cities of the land of Ankarya, the most built-up part of that land and the richest in wealth and estates. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885}

## Details
Qawun is a large and populous city on the river Danube, with markets and crafts. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885} Al-Idrisi says that it and Baqsin are the two chief cities of the land of Ankarya. Both are described as noble and populous, with many people coming and going, and the land of Ankarya is the most built-up part of the region and the richest in wealth and estates. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885}

Baqsin lies to the west of Qawun, and the distance between them is sixty miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.884} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885} From Abrandas to Qawun, on the Danube, is seventy miles, and from Abrandas to Baqsin the same. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885}

Al-Idrisi gives the road from Aqra to Qawun as one hundred miles and sixty miles, and from Aqra to Basu as eighty miles in the direction of the west and south. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885}

Near Buzana, two rivers that rise in the land of Bawamiya join the Danube. They run apart and come together again before they flow into the Danube. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.884} Qawun stands on this river, and Idrisi counts it among the cities of Ankarya. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.885}
