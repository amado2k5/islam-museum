## Overview
Tirqa is one of the cities of Wanqara, a large city with many people, but without a wall or enclosure. It is under the authority of the ruler of [[place/ghana|Ghana]], for whom they give the sermon and to whom they bring their disputes. Between Ghana and Tirqa is six days, and the road runs along the Nile. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 25}

## Wanqara
Al-Idrisi describes Wanqara as having populous lands and famous strongholds; its people are rich, gold dust is plentiful in their hands, and good things are brought to them from the edges and far corners of the earth. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 25}

## Neighbours
From the city of Tirqa to the city of Madasa is six days; it is a medium-sized city, thickly built, with sound districts, on the north of the Nile from which its people drink, a land of rice and large-grained sorghum, living mostly by fishing and trading in gold dust. From Madasa to the land of Saghmara is six stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 25}
