## Overview
A valley north of Medina, where [[person/zayd-ibn-haritha|Zayd ibn Haritha]] met [[group/banu-fazara|Banu Fazara]] twice. See [[event/raid-of-zayd-on-banu-fazara-and-umm-qirfa|The raid of Zayd on Banu Fazara]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/265}

## On the differing counts of expeditions
Al-Mas'udi mentions that whoever counted the expeditions at twenty-seven distinguished the Prophet's return from Khaybar to Wadi al-Qura as another expedition. See [[concept/khilaf-adad-al-maghazi|The disagreement over the number of expeditions]]. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.579}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says he treated Wadi al-Qura under the entry "al-Qura" and spelled out its derivation there, and here describes it as a valley between Medina and Syria, within the province of Medina, rich in villages, the adjective being "wadi". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.345} He states that the Prophet conquered it in the year 7 by force, after which its people made peace on payment of the jizya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.345} Citing Ahmad ibn Jabir, he relates that after Khaybar the Prophet went to Wadi al-Qura, invited its people to Islam, and when they refused and fought him took it by force, seized its property and set aside the fifth of the goods, leaving the palms and land with the Jews on terms like those given to Khaybar. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.345} He gives two views: that 'Umar expelled its Jews and divided the land among those who had fought for it, or that he did not, because it lay outside the Hijaz and in Yaqut's day was attached to the province of Medina; the conquest fell in Jumada II of the year 7. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.345} He cites verses by Abu Ya'la al-Ma'arri and by Jamil. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.345} Among those named after it he lists Yahya ibn Abi 'Ubayda al-Wadi, whose name was Yahya ibn Rija, a client of Quraysh described as reliable in hadith and said to have died in 240 AH, and 'Umar ibn Dawud ibn Zadhan, a client of 'Uthman, known as 'Umar al-Wadi the singer, an engineer in the days of al-Walid ibn Yazid who fled when al-Walid was killed, and the teacher of Hakam al-Wadi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.345}
