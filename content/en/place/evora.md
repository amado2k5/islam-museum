## Overview
Yabura is a town in the west of al-Andalus as Yaqut al-Hamawi records it, with two transmitters of hadith and letters named after it; the museum reads it as Évora in Portugal. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.424}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Yabura" and says it is a town in the west of al-Andalus, with no further description. He then names those attributed to it: Abu Bakr Abd Allah ibn Talha ibn Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Yaburi al-Andalusi, who heard and transmitted hadith and died at Mecca in 523, as Abu al-Hasan al-Maqdisi says, adding that more than one person narrated from him; and Khalaf ibn Fath ibn Nadir al-Yaburi, who settled in Cordoba, had the kunya Abu al-Qasim, narrated from Abu Muhammad Abd Allah ibn Said al-Shaqqaq and the judge Hammam ibn Ahmad and their like, was learned in letters and language and foremost in them, with goodness and religion, and died in Dhu al-Hijja 439. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.424}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut does not name Évora and gives no coordinates, so the link rests on the Arabic name in the record's label and the Wikidata identifier Q179948 recorded for it; his phrase "west of al-Andalus" fits that site without fixing it, and the museum does not count either as independent proof. The record's coordinates (38.57, -7.91) come from Wikidata and are marked approximate. On the same page Yaqut lists "Yabisa", an island near al-Andalus on the route of those who sail from Denia to Majorca, which is a different place from Yabura. The record's descriptions as a "province" and a "municipality" come from Wikidata, not from Yaqut.
