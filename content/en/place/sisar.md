## Overview
Sisar is a town in the mountains between Hamadhan, al-Dinawar and Azerbaijan. According to al-Baladhuri, Sulayman ibn Qayrat and Salam al-Tayfuri built it by order of al-Mahdi, as a refuge for his animals and sheep from the destitute (sa'alik). It was named after thirty mounds and a hundred springs. Districts of al-Dinawar and Azerbaijan were attached to it, and it became a district (kura). Al-Rashid stationed a thousand men in it. It was taken from Asim ibn Murra in the caliphate of al-Ma'mun and became one of the estates of the caliphate. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Sisar as a town on the border of Hamadhan, between Hamadhan and Adharbayjan. He gives the folk etymology that it is set low among thirty hills, and says that its fortress and town were founded in the days of al-Amin ibn al-Rashid. Its many springs earned it the older name Sadkhaniya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.297}

Pastures for the herds of the Kurds and others lay there until al-Mahdi sent a freedman, Salman ibn Qayrat, with a partner, Salam al-Tayfuri. They built a fort there and gathered herds in it, and the town was formed from the districts of Dinawar, Jawdhama in the Barza district of Adharbayjan, and Khanijir. Murra ibn Abi Murra al-Ijli held it in the time of the civil war between al-Amin and al-Ma'mun and checked the Kharijites; when al-Ma'mun's rule was settled, the town was taken from him and put under the crown lands. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.297}
