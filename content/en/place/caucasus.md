## Overview
Yaqut's name for the Caucasus range is al-Qabq, a Persian word. He describes it as a mountain joined to Bab al-Abwab (the Derbent pass) and to the land of the Lan, and as the end of the Armenian frontier. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.306} The range runs from the Caspian to the Black Sea. Yaqut says it ends at Trabzon, where the sea cuts across it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.216} The modern name Caucasus and the modern limits of the range are the record's, not Yaqut's.

## What Yaqut reports
Citing Ibn al-Faqih, Yaqut says the mountain of al-Qabq has seventy-two languages, so that no one can understand another's speech without an interpreter, and that it runs some five hundred farsakh toward the Byzantine lands as far as the Khazar and Lan lands. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.306} He also records the view that this is the al-Araj range between Mecca and Medina, which runs on into Syria. In the Lebanon entry he says the range continues from Lebanon through Antioch and Samosata, where it is called al-Lukkam, and on to the Khazar sea, where it is called al-Qabq. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.11}

## The Lan
Yaqut describes the Lan as a wide land and a numerous people whose territory borders Derbent in the Qabq mountains. Their country had no large famous city, Muslims lived among them, and Christians were the majority. They had no single king; each group had its own amir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.245}
