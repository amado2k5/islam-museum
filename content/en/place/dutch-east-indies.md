## Overview
The Dutch East Indies was an archipelago in south-east Asia ruled by the Netherlands. Shakib Arslan presents it in a chapter that includes a lecture by Sayyid Isma'il al-Attas, delivered in Cairo in 1929. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923}

The lecture covers the history of the islands, from prehistoric remains to Dutch colonisation. It says that the Muslims there numbered about fifty million, according to a census the author cites, and that agriculture rests on rice, sugar, coconut and other crops. Arslan notes that the official Dutch figures and those of French writers did not agree.

This is one source's picture of conditions on the islands in the 1920s, not a verified census. An additional source is needed: Dutch census data for 1920 to 1930. The prehistory section is a summary of the lecture and is not accepted as archaeology without a second source.
