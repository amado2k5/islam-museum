## Summary
Suq Banu Zandawi was a fortress in a flat tract of land, of little strength, holding a Friday market which the people of that district attended on that day, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.267}

## Details
On the road from Bijaya, al-Idrisi reports that from Mount Sahaw one comes to Suq Banu Zandawi, a fortress in a flat tract with little defensibility; it has a market held on Friday which the people of that district attend on that day {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.267}. This Berber tribe, he adds, is a people who populate these parts and possess a stronghold and means of defence {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.267}. The mention of the fortress follows his description of Wadi Shal and Suq Yusuf at the foot of a hard-to-climb mountain pierced by fresh water {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.267}. Al-Idrisi thus depicted Suq Banu Zandawi less as a citadel than as a weekly market-fort, visited by its district's people every Friday in a mountainous stretch of the Berber country.
