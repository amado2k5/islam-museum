## Summary
Babylon of Egypt is an ancient fortress on the Nile near Heliopolis, on the site of al-Fustat; Golius considers it to be the fortress described by Strabo. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 396}

Golius traces the name Babylon in ancient writers and holds that the fortress near Heliopolis on the Nile described by Strabo is the same fortress of Egyptian Babylon. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 396}

He notes that later Greek historians called al-Fustat, which in their time was known as Cairo, "Egyptian Babylon". {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 396}

During the Arab conquest of Egypt, al-Muqawqis, a governor for Heraclius, took refuge on an island near the fortress and concluded a treaty with 'Amr, according to the Arabic books from which Golius quotes. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 399}
