## Overview
A monastery in the districts of [[place/kufa|Kufa]]. al-Baladhuri reports, on the authority of al-Sharqi ibn al-Qatami, that a battle took place between Iyad and Banu Bahra' and Banu al-Qayn, in which a great number of Iyad were killed, and they buried them by the monastery. Afterwards people used to dig and bring out skulls, so it was called the Monastery of the Skulls. Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ib al-Kalbi gives another explanation: that Malik al-Rimah al-Iyadi killed a group of Persians and set up their skulls by the monastery. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.398}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Dayr al-Jamajim" and says it is outside Kufa, seven farsakhs from it, on the edge of the open land for one going to Basra. He gives several explanations of the name. From Abu Ubayda, a jamjama is a wooden cup, so the monastery was named because wooden cups were made there; a jamjama is also a well dug in a salt flat, and the place may be named for that. From Ibn al-Kalbi, when Tamim and Dhubyan fought Amir and Amir won and many of Tamim were killed, they built this monastery of their skulls in thanks for their victory; Yaqut rejects this as far from the truth and falsely put on Ibn al-Kalbi, because the battle of Amir with Tamim and Dhubyan was at Sha'b Jabala in Najd, not at Kufa. He reports what al-Baladhuri related from Ibn al-Kalbi, that "Bilada al-Rammah, and some say Bilal al-Rammah, which is better established", son of Mahraz al-Iyadi, killed some Persians and set up their heads at the monastery, which is why it was called the Monastery of Skulls. In Ibn al-Kalbi's Ansab al-mawadi' he found that Kisra had killed Iyad and exiled them to Syria; a thousand horsemen of theirs came back and settled in the Sawad, Kisra sent about 1,400 horsemen against them, the men of Iyad killed them to the last and made a dome of their skulls, and when Kisra heard he came out with their families weeping, was grieved and ordered a monastery built over them, which was called Dayr al-Jamajim. Another account says that Iyad and Banu Nahd fought on the spot, many of Iyad and Quda'a were killed and buried there, and people who dug there brought out their skulls. He adds that at this spot took place the battle between al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi and Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad ibn al-Ash'ath in which Ibn al-Ash'ath was broken and the Qur'an readers were killed, and he quotes verses of Jarir on it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.503-504} Under the plural al-Jamajim he repeats that Dayr al-Jamajim is a place mentioned among the monasteries, with the same explanation from Abu Ubayda. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.159} In the entry on Dayr Qurra he says that monastery stood opposite Dayr al-Jamajim and that al-Hajjaj camped there when Ibn al-Ash'ath was at Dayr al-Jamajim; that Ibn al-Ash'ath took Dayr al-Jamajim so that supplies would reach him from Kufa; and that Dayr al-Jamajim is the one nearer Kufa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.526}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut gives more than one explanation of the name, some of them the same as the record's from al-Baladhuri and some not in it: the cup and the well from Abu Ubayda, the story of Kisra and Iyad, the war between Iyad and Banu Nahd and the battle of al-Hajjaj and Ibn al-Ash'ath. He does not choose between them except to reject Ibn al-Kalbi's first account. The killer's name appears in the record as Malik al-Rammah ibn Mukhriz, from al-Baladhuri, and in Yaqut as "Bilada" or "Bilal al-Rammah", son of Mahraz; these are differences of reading between two texts and the museum does not choose. The plainest problem is the coordinates: the record's point (32.0333, 44.4) is only about 4.3 km from the museum's Kufa point (32.0511, 44.4402), whereas Yaqut puts the monastery seven farsakhs from Kufa on the Basra road, about 40 km at six kilometres to the farsakh. The recorded point cannot be its site and is most likely a stand-in near Kufa; the museum has not changed it and has sent the question for outside research. Yaqut says nothing about the Wikidata identifier Q2949566 recorded for it.
