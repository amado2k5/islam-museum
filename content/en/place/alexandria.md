## Overview
An Egyptian port on the Mediterranean. Before Fustat was founded it was the seat of rule in Egypt, and al-Maqrizi reports that it stayed a house of learning and seat of wisdom until the Muslims took it. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.422}

## Alexandria in al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that Alexander son of Philip the Macedonian renewed the city, and that the seat of the kingdom moved to it from Memphis, so that it became the seat of rule in Egypt. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.446}

He quotes Abu al-Salt Umayya ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Andalusi, who says that it was "the house of learning and seat of wisdom" until the Muslims took it in the days of Umar ibn al-Khattab. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.422}

When Amr ibn al-As came with the Muslim armies and took Alexandria, the seat of the kingdom moved from Alexandria to Fustat in Egypt, and Fustat became the seat of rule of Egypt after Alexandria. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.446}

## For Sprouts
Alexandria is an Egyptian port on the Mediterranean. It was Egypt's seat of kingship before Fustat and stayed the home of learning and seat of wisdom until the Muslims opened it in the days of Umar ibn al-Khattab.

## For Explorers
Alexandria is an Egyptian port on the Mediterranean. Al-Maqrizi reports that Alexander son of Philip the Macedonian renewed it, moving the royal seat from Memphis to make it the seat of kingship in Egypt; he quotes Abu al-Salt that it 'was the home of learning and seat of wisdom' until the Muslims opened it in the days of Umar ibn al-Khattab — when Amr ibn al-As took it with the Muslim armies the seat of kingship moved to Fustat. He records, from Ibn Abd al-Hakam and Abu al-Fida, that it was a triple city, each quarter walled, its streets ordered like a chessboard; that the Romans resisted Umar nine months and retook it later; and that the Franks besieged it in 562 AH until Saladin and his uncle Asad al-Din Shirkuh repelled them after four months.

## For Scholars
Alexandria is an Egyptian port on the Mediterranean, Egypt's seat of kingship before Fustat, and it remained the home of learning and seat of wisdom until the Muslims opened it. Al-Maqrizi reports that Alexander son of Philip the Macedonian renewed it, the royal seat moving from Memphis to make it the seat of kingship in Egypt; he quotes Abu al-Salt Umayya ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Andalusi that it 'was the home of learning and seat of wisdom' until the Muslims opened it in the days of Umar ibn al-Khattab — when Amr ibn al-As arrived with the Muslim armies and opened it the royal seat moved to Fustat, which became the seat of kingship in Egypt. From the geographers and historians: a triple city, each quarter walled, streets ordered like a chessboard; the Romans resisted Umar nine months then retook it after a time; and the Franks besieged it in 562 AH until Saladin with his uncle Asad al-Din Shirkuh repelled them after four months.
