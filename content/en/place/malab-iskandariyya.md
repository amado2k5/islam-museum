## Overview
Al-Mal'ab ("the playing field") is an ancient site in Alexandria, where its people held a festival every year. A ball was thrown there, and whoever it fell into the sleeve of was made king {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.241-242}.

## According to al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi, quoting al-Qada'i, lists the playing field among Alexandria's wonders, with the lighthouse and the pillars. People gathered there one day of the year and a ball was thrown; it fell into no one's lap but that of the king of Egypt {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.484}.

He says that Amr ibn al-As attended one of their festivals, the ball fell into his lap, and he afterwards ruled the land {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.484}.

## The episode of Amr ibn al-As
Al-Umari reports that [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] attended it in the Jahiliyya, and the ball fell into his sleeve. They said that the custom had been abolished, because such a thing cannot be owned {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.241-242}.

## The site afterwards
On the site of this playing field, the Banu Khalif built their palace, which is named after them. It is in [[place/iskandariyya|Alexandria]] {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.241-242}.
