## Overview
Tadla is a town in Berber country in the far west of the Maghrib. Al-Idrisi describes it on the road from Aghmat towards the east and north: four days from Aghmat to Dai and Tadla, and one stage between Dai and Tadla. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.241}

## Cotton
Al-Idrisi credits Tadla and Dai with a great deal of cotton, but he says Tadla grows more of it than Dai does. Much cotton leaves Tadla and is carried to every region, and the cotton cloth made in the far Maghrib is made from it, so its people need no cotton brought in from other lands. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.241}

## Road and neighbours
From Tadla it is four stages to the town of Tatn and its villages. Tatn is a small but civilised town, inhabited by a mixed Berber population, with farms, plenty of wheat, and flocks of livestock and sheep. From Tatn to Sala on the coast is two days, and from Sala to Fez is four stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.242}

East of Tadla and Dai live Berber tribes, among them Banu Wuliyhim, Banu Wuzikun and Mandasa, and on the mountain that descends to Dai live people of Sanhaja called the Amlu. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.242}

Al-Idrisi sums up both towns together: they have livelihoods, provisions, fertility and many kinds of plenty, and their people are a mixture of Berbers. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.241} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.242}
