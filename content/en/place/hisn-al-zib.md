## Summary
Hisn al-Zib is a handsome fortress on the shore of the Salt Sea, twelve miles from the city of Acre. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Acre to Hisn al-Zib is twelve miles, that it is a handsome fortress on the shore of the Salt Sea, and that from it to al-Nawaqir — three tall white mountains overlooking the seashore — is about eighteen miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}. He describes Acre before it as a great city, wide in its bounds, rich in estates, with a fine and secure anchorage and a mixed population, and notes that from Tiberias to Acre is two days {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}. Hisn al-Zib is thus one of the small strongholds of the Syrian coast between Acre and al-Nawaqir, which al-Idrisi makes a station on the coastal road leading from Acre toward the city of Alexandria and the western shore; its purpose was to keep watch over the seashore at a spot neighbouring the white mountains overlooking the water. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.365}
