## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes Qazvin as a famous city twenty-seven farsakhs from Rayy and twelve from Abhar, in the fourth climate, with longitude 75 degrees and latitude 37. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.342} Citing Ibn al-Faqih, he says the first to found it was Shapur Dhu al-Aktaf, that its fort is called Kashrin in Persian, and that between it and the Daylam stood a mountain where the kings of the land kept a frontier guard of cavalrymen (asawira) to hold the Daylamites back and protect the country. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.342}

He recounts that Uthman appointed al-Bara' ibn Azib over Rayy in 24 AH; al-Bara' conquered Abhar and then besieged Qazvin. Its people made peace on the terms given to Abhar except for the poll tax; seeing his insistence they embraced Islam, so their land became tithe land, and al-Bara' settled five hundred Muslims among them, including Tulayha ibn Khuwaylid al-Asadi, Maysara al-'A'idhi and a group of the Banu Taghlib, giving them land grants; they were called the "tanna'" (residents). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.342} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} He adds that when Sa'id ibn al-'As governed Kufa he raided the Daylam, came to Qazvin and made it a garrison town and a base for the Kufans against the Daylam, and that al-Hadi ordered a city built opposite it, known as Madinat Musa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343}

He tells how Harun al-Rashid, passing through Hamadhan, was met by Qazvin's people who described their struggle at the frontier; he entered the city, built its congregational mosque with his name on the door, endowed shops and rents for the city, and fixed their dues at a lump sum of ten thousand dirhams a year in place of the tithe on crops. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} Yaqut remarks that hadith transmitters have reported sayings in praise of Qazvin which the critical hadith scholars do not accept as sound, and that he omitted them to avoid length. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} He also says that al-Hajjaj sent his son Muhammad against the Daylam, who stayed in Qazvin and built a mosque, called the Mosque of the Bull, which stood until al-Rashid built the congregational mosque. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} He quotes verses by al-Hawli ibn al-Jawn and al-Tirimmah ibn Hakim and notes that countless people are named after Qazvin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.343} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.344}
