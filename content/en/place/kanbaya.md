## Summary
Kanbaya is a coastal town of al-Sind, reached from Mamhal across a continuous, waterless and uninhabited desert waste. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.170}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Mamhal to Kanbaya on the sea runs an unbroken waste with no inhabitant or companion and little water, which no one crosses for the wildness of its ground and the distance of its reaches {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.170}. Mamhal lies between India and al-Sind, and on the fringes of this waste dwell a nomad people called al-Mand whose pastures stretch to Mamhal, owning camels and sheep and ranging as far as al-Rur on the bank of the Mihran river and sometimes near the borders of Makran {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.170}. Kanbaya thus stands at the edge of that desolate coast.
