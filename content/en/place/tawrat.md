## Summary
Tawrat is a large, populated village on the salt river. Its people drink from springs dug in the bed of a valley that comes down from the east, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.262}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Tawrat on a road that passes through the country of the Banu Tarakish. From the fortress of al-Hadid is one stage; from al-Shu'ara to the tombs of the Banu Tarakish is another stage; and from there the road reaches Tawrat. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.262}

He calls Tawrat a large, populated village on a salt river. It has an inn, and its people drink from springs dug in the bed of a valley that comes from the east; that valley has no water of its own. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.262}

From Tawrat the road goes to al-Bab, a range of mountains through which the salt river cuts, with a narrow pass and a frightening place. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.262}

Al-Idrisi does not describe Tawrat's farming, population or markets in this passage. The entry records its place on the river and how its people got water. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.262}
