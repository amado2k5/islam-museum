## Overview
al-Muqattam: a mountain that extends from the first land of Egypt in the desert to near Aswan. al-Idrisi reports that ochre and lime are extracted from some of its places. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.132}

The author reports that the mountain has ancient remains and treasures that kings hid, and he ascribes to a widely told account the story of a priest with whom the mountain was associated. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.133}

## In Ibn Abd al-Hakam
Al-Layth reports that [[person/al-muqawqis|al-Muqawqis]] asked Amr ibn al-As to sell him the foot of al-Muqattam for seventy thousand dinars. Amr wrote to Umar, who told him to ask why he offered so much for ground that could not be farmed and gave no water. Al-Muqawqis said they found it described in their books as holding the plants of Paradise. Umar wrote: "We know of no plants of Paradise but the believers; bury there those of the Muslims who die in your land, and do not sell it for anything." {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 211} The first buried there was a man of al-Ma'afir named Amir. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 211} He reports from Ibn Lahi'a, from someone who told him, that five known Companions were buried there, among them Amr ibn al-As, Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Jaz' al-Zubaydi and Uqba ibn Amir al-Juhani. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 211}
