## Overview
Quhistan is the city nearest to al-Tabasayn at the gate of Khurasan. Abd Allah ibn Amir and al-Ahnaf ibn Qays came to it, and they met the Hephthalites (al-Hayatila), who were Turks. It is also said that they were a people of Persia whom Firuz exiled to Herat. They became helpers of the Turks against the people of Quhistan, and the Muslims defeated them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut explains that Quhistan is an Arabised form of Kuhistan, meaning land of mountains, and that many regions of Persia have a place with this name. The best known one lies on the edge near Herat and runs through the mountains as far as Nihavand, Hamadhan and Burujird, the range between Herat and Nishapur. Abd Allah ibn Amir ibn Kurayz conquered it in the year 29 AH. In his time, he says, these mountains were held by those he calls the Mulhida, the followers of al-Hasan ibn al-Sabbah. Citing al-Bashari, he lists among its towns Qayn, the capital, Tun, Janabidh, Tabas al-Inab, Tabas al-Tamr and Turithith. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.416}
