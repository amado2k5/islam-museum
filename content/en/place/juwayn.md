## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Juwayn is a notable district on the caravan road from Bastam to Nishapur, its main town Azadhwar, with a hundred and eighty-nine villages, and the home of many imams and scholars, the best known being Imam al-Haramayn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.192} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.193}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Juwayn is a fine and pleasant district on the caravan road from Bastam to Nishapur, called Kuyan by the people of Khurasan and arabised as Juwayn. Its borders join those of Bayhaq on the south and Jajarm on the north, and its main town Azadhwar lies at its western end; he says he saw it. He reports from Abu al-Qasim al-Bayhaqi two explanations of the name: that it was the name of one of its amirs, or that it comes from kuy. By his account it is a long district between two mountains in an open plain, divided in half: villages stand side by side from east to west in the northern half, and in the southern half qanats were dug to water the villages and nothing is built. Nishapur is about ten farsakhs away. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.192}

## Scholars of Juwayn
Many imams and scholars are attributed to Juwayn. Yaqut names Musa ibn al-Abbas Abu Imran al-Juwayni al-Naysaburi, a traveller in search of hadith who died at Juwayn in 323; Abd Allah ibn Yusuf al-Juwayni, the leading scholar of his age at Nishapur and father of Abu al-Maali, who died in 434; his brother Ali ibn Yusuf, known as the shaykh of the Hijaz, author of Kitab al-Salwa on the sciences of the Sufis, who died in 463; and Abu al-Maali Abd al-Malik, Imam al-Haramayn, with his Nihayat al-matlab, al-Shamil on the principles of religion and al-Irshad among his works, who died at Nishapur in Rabi II of 478. Yaqut also lists another Juwayn, a village of Sarakhs, from which came Abu al-Maali Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Juwayni al-Sarakhsi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.192} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.193}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Juwayn a region, with coordinates in Khurasan between Bastam and Nishapur, so it lies in the area Yaqut describes. The Juwayn that is a village of Sarakhs in Yaqut is a different place and is not what this record means. Imam al-Haramayn has his own record, [[person/imam-al-haramayn-al-juwayni|Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni]], and [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]] and Bastam have theirs.
