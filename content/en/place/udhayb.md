## Overview
Udhayb is a stage at the foot of a mountain on the road from Medina to al-Jar, and it has a well of sweet, flowing water, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 144}

The road from Medina runs first to Dhu Khashab, a stage, then to Udhayb, then to al-Jar. Al-Jar is a populous village on the bank of the salt sea. It was formerly a town near Jidda, and it is Medina's harbour, where ships come and go, although it has little trade. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 144}

From al-Jar to Jidda is about ten days by land along the coast. The sea sometimes draws away and sometimes comes close, and most of these stages cross dry sand and worn tracks, where travellers are guided by the sea and the mountains. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 144}

Al-Idrisi also names another Udhayb, near al-Qadisiyya in Iraq at the first edge of the desert. That is a different place. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 383}

The geographer Yaqut al-Hamawi mentions a village called al-'Udhayba between al-Jar and Yanbu'. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 332}
