## Overview
Samasta is an estate with palaces about two miles from the main Nile, populous with people, according to al-Idrisi. It has sugar cane farms, from which sugar and fanid are made. He counts it together with Tarfa among the inhabited estates of Egypt. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.131}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a damma on the first two letters, then a second plain sin, a ta and a shortened alif, and reports Abu al-Fadl as saying «Samasta» belongs to the district of al-Bahnasa, noting that some say «Samasta» with two fathas. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.250} He calls it a village in lower Upper Egypt, from al-Bahnasa on the western bank of the Nile, to which the «Samastan bundles» are attributed, bundles of rope that nothing of their kind surpasses. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.250} He names Abu al-Husayn Ahmad ibn Surur al-Samastawi the scribe, whom al-Silafi saw in Mecca in 497 AH and later in Alexandria and in Egypt; he knew books and their prices, lost his sight late in life and died in the Sa'id in 517 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.250} He also names Abu Bakr 'Atiq ibn 'Ali ibn Makki al-Samastawi al-Bundi, who died in Alexandria in 504 AH, and the ascetic Jabir ibn al-Ashall al-Samastawi, said to have worked wonders and to have drunk from the salt sea when thirsty. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.250}
