## Overview
Aswan: a small, populous city rich in wheat, vegetables and meat, according to al-Idrisi. It is the furthest point reached upstream on the Nile from the land of Egypt. It lies near the mountain of the cataracts, about twelve stages from it, and he counts it among the frontier posts of Nubia. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.39-40}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] vocalizes the name Uswan and notes that he found it in the hand of Abu Sa'id al-Sukkari as "Suwan", without the hamza; for him it is a large city and district at the far end of Upper Egypt and the beginning of Nubia, on the east bank of the Nile, in the second clime. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.252} In its mountains is the quarry of the columns of Alexandria; he quotes Abu Bakr al-Harawi, who saw the traces of the column quarries there and a column near a village called Bilaq, known as "al-Saqala", of which twenty-five cubits showed above the sand, each face seven cubits wide, which others said was the twin of the Pillar of the Columns at Alexandria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.252}

Yaqut dwells on the dates of Aswan, quoting al-Hasan ibn Ibrahim al-Misri: it has kinds of dates and fresh dates not found in Iraq, including fresh dates greener than chard; Harun al-Rashid ordered one date of every variety brought to him and they filled a *wayba*; and nowhere else, he says, do unripe dates turn into dried dates without first ripening. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.252} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.253} Among those named after it is the judge al-Rashid Abu al-Hasan Ahmad ibn Ali ibn al-Zubayr al-Ghassani al-Aswani, poet and author, who governed the frontier of Alexandria and was killed in 563 AH, and his brother al-Muhadhdhab Abu Muhammad al-Hasan, author of a book on genealogy, who died in 561 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.253}
