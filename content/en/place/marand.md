## Overview
Marand is a city in Azerbaijan that was once a small village, settled by Halbas, father of al-Bu'ayth. Then al-Bu'ayth fortified it, followed by his son Muhammad ibn al-Bu'ayth, who built palaces there. When he rebelled in the caliphate of al-Mutawakkil, Bugha al-Saghir fought him until he prevailed over him, and he demolished its wall and his palace. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed for the page number. The data reads "Bana al-Saghir", and the correct form is probably "Bugha al-Saghir".

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut calls Marand one of the well-known towns of Adharbayjan, two days from Tabriz, now in ruin since the Kurj (Georgians) plundered it and carried off its people. He gives Ptolemy's coordinates for it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.110}

Al-Baladhuri says Marand was a small village first settled by Jalis, known as Abu al-Bu'ayth. His son al-Bu'ayth fortified it, and his grandson Muhammad built a palace there. When Muhammad rebelled in the caliphate of al-Mutawakkil, Bugha al-Saghir defeated him, took him to Samarra, and pulled down the wall of Marand and the palace. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.110}

Yaqut names scholars called al-Marandi, among them Muhammad ibn Abd Allah, who related hadith in Damascus in year 433, and Abu al-Wafa Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Marandi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.110}
