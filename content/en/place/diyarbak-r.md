## Overview
Diyarbakir is a city in southeastern Anatolia in present-day Turkey; the museum's record places it at about 37.9 degrees north and 40.2 degrees east. The pages read for this record deal with the old regional name, Diyar Bakr, in Yaqut al-Hamawi, and with its greatest city, Amid; they do not speak of the modern city under its present name, and the page is to be read on that footing.

## Diyar Bakr in the Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says Diyar Bakr is a large, wide land named after Bakr ibn Wa'il ibn Qasit, whose limit he gives as the land west of the Tigris up to the mountain country overlooking Nisibis and back to the Tigris, and including Hisn Kayfa, Amid and Mayyafariqin; the name can pass the Tigris to Sa'irt, Hizan and Hini and what lies between, but does not go beyond the plain {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.494}. He quotes a verse by al-Babbagha' praising Sayf al-Dawla al-Hamdani in which the clouds of his hands water Diyar Bakr {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.494}, and he names the hadith transmitter Umar ibn Ali ibn al-Hasan al-Diyarbakri, who heard al-Jubba'i in Aleppo, as an example of its people {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.494}. He then distinguishes Diyar Bakr from Diyar Rabi'a, between Mosul and Ras al-Ayn, Nisibis and the Khabur, and from Diyar Mudar, the plain east of the Euphrates toward Harran, al-Raqqa and Saruj; people sometimes combine Diyar Bakr and Diyar Rabi'a, and the name al-Jazira covers all of them {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.494}.

Under Amid he calls it the greatest of the cities of Diyar Bakr, the highest in rank and the most renowned {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.56}, an old, strong town built of black stone on a rise above the Tigris, which surrounds most of it like a crescent {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.56}. Amid's own story is in the museum's record [[place/amid|Amid]]. The region is also linked to [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]] and [[place/mayyafariqin|Mayyafariqin]].
