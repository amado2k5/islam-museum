## Overview
A town in the Hijaz, south-west of Medina, near the wells around which the battle of Badr took place in the year 624 CE.

## Location

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut opens Badr with its philology from al-Zajjaj: its root is fullness. For him it is "a famous water between [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/medina|Medina]] below Wadi al-Safra, a night between it and al-Jar" — al-Jar being the coast; it is said to be ascribed to Badr ibn Yakhld ibn al-Nadr ibn Kinana, and it is said rather to be a man of Banu Damra who dwelt there and gave it his name; al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar says Badr son of Quraysh dug it and the Badr of the blessed battle was named for him {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.357}. There the famous battle took place by which God made Islam prevail in the month of Ramadan of the year two of the Hijra; he records the report of the blind al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib and Quraysh's lamentation over their dead — the report carried on its teller's name — then that between Badr and Medina lie seven barids: Dhat al-Jaysh, Ubbud, al-Margha, al-Munsarif, Dhat Ajdhal, al-Ma'lat and al-Athil, and that Badr al-Maw'id, Badr al-Qital, Badr al-Ula and al-Thaniya are all one place; and to Badr are ascribed all the Companions who witnessed it {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.358}.

## The museum's identification
The record's Badr is the water at which the battle was fought — Yaqut's own place, with no rival of the name in the entry. The museum's own measurement — not Yaqut's words — between the records' points Badr (23.77, 38.79) and Medina (24.4686, 39.6142) gives 114.2 km by the haversine formula on a sphere of R = 6371.0088 km, against the printed "seven barids" between them.
