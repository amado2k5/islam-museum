## Overview
Samura is a city in the kingdom of the Galicians (al-Jalaliqa). Al-Mas'udi described its fine construction and walls. Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir, ruler of al-Andalus, besieged it with a hundred thousand men or more. The battle between him and Radhmir, king of the Galicians, took place in Shawwal of the year 327 AH, three days after the eclipse. The advantage was first with the Muslims, then, after being besieged, they gave way, and about fifty thousand of them were killed, according to al-Mas'udi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.359-360}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Samura", with a fatha on the sin, a doubled mim with a damma, and a ra after the waw, and says only that it is the city of the Jalaliqa (the Galicians), adding that it is also said to be "Samra". The entry says no more. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.255}

## The museum's identification
The record agrees with Yaqut that Samura is the city of the Jalaliqa, as al-Mas'udi says in the record. Yaqut gives nothing more: no site and no mention of the battle al-Mas'udi relates. The link from the record to the modern Spanish city of Zamora rests on the Wikidata match recorded for the place and on the museum's reading, not on Yaqut's text.
