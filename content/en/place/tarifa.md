## Overview
Tarifa is a city on the strait. The report found in al-Maqqari attributes it to Tarif al-Barbari, client of Musa ibn Nusayr, who raided it with the help of Yulyan, lord of Ceuta. It is also said that it was Tarif ibn Malik al-Nakha'i, who settled there within the conquest of Tariq ibn Ziyad. It was the first of the Andalusian frontier posts that the Muslims trod, before the great conquest. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
In the preface to his book, describing the corners of al-Andalus and its sea-side, Yaqut names Jazirat Tarif: the strait begins, he says, at a spot known as the island of Tarif on the Andalusi shore, facing Qasr Masmuda opposite Sala at the far west of the mainland joined to Ifriqiya and Egypt; the strait's width there is twelve miles, then it runs south to al-Jazira al-Khadra' on the Andalusi shore opposite the city of Sabta, where its width is eighteen miles. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.263} He also has an entry "Turayfa," in the diminutive: it may be the diminutive of tarfa, one of the tarfa' plants, or of their saying "a she-camel tarfa" that does not settle on one pasture; and he makes al-Turayfa a water at the foot of Armam belonging to the Banu Judhayma of Banu Asad — elsewhere said to belong to the Banu Shakir ibn Nadla — while al-Hafsi makes it a village with water and palms of the Ahmal, the Banu Haml of Banu Hanzala, and Nasr makes it a barren tract whose water is sweetened for two or three days at the foot of Armam for Judhayma. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.34}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's "island of Tarif" in the preface is the strait rock off the record's town (the islet of Tarifa facing its shore); his diminutive "Turayfa" entry, however, belongs to places in Najd and the Hijaz, not to the Andalusi town. His derivation stops at tarfa and the tarfa' plants and does not mention Tarif the Berber, to whom the town is ascribed in the al-Maqqari account quoted in the overview.
