## Summary
Gabes is a city on the coast of Tunisia. Ibn Battuta reached it and stayed ten days because of continuous rain, then left it bound for Tripoli. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p. 11}

## Details
On his first journey Ibn Battuta came to the city of Gabes and lodged inside it, staying ten days because the rain kept falling. Ibn Juzay quotes rajaz verses about it: "Extinguished for the sweetness of nights gone by / beside the batha' of Gabes — / my heart at its memory / was like a brand of fire in the hand of Gabes." He then left Gabes bound for Tripoli. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p. 11}

The city appears a second time in the rihla: in Safar 750 he sailed in a Tunisian qarqura to Jerba, then travelled in a small boat to Gabes, where he stayed as the guest of the two brothers Abu Marwan and Abu al-Mabbas, sons of Makki, the amirs of Jerba and Faris, and attended the Prophet's birthday celebration with them; then he took ship to Sfax and continued overland to Tunis. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958 p. 438} Its recorded coordinates come from GeoNames for the modern city of the name. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Qabis with kasra on the ba; a city between Tripoli and Sfax, then al-Mahdiyya, on the sea shore with palms and gardens, west of Tripoli with eight stages between them; it has running waters, belongs to the workings of Ifriqiya in the fourth clime with latitude thirty-five degrees as printed, and its conquest came with Qayrawan's in the year 27 as told under Qayrawan. From al-Bakri he reports a noble city walled in ancient dressed rock, with a strong fortress, suburbs, funduqs, a congregational mosque and many baths, all ringed by a great ditch filled with water at need; it has three gates, its eastern and southern suburbs house Arabs and Afariq, it holds every fruit with bananas abundant — it provisions Qayrawan with fruits — and its mulberry yields silk worth five other trees', there being no silk in all Ifriqiya's workings save at Qabis; sugarcane is plentiful and a tall lighthouse guides the sailor. Its people say it had the healthiest air of any land until a talisman was found and dug up, releasing a grey pot from which plague came upon them — in their claim. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.289-290}

## The museum's identification
The record's Gabes is the same Ifriqiyan coastal city between Tripoli and Sfax. The printed eight stages to Tripoli stands beside the museum's haversine of about 307.4 km between the two records' points (the museum's calculation, not Yaqut's text). Its silk, unique in Ifriqiya's workings per al-Bakri, is the entry's signature note, and the talisman-and-plague tale is carried in the source's own "as they claim" frame.
