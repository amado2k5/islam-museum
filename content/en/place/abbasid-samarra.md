## Summary
Samarra was a city between Baghdad and Tikrit on the east bank of the Tigris, which has since fallen into ruin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.173} Yaqut lists several forms of its name, among them Samarra, Samarra with a long vowel, and Sur man ra'a, and notes that people shortened the name to Samarra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.173} He puts it about thirty farsakhs from Baghdad. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.173}

In its great mosque is the well-known cellar that the Shia, as Yaqut reports, claim their awaited Mahdi will come out of. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.173}

Before the city was built, the quarter of Karkh Samarra, also called Karkh Firuz after Firuz ibn Balash ibn Qubadh, already stood there; when Samarra was founded the quarter was joined to it and it stayed populated after the city declined. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.449} Turkish soldiers known as the Shabliyya lodged there in the days of al-Mu'tasim, and the palace of Ashnas, a Turkish client of al-Mu'tasim, stood there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.449}
