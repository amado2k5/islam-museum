## Overview
Muzdalifa is a site between 'Arafa and [[place/mina|Mina]]. Pilgrims spend the night there on the night of sacrifice. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## For Sprouts
Muzdalifa is the place between Arafa and Mina where the pilgrims spend the eve of the sacrifice night.

## For Explorers
Muzdalifa is the place between Arafa and Mina where the pilgrims sleep on the eve of the sacrifice, after standing at Arafa and before stoning the pillars at Mina.

## For Scholars
Muzdalifa is the place between Arafa and Mina where the pilgrims sleep on the eve of the sacrifice — the station between the daytime standing at Arafa and the stoning of Jamrat al-Aqaba at Mina on the morning of the tenth.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the spelling with a damma, a sukun on the zay, a fatha on the dal, a kasra on the lam and a fa', and says the reason for the name is disputed. He lists the explanations: that it is taken from izdilaf, meaning gathering, for which he cites a Qur'anic verse that uses the same root; that it means nearness, because the place brings people close to God; that people draw near to Mina after pouring out from Arafa; that people gather there; that Adam and Hawwa' drew together there after meeting at Arafa, which is also why it is called Jam'; that people come down on it in the early part of the night; and that pilgrims approach the sanctuary from it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.120-121} It is the pilgrims' night halt and the place where the prayers are joined when they return from Arafat, between Batn Muhassir and the two Ma'zams. He adds that it is al-Mash'ar al-Haram, where the imam leads the evening, night and dawn prayers; that its limit is the stretch from leaving Arafat until the Red Horn short of Muhassir, and Quzah, the mountain at the place of standing; that it is a farsakh from Mina; and that it has a prayer place, a water supply, a minaret and several tanks beside Mount Thabir. He ends with wine verses that he gives to Ibn Hajjaj, which he says were adapted from lines al-Mubarrad ascribed to Muhammad ibn Harun ibn Makhlad ibn Aban the secretary. The museum does not quote them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.121}

Other entries tie it to its neighbours. Under Jam' he says that Jam' is Muzdalifa, Quzah and the Mash'ar, named for the gathering of the people. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.163} Under Batn Muhassir he calls that valley the valley of Muzdalifa, notes that Muslim's book counts it with Mina, and gives a saying that all of Muzdalifa is a place of standing except the valley of Muhassir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.449} Under Muhassir he records three placings: between Mecca and Arafa, between Mina and Arafa, and between Mina and Muzdalifa as a valley on its own, belonging to neither. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.62}

## The museum's identification
The record, from GeoNames, says a place between Arafat and Mina where pilgrims spend the night of sacrifice. Yaqut agrees on the night halt and the position, and adds the names, the explanations of the name, the prayers, the limits and the farsakh to Mina. The record's point (21.36787, 39.94262) is about 4.5 km from the museum's Arafat point and about 7.1 km from its Mina point. A farsakh is a road measure of roughly 5 to 6 km, so the figure is of the same order, but the record has one point for a place that has limits, and the museum does not draw a line. Where Wadi Muhassir belongs is a question on which Yaqut records different views, and the museum keeps it as its own record, [[place/wadi-muhassir|Wadi Muhassir]].
