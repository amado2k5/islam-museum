## Overview
Qurya is an ancient city with an impregnable wall. Al-Idrisi mentions that most of its fruit is grapes and olive trees, and that it lies two stages from Qantarat al-Sayf. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.547}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Quriya", with a damma, a sukun, a kasra on the ra and a light ya, and says it is a city among the districts of Marida (Merida) in al-Andalus, once held by the Muslims, and halfway between it and Samura, the city of the Franks; the pronoun "it" most naturally refers to Marida. The entry says no more. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.412} Elsewhere, describing the Byzantine provinces, he lists a "Quriya or Qunya" among the towns of one province of Rum; that is another place, not this one. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.99}

## The museum's identification
The museum reads al-Idrisi's Quriya in the record and Yaqut's Quriya as one city, and the record is filed under the region of al-Andalus; al-Idrisi measures its distance from [[place/qantarat-al-sayf|Qantarat al-Sayf]] and describes its wall and its fruit, a measure that Yaqut does not give. In Yaqut it is set beside [[place/samura|Samura]], which he here calls "the city of the Franks", while in his own entry for Samura he calls it the city of the Galicians; so on the museum's reading he uses two descriptions for one city. Yaqut gives no coordinates. There is a problem in the record that this pass does not settle: its point (35.8, 11.03) is noted as the Kuriat Islands off Monastir in Tunisia, which fits neither Yaqut's placing near Marida nor al-Idrisi's measure from Qantarat al-Sayf, and the Qantarat al-Sayf record itself carries coordinates in Baghdad. Both records need review. The museum has changed neither the coordinates nor the Wikidata identifier, and does not say which modern town corresponds to Yaqut's Quriya.
