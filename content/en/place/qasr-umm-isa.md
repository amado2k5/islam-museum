## Overview
Qasr Umm Isa is a town in the land of Kawar, two days south of the town of al-Qasaba. It is not a large town, but its people are well off and have camels on which they travel east and west; their chief commodity is alum, the head of their wealth. Around the town are small palm groves and wells of sweet water from which they drink. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 117}

## The land of Kawar
Kawar lies in the bed of a valley that comes from the south and runs north, with no water on the surface, though when one digs in it a great deal of spring water is found. On this valley stands the town of al-Qasaba, small and well built, surrounded by palms and kinds of wild trees; its people are settled and wear wool loincloths, wraps and woollen cloaks. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 117}

## Neighbours
From Qasr Umm Isa to the town of Inklas is forty miles along the valley bed; it is among the largest of the towns of Kawar and the one with most trade. They have mines of pure alum of the highest quality, and its people travel east to Egypt and west to Warqlan (Ouargla) and the rest of the far Maghrib. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 117}
