## Overview
al-Mansura: a city in Sind, below the lake. al-Baladhuri reports that Amr ibn Muhammad ibn al-Qasim built it when his affair prevailed, and it is the one in which the governors settle today. Before that its site had been a thicket near old Brahmanabad. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes al-Mansura as the capital of Sind, a large and rich city with a great mosque whose pillars are of teak and an inlet of the Mihran river that surrounds the town. Its people are dignified and devout and trade widely, and they drink from the Mihran. The city is very hot and full of bugs. Yaqut gives the stages from it: six to Daybul, twelve to Multan and fifteen to Tawran. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.211}

He records four accounts of its name. Hamza says the old town of Hamanabad in Sind was renamed al-Mansura. Al-Mas'udi says Mansur ibn Jumhur, an Umayyad governor, gave it his name. Hisham says Mansur ibn Jumhur al-Kalbi built it after he rebelled against Harun and settled in Sind. Al-Hasan ibn Ahmad al-Muhallabi says Amr ibn Hafs al-Hizarmard al-Muhallabi built it in the days of al-Mansur. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.211}
