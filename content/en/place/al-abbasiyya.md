## Overview
Al-Abbasiyya: a city that Umar ibn Hafs al-Ataki built at the farthest edge of the land of the Berbers and named al-Abbasiyya, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Its destruction
Abu Hatim al-Ibadi fought him, and Umar was martyred, so the city he had built was torn down. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut notes that al-Abbasiyya is the name of several places. One is a hill of sand on the road from Mecca to Batn al-Aghr. Another is a village in the district of al-Harja in Upper Egypt. He also records a city near Qayrawan, founded by Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab, governor of Ifriqiya, and named for the Abbasids, and a quarter of Baghdad between the two Sarats, in front of al-Mansur's palace near the quarter known as the Basra Gate, named after al-Abbas ibn Muhammad ibn Ali. Yaqut thinks that quarter had fallen into ruin in his own time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.75}
