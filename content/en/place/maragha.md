## Overview
Maragha is a city in Azerbaijan that was called Afrahrudh. [[person/marwan-ibn-muhammad|Marwan ibn Muhammad]] camped near it on his return from the expedition of Mawqan and Jilan. There was much dung in it, in which the animals rolled, so people said "come to the village of Maragha", and then dropped the word "village". {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Its people had taken refuge with Marwan, who bought it. People grew numerous there and cultivated it. Then it was confiscated along with the estates of the Banu Umayya, and it became the property of one of the daughters of al-Rashid. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

In the caliphate of al-Rashid, Khuzayma ibn Khazim governed Armenia and Azerbaijan. He built its wall and established it as a garrison city, and settled a dense force in it. When Babak appeared, people took refuge in it. The agents of al-Ma'mun repaired its wall, and then people settled its suburb and it was fortified. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut calls Maragha a famous great town, the greatest and best known of the towns of Azerbaijan, and gives its position as longitude 73 and a third and latitude 37 and a third degrees. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.93} He says it used to be called Afrazahrud. Marwan ibn Muhammad, then governor of Armenia and Azerbaijan, camped near it on his way back from the raid on Muqan and Jilan; there was much dung on the ground, in which his animals and those of his men rolled, and the men began to say "build the village of al-Maragha" (the rolling-place), until people dropped the word village and said simply Maragha. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.93}

Yaqut adds that its inhabitants had turned to Marwan, who built it up, and that his agents won the people over so that they multiplied and made it prosper. It was later seized with the Umayyad estates and passed to one of al-Rashid's daughters. After al-Wajna' ibn Rawwad al-Azdi caused disorder, Khuzayma ibn Khazim, appointed over Armenia and Azerbaijan under al-Rashid, built its wall, fortified it and settled a large garrison there. When Babak al-Khurrami appeared at al-Badhdh, people took refuge in it, and its wall was repaired under al-Ma'mun by several of his officials, among them Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn al-Junayd and Ali ibn Hisham; people then settled the outskirts. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.93}

He names Abu Muhammad Ja'far ibn Muhammad ibn al-Harith al-Maraghi, who travelled to seek and collect hadith and settled in Nishapur. Yaqut cites al-Hakim that he wrote hadith for more than sixty years and died in Nishapur on 26 Rajab 356, over eighty years old. Yaqut adds that the town's centre remained, with remains, buildings, schools and fine Sufi lodges, and that it had literary men, poets, hadith transmitters and jurists. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.93}
