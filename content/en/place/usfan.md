## Overview
Usfan is a village on the road from Medina to Mecca, near Khulays, according to what our data states. Ibn Battuta and Ibn Jubayr mentioned it on their journeys. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958}

The coordinates come from the modern village of Usfan, and the ancient site may be near it. An additional source is needed.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut spells the name Usfan, with a damma on the first letter, and explains it as from the verb for crossing a desert without a guide or a goal, saying also that it was named for the torrent's rush, as al-Abwa' was named for the torrent's settling. Abu Mansur is quoted that it is a watering stage on the road between al-Juhfa and Mecca. Others say it lies between the two mosques, two stages from Mecca, and one report calls it a large village with a minbar, palms and fields, thirty-six miles from Mecca and the edge of Tihama, with a stretch from Usfan to Malal, called al-Sahil. Al-Sukkari puts it two stages from Mecca on the Medina road with al-Juhfa three, and says the Prophet fought the Banu Lihyan at Usfan, five years, two months and eleven days after his hijra. The entry ends with the verse of a Bedouin whom Yaqut does not name, about longing for home. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.121-122} Neighbouring entries tie it to other places: al-Hada'a, between Usfan and Mecca {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.395}; the well of Mu'awiya, between Usfan and Mecca {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.302}; and Ghuran, a valley between Amaj and Usfan {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.191}.

## The museum's identification
The record has a village on the Medina road to Mecca, near Khulays, from GeoNames, with Ibn Battuta and Ibn Jubayr as the travellers who mention it. Yaqut agrees that it is a village on the road and never mentions Khulays in the entry; he does not cite the two travellers. His distances can be set against the record's point (21.92306, 39.35594), which is the modern village: about 74 km from the museum's Mecca point, which is about 2 km per mile for his thirty-six miles and about 37 km per stage for his two stages; about 26 km from its Khulays point; and about 91 km from its al-Juhfa point, which fits his one stage between Usfan and al-Juhfa. The record's own note that the old site may lie near the modern village is not contradicted by Yaqut, who gives nothing to test it beyond these counts, and the units of mile and stage are loose.
