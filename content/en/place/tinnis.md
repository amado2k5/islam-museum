## Overview
Tinnis is one of the cities of the Lower Egypt region. Amr ibn al-As sent to it Umayr ibn Wahb al-Jumahi, with Damietta, Tuna, Dumayra, Shatta, Daqahliyya, Bana and Buwsir. It made peace with the people of its villages on the terms of Fustat, according to al-Baladhuri. It is connected with the event of [[event/four-lieutenants-egypt|the four lieutenants in Egypt]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.304}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
For [[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]], Tinnis is an island in the sea of Egypt close to the shore, between al-Farama and Damietta. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.823} He quotes al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Muhallabi that it was like Damietta but grander, that coloured cloth and the shot-silk *buqalamun* furnishings were made there, and that its lagoon, a day's sail long and half a day wide, was salt for most of the year because sea water entered with the north wind; when the Nile came in winter it turned sweet, and the people stored water in cisterns for the year. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.823} He notes the skill of the lagoon's boatmen, who could sail in two opposite directions with a single wind, and that Tinnis had no harmful vermin because its ground was very salty marsh. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.823}

He relates from a chronicle that the wall of Tinnis was begun in Rabi' al-Awwal 230 AH, when Isa ibn Mansur governed Egypt for Itakh the Turk under al-Wathiq, and finished in 239 AH under al-Mutawakkil, then reports the astrologers' claim that the horoscope of its building meant no one of non-Arabic speech would rule it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.823} He also passes on a story from Yusuf ibn Subayh, who said he saw five hundred men with inkwells writing hadith there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.823}
