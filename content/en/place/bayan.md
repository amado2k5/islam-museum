## Summary
Bayan was a small town on the east bank of the Tigris, fair within and without, with a minbar, whose people were eager in the pursuit of letters and learning, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.399}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the town of Nahr Tiri to Bayan is two stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.399}. Bayan stands on the Tigris on its eastern bank: a small town fine inside and out, containing a minbar, its people keen in seeking letters and learning and aspiring to rise above their equals among the people of the land {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.399}. From Bayan to al-Ubulla is one stage, and from Bayan to Hisn Mahdi one stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.399}. He also notes that along the Tigris to the city of Bayan are eighteen miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.400}. In his description the town thus combined modest size with a people honourable in their pursuit of learning and letters on the bank of the Tigris.
