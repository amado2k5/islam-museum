## Overview
A port city in southern Iraq, founded as a garrison for the Muslims around the year 636 CE, and a centre of grammar, theology and trade.

## For Sprouts
Basra is a historic city in southern Iraq, surrounded by rivers and palm groves, famous for grammar, language, and libraries.

## For Explorers
Basra is an ancient city in southern Iraq near the Shatt al-Arab, founded by Utba ibn Ghazwan under Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab in 14 AH. As the first garrison city established outside Arabia in Islam, it thrived through maritime commerce, emerging alongside Kufa as the cradle of Arabic grammar, literature, and jurisprudence.

## For Scholars
The earliest garrison city (misr) established by Muslims outside the Arabian Peninsula, founded in 14 AH (c. 636 CE) by Utba ibn Ghazwan under the instruction of Caliph Umar at the northern head of the Persian Gulf along the Shatt al-Arab. Evolving rapidly from a military base into a trans-regional maritime trade entrepôt linking the Middle East with the Indian Ocean, it emerged alongside Kufa as the preeminent intellectual cradle of Arabic linguistics. Basra gave birth to the prestigious Basran school of grammar codified by Sibawayh and al-Khalil ibn Ahmad, hosting luminaries in literature, philosophy, and sciences such as al-Jahiz and Ibn al-Haytham.
