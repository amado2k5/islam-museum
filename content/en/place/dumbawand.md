## Overview
Mount Damavand is the greatest mountain of Tabaristan. Al-Baladhuri reports that Sa'id ibn al-As conquered it, together with the plain of Tabaristan and Ruyan, in his campaign of 29 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the vowels of the name (Dunbawand) and calls it a variant of Dubawand, a mountain in the district of Rayy, and he states its longitude and latitude in the fourth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.475} He passes on Ibn al-Kalbi's explanation of the name: when Afridun seized al-Dahhak and imprisoned him at Dunbawand, he said to Armaʾil, a cook of al-Dahhak described as a Nabatean from al-Zab, the words «dunbawandi», meaning that he had found the tails and escaped through them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.475} He then reproduces the poet Mis'ar ibn Muhalhil's account of the mountain: a lofty peak whose summit never loses its snow in summer or winter, known as the mountain of al-Bayurasf, seen from Rayy as though two or three farsakhs away, with a sulphur spring on its flank that gives off smoke and mines nearby. Yaqut reports that common people said Solomon son of David had confined a rebellious spirit there and that Afridun had confined al-Bayurasf there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.476} He also quotes the report of Ali ibn Zayn, secretary of al-Mazyar, about men from Tabaristan who climbed to the top in five days, found it about a hundred jaribs across and counted seventy vents giving out sulphur smoke. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.476} Yaqut adds that Dunbawand was among the conquests of Sa'id ibn al-As in the time of Uthman, who took it together with al-Ruyan in 29 or 30 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.477}
