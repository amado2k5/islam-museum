## Overview
al-Jizah: a shore facing Egypt on the west bank of the Nile. A bridge connects it with about thirty ships. It has dwellings, buildings and farms, as well as construction and a market. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.323}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes al-Jiza as a small town on the west bank, facing Fustat in Egypt, with a large and wide district of its own that he counts among the best in Egypt. He reports from the biographers that after Amr ibn al-As took Alexandria and went back to Fustat he left part of his army at al-Jiza for fear of an enemy coming from that side, settling there the house of Dhu Asbah of Himyar, Hamdan, the house of Ru'ayn, a group of the Azd and a group of Abyssinians. When Amr was settled in Fustat and felt safe he ordered them to join him, but they disliked the idea, and he wrote to Umar ibn al-Khattab, who told him to build them a fort if they preferred. They refused the fort too, saying that their swords were their forts, and so they marked out quarters in al-Jiza that were still known by their names in Yaqut's day. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.200}

He adds that scholars are linked to the town by name, among them al-Rabi ibn Sulayman ibn Dawud al-Jizi, called the lame, who narrated from Asad ibn Musa and Abdallah ibn Abd al-Hakam and is described as trustworthy, dying in Dhu al-Hijja 256; his son Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn al-Rabi, a leading witness of Egypt's notaries; and Abu Yusuf Yaqub ibn Ishaq al-Jizi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.200}
