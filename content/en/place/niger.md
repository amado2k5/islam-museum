## Summary
A country of many ethnic groups in which, according to The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, eighty percent of the people are Muslim. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1750}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam says that eighty percent of the people of multiethnic Niger are Muslims. It lists the ways Islam came to the region: the Saharan and Sahelian trade routes of the seventh to ninth centuries, the Hausa, Malian and Songhay dynasties (about 1100 to 1300), merchant missionaries and the Sufi orders. The Tijani tariqah, it says, enjoys the greatest popularity among the Sufi brotherhoods, and the Qadiri, Sanusi and Shadhili orders are active as well. The heavy droughts of 1973-74 and of 1984-85 put many groups that had been on the margins in touch with associations and communities of Muslims in the towns. In the early 1990s, according to the Dictionary, tension arose between the Association Izalatoul Bidah wa Ikamatou Sunnah, also called the Izal, and followers of the Sufi brotherhoods. It names as a significant challenge for Niger today the building of a judicial process that is coherent and joins customary and Islamic law on legal rights, privileges, the safeguarding of women and families, and land tenure. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1750}
