## Overview
One of the well-known cities of [[place/al-andalus|al-Andalus]], on the sea coast. It has the impregnable fortress known as the Fortress of Khayran, built by Abd al-Rahman al-Nasir. It grew in importance in the state of al-Mansur ibn Abi Amir, who appointed his client Khayran over it, and it was named after him. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 198}

## Its crafts and buildings
al-Maqqari reports that it excels in the craft of silk brocade over all other lands, and that it has an arsenal. Its district includes iron and marble mines. Among its gates is the Gate of the Eagle, on which there is a figure of an eagle carved in ancient stone. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 198}

There were eight hundred looms in it for weaving silk bands, and a thousand looms for fine robes and rich brocade, and it had about a thousand baths and inns. He says that there was no wealthier people in the lands of al-Andalus, nor greater markets and treasures. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 199}

## Its fall
It fell by force on Friday, the seventeenth of Jumada al-Ula in the year 542 AH. {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari p. 466}
