## Overview
Zubala was a city in the past. According to al-Idrisi, in his time no city or fortress remained, but it is a place where travellers on the Hijaz road take shelter. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 381}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name Zubala with a damma on its first letter, and makes it a well-known halt on the Mecca road from Kufa, a flourishing village with markets between Waqisa and al-Tha'labiyya; from Abu 'Ubayd al-Sukuni it lies after al-Qa' from Kufa and before al-Shaquq, and in it are a fortress and a mosque of Banu Ghadira of Banu Asad. The day of Zubala is one of the days of the Arabs; it was named, they say, for its holding back of water (zabl al-ma'), and Ibn al-Kalbi says it was named for Zubala bint Mas'ar, a woman of the 'Amaliqa who settled in it; Muhammad ibn al-Hasan ibn 'Ayyash al-Zubali is given its nisba. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.129-130}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi (p.381), says Zubala was once a city, that in al-Idrisi's time neither a city nor a fortress remained, and that it is only a place where travellers on the Hijaz road take shelter. Yaqut's entry (vol 3 p.129-130) calls it a flourishing village with markets and transmits a fortress and a mosque in it; the difference between the two pictures is stated, not decided, and the entry gives no date for its flourishing or ruin. Yaqut's placing of it on the Mecca road from Kufa between Waqisa and al-Tha'labiyya fits the record's road description.
