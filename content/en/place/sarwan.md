## Overview
Sarwan is a small, well-settled town in Sistan. Its land is broad, its farms and rustaqs are many, its grapes are plentiful, and it lies two stages from Bust. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 457}

## Description
Al-Idrisi describes Sarwan as a small, civilised town, similar to al-Qarnayn in size and buildings. It has a wide land, farms and rustaqs, settlements and villages, and fruit of every kind. Its grapes are abundant, and its produce is considerable. Elsewhere he calls it small in size but populous and civilised, with villages, rustaqs, crops and many benefits, and with fruit and abundant produce of every kind. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 457}

## Roads and links
From Bust to Sarwan is two stages. The first stage is called Firuzqand and the second is the river of Sarwan. The lands of al-Dawar and al-Rakhaj are linked to it. Dartal is one stage away: the river crosses at Dartal and turns toward Sarwan. Sarwan is larger than al-Qarnayn and richer in produce, and it sends its fruit and grapes to Bust and elsewhere. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 457} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 467}
