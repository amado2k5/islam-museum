## Summary
Baeza (Biasa) was a walled town with markets and shops on an earthen hillock overlooking the Great River that runs down to Cordoba, twenty miles from Jaen, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of Jayyan to Baeza is twenty miles, and that each town is visible from the other on account of their nearness; Baeza stands on a hillock of earth overlooking the Great River that descends toward Cordoba {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}. It is a city with walls, markets and shops; around it are farmsteads and its saffron fields are many, and from it eastward to the town of Ibda is seven miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.569}. East of Jayyan, facing Baeza, stands a great fortress called Shudhar, to which the poet known as al-Shudhari is attributed {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.569}. In his picture it was thus a walled satellite of the larger centre Jayyan, set on an earthen eminence above the river road to Cordoba.
