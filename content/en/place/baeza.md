## Summary
Baeza (Biasa) was a walled town with markets and shops on an earthen hillock overlooking the Great River that runs down to Cordoba, twenty miles from Jaen, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of Jayyan to Baeza is twenty miles, and that each town is visible from the other on account of their nearness; Baeza stands on a hillock of earth overlooking the Great River that descends toward Cordoba {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.568}. It is a city with walls, markets and shops; around it are farmsteads and its saffron fields are many, and from it eastward to the town of Ibda is seven miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.569}. East of Jayyan, facing Baeza, stands a great fortress called Shudhar, to which the poet known as al-Shudhari is attributed {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.569}. In his picture it was thus a walled satellite of the larger centre Jayyan, set on an earthen eminence above the river road to Cordoba.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes Bayyasa as a large city in al-Andalus counted in the district of Jayyan, two farsakhs from Ubbada, and says its saffron is the famous saffron of the western lands. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.518} He records that the Byzantines entered it in 542 and were driven out in 552. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.518} He names among those attributed to it the hafiz Abu al-Abbas Ahmad ibn Yusuf ibn Tamam al-Ya'mari al-Bayyasi, whom Abu Tahir calls a brilliant poet and a careful man of letters with a great memory for the later Andalusian poets, who turned to asceticism at the end of his life. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.518} From him he relays the story of Abd al-Jalil ibn Wahbun al-Mursi, known as al-Dam'a, who praised al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad in a ninety-line ode and was rewarded with ninety dinars, one of them clipped, the reason being that in one line the poem had moved from the tawil metre to the kamil. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.518}
