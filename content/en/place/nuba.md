## Overview
Nubia is the land south of Egypt. The horses of the Muslims entered it in the days of Amr ibn al-As, and later Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh made a truce with its people with no poll-tax. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 331}

## The first raid
When the Muslims conquered Egypt, Amr ibn al-As sent horsemen to the villages around it, and he sent Uqba ibn Nafi' al-Fihri, who was his half-brother on the mother's side. Their horses entered the land of Nubia as the summer raids of the Romans enter. The Muslims met a fierce fight there, for the people shot them with arrows until most of them were wounded. They went back with many wounds and with eyes put out, and so they were called the "ones who lost their eyes" (dumat al-hadaq). {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 331}

## The truce
The matter remained so until Abd Allah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh was governor of Egypt. The people of Nubia asked him for peace and a truce, and he granted it, with no poll-tax but a truce of three hundred heads each year, and with the Muslims sending food to them in the same measure. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 331}

## The time of Umar
Al-Baladhuri gives the report of a man of Himyar who witnessed two campaigns against Nubia in the governorship of Umar ibn al-Khattab. He had never seen a people more fierce in war, and they shot arrows in great numbers. He said that a hundred and fifty eyes were counted as put out, and Umar said that these people were no better than peace. He refused to make peace with them and kept pressing them until he was removed, and then Abd Allah ibn Sa'd made peace with them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 332}

## Slaves and the truce
Al-Baladhuri also reports that the truce with the Asawid was without a covenant or pact, and was for the Muslims to give some wheat and lentils, while the others gave slaves. As for the peace with Nubia, it stipulated that the Muslims would not fight its people and its people would not fight the Muslims, that Nubia would give slaves, and that the Muslims would give food in the same measure. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 332} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 333}
