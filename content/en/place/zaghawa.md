## Overview
Zaghawa is a gathered town in the land of the Sudan. Al-Idrisi lists it among the famous cities of the first climate's third section, with Kuka, Tamlama, Manan, Anjimi, Nawabiya and Tajuwa. Around it live Zaghawa people who move with their camels. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.27}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Zaghawa as a gathered town. Around it live many Zaghawa people who move with their camels, and they have small trades and crafts for dealing among themselves. They drink from wells. Their food is millet, dried camel meat and caught fish, and they have plenty of milk. Their clothing is tanned hide. Al-Idrisi says they are the most affected by scabies among the people of the Sudan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.29}

From Zaghawa to Manan is eight stages. Manan is where its amir and agent live, and most of its men go naked and shoot with bows. From Manan to Tajuwa is thirteen stages, and Tajuwa is the capital of the Tajuwin people. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.30}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says that Zaghawa is given by some as a land in the south of Ifriqiya in the Maghrib and by others as a tribe of the Sudan south of the Maghrib, and he quotes Abu Mansur that they are a race of the Sudan, the adjective being zaghawi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.142} He quotes al-Muhallabi that they have two cities, Manan and Tarazki, in the first climate, and that their kingdom is one of the great kingdoms of the Sudan, about ten days' journey from Nubia, its land fifteen stages long in continuous settlement. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.142} In the same account al-Muhallabi names sorghum and cowpeas, then wheat, as their main crops and livestock as their wealth, and describes their religion as the veneration of their kings; this is al-Muhallabi's description as Yaqut reports it, not a statement in the museum's voice. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.142}
