## Overview
Saghaniyan is a land on the eastern side of the river. The report relates that its people came with the hosts of [[place/tukharistan|Tukharistan]], which reached thirty thousand, to fight al-Ahnaf. Its king charged at al-Ahnaf with a spear, but al-Ahnaf wrested it from his hand and killed him. Al-Ahnaf had earlier killed three of the drummers. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed: the page number is not given, and the exact location of the land is not stated.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Saghaniyan as a great province beyond the river whose districts adjoin Tirmidh, and notes that the Persians change the sad into a j and say Jaghaniyan. In an earlier passage he mentions it as one of the lands of the Hayatila beyond the river. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.144} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.408}

He quotes Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ahmad al-Banna al-Bashshari that it is a very populous and bountiful district whose chief town has the same name, like Palestine but wider; its waters come from rivers drawn off toward the Jayhun, though their supply is cut off from it for part of the year, and it joins the land of Tirmidh, with mountains and plains. He gives it sixteen thousand villages, on which Yaqut comments "so he said", and ten thousand fighters with their own supplies and mounts should someone rise against the sultan. Living there is cheap and easy, the congregational mosque stands in the middle of the market, every house has running water ringed with trees, there is much game and there are pastures in which a rider disappears. Its people follow the Sunna and the community and love strangers and the righteous, though it has few scholars and no jurists. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.408} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.409}

Yaqut adds that it was the stronghold of Abu Ali ibn Muhtaj when he rebelled against Nuh, resisting him from there, which he takes as a sign of its size. He notes that the name of origin takes two forms, Saghani and Saghani with a long a, and lists Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Ishaq ibn Ja'far al-Saghani, who settled in Baghdad, from whom Muslim ibn al-Hajjaj al-Qushayri and Abu Isa al-Tirmidhi transmitted and who died in 270, and Abu al-Abbas al-Fadl ibn al-Abbas al-Saghani, a prolific author who came to Baghdad on pilgrimage in 420 and from whom al-Khatib heard. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.408} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.409}

## The museum's identification
The museum links this record to Yaqut's Saghaniyan, the province beyond the river that adjoins Tirmidh, and the record's coordinates lie in the same region. The record's phrase "the eastern side of the river" comes from al-Baladhuri's account of al-Ahnaf's fighting; Yaqut's entry does not use it, so joining the two accounts is the museum's reading and not Yaqut's.
