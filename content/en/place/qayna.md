## Summary
Qayna is a small village on the caravan road from Mecca to Dhu Suhaym in Yemen. It has two wells and lies on the stage between a desert spring and the villages of Dawqa and Alayb. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Qayna in his account of the road caravans take from Mecca to Dhu Suhaym of Khawlan, a journey he counts as twenty stages. The road leaves Mecca for Malakan, a water stop where travellers rest, and then runs to Yalamlam, a mountain range that crosses from east to west and holds the miqat of the people of Tihama. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

From Yalamlam the traveller reaches a desert stopping place with a spring, and then Qayna, which al-Idrisi calls "a small village with two wells," one stage further on. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147} From Qayna the road goes to Dawqa and Alayb, two villages he describes as well populated, another stage away, and from there to al-Hasba. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

The passage gives Qayna only as a stop on the road: a village of two wells between a desert spring and two inhabited villages. It does not describe its markets, crops or people, so this entry adds nothing beyond what the text says.
