## Overview
Qalhat is a populous city on the sea shore in Oman. Its people drink from wells, and fish is caught there. According to al-Idrisi it is a great stage overland from Sur. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.155}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Qalhat» (Pleiades no. 431950550), classed as a settlement and an archaeological site, with activity attested from 1200 CE to 1599 CE. {cite:pleiades 431950550}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Qalhat with a fatha, a sukun and a final ta', thinks the word may be a plural of a term for a boil on the body, and then describes the place: a city of Oman on the sea shore, to which most of the ships of India are drawn, now the port of that country and the best of Oman's districts, flourishing and populous, though not old as a settlement; he does not think it became a city before the year 500 AH (1106 CE, a derived conversion). He says it belongs to the lord of Hormuz and that all its people are, in his words, Ibadi Kharijites who do not hide the fact. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.393} The next entry, with a thaʾ in place of the final ta', is a different name that he takes from al-Umrani as a place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.393}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi, has a populous city on the shore of Oman whose people drink from wells and catch fish, and which is a long stage inland from Sur. Yaqut agrees on a coastal city of Oman and adds its trade with the ships of India, its place as the port of the region, its ruler and its community, in his words; he has nothing on wells, fishing or Sur. His remark on how recent the city is as a settlement is a statement of his own and is not tested by the record or by the museum, and it is separate from the dates of activity listed from Pleiades on the record. The record's point (22.7, 59.3699) is marked as a locality, and Yaqut gives no coordinates or distances to test it. The label "Ibadi Kharijites" is Yaqut's, not the museum's.
