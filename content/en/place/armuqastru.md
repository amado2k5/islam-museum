## Summary
Armuqastru was an ancient city of lofty buildings and wide canals, of great size and cheap prices, standing on the slope of a pleasant hill overlooking the sea, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.897}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that Armuqastru lies two days' journey south of the city of Dasina, and describes it as an ancient city with lofty buildings, wide canals, great size and low prices, set on the slope of a pleasant hill overlooking the sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.897}. In his account of the coastal road he placed twenty-five miles between Brins and it, and reckoned one day from Armuqastru back to Brins {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.896}. Describing the sea crossing toward the northern shore, he counted three miles from Armuqastru to the river Dnu, so that it served as a point of embarkation for those crossing to the northern lands {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.909}. His description thus combines great antiquity, extensive building, cheap living and a commanding site above the sea.
