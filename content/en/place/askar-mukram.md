## Overview
Askar Mukram: a city in al-Ahwaz, according to al-Baladhuri. It is said that it was named after Mukram ibn Mutarrif ibn Saydan al-Bahili, whom Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr sent in pursuit of the killer of his father, and he marched until he encamped there. It is also said that it was named after Mukram ibn al-Farz. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Askar Mukram as a well-known town in Khuzistan, named after Mukram ibn Ma'za al-Harith of the Banu Ja'una clan of Sa'sa'a. Hamza al-Isfahani says Rustaqubadh, an Arabized form of the Persian Rustam Kawad, was a town of Khuzistan that the Arabs destroyed early in Islam, and that the town of Askar Mukram was built close to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.123}

Another account makes Mukram a freedman of al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf, sent to fight Kharzad ibn Bas, who had rebelled and fled to Iyadhaj and shut himself in a fortress named after Mukram. After a long siege, Mukram went out disguised to reach Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan, but was caught and sent to al-Hajjaj. An old village stood there, and Mukram built it up until it became a city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.123}
