## Overview
Isfahan is a great region in the mountains. According to al-Baladhuri, Abd Allah ibn Budayl conquered its land in 23 and 24 AH. He took Jay by a peace agreement after a duel with its marzban Fadusfan, made peace with the Jews, and prevailed over its plain and its mountain. He dealt with them over the tax in about the way he dealt with the people of Ahwaz. Its notables submitted to the tax and refused the poll tax, so they became Muslims. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says that Isfahan is the name of the whole region and of its great city, and that the city was at first Jay and later became Jewish. He places it in the fourth clime and gives its longitude and latitude as the astronomers reckon them, and says that people exaggerate its size. There are several accounts of the name. Ibn al-Kalbi says it is named after Isfahan ibn Falaj. Ibn Duraid says the name is compounded from asb, the word for a city in Persian, and han, a horseman, so that it means the land of horsemen. Hamza says the name comes from the army: the original form was Isbahan, the plural of isbah, meaning troops. Yaqut also records that only the men of Isfahan carried the banner of the Sasanian kings, and he connects this with the story of Dahhak and a smith of Isfahan called Kabi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.206}
