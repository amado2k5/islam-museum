## Overview
Marrakesh is a Moroccan city founded by the Almoravids as their capital around 1070 CE. Ibn Khaldun mentions that [[person/yusuf-ibn-tashfin|Yusuf ibn Tashfin]] laid out Marrakesh in 454 AH and settled there, making it the seat of his residence and his army. {cite:tarikh-ibn-khaldun-ibar p.1646}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer lists this place under no. 2110784 with the preferred name «Marrakesch», classed as populated place; its parent in the hierarchy is «Marokko», classed as administrative unit. Recorded names include «Marrakesh», «مراكش» and «Marrakech». {cite:idai-gazetteer 2110784}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Marrakesh as the greatest and most splendid city of the Maghrib, where the throne of the rulers of the Banu Abd al-Mu'min stood. It lies in the great interior, ten days' journey from the sea, in the midst of the lands of the Berbers. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.94}

He says that Yusuf ibn Tashfin first laid it out around the year 470 AH. Before that the place was a danger where robbers cut off caravans, and its people drank from wells until a small supply of water was brought from Aghmat. Abd al-Mu'min ibn Ali was the first to plant gardens there. The Darn mountain, from which Ibn Tumart emerged, lies three farsakhs to its south. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.94}

He gives a Berber sense of the name, "fast walking", as caravan people said it. His date of about 470 differs from the year 454 AH that Ibn Khaldun gives, cited above; both dates are reported here with their sources, and the difference is left open. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.94}
