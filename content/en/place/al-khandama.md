## Summary
A mountain overlooking [[place/mecca|Mecca]], at whose foot the [[event/khandama-skirmish|Khandama skirmish]] took place — the only clash in which weapons were used on the morning of the conquest day, between [[person/khalid-ibn-al-walid|Khalid ibn al-Walid]]'s column and a body of Quraysh and Banu Bakr {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/49-50}.

## The battle
At the mountain gathered Safwan ibn Umayya, Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and Suhayl ibn Amr among fighters of Quraysh and Banu Bakr {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/49}. When they met Khalid's cavalry they stood only briefly; some twelve polytheists were killed, then they fled, and three Muslims were martyred {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/50}.

## For young visitors
A rocky mountain at Mecca where some men of Quraysh tried to fight to keep the Muslims from entering, but the fighting ended quickly and all surrendered.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Khandama", with a fatha on the first letter, as a mountain at Mecca. He relates that when the Prophet came in the year of the Conquest, Safwan ibn Umayya, Ikrima ibn Abi Jahl and Suhayl ibn Amr gathered a force at al-Khandama to fight him. In the report Himas ibn Qays ibn Khalid, of Banu Bakr, had prepared a weapon, went out and fought with those of the polytheists at al-Khandama; Khalid ibn al-Walid turned on them, killing some, and the rest fled, and Himas returned defeated and asked his wife to bolt his door. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.392} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.393}

Yaqut quotes verses Himas spoke to his wife after his return, which speak of the day of al-Khandama and of Safwan and Ikrima fleeing, and verses of Budayl ibn Abd Manat ibn Umm Asram addressed to Anas ibn Zunaym al-Dili that speak of the day of the Khanadim and name Nufayl and Ma'bad. In the same place he lists al-Khandama and Abu Qubays among the mountains of Mecca. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.392} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.393}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Khandama, a mountain at Mecca, and the men who gathered there in Yaqut are those the record names from Ibn Hisham: Safwan, Ikrima and Suhayl, with Khalid ibn al-Walid turning on them. Yaqut gives no count of the dead; the twelve polytheists and three Muslims in the record come from Ibn Hisham alone. Yaqut also tells of Himas and his verse, which the record does not.
