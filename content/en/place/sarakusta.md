## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the vowels of the name as a fatha on the first and second letters, a damma on the qaf, a sukun on the sin and then the ta, and says it is a well-known town of al-Andalus whose districts adjoin those of Tutila, with sweet fruit that surpasses the rest of al-Andalus, built on a large river that rises in the mountains of al-Qila'. He says it is unique for the craft of sammur and for fine weaving, the cloth known as Saraqustiyya, and admits he cannot tell what the sammur he mentions is. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.212}

He adds that it has a mine of Dhar'ani salt, white and pure, found nowhere else in al-Andalus, that it has towns and strongholds, and that it has been in Frankish hands since 512 AH. Among those attributed to it he names Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Ibrahim ibn Yusuf al-Saraqusti, on whom al-Silafi reports, and Thabit ibn Hazm al-Awfi, who travelled east in 288 and died at Saraqusta in 313. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.213}
