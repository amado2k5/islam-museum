## Overview
Humayma is a settlement in southern Jordan that was the home of the Banu al-Abbas before their caliphate. Yaqut lists it in the Mu'jam al-buldan and, under the same name, a second place near Mecca. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.307}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as the diminutive of "hamma" and says that Humayma is a place in the land of al-Sharat, in the districts of Amman on the edge of Syria, which was the dwelling of the Banu al-Abbas. This is the place that matches this record. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.307}

He then lists a different place with the same name: a village in Batn Marr, in the area of Mecca, between Saru'a and al-Burayra, with a spring and palms. He quotes verses by a contemporary poet, Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Qurba al-Utharī, naming the water of Humayma along with Nakhla, Sawla and al-Zayma, which he heard in Egypt from Abu al-Rabi Sulayman ibn Abdullah al-Makki, known as Ibn al-Rayhani. The two places should not be confused. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.307}
