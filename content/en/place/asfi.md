## Overview
A city and island on the coast of the Far Maghrib. al-Idrisi relates the story of its naming, from the deceivers who brought their ships down to it. He also tells the story of Ahmad ibn Umar, known as Duqm al-Iwaz, who was among a part of the fleet of Yusuf ibn Tashfin. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.220}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Asfi", with two fathas and a kasra on the fa, and says no more than that it is a town on the shore of the Surrounding Ocean at the far Maghrib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.180} It is a one-line entry, surrounded by other entries that begin with the same letters but are Persian or Central Asian names, such as Asfijab and Asfidhban, which are other places and unrelated to it; the museum searched the text of the book for the spellings Asfi and Asafi and found no second entry for it.

## The museum's identification
Yaqut and al-Idrisi agree that it is a town on the coast of the Surrounding Ocean in the far Maghrib. Al-Idrisi adds the story of the name, of the sailors cast ashore, of Ahmad ibn Umar known as Daqam al-Iwwaz and of Yusuf ibn Tashfin's fleet; none of that is in Yaqut, and Yaqut does not call it an island, as the record does. The museum's identification with the present city of Safi in Morocco rests on the name, the coast and the record's point (32.3, -9.23), marked as an identified site that still needs a gazetteer check; Yaqut gives no coordinates to test it. What Yaqut calls the Surrounding Ocean is what the museum calls the Atlantic; that is the museum's gloss.
