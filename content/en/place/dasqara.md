## Summary
al-Daskara is a small populous town on the road from Baghdad to Hulwan and its borders, with date palms and crops. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.669}

## Details
Al-Idrisi, describing the road from Baghdad to Hulwan, says: from Baghdad to al-Nahrawan is twelve miles, thence to Dayr Barma twelve miles, and from Barma to al-Daskara twenty-four miles; al-Daskara is a small town with date palms and crops, and it is populous {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.669}. He mentions that outside it stands a clay fortress, empty within, which serves as a farm, said to have been a place where the king used to reside in some seasons of the year, and for that reason it was called Daskarat al-Malik {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.669}. From al-Daskara to Jalula is twenty-one miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.669}. He also counts it among the lands of Iraq and the Jibal together with Khaniqin, Qasr Shirin and al-Sirawan {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.655}. It was an agricultural halt on the old Khurasan highway. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.669}
