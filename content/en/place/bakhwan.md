## Summary
Bakhwan was a town of the Tughuzghuz dominion; in it was a king of the Khagan's people with troops, guards, forts and an administration, and it had a strong wall and markets, says al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.511}

## Details
Al-Idrisi lists it among the lands of the Khagan of the Tughuzghuz together with the city of Khagan called Tantbagh, the town of Masha and Jarmq. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.510} From Tantbagh to Bakhwan, between west and north, was twelve days; from Bakhwan to Jarmq four stages through villages, farms and continuous settlement, lying south-southwest of it; and from Bakhwan to the city of Tibet fourteen days. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.511} Jarmq itself was a sound and fertile town with two earthen walls and between them a deep moat seventy paces wide and four iron gates; it had no market except for the manufacture of weapons, and its governor lived there with horse and foot, guarding that side against the Tibetan kings. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.512}
