## Overview
Zuwayla is a city north of Wadan. Al-Idrisi attributes it to a man who built it and settled in it with his cousins. He mentions that this was in the year 306 AH, and that in his time it was populous. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 115} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 116}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut lists two towns called Zuwayla, and notes a quarter and a gate of Cairo with the same name. The first is Zuwayla of the Sudan, opposite Ajdabiya in the interior, between the land of the Sudan and Ifriqiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.159-160} A passage under al-Bakri's name, whose end the entry does not mark, describes an unwalled town in the middle of the desert, the first border of the Sudan, with a congregational mosque, a bath and markets where caravans meet and part, with some palms and a plot watered by camels. It says that when 'Amr took Barqa he sent 'Uqba ibn Nafi', who reached Zuwayla, so that the land between Barqa and Zuwayla passed to the Muslims; that the poet Di'bil al-Khuza'i is buried there, with a verse by Bakr ibn Hammad on his death there; that Ajdabiya is fourteen stages away; and that the townsmen guard the place by dragging a palm branch round it and following any track that leads out. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160} After the verse the entry adds that the historians say Di'bil was killed in 220 after fleeing to Tus, and does not reconcile this with the burial. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160} The second town is Zuwayla al-Mahdiyya, a bowshot from al-Mahdiyya, where 'Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi settled the commoners while he and his troops lived in al-Mahdiyya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160}

Other entries bear on the first town. Fazzan is a wide province between al-Fayyum and Western Tripoli, and its city is Zuwayla of the Sudan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.260} Tajarfit lies eleven days from Zuwayla, to its west, and eleven from Wadan, to its east. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.5} Wadan is ten days from Zuwayla, on al-Bakri's word. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.366}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, pp.115-116) calls Zuwayla a city north of Wadan, built by a man who settled in it with his cousins in 306 AH. In the four entries read (vol 3 p.159-160, vol 4 p.260, vol 2 p.5, vol 5 p.366) Yaqut gives no founder and no foundation year for Zuwayla of the Sudan, and the Tajarfit entry sets Zuwayla west of Wadan, not north. His entries support a town called Zuwayla in the Saharan land of Fazzan, near Wadan; that al-Idrisi's Zuwayla is the same town is the museum's reading. The record's point (26.1667, 15.1167) is 113.1 km from the museum's Fazzan point and 348.0 km from its Wadan point, on the south side of Wadan, although the record's text says north; no day-count is set against it. Zuwayla al-Mahdiyya and the Cairo gate are other places. See [[place/fazzan|Fazzan]], [[place/wadan|Wadan]] and [[place/ajdabiyya|Ajdabiya]].
