## Overview
Al-Ramij (al-Raij) is a large island of the Zanj archipelago, four hundred miles or more around, one crossing from the land of the Zanj. Most of its people's living comes from bananas, of which they have five kinds, the qand, the fili, the Omani, the Muriyani and the sukkari, a single banana sometimes weighing twelve ounces, wonderful in flavour and delicious. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 62}

## The mountain
A mountain called Jabal Wayra crosses the island, a strong mountain to which those cut off from the town retreat; many of them are there, and they sometimes cut the roads of the town. They hold the heights, fortified against anyone sent from the ruler of the island, strong and bold, well supplied with weapons and gear. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 62}

## Settlement and trade
The island has continuous settlements and many villages with their herds, and rice is grown there. Its trade is great and many people come in each year with all kinds of goods. It is said that when China fell into disorder and India into injustice and confusion, Chinese merchants shifted their trade to al-Ramij and the other islands there and dealt with its people because of their good manners and the generosity of their merchants, so that it is thriving and many travel to it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 62}

## Neighbouring waters
Near it is a small island with a very high mountain no one can climb, from which a great smoke rises by day and a fire burns by night, and from the foot of which spring sweet cold streams and hot brackish ones. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 62} Facing the coastal land of the Zanj are islands called the islands of al-Raij, many in number and broad in land. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 61}
