## Overview
Al-Ahwaz: a town known as Hormuz Shahr, and the capital of the land of Khuzistan. Al-Idrisi describes its site as spacious and its air as healthy. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 392}

## Its people
Al-Idrisi says that it has abundant livelihoods and many good things, and that its people are wealthy and civilised Arabs. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 392}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says that al-Ahwaz is the plural of huz, from an original huz, and that in Persian times the land was called Khuzistan. Al-Ahwaz is the name of the whole province, while the town most people meant by the name was the market of al-Ahwaz. He quotes al-Tawzi that the Persian name was Hurmushir, and Abu Zayd that it was Hurmuz-shahr, the great province to which all the others belong; some say Ardashir first built it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.284}

He quotes the author of Kitab al-Ayn that al-Ahwaz is seven districts between Basra and Fars, and lists the districts: the market of al-Ahwaz, Ramhurmuz, Idhaj, Askar Mukram, Tustar, Jundaysabur, Sus, Saraq, Nahr Tiri and Manadhir. He gives its revenue as thirty million dirhams. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.285}

He describes the land as subject to frequent fever, and quotes al-Mughira ibn Sulayman that the land of al-Ahwaz is copper that grows gold, and the land of Basra is gold that grows copper. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.285}
