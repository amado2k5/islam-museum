## Summary
Ghudash was an island in the western sea due east of the island of Qawsara, a hundred miles from it, possessing a safe anchorage. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.587}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that the island of Qawsara is small and fortified, with wells, coasts and olive trees, and in it many wild goats shy of man; it has on its southern side a safe anchorage sheltered from many winds {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.587}. Due east of it is the island of Ghudash, a hundred miles between them; it has a safe anchorage, and from Ghudash to a small island called Kamuna {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.587}. Elsewhere in enumerating the islands of that sea he says that the island of Ghudash lies east of the island of Namusha, two runs between them {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.588}. He notes that the island of Namusha has no anchorage nor landing place; it is cultivated and anchoring by it is a risk {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.588}. Ghudash thus appears in his scheme as a small island in the chain of islands of the western sea between Qawsara and Kamuna, distinguished by its safe harbour sheltering ships from the winds, its position linked to Namusha westward and the Island of the Book before it.
