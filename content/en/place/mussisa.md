## Overview
Mussisa (al-Masisa) is a city on the river Jayhan. Between it and the city of al-Mullawan there is a stone bridge, and its name in Greek is Mamistra. Around it are continuous gardens and crops, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.646}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut first sets out the spelling: al-Azhari and other lexicographers double the first sad, while al-Jawhari and Khalid al-Farabi alone give both a single sad, and Yaqut judges the first sounder. Two sets of astronomical coordinates follow, the second from Abu 'Awn's zij. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.144-145} The entry then says, without marking whose words they are, that it is a city on the bank of the Jayhan, among the Syrian frontier towns between Antioch and the land of the Rum, near Tarsus; that it is now held by Ibn Layun and after him his son; that it was a famous frontier fortress of Islam where righteous men once kept watch; that it has many gardens watered by the Jayhan and had a wall with five gates; and that, as the ahl al-siyar claimed, it was named for its builder Masisa ibn al-Rum ibn al-Yaman ibn Sam ibn Nuh. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.145} Al-Muhallabi is cited that fur garments were made there and carried to far lands, a fur reaching thirty dinars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.145} A village near Damascus has the same name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.145}

The Jayhan entry adds that the river passes Mussisa on an old broad bridge of Roman stone, that one enters the town across it, and that the river runs on four miles before it reaches the Syrian sea. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.196} The entry on the frontier lists two stages from Bayas to Mussisa, one from Mussisa to 'Ayn Zarba and one to Adhana. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.79}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, p.646) calls Mussisa a city on the Jayhan, with a stone bridge between it and al-Mullawan, continuous gardens and crops, and the Greek name Mamistra. Yaqut agrees on the river, the gardens and a stone bridge, but places the bridge at Mussisa on the Jayhan, not between it and al-Mullawan, and in the entries read (vol 5 p.144-145, vol 2 p.196, p.79) neither al-Mullawan nor the name Mamistra is given. That the record's town is Yaqut's Mussisa is the museum's reading, which his entries support only in the river and the bridge. See [[place/tarsus|Tarsus]] and [[place/adana|Adana]].
