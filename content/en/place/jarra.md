## Summary
Jarra was a populous city of the district of Sabur in the land of Fars, girt with a strong wall of mud brick, possessing an inhabited well-built estate and wide environs. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.409}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that among the cities of the district of Sabur is the city of Jarra, which is populous, surrounded by a strong wall of mud brick, and has an inhabited estate rich in buildings and wide environs {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.409}. The estates of al-Darkhwid and Tanbuk are close in size and populous, with farms, benefits and produce, and likewise the estate of al-Kimarj is fine of site, wide of extent, stretching and yielding every benefit {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.409}. Jarra is mentioned in the description of the cities of the district of Sabur together with Kazirun, which has a wall, a fortress, gates of wood and iron, an inner strong citadel and a populous suburb with markets, commerce and crafts, and in it public fruits and much good {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.409}. From Fasa to Kazirun is fifty-six miles, and from Kazirun to Jur forty-eight miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.409}. Jarra thus appears in his scheme among the walled populous cities of Sabur, adjoining al-Janjan, al-Ramijan and al-Khubdhan in the flourishing quarter of Fars with its markets, trade and fertile estates.
