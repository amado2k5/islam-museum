## Overview
Al-Sughd is the land beyond the river (Transoxiana), and its greatest city is [[place/samarqand|Samarqand]]. Its people came with the Turks and the people of Kush and Nasaf to Sa'id ibn Uthman, with a hundred and twenty thousand men. They met near [[place/bukhara|Bukhara]] and were defeated, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes al-Sughd as a region rich in water, with green trees, singing birds and pleasant gardens, which extends a five-day journey. Many villages lie between Bukhara and Samarkand, and its chief city is Samarkand. The name is sometimes spelled with an s-sound. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.222}

He records a scholar who took his name from the region, Abu al-Ala Kamil ibn Mukarram al-Tamimi al-Sughdi, who lived in Bukhara and transmitted from al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.222}

Yaqut gives a second entry, spelled with sad, calling it a remarkable district whose chief city is Samarkand. He reports one view that there are two Sughds, that of Samarkand and that of Bukhara, and another that the four paradises of the world are the Ghuta of Damascus, the Sughd of Samarkand, the river of al-Ubulla and the Shi'b Bawwan. Its villages run on without a break among trees and gardens from Samarkand to near Bukhara. He passes on al-Istakhri's preference for it over the other three: from Damascus the eye meets bare mountains within a league, whereas in the Sughd of Samarkand greenery, gardens and running streams are continuous over a stretch of eight days, and most houses have running water. He adds that the name was originally that of the valley and river from which these districts drink, and that al-Hazimi counted two Sughds. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.409}
