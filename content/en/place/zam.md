## Overview
Zam is a city approaching [[place/amul|Amul]] in size, with buildings, crops, trade and crafts, according to al-Idrisi. It lies on the road of Khurasan's travellers, and four stages from [[place/tirmidh|Tirmidh]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.481}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name Zamm, with a fatha on the zay and a doubled mim, and reports Abu Mansur's view that it comes from zimam, but says the sound view is that it is a Persian word that was Arabised and is originally pronounced lightly, as the Persians say it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.151} He describes it as a small town on the Jayhun road from Tirmidh and Amul, to which a number of scholars are attributed, among them Yahya ibn Yusuf ibn Abi Karima, Abu Yusuf al-Zammi, who related in Baghdad from Sharik ibn Abd Allah, Isma'il ibn Ayyash and Sufyan ibn Uyayna; Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari, Abu Hatim al-Razi and Ibn Abi al-Dunya related from him, and he was reliable and truthful. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.151} He also quotes Nasr that Zamm is a coastal town he thinks lies between Basra and Oman, and comments that this is how he put it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.151}
