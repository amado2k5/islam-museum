## Summary
A city of the Ahwaz districts, taken by force by Abu Musa al-Ash'ari together with al-Sus and Tustar, in al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 538}

## Details
In the reports on the conquest of al-Ahwaz, al-Baladhuri records that Abu Musa al-Ash'ari took al-Sus, Tustar and Dawraq by force. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 538} Its conquest thus belongs to the recovery of the Ahwaz lands after the people of several of its cities apostatized and rebelled, with Kurds rallying to them, until Abdallah ibn Amir took Ayadhaj after severe fighting. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 538} A trace of it survives in the narrative in that the mother of Waki' ibn al-Dawraqiyya al-Qur'iyyi — a man of Marw — was among the captives of Dawraq, so that her son was named for it; al-Baladhuri notes this when Bukayr pursued Ibn Khazim and fought him near Marw. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 584} Dawraq, then, is one of the cities of al-Ahwaz opened at the hand of Abu Musa.
