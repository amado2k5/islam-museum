## Overview
Raqqada is a city founded by Ibrahim, of the Aghlabids, near al-Qayrawan at a distance of four miles, and it was the seat of rule before the Fatimids replaced it with al-Mahdiyya. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 406}

Golius records that Ibrahim ibn Ahmad ibn al-Aghlab, nephew of Ahmad ibn Ziyad Allah, built four miles from al-Qayrawan a pleasant, healthy site he called "Raqqada", whose circumference was more than ten thousand cubits. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 406}

He meant by building it to take in the people of al-Qayrawan who had multiplied and the long-settled newcomers, so Raqqada became the seat of rule and the emirate. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 406}

When power passed to the Fatimids, its remains were demolished by Isma'il, grandson of 'Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi, and al-Qayrawan declined as their state weakened. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 406} {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Raqqada: a town in Ifriqiya with four days between it and al-Qayrawan; its perimeter was twenty-four thousand and forty cubits, most of it gardens, and nothing in Ifriqiya had sweeter air, fairer breeze or finer soil; it is said that whoever entered it stayed cheerful for no reason. He transmits two sayings on the name: some related that one of the Aghlabids suffered sleeplessness for days and was treated by Ishaq the physician of the atrifal, who ordered him out walking; when he reached the site of Raqqada he slept, so it was named for that and made a residence and a place of relief for the kings. Others said Abu al-Khattab 'Abd al-A'la ibn al-Samh al-Ma'afiri, leader of the Ibadi call at Tripoli, met the rulers of al-Qayrawan at its site, then a meadow, and killed them so that they lay one upon another — and it was named for their lying dead (ruqad). The known account is that Ibrahim ibn Ahmad ibn al-Aghlab built it, moved to it from Madinat al-Qasr al-Qadim and built in it remarkable palaces and a mosque, and its markets, baths and fondouks flourished; it remained the seat of the Aghlabids until Ziyadat Allah fled it before Abu 'Abdallah al-Shi'i, and 'Ubayd Allah lived in it until he moved to al-Mahdiyya in year 308, Ibrahim's foundation being in year 263; then decay entered it and it kept falling ruined until Mu'add ibn Isma'il came to power and demolished what was left, and nothing remained of it but its gardens. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.55} When Ibrahim built it and made it his royal seat he forbade the sale of nabidh in al-Qayrawan and permitted it in Raqqada — two verses by a wit of al-Qayrawan are quoted on it; and he records that 'Ubayd Allah, surnamed al-Mahdi, took it and drove out the Aghlabids in Rabi' I of year 297 and the poets praised him, until one of them — on whom Yaqut comments harshly — addressed him in verses of excess that cannot be quoted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.56}

## The museum's identification
The record's Raqqada is Ibrahim ibn Ahmad al-Aghlab's city near al-Qayrawan, and it is Yaqut's town that was the Aghlabid seat until Ziyadat Allah's flight and 'Ubayd Allah's move to al-Mahdiyya; its foundation in his telling is year 263 and the Mahdi's takeover year 297. The nearness is stated differently: the record's Golius gives four miles, Yaqut four days, and the museum's Raqqada point lies about 9.7 km from its al-Qayrawan point — the disagreement is left on record as it stands. The two name stories (the sleeplessness and the dead lying) are transmitted tales with no decision between them.
