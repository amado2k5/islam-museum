## Overview
Petra: the Nabataeans' capital in the northern Hijaz; a strong power none could rival until the Romans undid it.

## The place
The Nabataeans' civilisation — sons of Nabat ibn Isma'il in the genealogists' presentation — flourished in the northern Hijaz; they formed a strong government to which those at its fringes submitted, and made Petra their capital; none could rival them until the Romans came and undid them {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran}.

Sayyid Sulayman al-Nadwi inclined to the view that the kings of the Ghassanid house and the Ansar of the Aws and Khazraj were of Nabat ibn Isma'il's house and their remnants in those lands {cite:tarikh-ard-al-quran}.

## For young visitors
Petra is an ancient city carved in rock; it was the capital of a strong people called the Nabataeans.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Batra' as though the feminine of al-Abtar: a place named in the Prophet's campaign against Banu Lihyan. From Ibn Hisham: he passed by Gharab, then al-Makhid, then al-Batra'; and Ibn Ishaq, listing the mosques of the road to Tabuk, names a mosque at the edge of al-Batra', in the tail of the Kawakib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.335}

## The museum's identification
The record's al-Batra' is the Nabataean capital in the northern Hijaz on the road to Tabuk. Yaqut's entry says no more than a stage on the route of the campaign in Ibn Hisham and a mosque on the Tabuk road in Ibn Ishaq — no Nabataeans, no rock-cut city — so matching the record to this entry is the museum's reading, same name and same road, not what the source states outright.
