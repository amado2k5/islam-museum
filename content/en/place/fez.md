## Overview
Fez is a Moroccan city founded in the Idrisid period, around 800 CE. It is home to the Al-Qarawiyyin university, founded in 859 CE. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut calls Fez a famous, large city on the Maghrib side of the Berber lands and the most majestic of the Maghrib's cities before Marrakesh was laid out, and he fixes the spelling of its name with the unpointed sin. He describes it as built between two great hills with its buildings climbing both slopes, springs flowing down to a river that splits at the city into eight channels turning some six hundred mills and bringing water into every house; in the Maghrib, he says, only Granada is crossed by water in this way. He adds that purple and crimson cloaks are dyed there, that its citadel stands at its highest point, and that it has three mosques where the Friday sermon is given. Citing Abu Ubayd al-Bakri, he says it is two walled cities, the bank of the Qarawiyyin and the bank of the Andalusians, with more than three hundred mills and about twenty baths, and many Jews; he records the Maghribi proverb «Fas, a town without people». He dates the founding of the Andalusians' bank to 192 AH and the Qarawiyyin bank to 193 AH under Idris ibn Idris, and Idris's death at Walila to 213 AH. He quotes verses by several poets in praise and in blame of the city, gives the distance to Ceuta as ten days, and lists among its scholars Abu Umar Imran ibn Musa al-Fasi, the jurist of Qayrawan in his time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.230} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.231}
