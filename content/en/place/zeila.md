## Overview
Yaqut records Zayla as the name of a people of the Sudan at the edge of the Abyssinian lands, who are Muslims, and whose country is known by the same name. He also describes a village called Zayla, with the letter ayn, on the sea coast on the Abyssinian side. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.164}

## Yaqut's entry
Yaqut cites Ibn al-Ha'ik for the statement that Zayla is an island among the islands of Yemen, with a market to which goats were brought from Abyssinia, where their hides were bought and most of their skins thrown into the sea. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.164}

He gives a report from an older traveller that the Barbar were a group of the Sudan between the lands of the Zanj and the lands of Abyssinia, living in the open country in houses made of grass. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.164} The entry also describes marriage customs ascribed to these people, reported from a single source; they are not repeated here.

The text places Zayla on the Abyssinian coast. The identification with the modern town in Somaliland is the record's own, not Yaqut's.
