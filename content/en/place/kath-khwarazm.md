## Summary
Kath stood on the right bank of the Oxus (Amu Darya) near the modern town of Turtkul. Shohruxmirzo Ismoilov reports from al-Biruni that Afrigh built a palace there and made it his capital, and that coins were struck there in the names of the Afrighids from 305 CE until the Ma'munids ended their state in 995. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 158} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 159}

## The mint
The old Khwarazmian coins name no mint, but the Islamic-type coins of Iraq ibn Mansur, Ahmad ibn Muhammad and Muhammad ibn Ahmad (280, 348 and 366 AH) name "Khwarazm". {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 159} The author thinks that "Khwarazm" on Afrighid coins and "Madinat Khwarazm" (the city of Khwarazm) on Tahirid coins are probably two different cities: the first Kath, the second Jurjaniyya. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 159}

## After 995
In 385/995 Ma'mun ibn Muhammad attacked Kath and captured the last Afrighid. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 70} The city then lost its political importance but remained one of the two largest cities of Khwarazm economically; the author cites the *Hudud al-Alam* for it being a rich city that made bedding and coverlets, cotton and woollen clothing, felt and white sheets, with an advanced weaving industry. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 161} Ali ibn Ma'mun struck coins there in 391 and 392 AH, and there are other undated Kath coins, among them a dirham bearing the name "Kath" and the title Khwarazmshah. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 71} {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 161} From the name "Madinat Khwarazm" on Ma'mun ibn Ma'mun's coin the author infers that people and political circles distinguished Kath from the southern Khwarazmian cities led by Jurjaniyya. {cite:ismoilov-xorazmshohlar-tangalari-2025 p. 162}
