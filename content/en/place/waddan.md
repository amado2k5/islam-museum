## Overview
Waddan is a site between [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/medina|Medina]], near [[place/al-abwa|al-Abwa']]. Its distance from [[place/rabigh|Rabigh]] on the Medina side is twenty-nine miles. The Prophet made his first raid to it in the year 2 AH. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.198}

The book gives no coordinates for it, so none have been set yet.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Waddan, with a fatha, and says the name may come from the word for love; he lists three places of that name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.365-366} The first is between Mecca and Medina: a large village in the region of al-Fur', six miles from Hirsha and about eight miles from al-Abwa', near al-Juhfa, belonging to Damra, Ghifar and Kinana. Nusayb mentions it often in verse, and Yaqut gives lines that he addressed to Sulayman ibn Abd al-Malik. He quotes Abu Zayd that it is a stage from al-Juhfa and six miles from al-Abwa' on the pilgrim road, "on its west", his wording not showing whether "its" means Waddan or al-Abwa'; the passage goes on, in the voice of the source it quotes, to say that while its author was in the Hijaz the place was the seat of a chief of the Ja'fariyyin, the descendants of Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, whose estates lay in al-Fur' and al-Sa'ira, and who were in a long feud with the Hasanis until a Yemeni group, the Banu Harb, took over their estates. He names one man of Waddan, al-Sa'b ibn Jaththama al-Laythi, who settled there, came to the Prophet and died in the caliphate of Abu Bakr. The second is a long mountain between Fayd and al-Jabalayn. The third is a city in Ifriqiya which, he says, Uqba ibn Amir took in the year 46, in the days of Mu'awiya, though the account that follows names Uqba ibn Nafi'; he then gives al-Bakri's account, in which it is a city south of Ifriqiya, ten days from Zawila, of two towns, one of the Sahmiyyun and one of the Hadramiyyun, with a strong fortress, a date economy and little irrigated grain, three days from Tajarft; Bishr ibn Abi Artat took it in the year 23 and the people broke their treaty; and in 46 Uqba ibn Nafi' halted at Ghadamis, left his army there under Zuhayr ibn Qays al-Balawi, and rode on with four hundred horsemen to take Waddan and exact the three hundred and sixty heads that Bishr had imposed. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.365-366} Yaqut's other entries put Rabigh between al-Juhfa and Waddan, and give Tajarft as eleven days from Waddan and from Zawila, which does not agree with the three days between Waddan and Tajarft in al-Bakri's account; Yaqut does not reconcile them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.405} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.5}

## The museum's identification
The record's text and summary, from al-Mubarakfuri, are of the first Waddan, the Hijazi village between Mecca and Medina near al-Abwa', where the Prophet made his first raid in 2 AH; al-Mubarakfuri's twenty-nine miles from Rabigh is not in Yaqut, who gives six or eight miles from al-Abwa' and a stage from al-Juhfa. Yaqut's third Waddan is the Ifriqiyan town, and the record's point (29.1614, 16.139), with the note "Waddan oasis in the al-Jufra district, Libya", is a point of that kind. It lies about 2,383 km from the museum's al-Abwa' point and about 2,391 km from its Rabigh point, so the record's coordinates and its text describe two different places. The museum leaves the coordinates untouched in this packet and sends the question on as a proposal. The point lies about 348 km from the museum's Zawila point, which sits with al-Bakri's ten days from Zawila at about 35 km a day, and straight lines and days are loose measures.
