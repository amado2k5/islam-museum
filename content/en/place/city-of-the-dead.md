## Overview
The Qarafa, which this record calls the City of the Dead, was a quarter (khitta) of Fustat settled by a clan of the Mu'afir, the Banu Ghassan ibn Sayf. Yaqut says that in his time it was the burial ground of the people of Egypt: it had large buildings, wide grounds, a working market, shrines of the righteous and tombs of notable men, among them Ibn Tulun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.317}

## Tombs Yaqut records
Yaqut places the tomb of Imam Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i in the lesser Qarafa, in a madrasa of Shafi'i jurists, and says the area was among the pleasure grounds of the people of Cairo and Egypt on feast days. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.317} In the same section he lists the graves of Ashhab, Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim, Warsh al-Madani and Dhu al-Nun al-Misri, and says that the correct view places Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf in Medina, not in the Qarafa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.142} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.143} He also lists Alid graves, including one he ascribes to Ali ibn al-Husayn in the same dome as al-Shafi'i's tomb. He adds that the list could not be exhausted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.142}

## The mountain and the garden
Yaqut describes the Muqattam, the mountain that overlooks the Qarafa, the cemetery of Fustat and Cairo. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.176} He also describes Birkat al-Habash, a meadow of good soil on the Nile lying behind the Qarafa, which he counts among the finest pleasure grounds of Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.401}
