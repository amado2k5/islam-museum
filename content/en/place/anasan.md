## Summary
Anasan: a place in Basra, named after [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]] for an estate (qati'a) that Ziyad granted him, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503}

## Details
In Futuh al-Buldan, while listing Basra's estates and canals and the people after whom they were named, al-Baladhuri states that Anasan was named for Anas ibn Malik in connection with a qati'a from Ziyad — that is, land in the Basra sawad granted to him by Ziyad ibn Abi Sufyan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503} The notice appears among places known by their owners' names, such as Darjah Jank, one of the holdings of Thaqif, and the canal of Bishr, named for Bishr ibn Muslim, the brother of Qutayba, who presented al-Hajjaj with a horse that won a race and was granted seven hundred jaribs — four hundred by another report — for which he dug the canal. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503} In al-Baladhuri, then, Anasan is one of those sites whose name preserved that of its first holder, because it was tied to a grant of land rather than to a fortress or a town.
