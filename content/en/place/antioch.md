## Overview
Antioch is a great city on the Orontes. Yaqut al-Hamawi, in his entry on Antakiya, reports on the authority of al-Haytham ibn 'Adi that Antiochus, the third king after Alexander, was the first to build it, and calls it the capital of the 'Awasim, the frontier region of Syria, and one of the leading cities, praised for its beauty, its air, its sweet water and its abundant fruit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.358} On its conquest he gives the account that Abu 'Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah marched on it from Aleppo and besieged it, that its people made terms for the poll tax or departure, and that after they broke the treaty 'Iyad ibn Ghanm and Habib ibn Maslama took it; he adds an alternative account in which 'Amr ibn al-'As took it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.361} It remained in Muslim hands as a frontier fortress until the Byzantines took it in 353 AH; Sulayman ibn Qutulmish retook it in 477 AH, and the Franks took it in 491 AH, so that it was in their hands when Yaqut wrote. He also notes the tomb of Habib al-Najjar there, which people travelled from afar to visit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.362}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer of the German Archaeological Institute lists this place under no. 2283111 with the preferred name «Antiochia ad Orontem», an ancient Latin name, classed as an archaeological site and a populated place; its parent in the hierarchy is «Hatay (Provinz)», an administrative unit. Among the names it records are «Antioch» in English, «Antakya» in Turkish, «أنطاكية» in Arabic, «Antākiyyah» in transliterated Arabic and «Ἀντιόχεια ἡ ἐπὶ Ὀρόντου» in ancient Greek.
