## Overview
Shantuf is a fine town at the head of the gulf that runs down to Tinnis and Damietta. It lies on the western bank opposite the village of Zifta, where the Nile divides at its upper end, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.330}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says the village of Zifta adjoins the town of Shantuf, the one at the head of the gulf that runs down to Tinnis and Damietta. The fishing boats that catch the great fish all gather at Zifta, on the tip of the island where the Nile splits into channels. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.330}

Above Shantuf the Nile divides into two arms that run down and join the sea, and each arm sends out two gulfs to the sea. The two large gulfs begin at Shantuf. One runs east to Tinnis and throws off three channels there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.330}

From Misr to Zifta is about fifty miles, and facing Zifta on the western bank is Shantuf, a fine town. From Shantuf to Shanwan is twenty-five miles, and from Shanwan the road goes down ten miles to the village of the Shamiyyin, where sugar cane, cucumbers and the 'a'il crop are grown. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.331}
