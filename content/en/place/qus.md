## Overview
Qus is a city in Upper Egypt on the bank of the Nile, on the caravan road to Aydhab. Ibn Battuta and Ibn Jubayr mentioned it on their way to the Hijaz. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958}

An additional source is needed: the coordinates come from GeoNames for a modern place bearing the same name, and matching it with the ancient site has not been verified.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Qus as a large and great city, Coptic in name, and the capital of Upper Egypt. It lies twelve days from al-Fustat, on the east bank of the Nile, about a farsakh from Qift. Its people are very wealthy, and merchants who arrive from Aden land there, most of them from this city. The heat is intense because the city is close to the southern lands. He places it in the first climate and gives its longitude and latitude. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.413}

He also reports that the suburb of Qus is large and wide, with the houses of merchants and men of property. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.219}
