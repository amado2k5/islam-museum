## Summary
Al-Mismaqan was a flourishing city in Khuzistan on the river bearing its name, busy with exports and imports, rich in palms, and where rice was the chief grain. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.394}

## Details
Al-Idrisi counts al-Mismaqan among the prosperous towns of Khuzistan alongside al-Ahwaz, Askar Mukram, Tustar, Jundi Sabur, al-Sus and Ramhurmuz {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.392}. He records that from the Tustar river branches a stream called the river of al-Mismaqan running westward to the city of Askar Mukram {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.393}. He describes al-Mismaqan as well populated, with much coming and going of goods, ample livelihoods, mostly palm trees, abundant wheat, barley and grains, and rice as their leading grain which they grind and bake into bread they prefer to wheat bread, with sugar-cane yields surpassing those of other lands {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.394}.
