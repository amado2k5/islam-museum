## Summary
Al-Hira was the capital of the [[polity/lakhmid-kingdom|Lakhmids]], the Arab kings who were Persian governors on the edge of the desert. Its people, and the people of al-Anbar, submitted to [[person/ardashir-i-ibn-babak|Ardashir]], founder of the Sasanian state {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}.

## Its kings
In Ardashir's time, the governorship of al-Hira and of all who lived in the Iraqi and Jazira desert, from Rabi'a and Mudar, belonged to [[person/jadhima-al-abrash|Jadhima al-Abrash]]. Ardashir kept a unit of Persian soldiers with the king of al-Hira, to help him against the desert Arabs who rebelled against his rule {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}. After Jadhima, [[person/amr-ibn-adi|Amr ibn Adi]], the first of the Lakhmid kings, ruled al-Hira. The house of Lakhm continued through [[person/al-numan-ibn-al-mundhir|al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir]], then [[person/iyas-ibn-qabisa|Iyas ibn Qabisa]], then a Persian ruler, and then [[person/al-mundhir-al-marur|al-Mundhir al-Maghrur]] in 632 CE, to whom Khalid ibn al-Walid came with the armies of the Muslims {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27}.

## Subjection and religion
The tribes near al-Hira were subject to its king in name rather than in fact {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32}. Some kings of al-Hira embraced Christianity {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}. Weaving, tanning and similar crafts were practised by the people of Yemen, al-Hira and the outskirts of Syria {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.46}.

## For young visitors
Al-Hira is an old Arab city near Kufa. Its kings served the kings of Persia and guarded their frontiers.

## For Explorers
Al-Hira, capital of the Lakhmids — the Arab kings who served the Persians on the desert's edge; its people and Anbar submitted to Ardashir, founder of the Sasanian state. In his reign Jadhima al-Abrash governed al-Hira and the Arabs of the Iraqi and Jaziran steppe of Rabi'a and Mudar, Ardashir keeping a Persian unit with its king against the desert Arabs who broke his rule. After Jadhima, Amr ibn Adi, first of the Lakhmid kings, held al-Hira, and the house of Lakhm continued to al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir, then Iyas ibn Qabisa, then a Persian governor, then al-Mundhir al-Ma'rur in 632 CE, whom Khalid ibn al-Walid reached with the Muslim armies. The tribes near al-Hira were subject to its king in name more than deed; some of its kings adopted Christianity; and weaving and tanning were among the crafts of Yemen, al-Hira and the fringes of Syria.

## In al-Dinawari
[[person/abu-hanifa-al-dinawari|Al-Dinawari]] reports that Rabi'a ibn Nasr al-Lakhmi, after a dream interpreted by Shiqq and Satih, sent his son Amr to Yazdagird son of Shapur, or, some say, to Shapur Dhu al-Aktaf, who settled him at al-Hira: “on that day al-Hira was built”. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 54} It is one of the book's reports of ancient times. He also says Bahram Gur grew up there with al-Mundhir at al-Khawarnaq. {cite:akhbar-tiwal-amir p. 55}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut makes al-Hira a city that stood three miles from [[place/kufa|Kufa]] on a site called al-Najf — they claimed the sea of Persia once reached it — with [[place/al-khawarnaq|al-Khawarnaq]] near it on the east side about a mile away, and al-Sadir in the middle of the desert between it and Syria; it was the seat of the kings of the Arabs in the Jahiliyya from the time of Nasr, then of Lakhm, al-Nu'man and his fathers; its nisba is hariyy off the regular pattern and hayriyy by the pattern, and it is called al-Hira al-Rawha' {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.328}. On its naming he reports that Tubba' the Elder, bound for Khurasan, left the weak of his army there telling them "hayyiru bihi", settle here; from al-Zajjaji, that the first to settle it was Malik ibn Zuhayr ibn Amr ibn Fahm ibn Taym Allah; in some reports of the sira men, that the Nabataean king al-Ardawan built the hir and lodged his Arab allies in it; in Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Hamadhani's book, that Tubba'’s guide lost his way and was bewildered; and from Abu al-Mundhir Hisham ibn Muhammad the report of Bukht Nasr and Yuhanna and the confining of the Arab merchants in an enclosure on al-Najf — these reports carried on their transmitters' names {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.328-329}.

## The museum's identification
The record's al-Hira, the Lakhmid capital, is the city Yaqut describes beside Kufa, al-Khawarnaq and al-Najf. The museum's own measurement — not Yaqut's words — between the records' points al-Hira (31.89, 44.50) and Kufa (32.0511, 44.4402) gives 18.8 km by the haversine formula on a sphere of R = 6371.0088 km, against the printed "three miles"; the disagreement over its naming stands as printed.
