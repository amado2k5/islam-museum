## Summary
Al-Hira was the capital of the [[polity/lakhmid-kingdom|Lakhmids]], the Arab kings who were Persian governors on the edge of the desert. Its people, and the people of al-Anbar, submitted to [[person/ardashir-i-ibn-babak|Ardashir]], founder of the Sasanian state {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25} {cite:muhadarat-tarikh-al-umam-al-islamiyya 1/29-32}.

## Its kings
In Ardashir's time, the governorship of al-Hira and of all who lived in the Iraqi and Jazira desert, from Rabi'a and Mudar, belonged to [[person/jadhima-al-abrash|Jadhima al-Abrash]]. Ardashir kept a unit of Persian soldiers with the king of al-Hira, to help him against the desert Arabs who rebelled against his rule {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26}. After Jadhima, [[person/amr-ibn-adi|Amr ibn Adi]], the first of the Lakhmid kings, ruled al-Hira. The house of Lakhm continued through [[person/al-numan-ibn-al-mundhir|al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir]], then [[person/iyas-ibn-qabisa|Iyas ibn Qabisa]], then a Persian ruler, and then [[person/al-mundhir-al-marur|al-Mundhir al-Maghrur]] in 632 CE, to whom Khalid ibn al-Walid came with the armies of the Muslims {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.26} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27}.

## Subjection and religion
The tribes near al-Hira were subject to its king in name rather than in fact {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32}. Some kings of al-Hira embraced Christianity {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}. Weaving, tanning and similar crafts were practised by the people of Yemen, al-Hira and the outskirts of Syria {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.46}.

## For young visitors
Al-Hira is an old Arab city near Kufa. Its kings served the kings of Persia and guarded their frontiers.
