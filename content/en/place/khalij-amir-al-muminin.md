## Summary
Khalij Amir al-Mu'minin (Canal of the Commander of the Faithful) is an ancient canal connecting the Nile to the Red Sea, which the Arabs attributed to 'Umar ibn al-Khattab's excavation for provisioning Mecca and Medina, while Ptolemy attributed it to Trajan. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 396}

Golius mentions that Caliph 'Umar ordered the digging of a canal from the Nile to the Red Sea so that food could be transported to Mecca and Medina, that sands later buried it, that 'Umar died before its completion, and that no one attempted it after him. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 396} {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 399}

Ptolemy mentions that the river called "Trajan" is the same canal, and Golius suggests that Hadrian may have been its builder or restorer, as he carried out many works in those parts, noting that the book attributed to Ibn 'Abd al-Hakam states that the one who dug it was a Caesar named Adrianus. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 396} {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 397}
