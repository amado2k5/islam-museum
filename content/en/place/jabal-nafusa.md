## Overview
Mount Nafusa is a high mountain about three days long. It has villages, barley fields and running water, and al-Idrisi says the Masamda tribes live on it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.278}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Nafusa as mountains in the Maghrib beyond Ifriqiya, about three miles high or less, with two minbars in two towns, one of them Sarus in the middle of the mountain, where, he says, barley bread is tastier than any other food, and the other Jadu on the Nafzawa side. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.297}

He says the people of these mountains are shurat, Wahbi and Ibadi, defiant of obedience to the rulers; that the mountain is six days' travel long from east to west; and that it is three days from Tripoli and six from al-Qayrawan. He names the tribe of Banu Zammur there, with a very strong fortress called Tirfat holding some three hundred villages and several towns with no minbar, because they have not agreed on a man to follow in prayer; their mountain has many palms, olives and fruits, and sixteen thousand men can gather from the surrounding tribes when they call one another. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.297}

He adds that Amr ibn al-As conquered Nafusa, whose people were Christians, and that from Jabal Nafusa Amr returned with a letter that reached him from Umar ibn al-Khattab. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.297}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Jabal Nafusa, a mountain of the Maghrib with villages, crops, palms and olives. The two accounts differ in length: al-Idrisi in the record makes it about three days long, while Yaqut gives six days' travel from east to west, and the museum has not reconciled them. Yaqut also does not name the Masmuda, whom al-Idrisi gives as the people of the mountain; he names the Banu Zammur and describes the inhabitants by the sectarian terms he reports.
