## Overview
The Palace of Aws is a palace in [[place/basra|Basra]], attributed to Aws ibn Tha'laba ibn Ruqayy of the Banu Taym Allah, lord of the house of the two idols of Tadmur. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the Palace of Aws in Basra as ascribed to Aws ibn Tha'laba ibn Zufar ibn Wadi'a ibn Malik of Taym Allah ibn Tha'laba ibn 'Ukaba, a chief of his people who governed Khurasan in Umayyad days; he says that Ibn Abi 'Uyayna, whom he names, meant it in verses that praise a palace's beauty and set it above the palaces around it; the pronoun in the entry could refer to Aws or to the palace (described here, not quoted). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.356} Under Aws he sends the reader to the Basra palace in the chapter on palaces. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.280} Under Tadmur he says that Aws ibn Tha'laba al-Taymi, owner of the palace of Aws in Basra, passed two stone figures of girls left from the images there and spoke verses asking them whether standing so long does not tire them; the entry makes him the speaker. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.17} He places the Masjid al-Nadb in Basra near the Palace of Aws. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.279}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Baladhuri, is a Basra palace attributed to Aws ibn Tha'laba ibn Ruqayy of Banu Taym Allah, the man of the verse on the two idols of Tadmur. Yaqut agrees on Basra, Taym Allah and the two figures at Tadmur; his line of descent runs through Zufar, Wadi'a and Malik, which is not the record's "ibn Ruqayy", and the museum does not reconcile them. The record's coordinates (24.47, 39.61) and their note, about a fortress of Banu Aws in the Medina oasis, belong to Medina: the point is 0.5 km from the museum's Medina point and 1,048 km from its Basra point, so it fits neither the record's own text nor Yaqut. This is sent to outside research and is not changed here. See [[place/basra|Basra]] and [[place/tadmur|Tadmur]].
