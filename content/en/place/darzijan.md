## Overview
Darzijan is a large village below Baghdad on the west bank of the Tigris, which Yaqut links to al-Mada'in and to the family of al-Khatib al-Baghdadi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.450}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut fixes the name with a fatḥa on the first letter, a sukūn on the second and a kasra on the zāy, and calls it a large village below Baghdad on the Tigris, on the west side. He says it was the home of the father of Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Thabit al-Khatib al-Baghdadi, who used to preach there, and adds that he saw the place himself. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.450}

He quotes Hamza that Darzijan was one of the seven cities that belonged to the Persian emperors and that al-Mada'in was named from it, its original form being Darzindan, Arabised as Darzijan. Yaqut gives this as Hamza's account; it ties the village to the city of al-Mada'in on the Tigris. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.450}
