## Overview
Hulwan is a town at the foot of the mountains of Mah. Al-Baladhuri reports that Yazdegerd fled to it from al-Mada'in with his cavalry, his treasures, his women and his children, and then left it after Jalula. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name Hulwan, with a damma on the ha and a sukun on the lam, and notes that the word means a gift in Arabic and that there are several places called Hulwan; the one at issue is Hulwan of Iraq, at the end of the Sawad where it meets the mountains from Baghdad. He gives one account that it was named for Hulwan ibn Imran ibn al-Haf ibn Quda'a, to whom some king granted it. He describes it as once a large, thriving city, quoting Abu Zayd that in Iraq only Kufa, Basra, Wasit, Baghdad and Samarra were bigger, that its main fruit was the fig, that it stood near the mountains and sometimes had snow, and that it was unhealthy, with sulphurous water, fine pomegranates and figs and sulphur springs used against several illnesses. On the conquest he says that after Jalula Hashim ibn Utba advanced on Hulwan, Yazdgard fled to Isfahan, and Jarir ibn Abdallah took Hulwan by treaty; he dates it 19 AH according to al-Waqidi and 16 AH in the book of Sayf. He names Abu Muhammad al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Khallal al-Hulwani, from whom al-Bukhari and Muslim narrate in their Sahih collections, died 242 AH, and recounts the verses and the stories of al-Mansur about the two palms of Hulwan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.290} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.291}
