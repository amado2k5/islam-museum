## Summary
Arqa is a fine village that al-Idrisi places on the road from al-Mahjara to Saʿda in the land of Yemen. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

## Details
Describing the road toward Saʿda, al-Idrisi says that from Sadum one reaches al-Mahjara, a great village with springs and a deep, water-rich well, where stands a huge tree called Talhat al-Malik marking the boundary between the district of Mecca and the district of Yemen; thence to Arqa, a fine village, then to Saʿda, a small but civilized city containing tanning houses where good hides are tanned and carried to many lands of Yemen and the Hijaz, and from there to Sanaa a hundred and eighty miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}. Arqa is thus a stopping place on the caravan road between the Tihama of the Hijaz and Saʿda.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises Irqa with a kasra on the first letter and a sukun on the second, and says it is the feminine of the word that precedes it; it is a town east of Tripoli, four farsakhs from it, the last district of Damascus, set on the slope of a mountain about a mile from the sea, with a citadel on the mountain. He quotes Abu Bakr al-Hamadhani that it is a town of the 'awasim between Rafaniyya and Tripoli. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.109}

He records that Sayf al-Dawla ibn Hamdan raided it and quotes verses by his poet Abu al-Abbas al-Saffari about it. He lists scholars attributed to it, among them the hadith transmitters Urwa ibn Marwan al-Irqi, Wathila ibn al-Hasan al-Irqi, Ahmad ibn Hamza al-Tanukhi al-Irqi the Shafi'i, his brother Muhammad ibn Hamza, and al-Husayn ibn Isa al-Ansari. He ends by quoting Ptolemy for its coordinates: longitude 61 degrees 15 minutes, latitude 36 degrees 16 minutes. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.109}
