## Overview
Al-Ubulla is a harbour near [[place/basra|Basra]]. Utba ibn Ghazwan took it by force, and he wrote to Umar ibn al-Khattab that it is the port of Bahrain, Oman, India and China. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 477}

## The time of Khalid ibn al-Walid
When Khalid ibn al-Walid came to Basra in the year twelve AH, intending to go to Kufa, he helped Suwayd ibn Qutba al-Dhuhli in fighting the people of al-Ubulla, and left Suwayd in charge of his district, according to al-Baladhuri. Suwayd had been raiding the non-Arabs in the Kharayba district of Basra, and the people of al-Ubulla had gathered against him. Khalid left Basra by day and returned by night. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 338}

## The conquest
Utba ibn Ghazwan then attacked al-Ubulla and took it by force. Al-Baladhuri relates that Utba took al-Ubulla and the Euphrates district, and Abarqubadh and Dastmaysan, and that al-Mughira took Maysan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 477} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 480}

## Its canal
Al-Baladhuri says that the whole canal of al-Ubulla measures four farsakhs by the measure used for it. Its end facing Basra lies about three farsakhs from the city; in the pre-Islamic period that place was called al-Ijana, and the Arabs in Islam named it al-Jazzara. From it begins the canal known today as the Ijana canal. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 490}
