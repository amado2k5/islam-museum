## Overview
Al-Ubulla is a harbour near [[place/basra|Basra]]. Utba ibn Ghazwan took it by force, and he wrote to Umar ibn al-Khattab that it is the port of Bahrain, Oman, India and China. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 477}

## The time of Khalid ibn al-Walid
When Khalid ibn al-Walid came to Basra in the year twelve AH, intending to go to Kufa, he helped Suwayd ibn Qutba al-Dhuhli in fighting the people of al-Ubulla, and left Suwayd in charge of his district, according to al-Baladhuri. Suwayd had been raiding the non-Arabs in the Kharayba district of Basra, and the people of al-Ubulla had gathered against him. Khalid left Basra by day and returned by night. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 338}

## The conquest
Utba ibn Ghazwan then attacked al-Ubulla and took it by force. Al-Baladhuri relates that Utba took al-Ubulla and the Euphrates district, and Abarqubadh and Dastmaysan, and that al-Mughira took Maysan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 477} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 480}

## Its canal
Al-Baladhuri says that the whole canal of al-Ubulla measures four farsakhs by the measure used for it. Its end facing Basra lies about three farsakhs from the city; in the pre-Islamic period that place was called al-Ijana, and the Arabs in Islam named it al-Jazzara. From it begins the canal known today as the Ijana canal. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 490}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Ubulla with damma on both first letters and a doubled lam: the word is long debated — Abu Ali made it a fa'la noun, Abu Bakr weighed fa'la against af'ala, the date-cluster (fudra) was called ubulla, and al-Asma'i told how a Nabataean wine-seller woman named Hub once stood there, her seekers answered hub laka — "Hub is not here" — which the Persians slurred and the Arabs arabicised as al-Ubulla; al-Zajjaji held it was the date-cluster, not the dung. Then: al-Ubulla is a town on the shore of Basra's great Tigris, at the corner of the creek that enters the city, and it is older than Basra, for Basra was garrisoned only in Umar ibn al-Khattab's days while al-Ubulla was then already a city holding Kisra's troops and a commander. Khalid ibn Safwan said he had seen no land its match for scope, fertility of soil, smoothness for mounts, profit for merchants and concealment for the refugee; al-Asma'i counted the earth's three paradises as the Ghouta of Damascus, the river of Balkh and the river of al-Ubulla, and its five husks as al-Ubulla, Siraf, Oman, Ardabil and Hit; and the canal of al-Ubulla running to Basra was dug by Ziyad. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.76-78}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Ubulla is the record's old port of Basra at the creek's corner (by the two records' points about 31.1 km from Basra); its seniority to Basra, Kisra's garrison, Ziyad's canal and the praises of al-Asma'i and Khalid ibn Safwan are all as printed, and the Nabataean Hub tale is al-Asma'i's report as transmitted.
