## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says that Wadi Musa is named for Musa ibn Imran and is a valley south of Jerusalem, between it and the land of the Hijaz, a pleasant valley rich in olive trees. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.346} He gives the reason for the name as a report: when Musa came out of the wilderness with the Children of Israel he carried the stone from which twelve springs had flowed for their tribes, and on reaching this valley, knowing his end was near, he fixed the stone in the mountain there, twelve springs came out of it and divided among twelve villages, one for each tribe, and the stone stayed there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.346} He adds that the judge Jamal al-Din Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Yusuf told him he had seen it there, about the size of a goat's head. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.346}
