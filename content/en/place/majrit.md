## Summary
Majrit is a small city and a strong, inhabited fortress on the slope of the Sierra mountains north of Toledo, which in the Islamic age had a congregational mosque with an established sermon. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.552}

## Details
Al-Idrisi relates that at some distance north of Toledo rises the great continuous mountain known as al-Sharat, stretching from behind the city of Salim to near the city of Qalamriyya at the far end of the Maghrib {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.552}. In this mountain are sheep and cattle in such abundance that drovers supply them to all the lands; they reach the utmost in fatness and are proverbial throughout al-Andalus {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.552}. Toledo has iron and copper mines in its mountains {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.552}. Among its minbar-towns on the slope of this mountain is Majrit, a small city and a strong, inhabited fortress, which in the Islamic period had a congregational mosque and an established khutba {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.552}. It also had the city of al-Fahmayn, which was a civilized town {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.552}.
