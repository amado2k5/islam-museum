## Overview
Khujand is a well-known town beyond the river (Transoxiana) on the bank of the Syr Darya, counted with Farghana. Yaqut describes it in the Mu'jam al-buldan for its pleasantness and fruit, and gives an account of an army sent against it under Salm ibn Ziyad. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.347}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name with a damma on the first letter and a fatha on the second, then nun and dal, and places it in the fourth clime, longitude 92 and a half, latitude 37 and a sixth. He calls it a famous town of Transoxiana on the bank of the Sayhun, ten days east of Samarkand, a pleasant city with no more pleasant place or better fruit in that region, with a river running through the middle and a mountain joined to it. He cites Ibn al-Faqih for a verse by one of its people preferring it to its surroundings, and says the Persian form of its name has a meaning like "that which stirs the heart". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.347}

He reports that when Salm ibn Ziyad came to Khurasan for Yazid ibn Mu'awiya, he sent an army to Khujand while he was staying in Sogdia, with the poet A'sha of Hamdan among them, and it was defeated; he quotes the poet's verse wishing that his horsemen had not been routed at Khujand. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.347}

From al-Istakhri he cites that it borders Farghana though administratively separate, lies west of the Shash river and extends more than a farsakh, all houses and orchards, with a city and a citadel; its fruit surpasses that of other districts, its people are known for beauty and manliness, and since its land cannot grow enough grain, supplies are brought from Farghana, Ashar and elsewhere. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.347}
