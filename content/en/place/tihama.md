## Overview
Tihama is a low coastal land of Yemen, lying between the Red Sea and a range of connected mountains. It runs from al-Shurha to [[place/aden|Aden]] on the coast in about twelve stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 148}

## Boundaries and extent
Al-Idrisi says that Tihama is a part of Yemen, a set of interlocking mountains whose first part begins at the Red Sea and overlooks it. Its boundary to the west is the Red Sea, and to the east a line of mountains running from south to north. Its width, from the mountains to the district of Ghalafiqa, is a four-day journey. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 148}

## Towns and roads
To the east of Tihama lie Sa'da, Jurash and Najran; to the north lie Mecca and Jidda; to the south lies Sanaa, about ten stages away. The land holds the choicest Arabs of all the tribes, and Mecca is its pole and the destination of the people of the Arabian peninsula. From Mecca it is twenty stages to Sanaa, twenty to Zabid, and thirty to Damascus. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 148} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 149}

## Hali
Among its coastal towns is Hali, a small city on the sea and a port for those who come from Yemen and those who go up from the Qulzum. It has dues, and a levy on the ships going up and down; half of the revenue reaches the lord of Tihama. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 138}
