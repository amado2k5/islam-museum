## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name Ghur, with a damma on the first letter, a sukun on the second and a final ra. He describes it as mountains and a province between Herat and Ghazna, a cold, wide and desolate land with no famous city. Its largest place, he says, is a fortress called Firuz Kuh where their kings lived, and from it came the house of Sam, among them Shihab al-Din. He names a man linked to it by the nisba al-Ghuri: Abu al-Qasim Faris ibn Muhammad ibn Mahmud ibn Isa, of Baghdad, whom Yaqut suspects was of Ghurid origin; he reports that Faris transmitted from Ahmad ibn Abd al-Khaliq al-Warraq and from Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Sulayman al-Baghandi, that he was trustworthy, and that he died in 348 AH. His son Abu al-Faraj Muhammad ibn Faris, known as Ibn al-Baghandi, is described by Yaqut as pious, devout and truthful; he dictated in the Mosque of al-Mahdi and died in Sha'ban 409 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.218}
