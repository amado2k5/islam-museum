## Overview
Fustat: a city in Egypt. al-Baladhuri reports that it used to be called Yunah, and that the Muslims named it Fustat because they said, "This is the tent (fustat) of the people and their gathering place." Others say it was because Amr pitched a tent there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.298-301}

## The conquest and the layout
Amr ibn al-As came down at the gardens of Janan al-Rayhan, where its people had dug a trench. al-Zubayr climbed its fortress by a ladder with his sword drawn, and it was taken by force. al-Zubayr laid out plots and built his well-known house there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.298-301}

Its poll-tax was set at two dinars for every adult male except the poor, and its land was made kharaj land. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.298-301}

It is a site in the events of [[event/conquest-of-egypt|the conquest of Egypt]] and [[event/kharaj-milking-egypt|the kharaj of Egypt]].

## Fustat in al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi, quoting al-Jawhari, says that fustat was originally a tent of hair, and that the city of Egypt took its name from it. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.829}

When the Muslims laid out Fustat, the seat of the kingdom moved from Alexandria, which had been the residence of the king and the seat of government for more than nine hundred years, and Fustat became the seat of government where the amirs of Egypt lived. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.829}

The amirs then built al-Askar outside Fustat, and Ahmad ibn Tulun built al-Qata'i beside it. The amirs moved to these, the common people stayed in Fustat, and its building reached a density that exceeded that of most cities of the inhabited world, Baghdad excepted. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.829}

Al-Maqrizi also says that the Muslims laid out Fustat around the mosque of Amr ibn al-As and the Qasr al-Sham'. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.990}

When Shawar could not hold Fustat and Cairo together, he ordered the people to leave Fustat and join Cairo, and then had the fire set in Fustat, so that most of its houses burned, so that the Franks would not take it. After Mirrai left Cairo, the people went back to Fustat. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.829}

## The historians' description
al-Mas'udi reports that some of the pyramids can be seen from Fustat, several miles away. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p.542}

al-Maqdisi arrived there around the year 375 AH. He called it Misr, the copier of Baghdad and the pride of Islam. He reports that a single house was home to about two hundred souls, and that about ten thousand people prayed behind the imam on Friday. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim pp.162-163}

## The founding in Ibn Abd al-Hakam
[[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr]] wanted to settle in Alexandria. Umar asked whether water would come between him and the Muslims; told yes, when the Nile was in flood, he wrote that he did not wish the Muslims to settle where water would lie between him and them, and Amr moved to al-Fustat. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 132} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 133} Al-Layth reports from Yazid ibn Abi Habib that Umar wrote the same to Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and to his governor at Basra, so that Sa'd moved to Kufa and Amr to al-Fustat. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 133}

## The name
When Amr was about to march on Alexandria he ordered his tent struck, but doves had hatched their young in it. He said they had sought sanctuary with them, left the tent standing as it was, and put it in the care of the master of the fortress. When the Muslims returned and asked where to camp, they said: "The Fustat", Amr's tent, which stood on the spot later called Dar al-Hasa. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 133}

## The plots
Ibn Abd al-Hakam lists the tribal plots around the mosque and Amr's house: Quraysh, the Ansar, Aslam, Ghifar, Juhayna and the people of the Banner, then Bali, Lakhm, Ghafiq, Mahra, Tujib, al-Sadif and others. {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 141} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 161} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 164} {cite:futuh-misr-amir-1999 p. 166} See [[place/khitat-al-fustat|the Khitat of al-Fustat]].

## In Ragheb El-Sergani's programme What Did Muslims Bring to the World
In the fourteenth episode [[person/ragheb-al-sergani|Ragheb El-Sergani]] tells the story of how al-Fustat began: after the conquest of Egypt, which he says the Romans had occupied for nine hundred years before, [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] pitched his tent, and a dove came and nested on it and laid two eggs, and he was too tender-hearted to disturb it, so he left the tent standing. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:19:00} The Muslims then pitched their tents around it, and that became the site of the city, with the mosque built beside it, all so that the dove would not be disturbed from her eggs; this is his telling of the report, and this page gives other accounts of the origin of the name. {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-ragheb-el-sergani what-did-muslims-bring-to-the-world/ep14 00:19:30}
