## Summary
Barsaklafa was a city in the steppe on a shallow, fordable river, four days east of Dristra, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.897}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of Dristra through the steppe to the city of Barsaklafa is four days eastward, and that it is a city on a river easy to ford {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.897}. From Barsaklafa eastward to the city of Dasina is four days, Dasina being an urbanized city of wide districts, much cultivation and building {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.897}. Elsewhere he mentions a river that runs from Barsaklafa and passes south between the towns of Batrani and Barfuntu until it enters the sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}. He also names a second city, Maghali Barsaklafa, one day from Barfuntu — a prosperous town of middling size with a fair-sized river running near it {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}. The name thus carried two cities in his account: Barsaklafa of the steppe and Maghali Barsaklafa, both set on rivers.
