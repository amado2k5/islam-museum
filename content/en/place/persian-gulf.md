## Overview
Yaqut calls this sea Bahr Faris, a branch of the great Indian Ocean running from Tiz in Makran to ʿAbbadan; it is the water now called the Persian Gulf or Arabian Gulf. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.343}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Bahr Faris is a branch of the great Indian Ocean, that its Persian name according to Hamza is Zarah Kamsir, and that it runs from Tiz in the districts of Makran to ʿAbbadan, the mouth of the Tigris, which pours into it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.343}

He describes the Tigris dividing at al-Muhrizah, at the tip of the island of ʿAbbadan, into two arms: one goes to the right and pours near the coasts of the land of al-Bahrayn, where ships sail to al-Bahrayn and the land of the Arabs, and the coast runs south to Qatar, Oman, al-Shihr and Mirbat, as far as Hadramawt and Aden; the other goes left and pours on the Persian side, so that ʿAbbadan becomes an island between them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.343}

He then lists the places on the Persian coast: Mahruban, Jannaba with the island of Kharak opposite, Siniz, Bushihr, Najiram and Siraf, the island of al-Lar and the fortress of Hazu with the island of Qays ibn ʿUmayra opposite, which he calls in his day the most flourishing place in the sea and the seat of the sultan of the sea, then Hormuz with the island of Jasak opposite, and Tiz in Makran. Hamza gives the Persian name Zarah Afrank to the gulf running north towards al-Ubulla. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.343} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.344}

He concludes that the sea of Fars and the sea of al-Bahrayn and Oman are one, with the lands of the Persians on its eastern shore and of the Arabs on its western, and its length running from north to south. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.344}
