## Overview
al-Jurf: a place three miles from Medina according to the editors. The Prophet stopped there, and Ali ibn Abi Talib caught up with him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/163}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Jurf, first giving the word's senses (ground the torrents have swept away and eaten; or the bare flank of a smooth mountain), then al-Jurf as a place three miles from Medina toward Syria, where there were estates of Umar ibn al-Khattab and of the people of Medina, and in it the wells of Jushm and Jamal. They said it was named al-Jurf because Tubba' passed it and said "this is the erosion-edge of the land"; it had been called al-Ard, and it is there that Ka'b ibn Malik spoke his verse which Yaqut quotes; this Jurf occurs in several reports, and he also quotes verses of Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf al-Nadiri naming a well and the bottom of al-Jurf. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.128}

## The museum's identification
It is the same al-Jurf the record places three miles from Medina; the museum's al-Jurf point lies about 5.8 km from its Medina point, close to Yaqut's "three miles." Yaqut adds that it was once called al-Ard, that it held the wells of Jushm and Jamal, and estates of Umar ibn al-Khattab and the people of Medina.
