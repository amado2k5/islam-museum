## Overview
Tawda is a city that was self-standing. The Masmuda demolished its walls, then its people rebuilt it and farmed its land because of the goodness of its soil. It lies about twenty miles from Fez, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.249}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that Tawda was the first city in the West to fall into ruin and to lose its condition. The Masmuda uprooted it, pulled down its walls and levelled its houses, so that only its site remained, which is named after the Banu Tawda. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.249}

Then about a hundred men returned to it and rebuilt it. They farmed its land for the goodness of its soil, the healthy growth of its crops and the quality of its wheat. Its markets were busy and its produce plentiful, including its milk, butter and honey. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.249}

It lay near the Ghumara mountains, about three miles from the edge of the range. A wilderness separates it from Fez, crossed by the Sebou valley, and the Luṭa Berbers live there. Their settlement runs from Tawda to the Sebou valley and reaches the village of ʿAkasha. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.249}

Its position made it something like a frontier post, holding back the raiders of Ghumara from the lands around it. Al-Idrisi gives its distance from Fez in two ways: twenty miles from the Sebou valley on the road of the Banu Qawda, and two days from ʿAkasha. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.249}
