## Overview
Ahnas: a small, well-developed town with a large population and wide produce that brings together many crops, as al-Idrisi describes it. Everything eaten there is plentiful and cheap, and its markets and shops are comfortable. It lies on the gulf that comes down from the northern part of most of the Nile. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.130}

## Documents from the place
Documentary papyri of the Islamic period survive from Herakleopolis, the Greek name of the town the Arabic sources call Ahnas. One of them, listed in the Heidelberg register as a new edition of P.Rain.Cent. 121, is a compromise agreement dated 7 October 719 CE and placed at Herakleopolis in the Herakleopolite district of Egypt. {cite:papyri-info tyche;35;60}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut makes Ahnas (with a fatha) the name of two places in Egypt: one is a kura of the Lower Sa'id whose capital is called Ahnas al-Madina, whose districts were attached to the kura of Bahnasa; it is primeval and most of it has fallen ruined, it lies west of the Nile not far from al-Fustat. Some transmitters, as he relays, claimed that Jesus was born in Ahnas, that the palm tree mentioned in Mary's story in the Qur'an is found there, and that Mary stayed in it until Jesus grew up and they went to Syria; it has fruits and olives, and to it is ascribed Dihya ibn Mus'ab ibn al-Asbagh ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Marwan ibn al-Hakam, who rose against the sultan, made for the Oases and elsewhere, and was killed in year 169. Ahnas al-Sughra, also in the kura of Bahnasa, is a large village. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.284}

## The museum's identification
The record's Ahnas is the Egyptian town al-Idrisi describes, and it is Yaqut's kura capital; the claim of Jesus' birth and the Qur'anic palm there is attributed by him to unnamed "some" and is recorded as transmitted report, not affirmed, and the record's identification with Herakleopolis neither adopts nor denies it. Ahnas al-Sughra, the large village of Bahnasa, is another place.
