## Overview
Talaqan is a town in Khurasan. Al-Baladhuri reports, in a version other than Abu 'Ubayda's, that the people of Talaqan were among those who gathered with the people of Tukharistan against the Muslims, together with the people of Jawzjan, Fariyab and their neighbours, reaching thirty thousand men. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 572}

God then struck the faces of the unbelievers, and the Muslims killed them in great slaughter. Those who laid down their arms were granted safety. After this, al-Ahnaf ibn Qays took Talaqan by treaty. He also took Fariyab, though some say that Amir ibn Ahmar took it, and then marched on to Balkh. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 573}

In the fighting in Khurasan between 'Abd Allah ibn Khazim and Sulayman, Sulayman was killed. The remnant of his army gathered under 'Umar ibn Murthid at Talaqan. Ibn Khazim marched against them and killed 'Umar there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 583}

When al-Hajjaj appointed Qutayba ibn Muslim al-Bahili to Khurasan, the landowners of Balkh met him at Talaqan, and they crossed the river with him. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 590}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut lists more than one place called al-Talaqan. The first, in Khurasan, lies between Marw al-Rudh and Balkh, three stages from Marw al-Rudh. He quotes al-Istakhri that it is the largest city of Tukharistan, set on level ground a bowshot from the mountain, with a large river and orchards, and about a third the size of Balkh. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.6}

Among the people of merit who came from it, Yaqut names Abu Muhammad Mahmud ibn Khidash al-Talaqani, who heard from Yazid ibn Harun and Fudayl ibn Iyad, was in turn heard by Abu Ya'la al-Mawsili and Ibrahim al-Harbi, and died in 205 at the age of ninety. He also names the Sufi Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Talaqani, from whom al-Khatib al-Baghdadi and al-Humaydi narrated; citing Ghayth ibn Ali, Yaqut says he came from the Talaqan of Marw al-Rudh, travelled widely, settled in Tyre until he died there, and that his death was in 466, or 463 according to another report. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.7} Yaqut then treats a second Talaqan, a town and district between Qazwin and Abhar, which is a different place from ours. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.7}
