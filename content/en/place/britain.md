## Overview
Britain, according to the place card, is an island in northern Europe on which the [[place/united-kingdom|United Kingdom]] lies. This page is limited to what two books read for the museum say about the island and its kings before the modern age, and about its name: Ali Muhammad al-Salabi's *al-Dawla al-Uthmaniyya* (2001), where England and Scotland appear in the coalitions of the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, and the Arabic *Hadir al-Alam al-Islami* with the notes of Shakib Arslan, where Britain serves as a yardstick for the size of islands. The part played by the British state in the Muslim East in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries belongs on the page for the United Kingdom. Yaqut's *Mu'jam al-Buldan* has no entry for the island, and al-Qazwini's *Athar al-Bilad* has an entry for Ireland alone, so nothing is relayed here from an intermediate source. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.66} {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 1 p.371}

## England and Scotland in the crusading coalitions
Al-Salabi says the campaign called for against the Ottoman state by King Sigismund of Hungary and Pope Boniface IX drew fighters of several nationalities, among them England and Scotland, 120,000 in all, and set out for Hungary in 1396 (the book also prints 800 AH, which does not match that year). {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.66} See [[event/battle-of-nicopolis-1396|the battle of Nicopolis]] and [[place/scotland|Scotland]].

He relates that the kings of England, France and Castile and the emperor of Constantinople sent messages to [[person/timur-lang|Timur]] congratulating him on his victory over Bayezid, and that Europe believed it had been rid of the Ottoman danger for ever. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.70}

On Pope Pius II's crusade after [[person/mehmed-ii|Mehmed the Conqueror]] took Constantinople he says the European states excused themselves when the time came to march because of their own troubles: the Hundred Years' War had worn out England and France, and Britain was occupied with her constitutional affairs and civil wars. {cite:dawlat-uthmaniyya-salabi p.119} This is the author's account of why Britain was absent from that campaign; on the same page he uses "England" and "Britain" for one country.

## Britain as a yardstick in Arslan's notes
In Arslan's notes on the Dutch East Indies the size of Britain is the measure: Java and Madura, 131,508 square kilometres, equal England without Scotland, Wales and Ireland; Sumatra, 420,000 square kilometres, is larger than Great Britain; and the Dutch islands together, 1,900,000 square kilometres, equal Great Britain, France, Germany, Belgium, Holland, Switzerland, Denmark and Sweden combined. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 1 p.371} These are the figures of the note as printed and have not been checked.

## Note on names and sources
Al-Salabi writes "England", "Inklitra" and "Britannia", and *Hadir al-Alam al-Islami* writes "England", "Great Britain", "the English" and "Inklitra", and neither distinguishes in the passages read between the island and the kingdom. Because the graph holds two cards, "Britain" and "United Kingdom", the exclusions list proposes that one be considered an alias of the other; that is not settled here. The page numbers of *Hadir al-Alam al-Islami* are the printed ones.
