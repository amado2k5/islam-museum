## Overview
al-Nujayr: a fortress of Banu Kinda in [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.141-145}

## The siege
Al-Ash'ath ibn Qays and the notables of Kinda took refuge in it during their apostasy. Ziyad ibn Labid and al-Muhajir ibn Abi Umayya besieged it until they were exhausted, and they came down on terms of safe-conduct. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.141-145}

## After the siege
al-Baladhuri reports that women were found in the fortress who rejoiced at the death of the Prophet, and [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] ordered that their hands and feet be cut off. The captives of the fortress were returned for ransom, at four hundred dirhams per head. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.141-145}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name in its diminutive form and describes al-Nujayr as an impregnable fortress in Yemen near Hadramawt, to which the apostates fled with al-Ash'ath ibn Qays in the days of Abu Bakr; Ziyad ibn Labid al-Bayadi besieged it until he took it by force and captured al-Ash'ath, in the year 12 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.272} He tells the story that al-Ash'ath had come to the Prophet with the delegation of Kinda, who accepted Islam, and the Prophet sent Ziyad ibn Labid with them as collector; after the Prophet's death Ziyad called them to pledge to Abu Bakr and al-Ash'ath held back, so Abu Bakr wrote to al-Muhajir ibn Abi Umayya at Sanaa to reinforce Ziyad, and Ziyad prevailed over his opponents until they fortified themselves in al-Nujayr and he besieged them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.272} Yaqut reports that al-Ash'ath asked for a safe-conduct, that Ziyad secured seventy men and then agreed to send al-Ash'ath with his family to Abu Bakr, that they opened the fortress, and that Ziyad had about seven hundred of their nobles put to death, after which the people blamed al-Ash'ath for taking safety for himself and his household and not for them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.273} He adds that Abu Bakr rebuked al-Ash'ath when he was sent in bonds with Nuhayk ibn Aws, that al-Ash'ath asked to be spared for his wars and to marry Abu Bakr's sister Umm Farwa, and that this was done; al-Ash'ath stayed in Medina until Umar called the people to fight the Persians and he went out with them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.273} He also quotes Arram on a spring of the same name opposite the village of Safina, beside the spring of al-Nujara, both somewhat but not strongly salty. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.273}
