## Overview
Khoy is a place recorded in the German gazetteer. Yaqut describes it in the Mu'jam al-buldan, and the iDAI.gazetteer records it too.

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer lists this place under no. 2733880 with the preferred name «Khoy», classed as both an administrative unit and a populated place; its parent in the hierarchy is «Āẕarbāyǧān-e Ġarbī (Ostan)» (an administrative unit). Among its recorded names are «Khowy», «Khvoy» and «Khoi» and the Arabic-script form «خوی». It links the place to GeoNames no. 126972, GND no. 4712623-1 and Arachne no. 1210690. A comment in the record notes two caravanserais west of Khoy, 34 km and 41 km away, and the record carries the tag «caravanserai». {cite:idai-gazetteer 2733880}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut treats Khuwayy in one entry together with other places of the same name, among them a valley in the Arab lands. He then says that Khuwayy is also a well-known town of the districts of Azerbaijan, a fortress rich in good things and fruit, after which the Khuwi cloths are named. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.408} He names as men of the town the physician Abu Ma'ad Abdan al-Khuwayy, who related from al-Jahiz and from whom Abu Ali al-Qali related, and Yusuf ibn Tahir ibn Yusuf ibn al-Hasan al-Khuwayy, Abu Ya'qub, a man of letters from Khoy whom he calls a fine writer, a skilled jurist of good conduct and a pleasing poet. He wrote an ijaza for Abu Sa'd, lived at Nawqan in Tus and served there as deputy judge, and left works, among them a treatise on clearing the Qur'an of the stain of error and corruption; Yaqut reports Abu Sa'd's guess that he was killed in the clash at Tus in 549 AH or a little before. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.408} The entry also names Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Muslim al-Khuwayy, who related from Ja'far ibn Ibrahim the muezzin, with Abu al-Qasim Abdallah ibn Muhammad ibn Ibrahim ibn Idris al-Shafi'i among those who related from him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.409}
