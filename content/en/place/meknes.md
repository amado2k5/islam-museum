## Overview
Meknes is a fine city raised on high ground, with water flowing to its east, full of markets, baths and houses, and joined by gardens and crops. Al-Idrisi mentions that it was named after the Meknassa tribe. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.244}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Meknes as a town in the Maghrib, in Berber country on the mainland, fourteen stages from Marrakesh towards the east. It was two small towns on a white hillock with a fortress between them. Yusuf ibn Tashfin founded one, and the other is older. Most of its trees are olives, and Fez is one stage away. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.181}

He quotes Abu al-Asba' Sa'd al-Khayr al-Andalusi, who said that a Meknes in al-Andalus was a fortress in the district of Merida. He also records a second Meknes on the coast, on the road from Fez to Sale, with an anchorage for ships, from which wheat was carried to eastern al-Andalus. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.181}
