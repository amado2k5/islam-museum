## Overview
al-Farama: the eastern frontier port of Egypt. After Amr ibn al-As reached [[place/al-arish|al-Arish]], he came to al-Farama, where a people were ready to fight. He fought them, defeated them and took their camp, and then went on to Fustat. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.298}

It is a site in the events of [[event/conquest-of-egypt|the conquest of Egypt]]. See also [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes al-Farama as a city on the coast of Egypt, and records Abu Ali al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Farami, a transmitter who died in 334 AH, as taking his nisba from it. He cites al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Muhallabi's description of it as a pleasant fortress on the sea shore whose air was unhealthy, surrounded by salt marshes, with no crops and no water to drink except rainwater stored in cisterns and Nile water carried by boat from Tinnis. Its people were thin and sallow, and most of their trade was in dates' stones, barley and fodder, because caravans passed through often. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.255}
