## Overview
A place in the district of [[place/yanbu|Yanbu]]. The Prophet reached it in Jumada al-Ula in the year 2 AH to intercept the caravan of Quraysh returning from al-Sham, but it had passed him by some days, according to al-Mubarakfuri. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
In the 'ashara entries Yaqut records that the Prophet raided Dhu al-'Ushayra, which lies in the direction of Yanbu' between Mecca and Medina; Abu Zayd calls al-'Ushayra a small fortress between Yanbu' and Dhu al-Marwa whose dates surpass all the dates of the Hijaz except the Sayhani of Khaybar and the Barni and 'Ajuz of Medina; and al-Asma'i places the wadi Khaww near Qatan pouring into Dhu al-'Ushayra — a wadi with palms and waters of the Banu Abdallah ibn Ghatafan that empties into al-Rumma facing south, with Mubhil above it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.127} From Abu Abdallah al-Sukuni he gives Dhat al-'Ushayra — also called Dhat al-'Ushr — a stage of the Basrans toward al-Nabbaj after Masqat al-Raml, with the sand of al-Shiha between them, Samira' nine miles before it on an upland, belonging to the Banu 'Abs; Yaqut comments that this is the one al-Azhari mentioned, while the one the Prophet raided appears in al-Bukhari's book as "al-'Ushayra" or "al-'Ushayra'" — the weaker form — and it is also said al-'Usayra or al-'Usayra' with a plain sin; al-Suhayli reports that Qatada, asked about it, said al-'Usayr, and in his view the correct form is al-'Ushayra, a diminutive of the 'ushara tree with dhat prefixed; Ibn Ishaq places it in the land of the Banu Mudlij and Ibn al-Faqih names it among the wadis of al-'Aqiq. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.127-128} He also gives, as a separate entry, 'Ashira with a fatha then a kasra, in the sense of a clan: a place name, from al-Hazimi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.128}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Dhu al-'Ushayra is the raid-site of the record in the Yanbu' direction between Mecca and Medina; he locates it in the land of Banu Mudlij from Ibn Ishaq and describes the small fortress with prized dates from Abu Zayd. His other Dhat al-'Ushayra on the Basrans' road to al-Nabbaj is a different stage which Yaqut himself distinguishes, and the vocalisation opinions ('Ushayra', 'Usayra with sin) are reported as he gives them from al-Bukhari, al-Suhayli and Qatada.
