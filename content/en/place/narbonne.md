## Overview
Arbuna is a town on the eastern edge of al-Andalus as Yaqut al-Hamawi describes it, in Frankish hands in his day, which the museum reads as Narbonne in southern France. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.140}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Arbuna", with a fatha or damma on the first letter, a sukun on the ra, a damma on the ba, a silent waw, then nun and ha. He says it is a town at the edge of the frontier (thaghr) of the land of al-Andalus, now in Frankish hands, and that it lies a thousand miles from Cordoba, "as Ibn al-Faqih mentioned", adding "and God knows", so he reports the figure without vouching for it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.140} Elsewhere, describing the shape of al-Andalus by its corners, he places one corner in the east between the city of Arbuna and the city of Bardil, both now in Frankish hands, facing the islands of Mayurqa and Manurqa; Arbuna faces the Mediterranean and Bardil the Ocean, and between them is the mountain with the temple of Venus (al-Zuhra), which divides al-Andalus from the land of the Franks. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.263} He says no more.

## The museum's identification
Yaqut gives neither a modern name for Arbuna nor coordinates. The link to Narbonne rests on the Arabic name as it stands in the record's label, on the Wikidata identifier Q6555 recorded for it, and on his placing it on the Mediterranean at the border between al-Andalus and the land of the Franks, which fits that site; the museum does not count the last as independent proof. The thousand miles to Cordoba is Ibn al-Faqih's figure as Yaqut passes it on with a reservation, and the museum has not used it to test the record's coordinates (43.18, 3.00), which come from Wikidata and are marked approximate. The record's descriptions as a "province" and a "commune" come from Wikidata, not from Yaqut. Yaqut sees in it a frontier town and no more, so nothing of its Islamic-period history is drawn from him here.
