## Overview
al-Rabadha: a place near Medina, according to the editors. Ibn Ishaq reports that Abu Dharr was exiled to it, and that his appointed end overtook him there.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name al-Rabadha with two fathas and a dotted dhal, and relates from Tha'lab on Ibn al-A'rabi's authority that al-rabadha means hardship, while the Kitab al-'Ayn makes al-rabadh lightness of the legs in walking and lightness of the fingers in work; from Ibn al-Kalbi on al-Sharqi's authority it is one of the daughters of Yathrib ibn Qaniya in an old genealogy. He makes it one of the villages of Medina, three days away, near Dhat 'Irq on the Hijaz road for one who sets out from Fayd toward Mecca, and says that in it is the grave of Abu Dharr al-Ghifari, whose name was Jundab ibn Junada, who had gone out to it in anger at 'Uthman ibn 'Affan and stayed there until he died in the year 32. He also reads in al-Ahwazi's history that al-Rabadha was ruined in the year 319 through continuous wars between its people and Dariyya, after the people of Dariyya sought the aid of the Qaramita against them, so its people left it and it was ruined, though it had been among the best halts on the Mecca road. From al-Asma'i it lies in al-Sharf of Najd, the right-hand hima, and in Nasr's book it is one of the pilgrims' halts between al-Sulayla and al-'Umq; among those given its nisba is Musa ibn 'Ubayda al-Rabadhi, whom the Khawarij killed in the year 130. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.24-25} The book also has a separate entry, Abraq al-Rabadha, a place where an engagement between the people of the Ridda and Abu Bakr took place, one of the halts of Banu Dhubyuan that Abu Bakr made a hima for the Muslims' horses. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.68}

## The museum's identification
The record makes al-Rabadha a place near Medina to which Abu Dharr was exiled and where his appointed time overtook him, from Ibn Ishaq. Yaqut's entry (vol 3 p.24-25) agrees: three days from Medina on the Hajj road, Abu Dharr's grave there and his withdrawal to it in anger at 'Uthman; it adds the ruin of the year 319, the pilgrims' halt and the al-Rabadhi nisba. Abraq al-Rabadha (vol 1 p.68) is another place with its own entry and is not to be conflated. The record's point is (24.6319, 41.2906). See [[place/medina|Medina]].
