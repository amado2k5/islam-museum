## Summary
Kawar was a land of the Sudan near Kanem and Kuku; among its towns was Tamlama, in which is an alum mine sold at Kawar, which the merchants adulterate and carry to all quarters. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.29}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Kuku to the city of Ghana is a month and a half, and from the city of Kuku to the city of Tamlama eastward is fourteen stages; it is a small city of the land of Kawar, congregational, with many people and no wall, and in it a man who had risen in revolt {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.29}. It stands on a small mountain, yet an impregnable one whose slopes surround it on all sides; it has palms and flocks, its people naked and wretched; their drink is well water whose water is far below the surface {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.29}. In it is an alum mine of no great quality; they sell it at Kawar and the merchants mix it with the good alum and travel with it to all quarters {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.29}. From Tamlama to the city of Manan of the land of Kanem is twelve stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.29}. He notes that from behind the oases to the abodes of Kawar and Kuku those lands are not without palm groves and remains of buildings {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.40}. Kawar thus appears in his scheme as a Sudanese land between Kuku and Kanem, joined to the desert oases, from whose earth alum is drawn and traded to the regions.
