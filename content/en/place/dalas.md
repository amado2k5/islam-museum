## Overview
Dalas: a city on the east bank of most of the Nile. al-Idrisi reports that the polished coats of mail attributed to its craft were known by it, and that the Berbers and the worst of the Arabs spoiled its building, so that few of its people remained. This is the author's account of its history. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.131}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Dalas as a kura of Upper Egypt on the west of the Nile, "taken from the barr" (the wording is open), with villages and a wide jurisdiction; its city, Dalas, is counted in the kura of al-Bahnasa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.459} From it came Abu al-Qasim Hassan ibn Ghalib ibn Najih al-Dalasi, who narrated from Malik ibn Anas and al-Layth ibn Sa'd, was reliable, and died at Dalas in 223. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.459} In the list of the kuras of Upper Egypt that Yaqut takes from al-Quda'i, the kura of Dalas is among those named. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.139} Yaqut also cites verses under other headwords in which the word dilas is an adjective of coats of mail; those lines do not tie the word to the town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.213} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.374}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, p.131) calls Dalas a city on the east bank of most of the Nile, says the polished mail coats attributed to its craft were named for it, and says the Berbers and the worst of the Arabs spoiled its building. Yaqut puts it on the west of the Nile (vol 2 p.459) and, in that entry, says nothing of mail coats or of ruin. The two accounts name opposite banks, and the entries do not reconcile them; the museum does not choose. Yaqut's entry supports a town and kura named Dalas in Upper Egypt; that al-Idrisi's Dalas is the same place is the museum's reading. The record's point (29.1858, 31.1342) is not changed.
