## Overview
India, al-Hind to the Muslim geographers, is the Indian subcontinent; the museum records it as a region with an approximate central point at about 21 degrees north and 78 degrees east. {cite:geonames-gazetteer} {cite:wikidata} This page does not yet carry an account of India itself from a source read for this run, so the approximate geographic entry is all the museum's records support at present.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam says that, of India's population of one billion, fourteen percent are Muslim; it does not date these figures. It describes them as marked by economic and political diversity and divided further by region and language, naming Bengali, Deccani, Gujarati and Hindustani groups, along with Mappila, Oriyya and Punjabi ones. Most are Sunnis of the Hanafi legal tradition; about 10 percent are Twelver Shiis and a smaller share are Ismaili Shiis. When British India was partitioned at independence, it says, most Indian Muslims backed the division, though a sizeable minority sided with the Indian National Congress. Since independence they have been politically active, forming one of the world's biggest national Muslim communities. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1031}
