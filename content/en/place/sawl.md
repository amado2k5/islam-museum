## Overview
Sawl is a large village with markets, groups of people, palms and fruits, according to al-Idrisi. It lies at the mouth of the gulf called the Gulf of al-Munha. The author mentions that the canals of al-Fayyum were dug from it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.125}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives two entries with the same consonants. The first, vocalised Sawl, which is the one relevant here, is a village on the Nile at the beginning of Upper Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.435}

The second, vocalised Sul, is by his account a foreign word whose root in Arabic he does not know: a city in the land of the Khazars near the Bab al-Abwab (Derbend). He adds that it is not the place behind the name al-Suli, which comes from a man, a king of Tabaristan who became Muslim at the hands of Yazid ibn al-Muhallab. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.435}
