## Overview
Sabitla was, according to al-Idrisi, among the most beautiful of lands in aspect and the finest in air and soil, with orchards and gardens. He mentions that the Muslims conquered it and killed its great king there, and that it lies several stages from Qayrawan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.283}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name Subayṭila (a damma, a fatha, then ya, and a kasra on the emphatic ṭ) and gives it as a city of Ifriqiya. He adds, with no speaker named, that they claim it was the city of Jirjir, the Roman king, and that it is seventy miles from al-Qayrawan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.187} The whole text searched for the name, with diacritics removed and the taa marbuta and hamza forms merged, shows it only in this entry.

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, p.283) praises the city's air and orchards, says the Muslims conquered it and killed its great king, and puts it several stages from Qayrawan. Yaqut's entry (vol 3 p.187) mentions no conquest, no gardens and no killing; it names Jirjir as the king the unnamed speakers connect with the city, and whether he is al-Idrisi's "great king" the entry does not say. The two give the distance in different units, seventy miles against several stages, and the museum does not convert one into the other; its point (35.2297, 9.1294) is 100.8 km from its Qayrawan point.
