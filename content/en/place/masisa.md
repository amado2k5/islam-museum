## Overview
Al-Masisa is the Syrian frontier fortress on the Jayhan river. Al-Baladhuri mentions that the Muslims did not settle there until Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Malik built its fortress in the year 84 AH on its old foundations. He settled three hundred selected men there and built a mosque on its hill. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} See [[event/building-of-masisa|The building of al-Masisa]].

It had a church which Byzantine vanguards would visit. Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz wished to demolish it, but held back when it became known that it defended Antioch. Then Hisham, Marwan and Abu al-Abbas succeeded one another in building it, followed by al-Mansur, al-Mahdi and Harun. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name al-Massisah with a fatha, then a kasra, a doubled sad, a quiescent ya' and a second sad, as al-Azhari and the other philologists fixed it; al-Jawhari and Khalid al-Farabi alone say al-Musaysa with lightened sads, and the first is the sounder. Its longitude is sixty-eight degrees and its latitude thirty-seven, in the fifth clime — others say the fourth; Abu Awn in his Zij gives fifty-nine degrees longitude and thirty-six latitude, fourth clime. It is a city on the bank of the Jayhan among the Syrian marches between Antioch and the Byzantine lands, close to Tarsus; in his day it has been in the hands of Ibn Layun and his son after him for many years, while formerly it was among Islam's famous march-towns where the righteous kept ribat; it has many orchards watered by the Jayhan, and had a wall and five gates. The people of the siyar claim it is named for the one who built it, Masisa son of al-Rum son of al-Yaman son of Sam son of Nuh; al-Muhallabi says among the march's distinctions is that furs were made in the town of al-Massisah and carried to the horizons, a single fur sometimes fetching thirty dinars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.144-145} Al-Massisah is also a village of Damascus near Bayt Lahya — whence Yazid ibn Abi Maryam al-Thaqafi al-Masisi, whom Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik made governor of Aribat al-Shahr and then dismissed — and he tells the tale of Abu al-'Umaytir the Khariji, who rejoiced thinking his companions had taken the march-town, not the Damascus village. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.145}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's al-Massisah is the record's march-fortress on the Jayhan (Mopsuestia); Abdullah ibn Abd al-Malik's building of it in year 84 and the later march-builders come from the record's al-Baladhuri, while Yaqut describes it as a famous Muslim frontier town fallen in his own day (the seventh/thirteenth century) to Ibn Layun and his son, adds its costly furs and five-gated wall, and himself keeps the Damascus village of the same name apart.
