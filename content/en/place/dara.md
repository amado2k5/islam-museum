## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Dara is a town at the foot of a mountain between Nusaybin and Mardin, in the Jazira, with gardens and running water, and Yaqut gives an account of its founding by Alexander. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.418} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.419}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Dara is a town at the foot of a mountain between Nusaybin and Mardin, gives its longitude and latitude in degrees, and says it is in the lands of the Jazira, with gardens and running water. He notes that the mahlab with which the Bedouin perfume themselves is brought from its districts. He then relates that the camp of Dara son of Dara the king stood there when he met Alexander son of Philip the Macedonian, that Alexander killed him, married his daughter and built the city on the site of the camp, naming it after him. This is given as what people say, and Yaqut offers no source. He also cites verses of a rider who tells his leg between Harran and Dara to be patient until God provides a donkey; the verses are described here, not quoted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.418}

## Other places called Dara
Yaqut then lists a second Dara, a strong fortress in the mountains of Tabaristan, and a third, a valley in the country of Banu Amir that appears in verse of Humayd ibn Thawr. They are different from the town of the Jazira. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.418} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.419}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Dara an ancient city in Mardin Province, Turkey, with an identifier in the al-Thurayya gazetteer, which agrees with Yaqut's place between Nusaybin and Mardin. What Yaqut says about Alexander is an account of the origin of the name that people repeat, not an established fact. Mardin and Harran have records, [[place/mardin|Mardin]] and [[place/harran|Harran]].
