## Overview
Raqqa is a city on the Euphrates, one of the districts of al-Jazira. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407} It is part of [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]]. See [[event/conquest-of-raqqa|The conquest of Raqqa]].

## The conquest
Iyad ibn Ghanm conquered it by treaty after five days of siege, on terms of their persons, property and city. He confirmed the land in their hands in exchange for kharaj. A covenant of security was written for them with conditions: that they not establish a church, and that they not display a bell (naqus), a horn (ba'uth) or a cross. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## Al-Rafiqa
Beside Raqqa is al-Rafiqa, which was built in the year 155 AH on the model of the City of Peace, and in which troops from Khurasan were settled. Al-Rashid built its palaces, and Ali ibn Sulayman transferred to it the markets of Raqqa. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes al-Raqqa as a famous city on the Euphrates, three days from Harran. He counts it among the towns of the Jazira because it lies on the river's eastern bank, gives its position as longitude 64 and latitude 36 degrees in the fourth climate, and says it is also called al-Raqqa al-Bayda' (the White). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.59} For the name he quotes al-Asma'i, who says that al-riqaq is soft ground without sand. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.59}

He dates its entry into Muslim hands to the year 17 AH. Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, governor of Kufa, sent an army under Iyad ibn Ghanm; when news reached the people of al-Raqqa they reasoned that they lay between Iraq and Syria, both now held by the Muslims, and sent to Iyad to ask for peace, which he accepted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.59} Yaqut gives verses by Suhayl ibn Adi on the taking of the city and praise of it by the poets Rabi'a al-Raqqi and Ubayd Allah ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.59} He also reports the positions that Ptolemy gave for al-Raqqa al-Bayda' and al-Raqqa al-Wusta. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.60}

On the western bank, Yaqut says, there was another city known as Raqqat Wasit, with two palaces of Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik on the road to Hisham's Rusafa. A farsakh below al-Raqqa lies al-Raqqa al-Sawda', a large village with many orchards watered from the Balikh. He explains that "the two Raqqas" are al-Raqqa and al-Rafiqa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.60} Among the scholars named for the city he lists Abu Amr Hilal ibn al-Ala' al-Raqqi, quoting Ibn Abi Hatim on his forebear Hilal ibn Amr, and the poet Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Raqqi, known as al-Mu'awwaj, whose death he places in 307. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.60}
