## Overview
Fars is a large region named after its people. The Muslims conquered it from the sea side in the caliphate of Umar, beginning with the island of Abr Kawan, then Tawwaj and Rishahr, where its marzban Shahrak was killed, then Arjan, Shiraz, Sabur, Darabjird and others. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Fars as a wide province whose borders are Arrajan toward Iraq, Sirjan toward Kirman, Siraf on the coast of the Indian Ocean and Makran toward Sind. Its seat is Shiraz. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.226}

He gives the genealogists' accounts of its name. Some link it to Fars son of Alim son of Sam son of Noah; others to Fars son of Tahmurath, an early just king who had ten sons, each given a town named after him. Abu Ali holds that the word is Persian, arabized from Bars. Yaqut lists its five famous districts: Istakhr, Ardashir Khurra, Darabjird, Sabur and Qubad Khurra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.226}

He describes the opening of the conquest from the sea in the time of Umar, when Arfaja ibn Harthama crossed without leave to an island off Fars and Umar rebuked him. The conquest of the whole province was completed under Uthman. The marzban Sahrak had been killed at Rishahr after his army was defeated, and Fars was weakened after him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.227}
