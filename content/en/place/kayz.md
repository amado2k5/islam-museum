## Summary
Kayz is the largest city of Makran according to al-Idrisi, comparable in size to Multan, with many palms, continuous farmland, favourable prices and much trade. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.172}

## Details
Al-Idrisi counts Kayz among the cities of the land of Makran, alongside Dizak, Rasak, Bah, Band, Qasr Qand, Asqafa, Falfahra, Mashki, al-Tiz and al-Balin; these lands are contiguous, broad and wide, though wastelands, drought and hardship prevail over them {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.172}. He affirms that the largest of its cities is Kayz, which approaches Multan in extent, and in it are many palms, adjoining farms, agreeable prices and abundant commerce {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.172}. Describing the people of the region, he notes that the tongue of the Makranis is Persian and Makrani, both of which they speak; the common dress is the qaratiq, while their merchants and notables wear long-sleeved gowns and cloaks and wind kerchiefs and cloths bordered with gold, like the dress of the merchants of Iraq and Fars {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.174}.
