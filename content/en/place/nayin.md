## Overview
Nayin is a town in the district of Istakhr, which al-Idrisi lists with Kath, Maybudh, Fahraj and Rudhan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 427} He calls it a fine town with a wall of mud, with markets, shops and money in circulation. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 431} From Uqda to Nayin he counts seventy-five miles, and from Nayin to Isfahan seventy-eight. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 431}

Al-Idrisi says three roads, from Yazd, from Isfahan and from Nayin, all meet at Kiryan (Karin). Kiryan is a large village with no wall and about two thousand men. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 449}

On the road from Nayin toward Karin, Dar Mard lies one stage away; it is a water point with no settlement. Hawmala, one stage further, is a basin where rainwater collects. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 450}

These distances are given by al-Idrisi in stages and miles, as he recorded them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 431}

He adds that this district was said to have belonged to Kirman before it was entered in the registers of Fars. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 427}
