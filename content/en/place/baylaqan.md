## Overview
Baylaqan in Aran: a city built by Qubadh ibn Firuz. Salman ibn Rabi'a al-Bahili conquered it by treaty, on terms of safe-conduct for lives, property and walls, on condition of poll-tax and kharaj. Abd al-Aziz ibn Hatim renewed its building, and a truce and two Imarins were witnessed there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Baylaqan", with a fatha on the ba, a sukun on the ya, a fatha on the qaf, then alif and nun, and says it is a city near the Derbend called Bab al-Abwab, counted in Greater Armenia and close to Shirvan, though some count it among the districts of Aran. He gives two accounts of its origin: that King Qubadh was the first to found it when he ruled Armenia, or that its first founder was Baylaqan ibn Armani ibn Lanta ibn Yunan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}

He quotes Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir, that is al-Baladhuri, that Salman ibn Rabi'a marched in the days of Uthman ibn Affan, the date not recorded, to Aran and took al-Baylaqan by treaty on terms securing their blood, their property and the walls of their city, on condition of paying the poll tax and the kharaj, and then went on to Barda'a. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}

He reports that the Tatars came in the year 617 AH, killed everyone they found there, plundered and burned it; those who had fled came back and others joined them, and in his own time it stood firm. He names a native, Abu al-Ma'ali Abd al-Malik ibn Ahmad al-Baylaqani, who travelled for hadith to Khurasan and Iraq and died in Baylaqan after the year 496 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}

## The museum's identification
The record and Yaqut agree on the conquest: the text Yaqut takes from al-Baladhuri is the same account of Salman ibn Rabi'a that the record gives, on terms securing blood, property and walls and with jizya and kharaj. The record places the town in Aran, which is one of the two views Yaqut reports, since he himself puts it in Greater Armenia near the Derbend and Shirvan and notes that some counted it with Aran. Abd al-Aziz ibn Hatim's rebuilding and the truce and the two Imarins that the record mentions are not in Yaqut's entry. The record's coordinates fix a modern site; the museum reads them as this town, and Yaqut places it no more closely than near the Derbend.
