## Overview
Khiwan is a strong fortress with two water basins. It has vineyards whose grapes are large and sweet, from which raisins are made and carried to the neighbouring lands. It is on the road to [[place/saada|Saada]], according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Khiwan as a district of Yemen and a town within it. He quotes Abu Ali al-Farisi that the name follows the pattern fi'lan and is a nisba to a Yemeni tribe. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.415}

He quotes Ibn al-Kalbi that the idol Yaghuth was kept in a village called Khiwan, two nights from Sanaa on the side toward Mecca, and that the Hamdan tribe and those around them in Yemen worshipped it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.415} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.438}
