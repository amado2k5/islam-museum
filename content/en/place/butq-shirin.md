## Summary
Butq Shirin, a site at Basra named for Shirin, wife of Kisra ibn Hurmuz. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 511}

## Details
In his list of Basra's estates and canals, al-Baladhuri records that Butq Shirin was named for Shirin, wife of Kisra ibn Hurmuz. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 511} A butq in their usage is a breach in a riverbank, so the place was a gap on the shore of one of Basra's waterways that bore that Persian woman's name. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 410} Al-Qahdhami adds that the Nahr Adi had been a creek off Basra's river until Adi ibn Arta'a al-Fazari, Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz's governor, cut it open at Butq Shirin — so the site recurs in another report as a station on a bank connected with Umayyad irrigation works. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 514} In al-Baladhuri, then, Butq Shirin is one of the sites of the Basra sawad whose name was bound up with Shirin and with the granting and digging of land, like the rest of that section.
