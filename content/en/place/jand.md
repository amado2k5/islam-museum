## Overview
Jand is a medieval city on the Syr Darya in the Turkic lands. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan as a great city whose people were Muslims of the Hanafi school, and names Ya'qub ibn Shirin among its men of learning. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.168}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut says Jand is the name of a great city in the land of Turkestan, ten days from Khwarazm toward the Turkic lands beyond the river, near the river Sayhun, and that its people are Muslims who follow the school of Abu Hanifa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.168}

He adds that in his time it was in the hands of the Tatars and that he did not know its condition, so he records that news of it had been cut off. He names the judge, littérateur, poet and grammarian Ya'qub ibn Shirin al-Jandi, one of the foremost to have studied under Abu al-Qasim al-Zamakhshari, who settled in Khwarazm. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.168}

To avoid confusion, Yaqut gives a separate entry for al-Jand in Yemen, named for Jand ibn Shahran, with a mosque attributed to Mu'adh ibn Jabal; that is a different place from this one. It is on the next page of volume 2. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.169}
