## Overview
Jannaba is a large, populous and flourishing city with markets, according to al-Idrisi. It has fine linen garments of many kinds, and various manufactured goods. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.412}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Jannaba: a small town on the coast of Fars; the astrologers place it in the third clime, longitude 77 degrees from the west and latitude 30 degrees from the south. He says he saw it more than once: it does not stand on the open sea — boats reach it through a salt-water inlet some three miles or less between the city and the sea; opposite it in mid-sea is the island of Khark, to its north toward Basra lies Mahruban and to its south Siniz; it is a small roadstead where ships bound for Fars anchor. Some siyar people said it was named for Jannaba ibn Tahmurath the king, and its people drink from brackish wells. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.165} He corrects al-Hazimi, who made it a district of al-Bahrayn between Mahruban and Siraf: Mahruban and Siraf are on the coast of Fars like Jannaba, while al-Bahrayn is on the Arab coast opposite Fars; between Jannaba and Siraf are fifty-four farsakhs. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.166} He then records that from it came Abu Sa'id al-Hasan al-Jannabi al-Qarmati, who proclaimed the Qarmati doctrine: he had been a pedlar, was banished from Jannaba, settled in al-Bahrayn as a merchant and won its people to his creed until they answered him there and in what adjoins it; then his son Sulayman ibn al-Hasan rose, of whom the book he quotes records the killing of pilgrims of God's House, the cutting of the Mecca road in his days, the carrying of the Black Stone to al-Qatif and al-Ahsa' in the land of al-Bahrayn where it stayed twenty-one years and was returned for a payment, and that his uncle and relatives who were held at Shiraz were his opponents in the way, seeking what is right, and were cleared of the Qarmatis and released. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.166}

## The museum's identification
The record's Jannaba is the large populous coastal city of Fars in al-Idrisi, and it is Yaqut's small roadstead which he saw himself; his description of its salt-inlet approach and the island of Khark opposite matches its site, and his fifty-four farsakhs to Siraf is in the usual farsakh range against the museum's points for Jannaba and Siraf (about 277.4 km). Abu Sa'id al-Jannabi's notice is ascribed to the town he came from, and the account of him and his son is quoted, as Yaqut states, from "the disputed book" between al-Balkhi and al-Istakhri.
