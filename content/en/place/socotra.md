## Overview
Socotra is an island wide in extent and of noble rank, with a splendid land and flourishing trees; most of its plants are aloe, and no aloe surpasses its aloe in quality, such as that made in Hadramawt of Yemen, al-Shihr and elsewhere. It adjoins the lands of Yemen on the north and west, indeed counted as part of it and attributed to it, and across from it on the Zanj side are the cities of Malindi and Mombasa. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 50} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 51}

## The people
Most of the people of the town of Socotra are Christians. Al-Idrisi gives the reason: when Alexander overcame the king of Persia and his fleets raided the islands of India, Aristotle had charged him to seek the island of the aloe; on reaching Socotra he liked the goodness of its soil and the mildness of its air, and wrote to his teacher, who told him to move its people out, replace them with Greeks and charge them to guard the aloe tree. Their descendants stayed and entered the Christian faith when it appeared, and their remnants are there to his time together with the rest who live there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 51}

## The aloe
The leaves of the aloe are gathered in July, their juice extracted and boiled in copper pots and others, put in skins and dried in the sun of August; hundredweights of it are sold on the island and shipped to all God's lands in East and West, and its aloe is named for it and known by it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 51} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 52}

## Al-Hamdani's account
Al-Hamdani, who came earlier, counted it at about eighty farsakhs, with about ten thousand fighting men of the Mahra tribes, who are Christians. Its people relate that a group of people from the land of the Romans were cast there in the time of Kisra, and the Mahra lived alongside them, and some of them became Christians. It has many palms, ambergris, dam al-akhawayn (dragon's blood) and much aloe. The people of Aden say that the Romans did not enter it, but that monks settled there and then died out. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p. 36}
