## Overview
Wahran is a coastal town near the salt sea, facing al-Mariyya on the shore of al-Andalus. It has a small harbour at its gate and a large one two miles away, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.252}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says the road from Tlemcen to the coastal town of Wahran is two large stages, though some say three, passing the wadi of Waru and the village of Tanit. Wahran lies near the bank of the salt sea. It has a wall of well-built earth, fixed markets, many crafts and brisk trade, and it faces al-Mariyya across the sea. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.252}

The small harbour at its gate shelters nothing, but two miles away is the large harbour, where big ships and voyaging vessels anchor. It is sheltered from the wind and has no equal among the sea-walled harbours of the Berber lands. Its people drink from a wadi that runs into the town from the land, with orchards and gardens along it. Honey, butter, cheese, cattle and sheep are cheap. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.252}

Ships of al-Andalus call there often, and its people are landowners of substance who take pride in their dignity. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.252}
