## Summary
Bulghar was a city containing Christians and Muslims, with a congregational mosque for the Muslims; the city of Suwar lies near it, and its buildings are of wood, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of Slaw to Kuyaba, in the land of Bulghar, is eight stages, Kuyaba being the city of the Turks called Rus {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.917}. He describes Bulghar as numerous peoples and a great population whose settled area extends nearly to the settled lands of the Romans {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}. Bulghar is the name of the city: among its people are Christians and Muslims, it has a congregational mosque for the Muslims, the city of Suwar lies near it, and its buildings are of wood — its people shelter in them in winter and in summer resort to tents {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}. He relates from al-Hawqali that the day among the Rus and Bulgharians shortens in winter to three and a half hours {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.918}. Bulghar thus combined in his picture mixed faiths, a wide settled area and wooden houses in the far north.
