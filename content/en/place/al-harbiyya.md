## Overview
al-Harbiyya: a district of [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]]. al-Baladhuri reports that it was named after Harb ibn Abd Allah al-Balkhi, who was in charge of the police of Jafar ibn al-Mansur at Mosul. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.414-417}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut describes al-Harbiyya as a large, well-known quarter of Baghdad near Bab Harb, close to the graves of Bishr al-Hafi and Ahmad ibn Hanbal and others. It is named after Harb ibn Abd Allah al-Balkhi, one of al-Mansur's commanders, who held the police of Baghdad. Turkish raiders from Derbent killed Harb at Tiflis in the year 147 AH, and the neighbouring quarters were destroyed. The Harbiyya alone survived; its people walled it, and it had markets and a mosque where the Friday prayer was held. Scholars who took their nisba from it include Ibrahim ibn Ishaq al-Harbi, born in 198 and died in 285 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.237}
