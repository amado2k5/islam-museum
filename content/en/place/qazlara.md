## Overview
Al-Idrisi describes Qazlara as a large, populous city with busy markets and trade, and with gathered produce and abundant grain. Its people are stout champions who defend their side and guard their land, and their neighbours fear them because their word is united and their resolve firm. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}

Qazlara lies in the region of Shasuniya, a land of close settlement and many villages. It is bordered to the west by the land of Ifriqiya and to the east by the land of Izwada, and to the south it is bounded by Shasuniya itself. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}

From Qazlara, Hala lies fifty miles east, and Herbord, in the land of the Lombards, forty miles west. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901} Mashla is seventy miles away, and Nayun Burk, a large and well-known capital, eighty miles. Waniya is one hundred and twenty-five miles distant. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.902}
