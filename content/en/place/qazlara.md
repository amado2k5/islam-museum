## Overview
Qazlara is a large, populous city with much coming and going of goods. Al-Idrisi places it in the region of Shasuniya, a land of close settlement with many villages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}

## Distances
From Nayun Burk, to the south, it is a hundred miles to Qazlara, four stages. From Qazlara to Mashla, to the east, is a hundred miles, and from Qazlara to Hala, fifty miles east. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.890} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}

Al-Idrisi gives the distance from Qazlara west to the town of Herbord, in the land of the Lamaniya, twice: once as sixty miles and once as forty miles. The text differs on this point, and the two figures should be read together. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.890} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}

## Its people and produce
Qazlara is a large, flourishing city with much coming and going. Its people are stout defenders who protect their land, and their neighbours fear them because of the unity of their word and their firm resolve. The town has plentiful goods, grain and trade. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}

## The region
Qazlara is one of the towns of Shasuniya, a densely built region of many villages whose land is populous and whose produce is abundant. Its other towns are Mashla, Hala, Nayun Burk and Wazira, and the region lies on the Sea of Darkness. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.901}
