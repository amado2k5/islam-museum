## Summary
Al-Turbal was a structure in the middle of the city of Jur in the land of Fars which the Persians venerated; the Muslims destroyed it, according to al-Mas'udi. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 551}

## Details
Al-Mas'udi says in Muruj al-Dhahab that in the middle of the city of Jur — the town from which Jur rose-water is carried and to which it is ascribed — there was a building the Persians held in veneration called al-Turbal, which the Muslims demolished. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 551} He also records a fire temple at Jur built by Ardashir ibn Babak which he himself had seen; it stood above a remarkable spring, had a festival of its own, and was one of the pleasure-spots of Fars. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 551} Between Jur and the town of Kuwar are ten farsakhs, and the rose water produced at Jur and Kuwar is the finest made anywhere in the world, thanks to the soil's health and the air's purity. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 551} From Kur to Shiraz, the capital of Fars, is ten farsakhs, and Jur, Kuwar and Shiraz and the other districts of Fars have chronicles and monuments the Persians recorded, as he notes. {cite:muruj-al-dhahab p. 551}
