## Overview
Qartajanna, Carthage, is an old city near Tunis on the coast of Ifriqiya. It was among the wonders of the land in its building, and in al-Idrisi's time it lay in ruin with no one living in it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 286}

## Ancient structures
Remains of a famous building of the Romans survive there, such as the structures called al-Tayatir. These are rounded buildings without equal in the buildings of the earth for their scale and capacity. Their stone carries figures of people, craftsmen, animals and ships, all wrought with the finest craft, and the upper part of the building is smooth. It is said that the building was a playing-field and a place of assembly at a certain season and on a certain day of the year. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 286} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 287}

## The cisterns
Among its marvels of building are the cisterns (dawamis), which number twenty-four in a single row. Each has vaults at its top, and between each cistern and the next are shafts and channels that carry water from one to another, all of stone. Water once flowed to them from the spring of Shuqar near al-Qayrawan, a distance of three stages, and it was cut off after the city was ruined. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 287}

## Ruin and surroundings
Al-Idrisi says that only a raised patch called al-Mu'allaqa is still settled, walled with earth, and inhabited by Arab chiefs known as the Banu Ziyad. Digging in its ruins never stops, and the removal of marble from it never ends. The city is surrounded by raised land, plains, farms and abundant produce. From the mouth of the lake to the city is three and a half miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 286} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 288}
