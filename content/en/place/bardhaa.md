## Overview
Bardhaa: a city in Aran, which al-Baladhuri describes as the frontier city of the whole region. Qubadh ibn Firuz built it when his commander followed, who had conquered the land between the river al-Ras and Shirwan. It is one of [[place/arran|Aran]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.273}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
For [[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]], Bardha'a is a town at the far end of Azerbaijan; he quotes Hamza al-Isfahani that the name is an Arabized "barda dar", Persian for "place of captives", because a Persian king settled captives taken from beyond Armenia there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.523} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.524} He cites Ibn al-Faqih that it is the city of Arran and the last limit of Azerbaijan, first built up by King Qubadh, on level ground and built of brick and plaster. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.524}

He quotes al-Istakhri's description: a very large city, more than a parasang square, pleasant and fertile; between Iraq and Khurasan, after Rayy and Isfahan, no city was larger or more fertile; its gardens interlace, and it yields fine hazelnuts, chestnuts, figs and silk; three parasangs away is the river Kur, from which the salted fish *shurmahi* is carried far and wide. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.524} At its "Gate of the Kurds" a market called al-Kurraki was held on Sundays, drawing people from everywhere, even Iraq, so that the name replaced the day's and some counted the week as "Friday, Saturday, al-Kurraki, Monday". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.524}

Yaqut then says this is an old description: he met a man of Bardha'a who told him that the traces of ruin there were many, with only a few people left, as in a village, and houses fallen down. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.525} He records that [[person/salman-ibn-rabi-a|Salman ibn Rabi'a al-Bahili]] marched on it in the days of Uthman ibn Affan after taking Baylaqan; its people shut the gates, he raided its villages, and they made peace on the terms of Baylaqan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.525} Yazid ibn Mazyad al-Shaybani died there, and Yaqut quotes lines from Muslim ibn al-Walid's elegy on him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.525}
