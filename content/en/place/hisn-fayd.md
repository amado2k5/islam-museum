## Overview
Fayd, with an open fa, is a known place on the southeast slope of the south of Salma. Palm gardens stretch there for two or three miles, grain is grown in it, and it counts as part of Najd. Its name appears in events and in Arab poetry, according to the editor's note in the Akwa al-Hawali edition. {cite:hamdani-akwa-hawali p.93}

The text calls it "Fayd" and not "the Fortress of Fayd"; an additional source is needed to establish its relation to the fortress.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives "Fayd", with a fatha, a sukun and an undotted dal, and offers several derivations. From Ibn al-A'rabi, fayd is death and also the few hairs above a horse's lip; from al-Mu'arrij, that his kunya Abu Fayd comes from a halt on the Mecca road; some say it is the saffron flower, and al-Zajjaji derives it from "to gain a benefit" and says it was named for Fayd ibn Ham, the first to settle there. He then calls Fayd a small town halfway along the Mecca road from Kufa, still inhabited in his day, where pilgrims leave their provisions and heavy baggage with its people and take them back on return, giving something to those who kept them; its people are a help to the pilgrim in that cut-off place and live by storing fodder all year until the pilgrims come and buying it from them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282} From al-Sukkuni he reports that it is the middle of the pilgrim road from Kufa to Mecca, and in three parts, a third for the Umariyyun, a third for the family of Abu Salama of Hamdan and a third for Banu Nabhan of Tayyi'; that it is six nights from Wadi al-Qura by al-'Arima; and that there is no road to Syria below it because of the sands in those parts. He says that Zuhayr's "Fayd al-Qurayyat" is another place, adding "and God knows". From al-Hazimi he gives it as near Aja' and Salma, the two mountains of Tayyi', in words that the printed text renders obscurely, and names from it Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Duris al-Faydi, Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn Abi Mawatiya al-Faydi and Abu Ishaq Isa ibn Ibrahim al-Faydi al-Kufi, who settled there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282-283} Under "Adhana" he notes a marker (khayal) of the protected pasture (hima) of Fayd about twenty miles from it, the markers being signs set on the bounds of a hima. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.52}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut calls the place "Fayd" and a small town, not a fortress, so the record's slug "Hisn Fayd" does not come from him; the record itself notes that al-Hamdani's text names it "Fayd" alone. Al-Hamdani's place, as the record has it, is on a slope south of the south-eastern side of Salma in Najd, which agrees with al-Hazimi's statement in Yaqut that it is near Aja' and Salma. The museum measured that the record's point (27.1189, 42.5206) is about 579 km from its Kufa point and about 690 km from its Mecca point, about 46 per cent of the straight-line distance of about 1,267 km between Kufa and Mecca, which agrees with Yaqut's "the middle of the road"; Yaqut gives no coordinates. Zuhayr's "Fayd al-Qurayyat" remains, by Yaqut's account, another place and the museum has not identified it. The palm and grain farms in the record, from al-Hamdani, differ from what Yaqut says of its people's living, but that is not a contradiction: each describes one side of it.
