## Summary
Bayas is a town of the Syrian coastlands which al-Idrisi describes as resembling the Ghawr country, with abundant fine fruits and plentiful bounty. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.647}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Iskandaruna to Bayas is a light stage and from Bayas to al-Masisa one stage, making forty miles from Iskandaruna to al-Masisa {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.646}. He describes Bayas as a place resembling the Ghawr country, with many goodly fruits and abundant bounty {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.647}. Elsewhere he lists it among the posts of the coastal frontier after al-Suwaydiya and Iskandaruna and before al-Tinat, al-Mithqab and the river of al-Masisa, noting that from Bayas inland to al-Haruniya is fifteen miles and from al-Haruniya to Mar'ash of the frontier of al-Jazira is one stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.652}. Bayas is thus one of the coastal stages of Syria toward the frontier of al-Jazira.
