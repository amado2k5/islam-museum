## Overview
Yaqut describes Tarabulus as a city on the sea shore, with a wall of heavy stone, a fine congregational mosque, markets, and a harbour sheltered from most winds. Around it lie orchards and a salt marsh from which much salt is raised. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.25}

## Yaqut's entry
Yaqut cites Ibn Bashir al-Bakri that Tarabulus, in Roman and Greek, names three cities, and that the Greeks called it Tarabulitsa. He says it is also called the city of Ilyas, and that it is said Ashbarus Caesar first built it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.25}

The entry describes many markets and a mosque known as Masjid al-Shi'ab, with many ribats around it where pious men lived; the most famous of these was that mosque. It gives the distances: three days east and west to a place called Bani al-Sabri, and two days south to the frontier of Hawwara. Inside the city is a well called Bir Abi al-Kunud, and the sweetest of its wells is Bir al-Qubba. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.25}

The places named fit the Libyan city. The link to the modern city is the record's own identification; the text names Hawwara and the Berbers but not Libya.
