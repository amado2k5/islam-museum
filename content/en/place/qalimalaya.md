## Summary
Qalimalaya is a large, populous city, rich in crops and game, on a high hill full of game. Al-Idrisi says that the lord of Constantinople hunts there. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.899}

## Details
Qalimalaya is a large, populous city, rich in crops and game, and the lord of Constantinople hunts there. It stands on a high hill full of game. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.899} From Bulya Basisqus, a day's journey to the east, one reaches it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}

From Qalimalaya to the city of Madinus is twelve miles, and Madinus is a populous and fine city to the east. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.899} From Qalimalaya to Bizui is fifty miles to the south. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.899}

From Bizui to Constantinople is twenty-eight miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.899} Idrisi thus places Qalimalaya within a chain of nearby places around Constantinople, joined by short distances in miles.

He also gives a route from Madinus to Milisiya to the east, though the number in the surviving text is not clear, and from Milisiya to the sea, where the city of Aymin stands on the coast of the Black Sea, six miles to the south. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.899} This description links the city to the Black Sea through Aymin and Milisiya.

From Istinus to Bulya Basisqus, to the south, is three days. Bulya lies near a mountain from which a river flows through the city. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898} This spatial account surrounds Qalimalaya from several directions.
