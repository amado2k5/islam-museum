## Overview
This page treats the name Iran as the medieval Muslim geographers knew it: a name for a group of Persianate Islamic lands, not the borders of the modern state. Yaqut al-Hamawi gives the entry "Iran Shahr" a long discussion of the name's meaning and extent in the Mu'jam al-Buldan, followed by a short entry "Iran". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.289}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut reports from Abu al-Rayhan al-Khwarazmi that Iran Shahr is one name covering Iraq, Fars, the Jibal and Khurasan. The Persians, he adds, say that Iran is the name of Arfakhshad son of Sam son of Noah, and that shahr means "land" in their tongue, so the whole means the land of Arfakhshad. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.289}

He also quotes Yazid ibn Umar al-Farisi, who said they compared the Sawad of Iraq to the heart and the rest of the world to the body, calling it Dil Iran Shahr, "the heart of Iran Shahr", and called Iran Shahr the middle region of the whole world. From al-Asma'i, as relayed by Hamza, he quotes that Iraq was called Dil Iran Shahr, the heart of the lands of the Persian kingdom, and that the Arabs arabised the middle word of the phrase and said "al-Iraq". Yaqut gives these as reports from others. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.289}

Yaqut then sets out two Persian accounts of how the world was divided, presenting them as what the Persians claim. In the first, King Tahmuras, whom they hold to stand where Adam stands for others, assigned the world to the great men of his realm and gave the ten sons of Iran son of al-Aswad son of Sam son of Noah the lands of Khurasan, Sijistan, Kirman, Makran, Isfahan, Gilan, Sandan, Jurjan, Azerbaijan and Armenan. In the second, King Afridun divided the earth among his three sons: Salm received the west, and the kings of Rum descend from him; Iran, that is Iraj, received Babil and the Sawad, which was named Iran Shahr, "land of Iran", meaning Iraq, the Jibal, Khurasan and Fars, and the Persian emperors descend from him; Tuj received the east, and the kings of the Turks and China descend from him. Yaqut quotes verses that describe this partition. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.289}

Yaqut adds that in al-Baladhuri's book Iran Shahr is Nishapur, Quhistan, the two Tabasayn, Herat, Bushanj, Badghis and Tus, whose name is Taberan, so on that account it is a part of Khurasan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.289} This differs from the wider limit given from Abu al-Rayhan, and Yaqut does not choose between them in this entry. See also [[place/khorasan|Khurasan]], [[place/fars|Fars]], [[place/iraq|Iraq]], [[place/nishapur|Nishapur]], [[place/herat|Herat]] and [[place/tus|Tus]].

The separate entry "Iran" says only that it is half of the name before it, that it appears in some verse in that form, and that both mean the same. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.289} Fars is a province of its own in Yaqut, with its own entry {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.226}, and is not to be confused with Iran Shahr.
