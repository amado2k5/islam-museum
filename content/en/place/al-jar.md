## Overview
al-Jar: the port of the Hijaz. From the year 21 AH the sea-borne loads of food from the kharaj of Egypt used to arrive there. Sa'd al-Jar took charge of collecting them, and they were carried to Medina, where they were divided among the people in a house by measure. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The loads continued into the caliphate of Abu Ja'far and after it, with interruptions during the first strife and the time of the Umayyads. See [[event/umar-food-transport-egypt|the transport of food from Egypt in the time of Umar]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives al-Jar with a light ra and glosses it as the one you shelter so that he is not wronged. He describes a city on the shore of the Qulzum (Red) Sea, a day and a night from Medina, about ten stages from Ayla and about three stages from the shore at al-Juhfa, in the second climate. It is a harbour where ships put in from Abyssinia, Egypt, Aden, China and the rest of India; it has a minbar and is populous, its people drink from the lake, which is the spring of Yalil, and it has many palaces, half of the city being on an island and half on the shore. Opposite lies an island a mile by a mile, reached only by boat, the anchorage of the Abyssinians in particular, called Qaraf, whose inhabitants are merchants who have their water brought from two farsakhs away; he gives all this on the authority of Abu al-Ash'ath al-Kindi from Arram ibn al-Asbagh al-Sulami. He adds that the whole sea from Jidda to near the city of al-Qulzum was called al-Jar, and that Sa'd al-Jari is Sa'd ibn Nawfal, a freedman of Umar ibn al-Khattab, whom Umar appointed over al-Jar. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.92} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.93}
