## Summary
Jabal al-Rumat (Archers' Hill) is a hill southeast of the Muslim camp at Uhud, where the Prophet stationed fifty archers under Abd Allah ibn Jubayr al-Ansari to prevent the Meccan cavalry from outflanking the army. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 255}

## Details
Before the battle of Uhud, the Prophet posted a company of skilled archers, fifty men strong, on a hill on the northern bank of Wadi Qanat — later known as Jabal al-Rumat — southeast of the Muslim camp, some hundred and fifty metres from the army's position, under the command of Abd Allah ibn Jubayr ibn al-Nu'man al-Ansari. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p. 255}

Their task was to guard the flank against the Quraysh cavalry's turning movement: the Prophet ordered them to keep the horses off with arrows so that the idolaters could not come upon the Muslims from behind, whether the day went for them or against them. They did their work at first, repelling the cavalry with arrows, but when the battle seemed won most of them left the hill, and Khalid ibn al-Walid's horsemen swept over it and fell on the Muslims from behind. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}
