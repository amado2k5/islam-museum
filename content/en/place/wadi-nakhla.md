## Overview
Wadi Nakhla is the valley between Mecca and al-Ta'if where Samura, the tree of al-Uzza, the greatest idol of Quraysh, stood. It is also the place where the Prophet met the jinn of Nakhla.

## The place
Quraysh revered al-Uzza, a tree (samura) in Wadi Nakhla. Khalid ibn al-Walid destroyed it after the conquest of Mecca, on the Prophet's orders {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

In this valley the jinn heard the Qur'an from the Prophet on the night of Nakhla, and the context of Surat al-Jinn was revealed there {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
Wadi Nakhla lies between Mecca and al-Ta'if. The tree of al-Uzza stood there, and there the jinn heard the Qur'an from the Prophet at night.

## In the introduction to The Sealed Nectar
The Arabs took [[object/al-uzza|al-Uzza]] at Wadi Nakhla after Manat and al-Lat. These three were their greatest idols {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35} {cite:mukhtasar-sirat-al-rasul p.12}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Under the letter nun with kha Yaqut records two Nakhla wadis of Hudhayl two nights from Mecca that join at Batn Marr and Sabuha: Nakhla al-Shamiyya, and Nakhla al-Yamaniyya, a wadi into which Yad'an flows, holding a mosque of the Prophet and the place where Hawazin pitched camp on the day of Hunayn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.277} From Abu Ziyad al-Kilabi he says Nakhla is a wadi of the Hijaz two nights' journey from Mecca where the Yemen and Najd pilgrim caravans join, its upper part al-Waba'a being the Yamaniyya and the other the Shamiyya, which is Dhat 'Irq; upper Nakhla belongs to Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr and its lower part is the orchard of Ibn 'Amir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.278} The book also has Nakhla Mahmud, a place in the Hijaz near Mecca, and the day of Nakhla, one of the Fijar days. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.277} Under the entry al-'Uzza he says from Ibn Habib that it was a tree at Nakhla, and from Abu al-Mundhir that it stood in a wadi of Nakhla al-Shamiyya called Harad opposite al-Ghumayr on the right of one ascending from Mecca toward Iraq, nine miles above Dhat 'Irq toward al-Bustan, that a house was built over it in which a voice was heard, and that it was the greatest of the idols to Quraysh; the entry opens by quoting the Qur'anic verse that names al-'Uzza, which the museum does not reproduce. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.116} The entry Dar Nakhla is the site of Medina's market, not this wadi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.423}

## The museum's identification
The record makes Wadi Nakhla the wadi between Mecca and al-Ta'if that held the samura tree of al-'Uzza and the place of the Prophet's meeting with the jinn of Nakhla. Yaqut's entries attest the worship of al-'Uzza in Nakhla al-Shamiyya, in the wadi called Harad, on the two reports of Ibn Habib and Abu al-Mundhir (vol 4 p.116), and set out the two Hudhayl wadis that join at Batn Marr and Sabuha (vol 5 p.277-278); the entries read do not mention the night of the jinn, which stays with the record's text. The record's point (21.45, 40.05) is 23.4 km from the museum's Mecca point and 42.8 km from its al-Ta'if point. See [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/taif|al-Ta'if]].
