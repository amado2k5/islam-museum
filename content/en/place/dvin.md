## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Dabil is a city of Armenia bordering Arran, a frontier town that Habib ibn Maslama took in the days of Uthman ibn Affan, and Yaqut gives the text of the safe conduct he granted its people. The entry also covers a sand ridge between al-Yamama and Yemen and a village of Ramla. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.438} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.439}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
The entry opens with the vowelling, a fatha on the dal and a kasra on the ba, then a language note: the dabil is the long dune facing the open desert, and a place bordering the outskirts of al-Yamama, said to be a sand tract between al-Yamama and Yemen, with verse that is described here, not quoted. Yaqut then says Dabil is also a city of Armenia bordering Arran, a frontier town taken by Habib ibn Maslama in the days of Uthman ibn Affan while Muawiya governed Syria; he overcame it and its villages and made terms with its people. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.438} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.439}

## The safe conduct
Yaqut gives a letter that Habib ibn Maslama al-Fihri wrote for the Christians, Magians and Jews of Dabil, present and absent, assuring them for their lives, property, churches and the wall of their city, with jizya and kharaj owed by them and fulfilment of the pact owed by the Muslims for as long as they kept faith. He cites verses that mention Dabil and verses of Abu Yaqub al-Khurayimi that recall the day of Dabil as a terrible affair; the verses are described here, not quoted. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.439}

## People of Dabil
Yaqut attributes to Dabil Abd al-Rahman ibn Yahya al-Dabili and Jidar ibn Bakr al-Dabili. The entry also lists a Dabil among the villages of Ramla, to which belongs the jurist Abu al-Qasim Shuayb ibn Muhammad al-Dabili, known as Ibn Abi Qatran. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.439}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Dvin a village in Ararat Province, Armenia. Yaqut does not say Dabil is Dvin; he places it in Armenia bordering Arran and calls it a frontier town taken by Habib ibn Maslama. The link between Dabil and Dvin is the museum's reading, based on the position of the old city in Armenia, and a source that treats the identification should confirm it.
