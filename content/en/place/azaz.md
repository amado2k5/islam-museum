## Overview
Azaz is a small fortified town north of Aleppo. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan with its healthy air, the local reports about it, and a note on a namesake elsewhere. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.118}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut fixes the pronunciation as ʿAzāz, with the first letter vowelled with a fatḥa and the zāy doubled, and says it was sometimes written with an alif at the start. He explains al-ʿazāz as hard ground. He describes the place as a small town with a fortress and a district (rustāq) lying north of Aleppo, a day's journey away, with good air and sweet water, and says no scorpion is found there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.118}

He adds, as a report rather than a certainty, that its soil, if taken and laid on a scorpion, kills it, and that it has none of the vermin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.118}

He also records that Abu al-Faraj al-Isfahani, in the Kitab al-Diyarat, places ʿAzaz at al-Raqqa, and cites two lines by Ishaq al-Mawsili in which the poet's heart is bound to Tall ʿAzaz by a gazelle-like beloved who lives in Syria and joins Iraqi refinement to Hijazi gentleness. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.118}

Yaqut gives the nisba al-ʿAzazi for the Aleppo town, naming Abu al-ʿAbbas Ahmad ibn ʿUmar al-ʿAzazi, who narrated from Abu al-Hasan ʿAli ibn Ahmad ibn al-Marzuban. He quotes Nasr that there is also a ʿAzaz in Yemen, so the reader should not confuse the two. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.118}
