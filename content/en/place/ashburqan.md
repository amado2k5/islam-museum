## Summary
Ashburqan was a flourishing town of the Juzjanan, with a little running water and the crops of its people upon it, lying fifty-four miles from al-Faryab, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.478}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from al-Faryab eastward to Ashburqan is fifty-four miles, that Ashburqan belongs to the towns of the Juzjanan, and that Juzjanan is the name of the district, not a city {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.478}. It is a prosperous town with a little running water; the crops of its people are upon it, its gardens are few and it has no fruit of its own, produce being brought to it from the neighbouring lands, and from Ashburqan to Balkh is fifty-four miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.478} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.479}. Among the towns of Juzjanan is Anbar, one stage from Ashburqan in a direction between west and south, and from Ashburqan to al-Yahudiyya is a road of two stages and a fraction {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.479}. In his picture it was thus one of the peopled links of Juzjanan, its site lying amid the roads to Faryab, Balkh, Anbar and Yahudiyya.
