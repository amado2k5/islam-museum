## Overview
al-Azhar Mosque: a mosque in [[place/cairo|Cairo]]. al-Suyuti reports that the Fatimids began building it in Rabi al-Akhir in the year 359 AH and finished it in Ramadan in the year 361 AH. {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p. 621}

An additional source is needed: any report of its history after that.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam dates the founding of this Cairo university to 969 or 970 and suggests that its name may honour Fatimah, the Prophet's daughter, known as al-Zahra, "the brilliant", the ancestor for whom the Fatimids, who founded Cairo, are named. Before the modern period, it says, al-Azhar had no formal admission procedure, no departments, no written tests, no marks and no diplomas; its curriculum took in jurisprudence, hadith, Qur'anic exegesis, grammar and rhetoric, together with the sciences. Early modern efforts to update the university by adding subjects and requiring annual examinations were cancelled under pressure from protesting students and faculty. From 1961 the usually conservative institution took on modern standards of higher education and widened its range with new colleges for medicine, engineering, education, agriculture, commerce and science. Women are admitted but taught in separate colleges. Outside Egypt, the Dictionary adds, it is held in high regard as a defender of Sunni Islam and of Arabic. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e265}
