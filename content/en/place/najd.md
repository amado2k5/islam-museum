## Summary
Najd is the elevated region of Arabia facing the Hijaz. In *Sifat Jazirat al-Arab*, al-Hamdani divides the land of the Arabs by a mountain chain running near the western coast from Ayla to Aden: the western slope is Tihama and the Hijaz, while the land rising eastward is the Najd of the Arabs and the Najd of Iraq and al-Samawa {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.87-88}.

## Al-Hamdani's boundary
Al-Hamdani makes this western mountain chain the dividing line: what lies below it toward the sea is Tihama, and what rises beyond it toward the eastern Arab lands and toward the Najd of Iraq and al-Samawa is Najd; the rest of the book details its mountains, waters and landmarks {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p.87-88}.

## For young visitors
Najd is the raised part of Arabia, opposite the low-lying Hijaz along the sea, as the geographer al-Hamdani describes it in his book on the land of the Arabs.
