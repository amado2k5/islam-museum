## Summary
Bust, a city of Sijistan, taken by Abd al-Rahman ibn Samura by compact together with Zabil. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 555}

## Details
Al-Baladhuri relates that Abd al-Rahman ibn Samura, on reaching the land of al-Dawar, besieged its people at the mountain of al-Zur and then made peace with them; he entered before al-Zur, an idol of gold whose eyes were two rubies, cut off its hand and took the rubies, telling the marzban: the gold and jewels are yours — I only wished to show you that it can neither harm nor help; and he took Bust and Zabil by compact. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 555} In another report he came to Khawash and Quzan Bust and took it by force, then marched on to Wazan, whose people fled. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 558} When Rutbil prevailed as far as Bust, al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad marched out, fought him at Bust and routed him; later Abdallah ibn Umayya camped at Bust and Rutbil settled with him for a million; and in the desert of Bust many men perished of thirst and hunger, Ubaydallah ibn Abi Bakra dying of grief. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 561}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Bust in two entries: Bust with a final ta, a wadi in the land of Irbil toward Azerbaijan in the mountains; and Bust with damma, a city between Sijistan, Ghaznin and Herat — "I suppose it belongs to the workings of Kabul, for the measure of what we find of its reports in the histories and conquest books requires it" — of the hot-tempered lands, a large place whose district today is called Karamsir, "the hot country"; rich in rivers and gardens but with ruin plain upon it. A scholar asked about it answered: "it is like its dual", meaning bustan, a garden. Of its people he names al-Khattabi Abu Sulayman Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Busti, author of Ma'alim al-sunan and Gharib al-hadith; the qadi Abu Muhammad Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Busti, who died in 307; the poet and clerk Abu l-Fath al-Busti, author of al-Tajnis, who died at Bukhara in 400; and he gives a long notice of Abu Hatim Muhammad ibn Hibban al-Busti, prolific in hadith and journeys, travelling between al-Shash and Alexandria and keeping to Ibn Khuzayma. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.414-416}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Bust is the record's city of Sijistan and Ghazna; he himself separates it from the Irbil wadi in the preceding headword, his "I suppose it of Kabul's workings" is his own stated guess, and the visible ruin describes his age, not a judgement on its past; the "like its dual" reply (bustan) is reported as given.
