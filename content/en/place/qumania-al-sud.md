## Summary
Qumāniya al-Sūd is the city to which the Qumanians are attributed. It takes its name from a river that runs into its land, dives under the spurs of a mountain and comes out into the sea, with black water like smoke, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}

## Details
Al-Idrisi places Qumāniya al-Sūd in a chain of towns on the way to the land of the Qumanians. From Kīra to Qumāniya al-Sūd is twenty-five miles, and between them lies a great mountain that is hard to cross and long in its length. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}

He explains the name by the river, which joins the land of the city, then sinks under some of the spurs of that mountain and afterwards comes out into the sea. Its water is black like smoke. He says the fact is well known and not denied. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}

From Qumāniya al-Sūd to a city called Mutlaqa, also named Qumāniya al-Bayḍ, is fifty miles; he calls Qumāniya al-Bayḍ a large, populous city. From there to Mātrīqā, which he also calls Maṭrakh, is one hundred miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.916}

The passage gives no account of Qumāniya al-Sūd's farming, population or markets. It rests on the explanation of its name and its place between Kīra and Qumāniya al-Bayḍ. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.915}
