## Overview
Tabala is a small town of the districts (makhalif) of Mecca, with running springs, abundant palms and farms. It lies in a depression between hills, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut opens with a caution: the Tabala named in Muslim's Sahih is, he says, a place in Yemen, and he thinks it is not the Tabala of al-Hajjaj, which he describes as a well-known town of Tihama on the Yemen road. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.9-10} He quotes al-Muhallabi that it lies in the second climate at latitude 29 degrees. The people of Tabala and Jurash entered Islam without a fight, and the Prophet left both in their hands on the terms on which they had accepted, laying a dinar on each adult of the People of the Book among them and a duty of hospitality towards Muslims; he dates its conquest to year 10. It was proverbial for fertility, and Yaqut names Labid for a verse that uses it as a comparison. The saying "easier than Tabala to al-Hajjaj" comes, on the word of Abu al-Yaqzan, from al-Hajjaj's first post: when he came near and was told that only a low hill hid the town from him, he thought the post beneath him and turned back without entering. Yaqut gives its distance as 52 farsakhs from Mecca, about eight days' travel, six days from al-Ta'if and one day from Bisha. As to the name, one report derives it from Tabala daughter of Maknaf of the Amalekites, and al-Kalbi from Tabala daughter of Madyan son of Abraham. Yaqut also names al-Qattal for verse that mentions its grazing and gives one scholar of Tabala, Abu Ayyub Sulayman ibn Dawud al-Tabali. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.9-10}

Other entries add to it. Under Dhu al-Khalasa he reports, on the word of Abu al-Mundhir, that this shrine was a white stone carved like a crown at Tabala between Mecca and Yemen, seven nights from Mecca, tended by the Banu Umama of Bahila and revered by Khath'am, Bajila and the Azd of al-Sarat, and that Jarir ibn Abd Allah destroyed it and burned it when the Prophet sent him; a report in the same passage says that today it is the threshold of the mosque of Tabala, while other writers he quotes place the shrine at al-Abla' or al-Abalat. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.383-384} Under Bisha he gives 24 miles between Bisha and Tabala. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.529} Under Fatq he says that the books of the campaigns have the Prophet send Qutba ibn Amir to Tabala in year 9 to raid Khath'am. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.235}

## The museum's identification
The record has only al-Idrisi's text: a small town of the districts of Mecca with running springs, many palms and gardens, in a hollow among low hills. Yaqut does not mention springs or palms, only its proverbial fertility, and mentions no hollow except that Labid's verse calls its low grounds (ahdam) fertile; the story of the hill that hid it from al-Hajjaj sits easily beside al-Idrisi's low hills, but that is the museum's observation, not Yaqut's. The Tabala of Muslim's Sahih in Yemen is a different place from this record, on Yaqut's own reading. The record's point (19.9972, 42.2319) is about 296 km from the museum's Mecca point, about 236 km from its al-Ta'if point and about 39 km from its Bisha point. Set against Yaqut's counts these come to about 5.7 km per farsakh, about 39 km a day on the six-day road to al-Ta'if, and about 1.6 km per mile for the 24 miles to Bisha. None of this proves the identification, since a straight line is shorter than a road, and the units of farsakh, day and mile are loose. Yaqut's latitude of 29 degrees, quoted from al-Muhallabi, does not fit the record's latitude of about 20 degrees; the museum leaves it as a figure in the source Yaqut cites and does not correct either.
