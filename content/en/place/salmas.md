## Overview
Salmas is a town in Armenia, set back from Lake Kabudhan. The lake is salt water with no animals and no fish in it, and many boats cross it, coming from Urmia, Maragha and the districts of [[place/tabriz|Tabriz]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 682}

## Setting
Salmas has fertile villages and continuous farmland on every side. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 682} The page gives the distance from the lake to Maragha as fifteen miles on its east side, and to Urmia as six miles on its west side. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 682} Al-Idrisi places Salmas in the sixth part of the fifth section, with most of the towns of Armenia and part of Azerbaijan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 820} In another passage he lists its towns together with Mund, Barda'a, Janza and Khoy as the populous lands of Armenia. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 655}

## Distances
From Marsan to Salmas is one stage, and Salmas is in Armenia. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 682} From Urmia to Salmas is forty-five miles, which is two stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 826} From Salmas to Khoy is twenty-seven miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 826} From Ardabil to Maragha is about one hundred and twenty miles, and from Maragha to Urmia on the lake road is seventy-two miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 826}

## Craft
Armenian tekk, a kind of cloth whose name the text does not explain, is made at Salmas and also worked at Marand, and it is among the goods carried to other regions. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 825} The tekk made at Mayafarqin is said to rival the tekk of Salmas, and perhaps to surpass it in quality. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 663} The word is left in its Arabic form because the text does not define it.
