## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan al-Dinawar is a city of the Jibal near Qirmisin, rich in fruit and crops, from which many scholars of literature and hadith came. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.545}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the vowelling of the name, a kasra on the dal and fathas on the nun and waw, and calls it a city of the district of al-Jibal near Qirmisin, a little over twenty farsakhs from Hamadan, and four stages from Shahrazur. He puts its size at about two thirds of Hamadan and says it has many fruits and crops, waters and a lookout, and that its people are of better disposition than the people of Hamadan, which is his own judgement and not a fact about the place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.545}

## Its people
Yaqut says many people of literature and hadith are attributed to al-Dinawar and names the hafiz Abu Muhammad Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Wahb al-Dinawari. He lists those he heard from, among them al-Faryabi in Jerusalem and the two Razis Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim, and some who transmitted from him, among them Abu Ali al-Husayn ibn Ali and Abu Bakr ibn al-Ja'abi. He then gives the verdicts on him: from al-Hakim, quoting Abu Ali the hafiz, that he was a man of hadith and a hafiz, and that it had reached Abu Ali that Abu Zur'a could not keep up with him in discussion; and from al-Sulami, who asked al-Daraqutni about him, the answer that he fabricated hadith. Yaqut sets out both without choosing. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.545} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.546}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record lists Dinavar as a district in Iran. That is the museum's reading, not Yaqut's text, which describes a city of al-Jibal near Qirmisin and not a modern administrative district.
