## Overview
Ascalon is an ancient city on the coast of Palestine between Gaza and Bayt Jibrin. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan, with the explanation of its name, its standing among the people of Syria, its capture by the Franks, its recovery by Saladin and his later razing of it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.122}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name with a fatha, a sukun on the second letter, then qaf, and gives its position in the third clime, with longitude 55 and latitude 33. He thinks the name is non-Arabic as far as he knows, and passes on a view that "asqalan" means the top of the head, so that if Arabic it would mean that the city is at the top of Syria. He describes it as a city of Syria in the district of Palestine on the sea coast between Gaza and Bayt Jibrin, called the bride of Syria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.122}

He says that a number of Companions and Followers settled there and many people transmitted reports there, and that it stayed prosperous until the Franks took it on 27 Jumada II 548 AH. They held it for thirty-five years until Saladin (Yusuf ibn Ayyub) recovered it in 583. The Franks then grew strong again and took Acre and marched on Ascalon, and Saladin, fearing that what had befallen Acre would befall it, razed it in Sha'ban 587. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.122}

On its merit he cites a report attributed to the Prophet that he gave glad tidings of the two brides, Gaza and Ascalon, and notes that Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan first conquered it in the caliphate of Umar ibn al-Khattab, and that Abdullah ibn Umar said that everything has a summit and the summit of Syria is Ascalon. He adds that the name also belongs to a village or quarter in Balkh, from which Isa ibn Ahmad al-Asqalani took his name, a different place not to be confused with the Syrian city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.122}
