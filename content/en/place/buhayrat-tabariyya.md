## Overview
Lake Tiberias: its waters come from the rivers of Baysan, al-Yarmuk and al-Hadd, and its water flows until it falls into Lake Zughar. al-Idrisi describes it as a place between two mountains. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.355}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Lake Tiberias its own entry among the lakes. He first reports from al-Azhari that it is about ten miles by six, and that the draining of its water is a sign of the coming of the Dajjal; he relays a report about the descent of Isa and the emergence of Yajuj and Majuj, who pass the lake and drink all that is in it, and he adds that such a report, impossible as it is to reason, has many parallels in people's books, and God knows best. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.351} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.352}

He then describes what he saw himself: he had seen it many times, and it is like a pond ringed by mountains, receiving the overflow of many rivers that come from the direction of Banias, the coast and the Greater Jordan. A great river leaves it, watering the land of the Lesser Jordan, which is the Ghawr, and flows into the Stinking Lake near Jericho, which is the lake of Zughar. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.351} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.352}

He adds that the city of Tiberias lies at the foot of the mountain overlooking the lake, that its water is sweet and drinkable though not truly sweet and rather heavy, that a rock juts from the middle of the lake which people claim is the tomb of Sulayman ibn Dawud, and that about fifty miles lie between the lake and Bayt al-Maqdis. He quotes verses of al-Mutanabbi that mention the lake in a description of a lion coming to drink. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.351} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.352}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Lake of Tiberias: he describes it ringed by mountains, receiving the overflow of many rivers and giving rise to a river that ends in the lake of Zughar, which agrees with al-Idrisi's account in the record of its outflow to the lake of Zughar. The rivers al-Idrisi names, those of Bisan, the Yarmuk and al-Hadd, are not named by Yaqut, who speaks of the direction of Banias, the coast and the Greater Jordan. That the lake is today the Sea of Galilee, as the record's coordinates have it, is the museum's identification and not Yaqut's wording.
