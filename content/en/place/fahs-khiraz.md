## Summary
Fahs Khiraz is a broad tract in the lands of the Maghrib adjoining the village of Makul, twelve miles long and without water. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the village of Makul stands on a wide plain and adjoining it is a tract called Fahs Khiraz, twelve miles long with no water in it; Makul itself is like a great fortress, inhabited by Berbers, with a thriving market for all the goods and merchandise brought to it, and it has many crops, herds and cattle {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}. He notes that the road runs across Fahs Khiraz, and that at its far end there is a valley with ever-flowing water and thickets where lions appear openly to the people, accustomed to them by night and day, not hiding in their coverts {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}. He also reports that ostriches in this tract are beyond counting, and that their eggs are carried from it to every land {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.238}.
