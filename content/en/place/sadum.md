## Summary
Sadum Rah is a large village with settled inhabitants and joined buildings, and many springs, vineyards and palms. Al-Idrisi places it on the road from Mecca to Yemen. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Sadum in his account of the road from Mecca to Yemen, naming the villages in order. After al-Najm, a settled village with a well, the traveller reaches Sadum Rah, which he calls a large village with inhabitants and continuous buildings. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

It has many springs, vineyards and tall palms. Al-Idrisi says Jirash lies eight miles away, and that Jirash and Najran are similar in size, with many palms, tanneries for hides, and inhabitants known for their trade and goods. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

From Sadum the road continues to al-Mahjara, a great village with springs and a deep, abundant well, and with a large tree called Talhat al-Malik. Al-Idrisi treats it as the boundary between the district of Mecca and the district of Yemen, and from there the road runs to Urqa and then Saada. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

The passage gives Sadum only as a stop on the road, so this entry adds nothing beyond its springs, vineyards and palms.
