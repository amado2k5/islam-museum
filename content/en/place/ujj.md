## Overview
Ujj is a city on the bank of Lake Buruan, from which its people drink. It has markets and crafts sufficient for it, and it lies a short distance from Buruan, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.514}

## Details
Al-Idrisi describes Lake Buruan as large, forty farsakhs long and seventy-two miles wide. Its water is sweet and it holds a great deal of fish, which the people of Buruan and of Ujj catch. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.514}

Buruan and Ujj are two towns in the land of Tibet on the bank of this lake. They are equal in size, stand on hillocks above the shore, and both drink from the lake. Each is a town in its own right. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.514}

Many large rivers flow into Lake Buruan from every side. Ujj and Buruan have markets and crafts enough for their own needs, so they do not depend on the crafts of other lands. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.514}

Close to the two towns, to their south, rises a mountain bent in the shape of the letter dal. Few reach its summit without great effort, and in its heart lies low ground with a square palace that has no door. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.514}

From the Thinkh river, which is also called the Sharmakh, it is five stages eastward to Lake Buruan, through villages and thickets of the Tibetan Turks. The route has fortresses and strong castles. On the mountain grows a great deal of Chinese rhubarb, which is shipped to many regions. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.514}
