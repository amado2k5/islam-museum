## Overview
Zabil is a land of Sijistan. Al-Baladhuri reports that Ibn Amir appointed Abd al-Rahman ibn Samura to Sijistan. He came to Zaranj, besieged its marzban in his palace, and made a settlement for two million dirhams and two thousand slaves. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 555}
The same passage says that Bust and Zabil were taken by covenant, that is, by a settlement made under a formal agreement. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 555}
Later on the same page, in a passage about Zabil and Uthman, Wakī is quoted as saying that the agreement he made with them was a lesser contract than a full covenant. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 556}
Ibn Samura stayed in Zaranj until the affairs of Uthman were disturbed, and then left Sijistan. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 556}
Muawiya later appointed him again over Sijistan, and he died in Basra in the year 50 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 558}
A later passage says that Bust itself was taken by force, which differs from the account of the covenant given earlier. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 558}
Al-Bakri, in his dictionary, describes Zabil as a land of al-Sind, reports Ibn Sirin's dislike of the enslavement of the people of Zabil, and glosses walth as a contract of covenant. {cite:mujam-ma-istaajam-bakri p. 447}
