## Summary

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
Turkey is presented by The Oxford Dictionary of Islam as one of the states succeeding the Ottoman Empire and the Islamic world's first secular state: a republic proclaimed in 1923 under Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, the caliphate swept away in 1924, and every school brought under state control. By 1929, the entry says, the Sufi orders and shrines were shut, the fez banned and hats made mandatory for men, the Gregorian calendar and the 24-hour clock in force, a Swiss-modeled civil code had displaced the Islamic codes, and Latin letters had replaced the Ottoman script. Kemalism rested on six principles — republicanism, nationalism, populism, statism, secularism, revolutionism — and the army, which stood guardian over secular democracy, ousted governments in May of 1960, March of 1971 and September of 1980. The entry closes that this record of imposed secularism and unchecked military power clashed with the hopes of practicing Turkish Muslims, pointing to the 1998 headscarf ban in universities — under which veiled women cannot attend class, sit exams or graduate — and calls religious freedom in Turkey a fast-growing international human-rights concern. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2405}
