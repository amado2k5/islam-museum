## Summary
Al-Baylaman was a district of [[place/sind|Sind]]. [[person/muhammad-ibn-al-qasim|Muhammad ibn al-Qasim]] sent an army there; the people did not fight but offered obedience, and the people of Sursant likewise made peace — a place the Basrans used for summer raiding. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 618}

## Details
In his account of the conquests of Sind, al-Baladhuri relates that Muhammad ibn al-Qasim, after taking al-Rawr and Baghrur and giving gifts to the people, sent an army to al-Baylaman; its people offered no resistance and gave their obedience, while the people of Sursant made peace — a raiding ground for the Basrans in his day whose inhabitants would depart in the summer heat {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 618}. Muhammad then came to al-Kiraj, where Dawhar came out to fight him; the enemy was routed and Dawhar fled — some say he was killed — and the townspeople submitted to Muhammad's judgement, so he slew and took captives {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 618}. When al-Walid ibn 'Abd al-Malik died and Sulayman succeeded, he appointed Salih ibn 'Abd al-Rahman over the kharaj of Iraq and Yazid ibn Abi Kabsha al-Saksaki over Sind, whereupon Muhammad ibn al-Qasim was carried off in chains with Mu'awiya ibn al-Muhallab {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 618}. Al-Hajjaj had reckoned that he spent sixty thousand thousand upon Muhammad and received one hundred and twenty thousand thousand, saying "we have quenched our rage and taken our vengeance" {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 618}.
