## Summary
A valley in [[group/hawazin|Hawazin]]'s territory between Mecca and Ta'if; Malik ibn Awf encamped there with the tribes' hosts and wealth before the battle of Hunayn, Hawazin's fugitives rallied there with [[person/durayd-ibn-al-simma|Durayd ibn al-Simma]] after the rout, and there the [[event/expedition-to-awtas|Awtas detachment]] took place {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/80-81, 97-99}.

## In Ibn Hisham's Sira
Durayd ibn al-Simma describes the land of Awtas when they camped at it: "A fine field for cavalry — no rugged ground to break hooves, no soft flat to sink in" {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/81}. There the decisive battle of Abu Amir al-Ash'ari's detachment turned, and Durayd ibn al-Simma was killed {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/93-98}.

## For young visitors
A mountain valley suited to cavalry movement where Hawazin's tribes camped with their wealth — and where the second battle after Hunayn turned.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut makes al-Awtas a wadi in the land of Hawazin where the Prophet's battle of Hunayn with Banu Hawazin took place, records his saying "the furnace grew hot" (hami al-watis) when the war flared and that he was the first to say it, and from Ibn Shubayb that the Ghawr runs from Dhat 'Irq to Awtas, that Awtas lies on the road itself, and that Najd runs from the boundary of Awtas to al-Qaryatayn. He relates that when the polytheists camped at Awtas, Durayd ibn al-Simma, who was with Hawazin as an aged elder, said: "A fine field for horses — neither rugged ground that bruises nor smooth ground that slips," and he quotes verses on it by Ibn Faris and by Abu Wajza al-Sa'di. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.281} Under the entry Harrat Awtas he counts its day among the days of the Arabs. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.245}

## The museum's identification
The record, from Ibn Hisham, makes Awtas a wadi in Hawazin's land where the tribes camped with their wealth and where the fighting of Abu 'Amir al-Ash'ari's detachment and the killing of Durayd ibn al-Simma took place. Yaqut's entry (vol 1 p.281) agrees on the wadi, on Hawazin and on the Hunayn engagement, and gives Durayd's same words, calling him an aged elder with Hawazin; it names neither Malik ibn 'Awf nor the detachment nor Durayd's death, so the battle detail stays with the record's Ibn Hisham text. The entry gives no distance; the record's point (21.4333, 40.35) is 54.2 km from the museum's Mecca point. See [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/taif|al-Ta'if]].
