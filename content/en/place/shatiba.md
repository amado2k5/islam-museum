## Overview
Shatiba is a fine city with a citadel whose beauty and strength are a proverb. The paper made there, al-Idrisi says, has no equal in the inhabited world and spreads to East and West. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 556}

## Distances
From Shatiba to the island of Shuqr is twelve miles, to Denia twenty-five, and to [[place/valencia|Valencia]] thirty-two. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 556} From Shatiba west to Bukayran is forty miles, and the fort of Bukayran is strong and as populous as a city. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 557}

## The province
Shatiba is listed in the province of Arghira with Shuqr, Denia and many forts. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 538} Denia lies on the sea, with a populous suburb, a strong wall and a very strong citadel; it is the shipyard where fleets are built and from which the fleet goes out to raid and ships sail to the far East. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 557}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut spells the name with the unpointed ta and a single ba, and describes Shatiba as a large, old city in eastern al-Andalus, east of Cordoba, from which many learned men came; fine paper is made there and carried to the rest of al-Andalus. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.309} He suggests that the name may derive from {shatba}, the fresh green palm frond, and cites al-Azhari on the sense of the verb {shataba}. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.309} Among those he lists as its nisba-bearers are Abd al-Aziz ibn Abd Allah al-Sa'di al-Shatibi, who came to Damascus as a student, travelled to Iraq, wrote on the unusual words in the hadith collection of Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam and died in Ramadan 465 in the Hawran; and Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Khalaf al-Shatibi, a Qur'an reader who recited in Damascus in several transmissions and wrote {al-Muqni' fi al-qira'at al-sab'}. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.309} On the next page he gives verses by Abu Bahr Safwan ibn Idris of Murcia that satirise the people of Shatiba. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.310}
