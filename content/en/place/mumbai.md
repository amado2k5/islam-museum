## Overview
Mumbai is the city that Arabic books of the early twentieth century call Bombay, spelled in a few variants. The name appears in Hadir al-Alam al-Islami, the Arabic translation of the American Lothrop Stoddard's book with notes by Shakib Arslan. The name does not appear in Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan or al-Qazwini's Athar al-Bilad, as far as they were searched for this page, so the page rests on that one book, which dates from the early decades of the twentieth century. Most of what it says about the city is passing mention within accounts of British India, the Gulf and East Africa, not a description of the city itself. See [[place/india|India]].

## Bombay on Arslan's map of India
In his notes Arslan lists the divisions of India, naming states some of which are Muslim and some Hindu, and then a third part, directly under England, which he puts at 230 million people and whose main lands he gives as Bengal, the Punjab, Agra and the presidencies of Madras and Bombay. He says he chose to give these divisions because readers seldom find them in Arabic books and because he wished to show the proportion of Muslims to Hindus and where they live in India. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 1 p.337} See [[place/punjab|the Punjab]] and [[place/bengal|Bengal]].

Speaking of the Afghans, the book says that men of their tribes serve as soldiers in the British army, and that officers of this kind are to be seen in Madras, Bombay and Hyderabad. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 2 p.204} See [[place/afghanistan|Afghanistan]].

## The Muslim press and printing
On the newspapers that spread the call for pan-Islamic unity, the book says that every Muslim land is in constant touch with the others, and that in Basra and Bombay one sees the papers of Constantinople, and in Muhammara, Karbala and Port Said those of Calcutta. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 t.s01 p.160} This comes from the single-volume scan t.s01, printed page 160. See [[place/istanbul|Istanbul]].

In its section on the Babi movement and what grew from it, the book lists among the writings of Baha the Kitab al-Aqdas, and says it was printed in Bombay and St Petersburg. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 4 p.357} This page records only the place of printing.

## Bombay in the history of the Gulf and East Africa
In its history of Oman the book says that the fleet of Sayf ibn Sultan, who in the book's words recovered the Omanis' former East African coast from the Portuguese in 1694, overran the island of Salsette near Bombay in India and the towns of Barsalur and Mangalur, and that the raja of Karnatik could not defend them. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 4 p.336} See [[place/oman|Oman]].

In its account of Muscat, Arslan says that Turki, brother of Thuwayni, who had been living in India, came from Bombay to Muscat, took over the emirate and drove out Azzan ibn Qays, with the Wahhabis backing his move; matters were settled for him from 1874 on the book's reckoning, and he overcame the rebels with England's help. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 4 p.345} See [[place/muscat|Muscat]].

In its account of Zanzibar the book says that Sayyid Barghash ibn Sa'id took power on 7 October 1870 after the death of his brother Majid and held it until his death on 7 March 1888. He had contested the rule with Majid, and the English took him to Bombay, where he stayed two years before making peace with his brother and returning to Zanzibar. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 4 p.348} See [[place/zanzibar|Zanzibar]].

In a note added after the book's first edition, it says that when the people of Afghanistan rose against King Amanullah and the rebels beat his troops, he fled from Kabul to Kandahar and from there to India, where he took ship at Bombay, came to Europe and chose to live in Rome; Nadir Khan took the Afghan throne after him. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 2 p.214} See [[person/amanullah-khan|Amanullah Khan]] and [[place/kabul|Kabul]].

## In the history of the Delhi sultanate
In Muslim India's history Arslan says the sultanate of India passed in 1290 CE from the Mamluks to the Afghan Khalji house, and that its sultan Ala al-Din overran the land of the Mahrat, which the book says lies in what is now the country of Bombay, laid tribute on its raja, took towns and came back with much booty. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 4 p.293}

## A description of housing in Bombay
Discussing the poor state of workers in the East, the book quotes a report by an Indian officer of the health service on overcrowded dwellings in Bombay. He describes homes that breed disease and epidemics, whose occupants crowd one another like herds in their pens, and the author says the situation is the same in the other towns and industrial centres of India. {cite:stoddard-arslan-hadir-1923 4 p.251} This is a quotation from a report that this work has not checked, and it dates from the early twentieth century.

## What was not found
The books examined say nothing of the city's founding, its mosques or its scholars, so this page does not treat them. The place card describes the city today and rests on two other sources.
