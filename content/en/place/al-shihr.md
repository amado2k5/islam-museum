## Summary
Al-Shihr was a land and its capital on Yemen's eastern coast adjoining Hadramawt, home to the Mahra tribes, famed for swift camels and frankincense groves. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.154}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says the land of al-Shihr adjoins Hadramawt on its east and holds the Mahra tribes, pure Arabs, whose bred camels are unmatched for speed {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.154}. He adds that the chief town of the Mahra country is called al-Shihr, and that the Mahra tongue is barely intelligible, being the ancient Himyaritic language {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.155}. Most of this land is desert peopled only by Mahra's camels; their wealth is camels and goats, their food fish and dates, their drink milk, and their country is said to stretch nine hundred miles long {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.155}.
