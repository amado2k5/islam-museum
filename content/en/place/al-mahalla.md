## Overview
al-Mahalla: a large town with flourishing markets and trade. It lies on the Balqina canal. al-Idrisi reports that it faces another town to the east. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.340}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vowels al-Mahalla with a fatha, notes that mahall and mahalla mean the place where one alights, and calls it a famous city of Egypt that is in fact the name of several places. He lists Mahallat Dakla, the largest and best known, between Cairo and Damietta; Mahallat Abi al-Haytham; Mahallat Sharqiyun, which is al-Mahalla al-Kubra and has two sides, Sandafa and Sharqiyun; Mahallat Manuf, a market town in al-Gharbiyya; Mahallat Naqida in the western Hawf; and Mahallat al-Khulafa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.63}

He says he does not know which of these Radi al-Dawla Dawud ibn Miqdam ibn Muzaffar al-Muhalli belonged to, a son of the soldiery who studied literature and wrote good verse, mentioned by Ibn al-Zubayr in his book al-Jinan. He then quotes lines in which Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Sa'ati longs for al-Mahalla. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.63} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.64}

Right after, he lists another place of the same name, with a kasra on the h, a village of the district of Dhamar in Yemen, which is not the Egyptian town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.64}
