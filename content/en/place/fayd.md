## Overview
A place in the land of [[group/tayy|Tayy]], which the Prophet granted to [[person/zayd-al-khayl|Zayd al-Khayl]] together with lands with him, and he was given a document to that effect in the [[event/delegation-of-tayy|delegation of Tayy]]. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224}

## What the editors say
The editors of the 1936 edition are content to say that Fayd is a place name. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/224}

## What is not covered here
The location of Fayd on the Iraqi pilgrim road, and its coordinates, will be added from Yaqut's dictionary of places or from Wikidata.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Fayd, with a fatha, a sukun and an undotted dal, and lists meanings: death (Ibn al-A'rabi), the hairs above a horse's lip, and, for al-Mu'arrij, a station on the Mecca road, and the saffron flower. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282} He then says it is a small town halfway along the road to Mecca from Kufa, still flourishing in his time, where pilgrims leave their provisions and heavy baggage with its people and take them back on return, giving a gift to those who kept them; its people live by storing fodder all year until the pilgrims arrive and buy it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282} He quotes al-Zajjaji that it was named for Fayd ibn Ham, the first to settle it, and al-Sakuni that it is the halfway point of the pilgrim road from Kufa to Mecca, held in thirds (one for the 'Umariyyin, one for the family of Abu Salama of Hamdan and one for Banu Nabhan of Tayy), six nights from Wadi al-Qura by way of al-'Urayma, and that there is no road to Syria "dun Fayd", a word that can mean short of it or beyond it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282} He adds, as what "they said" without naming them, that Zuhayr's "Fayd of al-Qurayyat" is another place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282} From al-Hazimi he gives that Fayd, with a ya', is near Aja and Salma, the two mountains of Tayy, and lists reporters named after it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.282}

His entry on Aja, on al-Sakuni's word, calls it one of the two mountains of Tayy, west of Fayd, a journey of two nights between them. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.94} In the entry on al-Aknaf, Muhalhil ibn Zayd al-Khayl al-Ta'i writes to Tulayha that they are at the Aknaf, in the mountains of Fayd, which are the flanks of Salma. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.241}

## The museum's identification
The record, from Ibn Hisham, has a place in the land of Tayy in Najd that the Prophet granted to Zayd al-Khayl with other lands, with a document, in the delegation of Tayy; the editors of the 1936 edition say only that it is a place name, and the page leaves its position to Yaqut. Yaqut fills that gap: Fayd is a small town halfway along the pilgrim road from Kufa to Mecca, held in thirds among three groups, six nights from Wadi al-Qura, and near Aja and Salma, the two mountains of Tayy, on al-Hazimi's word. That agrees with the record's place in Tayy's country in Najd, and adds a link: Zayd al-Khayl's son Muhalhil names Fayd's mountains in the Aknaf entry. Yaqut does not mention the Prophet's grant to Zayd al-Khayl. The record's point (27.118, 42.52) is marked as a site and lies 98.1 km from the museum's Aja and Salma point and 93.8 km from its Ha'il point; Yaqut, on al-Sakuni's word, puts Aja west of Fayd at two nights, which is not set directly against kilometres. The museum reads the record's Fayd as the one on the road, not the "Fayd of al-Qurayyat" in Zuhayr's verse, which Yaqut notes as another place; this is the museum's reading.
