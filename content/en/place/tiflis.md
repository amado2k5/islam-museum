## Overview
Tiflis is a town in the district of Jarzan al-Qarmaz, in Armenia. Al-Baladhuri lists it among the first Armenia, with Sisajan and Arran. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.273}

When Habib ibn Maslama arrived at Tiflis, he wrote its people a letter of safety and peace, in return for acknowledging their lowly status and paying jizya at one dinar on every house. He made it a condition that they should not gather families of rank together to lighten the jizya, and that they should not be separated to increase it. They were to give counsel to the Muslims and help them against their enemies as far as they could, to host a needy Muslim for a night in a fitting way, and to pass on any Muslim left stranded among them to the nearest group of believers. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.283}

Al-Jarrah ibn Abd Allah al-Hakami then wrote a letter to the people of Tiflis confirming the safety that Habib had granted them. He made peace with them over their land, their vineyards and their mills, on condition that they pay one hundred dirhams a year for them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.284}

It is part of [[place/armenia|Armenia]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
For [[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]], Tiflis is a town of First Armenia, or of Arran according to some, the chief town of the Jurzan district near Bab al-Abwab, an ancient city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.800} He quotes the epistle of Mis'ar ibn Muhalhil, who called it a city "with no Islam beyond it", with the river Kur running through it turning mills, a great wall, and very hot baths that need no fire because they stand on a hot spring; Yaqut adds that people of Tiflis told him of this bath and that it belonged to the Muslims, no one else entering it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.800}

He records that the Muslims took it in the days of Uthman ibn Affan, when [[person/habib-ibn-maslama|Habib ibn Maslama]] marched into Armenia and the envoy of the patrician of Jurzan came asking for peace; Habib wrote the people of Tiflis a guarantee of safety for their persons, churches, monasteries and religion against a jizya of one dinar per household, and Yaqut gives the text. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.800} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.801} It remained in Muslim hands, he says, until 515 AH, when a Christian people called the Kurj (Georgians) came out of the Abkhaz mountains while the Seljuq brothers Mahmud and Mas'ud, sons of Muhammad ibn Malikshah, were at odds; they defeated the governors of Armenia, besieged Tiflis, took it by force and killed many Muslims, then "treated its people well and made them their subjects". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.801}
