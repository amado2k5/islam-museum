## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives Kerman with fatha, then a silent letter and a final nun, and says the fatha is the better attested vowel though a kasra is sometimes reported. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.454} He describes a famous province and large inhabited region between Fars, Makran, Sijistan and Khurasan, rich in palms, crops and livestock and compared with Basra for the quantity of its dates; among its towns he names Jiruft, Bamm, Sirjan and Bardsir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.454} Citing al-Bashshari, he says it is a region that resembles Fars, Basra and Khurasan in different respects, whose people are Sunni and good, but that it had fallen into ruin after having been one of the most prosperous lands under the Seljuks. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.454}

On the name, he cites Ibn al-Kalbi that it comes from Kerman ibn Falluj, a descendant of Japheth son of Noah, and others that it comes from Kerman ibn Fark ibn Sam. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.454} He tells the story of philosophers whom a Persian king settled there and who brought the water up from deep wells, and notes that the Persian emperors levied sixty million dirhams from it, the region measuring a hundred and eighty farsakhs each way. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.454}

On the conquest, he says Uthman ibn al-As crossed from Bahrain to Fars and met the marzban of Kerman on the island of Barkawan; later Ibn Amir appointed Mujashi' ibn Mas'ud al-Sulami, who took Maymand, Sirjan and Jiruft, while Abu Musa al-Ash'ari sent al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad al-Harithi, who took the area around Sirjan, and land of those who fled was granted to Arabs. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.455} He records that al-Hajjaj appointed Qatan ibn Qabisa al-Hilali over Fars and Kerman, and that the "jaiza" (reward) got its name in his time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.455} Yaqut adds that there is also a town called Kerman between Ghazna and India, under Ghazna. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.455}
