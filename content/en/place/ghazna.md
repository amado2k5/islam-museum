## Summary
Ghazna was a noble populous city of many markets, possessing trade and wealthy merchants, and from it one entered the lands of India. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.460}

## Details
On the road from Bust to Ghazna, al-Idrisi says that from Bust to the ribat of Firuz is a stage, then to the ribat of Mighun a stage, then to the ribat of Kathir a stage, then to al-Rukhaj, a city called Banjway, a stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.459}. Banjway is one of the cities of al-Dawar and Balis; its people are Sufis and it has much produce, cattle and livestock, from which great revenues are raised {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.459}. Then to Khaysan a stage, Khaysan being the first of the bounds of Ghazna, then to the village of Khasraji a stage, then to the ribat of Hadhwa, an inhabited village, a stage, then to Ghazna a stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.460}. Ghazna is a noble populous city of many markets, with trade and wealthy merchants, and from it one enters the lands of India {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.460}. The author counts it among the cities of the land of Sijistan and what adjoins it in the account of the famous towns such as Zaranj and others in the part comprising Sijistan and its borders {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.454}. In his scheme it is a populous frontier city on the threshold of India, linked westward to the road of Bust and to the lands of al-Rukhaj and al-Dawar.
