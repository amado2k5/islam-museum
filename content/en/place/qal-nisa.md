## Overview
Qal'at al-Nisa is a well-built fortress. Al-Idrisi describes it as overlooking connected settlements, with many benefits in crops, fruit and trees. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.611}

## Distances
From Sabuqa to Qal'at al-Nisa is twelve miles on the road to Karkant. From Naru it is twenty-one miles, towards the east and north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.611} From Qal'at al-Nisa, Qasriyani is eighteen miles away and Qarqudhi lies fifteen miles to the south. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.611} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.612} Al-Hajar al-Muthaqqab is seven miles to the west with a little north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.613}

## The salt river
To the east of the fortress, close by, runs the salt river. Al-Idrisi says it passes east of Qal'at al-Nisa at about five miles, and then goes on to the land of al-Hajar al-Muthaqqab. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.612} Before that, the valley runs over salt flats, and its water becomes salty. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.612}

## Neighbouring places
Qasriyani is a town on the top of a mountain. It has a strong fortress, a secure stronghold, markets laid out well, well-built houses, crafts and goods. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.611} Qarqudhi is a good town on a mountain peak, one of the most secure heights, with fine land and excellent crops. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.612} Al-Hajar al-Muthaqqab is a strong fortress and a secure stronghold, with wide territories, a well-populated countryside and abundant water. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.613}
