## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says Amul, with a damma on the mim and lam, is the name of the largest city of Tabaristan in the plain, since Tabaristan is both plain and mountain, and places it in the fourth climate. He gives its distances as eighteen farsakhs from Sariya, twelve from al-Ruyan and twenty from Salus on the Gilan side. He reports that Tabari prayer rugs and fine carpets are made there, that when its people first accepted Islam it was a garrison post of two thousand men, and that many scholars came from it, usually called al-Tabari after Tabaristan, among them Abu Jafar Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari, author of the famous commentary and history, whose origin and birth were in Amul and who died in 310 AH. He adds that the Friday sermon was given in this city and across Tabaristan and its revenues were sent to Khwarazmshah Ala al-Din Muhammad ibn Tikish until he fled the Tatars in 617 AH. He distinguishes it from Amul on the Jayhun (Oxus), a different city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.57} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.58}
