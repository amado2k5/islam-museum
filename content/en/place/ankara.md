## Overview
Ankara is a city in [[place/anatolia|Anatolia]] and, according to the record's data, today the capital of Turkey. In the medieval Islamic sources it appears in Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan, which says, as far as it had reached him, that Anqira is the Arabic name of the city called Ankuriya {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.271}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the vocalisation of the name, with a fatha on the first letter, a sukun, a kasra on the qaf, then ra and ha, and says that, as far as it had reached him, it is the name of the city called Ankuriya {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.271}.

In the entry he tells how Imru' al-Qays went to the Byzantine emperor to ask for help against the killers of his father, the emperor's daughter having, in the story, fallen in love with him. The emperor promised that troops would follow him once he reached Syria, or that he would order his forces in Syria to help him; but when Imru' al-Qays reached Ankara the emperor sent him poisoned garments, and when he put them on his flesh fell away. Knowing he was dying, he spoke verses saying that a thrust and a speech would remain "tomorrow at Ankara" {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.271}.

Yaqut also reports Ptolemy's figures for the city: a longitude of fifty-eight degrees and a latitude of forty-nine degrees forty minutes, in the seventh climate, together with its ascendant and house of life in the manner of the ancient astronomers {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.271} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.272}.

He adds that [[person/al-mutasim|al-Mu'tasim]] took it on his way to [[place/amorium|Amorium]], and quotes a line of [[person/abu-tammam|Abu Tammam]]'s famous poem on Amorium that speaks of "the day of Ankara" and the desolation left there {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.272}.

## The other Ankara near al-Hira
Yaqut says that Ankara is also the name of another place in the district of al-Hira, the one named in a poem of al-Aswad ibn Ya'fur al-Nahshali, and he relays an anecdote from al-Asma'i about the judge Sawwar ibn Abd Allah and those verses. He then says that some scholars held that the Ankara of al-Aswad's verse is the Ankara in the land of the Byzantines, where the tribe of Iyad settled when Khosrow drove them out of his country; he finds this view very good and says he thinks it the correct one {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.272}. Yaqut thus first distinguishes two places of the same name and then, on the view of those scholars, joins them; this page attributes that view to them.

## A note on scope
The sources read for this page give nothing on Ankara in the Ottoman or modern periods, so none is stated.
