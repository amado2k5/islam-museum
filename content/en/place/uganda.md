## Summary
Country in East Africa; about a sixth of its people are Sunni Muslim. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2410}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
Sixteen percent of Ugandans, in the Dictionary's figures, are Sunni Muslims whose forebears reached the country in the nineteenth century, entering through Kenya from the east and through Egypt and Sudan from the north. Shafii law predominates in central Buganda, Maliki in the north; the coming of Islam brought no Arabization — unlike North Africa — and Arabic is little spoken. Since independence from Britain in 1962 the country was, the entry says, governed by Protestant Christians except during Idi Amin's rule of 1971 to 1979. Uganda entered the Organization of the Islamic Conference in the early 1970s, and both Ids have been national holidays since Amin's time. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2410}
