## Overview
Massisa (al-Massisa, Mopsuestia) is a town in Cilicia on the river Jayhan. The Arab geographers counted it among the frontier towns of Syria. The Abbasids rebuilt it, and William of Tyre records that an earthquake destroyed it in 1114 CE. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 529}

Golius notes that Massisa lies on the Jayhan, which he calls the Greek Pyramus. The river reaches the sea after about four farsakhs. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 529}

Golius, citing Ibn Hawqal, says that Massisa was once two towns facing each other across the river, one of them called Kafr Bayja. Al-Mansur and his successors al-Mahdi, al-Rashid and al-Ma'mun built it up, and al-Mu'tasim completed it. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 529}

In 1114 CE a great earthquake destroyed Massisa, Marash and other towns, according to William of Tyre. Golius, citing church books, adds that Theodore, the bishop of the town, held the view of Nestorius about Christ. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 530}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut gives the name with a fatha, then kasra, a doubled sad and a final sad, and notes that al-Azhari and other lexicographers pointed the first sad as doubled, while al-Jawhari and al-Farabi said Massisa with both sads light. Yaqut judges the first form the more correct. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.144}

Yaqut describes it as a town on the bank of the Jayhan, a frontier town of Syria between Antioch and the lands of the Byzantines, close to Tarsus. It had a wall and five gates. According to the tellers of history, its builder was Massisa, son of Rum, son of Yaman, son of Sam, son of Noah. It was among the well-known frontier towns of Islam, and righteous men once garrisoned it. It had many orchards watered by the Jayhan. Yaqut says that for many years it had been in the hands of Ibn Leon and his son after him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.144}

Yaqut quotes al-Muhallabi that the frontier town had a distinctive craft: furs were made there and carried to far regions, and a single fur sometimes fetched thirty dinars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.145}

Yaqut also records a second Massisa, a village of Damascus near Bayt Lahya. Several scholars bear the nisba al-Massisi, among them the Shafi'i jurist Abu al-Qasim Ali ibn Muhammad al-Sulami al-Massisi, whom he takes from al-Sam'ani's Kitab al-Ansab. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.145}
