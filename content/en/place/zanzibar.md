## Summary
[[place/zanzibar|Zanzibar]] is an island off the East African coast, and its coordinates are the ones recorded in the geographic-names database. {cite:geonames-gazetteer} In the nineteenth century it became a commercial capital: [[person/said-bin-sultan|Said bin Sultan]] moved his capital there in the 1830s, and his son [[person/majid-bin-said|Majid]] became its sultan when his father died on 19 October 1856. {cite:wikipedia-said-bin-sultan-en} {cite:britannica-said-ibn-sultan} {cite:wikipedia-majid-bin-said-en}

## The Omani capital
Britannica says Said made Zanzibar the leading power in East Africa and the commercial capital of the western Indian Ocean. {cite:britannica-said-ibn-sultan} His English article says he introduced cloves to Zanzibar and Pemba, and that the port became the port of ivory, slaves and spices. {cite:wikipedia-said-bin-sultan-en} The museum records the slave trade as the sources describe it, without praise or blame.

## Later stops in the story
The sultanate, the protectorate and the revolution are separate events linked to this place: Majid's accession, his death in 1870, the acceptance of the British protectorate in 1890, the revolution of 12 January 1964, and the assassination of [[person/abeid-karume|Abeid Karume]] on 7 April 1972. Each date is attributed to its source on the event record.
