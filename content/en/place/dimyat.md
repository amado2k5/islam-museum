## Overview
Damietta: a city of the northern Delta on the mouth of the Nile. According to the conquests of the lands, Amr ibn al-As sent to it Umayr ibn Wahb al-Jumahi, together with Tanis, Tunah, Dumayra, Shata, Daqhaliya, Bana and Busir, and the people of their villages made peace on the same terms as the judgement of Fustat. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.304}

In the museum's data Damietta is linked to [[event/four-lieutenants-egypt|the event of the four commanders in Egypt]], and [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] is the one who sent Umayr ibn Wahb to it.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Damietta as an old city between Tinnis and Cairo, on the angle between the salt sea and the Nile, known for its good air and for the fine weaving of linen cloth. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 pp.472-473}

He calls it one of the frontier posts of Islam and gives a report in praise of it, with Alexandria. Where the Nile meets the sea north of Damietta, at a place called al-Ashtum, the river is about a hundred cubits wide; two towers with an iron chain between them, guarded, let no boat leave for the sea or enter without permission. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 pp.472-473}

Quoting al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Muhallabi, he notes that the weavers of the fine cloth in Damietta and Tinnis were Copts of the humbler sort, and that most of their food was fish. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.473}
