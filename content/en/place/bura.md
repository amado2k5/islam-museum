## Summary
Bura was a populous city of much trade and continuous building, abounding in goods, with wheat, rice and sweet-tasting aqil trees; two days downstream from Tukha on the river Kulha, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.206}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that from the city of Tukha to the city of Bura is two days descending the river Kulha, or four days by land; Bura is populous and full of commerce, its buildings continuous and its goods abundant, producing wheat, rice and aqil trees pleasant to eat {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.206}. From Bura to the city of Kashghara is eight stages, from Bura to the city of Isqira seven stages, and from Isqira to Kashghara eight stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.206}. Bura thus stood in his scheme on the great Chinese river Kulha between Tukha, Isqira and Kashghara, combining a large population, unbroken urban fabric and the river trade that made it one of the ports of those lands.
