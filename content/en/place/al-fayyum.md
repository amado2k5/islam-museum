## Overview
al-Fayyum: one of the districts of Upper Egypt. Amr ibn al-As sent to it Kharija ibn Hudhafa al-Adawi, together with al-Ashmunayn, Ikhmim, al-Bushrudat and the villages of Upper Egypt. The people of its villages made peace on the same terms as the judgement of Fustat. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.304}

It is a site in the account of [[event/four-lieutenants-egypt|the four commanders in Egypt]]. See also [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]].

## Fayyum in al-Maqrizi
Al-Maqrizi says that the site of al-Fayyum was where the Nile's water drained away, and that when Joseph took charge of Egypt's affairs, he made it habitable. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.711}

Citing Ibn Abd al-Hakam, al-Maqrizi reports that once the conquest was complete, Amr ibn al-As sent cavalry raids to the villages around it, and al-Fayyum remained for a year without the Muslims knowing where it was, until a man came and told them of it. {cite:khitat-maqrizi-1998 p.733}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives al-Fayyum as a name of two places, one in Egypt and the other near Hit in Iraq, and describes the Egyptian one as a western province four days from Fustat, with two days of waterless, pastureless desert between, lying in a hollow like a circle, with the Nile said to stand higher than it. He relays, from Ibn Abd al-Hakam through Hisham ibn Ishaq, that the land was then called al-Jawba and that Pharaoh's ministers made it a test for Joseph, who dug three canals (al-Manhi from above Ashmun to al-Lahun, an eastern canal that is the Fayyum canal, and a western one at the village of Tayhamt); the work took seventy days, and the king called it the work of a thousand days, hence the name. Joseph then settled a household from each district of Egypt there to build a village, the first being called Shananah, and shared the water by measure; this report adds that surveying began then. Yaqut quotes Ibn Zulaq that Joseph built it by revelation with 360 villages yielding a thousand dinars a day, and Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Tarkhan that in 355 the Fayyum was contracted for Kafur at 620,000 dinars. He says its regulations later changed with successive governors until it stood at a tenth of what it had been, as far as he knew, and that Marwan ibn Muhammad, the last Umayyad caliph, was reportedly killed in one of its districts. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.286} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.287} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.288}
