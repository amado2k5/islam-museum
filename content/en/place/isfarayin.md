## Overview
Isfarayin was a fortified small town in the region of Nishapur, halfway along the road from Jurjan. Its old name was Mahrajan, which some kings gave it for its greenness, and its district held 451 villages. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.177}

## Yaqut's entry
Yaqut notes that a village called Mahrajan also lay among its lands. He gives two accounts of the name. Abu al-Qasim al-Bayhaqi held that it came from Asbarayin, with a b, where asbar is the Persian word for shield and ayin means custom, so that its people were known in early times for carrying shields. Another account says Isfandiyar founded the town and it took his name, which changed over time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.177}

Yaqut quotes verses by Abu al-Hasan Ali ibn Nasr al-Fandurji, who longed for Isfarayin and its people. He also says that many leading imams were ascribed to the town, and he begins the list with Yaqub ibn Ishaq ibn Ibrahim al-Isfarayini, one of the memorizers of hadith. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.177}
