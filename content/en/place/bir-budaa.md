## Summary
Bir Buda'a is a well of the Prophet's time, to which [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] gives a chapter of its own in the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan]], "what has come about the well of Buda'a". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45} There he relates from [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]] that the Prophet was asked, "Shall we perform ablution from the well of Buda'a, a well into which menstrual rags, dogs' flesh and filth are thrown?", and replied, "Water is pure; nothing makes it impure." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45-46}

## The two versions
- Abu Usama's version, from al-Walid ibn Kathir, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, from 'Ubayd Allah ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Rafi' ibn Khadij, from Abu Sa'id (report 66); Abu Dawud notes that some said "'Abd al-Rahman ibn Rafi'". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45-46}
- Muhammad ibn Ishaq's version, from Salit ibn Ayyub, from 'Ubayd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Rafi' al-Ansari, from Abu Sa'id (report 67), in which the Prophet is told that water is drawn for him from it and that dogs' flesh, menstrual cloths and human excrement are thrown into it. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 46}
- The annotator quotes Imam Ahmad: "The report of the well of Buda'a is sound." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 46}

## What Abu Dawud saw
Abu Dawud says he heard [[person/qutayba-ibn-said|Qutayba ibn Sa'id]] relate that he asked the keeper of the well about its depth, and was told that at its highest the water reached the groin, and when low it was below the waist. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 46} Abu Dawud then says that he measured it himself with his cloak, stretching it across and measuring the cloth, and found the width to be six cubits; he asked the man who opened the garden for him whether its structure had been changed, was told no, and saw discoloured water in it. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 46}

## Al-Khattabi's explanation
[[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|Al-Khattabi]] rejects the idea that people threw refuse into the well on purpose: such conduct, he says, would not be imagined even of a non-Muslim, let alone a Muslim, in a land where water was precious and needed. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45} He explains that the well lay on sloping ground where floods swept refuse from the paths and courtyards into it, and that its water was so abundant that what fell in did not change it, so they asked about its ruling. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45} In his view the answer "nothing makes it impure" refers to abundant water like that of this well, and so does not contradict the report of [[concept/qullatayn|the two qullas]], since its water reached that measure. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 45-46}

## Not covered here
This volume does not fix the well's location on the ground; that remains to be taken from geographical works and the sira.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
In his chapter on wells Yaqut lists the well of Buda'a, with a damma on the b, also reported with a kasra, says it is in the house of Banu Sa'ida, and refers to what he gives under Buda'a. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.299}

Under Buda'a itself he says the name has a damma, though some give it a kasra and the first is more common; it is the house of Banu Sa'ida in Medina and its well is well known. There, he says, the Prophet gave the ruling that water is pure so long as it is not altered, and it has properties belonging to the people of Medina. He adds, from al-Qa'nabi's explanation in the book of al-Bukhari, that Buda'a has palm trees in Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.442}

He relates that the Prophet came to the well of Buda'a, performed ablution from the bucket, returned it to the well, spat into it and drank from its water, and that when someone fell ill in his days he would say to wash him with water of Buda'a, and the patient, once washed, seemed freed from a bond. He adds a saying of Asma bint Abi Bakr that they washed the sick from the well for three days and they recovered. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.442} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.443}

Yaqut then relays, from the book al-Hawi by Abu al-Hasan al-Mawardi, the report of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri that the Prophet was told he performed ablution from the well of Buda'a, into which menstrual cloths, dog flesh and what people clear away were thrown, and said that nothing makes water impure; al-Mawardi cites it in a disagreement with Abu Hanifa. He gives two objections and al-Mawardi's answers. The first is that the well is a flowing spring feeding orchards; in reply he quotes Abu Dawud, who measured its width with his cloak and found six cubits, asked the man who opened the garden whether its structure had been changed and was told it had not, and saw discoloured water in it, and who heard Qutayba ibn Sa'id say that the well's keeper told him the water at most reaches the groin and, when lower, below the waist-wrap. The second objection is that it cannot be attributed to the Companions that they threw such things into the well; Yaqut relays al-Mawardi's reply that this attribution to them is not sound. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.443}
