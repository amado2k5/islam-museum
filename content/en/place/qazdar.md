## Summary
Qazdār is a large, populous city of fair size, with markets, trade and good conditions, and districts and settled villages that depend on it, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.175}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reaches Qazdār from Ṭuwārān, a fortified city with gates, parks and continuous fields. Its chief town is also called Ṭuwārān, after the valley. From Ṭuwārān to Qazdār is four stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.175}

He calls Qazdār a large, populous city of fair size, with markets, trade and good conditions, and districts and settled villages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.175}

To its west lies Kīzkānān, where the governor of Ṭuwārān lodges. Kīzkānān is a civilised town with many people and low prices, with gardens, vineyards and fruit, but no palms. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.175}

From Ṭuwārān to Mustanj, in the middle of the desert, is three stages; Mustanj is a small town with little fruit. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.175}
