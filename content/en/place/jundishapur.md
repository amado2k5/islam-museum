## Overview
A great city in the districts of Ahvaz. Al-Baladhuri reports that [[person/abu-musa-al-ashari|Abu Musa]] made peace with it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The peace was on the condition that none of its people be killed or enslaved, and that their property not be touched, except their weapons. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Jundishapur as a city in Khuzistan built by Shapur ibn Ardashir, from whom it takes its name, and in which he settled captives from Rome and a company of his soldiers. Hamza says the name is an Arabized form of Andishapur, meaning better than Antioch. Ibn al-Faqih tells a story of the companions of Shapur who lost him and searched for him, and he uses it to explain the names of Nishapur and Jundishapur. When they came to Nishapur and did not find him, they said that he was not there, and the town got its name from that; later they found him at Jundishapur. Yaqut calls it a fertile city of wide plenty, with palms and crops. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.170}
