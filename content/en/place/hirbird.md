## Summary
Hirbird is a great populous city adjoining the land of Shasuniya, which al-Idrisi places seventy miles east of the city of Qasila. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.873}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from the city of Qasila to the city of Hirbird is seventy miles eastward, that Hirbird adjoins the land of Shasuniya, and that it is a great populous city of abundant wealth, its riches ample and its farmlands continuous and unbroken {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.873}. He adds that from the city of Hirbird to the city of Bansa, south-southwest, is sixty miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.873}. He also counts it among the lands of Shasuniya together with Wadhanbarka, Niyun Bark, Hala and Mishla {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.882}. Hirbird is thus one of the chief cities of that part of northern Europe, uniting broad prosperity, plentiful wealth and unbroken cultivation around it, and al-Idrisi places it on the borders of the land of Shasuniya, linking Qasila and Bansa among the roads of that country. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.873}
