## Overview
Qarqub is a city of Khuzistan, counted by al-Idrisi among its towns, one stage from al-Sus. The Qarqubi fabric is named after it throughout the world, and a gold-threaded brocade is made there of a kind seldom found elsewhere on earth, the Qarqubi brocade. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 396}

## Craft
Al-Idrisi says that Qarqub has, like al-Sus, its tiraz workshops, where robes, brocade, khazz silk and other fine, costly cloth are woven for the sultan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 397}

## Neighbours
To the north, one stage from Qarqub, lies the town of al-Tayb, which is not large but handsome and rich in good things. Tikak are made there that resemble the Armenian tikak, with no equal in the lands of Islam after those of Armenia, along with costly, well made barakanat and many other crafts; from al-Tayb to Wasit is two stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 397} Qarqub also appears in the list of Khuzistan towns that includes al-Ahwaz, Askar Mukram, Tustar and al-Sus. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 392}
