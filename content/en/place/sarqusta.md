## Overview
Sarqusta is a city of al-Andalus, one of the chief cities of its lands: large in extent, with wide streets, gardens and orchards joined together, and a strong stone wall. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 554} It stands on the bank of the great river called Ibra, and al-Idrisi calls it the White City because of its abundance of gypsum and lime. He also notes a great bridge by which one crosses into the city. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 554} Yaqut describes the Sarqusta river as a noble, large river with broad banks. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 114} Al-Idrisi places Sarqusta in the district of Arnit, with Washqa and Tutaila. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 538} He gives the distance from Sarqusta to Washqa as forty miles, to Duruqa as fifty, and to Tutaila as fifty. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 554} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 733} Yaqut, however, puts the distance between Tutaila and Sarqusta at seventeen farsakhs, and places Arnit twenty-seven farsakhs from Sarqusta. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 796} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 211} Other places in its region include Barusha, which Yaqut places west of Sarqusta, and Baltash, a district of al-Andalus near Sarqusta with a river of its own, and Bays, a locality in its region. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 438} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 674} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 737}
