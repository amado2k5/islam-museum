## Summary
Balis is a small Syrian town on the western bank of the Euphrates opposite al-Raqqah; Yaqut mentions that the Euphrates shifted away from it to the east, and Sulayman Shah, grandfather of Osman, drowned near it. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 500}

Golius states that Balis is a small Syrian town on the western bank of the Euphrates, opposite al-Raqqah to the east, and that it is the first place encountered on the road from Iraq toward Syria. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 500}

Golius quotes Yaqut, who lived in the sixth century AH, that the Euphrates gradually moved away from Balis toward the east until it was about four miles distant from it in his time. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 500}

At this site, according to Abu al-Fida', Sulayman Shah, grandfather of Osman who gave the Ottoman dynasty its name, drowned while attempting to cross the Euphrates. {cite:farghani-elements-astronomy p. 501}
