## Summary
A large region seven stages from Mecca toward Yemen. The sources of the biography describe it as having seventy-three villages, and as the home of a Christian community whose delegation came to Medina in 9 AH {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.450}.

## About the place
Al-Mubarakpuri describes Najran as a large country of seventy-three villages, a day's journey for a swift rider. It could muster a hundred thousand fighters, of the Christian faith {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.450}. Its delegation came in 9 AH: sixty men, twenty-four of them nobles, led by al-Aqib, al-Sayyid and the bishop {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.450}. The Prophet made peace with them on terms of the jizya, under the protection of God and His Messenger, with full freedom in their religion. Islam began to spread among them after that {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.451}.

## For young visitors
An ancient city in the south of the Arabian Peninsula, whose people were Christian. A delegation from them came to the Prophet and made peace with him.

## In the introduction to The Sealed Nectar
The people of Najran became Christian at the hands of the ascetic [[person/faymiyun|Faymiyun]], around the time of the first Abyssinian occupation of Yemen {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira 1/31-34}. Then [[person/dhu-nuwas|Dhu Nuwas]] attacked them and dug the trench for them, and burned them in October 523 CE. They are the People of the Ditch mentioned in Surat al-Buruj {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40}.
