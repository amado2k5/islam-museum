## Summary
A country in which, according to The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, some 1.2 million of 15 million people are Muslim. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2250}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam says that Sri Lankan Muslims, often called Moors, are largely Sunnis who follow the Shafii school of law. It describes a small community of some 1.2 million people among the country's 15 million, mostly living in towns and cities, which traces its ancestry through migration and conversion going back to the seventh century. According to the Dictionary, the Muslims of Sri Lanka were forced to develop apart from the other Muslims of the subcontinent for three centuries, during the Portuguese and British occupations. It says that in the nineteenth century the Sufi orders revived piety and ritual life, Islamic learning, observance and the norms of scripture, and that Muslims backed the nationalist movement and have had a place in the governments ruling Sri Lanka since 1947. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e2250}
