## Overview
Al-Ahsa: a town on the Persian Gulf, pleasant but small, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 386}

## Location and history
Al-Idrisi says that the Qarmatians were in it. It lies two stages from al-Qatif. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 386}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut explains that al-Ahsa is the plural of hasy, a water that the sand soaks up; when it reaches a hard layer it stays there, and the Arabs dig down through the sand to draw it out. He quotes Abu Mansur on what he heard from the Tamim about this, and on the way the hard rock beneath the sand holds the water. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.111}

He lists many such waters in the desert: the Ahsa of Banu Sa'd opposite Hajar, the Ahsa of the Judayla of Tayy at Aja, the Ahsa of Qatif, and one on the Mecca road opposite al-Hajir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 pp.111-112}
