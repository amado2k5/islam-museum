## Overview
Yazd is a populous, cheap city, thirty miles east of [[place/kath|Kath]], at the head of a desert connected to the road of Khurasan, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.430}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Yazd» (Pleiades no. 29811), classed as a settlement, with activity attested from 1200 BCE to 2000 CE. Names recorded there include Yazd, Yazd, Yezd, Yazd, Yezd. {cite:pleiades 29811}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Yazd as a middling town between Nishapur, Shiraz and Isfahan, counted among the districts of Fars and then of the district of Istakhr. Its district capital is called [[place/kath|Kutha]], and it is seventy farsakh from Shiraz. He names Abu al-Hasan Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Ja'far al-Yazdi, who transmitted from Muhammad ibn Sa'id al-Harrani. Muhammad ibn Najm al-Yazdi came to Baghdad as a pilgrim and transmitted there in Safar 560 AH at the Gate of the Stations, from Abu al-Ala Ghiyath ibn Muhammad al-Uqayli. Yaqut also records al-Yazidiyya, a town in the province of Shirwan that is also known as Shamakhi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.435}
