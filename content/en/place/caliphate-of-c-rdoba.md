## Summary
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam dates the Umayyad rule at Córdoba from 756 to the collapse of the caliphate in 1016. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e458}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam begins with the Umayyad prince Abd al-Rahman ibn Muawiyah, who, once the Umayyad dynasty had fallen in Damascus, took power over al-Andalus from Córdoba in the year 756. Cultural and scientific life there reached its height in the tenth century, in the reign of Abd al-Rahman III, who adopted the title of caliph, and of his son al-Hakam II, a well-known lover of books who supported artistic and scientific work. The Dictionary says the caliphate finally came to an end in 1016, after a run of weak caliphs and the country's political fragmentation, and that through the unsettled eleventh century, in a time of small rival states, a number of puppet caliphs appeared. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e458}
