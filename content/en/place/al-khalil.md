## Overview
al-Khalil: a town with a mosque, in which the grave of Ibrahim is mentioned, and with graves facing one another around it. It has dense mountains with trees like the olive. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.363}

## Al-Umari's visit
Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari visited it in the year 745 AH. He described its sanctuary and its daily meal, which distributed thousands of loaves to those who came. At the place of the trust's administrator, Nasir al-Din al-Tamimi al-Dari, he saw the Prophetic document concerning the grant of Hebron and Aynun. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.168-176}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes al-Khalil as a town with a fortress, buildings and a market, near Jerusalem, a day's journey apart, where the tomb of Abraham the Friend lies in a cave under the ground. A shrine stands there with visitors, attendants and hospitality for the visitors. Its original name was Hebron, and some say Hibra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 pp.387-388}

He notes what the Torah says: that the Friend bought a plot from Ephron the Hittite for four hundred silver dirhams and buried Sarah in it. He quotes al-Harawi, who entered Jerusalem in 567 AH, on the report of elders who told him that in 513 AH, in the days of King Baldwin, a part of the cave collapsed and a group of Franks entered it with the king's permission. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 pp.387-388}
