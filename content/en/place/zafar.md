## Overview
Zafar was a city which was the capital of Yahsib, and in it was the famous palace of Raydan. Most of it is now in ruin, and remnants of its people remain in it, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.152}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Ẓafār» (Pleiades no. 39423), classed as a settlement and a modern settlement, with activity attested from 30 BCE to 2100 CE. Names recorded there include Sapphar, Saphar, Tafra. {cite:pleiades 39423}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut places Zafar in the first clime, at longitude seventy-eight and latitude fifteen degrees, and says that there are two places called Zafar in Yemen. One is near Sanaa, and the onyx-like stone called al-jaz' al-zafari is named after it, and it was the seat of the kings of Himyar. He records the anecdote from al-Asma'i of an Arab who met a Himyarite king on a roof. The king said "thib", which Yaqut explains is Himyarite for "sit"; the man jumped and broke his bones, and the king said "we have no Arabic of yours here" (the word given is "arabiyyat", with the Himyarite final t). With this he records the saying "man dakhala Zafar hammara", left untranslated here because Yaqut does not gloss it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.60}
