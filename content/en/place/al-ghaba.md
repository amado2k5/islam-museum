## Overview
A place in the area of [[place/medina|Medina]], mentioned by Ibn Ishaq in [[event/raid-of-ibn-abi-hadrad-on-al-ghaba|the raid of Ibn Abi Hadrad on al-Ghaba]]: Rifa'a ibn Qays al-Jushami stopped there with a large clan of his people, and Ibn Abi Hadrad and his two companions lay in ambush around him and killed him. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/278} It is also known by its name in [[event/expedition-of-dhu-qarad|the expedition of Dhu Qarad]], in which the Prophet's herd of milch camels was raided. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/289}

## Markers of weakness and doubt
- Its exact site and its coordinates are not stated in the text (needs_source).

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records al-Ghaba: a place near Medina on the Syrian side holding property of Medina's people; it is the place named in the sabaq hadith "from al-Ghaba to such a place and from athl al-Ghaba"; in al-Zubayr's estate it had been bought for a hundred and seventy thousand and was sold from his estate for sixteen hundred thousand — some, he notes, misread it as al-Ghaya. Al-Waqidi makes al-Ghaba a barid from Medina on the Syria road, and the Prophet's minbar was made from al-Ghaba's tamarisk. Muhammad ibn al-Dahhak reported from his father that al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib would stand on Sal' and call his servants at al-Ghaba and they heard him at the last part of the night — eight miles lie between Sal' and al-Ghaba. And Muhammad ibn Musa al-Hazimi said: from the Prophet's emigration to his raid on al-Ghaba — the raid of Dhu Qarad — when al-Siba' raided his milch-camels and sought what they might eat, were five years, four months and four days. Al-Ghaba is also a village in al-Bahrayn. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.182} Under Zaghaba he reports from Ibn Ishaq that when the Prophet finished with the Trench, Quraysh advanced and camped at the meeting of the watercourses of Ruma between al-Jurf and Zaghaba; Abu Ubayd al-Bakri reads it with a damma on the zay, while al-Tabari writes "between al-Jurf and al-Ghaba" and prefers that reading because Zaghaba is not known — and Yaqut objects that Zaghaba is attested in a sound hadith. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.141}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's al-Ghaba is the record's place near Medina on the Syrian side: the Dhu Qarad raid and Rifa'a ibn Qays's camp there come from the record's other sources, while Yaqut adds the minbar's tamarisk, al-Zubayr's holding and its distances (a barid from Medina; eight miles between it and Sal' per Ibn al-Dahhak) and al-Hazimi's dating of the raid (five years, four months, four days from the Hijra); by the two records' points it lies about 15.6 km from Medina. Al-Tabari's reading "al-Ghaba" for Ibn Ishaq's Zaghaba concerns Quraysh's stand after the Trench and is a variant Yaqut transmits with his objection.
