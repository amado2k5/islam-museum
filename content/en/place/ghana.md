## Summary
Ghana the Great was two cities on the two banks of a sweet-water sea, the largest land of the Sudan in extent, the most populous and the widest in commerce; its people were Muslims and its king just in al-Idrisi's account. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.22}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Mill to the city of Ghana the Great is about twelve stages through trackless sands without water {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.22}. Ghana is two cities on the two banks of the sweet-water sea, the largest of the lands of the Sudan in extent, the greatest in population and the widest in trade; wealthy merchants repair to it from all the surrounding lands and from the whole of the farthest Maghrib {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.23}. Its people are Muslims and its king is said to be of the progeny of Salih ibn Abdallah ibn al-Hasan ibn al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib; he preaches for himself yet under the obedience of the Abbasid Commander of the Faithful, and he has a palace on the bank of the Nile of firmly joined and well-wrought construction, built in the year five hundred and ten {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.23}. The author relates that in his palace he has a gold ingot weighing thirty ratls of a single natural piece, formed complete without smelting or hammering, used as the tether of his horse, in which he boasts over all the kings of the Sudan {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.23}. He describes his justice and conduct: commanders ride to his palace each morning, each with a drum beaten over his head; the king then walks the lanes of the city, and whoever has a grievance presents it until it is settled {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.23}.
