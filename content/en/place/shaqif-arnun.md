## Overview
The castle of al-Shaqif — the Franks' Belfort — above the Litani river in Tiberias's district; 'Izz al-Din Farrukhshah took it in 578 AH, and al-Malik al-Salih Isma'il surrendered it to the Franks in 638 AH {cite:kamil-ibn-al-athir p. 110-111} {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 189}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records the name as "Shaqif Arnun", explaining that a shaqif is like a cave and that it is attached to Arnun, the name of a man who was either a Roman or a Frank. He describes it as a very strong fortress in a cave of the mountain near Banias, in the land of Damascus, between it and the coast. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.356}

Directly after it he lists other places called Shaqif and keeps them apart: Shaqif Tiron, a strong fortress near Tyre; Shaqif Dirkush, a fortress in the Aleppo region south of Harim; and Shaqif Dubbin, a small fortress near Antioch. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.356}

## The museum's identification
The name agrees: Yaqut's Shaqif Arnun is a fortress in a cave of the mountain attached to Arnun, and this suits the record's Beaufort above the Litani. His phrase "near Banias in the land of Damascus" is only a rough placing; he does not mention the Litani, Beaufort (Belfort) or Habs Jaldak, which come from the record's other sources, and joining the two descriptions is the museum's reading. Shaqif Tiron, Shaqif Dirkush and Shaqif Dubbin are other places and are not part of this record.
