## Overview
Al-Idrisi describes Shansha as a handsome city with many trees and farms, containing presses for sugar cane and general abundance. The Shansha canal reaches it from the east, and travellers pass along it to the town. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.335}

From Shansha one descends eastward to al-Buhat, twenty-four miles away. Al-Buhat is a populous town with markets and many benefits, and it has an old wall built of stone. From there Safnas lies eighteen miles off, a small civilized town. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.335}

From Safnas the traveller turns west inland to Tanah, on the east bank of the Tinnis gulf, twenty-five miles from it. Then comes Lake al-Zar, close to al-Farama. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.335}
