## Overview
The Biqaʿ is the wide land between Baalbek, Homs and Damascus that Yaqut describes in the Mu'jam al-buldan under the name Biqaʿ Kalb. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.470}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut explains the name as the plural of buqʿa, a patch of land, and says a place called Biqaʿ Kalb lies near Damascus: a wide land between Baalbek, Homs and Damascus with many villages and abundant sweet water. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.470}

He says most of the drinking water of these estates comes from a spring that issues from a mountain, called ʿAyn al-Jarr, and that the grave of the prophet Ilyas (Elijah) is in this Biqaʿ, a statement that is Yaqut's own. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.470}

The entry gives no dates and no detail on settlement; it fixes the place, its water and its shrine. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.470}
