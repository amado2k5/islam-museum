## Summary
The Bab al-Saghir cemetery is one of the oldest burial grounds of Damascus, lying south of the Bab al-Saghir, one of the city's gates. This page sets out what Yaqut al-Hamawi's *Mu'jam al-buldan* says about the gate, and about the graves that Damascenes pointed out in the cemetery. It separates what Yaqut states himself from what he reports as tradition.

## The gate and the river
Yaqut says that the Barada river enters the middle of Damascus, where some of its water feeds the city's channels and conduits, and that the rest leaves the city and waters its fields on the side of the Bab al-Saghir and the eastern gate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.378} The gate is therefore located by the water that reached the gardens from it. The passage does not describe the building of the gate.

## The graves south of the gate
In his account of Damascus Yaqut lists companions, followers and pious people whose graves were visited there. South of the Bab al-Saghir, he says, lie the grave of Bilal ibn Rabah, that of Ka'b al-Ahbar, and those of three of the Prophet's wives, whom he does not name; also the grave of Fidda, a slave-girl of Fatima, Abu al-Darda and Umm al-Darda, Fadala ibn Ubayd, Sahl ibn al-Hanzaliyya, Wathila ibn al-Asqa, Aws ibn Aws al-Thaqafi, Umm al-Hasan daughter of Ja'far al-Sadiq, Ali ibn Abdallah ibn al-Abbas, Sulayman ibn Ali ibn Abdallah ibn al-Abbas and his wife Umm al-Hasan daughter of Ali ibn Abi Talib, Khadija daughter of Zayn al-Abidin, Sakina daughter of al-Husayn, and Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.468} For Khadija daughter of Zayn al-Abidin, Yaqut adds that the sounder view is that she is buried in Medina, so her grave is not counted in Damascus on this page.

## What is tradition and what is Yaqut's view
Yaqut describes many of these graves with words such as "they narrate" or "they claim", so they are local reports of his time, not a verified survey. He also says that the grave of Umm Atika, sister of Umar ibn al-Khattab, is claimed to lie south of Damascus, and that a grave which people narrate as that of Suhayb al-Rumi and his brother is there, while the sounder report is that Suhayb is in Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.468} The names are therefore reported as tradition, attributed to Yaqut, and not as a finding that each grave exists.

## Note on the source
The part of the *Mu'jam* read here is a geographical and anecdotal description from the seventh century AH. It does not describe how the cemetery was founded or how its graves were marked. Every grave name in it is taken from report, and no other Damascene source that checks these names was read in this pass. The list stands as Yaqut's report and not as a confirmed record of the burials.
