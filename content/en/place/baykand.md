## Overview
Baykand: a city beyond the river, two farsakhs from its dependency Ramdin, according to al-Baladhuri. Ubayd Allah ibn Ziyad came to it with twenty-four thousand men, defeated the hosts of Khatun the Turk, made peace with it for a million, and entered it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name Baykand with a kasra on the first letter, a fatha on the kaf and a sukun on the nun, and describes it as a town between Bukhara and the Oxus, a stage from Bukhara, mentioned in the accounts of the conquests, once large, handsome and rich in scholars, and ruined long before his time. Quoting the author of the book of climes, he says that every town in Transoxiana has farmland and villages except Baykand alone, yet it had more ribats than he knew of in any town of the region, about a thousand by report, with a strong wall and a congregational mosque built with great care and a mihrab more ornate than any in Transoxiana. Among those named after it he lists Abu Ahmad Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Baykandi, from whom al-Bukhari narrated; the prolific hadith master Abu al-Fadl Ahmad ibn Ali al-Sulaymani al-Baykandi, who wrote over four hundred short works and died in 412; and Ismail ibn Hamdawayh Abu Said al-Baykandi, who came to Damascus in 229. He lists a separate Baykanda, a village of Tabaristan on the river Babul, as a different place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}
