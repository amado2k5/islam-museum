## Overview
Zawila is one of the cities of the Maghrib, between Barqa and Fezzan. Al-Baladhuri mentions that Uqba ibn Nafi' al-Fihri reached it in his first governorship of the Maghrib, appointed by Amr ibn al-As. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 314-315}

## What Uqba imposed on its people
Uqba imposed on its people, and on those between it and Barqa, what he saw they could bear. For the detail, see [[event/uqba-zawila-appointment|The appointment of Uqba and Zawila]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 314-315}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan

Yaqut names two towns called Zawila. The first is Zawila of the Sudan, opposite Ajdabiya across the desert, between the lands of the Sudan and Ifriqiya. Quoting al-Bakri, he calls it an unwalled town in the middle of the desert and the first border of the Sudan lands. It had a mosque, a bath and markets where travellers gathered from every direction, and from it their routes branched. It is fourteen stages from Ajdabiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160}

Yaqut records that after Amr ibn al-As took Barqa, Uqba ibn Nafi' went on as far as Zawila, so that the land between Barqa and Zawila came under Muslim rule. The poet Di'bil ibn Ali al-Khuza'i is buried at Zawila; the chroniclers say he was put to death in 220 AH, after he had satirised al-Mu'tasim. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160}

Yaqut describes how the people of Zawila guarded their town. The man on watch tied a large bundle of palm fronds to a beast, so that the fronds dragged on the ground, and rode it around the town. In the morning he and his companions mounted camels and followed any track leading out of the town, until they caught whoever had made it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160}

The second Zawila is in Tripoli, between the Maghrib and the south. Slaves were brought from it to Ifriqiya. Yaqut also mentions Zawila al-Mahdiyya, a town in Ifriqiya built by al-Mahdi Ubayd Allah, grandfather of the Fatimids who ruled Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160}
