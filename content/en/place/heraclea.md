## Summary
Heraclea is a city on the coastal road from Constantinople to Trebizond, which al-Idrisi places eight days overland from Masna. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.906}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the road hugging the coast from the city of Constantina to the coastal city of Trebizond adjoining the land of Armenia passes the city of Masna at the mouth of the gulf; from there one travels eastward along the southern shore to the island of Dafnisiya, a hundred miles — a small uninhabited island one mile from the mainland {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.906}. From this island to the mouth of the river Zaghra, a great river that carries large ships with a very wide mouth where it enters the sea; and from the mouth of this river to the city of Heraclea is sixty miles, making eight days overland from Masna to Heraclea, the whole coast being gulfs, cliffs and rugged mountains {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.906}. From Heraclea to the mouth of the river Bartanu is eighty-five miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.906}. He also names the land of Heraclea on the southern shore of the Sea of Pontus {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.905}.
