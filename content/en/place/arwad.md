## Overview
An island near Constantinople, according to al-Baladhuri. Junada ibn Abi Umayya conquered it in the year 54 AH, and Mu'awiya settled Muslims in it. Among its conquerors were Mujahid and Tabi', son of the wife of Ka'b al-Ahbar. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Arwad: an island in the sea near Constantinople; the Muslims opened it in 54 AH under Janada ibn Abi Umayya in the days of Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan, and Mu'awiya settled it. Among its openers were Mujahid ibn Jabr the Qur'an-reader and Tubay' the son of Ka'b al-Ahbar's wife; there Mujahid taught Tubay' the Qur'an — though it is also said he taught him at Rhodes. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.162} He repeats the same notice inside his "Aqrītish" entry, citing Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir (al-Baladhuri): Janada ibn Abi Umayya al-Azdi raided Crete after he had opened the island of Arwad in 54 AH in the days of Mu'awiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.236}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Arwad is the record's island: his report of its opening in 54 AH under Janada and its settlement by Mu'awiya is the same al-Baladhuri account the record carries, in fuller wording, and adds that Mujahid taught Tubay' the Qur'an there — or, in another saying, at Rhodes. "Near Constantinople" is the source's own phrase; the museum's Arwad point lies about 4.0 km off its Tartus point on the Syrian coast, the island's known location.
