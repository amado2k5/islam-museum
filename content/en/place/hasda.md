## Summary
Hasda is a village al-Idrisi mentions on the Tihama road; it has a well of little water and few inhabitants. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}

## Details
Al-Idrisi, tracing the halts of this road, says: then to Bisha Yaqzan, a small civilized town of good dwellings and pleasant site with visible water and a few palms; then to the village of Hasda, which has a well of little water and few inhabitants; then to Bayat, a great village with many people and many palms and a spring of sweet water; then to Sabkha, a desolate halt with no inhabitants; then to Kutna, a great village with springs, vineyards and tall palms {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}. Hasda is thus one of the small caravan halts in that part of the land of Yemen, its people's water a well sufficient for the few who dwell there, lying between Bisha Yaqzan and great Bayat on the chain of successive stations that al-Idrisi describes in this passage of his book {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.146}.
