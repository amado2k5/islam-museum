## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name Filastin with its vowels, notes that the Arabs decline it in two ways, and gives the adjective Filastī. He calls it the last of the districts of Syria on the Egyptian side, with Bayt al-Maqdis as its capital and Ascalon, Ramla, Gaza, Arsuf, Caesarea, Nablus, Jericho, Amman, Jaffa and Bayt Jibrin among its well-known cities. For its bounds he reports that it is the first of the Syrian military districts from the west, three days' ride long from Rafah to al-Lajjun and about three days wide from Jaffa to Jericho, that Zughar and the Sharat mountains as far as Ayla are joined to its jund, and that it is mostly hills with little plain. For the name he lists several accounts tracing it to a man called Filastin or Fulayshin of Noah's line, with differing genealogies. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.274} He cites Ibn al-Kalbi that the holy land and the blessed land of the Qur'an are Filastin, and al-Bashari that a village in Iraq is also called Filastin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.275}
