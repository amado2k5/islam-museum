## Summary
Faruy is a city on the skirt of a mountain that overlooks it, which al-Idrisi places one day from Thumiyans in the land of the Greeks. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.796}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Thumiyans to the city of Faruy is one day, that Faruy is a city on the skirt of a mountain overlooking it, that from Faruy eastward to the city of Constantina is one hundred and sixty miles, and from Faruy to Lightlaghu westward one day — Lightlaghu being a large populous city on the summit of a mountain {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.796}. He adds that between this Faruy and the city of Constantina in the desert, between west and south, is forty miles, and that between Faruy and the mountain adjoining it on the north is twenty miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.796}. In its neighbourhood he mentions the river Akhilun, which is crossed between Adharnubli and Lumiyans {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.796}. It is thus one of the mountain-foot cities on the western approach to Constantinople. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.796}
