## Summary
An oasis north of Medina, which was home to Jewish communities and the target of the Islamic campaign of 628 CE.

## In the introduction to "The Sealed Nectar"
Many Jewish tribes settled in Yathrib, Khaybar and Tayma after the Romans occupied Palestine in 70 CE, and built villages, towers and fortresses there. Khaybar was one of the well-known Jewish tribes when Islam came {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.40} {cite:qalb-jazirat-al-arab p.151}. The Banu Sulaym lived from the Wadi al-Qura to Khaybar and east of Medina {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.22}. See [[concept/judaism-in-arabia|Judaism in the Arabian Peninsula]].

## For Sprouts
Khaybar is a green oasis north of Medina, with forts and palm groves where a Jewish community lived long ago. The Muslims took it in 628 CE, and its people kept farming the land until they were moved away later. Old fort ruins still stand among its fields today.

## For Explorers
Khaybar is a water-rich oasis about 150 km north of Medina, where Jewish communities lived in forts scattered among the palm groves — the best known were Nata, al-Shiqq and al-Katiba. The Prophet conquered it in 7 AH (628 CE), soon after the Hudaybiyya truce, taking the forts one by one; the hardest fighting was at the Qamus fort, taken by Ali. The Prophet let the people stay and farm their land in return for half the harvest, until Umar ibn al-Khattab later removed the Jews of Khaybar for reasons the sources describe differently. Some families remained into the twentieth century, and the fort ruins still stand among the palms today.

## For Scholars
Khaybar is a cluster of oases on volcanic soil some 150 km north of Medina — the richest farmland in the Hijaz — whose seventh-century inhabitants were Jewish communities spread across villages and forts including Nata, al-Shiqq, al-Katiba, al-Watih and al-Sulalim. The Prophet took it in Safar 7 AH (628 CE) on returning from al-Hudaybiyya, capturing the forts in turn; the most famous, the Qamus, fell to Ali ibn Abi Talib, and Marhab was killed in the duel of the well-known account. The inhabitants were not expelled: the Prophet confirmed them on their land for half the produce 'of what God brings forth', and divided the spoil among the combatants with shares for his household. The Jews of Khaybar stayed until Umar ibn al-Khattab removed them during his caliphate — the sources say in fulfilment of the Prophet's wish that two religions not share the Arabian peninsula, and they differ on the date and reasons. In fiqh Khaybar became the model sharecropping contract (muzara'a), and in history the early example of an agricultural treaty.
