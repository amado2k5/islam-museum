## Overview
Shibam is one of the two cities of Hadramawt, a strong fort holding its people on the summit of the mountain of Shibam, a mountain so strong that its top is reached only with effort. At the top are many populous villages, fields, running water, crops, palms and great fertility. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 154}

## The stones
In this mountain are found agate, amethyst and onyx stones. When found they are stones covered with thick coatings that only those who seek them know by their marks; they are carried off and, once worked and polished, show their beauty and clear substance. The seekers say they find these stones in gravelly valleys whose pebbles are coloured in many fine hues, pick them out and bring them to the craftsmen, and merchants export them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 154}

## The road
From Marib to the city of Shibam in Hadramawt is four stages, and the two cities of Hadramawt are [[place/tarim|Tarim]] and Shibam. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 154} The land of al-Shihr adjoins Hadramawt on the east. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 154}

## The name
Shibam is a city in the land of [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]], with another city about a stage from it, and the two stand on the ascending road from [[place/aden|Aden]], according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 56} Al-Hamdani derives its name from the people of [[place/shabwa|Shabwa]], who left and settled in Hadramawt when Himyar and Madhhij fought each other; he holds that the original form was "shabah", and then the mim replaced the ha'. {cite:sifat-jazirat-al-arab p. 70}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer lists this place under no. 2751839 with the preferred name «S̲h̲ibām», classed as archaeological site and populated place; its parent in the hierarchy is «Ḥaḍramawt (muḥāfaẓāt)», classed as administrative unit. ## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a kasra on the first letter, and notes that in the language shibam is a stick placed across a kid's mouth to stop it suckling, while shabam means cold. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.318} He quotes al-Hamdani's description of Mount Shibam near Sanaa, then reports, from a man of Shibam, that Yemen has four places of the name: Shibam Kawkaban, west of Sanaa; Shibam Sukhaym, east-southeast of Sanaa about three farsakhs away; Shibam Haraz, west toward the south, two days' travel; and Shibam of Hadramawt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.318}

Of Shibam of Hadramawt he says it is one of the two cities of Hadramawt, the other being Tarim, and that his informant had seen all these places. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.318} Citing Umara al-Yamani, he reports that Husayn ibn Abi Salama, a Nubian slave who was vizier to Abu al-Jaysh ibn Ziyad, ruler of Yemen, built large congregational mosques and tall minarets and dug wells from Hadramawt toward Mecca; the first of these works were at Shibam and Tarim, and the building continued from them to Aden over twenty stages, each with a mosque, a minaret and a well. Husayn held power in Yemen for thirty years and died in 432. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.318}

Yaqut adds his own remark that the name belongs to a Yemeni tribe and that these places are named for clans of it, and quotes Ibn al-Kalbi that among the sons of As'ad ibn Jusham ibn Hashid of Hamdan was Abd Allah, who is Shibam. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.318}
