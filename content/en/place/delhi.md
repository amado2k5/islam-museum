## Overview
A city in northern India. It was the capital of the Delhi Sultanate from the thirteenth century CE, and then the capital of the Mughals. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

An additional source is needed for the city's history.

## In Ibn Battuta's Rihla
In the second part of his Rihla [[person/ibn-battuta-muhammad|Ibn Battuta]] gives Delhi sections on its description, walls, gates, congregational mosque, shrines and scholars, and on the kings who held it. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.568} He describes the sultan's palace there, "Dar Sara", with its many gates; at the first sat players of trumpets, horns and shawms who sounded them whenever an amir or notable arrived, and outside it were benches where the executioners sat. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.355}

He relates that Sultan [[person/muhammad-ibn-tughluq|Muhammad Shah]] moved its people to Dawlatabad, forty days away, after they had written insulting notes and thrown them into the audience hall at night: he bought their houses and ordered them to move, they left their belongings behind, and the city was left "empty"; then he wrote to people of other towns to move there and repopulate it, which ruined their towns while Delhi was not refilled "because of its vastness and size, for it is one of the greatest cities in the world". {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.385} He says that when he entered it he found it empty but for a little habitation. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.385} There the sultan made him a judge of the Maliki school. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-khayriyya-1904 p.412}
