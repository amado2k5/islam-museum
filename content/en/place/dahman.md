## Summary
Dahman is a village that al-Idrisi mentions on the road from al-Yamama to al-Basra, one stage beyond the fortress of Kazima. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that Kazima is an impregnable fortress on a lofty-peaked mountain, and that travellers pass these four stages in the company of the Arabs, halting at waters, wells and springs; from Kazima to the village of Dahman is one stage, then to al-Basra one stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. He adds that the whole of this road from al-Yamama to al-Basra is fifteen stages, from al-Yamama to al-Bahrayn about thirteen stages, and likewise from al-Yamama to Oman about thirteen stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. Dahman is thus a small halting-place on the caravan road between al-Yamama and al-Basra, through which travellers pass between Kazima and al-Basra in stages counted by marches, one of the stops on which the people of that road rely for their halts and their water {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}.
