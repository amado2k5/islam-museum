## Overview
Qumar is an island to which the traders of aloes-wood (oud) come. It has the "sinfi" aloes-wood, sandalwood and rice. Al-Idrisi mentions that its people wear loincloths (futa), are known for justice and fairness, and burn their dead with fire. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.83}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the Qumari aloes-wood takes its name from this island, where it is known. Traders who reach the island buy it from its people and then carry it to all lands. It is good wood, but the "sinfi" aloes-wood is better. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.83}

He describes how the wood is taken. It is cut from the roots at a set season, after its branches have been cut some months before. The top is carved, the soft part removed and the hard core extracted. It is then scraped and polished a great deal before being packed into containers. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.83}

The island also has sandalwood and rice. Its people wear loincloths, sit with the merchants, and are known for clear justice and full fairness. Their custom is to burn their dead with fire. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.83}

Five days' sailing separate Qumar from the island of Tanuma. Along the coast lies the island of Sanf, three miles away, which produces the "sinfi" aloes-wood. That wood is held to be better than the Qumari kind because of its quality and weight, so that it sinks in water. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.83}
