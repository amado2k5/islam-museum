## Overview
al-Hadath: a frontier port on the Jazira side, at the Hadath pass. The Umayyads called it al-Salama, seeking a good omen. Habib ibn Maslama conquered its old fortress in the time of Umar. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.267-269}

## Building the city
al-Hasan ibn Qahtaba returned from a raid in the year 162 AH and chose the site for the city. al-Mahdi ordered that it be built before Tarsus, and Ali ibn Sulayman ibn Ali built it of mud brick. It was named al-Muhammadiyya, then al-Mahdiyya and al-Hamdiyya, because al-Mahdi died when it was finished in the year 169 AH. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.267-269}

Four thousand fighters were assigned to it from al-Sham, the Jazira and Khurasan, with a stipend of forty dinars and three hundred dirhams for each man. Two thousand men were moved to it from Malatya, Shimshat, Samosata, Kaysum, Dulak and Ruban. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.267-269}

Its building collapsed under the snow and the Byzantines took it. Then al-Rashid rebuilt and fortified it and garrisoned it with men. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 pp.267-269}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes al-Hadath as a strong fortress among the frontier posts between Malatya, Sumaysat and Mar'ash, also called al-Hamra' because its soil is red; its citadel stands on a mountain called al-Uhaydib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.227} Citing the book of Ahmad ibn Yahya ibn Jabir, he says the fort was taken in Umar's time by Habib ibn Maslama al-Fihri on behalf of Iyad ibn Ghanm, and that the Umayyads called the Darb al-Hadath "Darb al-Salama" as a good omen because Muslims had suffered losses there; he gives as another explanation of the name a youth (ghulam hadath) who fought the Muslims at the pass. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.227}

He then tells of the founding of the city: after al-Hasan ibn Qahtaba's raid, al-Mahdi ordered in 162 AH that it be built there, and Ali ibn Sulayman, then governor of the Jazira and Qinnasrin, raised it in mud brick; it was named al-Muhammadiyya and al-Mahdiyya, and al-Mahdi died in 169 as it was finished. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.227} Four thousand fighters were settled in it and two thousand men were moved there from Malatya, Sumaysat, Shimshat, Kaysum, Duluk and Ra'ban. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.228} Citing al-Waqidi, he reports that winter rains brought down the weak wall and the Romans occupied the town, until al-Rashid restored it and garrisoned it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.228} Yaqut adds that he found nothing further until the days of Sayf al-Dawla ibn Hamdan, who rebuilt it in 343 AH and drove the Domestic (al-Dumustuq) back in defeat; he quotes verses by al-Mutanabbi and Ibn Kujak on this, and then lists some hadith transmitters named after the town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.228}
