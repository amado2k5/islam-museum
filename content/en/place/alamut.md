## Overview
Alamut: the Ismaili fortress of the Batiniyya in the mountains near Qazvin, the home of their leader al-Hasan ibn al-Sabbah. The sources report that the armies withdrew from the siege of the Batiniyya at Alamut in the year 511 AH, when news reached them of the death of the sultan Muhammad ibn Malikshah. {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 505}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes Alamut as a fortress perched in the mountains of northern Iran, about forty-five kilometres north-east of [[place/qazvin|Qazvin]], standing on a peak of unreachable rock in the middle of the Alborz range. It reports a legend that an eagle showed a Daylami ruler the site, which would explain the name in the Daylami dialect, and says a Justanid ruler of Daylam built the fortress in 860, after which the area came under Zaydi Alid influence. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e102}

In 1090 [[person/hasan-i-sabbah|Hasan-i Sabbah]] seized it and made it the seat of his Nizari Ismaili state and the base of his challenge to Seljuk power in Iran; the Dictionary adds that it became notorious for its assassins. The Mongols captured it in 1256, demolished part of it and burned its well-known library; the Safavids restored it and used it as a prison into the seventeenth century, after which it was left to the weather and to treasure-seekers. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e102} The siege of 511 AH told in the record above is a separate episode.
