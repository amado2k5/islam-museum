## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Khurasan as a wide land. Its western edge, facing Iraq, lies at Azadwar, the seat of Juwayn and Bayhaq; its eastern edge, facing India, reaches Tukharistan, Ghazna, Sistan and Kirman. Its great cities include Nishapur, Herat and Merv, which was its capital in earlier times, together with Balkh, Talaqan, Nasa, Abiward and Sarakhs. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.350}

He gives several accounts of the name. Some say that Khurasan and Haytal, two sons of Alim son of Sam son of Noah, each settled a land, one beyond the Oxus and the other on this side of it, and that each place took the name of its settler. Others take khur to mean the sun in Persian and asan to mean origin or place, and others take it to mean a plain. He notes several forms of the adjective. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.350}

He records the conquest in the year 31 AH, in the days of Uthman, under Abd Allah ibn Amir, and says most of its cities were taken by force or by treaty. He quotes al-Baladhuri's division of the land into four quarters, which includes Transoxiana, and explains that this wider reckoning arose because all these lands were under one governor of Khurasan. Yaqut holds that the correct limit is the one he gave first, and that the lands beyond the Oxus were a separate province of the Hephthalites. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.350} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.351}
