## Overview
Arjish, the medieval name behind Erciş, is an old town near Khilat in Greater Armenia that Yaqut records in the Mu'jam al-buldan, with a second entry for the lake of Khilat that carried the name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.144}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut calls Arjish an ancient town of the districts of Greater Armenia near Khilat, most of whose people are Armenian Christians. He gives its position in degrees in the manner of the geographers: sixty-six degrees and a third and a quarter of length, and forty degrees and a third and a quarter of breadth. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.144}

He names as its nisba the pious jurist Abu al-Hasan ʿAli ibn Muhammad ibn Mansur ibn Dawud al-Arjishi, born at Abu Ishaq's khanqah in the Arjish district, who studied Shafiʿi law and lived in Aleppo as a devotee at the Zajjajin school, content with little and refusing anything beyond twelve dirhams. Yaqut says he met him and stayed with him in that school, and found him devout and given to silence; he mentions him because he admired his way of life. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.144}

A separate entry under the same name describes the lake of Khilat, where the tarikh fish is caught. Yaqut quotes Ibn al-Kalbi that it is among the wonders of Armenia: for ten months of the year no frog or fish is seen in it, and for two months fish appear, can be taken by hand and are carried to distant lands. He relays a report that Qubadh sent Balinas to cast a talisman on it, and then comments that this is the raving of the Persians and that there is a hidden cause. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.350}

From the Kitab al-Futuh he relays that Habib ibn Maslama al-Fihri, sent by ʿUthman ibn ʿAffan, came to Arjish, sent forces over its districts, took the poll tax and agreed the land tax with the people, but did not touch the tarikh lake, which stayed open to all. The report continues on the next page into the time of Muhammad ibn Marwan and is not summarised here. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.350}
