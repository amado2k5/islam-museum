## Overview
A Jaziran city on the flank of an overturned mountain, girdled by a high citadel on a hillock; it was the seat of the Bektakinid kingdom in the sixth and seventh hijri centuries — its most famous king Muzaffar al-Din Kukburi — and the Tatars took it by the sword in 634 AH {cite:shadharat-dhahab p. 162}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
[[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]] vocalizes the name Irbil, on the pattern of *ithmid*, saying the hamza cannot take *a*, and suggests that, if the name is Arabic, it may derive from *rabl*, a plant that puts out green leaves without rain, or *ribal*, dense tangled vegetation, as the months were named after the conditions they fell in. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.176} For him it is a strong citadel and a large town in a wide open plain; the citadel, with a deep moat, stands on a high, broad-topped mound of earth and holds markets, houses and a mosque, like the citadel of Aleppo but larger; it lies between the two Zabs, counted among the dependencies of Mosul, two days away. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.176} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.177}

He says that in his day a large town had grown up in the citadel's suburb, its walls, markets and covered bazaars built by the amir [[person/muzaffar-al-din-kokburi|Muzaffar al-Din Kökböri, son of Zayn al-Din Ali Küçük]], so that strangers flocked there and it became one of the great cities. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.177} Despite its size, he says, it was more like a village than a city; most of its people were Kurds who had taken up Arabic; it was seven days from Baghdad by caravan; it had no gardens or running river, most crops being watered by underground channels; its well water was as sweet as the Tigris; and its fruit came from the nearby mountains. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.177} He says that when he visited he found no man of learning there except Abu al-Barakat al-Mubarak ibn Ahmad, known as al-Mustawfi, a man of letters who was close to the ruler and held a post like a vizierate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.177} These judgements are Yaqut's.
