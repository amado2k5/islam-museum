## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes Ceuta as a well-known town among the chief cities of the Maghrib, with one of the best harbours on the sea. It lies on the Berber shore facing the island of al-Andalus at the narrowest point of the strait, and it is a strong town, said to resemble al-Mahdiyya in Ifriqiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.182}

He notes that it is ten days from Fez, and that a number of scholars were called after it, among them Ibn Marana al-Sabti, who knew arithmetic, the rules of inheritance, geometry and law better than most. He also reports that al-Mu'tamid ibn Abbad said he would have liked to have three people from Ceuta with him: Ibn Ghazi the preacher, Ibn Ata the secretary, and Ibn Marana the expert in inheritance. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.182} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.183}
