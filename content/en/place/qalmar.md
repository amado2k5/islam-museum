## Overview
Qalmar is a large city in the land of Finmark, at the far north on the Dark Sea. Al-Idrisi describes it in the seventh climate among the few inhabited places of that region. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953}

## Distances
From Saqtun, which stands on the river Qatlu, to Qalmar is two hundred miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.950} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.951} From Qalmar westward to Saqtun is also two hundred miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953}

## The people
Apart from Qalmar and the city of Abura, Finmark has no inhabited places. Both are large cities, but nomadism prevails over them and hardship is evident among their people. The food they have is less than they need, and the rain falls on them constantly. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953}

## Access to the islands
The approach to the people of the sea islands is closest from the city of Inhu. They may also be reached from Qalmar and from Raghuldda. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.956} Three channels lie between the islands and them, and few who enter the area reach them, because the sea is often clouded and very dark, with no light on it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.956}

## Surroundings
The lands around Qalmar are thinly settled. Al-Idrisi does not name a ruler there, and describes the country as a mix of empty northern land and scattered villages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.953} Al-Idrisi does not give a modern identification for the city, and this entry does not supply one.
