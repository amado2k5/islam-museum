## Overview
Tustar is a town in Khuzistan, set on raised ground, beside the Tustar river, the largest river of the province, which is also called the Dujayl of Ahwaz. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 393} Sabur the king built the Shadhurwan at its gate, a dam of stone, iron and lead across the river's bed, so that the water rose up to the town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 791} Tustar made fine cloth and turbans, and the grave of al-Bara ibn Malik al-Ansari is there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 791} Al-Baladhuri relates that Jaban, one of the great men of the Persians, gathered a large host at Tustar. When this reached Khalid, he sent al-Muthanna ibn Haritha and Hanzala the scribe against him, and at their approach Jaban fled. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 343} In the caliphate of Umar, Abu Musa al-Ash'ari marched on Tustar, where the enemy was strong, and asked Umar for help; Umar ordered Ammar ibn Yasir to march with the people of Kufa. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 535} The people of Tustar fought hard. Al-Bara ibn Malik fell at the gate, and Hurmuzan entered the city. Yaqut gives the battle losses as nine hundred killed and six hundred taken. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 535} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 792} Abu Musa would grant Hurmuzan safety only on Umar's judgement, and Hurmuzan was brought to Umar, who spared him and set him a stipend. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 537} Some reports say Tustar was taken by treaty and then the terms were broken; others say Abu Musa took Sus, Tustar and Dawraq by force. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 537} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 538}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Tustar with damma then sukun: today the greatest city of Khuzistan, the arabicised form of Shushtar. He transmits al-Zajjaji's tale that it was named for Tustar ibn Nun, a man of Banu Ijl who conquered it, and dismisses it as worthless; the correct account, he says, is Hamza al-Isfahani's: Shushtar is Shush with dotted shins, meaning the pleasant, the good, the lovely, and the -tar carries the comparative sense — "more pleasant" — as buzurg gives buzurg-tar. Susa was laid out in the shape of a falcon, Tustar in the shape of a horse, and Jundi-Sabur like a chessboard. Khuzistan has many rivers and the greatest is the river of Tustar, on which king Sabur built the Shadhrawan at Tustar's gate until its water rose to the city, which stands on high ground; that Shadhrawan is one of the wonders of building, near a mile long, of dressed stone and rock with iron columns and lead plates, and it is said no structure on earth is more solid. At Tustar is the tomb of al-Bara' ibn Malik al-Ansari; fine cloth and turbans were made there; Ibn al-Muqaffa' says the first walls set on earth after the Flood were those of Susa and Tustar, their builder unknown. The men of Kufa and Basra disputed its allegiance before Umar ibn al-Khattab, who assigned it to Basra for nearness; and al-Baladhuri's conquest account has Abu Musa al-Ash'ari march on Tustar from Surraq, where the enemy's strength gathered, Umar sending the Kufans under Ammar ibn Yasir with Jarir ibn Abdallah al-Bajali at their head. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.29-30}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Tustar is the record's Shushtar, capital of Khuzistan in his day; the Shadhrawan, the horse-shaped plan, the rejection of the Tustar ibn Nun etymology and the Kufa–Basra dispute are all reported as printed.
