## Overview
Tustar is a town in Khuzistan, set on raised ground, beside the Tustar river, the largest river of the province, which is also called the Dujayl of Ahwaz. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 393} Sabur the king built the Shadhurwan at its gate, a dam of stone, iron and lead across the river's bed, so that the water rose up to the town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 791} Tustar made fine cloth and turbans, and the grave of al-Bara ibn Malik al-Ansari is there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 791} Al-Baladhuri relates that Jaban, one of the great men of the Persians, gathered a large host at Tustar. When this reached Khalid, he sent al-Muthanna ibn Haritha and Hanzala the scribe against him, and at their approach Jaban fled. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 343} In the caliphate of Umar, Abu Musa al-Ash'ari marched on Tustar, where the enemy was strong, and asked Umar for help; Umar ordered Ammar ibn Yasir to march with the people of Kufa. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 535} The people of Tustar fought hard. Al-Bara ibn Malik fell at the gate, and Hurmuzan entered the city. Yaqut gives the battle losses as nine hundred killed and six hundred taken. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 535} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 792} Abu Musa would grant Hurmuzan safety only on Umar's judgement, and Hurmuzan was brought to Umar, who spared him and set him a stipend. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 537} Some reports say Tustar was taken by treaty and then the terms were broken; others say Abu Musa took Sus, Tustar and Dawraq by force. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 537} {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 538}
