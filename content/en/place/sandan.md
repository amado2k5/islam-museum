## Overview
Sandan is a large, civilised city frequented by wealthy merchants. It is extensive in area and abundant in crops, palms and coconuts, according to al-Idrisi. To its east is an island which bears its name. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.182}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with fatha on the first letter and a final nun, quotes Nasr that it is the capital of the land of India, and says he does not know what Nasr meant, since a capital in common usage is the greatest city of a district. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.266} He says he knows of no city in India called Sandan that could rank as such a capital; Sandan, he says, is a city adjoining Sind, about ten stages from Daybul and al-Mansura, and nothing described of it suggests it deserves to be India's capital. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.267} He adds that it lies about half a farsakh from the sea and about fifteen stages from Saymur, and cites a line of al-Buhturi that mentions it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.267}
