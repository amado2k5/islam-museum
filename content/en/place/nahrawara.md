## Overview
Nahrawara is a city in the land of India, ruled by a great king named Balhara. It lies on a continuous plain without mountains, eight stages from Burj, and the road between them is travelled by ox-drawn carts, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says the road from Burj to Nahrawara runs eight stages across a plain with no hills. Travel is by ox-drawn carts, which carry the travellers' goods. Between Burj and Nahrawara lie two towns, Junawal and Dulqa, similar in size and character. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}

Its king has armies and elephants, which are the core of his war. He wears a gold crown on his head and robes woven with gold, and he rides horses on ordinary days. Once a week he rides out with about a hundred women around him, dressed in gilded tunics and wearing gold and silver bracelets, and no one walks beside him except them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.188}

His viziers ride with him only when he goes out to fight a rebel, a governor who has wronged his agents, or a neighbouring king who attacks his land. The rule is hereditary in name and line, and it passes from his ancestors without change. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.188}
