## Overview
Sumaysat (Samosata) on the Euphrates is one of the districts of [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]]. Iyad sent to it Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal and Habib ibn Maslama, who prevailed over some of its villages and fortresses. Then its people made peace with him on the terms of the people of al-Ruha. Then they broke those terms, and Iyad returned to them and besieged them until he conquered it, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vowels the name with a damma on the first letter and a fatha on the second, and says Sumaysat is a city on the shore of the Euphrates at the edge of the lands of Rum, on the west bank, with a citadel on one side of it inhabited by Armenians; in his time it was held by al-Malik al-Afdal Ali, son of al-Malik al-Nasir Yusuf ibn Ayyub, that is Saladin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.258} He quotes a verse of al-Mutanabbi that mentions it and gives its coordinates as a longitude of 54 and two thirds degrees and a latitude of 36 and a third, adding that the zij of Abu Awn places it in the fourth climate. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.258} He names as its nisba-bearer Abu al-Qasim Ali ibn Muhammad al-Sumaysati al-Sulami, known as al-Jumaysh, who died in Damascus in Rabi' II 453 AH and was buried in his house at the Bab al-Natifaniyyin. That house he had endowed for poor Muslims and Sufis, its upper part for the mosque, and most of his wealth for works of piety; Yaqut reports from Ibn al-Akfani's Kitab al-Aradat that he was born in Ramadan 377 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.258}
