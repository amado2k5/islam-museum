## Overview
Ayn Zarba: one of the frontier cities of al-Sham. al-Baladhuri has reports about its building and fortification.

## Building
al-Rashid ordered its city to be built and fortified in the year 180 AH. He summoned to it a group of people from Khurasan and others, and assigned them dwellings there. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.234}

## Raids on it
The Byzantines raided it while al-Qasim ibn al-Rashid was stationed at Dabiq, and they drove off the livestock of its people and took some captive. The people of al-Massisa and its volunteers rushed out against them and recovered what had been taken. al-Qasim sent from it to fortify and repair it and to increase its garrison. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.234}

## Afterwards
al-Mu'tasim settled in it a group of the Zutt, who had overwhelmed the marshes between Wasit and Basra, and its people benefited from them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.235}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut's main entry is under the spelling Ayn Zarbi, with a fatha on the zay, a sukun on the ra', a ba' and a short alif; he suggests the name may come from the word for a sheep pen. He calls it a town of the frontier in the district of al-Masisa. On the authority of Ibn al-Faqih, its renewal and building were the work of Abu Sulayman the Turk, the servant al-Rashid had put over the frontiers, in about the year 190. Then the Byzantines took it and ruined it, and Sayf al-Dawla ibn Hamdan spent three million dirhams to rebuild it, after which the Byzantines took it again in his days, as he says he told under Tarsus; he writes that it is still in their hands, that its people are now Armenians, and that it belongs to the lands of Ibn Layun. Two scholars are named for it: Abu Muhammad Isma'il ibn Ali the poet al-Ayn Zarbi, of whom Yaqut gives two lines of verse, and Muhammad ibn Yunus ibn Hashim al-Ayn Zarbi, a reciter known as al-Iskaf, who counted the verses of the Qur'an and died on 18 Dhu al-Hijja 411. Then, in a passage that opens with al-Waqidi's name, he says that in 180 al-Rashid ordered the building and fortifying of the city of Ayn Zarbi, summoned a levy from Khurasan and others and gave them house-lots; the passage goes on to al-Mu'tasim's days, when some of the Zutt who had overrun the marshes between Wasit and Basra were moved to it and its surroundings, to the benefit of the frontier people. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.177-178}

A short entry under Zarba sends the reader to the entries under 'ayn, and calls it a frontier town near al-Masisa. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.136} Under the frontier (al-Thaghr) he counts one stage from al-Masisa to Ayn Zarba and one from al-Masisa to Adhana. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.79} Under Adhana he reports Ibn al-Faqih on the building of that town in 190 by Abu Salim, a Turkish servant of al-Rashid whom he made governor of the frontiers, who also built Tarsus and Ayn Zarba; in that entry the name is Abu Salim, in the Ayn Zarbi entry Abu Sulayman, and the museum keeps both as they stand. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.133} Under Sisiya he says that it lies on Ayn Zarba. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.297}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Baladhuri, describes al-Rashid's order in 180, the levy of Khurasanis and others with their house-lots, the Byzantine raid in the time of al-Qasim, and al-Mu'tasim's settling of the Zutt. Yaqut has the 180 order, the Khurasani levy and the Zutt, but he gives them in a passage opened by al-Waqidi's name, and the part on al-Mu'tasim, which cannot be al-Waqidi's own words because he died before that reign, follows without a new name; the museum does not know whether Yaqut took the passage from al-Baladhuri, whom he does not name here; he adds the renewal in about 190 by Abu Sulayman on the word of Ibn al-Faqih, the later losses to the Byzantines and the repair under Sayf al-Dawla, none of which is in the record. The two reports do not contradict one another on their face, since one speaks of ordering a city and the other of renewing and building it, but Yaqut does not reconcile them. The record's label "Anazarbus" and the identification with the site it names in its coordinate note, Dilekkaya in the province of Adana, are the museum's own and are not in Yaqut. The record's point (37.2486, 35.9011) lies about 41 km from the museum's Masisa point and about 58 km from its Adhana point; Yaqut's "one stage" from al-Masisa is of the order of that straight-line figure, and straight lines and stages are loose measures.
