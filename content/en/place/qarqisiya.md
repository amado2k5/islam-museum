## Overview
Qarqisiya, at the confluence of the Khabur with the Euphrates, is one of the districts of [[place/jazira|al-Jazira]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

Al-Baladhuri relates that Iyad sent [[person/habib-ibn-maslama|Habib ibn Maslama]] al-Fihri to it, and he conquered it by treaty on the terms of the treaty of [[place/raqqa|Raqqa]]. Then its people broke the covenant, and [[person/umayr-ibn-sad|Umayr ibn Sa'd]] made peace with them on the same terms as their first treaty, when he took the Khabur route to the fortresses of the Euphrates. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

He mentions that the rahba (courtyard) below it had no ancient trace, and was built in the caliphate of al-Ma'mun. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

An additional source is needed: the dates are not established.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut vowels the name Qarqisiya' with a fatha, then a quiescent qaf, another qaf, a quiescent ya', a broken sin, another ya' and a long alif — it is also said with one ya'. From Hamza al-Isfahani he says Qarqisiya is the Arabicised Kirkisiya, taken from kirkis, the name of a horse-racing course the Arabs call al-halba. He records that Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, while at al-Mada'in in year 16, dispatched an army to Hit and Qarqisiya under Amr ibn Malik al-Zuhri, and they submitted to his judgement; he quotes Amr's verses on the march to Qarqisiya and the people's acceptance of the jizya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.328} For him it is a town on the Khabur river near Rahbat Malik ibn Tawq at six farsakhs, at the Khabur's outlet into the Euphrates, lying in the triangle between the two rivers; it is said to be named for Qarqisiya son of Tahmurath the king. From Ptolemy he gives longitude 64 degrees 45 minutes and latitude 35 degrees, fourth clime; from the Zij's author, longitude 64 degrees and latitude 33 and a quarter. When Iyad ibn Ghanm conquered the Jazira in year nineteen he sent Habib ibn Maslama al-Fihri to Qarqisiya, who took it on the like of the al-Raqqa treaty; when Iyad died and Umayr ibn Sa'd governed the Jazira and Ra's Ayn he worked the Khabur and what lies along it until he reached Qarqisiya, whose people had broken faith, so he re-compounded with them on their first treaty. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.328-329}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Qarqisiya is the record's town at the Khabur's confluence with the Euphrates near al-Rahba; his conquest account — Habib ibn Maslama in year 19 on al-Raqqa's terms, then Umayr ibn Sa'd's re-treaty after the breach — is what the record tells from al-Baladhuri in much the same shape, and Yaqut adds Sa'd's army of year 16 under Amr ibn Malik al-Zuhri with its link to Hit, the name's vocalisation, the naming opinions and the astronomical figures.
