## Overview
Baniqya: a town in the Sawad, according to al-Baladhuri. Jarir ibn Abd Allah al-Bajali made peace with it for a thousand dirhams and a cloak. It is one of three towns of the people of the Sawad that had a covenant, together with al-Hira and Alis, according to the saying of Ibn Mughaffal al-Muzani. Dirar ibn al-Azur said a poem about it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Baniqya, with a broken nun: a district in the environs of al-Kufa mentioned in the conquest accounts and in the tales of Abraham the Friend; he relates from the tales of Abraham that he stopped at Baniqya, which was twelve farsakhs long, and that its people used to be shaken every night, but on the night he lodged with them they were not shaken. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.331} He then quotes Ahmad ibn Yahya (al-Baladhuri) in the Futuh: Khalid ibn al-Walid sent Jarir ibn Abdallah to Baniqya; Sabahri ibn Saluba came out to him, excused himself, and made peace for a thousand dirhams and a taylasan — saying that no people of the Sawad held a covenant except the people of al-Hira, Ulays and Baniqya, which is why they said no land below the mountain could rightly be sold except the land of the Banu Saluba and the land of al-Hira. He records Ishaq ibn Bashir that al-Walid marched from al-Hira to Saluba the lord of Baniqya and Samiya and treated for a thousand dirhams of six-weight, writing them a document still known among them, and that when he camped at Baniqya on the Euphrates shore they fought him a night until morning — with Dirar ibn al-Azwar al-Asadi's verse on it ("I lay sleepless at Baniqya — who would not, after what war dealt me at Baniqya") — until they asked for peace; the treaty text runs: payment of jizya on yourself, your protection and the people of your village Baniqya-and-Samiya at a thousand dirhams, with God's covenant and the Prophet's; and he gives two transmissions of the year, 12 and 13. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.332}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Baniqya is the record's Sawad town: his report is quoted from the same al-Baladhuri on which the record rests, and it adds the treaty text, the Abraham tale and the two transmissions of the year of the peace (12 and 13).
