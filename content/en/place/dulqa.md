## Summary
Dulqa was an inland Indian town on a river that reaches the sea forming an inlet, less than a stage from Jinawal and near it in size. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that between Buruj and Nahrwara are two cities, one named Jinawal and the second Dulqa, close in size, with less than a stage between one and the other {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. The city of Dulqa stands on a river that reaches the sea and forms an inlet, and on its west is the city of Buruj {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. Jinawal and Dulqa lie at the foot of a mountain stretching across from the side of Shamutima {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. The author counts Dulqa among the inland towns of the Indian lands in the eighth part of the second climate, together with Jinawal, Nahrwara, al-Qandahar, Zuwayla, Lata, Udhghust, Kabul, Khwash, Hask and others {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.186}. He notes that in the lands of Nahrwara and what neighbours them travellers move only on ox-carts, carrying their goods upon them while the oxen draw them and go with them where they will, each cart having a driver and a guide {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.187}. Dulqa is thus for him a town on a riverine inlet of western India, adjoining Jinawal and linked to Buruj on the west.
