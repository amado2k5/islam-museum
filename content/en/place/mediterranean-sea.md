## Overview
This page rests on one entry of Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan, which treats the sea under the name "Bahr al-Maghrib", names the shores it washes from Sale to Constantinople, and lists the many names by which, he says, one and the same sea was known {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.345}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says that Bahr al-Maghrib is the sea of al-Sham and of [[place/constantinople|Constantinople]]. It takes its water from the encircling ocean, then runs eastward: on its north side it passes [[place/al-andalus|al-Andalus]], then the lands of the Franks up to Constantinople, going by the Pontus, while on the south it runs along many countries, the first of them Sale, then Ceuta, [[place/tunis|Tunis]] and others, Tripoli, [[place/alexandria|Alexandria]], then the Syrian coast to [[place/antioch|Antioch]], where it meets Constantinople. Among its islands he names Mallorca, Sicily, [[place/iqritish|Crete]], [[place/qubrus|Cyprus]] and [[place/rhodes|Rhodes]], and many more {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.345}.

He gives the tale he found in books on the history of Egypt and the Maghrib: after the pharaohs, kings of the Banu Dalluka, among them Darkun ibn Malutis and Zamatra, skilled in cunning, force and sorcery, opened a breach in the encircling ocean from the west when the Byzantines tried to take their land, and the sea drowned many cities and kingdoms and became a barrier between the lands of the Byzantines and Egypt. The sea he has described is this one. Yaqut presents this as something he read {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.345}.

On that basis he says that the sea of al-Andalus, of the Maghrib, of Alexandria, of al-Sham, of Constantinople, of the Franks and of the Byzantines (Bahr al-Rum) are all one sea, not joined to the Indian Ocean except through the encircling ocean. The nearest point between the two seas is at al-Farama, on the coast of the Maghrib sea, which is only four days from the shore of the Red Sea (al-Qulzum) {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.345}. The ocean joins this sea at a place called al-Zuqaq, where the northern and southern shores, the lands of al-Andalus and the Berbers, are four farsakhs apart; for the strait see [[place/strait-of-gibraltar|the Strait of Gibraltar]] {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.345}.

Yaqut closes with a thought experiment: someone could go from Sale to Ifriqiya, then along the coasts of Egypt and al-Sham to the frontier towns as far as Trebizond, cross Mount al-Qabq and round the edges of the Turks' lands to Constantinople, so that the sea lay on his south side where it had been on his north, and pass the Frankish coasts into al-Andalus and come back to Sale without crossing a sea or boarding a ship. The distance is great and the road hard, he says, through nations of different religions and tongues, mountains and wild country {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.345}.

## A note on scope
This page uses only Yaqut's Bahr al-Maghrib entry and the sentence on the strait in the Bahr al-Hind entry that follows it on the same page. It says nothing of the Mediterranean in later centuries or of its modern shores, and the record's summary line "Site in France" comes from the record's Wikidata fields, not from any source read here.
