## Overview
Lebanon, in the record's data, is a Levantine state on the Mediterranean whose capital is Beirut. The medieval source read for this page, Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan, treats "Lubnan" as the name of a mountain, not of a state, and this page reports only that, kept apart from the modern country; for the mountain on the site see [[place/mount-lebanon|Mount Lebanon]] {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.11}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vocalises the name with a damma and a final nun. He begins with a saying: a man asked another for a small need and was told that it would not be met until it was "Lubnaniyya", that is, like Lubnan, which, he explains following al-Azhari, is the name of a mountain and a noun of the form fa'lan that takes full inflection {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.11}.

He describes Lubnan as a mountain overlooking [[place/homs|Homs]] that comes from al-Arj, between [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/medina|Medina]], and runs on until it joins [[place/al-sham|Syria]]. As it passes through the regions it takes other names: in Palestine it is Jabal al-Hamal, in al-Urdunn Jabal al-Jalil, at Damascus Sanir, and at Aleppo, Hama and Homs Lubnan. Near [[place/antioch|Antioch]] and al-Massisa it is called al-Lukkam, then it extends to Malatya, Sumaysat and Qaliqala, as far as the Caspian, where it is called al-Qabq {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.11}.

Yaqut passes on, with "it is said", that the mountain holds seventy tongues so that no people understand another without an interpreter, that in it lies a notable district of Homs, that every fruit and crop grows there without anyone sowing, and that the abdal among the righteous are found there. He also quotes verses of Ahmad ibn al-Husayn ibn Haydara, known as Ibn al-Khurasani al-Tarabulsi, who, leaving his town in anger, says that the waters of Lubnan are sweeter and more refreshing to him than those of the Tigris {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.11}.

## The two mountains of the same name near Mecca
Immediately after this, Yaqut lists "Lubnan" as the dual of lubn: two mountains near Mecca called al-Lubn al-Asfal and al-Lubn al-A'la, above which is a mountain called al-Mabrak, where the elephant knelt at Arna, and which is close to Mecca {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.11}. They are a different place from the Syrian mountain and are not to be mixed with it.

## A note on scope
The sources read for this page say nothing about the modern state of Lebanon, its coast, cities or history, so none is stated. Beirut and the other coastal towns appear in Yaqut under their own entries, which are not read here.
