## Overview
Al-Suwariqiyya is a village that [[person/al-hadi-yahya-al-rassi|al-Hadi]] and his party reached on the road to Yemen; they had meant to take a way that would bring them out at Turaba and Bisha, found the way they had hoped for hard, and turned back. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.38} {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.39} A footnote refers to Sifat al-Jazira and Mu'jam al-Buldan. {cite:sira-hadi-ila-al-haqq p.38} The text locates it no more closely, so no coordinates are given. See [[event/al-hadi-road-to-saada-well-and-zubayd]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a qaf after the ra and the nisba ya, notes the diminutive variant al-Suwayriqiyya, and calls it the village of Abu Bakr between Mecca and Medina, in Najd, once held by Banu Sulaym. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276} He gives an exchange in which the Prophet asks its name and is told it is Ma'isim, with the remark that little can be had there beyond a little palm and crops. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276} Quoting Arram, he describes a large, lively village with a minbar, a congregational mosque and a market visited by merchants, belonging especially to Banu Sulaym; its water is somewhat salty, so they draw sweet water from wells in the valleys of Suwariq and al-Abtan; they have fields and palms, bananas, figs, grapes, pomegranates, quinces and peaches, and camels, horses and sheep, and supply the Hijaz and Najd road along the pilgrim route as far as Dariyya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276} Among those named for it he lists Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Atiq ibn Najm al-Suwariqi al-Bakri, a noble jurist and poet who went to Khurasan and died at Tus in 538; Abu Sa'd took some of his verse. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.276}
