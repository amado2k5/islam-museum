## Overview
Qalibli is a large, old and populous city that al-Idrisi describes on the coast of the land of Tarant (Taranto), the last stop on a coastal road that starts east of Tarant. He says it stands on a spot enclosed by sea and lagoon on every side but the one facing north, and that sea water enters it twice by night and twice by day; that line is garbled in the manuscripts, so it should be read with caution. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 631}

## The road to it
From Tarant eastward to Istura, a wadi where ships anchor, is twelve miles; from there to the wadi of Aqluwiya, three miles; then to the anchorage of Qitulqa, which has a spring, twelve miles; then to the anchorage of Majlud, a large one sheltered from every wind with pools and wells around it, twelve miles. From there to the small anchorage of Nudhras is three miles, and then to Taraja, a good anchorage with plenty of sweet water, twelve miles. From Taraja to Qalibli is six miles. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 631}

## Distance from Tarant
Elsewhere al-Idrisi gives sixty miles by sea from Tarant to Qalibli. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 775}
