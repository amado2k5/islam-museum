## Overview
Nafzawa is a city about a day's journey from Tawzar. Al-Idrisi mentions that between the Nafusa mountain and Nafzawa there is another city. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.279}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut calls Nafzawa a city of the districts of Ifriqiya and, quoting al-Bakri, says the journey to it from Qayrawan takes six days westward. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} He reports that the city has a large spring, called Tawarghi in Berber, whose bottom cannot be reached; that it has a wall of stone and brick, six gates, a congregational mosque, a bath and busy markets; that it is rich in palms and fruit with many springs around it; and that to its south lies an ancient town called al-Madina, walled and with a mosque and market. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} He gives the distances as three days to Gabes, two stages to Gafsa and three stages to Qaytun. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} He then describes the road from it toward Qastiliya across a soft, saline ground that can be crossed only by following planted posts and guides, says that anyone who strays sinks into ground like wet soap and that armies and groups have perished there, and that this ground reaches as far as Ghadames. He adds another view, that Nafzawa belongs to al-Zab al-Kabir in the Jarid. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296}
