## Overview
Tehran is today the capital of [[place/iran|Iran]], at the foot of the Alborz mountains, and the Qajars made it their seat in 1788. {cite:wikidata} Centuries earlier Yaqut al-Hamawi, in his Mu'jam al-buldan, knew it as a village, not a city: Tihran is a Persian name, which the Persians say as Tehran because they have no letter ta' with the emphatic sound, and it is one of the villages of [[place/rayy|Rayy]], about a farsakh from it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 p.51} He passes on what a trustworthy man of Rayy told him: that it was a large village built underground, which nobody could approach except with its people's consent, that its people had rebelled against the sultan many times and he could do nothing with them but humour them, that it had twelve quarters, each fighting the next and none admitting people from another, and that it was full of closely planted orchards. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 pp.51-52}

Yaqut gives it a transmitter, Abu 'Abdallah Muhammad ibn Hammad al-Tihrani, who heard from 'Abd al-Razzaq ibn Hammam among others and was narrated from by the leading scholars; he quotes Ibn Yunus that he travelled in search of hadith and was a trustworthy and discerning hadith man, who came to Egypt, left it, and died at Ascalon in 261 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 pp.51-52} He adds that there is a second village called Tehran in the district of Isfahan, from which a group of hadith transmitters came, among them 'Aqil ibn Yahya al-Tihrani Abu Salih, a trustworthy man, who died in 258 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 v.4 pp.51-52}
