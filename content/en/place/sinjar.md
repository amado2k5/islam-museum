## Overview
Sinjar is a town in northern Iraq on the road to al-Jazira. Ibn Battuta passed through it on his way from Nisibin to Mosul. {cite:rihla-ibn-battuta-1958}

## Note
The coordinates are taken from a modern settlement of the same name in the GeoNames gazetteer, and matching it to the ancient site needs review. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut calls Sinjar a famous city of the Jazira region, three days from Mosul, lying at the foot of a high mountain. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262} He gives the vowels of the name: kasra on the first letter, a silent nun, then jim and ra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262}

He reports that the townspeople say Noah's ark grazed the mountain as it passed, and that Noah remarked it was a mountain's tooth that had wronged them, hence the name; Yaqut states plainly that he cannot verify this. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262} He cites Ibn al-Kalbi that Sinjar, Amid and Hit were named for their builders among the Banu al-Balanda, and that Di'r, said to be the man who drew Joseph out of the well, was brother of the builders of Amid and Hit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262} Another report, via al-Hamadhani, has the ark afterwards come to rest on Mount al-Judi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262} Hamza al-Isfahani, he says, held the name to be an Arabization of "Sinkar" and described a pleasant, thriving city with a river running through it and a valley in front of it of orchards, palms, citrons and bitter oranges, three days from Nasibin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262}

Yaqut also relays al-Zamakhshari's statement that Sultan Sanjar, son of Malik Shah, was born there and took the name from it, and gives the city's coordinates from the astronomical table: longitude 30 degrees, latitude 35 and a half and a third. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262} He adds that a number of scholars, literary men and poets came from it, and quotes verses by Khalid al-Zubaydi, who grew homesick after drinking Sinjar's wine, with a reply by a man of the Namir ibn Qasit. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.262}
