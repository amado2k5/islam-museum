## Overview
A great green tree on which Quraysh and the Arabs used to hang their weapons and slaughter animals beside it. The Companions passed by it on their way to Hunayn, and the account reports that the Prophet said: "This is what the people of Moses did." {cite:azraqi-akhbar-makka}

Its location has not been fixed by coordinates. An additional source is needed.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Dhat Anwat under the heading al-Anwat. He says it was a great green tree to which the people of the Jahiliyya came every year in veneration, hanging their weapons on it and sacrificing beside it, and that it was near Mecca. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.273} He adds, in a phrase that carries no named reporter ("it is mentioned that"), that when they came for the pilgrimage they hung their cloaks on it and entered the sanctuary without cloaks, out of respect for the House, and that this is why it was called anwat, from nata, to hang. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.273} The entry is short. The word does not occur elsewhere in the book: the whole text was searched with vowel marks removed and alif and hamza forms merged.

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Azraqi, has a great green tree on which Quraysh and the Arabs hung their weapons and beside which they sacrificed, which the Companions passed on their way to Hunayn when the Prophet is reported to have said that this is what the people of Moses did; it asks for a further source for the place. Yaqut agrees on the great green tree, the hanging of weapons and the sacrifice, and adds the yearly veneration, the hanging of cloaks at the pilgrimage and the origin of the name; he says it was near Mecca. He says nothing of Hunayn, of the Prophet's words or of the people of Moses. The record's point (21.48, 39.98) is marked approximate, was estimated by the museum between Mecca and Wadi Hunayn, and lies 17.2 km from the museum's Mecca point; Yaqut gives no more than "near Mecca", so his text neither confirms nor rules out this point.
