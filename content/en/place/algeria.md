## Summary
Algeria is a state of the Maghreb. The Oxford Dictionary of Islam has a short entry titled Islam in Algeria which is mostly the modern political history of the country. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e114}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The entry says that Ottoman Algeria kept a strong tradition of religious and political self-rule and that it took France fifty-two years, from 1830 to 1882, to conquer the country; the [[place/ottoman-empire|Ottoman]] period is the one it sets this against. It adds that colonial policy and population growth impoverished the traditional countryside and produced a new urban society, that at independence in 1962 the country was poor and mostly rural, and that one million of nine million Algerians died in the war of independence while two million were left homeless. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e114}

According to the entry the National Liberation Front set up industrial and educational programmes, but the state-run economy stagnated when oil prices fell in the 1980s. After widespread riots it introduced multiparty elections in 1988, and the Islamic Salvation Front became its strongest challenger, calling for an Islamic state and for privatisation. The army then intervened and annulled the election results, and the Front was outlawed in 1992. A constitutional revision in 1996 produced a parliament of two chambers, and in the 1997 elections the National Democratic Rally took the overwhelming majority of the elected seats. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e114}

The entry is mostly political history rather than a description of religion: it gives no figures for the Muslim population or for religious institutions. All of this is the Dictionary's own wording and has not been compared here with another source. See also [[place/algiers|Algiers]].
