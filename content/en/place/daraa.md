## Overview
Draa: a land of connected villages that is not enclosed by a wall or a ditch. Its people grow henna and carry it to all parts. From Draa to the land of Sus al-Aqsa is four stages, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.227}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut describes Daraa as a small town in the Maghrib, to the south-west, four farsakhs from Sijilmasa, lying to its west; most of its merchants, he says, are Jews. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.451} He states that its chief produce is very dry cane, which crumbles to powder when pounded. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.451} He names two men with the nisba al-Dar'i: Abu Zayd Nasr ibn Ali ibn Muhammad, who heard Sa'd ibn Ali al-Zanjani in Mecca, and the jurist Abu al-Hasan al-Dar'i. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.451}
