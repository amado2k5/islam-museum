## Overview
Al-Akhdar: a place mentioned by Abu Ruhm al-Ghifari on a night when he travelled near the Prophet on the road to Tabuk. {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa 4/172}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as al-Akhdar, with a dotted dad, as in the colour green, and says it is a station near Tabuk, between it and Wadi al-Qura, where the Messenger of God halted on his march to Tabuk, and where there is a mosque with his place of prayer. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.123} He then names other places of the same name: Akhdar Turba, a wadi where the torrents coming down from the Sarat gather, or, it is said, a river three days long and one day wide, also called al-Akhdarayn; and an al-Akhdar in the Jazira that belonged to al-Namir ibn Qasit; and he adds that many places, Arab and Persian, are called al-Akhdar. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.123} His entries on Tabuk (vol. 2 p.14 and vol. 5 p.135) and on Wadi al-Qura (vol. 2 p.74 and vol. 5 p.345) do not name al-Akhdar; the whole book was also searched for al-Akhdar close to Tabuk or Wadi al-Qura, and only this entry matched.

## The museum's identification
The record, from Ibn Hisham, has a place named by Abu Ruhm al-Ghifari in the night he rode near the Prophet on the road to Tabuk, and the page gives no description of where it is. Yaqut adds that it is a station near Tabuk, between it and Wadi al-Qura, where the Prophet halted on his march, with a mosque holding his place of prayer; he does not mention Abu Ruhm or the incident. The record's point (28.1181, 36.6436) is marked as a site, its note calls it a fort on the Syrian pilgrim route south of Tabuk, and it lies 30.0 km from the museum's Tabuk point and 210.7 km from its Wadi al-Qura point, so it falls between the two as Yaqut says; this is the museum's reading, since he gives no distance. Because Yaqut says many places are called al-Akhdar, the museum does not assert that the fort named in the note is the Prophet's halting place itself.
