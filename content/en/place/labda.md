## Overview
Lebda is a town with many buildings on the sea. The Arabs gained power over it and drove out its people, and only remains of it are left. Al-Idrisi says its inhabitants were of the Hawwara Berbers. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.308}

In the account of Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari it is in ruins. It has two great idols of white marble, and most of its building is of marble. A valley used to flow to it and end in the sea, until drifting sand overcame it, cut off the valley's supply, and emptied its inhabitants. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.243-244}
