## Summary
Kazima is an impregnable fortress on a lofty-peaked mountain on the road from al-Yamama to Basra, where travellers halt with the Arabs among waters, wells and springs. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from Taqja, a small village whose land adjoins the land of Bahrayn, to the town called Kazima is four stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. Kazima is an impregnable fortress atop a high-peaked mountain, and those four stages are halted at by travellers in the company of the Arabs, among waters, wells and springs {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. From Kazima to the village of Dahman is one stage, then to Basra one stage, making fifteen stages in all on this road from al-Yamama to Basra {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. From al-Yamama to Bahrayn is about thirteen stages, and likewise from al-Yamama to Oman about thirteen stages {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.162}. Thus Kazima stands midway on a watered overland route linking the Yamama plateau with the Gulf coast and the port of Basra.
