## Overview
al-Imadiyya is a strong fortress north of Mosul and one of its districts, built up by Imad al-Din Zangi in 537 AH. Yaqut records it in the Mu'jam al-buldan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.149}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes al-Imadiyya as a strong, secure and great fortress north of Mosul, within its dependencies. He says that Imad al-Din Zangi son of Aq Sunqur rebuilt it in 537, that before that it was a fortress of the Kurds who had dismantled it because of its size, and that Zangi restored it and named it after himself. He gives the name of the earlier fortress as Ashab. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.149}
