## Overview
Ansina: an ancient city on the east bank of the Nile, with gardens and many fruits. al-Idrisi reports that it is known as the city of the sorcerers, and that Pharaoh brought them from it for the appointed day of their meeting with [[person/musa|Musa]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.124}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut calls Ansina an ancient city of Upper Egypt on the east of the Nile. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.265} A passage under Ibn al-Faqih's name, whose end the entry does not mark, speaks of a village in a district of Egypt called Ansina whose people are all transformed, with figures said to be of stone, among them a man with his wife and a woman kneading, and of buildings called barabi and many remains, which Yaqut promises to treat under al-Barabi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.265} The astrologers' figures for its position follow. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.265-266} Al-Dinawari is quoted, with the end of his words marked, that the lubakh grows only at Ansina, that ship planks are sawn from it and sell for about fifty dinars, and that two planks pressed together and put in water for a year join into one; Yaqut answers in his own voice that he has seen the lubakh in Egypt, a tree whose fruit resembles the unripe date in colour and shape and which grows throughout Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.266} The entry ends with men of learning named al-Ansinawi. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.266}

Other entries add to it. In the entry on al-Barabi Yaqut guesses the Coptic word means a place of worship, a strong building or a place of sorcery; he repeats an account, introduced with "it is said", of the queen Dalluka and an old sorceress who built one at Memphis, and says in his own voice that houses of such barabi remain in Akhmim, Ansina and other places of Upper Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.362} Under Hafn he gives the hadith that al-Muqawqis sent Mariyya to the Prophet from Hafn, in the district of Ansina; the same sentence adds that al-Hasan ibn 'Ali spoke to Mu'awiya for the people of Hafn, who were relieved of the land tax, and the entry does not show whether that part belongs to the hadith. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.276} In the entry on Egypt, a passage on the measuring of the Nile, partly under al-Quda'i's name with no marked end, says that Dalluka built a small gauge at Ansina and another at Akhmim. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.178}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, p.124) calls Ansina an ancient city on the east bank, with gardens and fruit, known as the city of the sorcerers whom Pharaoh brought for the appointed day with Musa. Yaqut agrees that it is ancient and east of the Nile (vol 1 p.265). The Ansina entry (vol 1 p.265-266) does not mention Pharaoh's sorcerers; it gives Ibn al-Faqih's account of the transformed village instead. The Barabi entry joins sorcery to the buildings that remain at Ansina but speaks of a queen's protective works, not of Pharaoh's sorcerers. So the sorcerers' tradition rests here on al-Idrisi alone; the entries read neither state nor deny it. See [[person/musa|Musa]].
