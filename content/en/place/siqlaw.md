## Summary
Siqlaw is a large, fine city with buildings and markets, on the bank of a river in the land of Qarantara. Its mountains hold good iron ore. Al-Idrisi says the quality of its iron is not matched by the iron of any other place in cutting. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878}

## Details
Siqlaw lies in the land of Qarantara, the country between the river Danube and the valley of Dhurwa. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878} Al-Idrisi names among the chief towns of this land Bizawara, Buzana, Nitram, Balghuraba, Siqlaw and Buza. All of these lands border the Venetians, and he says that wars between them were sometimes very fierce and never ceased. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878}

He places the land of Qarantara next to the land of Ankalaya to the west, with the Venetian lands lying to its south, the land of Ankarya to its east, and the land of Bawamiya to its north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878} From Buza to Siqlaw is three days' journey, and Siqlaw stands on the bank of the river on its southern side. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878}

He describes Siqlaw as a large and fine city, with buildings, markets and abundant goods, and with iron of good quality in its mountains. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.878}

His account gives no foundation date and no single event for Siqlaw, so no incident is attributed to it here. What he records concerns its site, its buildings, its markets, its iron and its road to Buza.
