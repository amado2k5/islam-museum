## Overview
Kalwadha is a land in al-Sawad on the road to al-Mada'in. Al-Baladhuri reports that some Muslims reached it in their raids, and they lived on what they gained from those raids. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut writes the name with a final alif that is spelled like a ya and calls Kalwadha a tassuj near Baghdad, on its eastern side; the words that follow, which relate it to the western side of Nahr Buq, can be read in two ways and are left open here. It is now in ruins, with a trace remaining, one farsakh from Baghdad downstream. Poets mention it, and he names verses by Abu Nuwas and by Muti' ibn Iyas. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.477} He names one of its scholars, Abu al-Khattab Mahfuz ibn Ahmad al-Kalwadhani, a Hanbali jurist, born in Shawwal 432 and dead in 515, and reports that the ahl al-siyar say it was named for Kalwadha ibn Tahmurath, the king. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.477-478}

Other entries place it. In the Baghdad entry a dihqan, reported through Sulayman ibn Mukhtar, tells al-Mansur that Baghdad stands among four tassujes: Qatarrabul and Badawriya on the west side, Nahr Buq and Kalwadha on the east. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.458} Under Nahr Buq he writes that "they claimed" the south of Baghdad to be Kalwadha and the north to be Nahr Buq, with no speaker named. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.318} Al-Firk is a village that was near Kalwadha. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.255}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Baladhuri) calls Kalwadha a land of the Sawad on the road to al-Mada'in, reached by some Muslims in their raids. Yaqut's entry (vol 4 p.477-478) mentions neither the raids nor the road to al-Mada'in. His Baghdad entry reports from the ahl al-siyar raids on the Sawad after the fall of Mihran, naming Sura, Kaskar, al-Sarat, al-Falalij and the Istans, not Kalwadha. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.457} That the record's Kalwadha is the tassuj Yaqut describes is the museum's reading. The record's point is 5.4 km from the museum's Baghdad point; Yaqut gives one farsakh, and the museum does not convert it.
