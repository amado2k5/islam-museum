## Overview
al-Sunh: a quarter on the upper side of [[place/medina|Medina]], named after the fortified tower "al-Sunh" that the Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj built. It is one of the quarters of Medina, as Yaqut has it. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.210-211}

The house of [[person/abu-bakr|Abu Bakr]] was in it when he married Mulayka, or Habiba, the daughter of Kharija ibn Zayd. It lies about a mile from the house of the Prophet. {cite:samhudi-wafa-al-wafa p.210-211}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Sunh, with a damma on the sin, a sukun on the nun and an undotted ha at the end. He says it may be a plural of sanih and explains the sanih as the gazelle or bird that shows you its right side, as when a gazelle crosses from your left to your right; in the text the museum holds, the sentence then breaks off at the word for "plural" and moves on, so words may be missing there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.265} He says it is one of the quarters of Medina, that the house of Abu Bakr was there when he married Mulayka, or, as some said, Habiba, daughter of Kharija ibn Zayd of the Ansar, that it lies at an edge of Medina among the homes of Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj in the 'Awali, and that a mile separates it from the house of the Prophet. He names Abu al-Harith Habib ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Habib ibn Yasaf al-Ansari al-Madini as ascribed to it, who reported from Hafs ibn 'Asim, and from whom Malik ibn Anas, Shu'ba ibn al-Hajjaj and others reported. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.265} In the same entry he adds a second al-Sunh in Najd near the mountain of Tayyi', where Khalid halted in the war of the Ridda and 'Adi ibn Hatim came to him with the submission of Tayyi'; the entry gives Khalid no further name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.265} The next entry, Sunhat al-Jarr, is a separate place in Medina, and the entry does not tie it to the quarter. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.265}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Samhudi, gives the quarter, the tower "al-Sunh" of Banu al-Harith, the house of Abu Bakr and the mile to the Prophet's house; its remark that Yaqut counts al-Sunh among the quarters of Medina is borne out by his entry. Yaqut does not mention the tower, which comes from al-Samhudi alone. He agrees on Banu al-Harith, on Mulayka and Habiba as the alternative names and on the mile. The record's point is 1.9 km from the museum's Medina point, and Yaqut gives no direction other than the 'Awali. The Najd al-Sunh is a different place from the record's.
