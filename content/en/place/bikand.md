## Summary
Bikand is a city on the road to Bukhara, which al-Idrisi places one stage from the city of Farbar, midway along the road between Farbar and Bukhara. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.493}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Farbar to the city of Bikand is one stage, that it lies in the middle of the road to Bukhara, and that it is a middling city with buildings, a functioning market, farms and crops, a strong wall, and a congregational mosque of ornamented construction whose qibla he describes as unmatched in its decoration and beauty; it is, he says, a city that stands on its own {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.493}. He adds that from Bikand to Baghara is one stage, and then describes Bukhara as a city that surpasses other cities in greatness {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.493}. In his account of Bukhara's waters he mentions that their surplus falls into a great pool in the district of Bikand near Farbar, called Samjan {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.494}.
