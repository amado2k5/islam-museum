## Summary
Bikand is a city on the road to Bukhara, which al-Idrisi places one stage from the city of Farbar, midway along the road between Farbar and Bukhara. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.493}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Farbar to the city of Bikand is one stage, that it lies in the middle of the road to Bukhara, and that it is a middling city with buildings, a functioning market, farms and crops, a strong wall, and a congregational mosque of ornamented construction whose qibla he describes as unmatched in its decoration and beauty; it is, he says, a city that stands on its own {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.493}. He adds that from Bikand to Baghara is one stage, and then describes Bukhara as a city that surpasses other cities in greatness {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.493}. In his account of Bukhara's waters he mentions that their surplus falls into a great pool in the district of Bikand near Farbar, called Samjan {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.494}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut vowels the name with a kasra on the b, a fatha on the k and a sukun on the n, and says it is a town between Bukhara and the Oxus, one stage from Bukhara, mentioned in the accounts of the conquests; it was a large, fine town with many scholars and has long been in ruins. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}

He quotes the author of the Book of the Climes as saying that every town beyond the river has farmland and villages except Bikand, but that it had more ribats than he knew of in any town of Transoxiana, about a thousand by the report that reached him, with a strong wall and a congregational mosque built with great care, its mihrab decorated more finely than any other beyond the river. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}

Among people named after it he lists Abu Ahmad Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Bikandi, who transmitted from Abu Usama and Ibn Uyayna and from whom al-Bukhari transmitted; the hafiz Abu al-Fadl Ahmad ibn Ali ibn Umar al-Sulaymani al-Bikandi, who travelled to Iraq, Syria and Egypt, wrote over four hundred short works and died in 412; and Isma'il ibn Hamdawayh Abu Sa'id al-Bikandi, who came to Damascus in 229 and who, according to Ibn Yunus, died in 273. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.533}
