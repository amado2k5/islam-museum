## Overview
Jaffa is a city on the shore of the salt sea, about twenty miles from [[place/ramla|Ramla]], and it has a harbour for ships, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.356}

Al-Muqaddasi named it the treasury of Palestine and the port of Ramla. He describes it as small, with a strong fort, its whole sea gate of iron, its congregational mosque overlooking the sea, and its harbour good. {cite:ahsan-al-taqasim p.142}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut writes the name (Yafa) with fa and short ending, and calls it a city on the coast of the Syrian sea, in the district of Palestine between Caesarea and Acre, in the third climate, giving its longitude and latitude. He cites Ibn Butlan's letter of 442 AH that it is a place of dearth where a newborn rarely survives. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.426} He records that Saladin took it in 583 AH when he opened the coast, the Franks seized it in 587, and al-Malik al-'Adil Abu Bakr ibn Ayyub recovered it in 593 and laid it in ruins. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.426} The adjective of relation is «Yafuni», and he names Abu al-'Abbas Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Ibrahim ibn Umayr al-Yafuni, who heard hadith in Damascus and Palestine and from whom al-Tabarani narrated. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.426}
