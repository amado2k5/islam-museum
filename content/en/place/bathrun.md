## Summary
Bathrun is a town in western Oman which al-Idrisi reports as the place where most of the Shurat of his day were gathered and settled. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.158}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that the Shurat predominate among the people of the land of Oman, and that most of the Shurat in his time were gathered together settling a town called Bathrun in western Oman, where they have villages and buildings, being fortified by a mountain they have while wells lie at its foot {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.158}. In the same passage he notes that the bounds of the land of Oman are nine hundred miles around, that it is on the whole a hot country, that a little snow falls on the summit of Jabal Sharam, and that continuous deserts lie between Najd and the land of Oman {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.158}. Bathrun is thus a centre of the Kharijite Shurat in sixth-century Oman, fortified by the mountains.
