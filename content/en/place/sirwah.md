## Overview
Sirwah was the capital of the Mukarribs of [[polity/kingdom-of-saba|Saba]] in the centuries before 650 BCE, by the estimate of al-Rahiq al-Makhtum. Its ruins lie a day's journey to the west of [[place/marib|Ma'rib]], and are known as Khuraybah. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:tafhim-al-quran 4/195-198}.

## The dam
In the time of the Mukarribs, construction began on the dam known as [[building/marib-dam|the Dam of Ma'rib]], which has a great place in the history of [[place/yemen|Yemen]]. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.23} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24} Then the kings of Saba gave up the title "Mukarrib" and took Ma'rib as their capital instead of Sirwah. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.24}

## For young visitors
Sirwah was the first capital of the kingdom of Saba in Yemen, before Ma'rib, and old ruins of it remain.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut spells the name with a kasra on the first letter, a sukun, a waw, an alif and a final ha', and gives the word's sense through Abu Ubayda and al-Zajjaj as a lofty building, a palace or fortress. He describes Sirwah as a fortress in Yemen near Marib, which it is said Solomon son of David built. He cites a line of verse that Ibn Duraid recited in his Amali for an unnamed poet, about someone who settled at Sirwah and built a prayer niche high on its slopes. Ibn Abi al-Dumayna is named for the statement that Sa'd ibn Khawlan ibn Imran ibn al-Haf ibn Quda'a held the kingship at Sirwah, and Yaqut gives verse from an unnamed man of Khawlan and from Amr ibn Zayd al-Ghalibi of the Banu Sa'd ibn Sa'd on the same theme, the second saying that Sa'd ibn Khawlan's kingship held for eighty years before it shook; another poet says of them that they wintered at Sirwah for fifty years and spent the summer and grazing season in the country of Marib. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.402-403} Three other entries touch it: Salhin, where a story has devils build palaces, among them Sirwah, for a king of Hamdan when Solomon married Bilqis {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.235}; Sirdah, where Yaqut thinks al-Umrani's fortress built by the jinn for Solomon is Sirwah misspelt {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.401}; and Qiyawan, where al-Harith ibn Amr al-Harbi of Khawlan names Sirwah as the home of his people {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.424}.

## The museum's identification
The record's text, from al-Mubarakfuri and Tafhim al-Quran, makes Sirwah the capital of the mukarribs of Saba before 650 BCE, with ruins a day west of Marib known as Kharibah, and the dam begun in that age. Yaqut's entry does not mention Saba, the mukarribs, the dam or the name Kharibah, and he does not call it a capital: his Sirwah is a fortress near Marib, tied to traditions about Solomon and to Sa'd ibn Khawlan. The museum's identification of the two therefore rests on the name and the nearness to Marib, which is its own reading and not a statement of Yaqut's. The record's point (15.4517, 45.0167) lies about 34 km due west of the museum's Marib point, which is consistent with "near Marib" and with al-Mubarakfuri's day's journey; Yaqut gives no distance or direction.
