## Overview
A populous village on the eastern bank of the Nile. Around it are gardens and continuous settled lands of reed and grapes, and it lies half a day from al-Qays, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.124}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records "Munyat Abi al-Khasib" and calls it a fine, populous city on the bank of the Nile in the nearer (lower) Sa'id. He says that Abu al-Lamti, one of the chiefs of those parts, built a fine congregational mosque there, and that in the direction of its qibla stands the maqam of Ibrahim. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.218}

Elsewhere in the Mu'jam he has "the river of Abu al-Khasib" at Basra, named for a freedman of Abu Ja'far al-Mansur, which is a different place with no tie to the town in Egypt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.315}

## The museum's identification
This record is Yaqut's Munyat Abi al-Khasib, a town on the Nile in the Sa'id, which agrees with al-Idrisi's prosperous village on the Nile in the record. Yaqut writes "Abi al-Khasib" where the record has "Ibn al-Khasib", and putting the two names together is the museum's reading, resting on the same town and the same river. Yaqut does not say that the town is on the east bank or how far it is from al-Qays; those details are al-Idrisi's alone.
