## Summary
Messina is a city on the Strait of Messina in Sicily, into which Ibn Jubayr's ship was driven by the storm and where he landed on his westward journey in 580 AH. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p. 103-104}

## Details
On Ibn Jubayr's return voyage from Sicily toward al-Andalus, the storm struck his ship at the head of the strait, where the sea narrows to about six miles — three miles at its narrowest point — running from the mainland shore to the shore of Sicily; the sea in this strait pours like a torrent and boils like a cauldron from the pressure of its confinement. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p. 104}

Around midnight of Sunday the third of Sha'ban, as they drew level with Messina, the sea's surges overwhelmed them and the ship grounded on its hull upon the shore; then God's providence reached them at dawn, and behold, Messina lay before them less than half a mile off. Rescue boats came out, and King William of Sicily himself came down with a company of his men to look at the scene; their landing there was the end of their terror. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p. 103-104}
