## Overview
Sinn Sumayra is an eastern fortress in al-Jabal. According to al-Baladhuri, the Muslims paused there when they campaigned in the mountains, and it was named after Sumayra, a woman of Dabba among the emigrant women, who had a tooth (sinn). {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the name as Sinn Sumayra, with a kasra on the first letter, a doubled n, and Sumayra in the diminutive, and cites Ibn al-Sikkit's explanation of a line by Kuthayyir. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.269} He says it is a mountain beyond Qarmisin, on the left of the road to Khurasan. He reports the account that the Muslim armies, marching toward Nahawand, passed a long mountain overlooking the others and someone said it was like Sinn Sumayra. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.269} Sumayra, he explains, was a woman among the emigrants from Banu Mu'awiya ibn Ka'b ibn Tha'laba ibn Sa'd ibn Dabba, who had one tooth standing out above her other teeth, and the mountain was named after her tooth. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.269}
