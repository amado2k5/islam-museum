## Overview
Ajdabiya: a town on level stony ground in the lands of the Maghrib. Al-Idrisi describes it as having kept only two castles in the desert, with no running water in it or around it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 311}

## Location
According to al-Idrisi, it lies six stages from [[place/barqa|Barqa]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 311}

An additional source is needed: its coordinates and its founding date are not given here.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut first discusses the spelling and the form of the name: if Arabic, it could be a plural of jadb, treated as a singular because it is a name, but he thinks it more likely foreign. He places the town between Barqa and Western Tripoli, about a month's journey from Zuwayla on Ibn Hawqal's word. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.100} A passage under al-Bakri's name, whose end is not marked, describes a large city in a rocky desert, with wells cut in the rock and sweet water, a spring, small gardens, a few palms and no trees but arak; a well-built congregational mosque raised by Abu al-Qasim, the son of 'Ubayd Allah al-Mahdi; baths, inns and busy markets; people who are mostly Nabateans, with some pure Lawata; a sea anchorage called al-Madur with three castles, eighteen miles off; vaulted brick houses instead of timber roofs, because of the wind; low prices and many dates, brought from Awjila. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.100} A speaker introduced only as "another" calls it a city of many palms, with Awjila between its west and south and counted as its dependency. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.100} The entry's last sentences, with no source named, put it in the fourth climate, count it among the conquests of 'Amr ibn al-'As, taken with Barqa by treaty for five thousand dinars, with many Berbers becoming Muslim, and name the writer Ibn al-Ajdabi as a man of the town. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.100-101}

In the Zuwayla entry the distance to Zuwayla is fourteen stages, on al-Bakri's authority. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.160} In the Surt entry a sentence after a passage under al-Bakri's name, whose end is not marked, gives six stages from Surt to Ajdabiya; the same entry puts Ajdabiya inland to the south of Surt. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.206} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.207}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Idrisi, p.311) describes a town on level stony ground that has kept only two castles in the desert, with no running water, six stages from Barqa. Yaqut's entry (vol 1 p.100-101) gives no distance to Barqa and says nothing of ruin; the town it quotes from al-Bakri has markets, baths and many people. The entries do not say why the pictures differ, and the museum does not guess. The six stages in the Surt entry run from Surt, not from Barqa, and its speaker is unmarked. The record's point is 204.8 km from the museum's Barqa point and is not tied to any stage count. See [[place/barqa|Barqa]], [[place/awjila|Awjila]] and [[place/zuwayla|Zuwayla]].
