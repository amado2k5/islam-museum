## Summary
Chad is a country that, in the account of The Oxford Dictionary of Islam, received Islam before the eleventh century. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e416}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam traces the arrival of Islam in Chad to before the eleventh century, brought by Berbers, Muslim merchants and wandering Sufi preachers; their orders, it says, later took a leading part in opposing French colonial advances in the 1800s. Before colonial rule the north, which was Muslim, was the country's social and economic heart, but when independence came in 1960 the Christianized south had overtaken it in political and economic development. The Dictionary says bloody riots over oppressive taxation in 1965 sharpened that divide between Muslims and Christians. It describes the recent Islamic revival as drawing on the thinking of Sudan's National Islamic Front, and records that Hissein Habre, in power from 1982 to 1990, faced an indictment in Senegal listing 97 political killings, along with 142 instances of torture and 100 disappearances under his rule in Chad. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e416}
