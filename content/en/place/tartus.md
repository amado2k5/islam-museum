## Overview
Tartus is recorded in the German Archaeological Institute's iDAI.gazetteer as an archaeological site and populated place at about 34.89° N, 35.89° E, and in Pleiades as a findspot of a cuneiform inscription. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2298659} {cite:pleiades 9832296}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Ṭarṭūs» (Pleiades no. 9832296), classed as a findspot. {cite:pleiades 9832296} Recorded names include «Ṭarṭūs» [Arabic, 1700 CE – 2100 CE]; «Anṭarṭūs» [Ottoman Turkish]. {cite:pleiades 9832296} Pleiades describes it simply as the find place of a cuneiform inscription. {cite:pleiades 9832296}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The German Archaeological Institute's iDAI.gazetteer (no. 2298659) records this place under the preferred name «Tartus», typed as a archaeological site and populated place, with the further names «Tortosa», «Antaradus» [Latin], «Ἀντάραδος» [Greek], «طرطوس» [Arabic]. Its parent unit is «Nahiyat Markaz Tartus», an administrative unit. The record carries the identifiers GeoNames 163345 and GND 4395244-6. {cite:idai-gazetteer 2298659}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives the form of the name (Tartus, on the pattern of «qarbus») and calls it a town of Syria overlooking the sea near al-Marqab and Acre, held in his day by the Franks. He names as one of its people Abu Abd Allah al-Husayn ibn Muhammad ibn al-Husayn al-Khawwas, a Qur'an reciter, who narrated from Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, with Abu Bakr Ahmad ibn Muhammad ibn Yunus al-Nasawi narrating from him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.30}
