## Overview
Jabal Shulayr is the snow mountain of al-Andalus, two days long and extremely high, with snow on it always, in winter and in summer. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 569} From it begins the river of snow called Shunayl, south of [[place/granada|Granada]]. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 569}

## Setting
Al-Idrisi says that Granada and Wadi Ash lie north of the mountain, that its southern face looks out on the sea, and that at its foot on the sea side are Burja and Dalaya. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 570} Travelling through the plain of Abla, which is twelve miles long, the snow mountain Shulayr lies on the traveller's left. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 567}

## Forts and rivers
At the foot of the mountain are many forts, among them Hisn Fariyra, to which walnuts are attributed (they crack without pounding and none from other lands equals their taste), and Hisn Dhalar, whose pears are marvellous, one weighing a pound of al-Andalus. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 567} West of the village of Adhra comes down a great river rising in Jabal Shulayr, which joins the waters of Burja and others and falls into the sea at Adhra. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 564}
