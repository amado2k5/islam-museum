## Overview
Hit is a town on the lower Euphrates, and the site of the [[event/conquest-of-hit|conquest of Hit and the fortresses of the Euphrates]]. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

The army of Kufa, sent by Ammar ibn Yasir under the command of Sa'd ibn Amr ibn Haram, conquered it; it is also said under the command of Mudhlaj ibn Amr al-Sulami. Its people were granted safe conduct, and half of their church was excepted from it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

It is the last place where the conquest of the Jazira from the side of Umayr ibn Sa'd met the conquest of Iraq from the side of Kufa. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In the Pleiades gazetteer
Pleiades, the open gazetteer of ancient places, lists this place under the title «Idu/Diacira/Hit» (Pleiades no. 894008), classed as a settlement, with activity attested from 2000 BCE to 2000 CE. Names recorded there include Idu, Diacira, Hit. {cite:pleiades 894008}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut enters Hit (with a kasra): Ibn al-Sikkit said it was named Hit because it lies in a depression (hawwa) of the earth; Ru'ba uses hit for a depression; and Abu Bakr said its root is Hawt, the waw turned to ya' because it is quiescent after a kasra — this being the doctrine of the language and grammar people; while the traditionists said it was named for its builder Hit ibn al-Sabandi (also read al-Bulandi) ibn Malik, of the descendants of Madyan ibn Ibrahim. It is a town on the Euphrates in the direction of Baghdad above al-Anbar, with many palms and wide resources, adjoining the steppe; its longitude is 69 degrees from the west and its latitude 32 and a half and a quarter degrees, in the third clime. Sa'd dispatched an army to it in year 16, and it extended from him until it met the people of Qarqisiya (Amr ibn Malik al-Zuhri has verses on it); in it is the tomb of 'Abdallah ibn al-Mubarak, and on it Abu 'Abdallah Muhammad ibn Khalifa al-Sanbasi, the poet of Sayf al-Dawla Sadaqa ibn Mazyad, has a poem longing for Hit, its chambers, its rustaq and its water-wheels; a number of scholars are ascribed to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.420; 5 p.421} He also has a Hit that is a cave under a mountain ridge in al-Yamama, and a Hit among the villages of Hawran in the Luwa district of Damascus, home of the poet Nasrallah ibn al-Hasan al-Hiti who died in year 565. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.421}

## The museum's identification
The record's Hit is the Euphrates town, and it is Yaqut's town "on the Euphrates above al-Anbar" (about 53.4 km between the museum's Hit and al-Anbar points); his army of Sa'd in year 16 agrees with the record's al-Baladhuri conquest, and the tomb of Ibn al-Mubarak is added report. The Yamama cave and the Hawran village are other Hits in his book and are not to be conflated with it.
