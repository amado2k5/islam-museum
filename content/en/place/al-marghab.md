## Overview
al-Marghab: a place in [[place/basra|Basra]]. The source reports that Bashir ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Bakra dug there the marghab, the water channels and the crossing canals by al-Taghlub, and named it "Marghab Marw". It was part of the estate of Hilal ibn Ahwaz al-Mazini, which was eight thousand jaribs. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Marghab, with a dotted ghayn and a final ba', for three places: a village of the villages of Herat, then of the villages of Malin, to which is ascribed Muhammad ibn Khalf ibn Yusuf the man of letters and Sufi of Herat, who lived there and was heard from in 530 AH; {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.107} "al-Marghab" a river name at Marw al-Shahijan; and "al-Marghab" a river at Basra — quoting al-Baladhuri that Bashir ibn Ubaydallah ibn Abi Bakra dug the Marghab and named it after Marw's Marghab, that the grant in which it lay belonged to Hilal ibn Ahwaz al-Mazini, granted him by Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik, at eighteen thousand jaribs in Yaqut's printing, so Bashir dug the Marghab, the canals and the crossings by force and said "this is a grant of mine"; Humayri ibn Hilal disputed it, and Khalid ibn Abdallah al-Qasri wrote to Malik ibn al-Mundhir ibn al-Jarud, then over the levies of Basra, to leave Humayri and the Marghab and its land alone; and he records Bashir's saying to Salim ibn Qutayba against litigation, then, seen litigating, his answer: "this is not that — this Marghab is eighteen thousand jaribs; litigation over it is honour." {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.108}

## The museum's identification
The record's Marghab is the third of the three: the Basra river dug by Bashir ibn Ubaydallah ibn Abi Bakra and named after Marw's Marghab; Yaqut's al-Baladhuri quotation is the same report the record carries, except that Yaqut prints "eighteen thousand jaribs" where the record has eight thousand — a printed difference left as it stands. The other two Marghabs (the village of Herat and the river of Marw al-Shahijan) are different places.
