## Summary
Barqana was a fortress crowned by an impregnable citadel, with a flourishing market, abundant waters, gardens and vineyards, one stage from Jarawa, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.248}

## Details
On the road through the Maghrib al-Idrisi reports that from Jarawa to Barqana is one stage; it is a fortress bearing an impregnable citadel with a prosperous market, many waters, gardens and vineyards {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.248}. Jarawa before it was likewise inhabited, six miles from the sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.248}. From Barqana to al-Ulawiyyin is one stage — a large village on a river coming to it from the south, with excellent fruits and plentiful goods {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.248}. From there to Tlemcen is a pleasant stage, Tlemcen being in his account an ancient city with a strong, well-built wall {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.248}. Barqana thus lay in his scheme on a chain of easy stages from Jarawa to Tlemcen, combining a defended site, a busy market and ample water and orchards.
