## Overview
Sohar is a city on the shore of the Persian Sea, one of the wealthiest cities of Oman in both ancient and modern times, according to al-Idrisi. Merchants come to it, and goods of Yemen are brought to it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.156}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Suhar with a damma and a final ra', and offers two roots for the name: a low hollow in a lava field, or a reddish colour. The entry first carries a tribal story on the word of Ibn al-Kalbi: when Quda'a scattered from Tihama, Juhayna and Sa'd Hudhaym, sons of Zayd, were the first to climb to Najd and spread over its deserts; a rider asked who they were, and they answered that they were sons of the desert, so the Arabs said these were Suhar, a name drawn from sahra'. He then gives verses in which Suhar stands for a tribe, by Zuhayr ibn Janab, Bishr ibn Suwada al-Taghlibi and al-Abbas ibn Mirdas al-Sulami, and concludes, after the first of them, that Suhar is of Quda'a. Next he turns to the place: Suhar is the capital of Oman on the mountain side, and Tu'am its capital on the coast side. A second description follows of a city of fine air, plenty and fruit, built of baked brick and teak, large, with none like it in those parts; one report says it was named for Suhar son of Aram son of Sam son of Noah, a brother of Rabab, Tasm and Jadis, and the linguists say it lies next to the mountain. He quotes al-Bashshari that no town on the China sea was greater, that it is prosperous, its fruit better than that of Zabid and Sanaa, with admirable markets, a town stretched along the sea, a great mosque on the coast with a tall minaret, sweet wells and a canal, and that it is the vestibule of China, the treasury of the East and Iraq and the relief of Yemen; the prayer ground lies among the palms and, half a farsakh off, a mosque where the Prophet's she-camel is said to have knelt. Yaqut comments that he does not know how the kneeling came about. The entry also says the Muslims took it in the days of Abu Bakr, in the year 12, by treaty, and names a poet of the town, Abu Ali Muhammad ibn Zawzan al-Suhari al-Umani, who left for Baghdad after a reverse and wrote verse of longing for it; the museum does not quote it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.393-394} Under Tu'am he repeats that Tu'am is Oman's coastal capital and Suhar its capital on the mountain side. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.54}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi, has a city on the shore of the Persian Sea, among the wealthiest of Oman in the past and in al-Idrisi's day, visited by merchants, with the goods of Yemen brought to it. Yaqut agrees on a wealthy trading city of Oman; al-Bashshari's description, with Suhar as the relief of Yemen, sits close to al-Idrisi's goods of Yemen. Yaqut's own first note, however, puts Suhar on the mountain side and Tu'am on the coast, while the description he quotes puts it on the sea; he does not reconcile the two, and the museum does not choose between them. The record's point (24.3461, 56.7075) is that of the modern coastal town of Sohar and is marked as a locality; Yaqut gives no coordinates to test it. The treaty of year 12 is Yaqut's report and is not in the record.
