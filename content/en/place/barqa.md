## Overview
Barqa: a region of the Lower Maghrib between Egypt and Ifriqiya, whose city is Antablus, according to al-Baladhuri. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

After the conquest of Alexandria, Amr ibn al-As made peace with it for thirteen thousand dinars of poll-tax. Its people used to send their kharaj to the governor of Egypt without anyone urging them, and al-Baladhuri reports that they were the most fertile people of the Maghrib, and that no strife entered it. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

In it is the village of Luwata of the Berbers, who were people of the covenant, and from it comes Zuwayla, where the first march of Uqba ibn Nafi came to an end. This place needs an additional source. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

See [[event/sulh-barqa|the treaty of Barqa]] and [[event/uqba-zawila-appointment|the appointment of Uqba over Zuwayla]].

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
For [[person/yaqut-al-hamawi|Yaqut]], Barqa is the name of a large region with towns and villages between Alexandria and Ifriqiya, whose city is called Antabulus, meaning "the five cities". {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.537} Its soil is red, "like khaluq perfume", so that its people's clothes are always reddened; Berbers surround it on every side, and it has much fruit, walnuts, almonds, citrons and quinces. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.538} In its city is the tomb of Ruwayfi', Companion of the Prophet; its people drink rainwater that runs in valleys into cisterns built for them by kings, and it has two harbours, Ajiya, with a market and a pulpit, and Talmuna. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.538}

Alexandria to Barqa is a month's journey; from Fustat it is two hundred and twenty parasangs, as he quotes al-Hamdani, and from Barqa to Qayrawan two hundred and fifteen. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.538} He says it was taken by treaty: [[person/amr-ibn-al-as|Amr ibn al-As]] made peace with its people for a jizya of thirteen thousand dinars; later most of them became Muslim and were settled on the tithe and half-tithe in 21 AH, on condition that no tax collector entered, they sending their tax to Egypt on time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.538} He quotes Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As as saying he knew of no place safer or more secluded for a man with a family than Barqa, and but for his property in the Hijaz he would have settled there. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.538} Among its people were Ahmad ibn Abd Allah al-Barqi and his brothers Muhammad and Abd al-Rahim, who all transmitted Ibn Hisham's *Sira*. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p.538}
