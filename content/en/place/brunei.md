## Summary
Brunei is a sultanate on the island of Borneo. The Oxford Dictionary of Islam has a short entry, Islam in Brunei, giving the share of Muslims, their school and the sultan's role. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e377}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The entry says Islam is the national religion of Brunei, that about sixty-five percent of the population is Muslim, mainly Malay Sunnis of the Shafi'i school, and that most of the rest are Kedayans (converts from the indigenous tribal groups) and Chinese converts. In its account Islam took hold in the fifteenth century, when a Muslim Malay became sultan. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e377}

It adds that the sultan traditionally bore the duty of maintaining Islamic life, though it was usually handed to appointed officials. Since the 1930s the sultans have used rising oil income to build a wide welfare system and to promote Islam, including paying towards pilgrimage costs, mosque building and the growth of the Department of Religious Affairs. The entry says the current sultan, meaning at the time it was written, offers the Malay Islamic monarchy as the nation's guiding ideology in place of Islamic theocracy. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e377}

The figures and judgements are the Dictionary's own wording and carry no date beyond that, so they should not be read as a current census. The entry links the subject to neighbouring [[place/malaysia|Malaysia]].
