## Overview
Ghadamis is a town that al-Idrisi places eighteen stages from the tribe of Azqar. The road to it crosses a land with little company and little water. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.113}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Ghadamis with a fatha, or a damma, on the first letter, and thinks the name is not Arabic but Berber. He calls it a city of the Maghrib, then in its south, stretching into the land of the Sudan beyond the country of Zafun. The hides tanned there, which carry its name, are among the best, he says, soft and glossy like fabrics of silk-and-wool. In the middle of the town is an ancient spring with traces of wonderful Roman building; the water rises in it and the townspeople share it out in known portions, so that no one can take more than his due, and they irrigate their crops from it. Its people are Berbers whom he calls the Tanawiriyya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.187} Three other entries touch it. Zafun is a wide province of the Sudan adjoining the Maghrib and the land of the Mulaththamun, the veiled people, with a mobile king. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.127} Under Nafzawa he gives al-Bakri on a treacherous soft ground between that town and Qastiliya which, he says, extends as far as Ghadamis. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.296} Under Waddan he reports that in the year 46 Uqba ibn Nafi' halted at Ghadamis, left his army there under Zuhayr ibn Qays al-Balawi and rode on to take Waddan. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.365-366}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi, has a city eighteen stages from the Azqar tribe across country with few people and little water. Yaqut gives no stages and does not name the Azqar: the museum searched the whole book for Azqar, Azkar and Azjar and found no tribe of that name, the one hit being part of an unrelated word. He places the city generally in the south of the Maghrib. The record's point (30.134, 9.497) is marked as a site and carries the note "Ghadames oasis city, Nalut District, Libya", which is a modern identification. Yaqut gives no coordinates or distances, so the museum's identification with the oasis of Ghadames rests on the name and on the general placing in the south of the Maghrib, and Yaqut's account of the spring does not by itself prove it.
