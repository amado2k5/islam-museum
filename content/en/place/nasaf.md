## Overview
Nasaf, which is Nakhshab, is a town beyond the river (Transoxiana). The book Futuh al-Buldan mentions that its people came with the Sughd, the Turks and Kush to Sa'id ibn Uthman, with a hundred and twenty thousand men, and that they met at Bukhara and were defeated. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut gives Nasaf, with a fatha on the first two letters, as a large city with many inhabitants and a farming district between the Oxus (Jayhun) and Samarkand, from which many scholars of every discipline came; he adds that it is Nakhshab itself. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.285} Citing al-Istakhri, he says it has a citadel, a suburb and four gates, lies on the road from Bukhara to Balkh on level ground, with mountains two stages away toward Kish and a waterless waste between it and the Oxus. One river runs through its middle, gathering the waters of Kish, and the seat of the emirate stood on its bank at a place called the head of the bridge. The city has many villages and districts and two pulpits besides the town; its farming districts have no other running river, which fails for part of the year, and wells water their gardens; the land is mostly fertile. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.285} Among its scholars he names Abu Ishaq Ibrahim ibn Ma'qil ibn al-Hajjaj ibn Khidash al-Nasafi, a distinguished hadith scholar who gathered sunna and Qur'anic commentary, related from Qutayba ibn Sa'id, Hisham ibn Amir al-Dimashqi and Harmala ibn Yahya al-Misri, and died in 294 AH. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.285}
