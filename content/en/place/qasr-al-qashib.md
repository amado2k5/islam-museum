## Overview
Qasr al-Qashib is a palace at Marib which al-Idrisi says was the palace of Bilqis, the wife of Sulayman. Marib was in ancient times a large city thick with people and famous in Arabia, and it held the palace of Sulayman ibn Dawud called Sirwah, of which only a worn ruin is left. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 153}

## The dam
At Marib stood the dam called al-Arim, whose fame is known among all nations. Most of its people were Saba', of the Himyarite Arab tribes, and they had across the water-course a great, soundly built dam that held the water back about twenty fathoms; each tribe had its fixed share of water to draw from it and spend on its fields by an equal division, and their houses stood beside it. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 153}

## Collapse and distances
Al-Idrisi relates that the great flood came upon them, lifted the dam and swept over the city and the villages near it, that the Arabs were scattered, and that traces of the city remained to which people of Hadramawt later returned and resettled it down to his time. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 153} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 154} From Zafar to the village of Marib is three stages, and from Marib to the city of Shibam in Hadramawt four stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 153} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 154}

Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari counts it among the famous monuments of Yemen, together with the dam of Ma'rib. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p. 232}
