## Summary
Darjah Jank at Basra, one of the holdings of Thaqif — so named, in al-Baladhuri, for the lawsuits fought over it, jank being Persian for clamour. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503}

## Details
In their list of Basra sites named for their people, al-Baladhuri's informants said: Darjah Jank is one of the properties of Thaqif, and it was called that because of the disputes that took place over it, jank in Persian meaning clamour. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503} It was thus an estate or site among the Thaqif holdings in the Basra sawad, upon which this name settled because of the quarrels and litigation it saw, the Persian word jank glossed as uproar and contention. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503} Its mention falls in the same passage as Anasan, named for Anas ibn Malik in a qati'a from Ziyad, and the canal of Bishr, named for Bishr ibn Muslim, the brother of Qutayba. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 503}
