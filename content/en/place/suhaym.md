## Summary
Suhaym, or Khawlan Dhu Suhaym, is a strong fortress in the Tihama lowlands of Yemen. Al-Idrisi says its people have strength and pride. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reaches Suhaym on the road from Sanaa. One stage along that road brings him to Haran al-Qarin, a small village that is nonetheless well settled, with running water and a few palms. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148} From there it is one more stage to Khawlan Dhu Suhaym, which he calls "a fortified castle; its people have strength and pride." {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}

The Mecca road to Dhu Suhaym, described on the preceding page, passes Yalamlam and Qayna, and ends at Suhaym from the Yemeni side. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.147}

Al-Idrisi places Suhaym among all the lands he has named in Tihama, which he describes as part of Yemen: a country of interlocking mountains, bounded on the west by the Red Sea and on the east by a range running from south to north. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.148}
