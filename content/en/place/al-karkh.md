## Summary
Al-Karkh was a quarter of [[place/baghdad|Baghdad]]. Al-Baladhuri reports that al-Mansur placed the market-centre in al-Karkh, ordered the merchants to build shops, and bound them to the ghella rent. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 415}

## Details
Writing on the foundation of Baghdad, al-Baladhuri says al-Mansur made al-Karkh the place where the markets met, ordered the merchants to erect their shops there and charged them the ghella {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 415}. This belonged to al-Mansur's ordering of his city — laying out its markets and regulating its inhabitants' livelihoods — after which al-Karkh grew into one of Baghdad's largest and busiest quarters {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 415}. He also records that al-Mansur handed a palace at al-Qatul to his mawla Ashinas the Turk, and that he granted estates in Baghdad to his commanders, companions and secretaries {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 415}. Among the names of the city's quarters he notes that al-Mukharrim took its name from Mukharrim ibn Shurayh ibn Hazn al-Harithi who settled there {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p. 415}.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut opens the entry by giving the vocalisation of al-Karkh, with a fatha, a sukun and a dotted kha, and says he does not think the word Arabic but Nabataean; he notes that its speakers use a verb meaning to gather water into one place. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.447} A separate paragraph on the Karkh of Baghdad relates that when al-Mansur built Baghdad he put the markets at the gates of the round city, and that after an envoy of the Byzantine emperor warned that merchants could act as spies, he ordered the traders out of the city and had a market laid out in ranked rows between the Sarat and the Isa canal. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.448} Yaqut reports from al-Khatib that al-Mansur laid no rent on the markets in his lifetime and that al-Mahdi imposed the kharaj on the shops, and adds another account in which the traders were moved to al-Karkh because the smoke of their stalls blackened the walls of the city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.448} He adds that al-Karkh stood first in the middle of Baghdad with the quarters around it, but in his own day was a single quarter amid ruins; he describes the neighbouring quarters and states that the people of al-Karkh were all Imami Shi'a, while those of Bab al-Basra, Nahr al-Qalla'in and Bab al-Muhawwal were Sunni. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.448}
