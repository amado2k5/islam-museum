## Overview
In the Mu'jam al-buldan Qartajanna is a great ancient city of Ifriqiya on the coast, twelve miles from Tunis, left in ruins so that Tunis was built from its stones, and razed by Hassan ibn al-Numan around 70 AH. A second entry concerns another Qartajanna in al-Andalus. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.323}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives the name with a fatha on the qaf, a sukun on the ra, an undotted ta, a jim and a doubled nun, and reports a view that the city's name was Qarta, with janna, garden, added for its pleasantness. He calls it an old town of the regions of Ifriqiya and gives, from Ptolemy, its position in degrees with astrological details he does not explain. He says it was a great city of lofty buildings, its walls of white marble, with countless columns of many colours, and that after it fell into ruin Muslims built several towns with its marble, and that it has lain ruined since the time of Uthman ibn Affan. He describes two red columns of hard stone in the king's hall, one standing and one fallen, each thirty-six spans around and more than forty cubits high. It lies on the sea, twelve miles from Tunis, and Tunis was rebuilt from its ruin and its stones. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.323}

## Water and Hassan ibn al-Numan
Yaqut says there was no flowing spring or channel near the city, so its builders brought water from the region of Kairouan, three days away, over arches and high columns carrying the channel, which the people there call al-hanaya. He relates from the chroniclers that Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan appointed Hassan ibn al-Numan al-Azdi to Ifriqiya; Hassan settled at Kairouan and asked which city was strongest, and was told Qartajanna, the royal seat. He fought its people hard; they asked for safe conduct and he gave it, they broke faith, and he returned, took the city and razed it, the first to order its destruction, about the year 70. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.323}

## Another Qartajanna
Yaqut closes with another city in al-Andalus, called Qartajanna al-Halfa, near Elche in the districts of Tudmir, also ruined because the sea took most of it, though some people remained; it was made on the model of the African city. That is a different place from the one this record means. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.323}

## The museum's identification
The museum's record calls Carthage a Tunisian town, while Yaqut describes the old city that lay in ruins. The link rests on the site: Yaqut puts the city on the coast twelve miles from Tunis, which is where Carthage lies, though he does not give the modern town's name. Hassan ibn al-Numan has a record, [[person/hassan-ibn-al-numan|Hassan ibn al-Numan]], and there are records for [[place/tunis|Tunis]] and [[place/ifriqiya|Ifriqiya]].
