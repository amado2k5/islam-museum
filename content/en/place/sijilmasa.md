## Overview
Sijilmasa is a city in the desert near the land of the Lamtuna, and it is the caravan route to the Sudan, according to al-Idrisi. It lies about forty days' caravan journey from Sala and Takrur, and between it and Awlil there are about forty stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 18}

## The city
Al-Idrisi calls it a large, populous city, a destination for those coming and going, with many gardens and orchards and pleasant districts. It has no fortress; it is palaces, houses and continuous buildings along a river of abundant water that reaches it from the east out of the desert and rises in summer as the Nile does. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 225}

## Farming and trade
The river waters its fields as the farmers of Egypt irrigate theirs, with large gains, and it has many palms and several kinds of dates, among them the barni. Its people grow cotton, cumin, caraway and henna and send them to the rest of the Maghrib. Its buildings are fine, though in al-Idrisi's time its opponents had destroyed and burned most of them. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 226}

## Roads
From Sijilmasa to [[place/aghmat|Aghmat]] and Rikka is about eight stages, and to Dar'a three long stages. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 226} The road to it is thirteen stages by Safru and Qal'at Mahdi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 243} Caravans go from Tlemcen to [[place/fes|Fez]], then Safru, Tadla, Aghmat, the Bani Dar'a and Sijilmasa. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 249} Al-Idrisi also gives the road from Egypt to Sijilmasa stage by stage, the road the Almoravids took in the year 530. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 344}
