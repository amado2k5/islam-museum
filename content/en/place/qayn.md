## Overview
Qayn is a city built of mud brick, with a wall of packed earth. It has a citadel (qasaba) with a ditch around it, a congregational mosque, and the seat of government inside the citadel. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 462}
Its people drink water brought to them in channels, its orchards are few, and its villages are scattered. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 462}
It is the chief town of Quhistan. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 462} It lies two stages from Yanabadh. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 462} Zuzan is three stages away. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 463}
Two stages from it, on the Nishapur road, is the place where Najahi clay is loaded, carried to all regions for eating. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 462} Al-Idrisi gives two distances for the road between Qayn and Nishapur, ten stages in one passage and about nine in another, and eight stages from Qayn to Herat. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 464} {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 691}
Al-Idrisi places Tabas three stages from Qayn and larger in extent than Yanabadh, and Khur two days away, a town smaller than Tabas. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 463}
