## Overview
Tabriz is the best-known city of Azerbaijan. Yaqut records that it was once a village, until Ruwad al-Azdi, who had seized power in Azerbaijan in the days of al-Mutawakkil, settled there. His son al-Wajna and his brothers then built palaces in it and walled it, and people settled there with him. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 766}

In earlier times the capital of Azerbaijan was al-Maragha; in Yaqut's time Tabriz was its capital and its largest city. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 164}

Yaqut describes it as a fine, populous city with strong walls of fired brick and gypsum, flowing rivers in its midst, and orchards all around; its fruit was cheap. He thought the best apricots he had tasted were the Tabriz apricots called al-Mawsul. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 766}

The Tatars passed through it when they ravaged the land in the year 618 AH; its people made terms with them by paying tribute, and the city was spared. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 767}

Several men of learning came from it, among them Abu Zakariyya Yahya ibn Ali al-Khatib al-Tabrizi, who studied with Abu al-Ala al-Ma'arri in Syria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 767}

Yaqut also records another place of the same name near Khilat in Armenia, whose apples were famed for quality, abundance and low price. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-yaqut p. 494}
