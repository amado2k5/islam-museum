## Overview
Farghana is a region beyond the river (Transoxiana), on the river Syr Darya. Salih ibn Muslim al-Bahili conquered from it Kasan, Awrasht, Sukhr and Akhsikat, its old city. Nasr ibn Sayyar was in his army. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Farghana with fatha then sukun, a dotted ghayn and a nun after the alif: a city and a broad kura beyond the River adjoining the lands of Turkestan, in a corner on the Khaytal side toward the sunrise, right of the traveller for the Turks' country; rich in good, wide of rustaq, said once to have held forty minbars; between it and Samarkand fifty farsakhs, and Khujanda is of its province. He gives Ptolemy's longitude, latitude and ascendants, then says the mountains stretching between it and the Turks bear grapes, walnuts, apples and every fruit, roses, violets and herbs, free to all with no owner and none to forbid the taker — as the pistachios of its mountains and of much of Transoxiana are free, unlike any other land. Al-Istakhri says Farghana is the name of the whole region, spread over the breadth of its towns and villages, its qasaba Akhsikath, and there are no more villages beyond the River than Farghana's, a single village's bounds sometimes stretching a stage. In Ibn al-Faqih's book Anushirwan built it, moved one of every household into it and named it Azharkhana, "of every house"; and Farghana is also said to be a village of Fars. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.253-254}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Farghana is the record's Transoxanian region, whose qasaba Akhsikath he gives on al-Istakhri's word (Khujanda in its province; the Fars village of the same name is another place). The fifty farsakhs to Samarkand is the printed figure; by the record's own point the distance is about 418.9 km, a museum calculation rather than a source number.
