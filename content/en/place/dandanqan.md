## Summary
al-Dandanqan is a city that al-Idrisi places two stages from Marw on the road to Markhas, a fine city with a wall and a fortress. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.477}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that al-Dandanqan lies two stages from Marw on the road to Markhas, and that it is a fine city possessing a wall, a fortress, markets, bathhouses and hostelries, with a congregational mosque and a minbar {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.477}. Among its neighbouring towns he mentions al-Qarinin, a fine, fortified, fertile city with a market, a congregational mosque where the sermon is held, and running waters {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.477}. al-Dandanqan is thus one of the flourishing cities of the Marw region, combining fortification with prosperity: a wall surrounds it, a fortress protects it, and within it are the signs of commercial life — markets and inns where travellers lodge, bathhouses for its people, and a Friday mosque in which the congregation gathers and the sermon is delivered from its minbar {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.477}.
