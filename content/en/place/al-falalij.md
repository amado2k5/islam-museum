## Overview
al-Falalij: a place mentioned by al-Baladhuri. Khalid came to it on his way back from Baniqya; a band of non-Arabs was there and they scattered, and Khalid met no resistance from them. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.343}

An additional source is needed to fix its location.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut's entry for al-Falalij (vol 4 p.270) is one sentence: citing al-Layth, it says the falalij of the Sawad are its villages, one of which is Fallujah. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.270} The Fallujah entry repeats al-Layth, names Great and Little Fallujah as two large villages of the Sawad of Baghdad and Kufa near 'Ayn al-Tamr, also called Upper and Lower, and quotes al-Sihah that fallujah is land made fit for sowing, hence the place on the Euphrates, with the plural falalij. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.275-276} The district of al-Bahqubadh al-A'la has Upper and Lower Fallujah and 'Ayn al-Tamr among its tassujes. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.174} The ahl al-siyar list al-Falalij among the places the Muslims raided after Mihran's fall, with Sura, Kaskar, al-Sarat and the Istans. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.457}

## The museum's identification
The record (al-Baladhuri, p.343) says Khalid came to al-Falalij on his way back from Baniqya, found a band of non-Arabs there who scattered, and met no resistance. Yaqut's entry does not name Khalid or a single site: falalij is a collective name for villages of the Sawad. The record's name may therefore point to such a village or district; that is the museum's reading. The record's point (32.55, 44.35) is 103.5 km from the museum's Fallujah point, so the record does not place it at Fallujah. The coordinates are not changed.
