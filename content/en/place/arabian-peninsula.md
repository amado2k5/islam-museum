## Overview
The Arabian Peninsula, Jazirat al-Arab, is the large region holding the Hijaz, Najd, Yemen, Oman and more. Yaqut al-Hamawi's Mu'jam al-Buldan has an entry "Jazirat al-Arab" on the reason for the name, its limits and its divisions. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.137}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut states that scholars differed on its limits and that the best account is the one Abu al-Mundhir Hisham ibn Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ib relayed with a chain to Ibn Abbas. On the name, the account says the rivers and seas surround the Arabs' land on every side so that it is like an island among the islands of the sea. It then traces the Euphrates and the Encircling Sea around it: from Iraq, Basra and Abadan to al-Qatif, Hajar, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman, al-Shihr, Hadramawt, Aden, Yemen, Jedda, al-Tur, Ayla and the Qulzum of Egypt, with the Nile joining from the upper Sudan on the west, and then the coasts of Palestine, Syria and Qinnasrin. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.137} See [[place/hadramawt|Hadramawt]] and [[place/oman|Oman]].

In this account the Arabs divided their peninsula into five parts in their verse and reports: Tihama, the Hijaz, Najd, al-Arud and Yemen. The mountain al-Sarat, the greatest of the Arabs' mountains, runs from the depth of Yemen to the edge of the Syrian desert; the Arabs called it the Hijaz because it "separates" (hajaza) the low land, Tihama, from the high land, Najd. What lies west of it to the sea coast is Tihama; what lies east of it, the plains of Najd to the edge of Iraq, is Najd; al-Yamama and Bahrain and what adjoins them are al-Arud; and what lies beyond Talith toward Sanaa, Hadramawt, al-Shihr and Oman is Yemen. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.137} The account places Mecca in Tihama, and Medina and al-Ta'if in Najd and the uplands. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.137} See [[place/tihama|Tihama]], [[place/hijaz|the Hijaz]], [[place/najd|Najd]], [[place/yemen|Yemen]], [[place/mecca|Mecca]], [[place/medina|Medina]] and [[place/taif|al-Ta'if]].

Yaqut then quotes other views. Ibn al-A'rabi held that the peninsula is what lies above Tih and is called an island because it cuts across the Euphrates and the Tigris. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.137} Al-Haytham ibn Adi held that it runs from al-Udhayb to Hadramawt; and al-Asma'i that its length reaches Aden Abyan and its breadth runs from al-Ubulla to Jedda. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.138} He also quotes al-Asma'i that it has four parts, Yemen, Najd, the Hijaz and the Ghawr, which is Tihama, and that it includes the Hijaz and Tihama, Yemen, Saba, al-Ahqaf, al-Yamama, al-Shihr, Hajar, Oman, al-Ta'if, Najran, al-Hijr, the lands of Thamud and of Kinda, the mountains of Tayyi and what lies between. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.138} See [[place/najran|Najran]].
