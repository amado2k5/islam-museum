## Overview
Hadramawt is a region in the south of the Arabian Peninsula, and it is part of [[place/yemen|Yemen]]. Al-Baladhuri reports that the Prophet appointed over it Ziyad ibn Labid al-Bayadi al-Ansari, and that his transmitters agree on this. Among the land the Prophet granted in it was a piece to Alqama ibn Wa'il al-Hadrami. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.93}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut enters Hadramawt (with a fatha then a sukun, and the ra' and mim vowelled): two compounded names, longitude 71 degrees, latitude 12 degrees; he details its declension, the nisba Hadrami, and that its mim may be vowelled with a damma. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.269} On its name there are sayings: some said it was named for Hadir Mayit, its first settler, later lightened by dropping the alif — Ibn al-Kalbi gives its Torah name as Hadir Mit — some for Hadramawt ibn Yaqtan ibn 'Amir ibn Shalikh, some for 'Amr ibn Qays ibn Mu'awiya ibn Jashm, some that he was 'Amir ibn Qahtan nicknamed because when he attended a battle he killed many in it, and Abu 'Ubayda said Hadramawt ibn Qahtan settled this place and it was named for him, so it is both a place-name and a tribe-name. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.270} For him it is a wide district east of Aden near the sea, ringed by sands called al-Ahqaf; in it is the tomb of Hud and near it the well of Barhut mentioned in its place; it has two cities, Tarim and Shibam, with fortresses and villages. Ibn al-Faqih calls it a mikhlaf of Yemen with sands between it and the sea, thirty farsakhs between it and the mikhlaf of Sida' and seventy-two between it and San'a'; others say eleven days' march, and al-Istakhri a month from Aden. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.270} As for its conquest: the Prophet corresponded with its people and they entered his obedience, and al-Ash'ath ibn Qays came to him among a dozen-odd Muslim riders and asked him to appoint a man of them, so he appointed over them Ziyad ibn Labid al-Bayadi al-Ansari and joined Kinda to him; when the Prophet died Banu Wali'a ibn Shurahbil ibn Mu'awiya apostatized. Yaqut tells the story: Ziyad seized a young she-camel from a Kindite lad for the sadaqa, and Haritha ibn Suraqa ibn Ma'di Karib loosed its tether and the apostates rallied to him; their four kings — Makhus, Mashrah, Jamad and Abdagha, sons of Ma'di Karib, each holding a wadi — were taken drunk in their stronghold and slaughtered; then al-Ash'ath ibn Qays came out against Ziyad and the Muslims were routed, so Ziyad wrote to Abu Bakr for aid and he sent al-Muhajir ibn Abi Umayya; they took refuge in the fortress of al-Nujayr and were besieged until al-Ash'ath asked for safe-conduct, was seized and sent to Abu Bakr a captive in year 12; he said "keep me for your war — I only stinted my property", so Abu Bakr pardoned him and married him to his sister Umm Farwa; he stayed in Medina until he went out to fight in Iraq and died at Kufa, and al-Hasan prayed over him after the peace of Mu'awiya. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.270; 2 p.271}

## The museum's identification
The record's Hadramawt is the southern-Arabian region of Yemen, and it is Yaqut's "wide district east of Aden"; his appointment of Ziyad ibn Labid al-Bayadi by the Prophet agrees with the record's al-Baladhuri source, and what is new is his detailed sequel of the Wali'a apostasy and the career of al-Ash'ath ibn Qays as he transmits it. The tomb of Hud, the well of Barhut and the Ahqaf belong to his description of the district, and Tarim and Shibam are its two cities.
