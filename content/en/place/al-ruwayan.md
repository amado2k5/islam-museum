## Overview
al-Ruwayan: a district of Tabaristan. al-Baladhuri reports that Sa'id ibn al-As conquered it together with the plain of Tabaristan and Dunbawand. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Ruwayan (with a damma, a sukun, a ya' and a nun): a large city of the mountains of Tabaristan and a wide kura — the largest city in those mountains, as they say the largest city of the Tabaristan plain is Amul and of its mountains Ruwayan. It lies in the fourth clime, longitude 76 degrees 35 minutes, latitude 37 degrees 10 minutes, with twelve farsakhs between Gilan and Ruwayan. Some transmitters said Ruwayan is not part of Tabaristan but a broad province of its own ringed by great mountains and many kingdoms; it had belonged to the kingdom of Daylam until 'Amr ibn al-'Ala', lord of the Jawsaq at Rayy, conquered it, built a city in it and gave it a minbar; its villages, as reported, could put out more than fifty thousand fighting men, and its kharaj as assessed by al-Rashid was four hundred and fifty thousand dirhams; among its towns is one called Kajja where the governor resides, its mountains join the mountains of Rayy and its approach is from the Rayy side, and the first to conquer it was Sa'id ibn al-'As in year 29 or 30 while he was 'Uthman's governor of Kufa. Among the scholars ascribed to it is Abu al-Mahasin 'Abd al-Wahid ibn Isma'il al-Ruwayni al-Tabari, the Shafi'i judge who was killed in the congregational mosque of Amul in year 501 — also said 502 — and others. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.104}

## The museum's identification
The record's al-Ruwayan is the kura of Tabaristan that Sa'id ibn al-'As conquered on al-Baladhuri's authority, and it is Yaqut's wide kura and mountain city (he writes it Ruwayan with a ya'); his dating of Sa'id's conquest to year 29 or 30 agrees with the record's source. The claim by "some" that it is a province of its own rather than part of Tabaristan is a transmitted disagreement the museum leaves attributed.
