## Overview
Ghadir Khumm is a pool and a stopping place on the pilgrims' road between Mecca and Medina, near al-Juhfa. The Prophet stopped there in 632 CE. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

The location is approximate, and the description of the stop needs an additional source.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Under ghadir Yaqut explains the word as any rain-water left behind in a pool, small or large, that does not last to the height of summer; and that Ghadir Khumm lies between Mecca and Medina, two miles from al-Juhfa, noting that Khumm is treated in its own place. Some linguists, he says, derive ghadir from ghadr (treachery): a man passes it when it holds water, comes back hoping for it and finds it dry, and dies of thirst. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.188} In the same entry he lists other Ghadirs: a water of Ja'far ibn Kilab; Ghadir al-Salb, a water of the Banu Judhayma (al-Salb being, for al-Asma'i, a marked mountain); a town or village half a day from Qal'at Bani Hammad in the Maghreb (whence the nisba of the teacher Abu Abdallah al-Ghadiri, from al-Salafi); from Abu Ziyad, al-Ghadir among the waters of al-Dabab three nights south of Hima Dariyya; and al-Ghadir al-Asfal of Rabi'a ibn Kilab. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.188}

## The museum's identification
Yaqut's Ghadir Khumm is the record's pool between Mecca and Medina, two miles from al-Juhfa by his account; he gives its position and the explanation of its name only — he records no halt and no event there, so the record's description of the 632 CE stop rests on another source. By the two records' own points, Ghadir Khumm and al-Juhfa stand about 14.0 km apart, where Yaqut gives two miles.
