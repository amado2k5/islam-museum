## Overview
The Umayyad Caliphate was the caliphal state ruled by the Banu Umayya from Syria, and its story in the sources gathers around Damascus: delegations met there to pledge allegiance to Yazid ibn Muawiya, Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan died there, al-Walid addressed the people from its pulpit, and al-Walid built its great mosque there. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.145} {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.321} {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.428} Muhammad Bey al-Khudari counts the life of the state from the general pledge to Muawiya on 25 Rabi al-Awwal 41 AH to the killing of Marwan II ibn Muhammad before the end of Dhu al-Hijja 132 AH, 91 years and nine months, and counts three caliphs from the branch of Harb ibn Umayya and ten from the branch of Abu al-As. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state pp.268-269} This page tells the state's history from al-Khudari's book, which is a set of university lectures on Islamic history later gathered and edited, and adds Hasan Ibrahim Hasan's description of the Umayyad Mosque.

## Muawiya ibn Abi Sufyan and Syria
Al-Khudari relates that Muawiya was born in Mecca fifteen years before the Hijra, became a Muslim on the day of the conquest of Mecca at the age of twenty-three, and after his conversion wrote in the Prophet's presence. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.269} Abu Bakr gave him command of an army sent as reinforcement to his brother Yazid ibn Abi Sufyan, and he led the vanguard in the conquest of Sidon, Arqa, Jubayl and Beirut; Umar then gave him Jordan, and when Yazid died in the plague of Amwas Umar gave him Yazid's work over Damascus and what went with it. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.269} Under Uthman all Syria was joined to Muawiya, and the governors of its provinces came under him. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.269} Al-Khudari adds that Umar saw in him good statecraft, firm management and trustworthiness, and that the Banu Abd Shams passed from leadership in the Jahiliyya to leadership in Islam. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.268} See [[person/muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Muawiya ibn Abi Sufyan]], [[place/damascus|Damascus]] and [[place/syria|Syria]].

On the rise of the caliphate, al-Khudari relates that when news of Uthman's death reached Muawiya he refused to pledge to Ali, accusing him of leniency over Uthman's killers and of sheltering them in his army; the Syrians pledged to him to seek Uthman's blood-right; then came Siffin, which ended in arbitration, after which the Syrians pledged the caliphate to Muawiya; Ali was killed and al-Hasan ibn Ali handed the matter to Muawiya, so the people of Iraq and Syria agreed on his pledge, and that year, 41 AH, was called the Year of the Community. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.269} Al-Khudari comments that Muawiya was not chosen by a general election of the people of loosing and binding but by the Syrians after the arbitration, and that the Iraqis' pledge came by force of events and then turned into acceptance by the whole community except the Kharijites. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.269} This is al-Khudari's analysis. See [[event/battle-of-siffin|the Battle of Siffin]] and [[event/am-al-jamaa|the Year of the Community]].

## Succession
Al-Khudari says the Umayyads made it their way that the caliph appoints his heir, that Muawiya named his son Yazid, and that he differed in asking the provincial governors to send delegations from their cities, put the matter to them at Damascus and asked them to choose for themselves, whereupon they put forward Yazid. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.145} He says some senior Quraysh Companions older than Yazid did not submit to Muawiya's will, and the great events of Yazid's reign followed: the going out and killing of al-Husayn ibn Ali and the dispute with Ibn al-Zubayr; Yazid named his son Muawiya, who withdrew, and the matter then settled on Marwan ibn al-Hakam. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.145}

Among the reports of Muawiya's time in Kufa, al-Khudari relates that Ziyad sent Hujr ibn Adi and his companions to Muawiya, that they reached Marj Adhra near Damascus, where he ordered eight of them killed, and that A'isha sent Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith to him about them but he arrived after they had been killed. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.274} This is al-Khudari's account from the sources.

## Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan and the coinage
Al-Khudari relates that before Abd al-Malik the Muslims used dirhams struck in Persian lands and dinars struck in Byzantine lands; in 84 AH Abd al-Malik struck Islamic dirhams and dinars, fixing the dirham at fourteen qirats and the dinar at twenty, with the name of God engraved first and, a year later, wording from the Sura of Sincerity (see [[passage/quran-112-1|Sura of Sincerity]]); he ordered al-Hajjaj to strike them in Iraq, jurists disliked them so they were called makruha, and then they were struck in the other provinces, and anyone who struck coin off the sultan's die was severely punished. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.320} See [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]].

He says that after the death of his brother Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan, Abd al-Malik named his sons al-Walid and then Sulayman as heirs, and that Said ibn al-Musayyib refused the pledge, was beaten and jailed by the governor of Medina, and Abd al-Malik wrote to the governor rebuking him. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.320} Abd al-Malik died at Damascus in mid-Shawwal 86 AH / October 705 CE after a caliphate of twenty-one years and a month and a half from the start of Ramadan 65 AH. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.320} Al-Khudari describes him as firm of will and steady of spirit, who gathered the turbulent community under one caliph, though the victims along the way were very many; among what he counts against him are his saying from the pulpit that he would behead anyone who told him to fear God, and his treachery to Amr ibn Said after granting him safety, which "they said" was the first act of treachery in Islam. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.321}

## Al-Walid and the conquests
Al-Khudari says al-Walid was given the pledge on the day his father died and climbed the pulpit at Damascus before entering his house. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.321} He names three commanders of his reign: Qutayba ibn Muslim, who conquered vast lands and joined them to the Islamic realm, where Islam spread; Musa ibn Nusayr, who conquered al-Andalus and brought Islam into Europe, whose story al-Khudari defers to a separate chapter; and Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik, who campaigned against the Byzantines each year and took fortresses including Tuwana, Amuriya, Heraclea and Tarsus. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.328} Al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf died in Iraq in Shawwal 95 AH, after twenty years as governor of the two Iraqs. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.328} See [[person/al-walid-ibn-abd-al-malik|al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik]], [[person/qutayba-ibn-muslim|Qutayba ibn Muslim]], [[person/musa-ibn-nusayr|Musa ibn Nusayr]], [[person/al-hajjaj-ibn-yusuf|al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf]] and [[place/al-andalus|al-Andalus]].

## The Umayyad Mosque and architecture
Hasan Ibrahim Hasan relates that al-Walid pulled down the church that stood on the site and built the mosque of Damascus in its place, lavishing such care on it that it was said he spent seven years of his state's land-tax on it; citing al-Masudi, he mentions an inscription in the mosque saying it was ordered built, and the church demolished, in Dhu al-Hijja 87 AH. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.428} He calls the mosque a marvel of Arab and Byzantine art that still keeps its splendour, and reports that people criticised al-Walid's extravagance and that he addressed them, answering that the treasury held stipends for eighteen years. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 pp.428-429} He records that Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz resolved to strip the mosaics because of the cost to the treasury, and gave this up after Byzantine envoys visited the mosque and their chief showed his amazement at the building. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.429} He also mentions that the mosque of Kairouan was built by Uqba ibn Nafi and that Hisham's governor found it too small, so the caliph ordered the neighbouring land bought; and he says Islamic architecture was born in the Umayyad age and then grew, uniting artistic elements from the provinces of the state. {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim 1 p.429} See [[place/qayrawan|Kairouan]].

## The fall of the state
Al-Khudari says the troubles in Syria and the Hijaz kept Marwan ibn Muhammad from Khurasan, which helped the partisans of the Abbasids and their chief Abu Muslim al-Khurasani take it and then Iraq. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.353} Abu al-Abbas al-Saffah was given the pledge at Kufa in Rabi al-Awwal 132 AH and sent his uncle Abdallah ibn Ali, who met Marwan on the river Zab in Jumada II and defeated him; Salih ibn Ali then pursued Marwan to Busir in Egypt, where he was killed before the end of Dhu al-Hijja, and with that the days of the Umayyad state ended and the Abbasid age began. {cite:khudari-umayyad-state p.353} See [[place/abbasid-caliphate|the Abbasid Caliphate]], [[person/marwan-ibn-muhammad|Marwan ibn Muhammad]], [[place/al-zab|al-Zab]] and [[polity/umayyad-caliphate|the Umayyad Caliphate]].
