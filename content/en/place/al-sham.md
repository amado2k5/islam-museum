## Overview
Al-Sham (Syria): the peninsula's north-western frontier; seat of the Arab kings who served Rome as agents, from the Daja'ima to the Ghassanids.

## The place
The peninsula's north-western side is a gateway into the African continent, and its north-eastern side a key to the continent of Europe {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

The Romans cultivated Arab kings there to keep the desert Arabs from mischief and to be a force against Persia: first Banu Salih the Daja'ima, then the Ghassanids after them until the battle of Yarmuk {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}.

## For young visitors
Al-Sham is the land north of the peninsula where Damascus is; Arab kings ruled it in service of Rome's Caesar.
