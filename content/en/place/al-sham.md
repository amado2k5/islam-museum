## Summary
Syria is the northern boundary of the Arabian Peninsula, together with part of Iraq, in the description of The Sealed Nectar {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.15}. It was the seat of the Arab kings who acted as agents of Rome, from the [[group/dajaima|Dajaima]] to the [[polity/ghassanid-kingdom|Ghassanids]] {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27}.

## Location
The north-western part of the peninsula is a gateway into the African continent, and the north-eastern part is a key to Europe. The north of the peninsula was therefore a point of descent for peoples and a centre for the exchange of trade, culture, religion and the arts {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.16}.

## The stories of Abraham and the tribes
[[person/ibrahim|Abraham]] migrated from Ur to Harran, and then to [[place/palestine|Palestine]], which he made the base of his call {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.18}. [[person/jafna-ibn-amr|Jafna ibn Amr]] al-Azdi went to Syria, where he and his sons settled; he was the father of the Ghassanid kings {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.17}. [[group/qudaa|Qudaa]] migrated to Syria when Ardashir took control of the Arabs settled on the edge of his realm {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.25}. In the author's account, the prophet Barmiya took Ma'add ibn Adnan to Syria at the time of Nebuchadnezzar's invasion {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.28}.

## The Arab kings there
The Romans made the Dajaima, from Qudaa, to stop the desert Arabs from causing trouble and to serve as a force against Persia. Then the Ghassanids prevailed over them, and the Romans made them kings over the Arabs of Syria until the Battle of Yarmouk in 13 AH {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.27}. The tribes of the Syrian desert were subject to the Ghassanids in name only {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.32}.

## Religion
[[person/amr-ibn-luhayy|Amr ibn Luhayy]] saw the people of Syria worshipping idols. He approved of it and thought it true, because Syria was the place of the messengers and the scriptures, so he brought Hubal to Mecca {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.35}. Many people of Syria followed the Sabian religion in ancient times {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.41}. Crafts were practised in the outskirts of Syria, along with Yemen and al-Hira {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq p.46}.

## For young visitors
Syria is the land north of the peninsula, where Damascus is. Its kings were Arabs who served the Roman emperor.
