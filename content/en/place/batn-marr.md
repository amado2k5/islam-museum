## Overview
Batn Marr: a place on the road from Mecca to al-Juhfa, about sixteen miles from Mecca. In it is a water spring in a sandy channel, and around it are small palm groves, to which a people of the Arabs resort. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.141}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut lists Batn Marr among the districts of Mecca, gives the name with a fatha on the mim and a doubled ra', and says that the Wadi of the Two Nakhlas meets there so that the two become one wadi; he sends the reader to his entries on Nakhla and Marr. For the place he gives two verses by Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali that name Batn Marr together with al-Raji' and Dhu Sidr (described here, not quoted). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.449} In the entry on al-Nakhla al-Shamiyya he describes two valleys of Hudhayl, two nights from Mecca, that join at Batn Marr and then form one valley; the Yemeni Nakhla comes down from Qarn al-Manazil, their meeting place is called al-Bustan, and he notes that ordinary people say "the garden of Ibn 'Amir" where al-Asma'i, Abu 'Ubayda and others say it is the garden of Ibn Ma'mar. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.277} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.414} In the entry on Marr he treats Marr al-Zahran as a place one stage from Mecca, quotes 'Arram that Marr is the village and al-Zahran the wadi, with many springs, palms and sycamore figs belonging to Aslam, Hudhayl and Ghadira, and quotes al-Waqidi that Marr is five miles from Mecca. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.104} He does not say in these entries that Batn Marr and Marr al-Zahran are one place. He names two villages in or by Batn Marr: al-Humayma, in Batn Marr, between Suru'a and al-Burayra', with a spring and palms, {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.307} and Wasit, between Batn Marr and the Wadi Nakhla, whose palms his friend the hafiz al-Najjar told him he saw from far off while at Batn Marr. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.351}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Idrisi, is a place on the road from Mecca to al-Juhfa about sixteen miles out, with a spring in a sandy watercourse and a few palms where Bedouin camp. Yaqut's entry for Batn Marr itself has no spring, palms or distance; those details appear in his entries for al-Humayma and for Marr. That the record's spring and palms are those of al-Humayma or of Wasit is the museum's reading, which neither Yaqut nor al-Idrisi states. Yaqut's figures do not agree with each other (one stage and five miles on al-Waqidi's authority under Marr, two nights under the Syrian Nakhla), and the museum does not choose among them. The record's point is 16.7 km from the museum's Mecca point. See [[place/mecca|Mecca]] and [[place/al-juhfa|al-Juhfa]].
