## Summary
Kayf is a handsome town built of mud brick and clay, populous with imports and exports and much trade; al-Idrisi says its people enjoy sound circumstances and wealth circulated in many kinds of commerce. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.475}

## Details
Al-Idrisi records that east of Balkh lies the rustaq called Kanj, containing three towns: Bin, Kayf and Baghshur {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.475}. Kayf itself he describes as a handsome town busy with inward and outward trade, whose people have sound circumstances and wealth turned over in many kinds of commerce; its buildings are of clay, and it has abundant running water, orchards, grain and vines {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.475}. Bin is larger than Bushanj, populous and rich in trade with wealthy inhabitants, while Baghshur equals Bushanj and nearly Kayf in size and has gardens, orchards and pleasure grounds {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.475}. As for distances: from Herat to Bin is one stage, from Bin to Kayf one stage, and from Kayf to Baghshur one stage {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.475}.
