## Summary
Al-Anjiya was one of the Zanj islands in the sea of Zanj; its city is called al-Anquja in the islanders' tongue, and most of its people were Muslims in al-Idrisi's time. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.61}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that facing the coastal lands of the Zanj lie islands called the Ranij islands, numerous and wide, whose people are very dark, and everything grown there — sorghum, sugar cane and camphor trees — is black in colour {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.61}. Among the Ranij islands in this section is the island of al-Anjiya, whose city where its people dwell is called al-Anquja in the Ranij language; its folk are a mixed population and most of them are Muslims at this time {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.61}. It is named in his account of the shipping trade between Oman and the islands of the Zanj.
