## Summary
Halla was a large congregational populous city of the land of Shasuniyya, a hundred miles west of the city of Iqraqu. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.890}

## Details
Al-Idrisi states that from the city of Iqraqu to the city of Halla in the western direction is a hundred miles; it belongs to the land of Shasuniyya, and Halla is a large congregational populous city {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.890}. Likewise from the city of Iqraqu to Niyun Bark is a hundred miles, Niyun Bark also being of the land of Shasuniyya; between Halla and Niyun Bark are forty miles, and from Niyun Bark to the city of Wadhanbarka westward sixty miles, a noble city {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.890}. The author counts among the towns of the land of Shasuniyya comprised in this part the cities of Harbrd, Wadhanbarka, Niyun Bark, Halla and Mashla {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.882}. From the city of Iqraqu to the city of Janazna is a hundred miles eastward, a populous fine city, and from Janazna to the city of Ratsalaba sixty miles {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.890}. Halla thus appears in his scheme among the great congregational cities of the land of Shasuniyya, west of Iqraqu in the lands of Baluniyya, on a web of roads joining Niyun Bark, Wadhanbarka and Mashla.
