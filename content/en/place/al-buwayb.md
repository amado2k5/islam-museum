## Overview
al-Buwayb: a place near the Euphrates bridge. al-Baladhuri reports that Mihran ibn Mihrbandadh was killed there in the year 14 AH. It is also reported that both its sides were filled with bones until they were level, and that dust then covered them in the time of the strife. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.354}

In the time of the Sasanian kings, the area between al-Sukun and Banu Sulaym was an outlet for the Euphrates, which discharged into the interior. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407 p.354}

The event is recorded by a single source, so it needs an additional source.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut has two entries under the name al-Buwayb, "the little gate". The first is a gap between two mountains, which, on Ya'qub's authority, is the entrance by which the people of the Hijaz come into Egypt; Yaqut names the poet Kuthayyir 'Azza as the one whose verses on it speak of a cloud flashing toward al-Buwayb and of longing. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.512} The second is a river that was in Iraq at the site of Kufa: its mouth, at Dar al-Rizq, took water from the Euphrates; a battle was fought at it in the conquests between the Muslims and the Persians in the days of Abu Bakr al-Siddiq; it ran to the place of the house of Salih ibn 'Ali in Kufa and ended in the old Jawf, and in flood time it served as an overflow channel for the Euphrates. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.512} A clause on ships at the Jawf is left aside because its wording is unclear to the museum.

In a search of the whole text, with diacritics removed and the hamza forms merged, the name occurs outside this entry only in verses (vol 3 p.5, vol 5 p.120) and, as the name of an ancestor, in a genealogy under Sinjar and Hit (vol 3 p.262, vol 5 p.421). {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.5} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.120}

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Baladhuri, places the killing of Mihran ibn Mihrbandadh at al-Buwayb in the year 14 and calls it a place near the Euphrates bridge. Yaqut's river entry fits a battle site at Kufa in the conquests, but it names neither Mihran nor a year, and it puts the battle in the days of Abu Bakr, who died in 13 AH, a year before al-Baladhuri's date. The museum reads Yaqut's river and the record's place as the same battle site (its reading; the entry does not say so) and does not choose between the two dates. In the Baghdad entry Yaqut reports from the ahl al-siyar that God destroyed Mihran in the land of al-Hira and that the Muslims then raided the Sawad; that passage does not name al-Buwayb. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.457} The gap on the Hijaz road is a different place. The record's point (31.95, 44.4) is approximate; the entry gives no distance from Kufa. See [[place/kufa|Kufa]].
