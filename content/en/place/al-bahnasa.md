## Overview
al-Bahnasa: a populous, well-developed town that brings together many peoples, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 130}

## Weaving
It had weaving workshops that wove the curtains known as "Bahnasiyya", for the elite and the common people alike. The names of the workshops are woven into the fabrics. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p. 130}

## In the Futuh al-Sham
The [[work/futuh-al-sham-waqidi|Futuh al-Sham]] ascribed to al-Waqidi closes with a long account of the [[event/conquest-of-al-bahnasa|conquest of al-Bahnasa]] and the merits of its cemetery. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.296} It places the city on the bank of the Bahr Yusuf, reports some exegetes' view that it is the hill on which God sheltered Jesus and his mother, and tells that they stayed there twelve years. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.198} {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.200} In Umar's letter to Amr it is "stronger and better fortified" than Ahnas, ruled by a patrician the book calls al-Batlius. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.206} It relates that after the conquest the city had forty ribats and a mosque built beside the demolished church before the mosque of al-Hasan ibn Salih, with a quarter of the Ashraf on its western side and a quarter for each tribe. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.289} {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.290} It places five thousand Companions in its cemetery and counts Bishr al-Hafi, al-Sari al-Saqati and Abu Madyan among its visitors. {cite:futuh-al-sham-ilmiyya-1997-v2 p.290}
