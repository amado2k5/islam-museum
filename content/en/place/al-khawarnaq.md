## Overview
al-Khawarnaq: an ancient Persian palace at al-Hira. al-Baladhuri reports that al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir built it for Bahram Gur ibn Yazdajird, who had been in al-Nu'man's care. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## After the rise of the Abbasid state
al-Baladhuri reports that al-Khawarnaq was granted as a fief to Ibrahim ibn Salama, one of the missionaries in Khurasan, who was the grandfather of Abd al-Rahman ibn Ishaq the judge in the City of Peace in the time of al-Ma'mun and al-Mu'tasim. Ibrahim built the dome of al-Khawarnaq in the caliphate of Abu al-Abbas; there had not been one before that. {cite:futuh-buldan-maarif-1407}

## The story of Sinimmar
In the well-known account reported by Ibn Fadl Allah al-Umari, the builder Sinimmar completed it for al-Nu'man in twenty years. When he said that if he wished he could make it turn with the sun, he was ordered to be thrown from the top of its battlements, and he became a proverb for the evil of a reward. {cite:umari-masalik-1924 p.230}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut separates three places of this name: a place in the Maghreb, where he quotes Abu al-Fath ibn Jinni's Nawadir that al-Asma'i asked al-Khalil ibn Ahmad about it and was referred to the kharnaq, the young hare — while al-Asma'i held it to be Persian khurnagah, a place of eating and drinking, which the Arabs arabised; a village half a farsakh from [[place/balkh|Balkh]] called Khabnak, likewise a Persian name arabised; and "the Khawarnaq the Arabs sang of in their poetry and made a proverb in their tales is neither of these two — it is a place at Kufa" {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.401}. Abu Mansur calls it a river, while the people of athar and akhbar hold it to be a palace behind [[place/al-hira|al-Hira]]; from al-Haytham ibn Adi, the one who ordered its building was al-Nu'man ibn Imri' al-Qays, who reigned eighty years and built it over sixty, through a Roman named Sinnimar; he records the report of the single brick whose removal would fell the palace and Sinnimar's being cast from its top, for which the Arabs made the proverb of Sinnimar's requital — the report carried on its tellers' names — then the report of al-Nu'man looking out from it over al-Najf and the gardens, his minister's answer "what God has in the hereafter", and his leaving his kingdom that very night, with Adi ibn Zayd's verses {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.402-403}.

## The museum's identification
The record's al-Khawarnaq is the proverbial palace behind al-Hira — the third of Yaqut's own three — not the Maghreb place nor the Balkh village. The museum's own measurement — not Yaqut's words — between the records' points al-Khawarnaq (31.9167, 44.50) and al-Hira (31.89, 44.50) gives 3.0 km by the haversine formula on a sphere of R = 6371.0088 km, against the "about a mile" Yaqut prints in his al-Hira entry.
