## Overview
Al-Hudaybiyya is a place on the boundary of the sacred precinct of Mecca, where the peace agreement between the Muslims and Quraysh was concluded in 628 CE. {cite:geonames-gazetteer}

Its location is approximate: it is identified with the Shumaysi area west of Mecca, and the nearest match in the gazetteer is about 14 kilometres away. An additional source is needed for the historical account.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut describes al-Hudaybiyya as a medium-sized village, not a large one, and says it was named after a well there beside the mosque of the tree under which the Prophet received the pledge. He quotes al-Khattabi in his Amali for another explanation, that it was named after a crooked tree that stood on the spot, and he notes that a hadith calls it a well. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.229}

Yaqut sets out the disagreement over how the name is pronounced. He reports from al-Shafi'i that the correct form doubles the y of al-Hudaybiyya and lightens al-Ji'rana, and that whoever insisted on the lighter form was wrong; he adds another view that both are right, since the people of Medina stress it and the people of Iraq lighten it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.229}

On distance he says that it is one stage from Mecca and nine stages from Medina. He writes that part of it lies in the profane land and part in the sacred precinct, that it is the farthest point of the profane land from the House, and that it sits at a kind of corner of the precinct rather than along its length or breadth, which is why it is more than a day from the Sacred Mosque; Malik ibn Anas, he adds, holds that all of it belongs to the precinct. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.229} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230}

He quotes Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi that the Prophet made the lesser pilgrimage of al-Hudaybiyya and agreed a truce with the polytheists when five years and ten months of the Hijra had passed. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 2 p.230}

Elsewhere in the book Yaqut gives a report that the camel of the Messenger of God knelt down at the pass of al-Mirar, which is the descent to al-Hudaybiyya. When the people said she had balked, he answered that she had not and that balking was not her nature, and that the One who held back the elephant had held her back. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.92}

## The museum's identification
This record is the al-Hudaybiyya of the Mu'jam al-buldan, the place of the Treaty of al-Hudaybiyya. The record's statement that the site is identified with the area of al-Shumaysi west of Mecca, and that its coordinates are approximate, comes from the gazetteer and not from Yaqut, who does not name al-Shumaysi and gives only a distance of one stage from Mecca.
