## Summary
Asawal is a city near Janawal in the lands of India which al-Idrisi groups with its two neighbours as resembling one another in character and condition. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.188}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that near the city of Janawal lies the city of Asawal, and that these three towns resemble one another in qualities, size, the state of their people and the similarity of their dress, each having its own trade and attainable profits {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.188}. In the same passage he describes nearby Nahrawara, whose great king is called Balhara and who commands armies and elephants, and notes that Muslim merchants reach it, travel about in it, and that travellers are treated with honour by its king {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.188}. Asawal is thus one of the group of cities in that Indian region which al-Idrisi described together.
