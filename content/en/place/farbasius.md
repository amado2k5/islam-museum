## Summary
Farbasius is a city of ancient construction, famous throughout the land, populous and rich in produce, which al-Idrisi places one day's journey from Mighali Barsiklafa. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}

## Details
Al-Idrisi says that from Mighali Barsiklafa to the city of Farbasius is one day, and that Farbasius is a city of ancient construction, famous in the land, populous and abounding in good things {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}. He adds that from it to the city of Aghrmini westward is half a day — an old and civilized city too, which had lain in ruins until Heraclius the Second rebuilt and repopulated it, its prosperity continuing to his own time, with ploughlands and crops {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}. He describes Mighali Barsiklafa before it as a populous city of middling size beside which a fair river runs {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}. Farbasius is thus one of the ancient towns of that part of the land of the Romans, its name joined with prosperity, abundance and antiquity of building. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.898}
