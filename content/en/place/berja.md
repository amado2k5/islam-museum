## Summary
Berja was one of the dependent towns (manabir) of [[place/almeria|Almeria]], larger than Dalaya, with markets, crafts, ploughlands and farms; a long stage separates it from Almeria, according to al-Idrisi. {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.563}

## Details
Al-Idrisi reports that among the manabir of Almeria are the towns of Berja and Dalaya; between Almeria and Berja is a long stage, between Berja and Dalaya about eight miles, and Berja is the larger of the two, containing markets, crafts, ploughlands and farms {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.563}. On the road to Malaga the traveller leaves Almeria for the village of al-Bajans on the sea at six miles, and from al-Bajans the road passes overland to Berja and Dalaya {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.563}. He also notes that a great river descending west of the village of Udhra from Mount Shalir gathers with the waters of Berja and others and pours at Udhra into the sea {cite:nuzhat-al-mushtaq-2002 p.564}. Berja thus stood in his account as the largest of Almeria's dependencies, joining market, craft and agriculture, its waters feeding a river that reached the sea.
