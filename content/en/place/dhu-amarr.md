## Overview
A water where a gathering of Banu Tha'laba and Muharib used to assemble. The army of Medina reached it in Muharram in the year 3 AH, stayed there, and then returned to [[place/medina|Medina]] without fighting, according to al-Mubarakfuri. {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

Its location is not certain in the sources loaded.

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut gives Dhu Amarr as a place the Prophet campaigned at. The entry then gives, under al-Waqidi's name, its place in the region of al-Nakhil, in Najd, in the lands of Ghatafan, and goes on, with no mark of where the quotation ends, that the Prophet set out in Rabi' al-awwal of the third year AH against a gathering of Muharib and others, who fled to the mountain tops; their leader was Du'thur ibn al-Harith al-Muharibi, and the Muslims camped at Dhu Amarr. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.252} He cites a verse by 'Ukkasha ibn Mis'ada al-Sa'di that names the place with Wasit, Dhat Kahf and Ghamr, and gives Dhu Ghamr elsewhere as a valley in Najd. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.252} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.211} He explains amar as stones set up as markers, taller than the arum according to Ibn al-A'rabi, and says there is also an Amar in Syria. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.252}

In his entry on Dhat al-Riqa' he places the story of Du'thur al-Muharibi in that campaign, dates it to the fourth year, and quotes al-Waqidi that it lies near al-Nakhil between al-Sa'd, al-Shuqra and the well of Arma, three days from Medina. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 3 p.56} So in Yaqut's text the Du'thur story appears twice, with Dhu Amarr and with Dhat al-Riqa'.

## The museum's identification
The record, from al-Mubarakfuri, has a water where a company of Banu Tha'laba and Muharib gathered, which the army of Medina reached in Muharram of the year 3 AH, where it stayed and then returned to Medina without fighting; its location is uncertain. Yaqut agrees that it is a place the Prophet campaigned at against a gathering of Muharib, but the passage given under al-Waqidi's name, whose end is not marked, dates the departure to Rabi' al-awwal of the year 3, not Muharram, and puts the place in the region of al-Nakhil in Najd, in Ghatafan's lands; the entry does not call it a water, does not mention Banu Tha'laba, the size of the force or a return without fighting, and says the people fled. Whether those statements are al-Waqidi's alone or Yaqut's too is not clear from the entry. The difference over the month stays open. The record's point (25.1, 41.2) is marked approximate and lies 174.8 km from the museum's Medina point; Yaqut gives no distance from Medina for Dhu Amarr, and for Dhat al-Riqa' al-Waqidi gives three days. His word al-Nakhil is a district here, and his own entry for al-Nakhil holds only a day of the Arabs, {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.278} so the museum does not identify a place from it.
