## Overview
Qina is a city of Upper Egypt (al-Sa'id), east of the Nile. Ibn Jubayr describes it as white and elegant in appearance, with grand buildings, and mentions that its women keep to their houses. {cite:rihla-ibn-jubayr-1907 p.370}

## In the iDAI.gazetteer
The iDAI.gazetteer lists this place under no. 2751436 with the preferred name «Qinā», classed as archaeological site and populated place; its parent in the hierarchy is «Genā (Muhāfazat)», classed as administrative unit. Recorded names include «Kainopolis», «Maximianopolis» and «Qina». {cite:idai-gazetteer 2751436}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-buldan
Yaqut records Qina (with a kasra on the qaf, mamduda apart): a Coptic word, a pretty city of the Sa'id with one day between it and Qus; some write it Iqna with a kasra'd alif at the front, and a kura is attached to it. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.399} The name has other places for him: Quna' is the name of a water; Qinna a district of Shahrazur on al-Hamdhani's authority; the monastery Dayr Qunna near Nahrawan by al-Safiya, to which notable secretaries were ascribed; and Qana is a place in Yemen and a mountain of Banu Marra of Fazara — some said Qana and 'Awarid are two mountains of Banu Fazara, for which Sibawayh cited verse. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 4 p.399; 4 p.400} He also has Qina (with a ya'): a village of the Hawf of Egypt in the kura of Bana, also called al-Bub. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 1 p.489}

## The museum's identification
The record's Qina is the Sa'id city, and it is Yaqut's "pretty city one day from Qus" (about 27.9 km on the museum's Qina and Qus points). His Qina-of-Bana is a different Hawf village, and the Yemeni and Fazari "Qana" places are waters and mountains, not the city. His note that the name is Coptic and can be written Iqna is consistent with the record's gazetteer names (Kainopolis, Maximianopolis) without naming them.
