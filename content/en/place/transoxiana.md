## Overview
Transoxiana, "what lies beyond the river", is the name the Muslim geographers gave to the lands beyond the river Jayhun (the Amu Darya). Yaqut al-Hamawi gives it an entry in the Mu'jam al-Buldan that defines its limit, describes its fertility and the strength of its people as a frontier of Islam, and records its ruin in his own time. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.45} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.46} {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.47}

## In Yaqut's Mu'jam al-Buldan
Yaqut says the term means what lies beyond the river Jayhun in Khurasan. What lies east of it was called the land of the Hayatila before Islam and "beyond the river" in Islam, while what lies west of it is Khurasan and the province of Khwarazm; Khwarazm, he notes, is not part of Khurasan but a region of its own. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.45} See [[place/khorasan|Khurasan]], [[place/samarqand|Samarqand]] and [[place/bukhara|Bukhara]].

He then relays a long description of its prosperity from an earlier author. In it Transoxiana is among the most pleasant and fertile of regions, its people inclined to generosity and quick to answer a call to good, and strong in arms. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.45} The account says its fertility passes description, that no place in it lacks cultivation, whether city, villages, water, fields or pasture, that its waters are sweet, that its cotton cloth exceeds local need and is carried abroad, that its Khujand silk has no better, that its iron exceeds its needs, and that it has gold, silver and mercury mines. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.46} Yaqut gives these as reports from another writer, not from his own sight.

The same account describes the region as a frontier of Islam ringed by Turkic peoples: the Ghuzziyya Turks from the borders of Khwarazm to Isbijab, then the Kharlukh Turks to the far edge of Farghana. It calls the whole boundary a war-frontier and says its people had a greater share in jihad than others. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.46} It adds that the people are the most obedient to their elders, and that the caliphs summoned men from there who became commanders and courtiers, such as the Faraghina and the Turks; their power grew until they dominated the caliphs, and it names al-Afshin and the family of Abu al-Saj from Ushrusana and al-Ikhshid from Samarqand. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.47} It closes the description by saying nothing in the world is more beautiful than Bukhara. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.47}

At the end of the entry Yaqut records what became of the region in his own time. Around 600 AH the Khwarazm-Shah Muhammad ibn Tekish took it, drove out the Khita and killed the kings of Transoxiana known as the Khanids; each district had had a king to guard it, and when he held them all he could not control them, sent his armies who plundered them and drove out the people. In 617 the Tatars came and destroyed what remained, and those lands stood empty. {cite:mujam-al-buldan-sader-1995 5 p.47}
