## Summary

[[person/al-zahrawi|Abu al-Qasim al-Zahrawi]] describes animal-intestinal filaments in a discussion of intestinal suturing in the surgical book of *al-Tasrif*. Leclerc's French translation preserves an important qualification: this approach and the neighbouring ant-closure account remain experimental {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. Azeem Majeed includes catgut sutures among his surgical developments; this page concerns that specific material contribution, not invention of all suturing or certification of a modern treatment {cite:majeed-islam-medicine-2005 pp.1486–1487}.

## What it is

The described material consists of thin filaments from animal intestines, used in the passage as a means of joining an internal wound; it is not a named brand or modern laboratory specification {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. “Animal-intestinal thread” keeps the description close to the passage, while catgut is the name used in modern historical writing; neither name by itself identifies a particular animal species {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211} {cite:majeed-islam-medicine-2005 pp.1486–1487}. Material and instrument are different subjects: this record is not about al-Zahrawi's stone drill.

## The world before it

The same chapter presents inherited wound-suturing knowledge and explicitly draws on Galen, discussing tissues and circumstances rather than offering one thread for every injury {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 pp.207–208}. Bringing wound edges together therefore did not begin with al-Zahrawi: he discusses an existing tradition and adds to its material account {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 pp.207–211}. This does not establish that Galen used the identical intestinal filament. The documented precursor is suturing practice, not proof of material identity.

## The story

The passage belongs to chapter87, following an extended discussion of abdominal wounds and treatment limits, not a separate inventor's card or celebratory discovery anecdote {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 pp.202–203,207–211}. On page211 the filament description follows an account attributed to physicians of the empirical school involving ants; an experimental qualification then applies to both approaches {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}.

That arrangement matters: the text compares alternatives while marking limits to what has been established. Not everything preserved in a medical encyclopaedia is a settled result {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. TDV places completion of *al-Tasrif* around390 AH/1000. This approximately dates the book, not a first operation, invention year or photographed specimen {cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp.132–133}.

## The maker(s)

Al-Zahrawi was an Andalusian physician associated with [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]], author of a30-book medical encyclopaedia, not only a surgical manual; his biography distinguishes broad fame from sparse early personal testimony {cite:tdv-zehravi pp.189–191} {cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp.132–133}. Abdel-Halim and colleagues explicitly call him a Muslim surgeon: that is the identity basis, not an inference from name or ancestry {cite:alzahrawi-stones-abdelhalim-2003 p.1283}. Sunni is the museum's declared frame, not proof of personal legal-school allegiance. The recorded contribution here is a describer's, not a modern patent holder's.

## How it works

The general idea is that the material joining tissue matters as well as an instrument's shape: the passage places animal-derived filament in an internal-suturing context {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. This explains function, not procedure. The museum does not reproduce preparation, needle use or injury-treatment steps.

The passage is not an experiment measuring absorption time, sterility or tensile strength, and does not specify the behaviour of every modern medical product with a similar name {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. Its explicit experimental qualification belongs to the evidence; it should not disappear to make the achievement sound more triumphant {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}.

## Impact on daily life

The surgical discussion connects material choice to an injured person's wound and prospects for healing: the thread is not an ornament detached from suffering {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 pp.202–203,211}. But this passage provides no documented patient series comparing outcomes or success rate that the museum can responsibly display as a number {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. Its established contribution is preserving a material idea in treatment literature, not a retrospective calculation of lives saved.

## How it changed history

*Al-Tasrif* preserved the material account in a surgical book with a long translation and teaching history; modern medical writing credits it among the author's developments {cite:tdv-tasrif-zehravi pp.132–133} {cite:majeed-islam-medicine-2005 pp.1486–1487}. Leclerc's page also preserves the experimental caveat, revealing medical writing as transmission, comparison and acknowledgement of limits rather than a list of completed discoveries {cite:zahrawi-leclerc-chirurgie-1861 ch.87 p.211}. This textual contribution is clearer than a slogan assigning him all absorbable sutures.
