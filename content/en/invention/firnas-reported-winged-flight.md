## Summary

Maqqari reports that [[person/abbas-ibn-firnas|Abbas ibn Firnas]] covered himself with feathers, fitted two wings and attempted flight, suffering injury on landing: a transmitted account, not a surviving experimental log with recalculable measurements {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. The museum records disputed details because Hasan Ibrahim Hasan describes unharmed survival, while modern retelling supplies a date and launch setting absent from the Arabic passage {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.378, scan382} {cite:lienhard-firnas-1910 narrative and postscript}. This is not certification of history's first airplane {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}.

## What it is

Maqqari's subject is a feather-covered human body with two wings, not surviving equipment or complete plans specifying wing shape and structural material {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. Hasan calls the craft a glider; the older Arabic passage describes wings and flight. These different levels of description remain distinct, rather than turning a single word into a complete modern aircraft {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.378, scan382} {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}.

## The world before it

Bird imitation is present within the account's feathers, wings and landing explanation: a conceptual precedent, not another dated invention in a confirmed technical chain {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. The University of Houston episode recounts Armen Firman's852 adventure, but its later caveat acknowledges no primary material for him and possible confusion of name, date and details; the diagram therefore does not present him as an independently established precursor {cite:lienhard-firnas-1910 narrative and postscript}.

## The story

Ibn Firnas grew up and studied in [[place/cordoba|Cordoba]], associated with its Umayyad courts as a scholar, poet and practitioner of several crafts {cite:tdv-abbas-firnas-kirbiyik biography}. Maqqari places flight beside timekeeping, a representation of the heavens and poetry, among stories of ingenuity rather than in a specialized flight-observation notebook {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 pp.374–375}.

Lienhard supplies875 and a tower, neither specified by the inspected Arabic passage;875 is accordingly uncertain, attributed modern chronology, not a contemporary witness date {cite:lienhard-firnas-1910 narrative and postscript} {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. TDV's death date274 AH/887 does not establish a jump date or prove that flight injuries caused death {cite:tdv-abbas-firnas-kirbiyik biography}.

## The maker(s)

Ibn Firnas is the account's attributed experimenter; the “inventor” relationship identifies its claimed apparatus maker, not established global priority {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. His Muslim basis is Hasan Ibrahim Hasan's explicit statement on the visually inspected page, not name, Berber origin or country {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.378, scan382}. ## How it works

The text supplies few elements: feathers covering the body, two wings, travel in the air and an injurious landing. Wing area, inclination and control mechanism cannot be recovered from it {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. These words alone do not establish effective flapping, stable gliding or a measured altitude; the display therefore offers neither construction plans nor a jumping experiment {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}.

Maqqari attributes the landing difficulty to a missing tail, comparing a bird's landing: an explanation within the narrative, not a modern aerodynamic test of the lost apparatus {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}. TDV repeats the missing tail and hard landing, but repetition in a modern biography does not independently supply engineering plans or measurements {cite:tdv-abbas-firnas-kirbiyik biography}.

## Impact on daily life

Hasan invokes an early aspiration to flight, not a public transport service or profession using a finished device {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.378, scan382}. Maqqari's narrated immediate consequence is personal injury; the gap is not filled with passengers or trade absent from the pages {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}.

## How it changed history

Modern retelling shows how the name became a symbol of aspiring to fly: the university episode mentions an airport named for him and admires the attempt's daring {cite:lienhard-firnas-1910 episode}. Symbolic stature does not measure a flight's success or turn narrated bird imitation into completed aeronautical science; memory and technical evidence remain distinct {cite:lienhard-firnas-1910 episode and postscript} {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374}.

## For young readers

Deleting one account does not make them agree {cite:nafh-tib-maqqari v.3 p.374} {cite:tarikh-islam-hasan-ibrahim v.4 printed p.378, scan382}.
