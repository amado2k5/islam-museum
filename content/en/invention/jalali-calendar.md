## Summary

Imagine a calendar whose month numbers stay orderly while its beginning slowly moves through the seasons. The Jalali reform sought to anchor New Year to the spring equinox. Supported by [[person/malikshah|Malik Shah]], it took its name from his title Jalal al-Dawla; Maliki and Malikshahi were alternative names. It did not invent the solar year, and its history is not one person's discovery of time {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.1–4}.

The project is commonly associated with [[person/umar-khayyam|Umar Khayyam]], whose participation MacTutor reports. Johannes Thomann, however, asks whether named observers can automatically be identified as the calendar's designers. We retain that disagreement, distinguish the epoch used for counting from an unknown invention day, and separate later leap rules from an original programme we cannot reconstruct completely {cite:khayyam-mactutor calendar} {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.4}.

## What it is

A solar-calendar reform connects the year's beginning to a seasonal astronomical event and reconciles a whole-number day count with the solar year by adding days in some years. Iranica describes New Year in relation to the sun's apparent entry into Aries before noon. Here the zodiacal term specifies an astronomical reference, not a claim that a constellation governs someone's life {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali subsection}.

A solar year does not make every month begin at the sun's entry into another zodiacal sign. The source distinguishes astronomically anchored years/seasons from historical thirty-day months, with additional days completing the year. Nor should the modern arrangement of six thirty-one-day months be projected back onto the eleventh-century reform {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali and Solar-Hijri subsections}.

## The world before it

Persian calendrical history long preceded the Seljuks and Islam. Thomann explains a system of twelve thirty-day months plus five additional days:365 days, roughly a quarter-day shorter than the seasonal year. An accumulating small difference moves month dates through the seasons, even while names and numbers remain perfectly orderly in an account book {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.2}.

That movement mattered socially. Reform narratives complain about tax payments falling before crops supplied the means to pay. Earlier Abbasid and other attempts are reported, but Thomann tests such narratives against recorded dates rather than treating every reform story as permanent implementation. An old need and a proposed remedy do not establish how successfully people adopted it {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.3}.

## The story

Thomann describes Malik Shah's summons to astronomers from Khurasan and the observing work surrounding the project. Yet the Nawrūznāma, attributed to Khayyam but probably not his work in the historian's assessment, says intercalation was not completed. A historical account discussed alongside it distinguishes reform from observatory construction and the end of observations after the sultan's death {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.3–4}.

We therefore invent neither a founding meeting with recoverable dialogue nor an original calendar notebook completed on a known day. The witnesses Thomann discusses contain1076 and1079 epochs, with a later tradition preferring the latter. Iranica interprets468 differently, as a fiscal-year number rather than the same Hijri year. The disagreement over how to read a number belongs in the history, not in a silently harmonized chronology {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.4} {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali subsection}.

## How it works

Begin by separating event from count. The equinox is an astronomical event; the day is a calendrical unit, and a written calendar year has a whole number of days. Connecting the beginning to the event requires a rule for choosing the day and accounting for common and leap years. Calling something “accurate” without specifying the epoch, months, added days and version tells us too little {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali subsection}.

In our modern paper model, not assigned to a particular manuscript, twelve30-day months make360 days; five extra days make365. Eight added days over33 years give365×33+8=12053 days, averaging365+8/33. This illustrates addition and distribution, not a securely recovered Khayyam algorithm.

Compare a model adding one day every four years: its average365.25 yields12053.25 days over33 years, a quarter-day more than the other model. Arithmetic difference alone does not establish perpetual agreement with the sky. Iranica presents differing later witnesses and rules, including four/five-year intervals, not a warrant to collapse all of them into one fixed original cycle {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali subsection}.

Indeed, its listed five-year-interval years include a transition from163 to192: a gap of29, not33. Readers can check the subtraction to see why “33-year cycles occur” is not equivalent to “every cycle is always33.” Historical conversion needs a dated text and a specified rule before it becomes an algorithm {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali subsection}.

## The spread

A description in astronomical tables does not prove use in every administrative office. Thomann looks for inscriptions, coins and documents; his Jalali examples occur particularly in astronomical/astrological materials, challenging the picture of comprehensive administrative replacement. This is a scholar's conclusion from his evidence, not proof that every unobserved use was impossible {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.5–6}.

In fragments dated1182/1183, he infers an equinox-anchored year while older Persian dates remain inside the table. An Ottoman example from1450 has a separate Jalali column rather than one exclusive date system. Here circulation concerns neighboring ways to count time: a calendar can travel within a specialized practice without erasing others {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.5}.

## What it was built on

The reform inherited month names, eras and calculation practices rather than starting from nothing. Reconciling solar motion with numbered days and attempts to realign seasonal festivals preceded it. Thomann reconstructs that setting through studies and historical accounts; this article does not claim independent reading of every Persian original or inscription in his references {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.2–3}.

Observations and tables formed the working environment, but their presence does not establish a technical chain from every astronomical instrument to this calendar. We add no unsupported causal link from Khayyam's cubic constructions to the reform, and do not make a calendar reform the invention of observatories or observation itself {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.4}.

## What was built on it

Later astronomical tables discussed its epoch, intercalation and conversion with varying formulae. Iranica describes stages of this treatment and modern changes to the chosen era, month names and month lengths. Historical continuity does not mean every rule stayed identical across centuries {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Jalali and Solar-Hijri subsections}.

It associates1925 with changed Solar Hijri month lengths: six months of31 days, five of30, and a final29 or30. We do not transplant this structure into1079, or present the article's historical formulae as current legal conversion software. Calendar, era and particular version must be distinguished before two dates can be compared responsibly {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Solar-Hijri subsection}.

## Impact on daily life

A complaint about taxes and crops supplies an understandable motive, not a Jalali tax receipt. Thomann separates motives reported in literature from implementation demonstrated in documents. Respecting that difference avoids imagining farmers whose lives were transformed by an order for which we lack that direct kind of evidence {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.3,5}.

Multiple dates in astronomical documents could support comparison and recognition of the same day. The historian also examines astrological functions in scheduling personal choices. We attribute that interpretation, without converting the document users' beliefs into predictive knowledge endorsed by the museum or advice to follow such practices {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.5–6}.

## How it changed history

The project's significance lies in equinox anchoring and its long afterlife in astronomical writing, not a slogan about the “most accurate calendar ever invented.” Its history becomes richer when patron, observer, scribe and user are considered separately rather than compressed into one famous signature {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.4–6}.

The programme/implementation distinction also teaches us how to read innovation. A book may describe an orderly system while an everyday document retains an older convention. Finding both is not a reason to erase one; their coexistence opens a question about actual practices of use {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 secs.1,5}.

## Today

The modern Solar Hijri calendar belongs to this heritage but is not a frozen copy of the medieval reform. Iranica discusses choosing the Hijra era and modern legislative changes. A solar year does not imply abolishing lunar Hijri dating or transferring its religious observances into a classroom arithmetic rule {cite:iranica-calendars-abdollahy Solar-Hijri subsection}.

Tables still invite verification: which era, an old or Jalali month, and which leap-year rule? A matching month name is insufficient, and many decimal places do not guarantee everlasting accuracy. The Berlin example shows why headings and neighboring columns must be read before a number is converted into a date {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.5}.

## In culture

Nowruz, royal naming and multilingual/multicalendar writing show that a date can express affiliation as well as a number. Thomann reads colored multiple dates in Iskandar Sultan's manuscript as chronological eloquence, not merely calculation. This is his interpretation of a particular object, not a judgment about every calendar user {cite:thomann-jalali-calendar-2021 sec.5}.

Nor should Khayyam's poetic image authenticate a technical account. Stanford's distinction between philosophical and poetic voices helps resist that compression. We infer no personal school from a disputed quatrain and reproduce no poetry as a substitute for documentation of the reform {cite:sep-umar-khayyam sec.2.7}.
