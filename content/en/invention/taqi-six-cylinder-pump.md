## Summary

In [[work/turuq-saniyya-taqi|Al-Turuq al-saniyya]], [[person/taqi-al-din-ibn-maruf|Taqi al-Din ibn Maruf]] described six tubes with pistons, weights and valves, actuated by projections distributed around a shaft and connected to a shared outlet. The intention is successive delivery rather than only one piston's pulse. We read the design in Ahmad Yusuf al-Hasan's edited text, not an original machine we discovered {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp47–50}.

This is a specific integration within an older history of pumps and cams, not invention of pressurized water or every later engine. ## What it is

Six inline reciprocating units have valves that admit water in one direction and prevent return. Rising and falling strokes perform different functions: a cam raises a piston through a rocker; a weight then drives it downward. Their common pipe collects delivery; it does not become an independent energy source {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp47–48}.

We call this a documented design, not measured output on an Ottoman farm. The text explains intentions and supplies a drawing; an editor interprets historical significance; modern researchers choose dimensions and build a model. These are three different people's activities. An intelligible drawing is a narrower claim than an assembled machine passing a lengthy wet test {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp49–50} {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 sections3–6}.

## How it works

The text describes six divisions in a wooden block, tubes, passages, tight covers and rockers connected to piston rods. A shaft projection presses a rocker's end, raising the piston at the other end. Once contact ends the piston returns under its weight. An inlet valve and delivery valve distinguish admission from discharge {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp47–48}.

The modern study explains rising motion lowering cylinder pressure, admitting water through one valve while preventing return through the other. On descent the valve states reverse, directing water toward delivery pipes. “Suction” is not a magical force lifting any column regardless of height. Pressure, sealing and resistance conditions matter; an animation alone cannot verify them {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 section2.1}.

The modern model distributes six cams sixty degrees apart for successive operation. However, the printed historical wording apparently shifts from the first to the third hand; we do not silently correct that into an authenticated exact firing order. The drawing conveys an arrangement, not a fully dimensioned manufacturing plan or measured timing for each valve {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp48–49} {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 section3.2}.

Our modern arithmetic example assumes one square centimetre of piston area and ten centimetres of travel: ten cubic centimetres, or ten millilitres, per ideal delivery stroke. Six identical units yield sixty millilitres per full revolution if each delivers once. Two revolutions per minute give a theoretical average of120millilitres per minute. These are neither the author's dimensions nor the2021 model's measured output; leakage, air and pressure losses are excluded.

Phasing does not multiply river energy freely. Draw six paper time windows beginning at0,60,120,180,240,300degrees. Twenty-degree delivery windows leave gaps; wider windows overlap. Equal start spacing alone therefore proves no perfectly steady instantaneous flow. This is a simplified timing illustration, not recovery of a historical cam profile.

The author allows one tube, prefers at least three for smoother delivery, and says two can achieve the purpose. Preference is not a physical limit prohibiting all pumping with fewer than three. Likewise, the instruction to exceed the lifted water's weight is not a complete modern calculation: piston area, lift height, losses and time to return before the next cycle also matter {cite:taqi-turuq-hasan-1976 pp48–50} {cite:cabeza-pump-2021 section4.1}.

## For young readers

Write six cards representing times in one paper cycle. Compare everyone's delivery together with successive delivery. No water, weights or tools are needed; do not experiment near a river or well. The paper exercise distinguishes number of pulses from their spacing. Can gaps remain despite six cards? That is a timing question, not a speed contest.

Then compare one equal delivery with six arithmetically, labeling numbers modern examples rather than old-machine measurements. Ask of any displayed picture: who drew it, when, and is it an original, model or explanation of a principle? Learning an invention can also teach reading evidence, without building equipment or assuming every attractive rendering is an artifact.
