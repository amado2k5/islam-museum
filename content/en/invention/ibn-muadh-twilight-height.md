## Summary

[[person/ibn-muadh-al-jayyani|Ibn Muadh al-Jayyani]] tried to infer the height of an atmospheric layer from the first dawn light and the last evening twilight. His [[work/ibn-muadh-twilight|twilight treatise]] connected something visible with a geometrical distance beyond direct reach. Calvo reports a solar depression of18degrees,parameters for Earth and Sun,and a result around52miles. That is a historical model's result,not an accurate measurement of the end of the entire atmosphere {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1-2}.

The work was long associated with [[person/ibn-al-haytham|Ibn al-Haytham]],but scholarly sources report Sabra's correction to Ibn Muadh. We distinguish authorship from the fame of another name,and formulating a quantitative question from the correctness of every assumption. This is not invention of air,twilight or a compulsory prayer-time rule {cite:tdv-ibn-muadh-dizer attribution-and-twilight}.

## What it is

The problem is inverse:observe light,then infer a distance. The Sun need not stand above an observer's horizon to illuminate every place above that observer;its rays may reach a layer while Earth's shadow covers the eye's location. Turning this picture into a height requires assumptions about Earth,the Sun,and what becomes illuminated and visible {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1-2}.

The treatise estimates a height;it does not describe a machine flying there. It does not directly measure air pressure or changing density. NASA now describes gas layers with different properties and says the exosphere has no definitive top altitude. A sharp assumed layer in a diagram therefore cannot be identified with the physical end of all atmospheric gas {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 abstract} {cite:nasa-atmosphere-ueyama exosphere}.

## The world before it

Twilight knowledge did not begin from nothing. TDV reports different angles used by Habash,al-Nayrizi,al-Biruni and Ibn Yunus,including19,18and17degrees,and calculations distinguishing morning from evening. That list is not a set of identical measurements and does not establish Ibn Muadh as the first person to use18 {cite:tdv-ibn-muadh-dizer twilight}.

Dawn and dusk mattered for astronomy and timekeeping,but the source distinguishes Ibn Muadh's concern with atmospheric height from determining prayer times. A natural-philosophical question does not become a ruling simply because its phenomenon also has religious uses. Different angles do not prove that every calculator used the same definition or observing conditions {cite:tdv-ibn-muadh-dizer twilight-purpose}.

## How it works

Calvo lists four inputs:a solar depression of18degrees,a mean solar distance of1110Earth radii,a linear Sun/Earth size ratio of5.5:1,and Earth's circumference of24000miles. The reported result is around52miles. We must not remove the finite distance and size,then claim a full historical reproduction using only parallel rays {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1-2}.

For understanding,we supply a simpler modern teaching model with explicit limits. Assume a circular Earth section of radiusR,straight parallel rays,a thin layer with a sharp upper boundary,and light arriving from an illuminated point on the observer's horizon. If solar depression isδ,thenα=δ/2arises between radii in the two right triangles. These are conditions of a diagram,not facts about the whole atmosphere.

The relation iscosα=R/(R+h),givingh/R=secα−1. Secant means reciprocal cosine,not another independently measured coefficient. Withδ=18,we haveα=9degrees. TakingR=3818length units givesh≈47.59185in the same units;atδ=19it gives≈53.08967. Both are museum recomputations of the simplified model. We do not equate47.59with the historical52.

TDV's web example switches from18to19degrees and contains a decimal-place error in its height expression. We do not copy0.1391instead of0.01391as a valid coefficient. Different inputs cannot be silently reconciled by erasing the source. Numerical checks test consequences of assumed geometry,not whether the real atmosphere has a sharp ceiling or whether refraction can actually be neglected {cite:tdv-ibn-muadh-dizer geometry}.

The simplified relation is not linear in angle:doubling the angle need not double height. A zero angle gives zero height in this geometry;scaling all lengths together at unchanged angle preserves ratios. These test the modern example's structure,not an additional ancient observation. They ask what the expression preserves and assumes,rather than accepting an answer because it has decimal places.

## The spread

Calvo reports the Latin translation associated with Gerard of Cremona and a Hebrew translation by Samuel ben Jehuda. Smith's abstract counts one Hebrew,25Latin and one Latin-derived Italian exemplar;his critical text used comparative transcription of22Latin manuscripts. These figures serve different purposes and cannot simply be added as a readership census {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1} {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 abstract}.

Calvo describes52miles as standard in the Latin West until the late sixteenth century. Smith's abstract discusses seventeenth-century revision in light of refraction and barometric studies. This is reception followed by correction,not uninterrupted success or evidence that every later writer independently observed the phenomenon {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF2} {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 abstract}.

## What it was built on

The question depends on distinctions between an observer's horizon,Earth's form and its shadow. Smith's notes explain the conical shadow by assuming a Sun larger than Earth. We do not turn this into light emitted by the observer's eye or confuse it with the vision theory in Ibn al-Haytham's optical work,with which some manuscripts were bound {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 notes3-and7}.

An angle alone supplies no length unit:an Earth scale is required. In our modern example,doublingR doublesh while leaving the ratio unchanged. Silently replacing a historical mile with a kilometre and celebrating precision would therefore be misleading. Consistent parameters and declared units are essential even when the underlying physical model itself needs revision.

## What was built on it

In Latin reception the figure became standard,then faced criticism connected with refraction. Calvo mentions Tycho Brahe's prominence in raising that issue and Kepler's subsequent reduction of the height,followed by later astronomers. These scholars are not co-authors of the earlier text. Revising an old number also does not erase the historical significance of formulating its problem {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF2}.

We draw no demonstrated line from the eleventh-century treatise to every current astronomical-twilight definition. The National Weather Service defines civil,nautical and astronomical twilight using the Sun's geometric centre at6,12and18degrees below the horizon. Matching18does not independently prove transmission or validate the height once inferred from it {cite:noaa-twilight-types definitions}.

## In culture

Names change in transmission between languages,and a text may acquire association with a famous author through its binding position. Smith notes name forms pointing to Ibn Muadh and limited explicit conflation with Ibn al-Haytham among the witnesses he consulted. A possible explanation for error is not evidence of intentional forgery by an unnamed copyist {cite:smith-muadh-twilight-1992 note3}.

Inheritance includes both error and revision. A magnitude can remain authoritative for a time,then be reconsidered as understanding of light,pressure and air changes. ## For young readers

Imagine on paper a circular Earth with a layer above it. Your location can enter shadow before everything overhead does. Ibn Muadh's question was whether the first light could reveal the layer's height. Answering required numbers and assumptions,not merely looking upward. The difference between seeing and inferring is the lesson {cite:calvo-ibn-muadh-bea PDF1-2}.

Then ask:what if the layer has no sharp roof? What if light changes direction? A calculation can correctly describe a drawing without describing the world as it is. Do not test this by looking at the Sun;the paper idea is enough to distinguish useful geometry from a physical conclusion needing further evidence {cite:nasa-atmosphere-ueyama exosphere}.
