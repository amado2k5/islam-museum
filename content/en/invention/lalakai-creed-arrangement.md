## Summary
Al-Lalaka'i arranged the chapters of his "Usul al-I'tiqad" in a distinctive way: each chapter states what has been transmitted on the topic, when the dispute arose and who began it, then argues from the Qur'an, hadith and sayings of the Companions and Successors with chains, ending with the miracles of the saints {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira}. This is a deliberate compositional choice that makes the history of doctrinal dispute the organising principle, not merely a theological classification {cite:shadharat-dhahab}.

## What it is
Al-Lalaka'i's "Sharh Usul al-I'tiqad" is a compact creed encyclopedia arranged in chapters, but its arrangement is not conventional: each chapter opens with what has been transmitted on the question, then identifies when the dispute arose and who began it, then adduces evidence from the Qur'an, hadith and sayings of the Companions and Successors with their chains, until it closes with what is reported of the saints' miracles at the end of the book {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. This is a "method" of presenting creed — a declared compositional approach — not merely a list of topics.

## The world before it
Before al-Lalaka'i there were books of usul al-din and creed in Islamic tradition, written by theologians or jurists according to their own principles. But not every creed book ordered its chapters by the history of dispute and began each chapter with what was transmitted and when the difference arose; that was a distinctive approach in the books of the people of hadith, who prized isnad and transmission {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. Al-Lalaka'i himself was a hadith master, so it was natural that he should employ the tools of his profession in a creed book.

## The story
Al-Lalaka'i dictated his book in Baghdad in 416 AH/1025 CE according to the transmission chain at the head of the book; this license is the recorded date of dictation, not necessarily the date of composition {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira}. The text describes the author's method as each chapter being "the course of what was transmitted on" the topic, with the time the dispute arose and who began it, then argument from the chains until the end {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira}. The book became famous among the Sunni tradition and remains a reference in creed {cite:shadharat-dhahab}.

## The maker(s)
Abu Bakr — or Abu al-Hasan — Muhammad ibn Ishaq al-Lalaka'i al-Naysaburi, a hadith master from Khurasan, who died around 418 AH/1027 CE according to the biographies {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. His Muslim identity is documented by his being of the people of hadith and his book being wholly composed of transmitted evidence; his Sunni label is the museum's declared frame because his work stood inside the Sunni tradition of religious sciences {cite:shadharat-dhahab}.

## How it works
Each chapter in the book follows two clear steps: first "the course" — stating what has been transmitted on the question of texts and sayings; second "the dispute" — identifying when the difference arose on the question and who first held or began it. Then comes the role of argument: the author cites the Qur'an, hadith and sayings of the Companions and Successors with the chain of each, until he finishes the book with a conclusion on the saints' miracles {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira}. In this way the reader learns not only "what the rule is" but "who differed and when".

## The spread
The book spread in the East and West, remaining a reference in creed among the Sunni tradition, and was transmitted through commentaries and epitomes {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. We do not establish here the name of each later commentary, but the mass of commentaries on the book attests to its fame {cite:shadharat-dhahab}.

## What it was built on
The approach was built on the tools of hadith science: isnad, transmission, and knowledge of the sayings of the early generations; and on the precedent of earlier creed books written before al-Lalaka'i {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. Al-Lalaka'i himself was a memoriser from the school of the muhaddithun, so his classification of creed was an extension of his method in hadith books.

## What was built on it
The "presentation of dispute along history" approach remained a reference for those who came after him in creed books and refutations; later books of usul al-din continued to present questions with mention of the dispute, even if they did not follow his exact ordering {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. We establish no later book here claiming to have taken his method directly.

## Impact on daily life
Its direct effect on people's daily lives is limited; it was a book for students of knowledge and preachers, not for the general public. But through its long fame it influenced the way creed was presented in schools and mosques: not as a list of abstract beliefs, but as questions with a history of dispute and transmitted evidence {cite:shadharat-dhahab}.

## How it changed history
It changed history in that it made "the history of dispute" an organising principle in a creed book, not merely a footnote; this approach preceded him in the books of the theologians, but here it employed the tools of the hadith scholars rather than those of the theologians {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. He did not invent the subject of creed or the refutation of opponents, but he re-arranged the presentation.

## Who invented it?
We do not claim that al-Lalaka'i invented the creed book or was the first to write on usul al-din; these are older subjects {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. What is documented is his method of ordering: stating what was transmitted, then the history of dispute and who began it, then argument by chains to the end; this is an organised improvement on an existing heritage.

## Today
Today there are many creed books with different arrangements, but al-Lalaka'i's approach of "the history of dispute" is still studied in the history of doctrinal method; the accompanying image is an Arabic manuscript on faith and its sources in Amsterdam's World Museum, contextual to the creed-book tradition and not a copy of al-Lalaka'i's book {cite:commons-creed-manuscript-tropenmuseum Description}. We establish no direct continuity of his method in today's books.

## Surviving examples
The most important witness is the book itself in the Basira edition, which contains the transmission chain dated 416 AH and the author's method {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira}. Al-Dhahabi also mentions its author and life in Shadharat al-Dhahab {cite:shadharat-dhahab}. The museum displays no original page of the book today.

## In culture
Al-Lalaka'i's approach represents a model of the meeting of two methods: the hadith scholars' method of isnad and transmission, and the theologians' method of ordering doctrinal questions. This meeting is what made his book unique in the heritage of usul al-din {cite:sharh-usul-itiqad-basira} {cite:shadharat-dhahab}.

## For young readers
Scholars wrote many things about faith, and people differed on some of them long ago. Al-Lalaka'i ordered his book's chapters in a helpful way: each chapter states what was transmitted, when the dispute arose, then argues from books narrated from the Prophet and his Companions and followers, so the reader understands where the difference lies.
