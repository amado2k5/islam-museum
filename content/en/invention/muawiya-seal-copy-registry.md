## Summary
How could an administration know that an arriving document carried the amount originally authorized rather than one changed along the way? Duri adds checking, sealing and a retained reference copy {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Umayyad}. This is an institutional contribution to documentary control, not invention of writing or seals, and not proof forgery became impossible. ## What it is
The registry links outgoing correspondence to checking, a retained copy and sealing in Duri's account; Suyuti reports appointment of an official and handing him a seal {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Umayyad} {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.332}. The subject is therefore people, records and documents, not just a metal ring. A seal carries a mark, an impression is its trace, and the bureau regulates use: these levels should not be conflated. Neither the original nor its retained copy is automatically a receipt for carrying out the order. The institution's name does not prove that it performed every function associated with archives or authentication today, or that every later seal directly descended from it.

## The world before it
Duri places the bureaux in an inherited Byzantine/Sasanian administrative world and mentions Iranian influence on a control mechanism attributed through Baladhuri to Ziyad ibn Abih {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Umayyad}. TDV describes Muawiya's use of Byzantine institutions and scribes/advisers from different backgrounds {cite:tdv-muaviye-aycan}. Writing, seals and administration did not begin from nothing. The contribution concerns organizing a function within government, not the earliest mark ever placed on a document. Precedents remain credited without erasing the distinction between one seal and correspondence linked to records and institutional controls. Administrative influence cannot establish every employee's faith or a complete technical genealogy without further evidence.

## How it works
**Checking.** Duri describes reading/commenting on correspondence and a scribe preparing the document, distinguishing administrative decision from a mark on paper {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Umayyad}. Reported approval does not establish that every factual assertion inside is true.

**Copy.** A retained copy offers, in modern educational explanation, a reference for comparing a returned text or amount in an account. Comparison presupposes an intact reference; its mere existence cannot prove that condition {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Umayyad}. No exact sequence of every historical movement is reconstructed.

**Seal.** A physical mark can make opening or alteration a matter for inspection, not make opening impossible or guarantee total secrecy. A visible seal alone cannot certify every employee's authorization or correct execution of an account.

## In culture
A seal communicates authority and trust, but meaning in a historical report differs from every modern decorative use. Suyuti names an official entrusted with a seal, connecting the mark to its custody and institutional use rather than only its attractive design {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.332}. The modern wax impressions contain a motif, not a portrait of a Companion or reconstruction of the reported historical legend {cite:commons-sealing-wax-eugster}. No letter-holder's or scribe's faith is inferred from the seal's appearance. Separating physical impression, copy and commissioning authority makes culture a study of changing practices rather than symbols with one timeless meaning or unknown people's private beliefs.
