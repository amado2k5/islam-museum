## Summary

How can a calculation on a sphere become a line that an architect can draw on a flat surface? Near the end of his investigation of qibla direction, [[person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad|al-Biruni]] supplies a practical connection between mathematical specialist and craft worker: two circle approximations specifically for [[place/ghazna|Ghazna]], following elaborate calculations of the direction of [[place/mecca|Mecca]] {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.241–256}. This entry is not about inventing the qibla or all spherical trigonometry. The author acknowledges earlier astronomers, then distinguishes the needs of calculators from those of architects {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.241,255}. Its subject is a documented procedure whose approximation can be examined, not an archaeological mosque proved to have used it.

## What it is

The method transfers a calculated direction to a horizontal circle with a known north–south line, obtaining a slanting radius and a wall line perpendicular to it {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256}. The first approximation divides the southern radius into thirds; the second divides the western radius into eighteen parts and uses a point near its outer end {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256}. This is not a drawing of a journey around the Earth. It represents a local angle in the town's horizon plane, whereas the preceding proof treats the problem on a sphere {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.242–255}. No radius in the builder's circle represents the distance between Ghazna and Mecca.

## The world before it

The qibla chapter opens by acknowledging simple methods used by earlier members of the astronomical profession, before introducing a more detailed computation {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.241}. Later, the author returns to a figure representing a method described in astronomical tables, or zijes; he does not place himself at the beginning of the entire subject {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.253}. Another established practice appears when he discusses the reference meridian: the Indian Circle is already in current use before he supplies an alternative {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.256}. These precedents remain in the account. The passage does not justify inventing a named discoverer for every operation or a direct transmission from an author it does not identify.

## The story

Al-Biruni connects determining the positions of towns with public benefit, making qibla direction an application of geographical measurement rather than an ornament added after the tables {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.241}. A Ghazna example, spherical proofs and alternative numerical methods follow, before an explicit change of audience to architects and artisans {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.242–256}. Tümer dates the composition of the Tahdid to416/1025: the record uses that documentary date, not an invented year for a mosque or the first trial of the circle construction {cite:tdv-biruni-tumer works}. The conclusion explains the particular local benefit of investigating Ghazna's position, while retaining a wider geographical purpose {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.258}.

## How it works

In the spherical proof, the author represents the zeniths of Ghazna and Mecca and draws a great circle through them. Its intersection with the horizon supplies the required direction relative to the meridian {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.242–243}. He also handles which side the angle lies on: east or west, and north or south of the east–west line. A sine value alone does not determine the quadrant {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.245–247}.

The architect's first construction begins with a circle and meridian. A point is placed one-third of the radius south of the center; a perpendicular extends west from that point to the circumference, and the center is joined to the intersection {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256}. In our modern unit-circle reconstruction, the southward component is1/3, giving an angle west of south of `acos(1/3)`, approximately70.528779°. These symbols and the decimal answer are our reconstruction of the instructions, not a formula printed in that notation by al-Biruni {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256 reconstructed geometry}.

For the more precise construction, divide the western radius into eighteen parts, count one part inward from its western endpoint, and extend a perpendicular south to the circumference {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.256}. The westward component is17/18 of the radius, so our reconstructed angle is `asin(17/18)`, approximately70.811864° west of south. It is not an angle of17/18 degrees, and the count does not begin at the center {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.256 reconstructed geometry}. The niche wall is perpendicular to the resulting direction, not parallel to it {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.256}.

To check that the result is a ratio rather than a building dimension, choose an imaginary radius of18 paper units. The first point is6 units south of the center; the second is17 units west of it. In both cases the circumference determines the perpendicular's endpoint, not a guessed additional length {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256 reconstructed geometry}. Scaling the whole drawing equally preserves its angle; moving only one point does not. These are modern teaching units, not historical measurements reported by the author or dimensions of a mosque. A small paper diagram can represent a direction without representing the distance between the towns.

## The spread

The inspected pages supply no list of mosques, named builders or documented itinerary of the construction. Addressing artisans establishes an intended audience, not a count of adopters {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–258}. Being reproducible on paper does not prove that every later qibla layout borrowed it. The record therefore invents neither diffusion arrows between cities nor a chain from Ghazna into modern architecture. What is traceable is the explanatory sequence within the book: positions, direction, practical approximation, then ways of establishing the reference meridian {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.241–258}. That is a documented teaching sequence even without a demonstrated history of field use.

## What it was built on

The calculations need the latitude of the town, the latitude of Mecca and their difference in longitude. They use sines, cosines and auxiliary arcs, with figures and ratios explaining the operations {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.241–254}. The practical construction also needs a correct reference direction; this is why the architectural instructions are followed by meridian determination {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.256–258}. Writing “south” at the top of a sheet does not establish true south, and dividing a radius cannot repair a mistaken reference line. The museum's [[invention/abu-wafa-spherical-triangle-methods|Abu al-Wafa spherical-triangle methods]] concern a different mathematical contribution. This passage does not demonstrate that they directly supplied this procedure, so no unsupported dependency edge connects the entries.

## What was built on it

Within the text, a calculated direction becomes a drawn line and then a relationship to the niche wall: the author states that application explicitly {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256}. The passage does not name the inventor of a phone app or a modern map projection, and functional similarity does not establish descent. The dependent-inventions field consequently remains empty, not because the method lacks value but because reception is a separate historical question. The conclusion also identifies finding the direction of travel to a destination as a wider geographical benefit; it does not present an assembled navigation device or prove that a particular traveler used Ghazna's two approximations {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.259}.

## Impact on daily life

The clearest described effect is making knowledge available between professions: a calculator handles elaborate quantities while an artisan needs a limited sequence of drawing operations {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256}. The architectural approximation avoids reproducing all the sexagesimal arithmetic on the building surface, but it does not dispense with the local result it approximates. Calling the niche wall perpendicular to the direction also helps distinguish the line of sight from the line of the wall {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.256}. The pages do not measure labor hours saved, construction cost or the error of an existing mosque. The museum supplies none of those numbers by intuition. This is a historical professional procedure, not a binding religious instruction to today's visitor.

## How it changed history

These pages show that application and proof were not opposites: the author explains the geometry, then changes the approximation level when the user changes {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.242–256}. The conclusion says corrected positions benefit Muslims and also mentions Jerusalem for Jews, east for Christians and the meridian for Sabians. These are the author's descriptions of religious communities, not our generalizations about every community or individual today {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.258–259}. The bounded historical importance is evidence of geography, computation and practical work being connected. It is not a claim that these pages alone changed every building, invented tolerance or founded modern cartography. The documented small procedure does not need those larger claims to matter.

## Who invented it?

The precise attribution is to the exposition and two approximations in the inspected book, preserving its acknowledgment of predecessors. These pages alone do not prove worldwide priority {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.241,253,255–256}. They also contain a significant textual problem: the division instruction on translated page245 reverses the numerator–denominator order used in the example on246 and supported by the proof on247 {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.245–247}. We neither repair it silently nor assign the error to the Arabic author without collating the original.

A second problem is a printed longitude difference of26°22′24″ paired with a sine of27;35,14 on a radius60 scale. Our modern arithmetic gives approximately0.444218 for the printed angle, whereas the printed sine divided by60 is approximately0.459787, close to the sine of27°22′24″ {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation p.242 numerical comparison}. This discrepancy is disclosed, not used as permission to alter the historical longitude or claim that the example passed a modern accuracy test.

## Today

The construction can teach approximation, not determine the qibla of another town. The first historical example reports70°48′15″ from south, the second70°47′13″ west of south, a difference of one minute and two seconds. We neither make them equal nor explain their entire difference as rounding without investigation {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.242,246}. In our reconstruction, the eighteenths construction is closer to those reported angles than the thirds construction. That is a bounded, checkable interpretation of the author's statement about improved precision {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256 reconstructed comparison}. Real-world directional accuracy needs separate coordinates, reference conventions and verification, particularly given the numerical discrepancy in this translation. The museum offers no live compass or surveying service.

## In culture

The move from computation to the craft worker's hand deserves a place in a museum: knowledge does not end at a number, since the author asks how someone can place it on a surface {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256}. But the diagram should not become a slogan suggesting that one direction serves all Muslims or that every niche bears this book's influence. Ghazna's specificity is part of the meaning, not a dispensable detail {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–258}. The entry distinguishes what the text says, what the museum has recalculated and what is not established, rather than producing a heroic image that confuses a Muslim author's contribution with first ownership of every idea he used.

## For young readers

Compare two equal paper circles with clearly marked imaginary directions. The first uses a point one-third of the radius southward; the second a point one eighteenth inward from the western endpoint. Their perpendiculars do not start at the same place {cite:biruni-tahdid-ali-translation pp.255–256 reconstructed paper activity}. Compare the rays from the center and ask which is closer to approximately71°, then remember that this comparison does not establish the direction of a real city. Keep it a paper geometry activity: no solar observations, rooftop work or mosque orientation. The important question behind “more accurate” is always: closer to which value, using which data?
