## Summary

How can an irregular small stone's volume be measured without making it a cube? The *Balance of Wisdom* transmits a conical vessel attributed to [[person/al-biruni-muhammad-ibn-ahmad|Abu Rayhan al-Biruni]], directing displaced water through a side tube into a balance pan {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. Weighing that water enables a comparison of sample volume and weight, but the tube introduces a problem: drops can remain inside it {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. This is a measurement refinement with operating difficulties, not the invention of buoyancy or discovery of gravity.

## What it is

The described apparatus has a neck, handle and side outlet; the drawing distinguishes the receiving balance pan from the vessel {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. It is not [[invention/balance-of-wisdom|al-Khazini's calibrated five-pan balance]], although his book preserves its description. A modern experimental study likewise distinguishes the simple weighing balance from the displacement container, rather than treating one new beam as performing every function {cite:yassi-hydrostatic-balances-2023 p.141}.

Here “specific gravity” means a ratio to reference water, not gravitational force or a universal constant; Ansari distinguishes relative densities, material standards and different table formats {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.201–202}. The terminology does not establish that the vessel embodied Newtonian theory. We call it an instrument for displacement and relative-density measurement.

## The world before it

The *Balance of Wisdom* places its work in an older hydrostatic tradition and names earlier participants; this witnesses the author's historical framing, not independent verification of every ancient story {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp.12–14}. Weighing and displacement knowledge therefore precede the attributed vessel. The narrower question is how moving water becomes something that can reliably be collected and weighed.

Ansari shows that transmitted tables can express the weight of displaced water, weights of equal volumes, or comparisons with reference metals and stones {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 p.201}. Similar headings do not guarantee identical quantities. Without knowing the denominator and reference, correct arithmetic can answer the wrong question.

## The story

Our evidence is not an autograph construction report dated to a particular day. It is a nineteenth-century publication of passages from al-Khazini's later book; the publisher explicitly acknowledges a deficient, erroneous manuscript and offers passages he can explain {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp.53–54}. That mediation belongs beside the attractive drawing, not outside the story.

Ansari says the Beirut manuscript of al-Biruni's treatise and Cheikho's communication were unavailable to him, so this part of his preliminary study relies on Wiedemann {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 p.199}. We do not present his1975 article as direct inspection of every original. Our broad eleventh-century setting and Ghazna location are biographical context, not a verified construction year or workshop {cite:britannica-al-biruni biography}. An unknown year cannot acquire an invented Hijri equivalent.

## The maker(s)

The transmitted illustration names Abu Rayhan's conical instrument, and Khanikoff's publication describes its specific-gravity purpose; this supports attribution of a particular design, not priority over all density measurement {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. Its designer, the later transmitter, the publisher working from a damaged manuscript, and modern reconstructors have distinct roles.

## How it works

The central move converts a question about volume into a weighing problem. The illustration shows a vessel with an outlet leading to a receiving pan: an immersed body occupies volume previously occupied by water, and displaced water is collected for weighing {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. The stone need not have a regular shape. Yet “put in a stone” alone tells us neither what was measured nor what was lost.

In modern notation, with sample mass `M` and completely collected displaced-water mass `m`, sample volume is `m/rho_water` and relative density is `M/m`. This is our mathematical explanation of the described principle, not notation recovered from the manuscript {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54} {cite:yassi-hydrostatic-balances-2023 p.141}. The ratio has no unit. Obtaining mass-per-volume density requires multiplication by water density under the reference conditions, not simply attaching a unit.

Collected displaced-water weight must also be distinguished from an object's apparent immersed weight. If air weight is `A` and apparent immersed weight `B`, hydrostatic comparison gives `A/(A-B)`, not `A/B`. In the collecting vessel, the denominator is already the displaced water's weight; it is not an immersed weight to subtract again. Ansari's distinctions between representations matter to this comparison {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.201–202}. Mixing the denominators changes the answer.

Consider our invented teaching example, not al-Biruni's measurements: a hypothetical84g sample displaces20g of water, giving4.2. Collecting only19.5g gives about4.30769, about2.56% too high. Losing water from the denominator raises the calculated relative density; it does not physically make the sample heavier. A drop retained in the tube is therefore not decorative detail.

The transmitted text itself reports water remaining in the tube and dripping slowly, followed by a drainage refinement; this is not proof of a complete modern theory of capillarity {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. The next page connects a narrow neck to sensitivity in water-level change but notes the difficulty of removing objects through a smaller opening {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.55}. Sensitive reading and convenient handling can compete.

Its millet-grain sensitivity comparison is a reported historical claim, not a calibrated milligram threshold or an uncertainty measured here {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.55}. Our physical explanation assumes a correct starting level and tare, full immersion, complete collection, and no dissolution or trapped bubbles; we do not attribute that entire modern checklist to the author. Nothing in this exhibit requires mercury or unidentified samples.

## The spread

Al-Khazini's book preserved the vessel's description and drawing; subsequent print made extracts accessible. That is a named textual transmission, not evidence of industrial adoption everywhere {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp.53–55}. Ansari compares later sources transmitting or reformatting tables {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.199–203}.

Agreement to many places can reflect a calculated derivative of one reading rather than repeated independent measurements. Ansari explains some very large-volume quantities as scaling existing numbers, not actual enormous samples weighed in the apparatus {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.201–202}. Another table need not mean another workshop, nor another decimal place another observed level of precision.

## What it was built on

The contribution uses inherited relationships between weight, volume and a reference liquid; it did not create displacement knowledge from nothing {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp.12–14,54}. Refinement can mean making a principle measurable and confronting obstacles to collecting water. The transmitted conical form alone does not demonstrate that every geometric detail is optimal.

We do not infer dependence on al-Biruni's Earth measurement merely because the same scholar is involved. Nor does a collecting vessel become a five-pan balance. Shared measurement history is not proof that one design directly generated every part of another; network links need evidence beyond chronological proximity and a common book title.

## What was built on it

After the device description, page55 discusses “our author” experimenting with Abu Rayhan's instrument: in this publication that means al-Khazini. His repeated trials and the following table must not automatically become al-Biruni's own observations {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp.55–56}. Distinguishing designer and later experimenter prevents one person's figures from becoming another's precision certificate.

Yassi and Yassi describe modern IROST reconstruction, with photographs of a vessel and ordinary balance {cite:yassi-hydrostatic-balances-2023 pp.140–141}. This is modern experimental research, not excavated original hardware or proof that every historical condition was recreated. Results concern the replica, its materials and adjustment, even when they illuminate the underlying principle.

## Impact on daily life

Ansari relates mineral and gem identification and valuation to properties including density, brilliance and hardness, rather than colour alone {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.204–205}. Measurement connects with material exchange, not abstract numbers only. But one property, on this evidence, cannot certify every stone's identity or market price.

We have no adoption counts or quantified fraud reduction. Nor does a material name establish sample purity: Ansari discusses purity and material form as complications when comparing historical and present densities {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 p.202}. A significant social need and a measured social outcome are separate claims; an appealing story cannot replace missing data.

## How it changed history

The vessel gives a legible example of turning comparison into measurement while preserving operating difficulty in the text itself {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp.54–55}. Its importance here lies in an inspectable drawing and reasoning, not a “first laboratory” award. Ansari adds another historical question: is a figure an observation or a transformation of an earlier observation? {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.201–203}.

Some close modern comparisons do not prove equal precision across all samples. Successful modern reconstruction cannot repair every manuscript gap or unit dispute. Design, transmitting text, sample, calculation and later experiment remain distinguishable layers; they need not collapse into one perfect account for the study of water and weight to matter.

## Today

Understanding the comparison does not require pretending old units are fixed gram values; Ansari explicitly declines a gram conversion for the mithqal because reference values vary {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 p.201}. Ratios cancel shared units but not water and sample conditions. He also credits al-Biruni with attention to water's temperature-dependent density {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 p.201}.

The exhibit is not a gem-testing laboratory. Dissolving, absorbent or air-trapping samples cannot simply inherit the paper example without further questions. We do not reproduce the historical metal-purification or mercury procedures. The aim is to understand how a measurement is constructed and can fail, not to give a household authentication recipe.

## In culture

Priority stories can merge scholar, instrument and every transmitted table into one moment of success. Ansari instead separates sources, representations and units {cite:ansari-biruni-physics-1975 pp.199–203}. The admitted difficulty of retained water offers a more human account of research than an infallible-machine story {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}.

Admiring a solution need not turn it into the discovery of gravity or superiority in every science. Nor does admiration settle disputed affiliation {cite:tdv-biruni-tumer pp.206–215}. Keeping distinctions visible does not erase achievement; it makes the achievement identifiable and inspectable.

## For young readers

The description sends water through a side outlet towards a weighing pan, while some can remain on the way {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p.54}. On paper only, divide84 by20 and then by19.5. Why does the second answer grow? We changed the water counted as collected, not the object's actual weight. This is our modern example, not a school experiment copied from the manuscript. Use words and numbers, not unknown substances or hazardous metals; ask what was measured and what never reached the pan.
