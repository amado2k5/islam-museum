## Summary
[[place/baghdad|Baghdad]]'s first inner markets occupied four gate roads and arcades, while Duri also records market places outside the walls {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Duri attributes to [[person/al-mansur|al-Mansur]] an order in773CE moving markets from the city toward [[place/al-karkh|Karkh]] for security {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Le Strange describes the shops leaving the arcades and those spaces becoming barracks for city police and horse-guard {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. In a later chapter he retells a security warning attributed to al-Tabari and describes markets moving to suburbs beyond the gates {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. These reports document a reallocation of commercial space, not the invention of markets, a measured security outcome, or proof that every seller moved together {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}.

## What it is
The change concerned the location and use of existing urban spaces: inner market roads gave way to commercial venues outside the walled centre {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Le Strange describes four gate roads lined by arcades, with shops first occupying those routes {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Duri says four other market places already stood outside the walls, so the transfer did not create Baghdad's first outside markets {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Duri names Karkh as the destination, while Le Strange also refers to suburbs outside several gates and singles out Karkh's market street {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. The accounts do not give a street-by-street register that would let us plot every relocated stall {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}.

Duri's wider description places merchants and craftspeople in Karkh south of the Sarath, while soldiers generally lived beyond the walls to the north and west {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. This is his account of Baghdad's spatial pattern, not evidence that every household was assigned a plot under one surviving zoning code {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. It helps explain why moving markets toward Karkh mattered to the city's organization without proving that the transfer created every later occupational quarter {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}.

## The world before it
Markets and trade already belonged to Baghdad's urban life before this reported order; the sources describe both inner arcades and outside market places {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. The arcades formed part of the pre-existing gate roads of the round-city plan, rather than a new structure built for the relocation {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Duri distinguishes the city's construction in762, governmental occupation in763, and completion in766 from the later market transfer dated773 {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Foundation and plan, Markets}. Le Strange says the markets were removed before many years had passed, but does not give a year in the chapter describing the arcades {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. The available accounts therefore place the move in an evolving city, not at its founding {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Foundation and plan, Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}.

## The story
Duri's chronology places the market order after the stages of Baghdad's establishment and dates the transfer to773CE {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Foundation and plan, Markets}. His account gives security as the stated reason for moving markets from the city to Karkh {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Le Strange recounts the episode in a later chapter as a story about a Greek envoy who warns al-Mansur about foreign merchants inside the city {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. Le Strange says that this anecdote is transmitted through al-Tabari and repeated by later authorities; the original medieval account was not checked for this record {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. The exact administrative steps, timetable, and participation of individual traders are not supplied in these passages {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}.

## The maker(s)
The order is attributed to al-Mansur, who is recorded here as political commissioner rather than sole designer or builder of the market district {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Duri discusses the city's architects and construction workforce, but the consulted market passage does not name a separate planner for the relocation {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Foundation and plan, Markets}. ## How it works
Before the move, four principal roads between the gates carried arcades and market shops inside the walls {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Duri's account adds that other markets existed outside the walls before the relocation {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. The reported order shifted the inner markets toward Karkh, while Le Strange also describes new commercial suburbs beyond several gates {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. Le Strange says the arcades cleared of shops were used as lasting quarters for city police and horse-guard {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. This is a change in spatial allocation and building use; the sources do not describe a technical procedure for moving each stall or reconstruct its exact route {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}.

Le Strange gives dimensions for the gate-road arcades: he describes squares about ten yards across and a road between them roughly seven and a half yards wide and one hundred yards long {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. He reports fifty-three archways and suggests that twenty-six stood on each side with one at the end; he also says teak double doors could close the road entrance {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. These are measurements and reconstructions reported in a later historical account, not a contemporary survey of the markets {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. For Karkh's main market street he gives forty ells, which he renders as twenty yards {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. Neither figure supplies the location of each shop or proves how the move was carried out {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}.

## The spread
The evidence consulted concerns Baghdad and its changing districts; it does not document this particular relocation being copied in another city {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}. Le Strange describes Karkh as a commercial centre of western Baghdad that continued to expand after its establishment {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. Duri describes further merchant quarters and distinct bazaars as Baghdad grew, but does not present them as a dated export of the original order {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Similar arrangements elsewhere would not by themselves prove direct transmission from Baghdad {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}. No route, named recipient city, or chain of planners is provided in the two accounts used here {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}.

## What it was built on
The reallocation used an existing city plan, gate roads, and arcades rather than introducing a new kind of marketplace {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}. Duri's description of four outside market places shows that commercial activity was not confined to the inner streets before the move {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Le Strange says Karkh was not included in the original plan of the round city and describes it as a later suburb associated with the commercial change {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. The sources do not identify a new market technology, trading instrument, or legal code as part of this episode {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}. Its documented basis is spatial administration applied to streets and markets already in use {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26}.

## What was built on it
After the relocation, Duri describes separate bazaars for different trades and crafts in Karkh, including markets for produce sellers, grocers, money changers, and booksellers {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. He also records merchant quarters linked with arrivals from several named cities as Baghdad expanded {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. These details belong to his broader account of growth; they are not all dated to the order of773CE {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Le Strange describes Karkh's principal market street and its later growth, but does not establish that every later commercial feature was a direct consequence of this single decision {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. The record therefore links the relocation with subsequent urban descriptions without turning sequence into a complete causal story {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}.

Duri lists several kinds of trade and reports that merchant groups had quarters and leaders as the city grew {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. This provides evidence about how commercial space was described, not a census of all occupations or residents in Karkh {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. ## How it changed history
Le Strange's account presents Karkh as a district that became a major commercial centre of western Baghdad and continued to grow over time {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. Duri describes Baghdad's wider expansion, including growing settlement on the eastern bank and the development of distinct merchant areas {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets}. Those developments show a changing city, but the cited passages do not isolate the market order as their sole cause {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. Le Strange also reports a later fire in Karkh and its rebuilding, illustrating that the district's history extended beyond its initial market arrangement {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.65–66}. The historical significance recorded here is the documented reallocation and reuse of urban space, not a measured economic or security result {cite:tdv-bagdat-duri Markets} {cite:lestrange-baghdad-1900 p.25–26,p.65–66}.
