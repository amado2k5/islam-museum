## Summary

In the [[work/hisab-kuttab-abu-wafa|arithmetic needed by scribes and officials]], [[person/abu-al-wafa-al-buzjani|Abu al-Wafa al-Buzjani]] explains multiplication through complements to the next ten. The procedure becomes particularly revealing when one intermediate quantity is called a debt, although both factors and the final product are positive. This entry is not about inventing debt or the world's first negative number. It concerns a specific rule and evidence that a negative intermediate was accepted within practical arithmetic {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}.

The historian's example is three multiplied by five. Their complements to ten are seven and five; subtracting five from three yields a debt of two. Multiplying that debt by ten and adding thirty-five gives fifteen. Modern symbols make the calculation legible here, but are not a facsimile of the author's verbal explanation or evidence that his book used our notation {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2} {cite:abu-wafa-mactutor arithmetic}.

## How it works

Here we explicitly use modern algebra. Let the factors be x and y in the same decimal decade, and let N be the next ten. Write their complements as u=N−x and v=N−y. Then xy=N²−N(u+v)+uv, or equivalently xy=(x+y−N)N+(N−x)(N−y). This reformulates the verbal rule reported by the historian; it does not claim that Latin variables were found in the manuscript {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}.

For the reported 3×5 example, the reference is 10, the complements are 7 and 5, and the correction is 3+5−10=−2. Combining −20 with 35 gives 15. Ignoring the sign and adding 20 to 35 would instead give 55, which is not the product. The debt language preserves the subtraction's meaning while the calculation passes below zero {cite:abu-wafa-youschkevitch-dsb PDFp2}.

In the museum's independent modern example, 23×27 uses reference 30 and complements 7 and 3. The correction contributes 20×30 and the complement product contributes 21, giving 621. These numbers are not attributed to an old manuscript or lesson. Expanding the identity cancels the extra terms and leaves xy exactly. This is not an approximation or an accuracy table: positive multiplication need not have exclusively positive intermediate steps.
