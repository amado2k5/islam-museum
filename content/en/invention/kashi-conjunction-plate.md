## Summary

The plate of conjunctions is an astronomical computing instrument attributed to [[person/al-kashi|Ghiyath al-Din al-Kashi]] by Petra Schmidl's astronomical biography. Its task is to estimate the time of an expected conjunction through linear interpolation: moving between known endpoint information under an assumption of uniform change. It neither observes planets unaided nor extracts their motion from nothing, and it cannot turn an approximation into observational certainty {cite:schmidl-kashi-bea Nuzha}.

Schmidl distinguishes it from the “plate of heavens,” a broader planetary-position calculator. Sadettin Okten likewise identifies two instruments and assigns interpolation to the conjunction plate. This record therefore does not recount [[invention/equatorium-al-zarqali|al-Zarqali's equatorium]], al-Kashi's [[invention/kashi-sine-one-degree|sine computation]] or his [[invention/al-kashi-pi-calculation|circumference calculation]] under a new name. ## How it works

Assume the difference changes linearly over the interval. Its total change is three degrees across twelve hours; reaching zero from plus2 requires two-thirds of that interval, eight hours.
