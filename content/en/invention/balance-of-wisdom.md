## Summary
Can an object's weight tell us that it is pure gold? One reading is not enough: [[person/al-khazini|Abd al-Rahman al-Khazini]] described a balance combining weighing in air, weighing in liquid and calibrated pan positions to investigate metals and gems, as Robert E. Hall explains {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. The instrument belonged to the setting of Sultan Sanjar's treasury at [[place/merv|Merv]], and its treatise was written in515/1121–1122 {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography; Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. This is not weighing invented from nothing, but a further refinement within a long hydrostatic-balance tradition {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}.

## What it is
The instrument has a long beam, a sensitive indicator tongue and five pans used in different arrangements, including one in which the object is immersed in a known liquid {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. The pans have different functions: some hold loads in air, one carries the submerged object, and two can move along the graduated beam {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Five pans therefore do not simply mean weighing five unrelated objects; their arrangement connects different readings within one measurement {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Hall discusses both “balance of wisdom” and “comprehensive balance,” linking sound discrimination of materials with the instrument's combined capabilities {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}.

## The world before it
The treatise itself traces a history beginning with Archimedes and continuing through later balances: its author does not present himself as the first person to discover weighing or water's support of an immersed body {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. Hall credits the indicator tongue to al-Razi; a third pan was known before al-Khazini, while al-Asfizari supplied the two movable pans and proposed specific-gravity marks on the beam {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Specific gravity concerns comparative material density, not the weight of an individual lump {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. The underlying theories also draw on earlier Greek and Arabic work; Hall does not credit al-Khazini with originating them all {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}.

## The story
Hall places the work at Merv under Sanjar's patronage and says the balance was constructed for the treasury {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. He also relates an older story that al-Asfizari's preceding balance was destroyed out of fear that it would expose fraud; this remains a transmitted account, not a modern eyewitness finding {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. Al-Khazini subsequently described a similar, further refined instrument and left a substantial technical account {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. The515/1121–1122 pair dates that treatise, not an exact manufacturing day, and not the later manuscript pictured here {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma} {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Origin}.

## The maker(s)
Abd al-Rahman al-Khazini is not Abu Jafar al-Khazin or Ibn al-Haytham; Hall warns against these confusions and against placing him in Baghdad without evidence {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. He reports Byzantine origin, enslavement in youth and mathematical and philosophical education; none of those facts establishes a religion {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. The basis used here is more specific: the author's own preface, printed in Arabic and English, identifies Muhammad as his prophet {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p. 3}. Sunni is the museum's explicit default frame for this documented Seljuq setting, not a declaration of his personal legal school; Hall's sections read name no contrary affiliation {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. His technical role is “improver,” without erasing al-Asfizari and earlier contributors {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}.

## How it works
An immersed object has a lower apparent weight because the liquid supports it: comparison of its air weight and the loss of weight in water supplies information about density in the procedure Hall describes {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Equal dry weights alone therefore do not establish equal materials; the volume displacing liquid also matters {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}.

The second principle concerns the lever arm: balance depends on the load's distance from the suspension as well as its magnitude {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Once pan positions are calibrated for known substances, the beam embodies part of the calculation; in particular arrangements, results can be read from its divisions rather than recalculated at every step {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Hall tentatively identifies al-Khazini's special refinement chiefly with marks for different substances and more than one reference liquid, not the invention of movable pans {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}.

Testing a binary alloy assumes that its two components are known: corresponding pan positions allow the distribution of counterweights to indicate their proportions {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. A significant qualification is that the mixture's volume must approximately equal the sum of its components' volumes; Hall notes that this is approximately true of many alloys, not a universal chemical-identification rule {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}.

Sensitivity also depends on suspension, careful construction and the long tongue, not merely the number of pans {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Hall reports al-Khazini's claimed sensitivity of one part in60,000 to one in100,000 for a load around4.5 kilograms, the range depending on a historical unit's value {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. This is an attributed historical claim, not a museum replication or a performance guarantee for every drawn copy.

Hall specifies a beam four cubits long, approximately two metres in his conversion, and an indicator around half a metre long: substantial dimensions for comparing small differences {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Parallel threads and crosspieces form the suspension, combining stability with amplification of tongue movement; the illustration alone does not fully explain this arrangement {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Pan-suspension rings settle into beam marks, making accurate positioning part of measurement rather than decorative graduation {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}.

Pan shapes also reflect their functions: Hall describes a pointed pan facilitating immersion, another with indented sides allowing close placement, and a running weight for balancing adjustments {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. His account stresses that the water must reach all parts of the tested object; merely suspending something over a vessel does not accomplish the comparison {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Component geometry and procedure therefore work together: a sensitive tongue cannot compensate for choosing the reference liquid or positioning the object incorrectly {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}.

## The spread
Evidence establishes circulation of the text more clearly than circulation of working instruments {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Hall identifies later encyclopedic and mineralogical use, including a work of Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, but finds no clear continuing line of practitioners of this balance {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Penn's later manuscript also witnesses copying of the scientific material {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Origin; Summary}. These observations are not sales figures or proof of a network of treasuries using the apparatus. Accordingly, no unsupported geographical spread route is plotted.

## What it was built on
The treatise comprises eight books and fifty chapters covering principles, liquid-measuring instruments, earlier balances, measurement methods and other applications {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Contents of Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. Its resources include Archimedes, Thabit ibn Qurra, Abu Sahl al-Quhi, Ibn al-Haytham, al-Biruni and al-Asfizari in different roles, not all as makers of the same instrument {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Hall particularly stresses the debt to al-Biruni in specific-gravity measurement and descriptions of refined apparatus {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. The work combines inherited texts with construction and calibration; each chapter is not a separate announcement of discovery {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}.

## What was built on it
The later development established here is textual: material was preserved and quoted in manuscripts and encyclopedias {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam} {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Summary}. In the nineteenth century Khanikoff supplied Arabic extracts and analysis published in English with the journal's publication committee {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p. 1}. This was neither a complete translation nor an infallible edition: the old publication acknowledges an incomplete manuscript, and Hall warns of translation errors {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 p. 3} {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. No direct causal line to every modern analytical balance is drawn.

## Impact on daily life
Testing metals and gems puts the instrument within the problem of trusting materials rather than their colour or advertised name {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma; The Balance of Wisdom}. Sanjar's treasury is its documented institutional setting, not evidence that every market owned one {cite:khazini-hall-dsb biography}. Under the stated comparisons and conditions, the procedure could distinguish a precious material from a substitute, but that is not proof of a measured decline in fraud across a city or period {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. The source provides neither adoption statistics nor merchants' or treasuries' accounts of savings; these effects are not invented to enlarge its importance.

## How it changed history
The book's value also lies in preserving descriptions of earlier apparatus and texts; preservation of a description does not mean preservation of its physical instrument {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma; Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Its historical introduction shows an author conscious of cumulative refinement, acknowledging predecessors and connecting accuracy to successive contributions {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. That offers a history of technology in which calibration, testing and transmission matter alongside the maker's name {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. It does not by itself establish the foundation of modern chemistry or a universal descent of later weighing devices from this one.

## Who invented it?
The answer becomes more precise when the object of the claim is specified: not the first balance, but al-Khazini's refined version and calibration procedures {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Hall explicitly credits al-Asfizari with the earlier movable pans and regards most of the fundamental theory as inherited {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. The museum therefore classifies this as an improvement and preserves Hall's qualification about the location of its distinctive contribution, rather than turning a tentative assessment into absolute priority for every component {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Al-Khazini and the Science of Weights in Islam}. Naming the predecessor makes the refinement more intelligible, not less valuable.

## Today
Penn Libraries makes manuscript LJS386 digitally accessible, with a catalogue explaining its contents and uncertain dating {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Origin; Summary}. The image helps readers examine beam, pans and indicator, but cannot measure suspension friction or the accuracy of a physical instrument's graduations {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Summary} {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Hall's discussion comparing historical precision with some later measurements remains an attributed historical assessment, not contemporary laboratory certification of the drawing {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. The display explains design and evidence rather than promising untested performance.

## Surviving examples
The illustrated witness here is LJS386 fol80v: a graduated beam, suspension, indicator and pans drawn in red and black, not a photograph of surviving metalwork {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Summary; fol80v reproduction}. Penn describes an undated copy probably made between the later thirteenth and earlier fourteenth centuries, expressed as1250?–1350?; this is broader than the circa1270 description attached to the Commons image {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Origin}. Its catalogue records98 leaves,26 diagrams and18 tables, with damaged leaf edges {cite:penn-ljs386-balance Extent; Summary}. A copy date is not an invention date, and survival of the book does not establish survival of al-Khazini's original balance.

## In culture
Hall reads the name “wisdom” in both technical and ethical senses: sound discrimination of genuine and adulterated materials, alongside a well-founded and carefully constructed instrument {cite:khazini-hall-dsb Kitab Mizan al-Hikma}. The author's preface brings knowledge, action and justice together, placing reliable measurement within a wider conception of fairness {cite:khazini-khanikoff-1859 pp. 3–4}. This describes a historical author's voice, not a religious judgement by the museum on makers or users. Ethical language also does not make an equation experimentally true: conditions and sensitivity still require examination independently of the name's appeal {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}.

## For young readers
Imagine two objects of equal weight in air, one larger than the other. When immersed, water supports them by different amounts; that difference supplies extra information about their materials {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. A drawing of a seesaw can explain the lever principle: moving a load away from the middle changes balance without changing its weight {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. But detecting a difference does not identify every unknown substance; the described test needs references and conditions {cite:khazini-hall-dsb The Balance of Wisdom}. Find the tongue and pans in the image, then ask what the drawing establishes and what would require an experiment.
