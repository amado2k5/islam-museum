## Summary

In the [[work/kitab-al-manazir|Book of Optics]],[[person/ibn-al-haytham|Ibn al-Haytham]] joined light and colour radiation,the eye's structure and the interpretation of what we see into a detailed account of vision. The eye does not reach out to touch an object with a ray:it is affected by what arrives from that object. The contribution is a mathematical,physical and perceptual synthesis,not merely reversing an arrow or inventing the first idea that something enters the eye from outside {cite:smith-alhacen-visual-perception-2001 intro-lii-lvii}.

The success of incoming light must remain distinct from the rest of the model's limitations. Alhacen located sensitivity in the crystalline humour and used visual spirit,a hollow optic nerve and geometry unlike the modern eye. This is not a retinal-image theory or completed neuroscience. It does not recount the [[invention/camera-obscura|dark-room experiment]] or [[invention/ibn-al-haytham-circular-reflection|circular reflection problem]] as newly discovered contributions {cite:smith-alhacen-visual-perception-2001 intro-lvii-lxii}.

## What it is

The account explains how a visible object affects ocular sensation and how we understand shape,distance and other characteristics. Smith organizes Books1–3as physical/anatomical foundations and conditions;transformation of impressions into perception;then errors when conditions transgress limits. It is consequently neither one ray diagram nor one isolated instrument {cite:smith-alhacen-visual-perception-2001 intro-xviii}.

Sensing light and colour differs from inferring corporeity,size and distance. A tall man's representation in the perceptual apparatus is not itself as tall as that man:interpretation,discrimination and memory intervene. Smith is analysing historical conceptual language,not attributing neurons or contemporary image processing to the author. ## How it works

Seeing has conditions:appropriate separation,facing object and eye,perceptible size,light,a transparent medium and a properly disposed eye. Saying “light enters” does not explain why everything is not visible in every circumstance. Missing conditions or exceeded limits can cause failure or error;they are not exceptions forgotten after drawing the ray {cite:smith-alhacen-visual-perception-2001 intro-liii-lvi}.

Each spot on a luminous surface radiates wherever transparency allows. Smith distinguishes the mathematical ray,an analytic representation,from an actual thin physical line. Radiation and surfaces are continuous;drawn rays are not invisible wires. This raises a problem:if radiation from each spot reaches many ocular locations,how does an ordered image emerge? {cite:smith-alhacen-visual-perception-2001 intro-liv-and-lx}.

The model privileges impressions arriving orthogonally at the crystalline surface,assuming concentric anterior surfaces. These directions make a stronger,undeviated impression,allowing a point-by-point selection. Further posterior refraction assumptions prevent crossing that would invert the order. Smith shows how geometry was accommodated to theory. It is not silently repaired into a contemporary eye model {cite:smith-alhacen-visual-perception-2001 intro-lix-lxii}.

Discrimination follows sensation. Light and colour are immediately sensed in this division;distance,size and corporeity require comparison and experience.
