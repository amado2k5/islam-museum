## Summary
The zij is the Islamic astronomical handbook-with-tables: a book that tabulates the parameters needed to compute the positions of sun, moon, stars and planets for any date. Its name comes from Middle Persian zih or zig, "cord", transferred from the loom's threads to the rows and columns of tables. More than two hundred zijes survive from the eighth to the fifteenth centuries, from Baghdad to Maragha and Samarkand — the single most characteristic product of Islamic astronomy {cite:wikipedia-zij-en} {cite:kennedy-islamic-astronomical-tables-1956}.

## What it is
A pre-computed computational book: chronology and eras, geographical longitudes and latitudes, star tables, trigonometrical functions (sine and tangent), the equation of time, planetary motions, eclipse computation, tables for the first visibility of the lunar crescent, and instructions for computing within the geocentric epicyclic model {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## The world before it
Before the zij, astronomical values were scattered across traditions: Ptolemy's Handy Tables (the Arabic Qanun) in Greek, the Sasanian Zij-i Shah in Persian, the Indian siddhantas with their own cycles. Every computation had to be re-derived or read off a foreign table {cite:wikipedia-zij-en} {cite:kennedy-islamic-astronomical-tables-1956}.

## The story
In Ma'muni Baghdad al-Khwarizmi produced the Zij al-Sindhind and Habash al-Hasib his zij, and the genre was born from the union of the three traditions. Across the centuries al-Battani composed the Zij al-Sabi at Raqqa, al-Tusi the Ilkhani Zij at Maragha (1259), and Ulugh Beg the Zij-i Sultani at Samarkand (1438/9); production extended to Constantinople and India {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## The maker(s)
A genre has no single inventor: al-Khwarizmi and Habash are the first named makers at Baghdad, then a lineage of al-Battani, al-Biruni, al-Tusi and Ulugh Beg. Kennedy records that most adopted earlier theory, so their contribution lay in improved trigonometrical, computational and observational technique {cite:wikipedia-zij-en} {cite:kennedy-islamic-astronomical-tables-1956}.

## How it works
The computer picks a date, reads the planet's mean position from a table, applies the equation corrections tabulated after it, and obtains the ecliptic longitude; sun-and-moon tables yield eclipses and crescent visibility, and the trigonometrical functions give solar altitudes for the hours of the day {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## The spread
From Baghdad the genre travelled east to Maragha, Samarkand and India — where nearly a hundred more zijes were composed into the eighteenth century — and west into al-Andalus, whence the Latin Toledan and Alfonsine table-books descend {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## What it was built on
Three roots: Ptolemy's Handy Tables, the Sasanian Zij-i Shah, and the Indian siddhantas; the zij imported their material and reorganised it into a comprehensive Arabic book {cite:wikipedia-zij-en} {cite:kennedy-islamic-astronomical-tables-1956}.

## What was built on it
Everything downstream in computational astronomy: the zijes of Ibn al-Shatir and Taqi al-Din, the Latin table traditions, the printed ephemerides, and today's planetary software {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## Impact on daily life
The zij served the calendar, prayer times, seasonal festivals and judicial astrology: the mosque timekeeper, the judge and the physician all read from its tables {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## How it changed history
It made computational astronomy a teachable, inheritable craft; it preserved eight centuries of accumulated observational values — the infrastructure on which the history of Islamic astronomy is studied {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## Who invented it?
No one person: it arose as a genre in Ma'muni Baghdad at the hands of a translating, observing generation, al-Khwarizmi and Habash al-Hasib first among them {cite:wikipedia-zij-en} {cite:kennedy-islamic-astronomical-tables-1956}.

## Today
No longer a working document after the computer, the zij remains the primary material of the field's history: its manuscripts are studied and its parameters harvested into databases such as PARAMS {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## Surviving examples
Best known: al-Khwarizmi's zij in its Latin translation, al-Battani's complete Zij al-Sabi, the Ilkhani and Sultani zijes; manuscript collections hold hundreds more {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## In culture
"Zij" became a loan-name for the tabulated handbook in European languages (azig), and the term runs through every history of medieval astronomy {cite:wikipedia-zij-en}.

## For young readers
Imagine a book full of tables: pick a date, read one line, and it tells you where the sun, moon and planets stand. Muslim astronomers made hundreds of these books — their computer a thousand years before computers.
