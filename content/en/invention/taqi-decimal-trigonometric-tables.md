## Summary

In [[work/jaridat-al-durar|Jaridat al-durar wa-kharidat al-fikar]], historians of science identify a particular computational development by [[person/taqi-al-din-ibn-maruf|Taqi al-Din ibn Ma'ruf]]: applying decimal fractions to trigonometric functions, astronomical tables and fractional parts of degrees. This does not mean that he invented decimal fractions or trigonometry. Rather, he extended an inherited arithmetic into an astronomer's working instrument. Ihsan Fazlioglu connects this achievement with the continuation of al-Kashi's arithmetic and describes decimal tables while retaining an exception for the fixed-star table. We do not remove that exception to produce a more spectacular headline {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea decimal-zij discussion and conclusion}.

The contribution is a refinement in organizing calculation and representing numbers. It differs from [[invention/decimal-fractions-al-uqlidisi|al-Uqlidisi's decimal-fraction operations]], and from invention of a calculator or modern decimal-point symbol.

## What it is

A zij is a work of astronomical computation and tables, not merely a list of stars. Fazlioglu describes the Jarida and Tashil zij al-ashariyya as expressing fractional parts of degrees decimally and carrying out calculations accordingly; he also mentions tangent and cotangent tables. TDV describes the Jarida as a small zij containing decimal tables {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea decimal-zij discussion} {cite:tdv-takiyyuddin-er-rasid work3}.

Three things must remain distinct: the angle being measured, the function connecting it with a ratio or a length at a chosen scale, and the notation used to write the resulting number. Moving a fraction into base ten does not change the triangle. Readability and checking depend on consistent conventions and on knowing what every place represents; “decimal” alone cannot explain an entire page.

## The world before it

Sexagesimal arithmetic, dividing a unit into sixty parts and each part into sixty again, already belonged to astronomical practice, as did traditions of sines and shadows. Fazlioglu connects Taqi al-Din with the Samarqand school and the works of al-Kashi, Qadizade and Ali Qushji, and describes his continuation of decimal arithmetic in theory and practice, not its appearance from nothing {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea education and mathematical works}.

Conversely, an earlier author is not automatically a directly documented predecessor whose book lay on his desk. Al-Uqlidisi provides a museum comparison; we do not draw a personal transmission edge without evidence. A single-inventor story would erase centuries of teaching, writing and correction. Calling every predecessor's contribution an invention of this particular zij would instead confuse general arithmetic with its specific application.

## The story

Taqi al-Din received religious and mathematical education, taught and served as a judge in Egypt, and returned to [[place/istanbul|Istanbul]] in1570 before becoming head astronomer in1571, according to Fazlioglu. The biography describes books and instruments reaching him and encouragement to pursue mathematics and astronomy. The tables thus belong to a scientific life involving institutions and relationships rather than an idea detached from work {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea biography}.

The passages used here do not establish an exact composition date for the Jarida. Our open interval ends at1585, the death year in the biography, not an assertion that the invention occurred then. ## How it works

We use explicitly modern teaching examples, not numbers purportedly transcribed from the Jarida. Half a degree is0.5degree and30arcminutes; half an arcminute is instead one120th of a degree. A degree contains60arcminutes and an arcminute60arcseconds. Changing notation never licenses dropping the unit. Likewise, the same teaching quantity can be written0.25 or one quarter, and0.75 or three quarters. The notation here is contemporary. TDV says that Taqi al-Din expressed fractional places verbally without the modern decimal separator. We must not add a point in a new drawing and then present it as his invention. ## How it changed history

The contribution reveals decimal computation entering Ottoman astronomical work, and reminds us that the history of trigonometry concerns more than names of functions or drawings of triangles. Organizing computational tools and explaining their use also belong to scientific knowledge. Fazlioglu's conclusion identifies the application of decimals to astronomy and trigonometry among Taqi al-Din's important contributions alongside his observatory activity {cite:taqi-fazlioglu-bea conclusion}.

That significance is not a claim that he alone caused modern science, or that every later school adopted his system. Decimal notation does not automatically produce more accurate observation: measurement quality, model correctness, number of places and calculation method require separate investigation.
