## Summary
Turning recurring distribution into ordered names and allocated shares is the subject of this story, not inventing writing or every pension in the world: reference accounts associate an early Islamic stipend register with [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]], while retaining older administrative traditions in conquered regions {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun} {cite:britannica-divan}. [[work/kitab-al-kharaj-abu-yusuf|Abu Yusuf's Kitab al-Kharaj]] transmits consultation, enrolment and differentiated grants, but its accounts differ in amounts and do not constitute a single original ledger {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed26–27} {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed45–51}. Modern “pension” terminology is not turned into proof of universal retirement insurance {cite:britannica-divan}.

## What it is
Diwan here means a register of recipients and allocations; the word later also meant the office administering government business, not every later institution founded at once by Umar {cite:tdv-divan-duri definition-and-development} {cite:britannica-divan}. Duri distinguishes annual ata from monthly provisions, describing forces, families, Medinans and some mawali arranged by tribe {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. This is broader than Britannica's emphasis on free Arab warriors: the difference remains visible rather than deleting families or imposing one stage's conditions on every period {cite:britannica-divan} {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. The subject is a particular recurring distribution register, not an alternative name for every tax and treasury.

## The world before it
Duri describes Sasanian and Byzantine precedents and the continuation of regional land-tax offices: administrative recordkeeping did not begin on a blank page {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Abu Yusuf reports earlier distribution under Abu Bakr on an equal basis, contrasting it with preference attributed to Umar {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed45–46}. Distribution before the register prevents confusing the first giving of money with organizing named recipients and recurring grants {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed45–49}. Older local offices do not erase the new arrangement; they locate it within a world of multiple administrative languages and experiences {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}.

## The story
Duri notes sources placing establishment in15AH/636CE but prefers authorities giving20AH/641CE; this preference is attributed, not converted into agreement by hiding the alternative {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Abu Yusuf's consultation report connects news from Iraq with choosing between dividing the land itself and retaining recurring resources for forces, descendants and future recipients {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed26–27}. Another account has an unnamed participant suggest registers after a large remittance: no name is invented for that participant and the narrative is not transformed into precisely dated meeting minutes {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed48–49}. These are traditions explaining a practice, not a complete founding archive.

## The maker(s)
**Declared Sunni frame:** Britannica explicitly identifies Umar as Muslim and credits the diwan to his administration; neither identity nor contribution is inferred from his name, family or territory {cite:britannica-umar-i}. The museum's frame does not assume personal membership in a later sectarian institution; the maker record retains its separately attributed traditional assessments.

**Historical reading:** commissioning did not mean solitary authorship of every operation. Duri describes three genealogically knowledgeable compilers, while Abu Yusuf reports Zayd ibn Thabit's role distributing grants to the Ansar {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun} {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed49}. Umar's recorded role is administrative commissioner, not sole inventor of accounting, and acknowledging shared implementation does not authenticate every transmitted detail equally.

## How it works
**Enrolment.** A name identifies inclusion in the recorded group; ordering makes a list usable, but a list is not a delivery receipt. This is a modern explanatory distinction, not a reconstruction of an original form {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Tribal organization in the account is not equivalent to a modern employee number or universal citizenship rule {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}.

**Allocation.** The description distinguishes entitlement from its amount, relating differentiated grants to precedence and relationship to the Prophet {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun} {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed46–48}. Reporting that criterion does not prescribe it today or mean everyone received the same allocation. Accounts include women and children, so every recipient cannot be recast as a warrior standing in one military rank {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed46–50}.

**Recurrence and resources.** Annual grants differ from monthly provisions in Duri's account; combining money and food as one interchangeable unit would erase that distinction {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. The land narrative concerns resources for garrisons and descendants rather than dividing the entire asset once among those present {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed26–27}. This does not supply a surviving budget for every year or a measurable modern percentage of military spending.

**Disbursement.** Abu Yusuf reports Zayd's distribution and Zaynab bint Jahsh receiving and redistributing her grant; these are transmitted execution narratives, not bank statements proving payment to every registered recipient {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed49}. In a modern paper exercise an allocation can remain undelivered or be marked delivered: the contrast explains why a recorded share alone cannot establish actual payment.

## The spread
Duri describes similar registers at Basra, Kufa and Fustat alongside older regional fiscal offices, not immediate eradication of previous machinery or uniform administrative language {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. He describes tribal registration continuing to the late Umayyad period and subsequent specialization into different offices, not one founding moment containing every later institution {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun-and-Umayyad}. A stipend list must remain distinct from postal, seal and correspondence offices even where the same word eventually names them {cite:britannica-divan} {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Umayyad}.

## What it was built on
The organization drew on earlier distribution, knowledge of names and genealogy, and an expanding state's resources; it did not discover currency or invent all accounting {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed45–49} {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Duri includes stories explaining the word's origin, but their presence as reports does not make them established etymology {cite:tdv-divan-duri definition}. Visitors can explore [[concept/ahl-al-diwan|ahl al-diwan]] while distinguishing later collected legal definitions from a first-generation institution: a shared term does not prove identical conditions in every century {cite:tdv-divan-duri development}.

## What was built on it
Diwan developed from register to office and to very different institutions across dynasties; Britannica outlines later eastern, Indian and Ottoman meanings without making them identical copies of Umar's list {cite:britannica-divan}. Duri's later sections describe expenditure, army, correspondence and supervisory bureaus, some much more complex: this is organizational change, not a package already complete in641CE {cite:tdv-divan-duri later-bureaus}. Resemblance between a list and modern payroll software does not by itself establish transmission of every technical procedure or legal definition.

## Impact on daily life
Recorded entitlements connect resources to named recipients and recurring amounts, rather than a one-off distribution that says nothing about the following year {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Yet distribution was disputed: Abu Yusuf contrasts equality and preference and supplies varying amounts and categories, not an identical salary for everyone {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed45–50}. Funding involved land and people providing revenues; institutional description does not erase those bearing taxation's cost or turn conquest into a benefit narrative without other participants {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed26–27} {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Historical explanation neither endorses every distinction nor issues a present-day fiscal ruling.

## How it changed history
The register organized repeatable administrative memory connecting named groups and allocations within state formation; it need not be the world's first writing to matter {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun-and-development}. The land account asks about people coming after the immediate recipients, emphasizing continuity of resources rather than only the current stock {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed26–27}. The literary evidence still does not yield year-by-year figures or prove that this register alone caused all military and social outcomes attributed to the state. Its explanatory reach is narrower than such a causal claim.

## Who invented it?
The specific attribution is register-based stipend organization under Umar, with consultation, compilers and older administrative and distributive practices, not every pension, bureau or public fund {cite:britannica-umar-i} {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Variation prevents inventing one salary table: Abu Yusuf reports12000for the Prophet's wives with qualifications in one account, but10000with12000for Aisha in another; the edition's printed48footnote explicitly acknowledges a different report {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed46–48}. Ansar allocations likewise vary, including3000in the printed49account; neither deleting a report nor adding figures as certain simultaneous payments respects the witness {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed46–49}.

## Today
“Pension” translates Britannica's terminology here without proving a retirement age, employee contributions or universal social insurance; those modern details are absent from this description {cite:britannica-divan}. A useful comparison isolates enrolment, allocation and payment, then asks what resembles or differs instead of declaring identity because there are names and sums {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. Abu Yusuf also reports a later intention to equalize before Umar died; an intention is not an implemented reform whose supposed results can be added to earlier practice {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed50}.

## In culture
The word also names poetry collections; Britannica connects its furniture meaning with administrative sitting space, without turning every poem book or sofa into a material remnant of Umar's register {cite:britannica-divan}. Such semantic breadth shows how one term can carry memories of writing, space and authority, but requires dating each use rather than compressing centuries into a single origin {cite:tdv-divan-duri definition-and-development}. The grant accounts also preserve questions about social ordering and preference, presented through attributed participants and disagreements rather than as a ruling for contemporary distribution {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed45–50}.

## For young readers
Write names on paper cards, add imaginary paper shares, then mark whether each share was delivered. This is a modern classroom exercise, not a historical payroll reconstruction. Do names alone reveal the total? Does a total prove everyone received a share? These questions distinguish enrolment, allocation and execution in the institution's history {cite:tdv-divan-duri early-Rashidun}. If two accounts give different figures, keep two source cards rather than inventing a third number: even the edition's inspected footnote recognizes variation {cite:kitab-al-kharaj-salafiyya-1392 printed48}.
