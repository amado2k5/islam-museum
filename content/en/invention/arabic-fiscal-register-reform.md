## Summary
A tax amount might remain the same while the language in which an official reads its account changes. Baladhuri, in Hitti's translation, reports that [[person/abd-al-malik-ibn-marwan|Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan]] ordered Syrian registers transferred from Greek in81AH and Sulaiman ibn-Sa'd undertook the work; other pages describe Iraqi accounts moving from Persian into Arabic {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.465–466}. This is a working-language reform within existing administration, not invention of Arabic, accounts or the diwan. Duri describes regional stages, not one day on which every language disappeared {cite:tdv-divan-duri Arabicization}. The accompanying643bilingual papyrus predates the reform; it is not Abd al-Malik's register {cite:commons-perf-558-recto}.

## What it is
Arabicization here transfers written accounts and their terminology into a language administration decides to use, changing the capacity to read and operate a register, not simply adding an Arabic cover heading {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.465–466}. Duri explains that diwan can mean a register or officials' workplace and that the kharaj office assesses and collects revenue {cite:tdv-divan-duri}. Document, institution, language and obligation are therefore distinguished. An Arabic page does not itself prove a tax amount fair, its collection correct, or every official able to read every account. [[concept/kharaj|Kharaj]] is a historical term with its own usages; this account offers neither a tax ruling nor a financial system to apply today.

## The world before it
The Syrian narrative names Greek registers. The Iraqi narrative begins with Persian accounts for the Sawad and Iraq, a chief secretary and an assistant knowing both languages {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.465}. Duri adds Coptic/Greek in Egypt and persistent local recording and dating practices {cite:tdv-divan-duri Arabicization}. This is inherited expertise, not empty books awaiting humanity's first accounts. A clerk's knowledge concerns what an entry means, not only the shapes of its letters. The picture contains Greek writing above and Arabic lower right on one papyrus; its metadata dates it643 {cite:commons-perf-558-recto}. One face cannot establish every bureau's language, but earlier bilingual evidence prevents official adoption being equated with Arabic's first administrative appearance.

## The story
Printed301reports Abd al-Malik's81AHcommission and Sulaiman ibn-Sa'd's transfer. Sulaiman requests one year's Jordan kharaj as assistance; the narrative says he presented the registers after that year, then describes Sarjun's sadness at the change {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301}. Sarjun is not the translator in this account: confusing executor and objector reverses their roles. The passage gives180000dinars for Jordan's tax assigned as assistance, not each clerk's wage table or an independently audited project account. Annual revenue, individual wage and final implementation cost are different things even when discussed within one story.

The Syrian passage connects the decision with a clerk-and-inkstand anecdote. It is an attributed explanation, not an experiment establishing one incident as the sufficient cause of broad regional reform {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301}. The separate Iraqi narrative, transmitted from al-Mada'ini and his teachers, makes Salih ibn Abd al-Rahman Zadan Farrukh's assistant. Salih proposes a partial trial translation, then receives al-Hajjaj's commission after Zadan's death during Ibn al-Ash'ath's unrest {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.465–466}. These routes are not merged into one meeting or one clerk. Their narrative sequence is not converted into a precise year absent from the passage.

## The maker(s)
**Explicit Sunni frame:** Suyuti transmits Abd al-Malik's Medinan worship and legal learning before accession; Yildiz describes his hadith and jurisprudential studies. This is named personal evidence, not inference from name, family or office {cite:tarikh-khulafa-minhaj-1434 p.356} {cite:tdv-abdulmelik-yildiz}. The Sunni frame is historical presentation, not membership in a later denominational institution or endorsement of every policy.

**Division of work:** Abd al-Malik commissions the Syrian reform; Sulaiman translates in Hitti, while Salih, al-Hajjaj and Zadan belong to a distinct Iraqi account {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.465–466}. TDV renders Sulaiman's name differently and mentions achievement within a year; this does not create another person or justify guessing anyone's religion {cite:tdv-abdulmelik-yildiz}. Language learning is not evidence of belief. Being associated with a clerk does not mean personally translating every register.

## How it works
**Knowing the reference:** The Iraqi narrative starts from Zadan's accounting knowledge, Salih's Arabic/Persian knowledge and a proposal to translate part as a trial {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.465}. In a modern educational explanation, transfer requires knowing names, amounts and relationships between fields. Changing letters without those relationships can leave readable forms with different meanings. This explains the problem, not a surviving Umayyad table or complete checking protocol.

**Terminology:** Printed466contains a dialogue about fractional equivalents and a repetition expression, with Hitti's explanatory footnotes {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.466}. Narrator, transmitted dialogue and translator's comment are separated. A gloss printed1916is not an81AHfinancial decree; Persian terms are not assigned meanings from superficial verbal resemblance. Accounts are more than vocabulary lists: a phrase may express a proportion or refer to a preceding entry, requiring different kinds of understanding.

**Modern example:** In a teaching table containing200units, a tenth is20and half a tenth10, whether headings are Arabic or another language. This is illustrative arithmetic for the narrative's fractional-equivalence problem, not historical tax quantities or a judgement about proper rates {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.466}. Preserving a result is insufficient: the entry's name, unit and purpose must remain clear. A correct heading can conceal a changed unit. Translation fidelity, arithmetical accuracy and truth of the recorded event are separate questions.

## The spread
Duri gives78AH/697for Iraq,81AH/700for Syria,87AH/706for Egypt and124AH/741–42for Khurasan, alongside persistent local languages into the second century {cite:tdv-divan-duri Arabicization}. These are regional stages, not opening dates for every office or completion dates for every copy. Our0700and0081come from his supplied Syrian year pair, not a new conversion or invented foundation day. Yildiz describes Egyptian adoption under a later administration; every later step is not credited to Abd al-Malik's own hand {cite:tdv-abdulmelik-yildiz}. Central policy may be shared while implementation conditions and staff knowledge differ.

## What it was built on
The work depended on older registers and their operators' expertise. The Iraqi narrative highlights the assistant's dependence on Zadan for introduction to the governor before roles changed {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.465}. Duri situates reform within an inherited administrative apparatus {cite:tdv-divan-duri}. Acknowledging precedents does not diminish translation work; naming the commissioner does not replace naming the translator. A language change does not establish that every accounting rule changed or every old register was destroyed. Material, language, people and function are distinct layers; their unknown details are not filled from a uniform picture of “invention.”

## What was built on it
Duri describes Abbasid development of the inherited diwan system under the vizierate, not direct transmission of every procedure from Syrian reform {cite:tdv-divan-duri}. Arabic is a working language here; the [[invention/muawiya-seal-copy-registry|seal-and-copy bureau]] concerns checking and reference copies, while the [[invention/abd-al-malik-epigraphic-dinar|epigraphic dinar]] concerns another design/monetary standard. Links clarify state functions without turning one invention into multiple names. Sharing a ruler or nearby date does not establish technical descent. A relationship needs evidence explaining what transferred and how, rather than similar lettering or slogans.

## Impact on daily life
Printed301places Sarjun's sorrow and Greek clerks' employment anxiety beside the new registers;466reports an inducement offered to Salih to abandon translation and his refusal {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.466}. These are voices about competing expertise and job interests, not independent unemployment statistics. Duri says some dhimmi staff lost positions, others remained, and mawali continued serving {cite:tdv-divan-duri Arabicization}. That variation is not erased by “everyone was replaced”; writing language does not establish personal religion. Changing who can read state accounts differs from changing how many people pay or how much income they have.

## How it changed history
Yildiz presents Arabic adoption as a major cultural achievement of Abd al-Malik; Duri places it alongside diwan, coin and tiraz reorganization {cite:tdv-abdulmelik-yildiz} {cite:tdv-divan-duri Arabicization}. These are attributed historical assessments, not a museum claim that language alone explains state success or just government. The maker's existing accounts of wars, power and policy remain preserved with their voices. Fairness to administrative skill requires neither erasing human costs nor compressing them into one verdict. State and population languages also differ: an office decision does not supply a complete map of speech in homes and markets.

## Who invented it?
**Historical transmission within the Sunni frame:** Baladhuri attributes Syrian commissioning to Abd al-Malik and translation to Sulaiman, alongside separate Iraqi names and a different narrative path. The museum does not claim independent authentication of every original chain or a contemporary witness to the inkstand episode {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.465–466}.

**Academic reading:** Duri distinguishes regional stages; the Iraqi narrative links Zadan's death to Ibn al-Ash'ath. His78AHsynthesis and that literary sequence are not forced into one documented decree date {cite:tdv-divan-duri Arabicization} {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.465–466}. The narrow attribution is commissioning a working-language reform implemented by translators within existing administration. Arabic and the register themselves were not one person's inventions. Different witness types remain visible without a final verdict eliminating them.

## In culture
Office language opens access to status and employment, as the Sarjun and Salih accounts suggest; it is not a measure of another language's or people's worth {cite:baladhuri-hitti-1916 p.301,p.466}. The papyrus contains two scripts on one material, not a contest whose exhibition must name a winner {cite:commons-perf-558-recto}. The museum describes changing writing authority and reading opportunities, not Greek or Persian as natural obstacles. Arabic writing is not identical with all Arab culture, Arabic readers with all Arabs, or state employees with believers in its religion. Function, language, background and identity must not be collapsed together.
