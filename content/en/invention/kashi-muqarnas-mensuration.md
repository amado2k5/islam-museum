## Summary

How can a ceiling assembled from hundreds of little faces become an arithmetic problem? In the [[work/miftah-al-hisab-kashi|Key to Arithmetic]],[[person/al-kashi|al-Kashi]] organized measurement of [[concept/muqarnas|muqarnas]],the tiered cellular construction articulating and decorating ceilings. He selects a module,counts elements by shape,and adds the surfaces of facets,roofs and connecting pieces. This is a calculation method within an older architectural practice,not an attribution of muqarnas itself to one inventor {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp193-194-and202-205}.

Yvonne Dold-Samplonius explains the procedure and its average coefficient,but also identifies a projection inconsistency and values whose derivation she does not know. Many printed digits therefore do not promise absolute accuracy for every curved surface. The reported1427/830AHdates completion of the book,not the first building measured with it or the invention of every muqarnas vault {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp196-and209-221} {cite:kashi-mactutor Key}.

## What it is

In the definition discussed by the study,a muqarnas is a staircase-like ceiling:a cell consists of two facets and a roof,and cells with bases in one horizontal plane form a tier. Surfaces may be flat or curved. Some visible pieces belong to cells;others fill gaps between them. Counting hollows in a photograph is not enough to determine their exposed surface area {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 p202}.

The contribution is mensuration in BookIVof an arithmetic text,not a plaster object,tile or observing instrument. The study distinguishes simple,clay-plastered,curved and Shirazi muqarnas. Element inventories and measurement procedures change with type. A convenient method does not make every real ceiling identical in geometry or curvature coefficient {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp209-220}.

## How it works

Calculation starts with a module,a standard length associated with a facet base and assigned the value one. Lengths and areas are first expressed through it,then converted to cubits or another unit. If one cubit equalsbmodules,numerical area in module-squared units is divided byb²to obtain square cubits. Dividing bybonly would confuse length conversion with area conversion {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 p209}.

For plane surfaces,facet bases are summed and multiplied by height;roof-element areas are added,and then tiers are combined. Figure8gives approximately0.707107for a45-degree rhombus,0.353553for its half,0.414214for an almond and0.292893for its biped complement. These are areas in squared-module units,not centimetres or volumes of cavities {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp210-213}.

An independent modern explanation gives a unit-sided45-degree rhombus area√2/2and half-rhombus√2/4. The illustrated almond and complement have areas√2−1and1−√2/2,summing to√2/2. Checking these plane identities corroborates the rounded planar values,not the exact area of every curved element. Doubling every length quadruples area:scale and units are part of the mathematical answer {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 fig8-p213}.

For curved muqarnas,cell bases are summed and multiplied by a coefficient;intermediate-element areas are then added. The historian explains the coefficient through a straight segment plus half a curved intersection line in a construction panel,using an average that unifies calculation. It is not a magical constant valid for any chosen curvature. She explicitly does not know how some intermediate-element values were obtained,and her reconstructions yield different numbers {cite:dold-samplonius-muqarnas-1992 pp214-221}.
