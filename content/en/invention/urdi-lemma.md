## Summary
A geometrical theorem in Mu'ayyad al-Din al-Urdi's Kitab al-Haya (d. c. 1266) that extends Apollonius' theorem: it replaces Ptolemy's equant with an epicycle turning about a centre at half the distance to the equant point, doing the equant's work without breaking uniform motion about the centre. It is the best-known fruit of the Maragha school's reform {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en} {cite:schmidl-urdi-bea-2007}.

## What it is
A theorem of theoretical astronomy: take an epicycle rotating about a centre at half the distance between the deferent's centre and the equant point, and it produces the same apparent planetary motion the equant imposed {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## The world before it
Ptolemy's model made the planet move uniformly not about the earth's centre but about a separate equant point — a breach of the physical principle Ibn al-Haytham attacked in the Doubts, which sent astronomers looking for a replacement {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## The story
Al-Urdi wrote the Kitab al-Haya in the setting of the Maragha observatory (founded 1259) that he had helped build and equip; Saliba argued in 1979 from Bodleian MS Marsh 621 that his non-Ptolemaic contributions preceded even al-Tusi's {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en} {cite:schmidl-urdi-bea-2007} {cite:saliba-islamic-science-2007}.

## The maker(s)
Mu'ayyad al-Din al-Urdi: Damascus engineer, geometry teacher and instrument maker, called to Maragha by al-Tusi — the treatise that carries his lemma {cite:schmidl-urdi-bea-2007}.

## How it works
Instead of measuring the planet's speed from an equant point off the centre, the lemma sets a small epicycle on the deferent rotating about the halfway centre; geometrically it yields the same speeding and slowing by legitimate uniform motion {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## The spread
Bar Hebraeus and Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi carried it in their works, and Ibn al-Shatir quoted it at Damascus — the channel by which Neugebauer (1957) proposed it reached Europe {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## What it was built on
Apollonius' theorem exchanging eccentric and epicyclic models — a Greek tool extended to solve an Islamic problem — and the Almagest-critique tradition brought to a head by Ibn al-Haytham's Doubts {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## What was built on it
The Maragha planetary models (the Tusi couple works alongside it), then Ibn al-Shatir's models, and along a long chain Copernicus's equivalent mechanisms {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## Impact on daily life
Not a daily instrument but a theoretical tool: it kept planetary computation faithful to Aristotelian physics — uniform motion about the centre — without sacrificing the tables' accuracy {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## How it changed history
It proved the equant problem solvable by geometry; on it rests the claim that the reform of Ptolemy began in Islam, not in the Renaissance — the argument carried by Neugebauer and Saliba {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en} {cite:saliba-islamic-science-2007}.

## Who invented it?
Mu'ayyad al-Din al-Urdi — it bears his name because the Kitab al-Haya is its documented witness, and Saliba argued his priority over al-Tusi {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en} {cite:saliba-islamic-science-2007}.

## Today
It is read as the clearest exhibit of the Maragha programme; Saliba's 1990 edition of the Kitab al-Haya places it at the reform's heart {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## Surviving examples
Its textual witness is Bodleian MS Marsh 621, which Saliba identified as a copy of the Kitab al-Haya; manuscripts of the Risalat al-Rasd appear in sales and collections {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## In culture
"Urdi", "Tusi" and "Ibn al-Shatir" have become linked names in the Copernicus story — the best-known debate in the history of Islamic astronomy {cite:wikipedia-muayyad-al-din-al-urdi-en}.

## For young readers
The old astronomers said the planet turns evenly around a point that is not the centre — an engineer feels that is wrong. Al-Urdi found a wheel turning around the right centre that gives the same effect, solving a thousand-year-old problem.
