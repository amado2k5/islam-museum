## Summary
The midfa — a metal tube loaded with gunpowder, mounted on a stock and fired by hand through a touch hole — is the first personal firearm in the Islamic textual record: described and drawn in Mamluk manuscripts of 1320-1352, certainly used by 1342, and documented as a metal cannon firing an iron ball by al-Qalqashandi between 1365 and 1376 {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en} {cite:needham-gunpowder-epic-1986}.

## What it is
The simplest metal-barrel firearm: pour powder down the bore, seat a projectile, touch fire to the vent — the tube recoils against its wooden stock and the ball flies. No lock, no trigger: the soldier is the firing mechanism {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## The world before it
Before it, gunpowder in the Mamluk world was a pyrotechnic's art: al-Rammah's furusiyya treatise of around 1280 gives purified saltpetre, fire lances and incendiary compositions — flames on a battlefield, not bullets {cite:partington-greek-fire-gunpowder-1960}.

## The story
A generation after al-Rammah, illustrated Mamluk military manuscripts of c. 1320-1350 show the midfa discharging projectiles from a tube on a stock; by 1342 the sultanate's cannon use is certain, and between 1365 and 1376 al-Qalqashandi describes a metal cannon firing an iron ball — the documentary chain of the weapon entering the army {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## The maker(s)
No single maker: the weapon grew inside the Mamluk military-treatise tradition that al-Rammah's generation codified, and its clearest named witness is the chancery scholar al-Qalqashandi, who wrote down what the arsenals fielded {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en} {cite:partington-greek-fire-gunpowder-1960}.

## How it works
A closed-end tube is fixed to a pole or wooden stock; gunpowder and a projectile are rammed down the bore; a fuse or ember touches the powder through a small vent; the charge burns to gas, the gas expels the shot — the fire lance's fire made into artillery the soldier can carry {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## The spread
From Mamluk Syria and Egypt the weapon appears across the Mediterranean in the same decades: Nasrid troops besieging Elche in 1331 shot iron pellets with fire, Moors used cannon at Algeciras in 1343 — roughly the century Chinese hand cannon had fought at Nayan's rebellion in 1288 {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## What it was built on
The Chinese fire lance — the gunpowder tube on a polearm documented in the Islamic world from al-Rammah's treatise — plus the saltpetre chemistry his book records; where the metal tube itself came from, Mongol transmission or local development, is the contested point {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en} {cite:needham-gunpowder-epic-1986}.

## What was built on it
The siege cannon that battered Constantinople in 1453 are its big descendants; the matchlock arquebus that added a trigger in fifteenth-century Europe is its small one {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## Impact on daily life
For the common soldier it changed the meaning of armament: courage and horsemanship met a weapon any recruit could learn in days — one reason Ayalon and Khan record the Mamluk establishment's coolness toward infantry firearms {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en} {cite:ayalon-gunpowder-firearms-1956}.

## How it changed history
It closed the pyrotechnic era and opened the gunpowder age in the Islamic world; within a hundred years of the midfa's first texts, cannon decided the fate of Constantinople {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## Who invented it?
Contested. Ahmad Y. al-Hassan read fourteenth-century manuscripts as recording hand cannon at Ain Jalut in 1260 — other historians place the first certain Mamluk use in the 1340s and Khan dates the metal cannon's arrival to the 1370s by Mongol transmission {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en} {cite:needham-gunpowder-epic-1986}.

## Today
Museum collections hold the midfa's descendants; the debate over its arrival is a live one in the historiography of gunpowder — the record keeps both dates honestly {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en}.

## Surviving examples
No excavated Mamluk midfa; the survivors are the illustrated Rzevuski manuscript figures of c. 1320-1350 showing the stocked tube among fire tubes and naphtha flasks, and the textual witnesses through al-Qalqashandi {cite:wikipedia-hand-cannon-en} {cite:needham-gunpowder-epic-1986}.

## In culture
In the furusiyya literature the firearm joined a warrior's written culture of horsemanship, archery and engines — a new page in an old book {cite:partington-greek-fire-gunpowder-1960}.

## For young readers
Imagine an iron tube on a stick: fill it with powder, push in a ball, touch a flame to a small hole — and the ball flies out with a bang. That simple shape is where the personal firearm begins in the books of fourteenth-century Mamluk soldiers.
