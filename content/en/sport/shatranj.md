## Overview

Chess is the game of India that entered Muslim lands through Persia and became the Muslims' game and then the world's: a game of kings on an eight-by-eight board, with its own books, contests and famous players in Islamic civilisation {cite:al-furusiyya}.

## In Muslim lands

The Muslims wrote whole books on chess: Abu Bakr al-Suli — the greatest player of the Abbasid age — wrote his famous work, a book is attributed to Ali ibn Abi Talib, and the historians record chess contests in Baghdad and Harun al-Rashid's dealings with the players. Among its champions was Sultan al-Zahir Baybars, who played it and played "courier chess" in war {cite:al-furusiyya}.

## In fiqh and today

The jurists differed on its ruling: the majority allowed it without gambling and without missing a prayer, the Hanafis disliked it, some forbade it — the museum presents their positions without ruling {cite:mawsuat-alfiqhia-alkuwaitiya}. Today chess is a world game with Muslim champions: Sultan Khan of India, and among today's names Alireza Firouzja and others, with federations and champions in every Muslim land.
