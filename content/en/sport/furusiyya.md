## Overview

Furusiyya is the science and art of horsemanship: mounting and dismounting, keeping balance in the field, the charge and the wheel, racing, sword-striking, lance-thrusting and shooting arrows from the saddle — mastered step by step until the horseman holds his seat and handles his weapons together {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. In the Muslim tradition it is more than a sport: it is an educational, military and technical discipline named by the scholars themselves, who wrote treatises on it until it became a science with its own terminology, manuals and schools {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the Qur'an and Sunnah

The horse appears in the Qur'an in the contexts of preparation, strength and beauty: the tethered horses of the verse of preparation, the horses Solomon loved until they absorbed his attention, and the standing chargers of al-Adiyat {cite:tanzil-quran}. In the Sunnah there are many reports: the good tied to the forelocks of horses, the reward for the one whose mount stumbles, the races the Prophet held and the prize in the hoof — «the horses are three: for one man a reward, against another a burden, and for another a covering» {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the furusiyya books

The people of knowledge and of horses wrote whole manuals: works on the rulings and etiquette of furusiyya, on teaching the horse and shooting from its back, on veterinary care and equine genealogy, and the Book of Furusiyya and the Military Appointments which gathered the arts of thrusting, striking, shooting and wrestling on horseback {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. The museum's objects tell it too: the Furusiyya Ground of Mamluk Cairo, the field of training and display {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}, and the decorated saddles, bridles and lances of Islamic art collections.

## In history

Furusiyya fields were public places in Islamic cities: the maydan below the Cairo citadel where games and reviews were held, fields in Damascus, Aleppo and the Ottoman cities, and the training continued as a regular tradition under the Mamluks and after them. Its influence reached the world in Western equestrian literature itself, where Arab and Islamic techniques are acknowledged. It lives today: Arabian horse racing, heritage furusiyya, and riding clubs teaching the young what their forebears taught.
