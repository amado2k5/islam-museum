## Overview

Ishalat al-hajar — lifting the great stone and raising it overhead — is among the oldest strength games of Muslim lands, especially under the Mamluks: the lifter takes the heavy stone to his shoulder, raises it above his head and casts it down, the lift a proof of strength {cite:canon-ibn-sina-print-v1}.

## In history

The historians record that ishala was among the games held in the fields of Mamluk Egypt and Syria: great stones of marble or rock kept in the maydan or at a man's door, and the strong man would lift it from the ground, bear it on his shoulder, then raise it until it stood above his head. The strong earned fame by it — Ibn Iyas and others name the strongmen of Cairo and Alexandria known for the lift.

## Today

Ishala is kin to modern weightlifting and to strongman sport: the atlas stones of strength contests are the very lifting of the stone under a new name. In Muslim lands popular strength sports survive from Azerbaijan to Morocco.
