## Overview

Horse-racing is the threefold contest of sabiq, muhallil and muzammil: the Prophet said «there is no prize except in khuff, hafir or nasl» — no lawful wager except in the camel's hoof, the horse's hoof or the arrowhead {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 229}.

## In the Sunnah

The Prophet held two races in the authentic reports: a race of horses that had been trained down (mudammar) from al-Hafya' to Thaniyyat al-Wada' and from Thaniyyat al-Wada' to the mosque of Banu Zurayq — Ibn Umar among those who raced — and a race of strong untrained horses from Thaniyyat al-Wada' to the mosque of Banu Zurayq, which Ibn Umar also won {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. The scholars named the two: the trained-down horse prepared days before the race, and the strong horse raced as it was.

## In fiqh

On this the jurists built the chapter of contests: the three of the prize — khuff for camels, hafir for horses, nasl for arrows — the muhallil who enters third between the racers taking the prize if he wins, and the muzammil, the finishing post itself {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 229}. Horse-racing stayed in Muslim lands: the fields of Iraq and the Gulf, the festivals of the Arabs and the Ottomans, and it became worldwide in modern equestrian racing.
