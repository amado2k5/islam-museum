## Overview

Spear-play — the display of handled lances — is among the famous martial displays of the seerah: the Abyssinians playing with their spears in the mosque on the day of the festival, the Prophet watching them and Aisha watching over his shoulder {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}.

## In the Prophet's Mosque

In the famous report of Aisha: on the day of Bu'ath — the day the Aws and Khazraj had fought before Islam — the Abyssinians came playing with their spears in the mosque; the Prophet called Aisha (or she asked him) and she watched over his shoulder, he shielding her with his cloak until she herself tired of the sight, then he sent her in {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}. In another narration Umar said: let me pelt them with stones, Messenger of God — and he said: leave them, Umar, they are safe {cite:sunan-abi-dawud}. Two reports joining the martial display — the whirling spears — with the mosque that housed it, the tenderness of a husband's courtesy and the Prophet's mercy toward them.

## In fiqh

The jurists built on it the permissibility of spear-play in the mosque: weapon-play for display is allowed where no harm and no sin is involved, and they counted it among the proofs that the mosque may carry social purposes on the festival {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3}. Weapon-displays — thrusting, striking and spear-play — were festival games in the cities of the Mamluks and the Ottomans.
