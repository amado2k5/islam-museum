## Overview

Hunting as sport — not for provision — is among the oldest sports known to man, and Islam gave it manners and limits: hunting for sport and display, hunting for food, and hunting forbidden in ihram or in excess {cite:quran}.

## In the Qur'an and Sunnah

The Qur'an gives the rulings of hunting in ihram: «lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food», «forbidden to you is the game of the land while you are in ihram», and the predators that may be killed {cite:quran}. In the Sunnah, reports on the trained hunting animals: «when you send your trained dog, mention the name of God» {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}, and reports on the falcon, the hawk and the taught dog — the training of raptors for the hunt.

## In history

Hunting was the sport of kings and amirs in Muslim lands: the falcons of the Abbasids a famous pursuit, saluki-coursing the sport of the desert, and the Mamluk and Ottoman sultans had their falcon houses and great hunting progresses. The people of Arabia remain falconers today, and the camel, falcon and saluki festivals of the Gulf are known gatherings.
