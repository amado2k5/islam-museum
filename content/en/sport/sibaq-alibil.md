## Overview

Camel-racing is among the contests in which the hadith made the prize lawful: «no prize except in khuff, hafir or nasl» — khuff being the camel's hoof, placing the camel race among the three {cite:usul-iqtisad-islami-0660 p. 229}.

## In the Sunnah and the language

To the people of Arabia the camel was the pillar of livelihood, and the hajin — the swift racing camels — a pride of their owners. Scores of reports speak of the hajin and the budn; the word khuff placed camel-racing among the three lawful wagers. The Qur'an names the camel in the verses of bounty — «and upon their backs and upon ships you are carried» — carrying in town and on journey, and racing in the sands {cite:tanzil-quran}.

## In history

Camel-racing has continued in Arabia and the Gulf to this day: the tracks of Saudi Arabia, the Emirates, Qatar and Oman hold the great camel festivals — beauty contests and races — and it is perhaps the oldest organised race of the Arab world, run today in some countries with robot jockeys in place of child riders.
