## Summary

The historian as an Ottoman state office bore the title vak'anüvis — from Arabic vak'a, event, and Persian nüvis, writer — the official charged with writing the events of his own age {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. The compound had earlier been used as an epithet for literary figures such as Talikizade Mehmed Subhi, İbrahim Mülhemi and the münşi Nergisi, before it became a permanent divan service in the early eighteenth century {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}.

## From şehname-writing to state service

Two accounts of the office's origin circulate: the more widespread holds it a continuation of the şehname-writing that had become a steady state service from Süleyman's reign, while the other traces it to Bayezid II's commissioning of Ottoman histories from İdris-i Bitlisi and Kemalpaşazade, with Abdurrahman Abdi — who wrote his vakayi'name on oral order — counted the first vak'anüvis {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. The entry distinguishes such occasional commissions from the standing post: the actual institutionalisation came under Grand Vizier Amcazade Hüseyin Pasha, who set Naima (d. 1716) to write up and order Şârihülmenârzâde Ahmed Efendi's historical drafts, granting him a purse of gift-money, a daily salary of 120 akçe and a berat {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. From Raşid Mehmed Efendi (d. 1735) the post ran as a continuous civil office {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}.

## Who was appointed

The office belonged to the Divan-i Hümayun's bureaux, so appointees were mostly men known for inşa composition and poetry, though ilmiye men were also named — and both kinds left their mark on their works {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. The qualities sought were 'skill and learning, comprehension, continuous composition and occupation with the spread of the sciences'; Vasif Ahmed Efendi, Mehmed Pertev and Ömer Amir are recorded as appointed for these merits, and when Mahmud II hesitated over the şeyhülislam's candidates, the 'capable and suitable' Sahaflar Şeyhizade Esad Efendi got the post {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. In his 1795 memorandum Vak'anüvis Halil Nuri added uprightness, experience and the keeping of secrets to the list {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}.

## The working method

Vak'anüvises wrote not only their own times but were charged with completing the periods their predecessors had left unfinished; on dismissal or death the predecessor's notes, drafts and documents were passed to the new holder, and Pertev and Şanizade even asked for their predecessors' papers to fix where to begin {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. Halil Nuri's 1795 report and the buyruldu it prompted set the reporting channels: matters fit for the vakayi'name were to be communicated to the vak'anüvis by the sadaret mektupçusu, beylikçi and âmedci with the reisülküttab's leave; appointments and dismissals came from the tahvil and ruus offices, ceremonies from the teşrifat bureau, by ilmühaber minutes — and the historians were expected to verify the events {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. In practice some state secrets were withheld in the later eighteenth century, which Halil Nuri blamed for shallow coverage, until Selim III ordered Edib Mehmed Emin to write events soundly and openly, without flattery and without hiding 'state secrets', and arranged monthly briefings on European affairs {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}.

## Pay and rank

Holders drawn from the divan bureaux or the ilmiye kept their primary posts and drew an extra allowance: the müderris Çelebizade Asım was granted the Taşköprü kaza as arpalık, expedition vak'anüvises like Enveri and Mütercim Asım were salaried, and Vasif Ahmed is said to have pushed his annual vak'anüvis income above 20,000 kuruş with various grants {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. Each year's fascicle was handed to the sadaret for presentation to the sultan, then corrected and completed as needed; Selim III and Mahmud II were the sovereigns most engaged with what their historians wrote {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}. When the vak'anüvis campaigned with the army, his stand-in at the capital was titled the 'rikâb vak'anüvisi' {cite:tdv-vakanuvis}.
