## Summary

The shamma' made the candles of wax or tallow by which the city's mosques, houses and shops were lit {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 17, p. 67} {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 145-146, pp. 143-144}. The hisba manuals treat wax among the apothecaries' adulterated goods {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 17, p. 67}, Lane's Cairo druggists sold wax candles beside their drugs {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XIV, p. 318}, and Evliya Çelebi describes an imperial wax-factory in Istanbul whose every candle had to bear the inspector's seal {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 147, p. 144}.

## The adulterated candle

Al-Shayzari warns that wax is adulterated with goat's tallow and with rosin, and that some makers sprinkle bean flour, soft sand or ground kohl into the wax while it is being shaped, then pack it inside the candle and cover it with pure wax; the way to discover the fraud is to light the candle and look at the inside {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 17, p. 67}.

## Wax and tallow at Istanbul

Evliya divides the trade in two. The tallow-chandlers numbered five thousand five hundred and one men with fifty shops, classed as assistants of the butchers because they stand in need of the tallow and grease of cattle, with Ins Ben Malek, buried at Kerbela, for their patron {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 145, pp. 143-144}. The wax-chandlers kept a single imperial establishment inside the Odun gate, governed by an agha and an inspector, whose hundred men made all the wax candles for the Sultan, the vezirs and the great men of Constantinople {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 146, p. 144}.

## The inspector's seal

Every candle manufactured at the imperial fabric had to be marked with the seal of the inspector of the wax-establishment, the Shemi-khanah-nazir, and anyone who cheated by filling the interior with tallow or tar was severely punished {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 147, p. 144}. A hundred wax-merchants in fifty-five shops, all Muslims in Evliya's count, accompanied their chiefs in the procession, their litters dressed with figures in wax {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 147, p. 144}.

## The chandlers at the procession

The private chandlers marched on wagons with ornamented candles in their hands, in lanterns and on poles, and lit torches and candles burning in purple, green and yellow flames — some of which, burning down, set off fireworks and rockets, Evliya says, to the great terror of the spectators {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 146, p. 144}. A separate corps of seventy-five chandlers of the At-maidan served the Janissaries, selling them three candles for a para with the loss made up from the imperial treasury under Sultan Suleiman's constitution {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 154, p. 145}.

## The Cairo candle-seller

In Lane's Cairo no separate candle-maker's shop appears; the druggist and perfumer, the attar, sold wax candles beside his other goods {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XIV, p. 318}.

## Today

The shamma's craft is transformed: electric light ended the candle's nightly work, and what survives is a smaller trade in ceremonial and decorative candles, far from the imperial factory and its seal {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 145-147, pp. 143-144}.

## Not yet covered

The passages read cover wax's frauds and the Ottoman chandlers' numbers and seals, but not the making itself — wicks, moulds, dipping — nor any named candle-maker or prices beyond the Janissaries' three-for-a-para; the craft between the hisba manual and the Ottoman city remains uncovered {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 17, p. 67}.
