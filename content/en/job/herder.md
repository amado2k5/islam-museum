## Summary

The herder pastures the flocks — sheep and goats, oxen and buffaloes — and in Ottoman Istanbul he marched as a guildsman: Evliya counts the Shepherds (Choban) nine hundred men in the great procession {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}. Beside the herders themselves walked the keepers of the milk: a thousand buffalo-milk sellers with two hundred shops, and eight hundred sellers of sheep's milk {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}.

## The guild and its patrons

Evliya gives the shepherds a double patronage, as he does for every guild: their first patron was Moses, and in the Prophet's time A'n ben Shadck, girded by Selman Pak and buried in Yemen {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}. The buffalo milkmen name Sofian Thun, girded by Selman and buried in the upper town of Mecca; the sellers of sheep's milk name Jethro, and in the Prophet's time Abder Wornid Lebani, girded by Hamza and buried at the foot of Mount Uhud {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}.

## In the procession

The whole company of the animal trades — flaying butchers, mandrajis, chiftlikjis, ekrekjis, sayajis, aghiljis, tokatjis and sütjis — marched dressed as shepherds, dragging sheep, goats, oxen, buffaloes and the great rams of Jirjeh in Egypt that carry five or six horns and stand the size of a pony {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}. The shepherds passed firing muskets, playing on the horn and the pipe called kawal, laughing and jesting, with halberds, pistols, slings and iron-pointed sticks in their hands {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}.

## The shepherd's dog

The herder's partner was his dog: Evliya describes dogs the size of asses and fierce as lions, brought from the shores of Africa, named Palo, Matshko, Alabash, Salbash, Karaman, Sarhan and others, wearing rich cloths, silver collars and rings of iron points about the neck {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}. They assail not only the wolves that enter stables and folds but would attack dragons and rush into fire, chase the eagle in the air and the crocodile in the rivers; the shepherds breed them with great care for purity and say they descend from the shepherd's dog that went into the cave with the Seven Sleepers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}. A leap from such a dog cost one sheep, a true-bred samsun dog five hundred sheep, and the best sold for five or six hundred piastres; the shepherds count the dogs companions and brethren and eat out of the same dish with them {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}.

## The nomad's measure

Ibn Khaldun's yardstick, quoted in the camel entry, puts the camel-herding beduin at the far end of pastoral life: those who keep camels alone are "more beduin" than the nomads who also raise sheep and cattle {cite:tdv-deve}. The Arabs called town-dwellers who worked the land ehl-i hadar, and the desert people ehl-i veber, after the camel-hair cloth of their tents {cite:tdv-deve}.
