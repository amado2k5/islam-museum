## Summary

The ibraji made sewing-needles and sack-needles — a small ware with a full hisba chapter to itself {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, pp. 89-90}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa polices the metal and the tempering; Evliya Çelebi counts the Istanbul needle-makers at two hundred men in fifty-five shops {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, pp. 89-90} {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 291, pp. 186-187}.

## Steel or soft iron

A trusty man of their own craft shall be appointed over the needle-makers, Ibn al-Ukhuwwa writes; he shall prevent them from mixing steel needles with those of soft iron, for when these are sharpened they may be mixed with those of Damascus steel {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, p. 89}. Each kind must be kept distinct, and the makers must be sworn to this {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, p. 89}.

## The tailoring needle

The best needles are the 'tailoring' needles called by them al-Musawwada; these are sharpened three times and polished, and the best have round eyes {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, p. 90}. The makers must be compelled to heat the needles and temper them, for steel when heated and tempered hardens while other metal in this process becomes softer {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, p. 90}. Sack-needles may be made either of steel or of soft iron; they are of different kinds named according to the number contained in a ratl {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, p. 90}.

## The needle-makers' cry

In the procession, the needle-makers (Ignehjian) were two hundred men with fifty-five shops; their patron Abul Kassem Alatar's tomb is not known {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 291, pp. 186-187}. They adorned their shops with different kinds of needles and pins — the ibreh, igneh, suzen and sundok — and passed crying 'Needles, needles, to sew what is torn' {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 291, p. 187}.

## The heddeh-smiths

Beside them marched the heddeh-smiths: the heddeh is a steel-plate with some hundred holes in it, through which gold and silversmiths pass different sorts of gold and silver wire {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 292, p. 187}. Some say the patron of these steel-plate smiths was the gold-beater Kanewi, Evliya notes, but they are mistaken — Kanewi lived in the time of the Seljukides; the true patron was Sa'd-ud-din the Egyptian, who came into the Prophet's presence, was girded by Selman, and whose tomb is at Belbeis {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 292, p. 187}.

## Wire by the wheel

The wire-makers (Teljian), two hundred men with forty shops, drew their light from Selman Pak and passed in the procession drawing iron and brass wires by means of wheels {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 294, pp. 186-187}.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the needle's grades, metals and guild, but not the drawing, eye-piercing and grinding of the workshop, the prices of needles, or the needle trade of Persia and al-Andalus {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LVII, pp. 89-90; evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 291, p. 187}.
