## Summary

The muaththir — commonly plural muaththirun, 'influencers' — builds an audience on social platforms and steers its opinion, taste, buying and devotion through regular content; the newest link in the public-speech chain that began with crier and preacher, then secretary, journalist and broadcaster — except it has no professional gate {cite:tdv-matbuat}.

## Without a gate

Every trade in the chain had a door: the muezzin is appointed, the khatib chosen, the katib rises through the chancery, the journalist enters the paper, the announcer is accepted by the section {cite:tdv-matbuat}. The influencer has no door: an account on YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat or X, measured by followers, views and shares, the platform's algorithm standing in for the berat and the appointment.

## Its kinds in the Islamic space

The da'wah influencer carrying his lessons and exhortations to a digital audience; the clip preacher condensing counsel into minutes; the teaching creator explaining lesson and skill; the podcast host; the educational, charitable and social maker — all speaking their followers' tongue {cite:tdv-matbuat}.

## Work and economy

The influencer produces regular content — video, post and podcast — measured by reach, not salary: paid not by waqf or chancery but by platform advertising, brand sponsorships, subscriptions and followers {cite:tdv-matbuat}. As he grows, agencies manage his account, schedule and contracts — a small media industry around one person.

## The trade's questions

The influencers raise the old and new questions of the press: hidden advertising, honesty in reporting, responsibility toward the audience, and speaking on matters without learning — all of which the preacher's and journalist's professions had to answer before {cite:tdv-matbuat}. And some influencers of the Islamic space train for the pulpits of yesterday, returning to the root.
