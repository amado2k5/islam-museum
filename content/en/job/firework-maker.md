## Summary

The fishekji made the rockets and set-pieces of the dynasty's rejoicings — princes' births, circumcision feasts, victory celebrations — and marched in the procession among the armourers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. A small guild of a hundred men in eighteen shops, they kept a manufactory outside the Suleimanieh hospital and shops across the suburbs {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. In Cairo, Lane still found the display of fire-works part of the ritual of cutting the canal dam {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXVI, p. 447}.

## The guild and its works

Evliya's entry is one of the fullest of the small guilds. The firework-makers (Fishekjian) are one hundred men with eighteen shops; their patron "is Jewish", and the greatest part of them belong to the armourers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. They exhibit their skill on the occasion of public rejoicings, on the birth of princes, and on the feast of circumcision {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. Their manufactory stands outside the hospital of the Suleimanieh, where they keep the repository of their tools and their magazine — the powder had to be housed outside the walls — and they also keep shops at Eyyub, Galata, Tup-khanah and Beshiktash {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}.

## The hundred thousand rockets

For the rejoicings of victories, the firework-makers "set on fire some hundred thousand rockets of different colours" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. Some mount straight into the sky; some go in an oblique direction, spreading stars around them; and some, divided into three parts, ascend at three different times — "when the first, having burned out, falls to the ground, the second is lighted, and then the third, which at last explodes with a noise that seems to rend the clouds" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. It is the clearest early description in the corpus of a staged, multi-effect display: a three-stage rocket conceived as a sequence, not a single burst.

## Avicenna as patron

The guild claims the philosopher as its own: "the first artist in fireworks was Avicenna (Abu Sina), who is the master and patron of all firework-makers" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}. Like the other patrons of the procession, it is the guild's tradition, not a documented succession — a learned patron for a craft that mixed chemistry and celebration. Evliya adds a pious speculation that the Cherubim, hearing the dreadful report of the rockets, recite the verse asking God to guard from evil {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 208, p. 158}.

## Fireworks in Cairo

Two centuries later the display still opened the ceremonial year. At the cutting of the Cairo canal dam, "the firing of guns, and the display of the fire-works, continue; and towards the close of the operation, the best of the fire-works are exhibited, when in the glaring sunshine they can hardly be seen" {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXVI, p. 447}. The craft that marched under the armourers in Istanbul served, in Egypt, the calendar of the Nile.
