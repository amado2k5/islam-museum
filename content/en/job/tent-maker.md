## Summary

The khayyami made tents and pavilions — the houses that folded and moved with armies, pilgrim caravans and sovereigns {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 370}. Evliya gives the craft a primordial inventor and a Companion-age patron, and Istanbul kept a whole quarter named for the tent-pitchers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 370-371}.

## Jemshid's first tent

The tent-makers (Khaimejian or Chadirjian) derive their handicraft from Jemshid, who made the first tent {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 370, p. 201}. In the Prophet's time their patron was Nassir ibn Abdallah al-Makki the tent-maker, who was girded by Selman and made the Prophet's tent of ihram — the coarse cloth of the pilgrim's dress {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 370, p. 201}.

## The procession of tents

In the procession the tent-makers dress fine tents on litters, and fine boys are seen working inside them, playing tricks with each other, some busy setting up awnings and mosquito-nets — the sayeban and namusie {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 370, p. 201}.

## The rope-makers

Beside them march the tent-rope-makers (Tanabjian), a hundred men with forty shops, who pass adorning their tents with all kinds of tent-ropes made of cotton in different colours {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 371, p. 202}.

## The quarter of the tent-pitchers

Istanbul's Mohalle Otakjian takes its name, like the artillerymen's quarter of Topjian beside it, from the circumstance that the tent-pitchers of the army had their station there when war was about to be made against Rumeli {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer the Otakjian quarter}.

## Cloth of the pilgrim

That the Prophet's own tent was pitched of ihram cloth — the plain, unsewn-textile register of the pilgrim's dress — is the guild's founding boast: the craft's finest client needed its coarsest cloth {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 370, p. 201}.

## Not yet covered

Evliya names the patrons, the litter-borne display and the rope-makers, but not the felting, weaving and cutting behind the tent-cloth, the tent's parts and sizes, or the army tent-pitchers' daily work between the wars {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 370-371}.
