## Summary

The social scientist — the sociologist as the modern university knows the calling — entered the Islamic world through the university chair, not through the madrasa or the guild {cite:tdv-ziya-gokalp} {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}. Two TDV entries mark the founders of the discipline in Turkey and in the Arab world: Ziya Gökalp, who gave Turkey's first sociology lessons at Salonica in 1911 and taught at the Istanbul Darülfünun from 1913 to 1919 {cite:tdv-ziya-gokalp}, and Ali Abdülvâhid Vâfî, counted the founder of sociology in Egypt and the Arab world, who carried the discipline from the Sorbonne into Dar al-Ulum, al-Azhar and Cairo University {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}.

## Gökalp: the first lessons

Gökalp's formation was not a sociologist's schooling — the entry records his uncle's lessons in Arabic, Persian, Sufism and kalam, the veterinary school, arrest and imprisonment, and the reading of Ghazali, Farabi, Ibn Rushd, Ibn Arabi and Rumi beside Western works {cite:tdv-ziya-gokalp}. He gave Turkey's first sociology lessons at Salonica in 1911, entered parliament in 1912, and taught at the Darülfünun between 1913 and 1919, before arrest, the Malta exile and the Telif ve Tercüme presidency of 1923; he died in an Istanbul hospital on 25 October 1924 {cite:tdv-ziya-gokalp}.

## Vâfî: the Arab world's founder

Vâfî was born at Umm Durman in Sudan in 1901, where his father taught at Gordon College, and began his studies at the Azhar schools after the family moved to Egypt, memorising the Qur'an and the foundational texts {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}. Graduating from Dar al-Ulum in 1925, he went to Paris on a ministry scholarship, took the Sorbonne's philosophy-sociology section in 1928 with four further proficiency certificates, and a doctorate before returning in 1931 {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}. He taught psychology, pedagogy and sociology at Dar al-Ulum for six years and lectured at al-Azhar University, then in 1936 was appointed to the sociology department of Cairo University's faculty of letters, where he chaired the department and Arabised the teaching of the science {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}.

## The career of a chair

The sociologist's career in these records is the academic cursus: department chair, deanship — Vâfî held deanships at Cairo University's education faculty and at Umm Durman's letters and social sciences faculties — learned societies, which he founded for sociology and philosophy in Egypt, and the state prize of 1987 {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}. He held posts at the Imam Muhammad ibn Saud, Constantine and Muhammad V universities, and wrote some fifty works across sociology, language and religion before dying at Cairo on 6 March 1992 {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}.

## Claiming Ibn Khaldun

The new discipline rooted itself in the older tradition: Vâfî's book on the genius of Ibn Khaldun treats the Muqaddima as the founding text of sociology and tells how, taking over the Cairo sociology course in 1936, he resolved to study the Muqaddima sociologically after noting that earlier teachers had neglected it {cite:tdv-vafi-ali-abdulvahid}.
