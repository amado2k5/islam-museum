## Summary

The kapudan pasha — the Italian capitán turned Ottoman title, used for fleet commanders from the mid-fifteenth century — was the head and administrator of the Ottoman sea forces {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. First a sancak beyi of Gelibolu, he rose with Barbarossa to beylerbeyi and pasha in 1534, was styled kapudan paşa from the mid-sixteenth century and kapudân-ı deryâ from the early eighteenth, and lost the title when the Bahriye Nezâreti was founded in 1867 {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}.

## The Gelibolu kapudans

The first kapudan in the organising of the Ottoman fleet was probably Sarıca Pasha, charged by Bayezid I in 1390 with building the dockyard and fleet as sancak beyi of Gelibolu; every kapudan until Barbarossa commanded the fleet in that rank {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. Though formally one sancak beyi among many, the kapudan's origins marked him out: dismissed vezirs — Zağanos, Mesih — and even a dismissed sadrazam, Mahmud Pasha, took the Gelibolu sancak and the fleet {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. Promotion carried the kapudan to the beylerbeyilik of a province — Süleyman's kapudans rose to Karaman, Rum, Şam and Rumeli — and the titles of the day ran 'Gelibolu emîri ve kapudanı' and 'emîr-i deryâ ve kapudan' {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}.

## Barbarossa and the pasha rank

In February 1534 Hayreddin Reis was appointed beylerbeyi of the new Cezâyir-i Bahr-i Sefîd eyalet on a four-million-akçe salary and became kapudan with the pasha title; the office thereafter was called beylerbeyi of Cezayir, 'mîrimîrân-ı deryâ', and later simply kapudan paşa {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. The eyalet's seat moved from Rhodes to Gelibolu and its six sancaks ran Gelibolu, Eğriboz, Karlı-ili, İnebahtı, Rhodes and Midilli by the mid-sixteenth century {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. Sokullu Mehmed and Piyale entered as Gelibolu sancak beyis and rose to the beylerbeyilik on merit; after Piyale's vezirate of 1567 the kapudanlık went in general to vezirs {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}.

## The duty

Lutfi Pasha defined the kapudan's task as the security of sea trade, and advised the post go to old corsair-bred men tried in sea affairs — Kâtib Çelebi adding that even a landsman kapudan must consult the corsairs who know the seas {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. Kılıç Ali Pasha's post-Lepanto berat charged him with guarding the coastal castles and towns and the islands, preventing harm from infidel, harami and levent ships, and tracking the enemy fleet; the navy sailed every year, and when the season closed the kapudan wintered in Istanbul leaving his derya beys on watch {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. Under the 1701 Bahriye Kanunnamesi he was the head of all derya beys, kapudans, reises and naval officials, made all naval appointments, granted the eyalet's timars and zeâmets, held tuğra authority for sea matters, kept the dockyard's building accounts, named the Black Sea flotilla's commander and heard cases wherever he landed {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}.

## Rank and revenue

The kapudan's annual has income ran 550,000 akçe as Gelibolu sancak beyi, 700,000 as Cezayir beylerbeyi and 885,000 akçe in the seventeenth century, plus the Galata mukâtaa and the Aegean islands' maktû tax — 70,000 kuruş in the seventeenth century, 300,000 in the eighteenth — and he owed up to a thousand cebelü retainers on campaign {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. He held his own divanhâne in the dockyard, received the hil'at at the Yalı Köşkü when the fleet sailed and returned, embarked on the paşa baştardası with a reserve galley, and from the early eighteenth century flew three lanterns and three flags on the great başkapudane galleon in war {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}.

## Rebuilding the fleet

After each of the three great naval defeats the kapudan rebuilt: Kılıç Ali after Lepanto in 1571, Cezayirli Gazi Hasan after Çeşme in 1770, Dârendeli İzzet Mehmed after Navarino in 1827; Barbarossa himself reorganised the fleet and the dockyard, and Güzelce Ali Pasha framed the Turkish kadırga type {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}. The title 'kapudan paşa' is first fixed on Sinan Pasha in 1551 and Piyale in 1565, while 'kapudân-ı deryâ' spread from the eighteenth century; both lapsed when the Bahriye Nezâreti replaced the office in 1867 {cite:tdv-kapudan-pasa}.
