## Summary

The adib is the man of letters — the word edîb, derived from the same root as edep (conduct) and edeb (letters), means at once the well-mannered person and the literary man {cite:tdv-adab}. Before the Tanzimat the word edeb covered the field now called edebiyat; the newer term, coined in the Tanzimat period on the pattern of the French-derived science names such as lisaniyat and ruhiyat, was in all likelihood made to answer the French littérature or belles lettres {cite:tdv-edebiyat}. Because the divan literature was almost wholly verse, the older language often let the word şiir stand for the whole field {cite:tdv-edebiyat}.

## A name with two meanings

The ÂDÂB entry fixes the double heritage of the literary man: edep names praiseworthy conduct and the knowledge that produces it, and the same root in Arabic also means literature, so that the edîb is both the refined person and the writer {cite:tdv-adab}. The older museum of the word already carried this sense; the newer word edebiyat gathered to itself, in step with the Western and especially French meanings, the nuances it bears today {cite:tdv-edebiyat}.

## The craft of the word

Literature in the entry's account is the one art that needs no material, tool or place — a wholly mental art whose substance is the word {cite:tdv-edebiyat}. Where the other arts can only hint through symbols open to interpretation, literature can voice everything human, from impressions of the material world to the conscious, the subconscious, the mystical and the metaphysical, plainly or through allegory {cite:tdv-edebiyat}. The adib owes this power to his material, language, which has grown far beyond any other artistic medium — yet literary language develops away from everyday speech toward metaphor and symbol, so that the writer's personal stamp on the language becomes a measure of his art {cite:tdv-edebiyat}.

## Oral and written

Literature's Western counterpart carries the notion of writing, yet the entry notes that oral literatures are recognised too — the epics of ages before writing, and the literary products of every period that never reached the page {cite:tdv-edebiyat}. Unlike the plastic arts, where the work exists as a single object one must confront to see, the literary work reaches every reader in the original form its author gave it {cite:tdv-edebiyat}.

## The sciences around the man of letters

The word edebiyatçı serves, the entry notes, both for the artist who produces literature and for those occupied with its theoretical questions — a breadth the other arts do not share, where the sanatkar stands beside the art historian and the musicologist beside the musician {cite:tdv-edebiyat}. Around the adib's work stand the literary sciences: literary history, the biographies of literary men, textual criticism and the editing of texts, the philosophy, psychology and sociology of literature {cite:tdv-edebiyat}.

## Not yet covered
