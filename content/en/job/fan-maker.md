## Summary

The mirwahi made fans of feathers — ravens', eagles' and peacocks' — in a guild of a hundred and five men and thirty shops in Evliya's Istanbul {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 345, p. 198}. Their patron carried the craft back to the Prophet's own day, and their neighbours the plume-makers dressed the turbans of the empire's colonels {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 346, p. 198}.

## The gift of wings

The fan-makers' patron is Jacob of Taif, who presented the Prophet with a fan of birds' wings {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 345, p. 198}. He was girded and directed by Anas ibn Malik, and his tomb is at Mosul {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 345, p. 198}.

## Feathers in the hand

Passing in the procession, the makers go on working: they make different kinds of plumes and fans of ravens' and eagles' feathers as they walk {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 345, p. 198}. The workshop is the street; the material is the wing.

## The sorguc-makers

Behind them come the makers of the sorguc, the plumes of feathers worn as distinction on the turban — fifty men with forty shops {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 346, p. 198}. Their first patron was Esama; Evliya saw no later one in the statute-books {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 346, p. 198}. They adorn their shops with all sorts of plumes called Surghtish and Chelenk, made of the feathers of herons and cranes, with the sorguc that the colonels of the Janissaries wear and the feathers of their ceremony caps, the kuka {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 346, p. 198}.

## Fans at the feast

The same feather work reaches the cooks' train, where the shops are laid out with precious china plates, tankards, basins, towels, and fans of peacock's feathers, which are moved by servants while the cooks are dressing dinners {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 170, p. 152}.

## Not yet covered

Evliya gives the guild's number, patron and feather kinds, but not the fan's frames and handles, its prices, the fan-maker's season, or the trade outside Istanbul {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 345-346, p. 198}.
