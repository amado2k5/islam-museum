## Summary

The kahhal — the oculist — is the eye-doctor of the Islamic city: he treats films and pterygia on the white of the eye, opens the veins that treat ophthalmia, and compounds the kuhls and collyria that give the craft its name {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, p. 58} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, p. 115}. The hisba manuals make the muhtasib his examiner: only the man who knows the eye's seven layers, three humours and three diseases, and the making of the salves, may practise {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, p. 58} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, p. 115}. In Evliya's Istanbul eighty oculists keep forty shops, and a hundred more merchants sell collyrium {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer vol. 1 pt. 2, secs. 53-54, p. 117}.

## The examination

The muhtasib examines the oculists on the book of Hunayn ibn Ishaq, the "Ten Discourses" — or "Ten Treatises" — on the Eye {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, p. 58} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, p. 115}. Those whom the test finds competent — who know the structure and number of the layers of the eye (seven), the number of the humours (three), the diseases of the eye (three) and their ramifications, and who know further how to compound the salves and mix the various drugs — the muhtasib licenses to undertake the treatment of eyes {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, p. 58} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, p. 115}.

## The oculist's kit

The practitioner must not be negligent with respect to the instruments of his craft: hooks for the removal of the sabal — the film that swells between the veins of the white — and the pterygium, a scour for lifting the rust-like scabs on the inner side of the lids, lancets for bleeding, and the case of kuhl-pencils {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, p. 58} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, pp. 115-116}.

## The cheats of the road

No trust must be put in most of the oculists of the roads, for there is no honesty in them: the muhtasib must prevent their onslaughts on men's eyes with incisions and application of kuhl, for they have no science or experience of the diseases and weaknesses that arise {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, pp. 58-59} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, p. 116}. Some make eye-salves whose basis is starch and gum, and stain them different colours — red with vermilion, green with turmeric and indigo, black with the juice of acacia-pods, yellow with saffron; others make salves of the horned poppy worked into a paste with dissolved gum, and kuhls of burnt myrobalan stones and pepper — all the knaveries in the matter of kuhls cannot be recorded, so the muhtasib makes them swear they will not resort to them if he cannot stop them treating people {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, pp. 58-59} {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, pp. 116-117}.

## Guild and procession

In Evliya's Istanbul the eye-doctors (kohhal) are eighty men established in forty shops, and the merchants of collyrium another hundred, exhibiting in the procession their unguents, collyria and instruments on litters and giving physic to men with diseases of the eye {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer vol. 1 pt. 2, secs. 53-54, p. 117}. Evliya gives the guild a patron girded by Selman in the Prophet's time, buried at Isfahan, whose tomb-dust is reckoned a specific against sore eyes — the kohl of Isfahan still famous among the poets — and an eldest patron, a Jewess of the time of Moses, who exercised the profession for two hundred years; this is Evliya's guild lore, told as he tells it {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer vol. 1 pt. 2, sec. 53, p. 117}. The kohhal-bashı, head of the oculists, marches behind the hekimbaşı with the jerrah-bashı, head of the surgeons, and the assistants of the proto-medicus carrying sticks {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer vol. 1 pt. 2, sec. 59, p. 118}.

## Masters of the craft

The Turkish hospitals kept separate establishments for the cerrah and the kehhâl, the eye-surgeon {cite:tdv-cerrahlik §I}. Ali ibn Isa el-Kehhâl, al-Jurjani and Ibn Zuhr went far in eye surgery; al-Zahrawi's Tasrif set out the surgical treatment of the eye among its instruments and operations {cite:tdv-cerrahlik §I}.

## Today

The kahhal's craft — salves, lancets, the examination on Hunayn's book — has been transformed into the licensed speciality of ophthalmology; the hisba frame of an examined practitioner and a distrusted itinerant is still the frame in which the public places its eye-doctors {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLV, pp. 58-59}.

## Not yet covered

The manuals give the examination, the kit and the frauds, but not the oculist's shop, his fees, his apprenticeship, the cataract-couching that made the kahhal's name famous, nor eye-doctors outside Cairo and Istanbul {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 37, pp. 115-117}.
