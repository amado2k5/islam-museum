## Summary

The button-maker cast the zurr in metal and wrapped it in silk; in Evliya's Istanbul his craft was three guilds — the brass button-makers with their factory under the Suleiman mosque, the tin-button-makers, and the silk-button-makers of the Trebizond work {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 329-330, 369}.

## The factory below the mosque

The button-makers' (Dogmeji) factory stands below the mosque of Sultan Suleiman in the market-place; when Suleiman erected the mosque he first built this factory on account of the great necessity for it, for the moulds of the brass-work of the windows of the Imperial mosques and other moulds are made in this place {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 329, p. 195}. The chief of the founders always resides there, but the shops — more than a thousand — are spread all over the town, and the men are thirteen hundred, because the founders of plate are counted as their fellow-assistants {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 329, p. 195}.

## Karim and Talha

The guild's first patron was Karim the alchemist, who found the traces of musk by means of the brass {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 329, p. 195}. In the Prophet's time their chief was Talha ibn Ubaydillah, who was converted to Islam at Medina and became one of the ten; he made brass plates and dishes, and he is buried at Damascus near Bilal the Abyssinian {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 329, p. 195}.

## Buttons of tin

The tin-button-makers (Kalai Dogmejian) are two hundred men with a hundred shops; their patron is Abu-l-Mevahib al-Ansari, girded by Selman and buried at Jaballa in Yemen {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 330, p. 195}. They pass working in their shops tin buttons and tin buckles {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 330, pp. 195-196}.

## Buttons of silk

The silk button-makers (Bogmejian-i Harir) are six hundred men with fifty shops; they adorn their shops with silk buttons of the Trebizond manufacture {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 369, p. 201}. After them walk in full array the heads of the tailors and the Doliman-makers with their nakibs, chaushes, kyayas, sheikhs and the eightfold Turkish music {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 369, p. 201}.

## A small guild beside them

The procession then shows the makers of the leaden plates on which barbers whet their razors — twenty-five men in ten shops, mixing lead to make round dishes for the whet — a new craft without a known patron in any statute book {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 331, p. 196}.

## Not yet covered

Evliya gives three guild censuses, the Suleimaniye factory and the patrons, but not the moulding technique of the button, the division between cast and wrapped buttons in daily dress, or prices {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 329-331, 369}.
