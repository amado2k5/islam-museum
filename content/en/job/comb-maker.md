## Summary

The comb-maker cut and finished the combs of the suq — box-wood for daily use, tortoise-shell for the best {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, pp. 91-92}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa gives the craft a whole hisba chapter: the wood that may be used, the green wood that warps, the press, the aligned teeth and the chisel-rounded points {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, pp. 91-92}. The comb appears again in the pilgrimage economy — Lane's aloes-wood combs carried home from the Hegaz — and in Evliya's tool-market of the shoemakers {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXIV, p. 488; evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 407, p. 209}.

## Only box-wood

"Combs for men and women must be made of no wood but that of the Greek box-tree," the chapter opens, "and it must not be green, otherwise it will warp as it dries and break" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, p. 91}. The best combs are made of tortoise-shell {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, p. 91}. Any other wood — the manual names orange-wood — shows itself in the combing: "splinters of wood appear and tear out the user's hair" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, p. 91}. The material rule is practical, not ceremonial: a comb lives or dies by the hardness, grain and dryness of its wood.

## Press and chisel

The workmanship rules are as exact. The workman must use good craftsmanship; the points must stand in line {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, p. 91}. After it is cut, the comb is pressed hard in a press for a time, for "it will not move properly unless it is true" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, p. 91}. The inside work must be well done so that the "mouth" — the intervals between the teeth — is small and the teeth fine; it must move sharply through the hair, and the teeth are rounded with a chisel, all splintering avoided {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LX, p. 91}. The sequence — seasoned box-wood, cut, press, chisel — is the whole craft in four moves.

## Combs of the pilgrimage

The comb travelled. Among the things a pilgrim brings home from the Hegaz — hard cakes from the Prophet's tomb, frankincense, palm-fibre for washing, rosaries, miswak sticks dipped in Zemzem-water, kohl and shawls — Lane lists "combs of aloes-wood" {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXIV, p. 488}. A small wooden craft, then, sat inside the great commerce of the hajj: goods made in the Hegaz, bought in its markets, carried back to Egypt as gifts and keepsakes {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXIV, p. 488}.

## Combs in the tool-market

In Istanbul the comb appears where a small tool belongs. The instrument-makers for the shoemakers — a hundred men in eighty shops, with a market near the old Bezestan by the bitter fountain — sell "lasts for slippers, boots, and shoes, glue, needles, awls, combs, presses, &c." {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 407, p. 209}. The comb-makers have no procession-guild of their own in the volume Evliya gives, but their product is everywhere: in the barber's hand, the pilgrim's bundle and the shoemaker's kit {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 407, p. 209; lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXIV, p. 488}.
