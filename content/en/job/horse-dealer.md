## Summary

The janbazan-esb traded horses as dealer, crier and go-between under an Inspector of the horse-market whose inspectorship was let every year for forty purses {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 231-233, p. 170}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa writes the broker's code over the same beasts: a register of sellers, disclosed blemishes, an honest commission {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLI, pp. 49-50}.

## Three hundred dealers

In Evliya's procession the horse-dealers were three hundred men, their patron Kanbur Ali, who was girded by Ali and perished with the martyrs of Kerbela {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 231, p. 170}. These horse-dealers are rich merchants: in the stables of each of them are found from forty to fifty Arabian horses {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 231, p. 170}. The greatest part of them are gipsies, but there are also people of all nations amongst them {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 231, p. 170}.

## Criers and go-betweens

The criers of horses at public sales are another three hundred men; they have the right of selling horses wherever they are sold by public sale {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 232, p. 170}. The go-betweens have no shops and act but as mediators between the parties who sell and buy horses at the market; their patron is Nejib-ud-din, the son of Kanbur Ali, girded by Ali, whose tomb is in the field of the martyrs of Kerbela {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 233, p. 170}. The troop passes leading many thousand horses by chains for show, with great noise and shouting, along with the Inspector of the horse-market, the Writer and the Janissaries attached to them {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 233, p. 170}.

## The broker's register

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa makes the cattle-broker's craft a matter of record: cattle-brokers may not sell an animal without knowing the seller or someone who knows him, and his name must be recorded in the broker's register, in case the animal is defective or has been stolen {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLI, p. 49}.

## The disclosed fault

The purchaser must be informed of any blemishes in the animal, of its age and habits {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLI, p. 49}. The broker must claim nothing for it except what he has from the merchant himself {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLI, p. 49}. And the broker may not for any reason turn away from the person who offers a high price to another who offers a smaller one {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLI, p. 50}.

## The muhtasib's stable

Al-Shayzari sets the same pattern for traders in animals: the dealer must be trustworthy, reliable and honest, well known for his integrity {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 34, p. 101}. The welfare of animals itself comes under the muhtasib's jurisdiction — they should not be left standing with loads on their backs nor bear too great a burden, and the loads must be taken off when the beasts stop {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley pp. 18, 37}.
