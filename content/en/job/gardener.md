## Summary

The bostancı was the gardener of the palace: the Ottoman Bostancı Ocağı, a bîrun institution of the palace service whose founding cannot be fixed exactly but reaches the reign of Murad I, worked first in the palace gardens and bağs and on the sultan's own kayıks {cite:tdv-bostanci}. Its men were chosen from the strongest and most presentable of the devşirme boys, the Bosnian Poturoğulları especially preferred {cite:tdv-bostanci}.

## Gardens and barges

The corps' first duties were the palace's own gardens and orchards and the sultan's barges, though a bostancı could be put to any palace work {cite:tdv-bostanci}. Those serving inside the palace were the gılmânân-ı bağçe-i hâssa; those outside in the imperial bağs and bostans were the gılmânân-ı bostâniyân {cite:tdv-bostanci}. Edirne had its own fully independent second corps of about ten bölüks tending the has bahçes there, and bostancıs stood wherever the sultans had gardens — Bursa, Gelibolu, even Amasya {cite:tdv-bostanci}.

## The establishment at Topkapı

Topkapı's bostancıs formed twenty bölüks of fourteen to forty-nine men each; in the late sixteenth century the has-bahçe men numbered 645 and the other bostancıs about 971 {cite:tdv-bostanci}. Outside the palace they worked in companies of twenty, thirty or a hundred under chiefs called usta, who were also the local inzibat officers; a bostancı posted to outside service went out as a kapıkulu cavalryman or to the Arsenal corps, gifted a horse and a "weapon-price" of money {cite:tdv-bostanci}. Each year the men received a kaput coat and the nefers wore the barata cap; like the other kapıkulu corps they drew their pay every three months {cite:tdv-bostanci}.

## From garden to watch

The corps' senior officer, the bostancıbaşı, helmed the sultan's own barge — the bostancıs pulling the oars — and answered for order over Topkapı, the Bosphorus and Golden Horn shores and the Princes' Islands {cite:tdv-bostanci}. He carried out executions, was the only man besides the sultan allowed a beard in the palace, and kept the registers of every yalı on the Bosphorus and the Golden Horn — without his leave no yalı could be built {cite:tdv-bostanci}. Evliya's fishermen's chapters show the same jurisdiction on the water: the Greeks of the harbour shore stood under the Bostanji-bashi and paid him a ducat for every stake they fixed in the sea {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer}.

## Rank and reward

The bostancıbaşı, always a palace-bred appointee, went out to service as sancak beyi, sometimes kapıcıbaşı or beylerbeyi, and some rose as high as grand vizier {cite:tdv-bostanci}. The three hundred bostancı hasekileri kept sixty men guarding the sultan; the bostancı kethüdası stood as the chief's deputy and until the early seventeenth century was usually his heir; the odabaşı represented the corps at the grand vizier's residence, and the hamlacılar — the lowest rank — rowed the hassa barges under their hamlacıbaşı {cite:tdv-bostanci}.

## The end of the corps

More loyal to the sultan than the other kapıkulu corps, the bostancıs kept out of the revolts of 1703; their faithfulness sent the first students of the mühendishâne from this corps under Mahmud I and tied Selim III's Nizâm-ı Cedid army to it {cite:tdv-bostanci}. When the Janissaries were abolished in 1826 and the capital's order passed to the new Asâkir-i Mansûre, the Bostancı Ocağı lost its reason and was closed the same year — not by simple extinction but reshaped with reforms and a new kanunnâme; the trained hassa soldiers formed from its men became the seed of the Hassa Ordusu and today's First Army, while the Edirne corps was abolished outright {cite:tdv-bostanci}.
