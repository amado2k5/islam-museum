## Summary

The muezzin is the person who gives the call to prayer (ezan) and the iqama; the office separated from the older town-crier (münadi) when the Prophet appointed Bilal ibn Rabah as the first muezzin in Medina, after the wording of the call was fixed in year 1 or 2 AH {cite:tdv-muezzin}. In the Prophet's time the office was held in Medina by Bilal and Ibn Umm Maktum, at Quba by Sa'd ibn A'iz al-Qaraz, and in Mecca by Abu Mahdhura {cite:tdv-muezzin}.

## A voice chosen for beauty

From the earliest period muezzins were chosen from among those with a beautiful, powerful voice, who recited the Qur'an well and knew music; the Prophet is related to have tested some twenty men and to have preferred the voice of Abu Mahdhura, teaching him the phrases himself {cite:tdv-muezzin}. Abu Mahdhura came to be counted the founder of the Meccan style of call, Sa'd ibn A'iz of the Medinan, and muezzins in the lands to which Islam spread took one of these two recitations as their basis {cite:tdv-muezzin}.

## Duties beyond the ezan

The muezzin's work went far beyond the five calls: he announced deaths and official decrees, walked before the caliph to the festival prayer carrying the anaza staff, recited eulogies when rulers set out or returned, and in Mamluk times gave the 'sultan's ezan' called in unison across the mosques of Egypt and the Hijaz {cite:tdv-muezzin}. Muezzins of great mosques divided duties among dozens of colleagues — the great mosques kept thirty to seventy muezzins, in some places one for each school's mihrab — under a chief muezzin, and in Mecca the müezzin-i zemzemi congratulated the emir each evening {cite:tdv-muezzin}.

## Paid by waqf and treasury

Muezzins drew salaries from early on: the entry reports regular pay from the caliphate of Uthman, a thousand dinars a year for muezzins on Harun al-Rashid's register, and Nasir-i Khusraw's observation that from Damascus to Kairouan every town and village had muezzins paid by the governors {cite:tdv-muezzin}. Waqf deeds set their pay precisely — the Sultan Hasan complex in Cairo assigned a chief muezzin and sixteen muezzins at fifty and forty dirhams a month with ten more in Ramadan — and muezzins in hospices often doubled as cooks, gatekeepers and attendants {cite:tdv-muezzin}.

## The Ottoman muezzin

In the Ottoman system the muezzin held a sultan's berat, counted among the askeri class exempt from personal tax, and served in mosques, ships, fortresses, schools and hospitals {cite:tdv-muezzin}. The hünkâr müezzinleri, the sultan's own muezzins, were taken into Enderun under the sermahfil, taught music and the etiquette of the office, and numbered fifteen in the sixteenth century; Evliya Çelebi — himself a muezzin by profession — counts seven hundred muezzins among the ehl-i hiref-i hâssa, the men of the imperial craft {cite:tdv-muezzin}. Two palace muezzins, Baltacı Mehmed Pasha and Bıyıklı Ali Pasha, rose to the grand vizierate {cite:tdv-muezzin}.

## Fiqh of the office

The jurists required the muezzin to be a Muslim of legal majority who knew the prayer times; whether he might take wages was debated — Hanbalis held payment for the call impermissible, Shafi'is and Malikis allowed it, and later Hanafi scholars accepted pay as volunteers grew scarce {cite:tdv-muezzin}.
