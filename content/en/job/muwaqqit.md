## Summary

The muwaqqit is the officer of the religious institution who fixes the times — above all the times of prayer — by the sun, moon and stars {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. His science is ilm-i mikat, the science of determining the prayer times, while the astronomer who pursues the same questions as research is called the mîkâtî {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. He worked in the muvakkithane, the timekeeper's office of the mosque complex {cite:tdv-muvakkithane}. The craft arose because one of the aims of Islamic astronomy was to let the prayers be performed at their true times and toward the qibla {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}.

## The word and the work

Mîkât comes from vakt, to fix a time; it means the time or place set for a task, and for that reason the stations where the pilgrim enters ihram are also called mawâqît {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. İlm-i mikat is the science of determining time — the prayer times in particular — by way of the sun, moon and stars {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. That the prayer times have entered is one of the conditions of the five daily prayers, and holds not only for the obligatory prayers but for the sunnas bound to them, the witr, the tarawih and the festival prayers {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}.

## From practice to tables

In the first years of Islam the prayer times could be fixed approximately, by practical methods anyone could apply; from the second century Hijri (eighth century CE) the scientific principles to be followed were laid down and have been kept, with small changes, to this day {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. In the ninth century the first tables of the "five times" (el-evkâtü'l-hamse, the legal hours) were prepared for every degree of longitude and every day of the year, together with tables for fixing the qibla, and the work reached its highest level in fourteenth-century Damascus {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. Fixing dawn and dusk astronomically meant knowing the sun's angle below the horizon: Habash al-Hasib, al-Nayrizi, al-Biruni and Ibn al-Haytham reckoned both angles at 18 degrees, Ibn Yunus and Ibn al-Shatir put 19 degrees before sunrise and 17 after sunset, al-Marrakushi 20 and 16, and today 18 and 17 degrees are accepted {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}.

## The muvakkithane

The place where the muvakkits perform their duties is the muvakkithane {cite:tdv-muvakkithane}. The surviving Ottoman examples recorded on the TDV page stand at the Dolmabahçe mosque in Beşiktaş, the Yenicami complex in Eminönü, the Yeni Vâlide complex in Üsküdar and the Beylerbeyi mosque — small buildings of the mosque complex where the timekeeper kept his instruments and tables {cite:tdv-muvakkithane}.

## A muvakkit's career

Mustafa b. Ali el-Muvakkit was born in Istanbul early in the sixteenth century; beside the traditional madrasa education he took lessons in mathematics and astronomy from Mîrim Çelebi and the other leading scholars of his day {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}. He served for a long time as muvakkit of the Sultan Selim mosque and was called by the nisba Selîmî; after 967 (1560) he became reisülmüneccimin at the palace, the müneccimbaşı, and was popularly known as Koca Saatçi — and Takiyyüddin er-Râsıd was appointed in his place when he died {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}.

## The craft's books

Mustafa b. Ali wrote in Turkish rather than Persian, following the earlier Selim-mosque muvakkit Könevî Mehmed, and gives his reason in the prefaces: that workers in the field might easily understand what was written {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}. Twenty-four works are known of him, three in Arabic and the rest in Turkish, nearly all on astronomy; his treatises on the use of the instruments became the reference works of the Ottoman muvakkits and kept their currency to the last days of the state {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}. They include the Ferah Fezâ on the rub'-i âfâkî instrument of his own invention, the Risâle-i Mukantarât of 1529 on the rub'-i mukantara surviving in nearly 120 copies, the Risâle-i Usturlâb-i Selîmî of 1544 on his improved astrolabe, and the Teshîlü'l-mîkāt of 1529 on the making and use of the rubu' board, surviving in nearly a hundred copies of five different contents — for he took the work up again and added his new experiences {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}. His İ'lâmü'l-ibâd of 1525 gives, for a hundred great cities from Morocco to China, the distance, latitude, longitude, qibla deflection and longest and shortest days, and tells in its preface that he saw Istanbul as the centre of the world {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}.

## Today

The prayer-time tables of the muwaqqit's science are still computed by the methods laid down from the eighth century, with the small changes the entry records — the dawn and dusk angles of 18 and 17 degrees now accepted among them {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat}. The office of muvakkit at the great mosques has passed to the religious-services appointments of the modern states; the TDV entries read do not record when the last muvakkithanes ceased to function {cite:tdv-ilm-i-mikat} {cite:tdv-muvakkithane}.
