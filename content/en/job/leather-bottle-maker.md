## Summary

The qirrab made the leather bottles and waterskins that carried water on the road, ghee to the market and powder to the arsenal. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa joins the craft to the tanners in one hisba chapter, because the bottle stands or falls on its hide {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, pp. 92-93}.

## Slaughtered skins only

The makers of leather bottles shall use only the skins of ritually slaughtered animals, and surprise visits must be paid to their shops to investigate whether they do this {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, p. 93}. The signs of differentiation are the smell and the roughness to the touch: in the animal that died of itself some hair-roots remain, and the skin cracks as it dries {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, p. 93}.

## Three layers, two layers, one

The bottles are graded by their layering: large bottles must be made of three layers, middle-sized ones of two, and small ones of a single layer which shall be stout and sound and free of patching {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, p. 93}.

## The qaraz tanning beneath the bottle

The same chapter fixes the tanning on which the bottle depends: goat-skins shall be tanned only with qaraz — the acacia fruit of Yemen — forty Egyptian ratls to every hundred small skins and sixty to every hundred large, each batch lying three days in a constant qaraz solution in a pit and then removed to another pit with an equal weight of qaraz, four times in succession to remove all grease {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, p. 92}. One deception is to repeat the process three times only, oak-galls being used the third time, which is harmful to the skins; the mark of it is that the skins blacken in the sun, and tanning in summer is better than winter-tanning {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, p. 92}. A pit of two hundred skins requires no fewer than two men, and the hides of oxen that died of themselves must not be placed with those of slaughtered beasts {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII, p. 92}.

## The Meshakjian of Istanbul

In Evliya's procession the jack-makers (Meshakjian) — the makers of jacks for carrying water on horses — were a hundred and five men with fifty shops, carrying on their horses all kinds of jacks, leathern bottles and water-pipes {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 396, pp. 208-209}. Beside them marched the oil-pot makers (Debbejian), a hundred men with forty shops, who adorn their shops with all kinds of leathern pots and vessels called debbe and boduj, for keeping oil, butter and powder {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 397, p. 209}.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the hisba rules and the guild census but not the stitching and greasing that made a skin watertight, the well-side trade at Mecca and Medina, or prices by the waterskin {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIII; evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 396}.
