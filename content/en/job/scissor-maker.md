## Summary

The scissor-maker hammered steel into shears — an edged-ware branch of the smith's craft {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 287}. The hisba manuals ruled the trade on one point above all: scissors may only be made of pure steel, never soft iron dressed up as steel {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXXVIII, p. 48}.

## The fraud of soft iron

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa forbids the blacksmiths to hammer out knives, scissors, pincers and the like from soft iron, which is of no use for the purpose, when they assure the purchaser it is steel — which is fraud {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXXVIII, p. 48}. Al-Shayzari gives the same ruling: such work is fraudulent, and only pure steel may be used for knives, scissors and razors {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 32, p. 99}.

## Old nails among new

The same chapter bars the subtler fraud: nails rejected and straightened out must not be placed with new nails in such fashion that the purchaser will unhesitatingly believe them to be new, and the same applies to all varieties of iron-ware {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXXVIII, p. 48}. Anyone who practises this illegality must be banished from amongst the Muslims {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXXVIII, p. 48}.

## The guild in Istanbul

Evliya counts the scissor-makers (Makrassjian) at a hundred men with thirty shops; they lay out their shops with scissors {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 287, p. 185}.

## Pawer the Uzbekian

The guild's patron is Pawer the Uzbekian, girded by Selman Pak; he is buried in the town of Tissi near the saint of the Turcomans, Ahmed Yesevi {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 287, p. 185}.

## A craft among crafts

The scissors sit in the smiths' section of the procession between the stirrup-makers, whose wares are inlaid with gold and silver, and the smiths of the shoe-irons — the small, exact, everyday edge among the great wares of iron {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 286-288}.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the steel rule, the guild census and the patron, but not the scissor's bow and pivot work, the distinction between tailor's shears and household scissors, or prices {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXXVIII; evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 287}.
