## Summary

The peksimatçı baked the ship's biscuit — bread baked dry so it could survive a voyage — a trade distinct from the town bread-bakers, with its own guild, ovens and patrons in Evliya's Istanbul {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125}. A thousand men in a hundred and five ovens baked for the Black Sea fleet from Galata, Kuni-cheshmeh and Yeni-köi {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125; the Galata narrative, pp. 69-70}.

## A thousand men, a hundred and five ovens

"The biscuit-bakers (Peksimatjian), a thousand men with an hundred and five ovens. They are most interesting personages in war-time" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125}. Their bake-houses stood at Galata, Kuni-cheshmeh and Yem-khi; in the public procession they passed "baking biscuits and distributing bags full of them to the people", and "biscuits of the weight of one or two quintals are carried by porters to adorn the procession" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125}. The comment about war-time is the key: biscuit was not a delicacy but a stockpile — the food a fleet or an army had to count before it sailed {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125}.

## Galata, bakery of the Black Sea

Evliya returns to the trade in his description of Galata: "on the seashore are an hundred houses of biscuit-bakers, for the ships that navigate the Black sea take all their biscuits from Galata and this place" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer pp. 69-70}. A hundred bake-houses on a single waterfront, feeding every ship that worked the northern sea — the biscuit trade was part of the port's infrastructure as surely as the rope-makers or the caulkers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer pp. 69-70}.

## Patrons of the oven

"Like the Bread-bakers, the patron of the Biscuit-bakers is Adam; and in the time of the Prophet, James Muhziri, who was girded by Ins Ben Malek" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125}. The chief of the guild — the Peksimatji-bashi — marched in the great procession among the officers of the market {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 125}. Evliya also notes, in the same breath, the guilds' patronage logic: the horse-millers alone have no patron, "because no saint can afford his patronage to the tormenting of animals" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 89, p. 125}.

## Biscuit in the Cairo market

The commodity crossed the whole region.
