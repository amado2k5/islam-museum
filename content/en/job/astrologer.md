## Summary

The munajjim — the astrologer — is the practitioner of ilm-i ahkam-i nucum, the discipline resting on the belief that the positions and movements of the stars form a system of signs from which knowledge of the future, the present and the past can be drawn {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. The discipline is in that sense the metaphysics of astronomy: near to it in the classifications of the sciences, yet kept apart by the 'ahkam' in its name {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. In the Abbasid age the munajjims attached themselves to caliphs and army commanders to fix the day and hour for every undertaking {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}, and under the Ottomans their head was the müneccimbaşı, the state official responsible for astronomy and astrology affairs {cite:tdv-muneccimbasi}.

## Two sciences of the stars

Al-Farabi records two sciences called ilm-i nujum: the first, ilm-i ahkam-i nucum, aims at interpreting the indications the stars give of events past and to come; the second, ilm-i nujum-i ta'limi, is mathematical astronomy itself {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Astrology, he writes, is the art of warning people of coming events by the methods of dream interpretation, of zecr — the reading of events taken as unlucky — and of irafa, drawing hidden meanings for the future out of past events {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Al-Farabi himself wrote a treatise on what is sound and unsound in the judgements of the stars, wishing to mark astronomy off from astrology by scientific method, and Ibn Sina kept the same attitude after him {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}.

## The judgement of the scholars

Ibn Khaldun treats the astrologer's art on two grounds: whether the stars exert a natural effect on the bodies made of the four elements, and whether that effect can be known by experience {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Empirical knowledge, he argues, is won by the regular observation of repeated events, and following the periodic movements of the stars takes centuries, so that no certain knowledge is reached; even Ptolemy in the Tetrabiblos had said such predictions are conjecture, and the claim of the stars as agents is rationally baseless {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. It is also religiously void, since the one Agent in creation is God; yet the chance hitting of astrological predictions kept raising the craft's repute, however harmful the art was to scholarly and civil life {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}.

## The art and its branches

Tashkoprizade's classification makes ilm-i ahkam-i nucum a branch of the natural sciences, still tied to the astronomical branches of zijes and calendars, of conjunctions and of malahim {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Its own branches are the divinatory arts: the ilm-i ihtiyarat that investigates lucky and unlucky times, the casting of lots by book, by sand-geomancy, by letters, and zecr {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. The literature divides into mewalid — casting a man's fortune from the stars' places at his birth, an art first practised by the Babylonians and developed on Sasanian soil under strong Indian influence — and ihtiyarat, fixing the moment most fit for an intended act {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Among the famous books the entry names Kushyar ibn Labban's Madkhal fi sina'at ahkam al-nujum, Ibn Hibinta's Mughni, Abu Sa'id al-Sijzi's Kitab Tahawil sini'l-'alam, and al-Biruni's Tafhim, the teaching book of the astrologer's craft {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}.

## At court

In the Abbasid period the caliphs and army commanders tied themselves to their astrologers: the munajjim determined which day and which hour a thing should be done or left {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Harun al-Rashid had a court munajjim, Kanaka al-Hindi, whose book stands in the entry's survey of the astrological literature {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}, and al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah's astrologer al-Isra'ili wrote a work Plato of Tivoli turned into Latin as the Liber capitulorum Almonsaris {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}. Fixing the moment most proper for the intended act was one of the astrologer's basic tasks {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum}.

## The müneccimbaşı

Under the Ottomans the astrologer's office was gathered into the müneccimbaşılık: the müneccimbaşı is the official responsible for the affairs of astronomy and astrology in the state {cite:tdv-muneccimbasi}. The TDV page on the munajjim refers the reader from the office-holder to the office {cite:tdv-muneccim}. The most famous holder is Mustafa b. Ali el-Muvakkit: after a long muvakkitlik at the Sultan Selim mosque he became reisülmüneccimin at the palace after 967 (1560), took the name Müneccimbaşı Mustafa Çelebi — popularly Koca Saatçi — and at his death was succeeded by Takiyyüddin er-Râsıd {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}. His books on the astronomical instruments, written in Turkish so that workers in the field could readily understand what was written, stayed the handbooks of the Ottoman timekeepers and astrologers to the last days of the state {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}.

## Today

The entries read do not trace a living successor to the office: the müneccimbaşılık was a state office of the Ottoman period, and the science it served had long been counted a pseudo-science — the TDV headline itself calls ilm-i ahkam-i nucum a 'sözde ilim' — by the scholars who classified it {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum} {cite:tdv-muneccimbasi}.

## Not yet covered

The entries give the classification, the literature, the scholars' judgements and the Ottoman office, but not the daily practice of a working munajjim, the fees and appointment of the müneccimbaşıs, the women and non-court astrologers of the towns, nor the craft outside Baghdad and Istanbul {cite:tdv-ilm-i-ahkam-i-nucum} {cite:tdv-mustafa-b-ali-el-muvakkit}.
