## Summary

The naqqaniq — sausage-maker — minced meat, seasoned it under supervision, stuffed it into clean casings and fried it; because the filling hid everything, the hisba books treat the trade as one of the most fraud-prone in the market and watch it accordingly {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XIV, p. 31; shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, pp. 61-62}.

## Near the muhtasib's booth

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa's short chapter fixes the geography of suspicion: "it is best that the places in which the sausages are made should be near the muhtasib's booth, for the frauds practised in connection with them are numerous" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XIV, p. 31}. Only "clean, sound and fat meat from the sheep" may be used — other meats go in fraudulently — and the cooking-pans must be changed at intervals {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XIV, p. 31}.

## The supervised recipe

Al-Shayzari's chapter on the sausage-makers gives the craft its full shape. The premises should stand near the muhtasib's booth "so that he can keep a special watch on them, for their frauds are so many they can hardly all be known" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, p. 61}. The muhtasib must order them to use only "pure, good, lean meat and to mince it finely on clean chopping blocks"; while they mince, "there should be someone with them with a fly-whisk to keep the flies away" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, p. 61}. Onions, spices and seasonings go in only "in the presence of the 'arif so that he may know their quantities by weight"; then the meat is stuffed "into clean intestines" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, p. 61}.

## The catalogue of frauds

What could go into a sausage that should not? Al-Shayzari lists it: "some of them adulterate the sausages with the meat of cooked heads, some with liver, kidneys and hearts. Others adulterate the sausages with tough and scrawny meat or mix in the tough meat of camels and cows. Some sprinkle water over the meat when they are mincing it" — the muhtasib must forbid that — "others fill sanbisak with grilled fish and seasonings, while some adulterate it with shelled beansprouts and onion" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, p. 61}.

## Inspect before the pan

Timing is the inspector's problem. "The muhtasib learns of all these frauds by looking into the sausages and seeing what they contain before they are fried. It is difficult to know once they are in the frying pan because the makers skewer them when they are almost ready and the adulterants come out and are cooked over the fire" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, p. 61}. The oil, too, is regulated: it "should not be old or rancid and should have a wholesome flavour and smell", and after frying the sausages are dusted with "wholesome and appropriate ground spices and seasonings" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, p. 61}.

## Thirty men, ten shops

The craft reached Ottoman Istanbul as a small guild: "the sausage-makers (Sujukjian) are thirty men, with ten shops. Their patron is not known. They adorn their shops with well-seasoned sausages of meat" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 181, p. 153}. A modest entry beside the great corporations — but the same trade the hisba manuals had fenced with rules centuries earlier {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XIV, p. 31; shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 15, pp. 61-62}.
