## Summary

The habbal twisted the ropes and cables an army, a fleet and a city cannot do without {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}. In Ottoman Istanbul his guild, the Orghanjian, kept its rope-works in the ditch of Galata behind the Imperial Arsenal and on the Ok-meidan archery ground, five hundred men strong {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}. Around it clustered the hemp merchants, the tent-rope makers and two guilds of halter-makers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 100, 371-372, p. 131, p. 205, p. 208}.

## The works in the Galata ditch

Evliya places the rope-makers' establishment in the ditch of Galata, behind the Arsenal, and on the Ok-meidan {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}. Their patron was Sheikh Abd-allah Ilabli, who is buried in Yemen {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}.

## What they twisted

In the Galata ditch they made every kind of large and small cable: anchor-cables (Jankurtaran), ground-ropes (Gumena), hauling-ropes (Palangar), date-fibre ropes (Khurmalif), esparto ropes (Ispartshina) and heavy hawsers (Khalat) {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}. In wartime, by land, they twisted ropes for the guns {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}. In the procession they passed still twisting ropes and crying 'Ay a Mola' {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 99, pp. 130-131}.

## The tent-rope makers

A distinct guild, the Tanahjian, supplied the camp's tents: a hundred men in forty shops, who marched adorning their tents with tent-ropes made of cotton in different colours {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 371, p. 205}. They passed in the train of the tent-makers, whose craft went back, in guild memory, to Jemshid and whose patron in the Prophet's time was Nassir Ben Abdullah Mekki, who made the Prophet's tent of ihram cloth {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 370, pp. 204-205}.

## Hemp merchants and halter-makers

The Kenderjian, the hemp merchants, sold not only hemp and cotton but ropes as well, three hundred men who served as assistants (yamak) to the boatmen and passed on wagons selling ropes and pack-thread {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 100, p. 131}. Two guilds made the horse-gear: the Kulanjian halter-makers, a hundred and five men in fifty-five shops, sharing the patron of the girdle-makers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 372, p. 205}; and the Yularjian, a hundred men in forty shops, who passed making halters in their shops {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 208}.

## A craft the whole procession needed

The rope-makers stood between sea and land trades: beside them marched the sailmakers, three hundred men in ninety shops, whose oldest patron was Seth {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 101, p. 131}, and the shipbuilders who hailed Noah as theirs {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 98, pp. 129-130}. Cables for the fleet, guy-ropes for tents, gun-tackle in wartime and pack-thread for every porter's load: the habbal's fibres ran through the whole Ottoman order {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 99-100, 371, pp. 130-131, p. 205}.
