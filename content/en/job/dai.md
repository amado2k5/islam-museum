## Summary

The dāʿī — from daʿwa, "to call, to invite" — is the one who calls people to his religion or doctrine; the word also appears strengthened as dāʿiya {cite:tdv-dai}. It circulated in the Mu'tazila, the Zaydiyya and the Abbasid period, but its classic home is the history of the Ismailis, the Qarmatians and the Druzes {cite:tdv-dai}. In the Ismaili daʿwa the dai was an officer appointed by the imam in a rank below the hujja, inside a hierarchy running from the dāʿī al-duʿāt — the imam's spokesman — through the dāʿī al-balāgh, the dāʿī mutlaq, the dāʿī ma'zūn and the dāʿī mahdūd down to the janāḥ {cite:tdv-dai}. And the dai was no mere preacher: he took covenant, compact and bay'a in the imam's name, administered, and at need commanded armies {cite:tdv-dai}.

## The ranks of the dais

The highest rank was the dāʿī al-duʿāt — sometimes styled the dai-i akbar or the bāb — who spoke for the imam and answered to the hujja above him {cite:tdv-dai}. Next the dāʿī al-balāgh, in effect the dāʿī al-duʿāt's secretary, bound to deliver the orders sent to the regions and to keep the secrets reaching their places {cite:tdv-dai}. Then the dāʿī mutlaq — also called dhū'l-massa or nakib — sent to a region with full powers, taking his orders from the dāʿī al-duʿāt alone; then the dāʿī ma'zūn, who learned his teaching from the mutlaq and depended on the dāʿī al-duʿāt for any transfer; then the dāʿī mahdūd or mahsūr, confined entirely to his own region and the mutlaq's authority; and below these the rank of janāḥ, divided into right and left wings, charged with supporting the mutlaq in propaganda and gathering intelligence — and called yad, "the hand", for their service to the dāʿī al-duʿāt {cite:tdv-dai}.

## The dai's training and qualities

The dai's scholarly formation mattered so much that a curriculum was set to leave him able to answer any questioner without embarrassment: in the Fatimid period dais studied Qur'an, tafsir and ta'wil, fiqh, all branches of hadith, da'wa theory, the manner of disputation, religious tales, knowledge of the heretics and zindiqs, philosophy — especially the emanation doctrine — logic, and the application of the sensible to the intelligible {cite:tdv-dai}. Dialogue-form handbooks were composed for them: al-Alim wa'l-ghulam attributed to Ibn Hawshab or to his son Ja'far b. Mansur al-Yaman, and Abu Hatim al-Razi's Kitab al-Zina served as the dais' pocket books {cite:tdv-dai}. Beyond learning he had to be pious, virtuous and exemplary, capable in organisation and command, sincere in his service, and obedient and loyal to the imam or the officer above him {cite:tdv-dai}. He drew a salary and held da'wa funds he was warned not to spend on himself nor to exceed {cite:tdv-dai}.

## The dai at work

Outside the periods when the imam ruled a state like a caliph, the dais worked in secret and by revolutionary methods; when the imam headed a state they rose to high administrative standing and the dāʿī al-duʿāt held a rank equal to the vizier for religious affairs {cite:tdv-dai}. The strong among them include Ibn Hawshab, called Mansur al-Yaman, founder of the first Ismaili state in Yemen; Abu Abdallah al-Shi'i, who bound the Berber tribes to him and defeated the Aghlabids, preparing the Fatimid state for the Mahdi; al-Mu'ayyad fi'l-Din, the great scholar of al-Mustansir-Billah's age and his doctrine's agent at the Buyid court; Hamdan Qarmat and his brother-in-law Abdan, founders of the Qarmatians; and Abu Sa'id al-Jannabi, who defeated the Abbasid army at Basra in al-Mu'tadid's reign and took all Bahrain {cite:tdv-dai}.

## Nizaris, Tayyibis and Druzes

The Nizari Ismailis kept the old system after their move to Iran in the Seljuk period; when no manifest imam was present, the dai of Isfahan led the community, and from Hasan Sabbah's time until the manifest imam Hasan Ala Zikrihi's-Salam appeared in 1164 the lords of Alamut stood as the hidden imam's natural dai — Hasan himself being reckoned above the dailik as hujja {cite:tdv-dai}. The word shaykh later came to be used for the dāʿī al-duʿāt, many second-rank dais were commissioned to carry out the shaykh's orders, and commanders called refik were given a military force, the fida'iyyun {cite:tdv-dai}. Among the Tayyibi-Musta'lis of Yemen the dāʿī mutlaq was made the community's widest authority in the imam's concealment; after the schism the Sulaymani mutlaq sat in Yemen and the Da'udi one at Bombay {cite:tdv-dai}. Among the first Druzes the dais were lower-ranking spirituals under the Tali'a, Muqtana Baha al-Din, in Hamza b. Ali's organisation, aided by officers called ma'zūn or mukāsir, and the system lapsed after Muqtana Baha al-Din's disappearance {cite:tdv-dai}.

## In hadith terminology

In the science of hadith the report of a "dai" narrator who propagates a belief contrary to the Sunni school is not used as proof, for fear he may conceal or corrupt reports against his own doctrine; Ibn Hazm of the Zahiris held that a truthful, retentive and meticulous narrator is accepted even if a dai {cite:tdv-dai}.
