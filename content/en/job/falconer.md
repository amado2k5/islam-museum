## Summary

The doğancı rears the falcon and hunts with it; doğan, from Turkish togan, names the bird of prey flown at game, and the man is also called bâzdâr {cite:tdv-doganci}. Hunting with raptors reaches back before history, and the Turkish-Islamic states kept hunt officers around the ruler — the kuşçu, the emîr-i şikâr, the hârisü't-tayr {cite:tdv-doganci}.

## The hunt officers before the Ottomans

The Seljuk sultans' chief entertainment was the driven hunt, and their emîr-i şikâr held the standing of an amir — his charge the sultan's hunting dogs and hawks and his company on the hunt; among them Sa'deddin Köpek later rose to the vizierate {cite:tdv-avci}. Mamluk Egypt kept a hunt emir of the rank of emîr-i aşere, with the hirâsetü't-tayr and the hayvandar as his two aides {cite:tdv-avci}.

## The Ottoman doğancı

Until 1478 the palace falconers were led by the şahincibaşı; after that year the Enderun ward called the doğancı koğuşu or hâne-i bâzyân took the lead under the doğancıbaşı, whose daily wage in the sixteenth century was twenty akçe, with a yearly allowance, festival tips, and his food, drink and dress on the palace {cite:tdv-doganci}. The ward stood in Topkapı's third court between the treasury and the Has Oda; its men ranked fifth among the kaftanlı Enderun staff, and a promoted doğancıbaşı could go out as şahincibaşı, çakırcıbaşı, mîrâhur, Janissary agha, sancak beyi, kaptan-ı deryâ or beylerbeyi — Hâfız Ahmed Paşa, Murad IV's grand vizier, came up from the doğancıbaşılık {cite:tdv-doganci}.

## The şikâr household

Early seventeenth-century accounts count 592 şikâr people at Topkapı — doğancı, şahinci and atmacacı — of whom only thirty were doğancıs {cite:tdv-doganci}. New men came from the devşirme acemi oğlanlar, served a period at the Edirne, Galata and İbrahim Paşa palaces, and entered Enderun as doğancı şâkirdi to continue their training; the traveller Tavernier records that the twelve senior doğancıs held the same privileges as the treasury-room staff {cite:tdv-doganci}. Mehmed IV, "Avcı", abolished the Enderun doğancı establishment during his reign, handing the doğancıbaşı's duties to the çakırcıbaşı of the bîrun {cite:tdv-doganci}.

## The provincial bird-keepers

In the sixteenth century Anatolia and Rumelia held 2,171 avcı-bird raisers, thick in the sancaks of Maraş, Gelibolu, Kars, Menteşe, Manisa, Silistre, Vidin and Niğbolu {cite:tdv-doganci}. They held timars, paid no money wage, were exempt from the öşür, çift resmi and avârız taxes, and the office passed father to son or to the nearest kinsman; those who failed to deliver their yearly birds paid the mürde-bahâ, 150 akçe for a Muslim and 300 for a non-Muslim {cite:tdv-doganci}. Their work had its own division — gürenceci, kayacı, kümeci, sayyâd, tuzakçı and yuvacı — and they were also charged with protecting the natural ground where they served; the institution, abolished officially by Mahmud II in 1830, left its name on the many villages, farms and quarters still called Doğancı or Doğancılar {cite:tdv-doganci} {cite:tdv-avci}.
