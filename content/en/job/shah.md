## Summary

Shah — the Persian ruler's title — came into the Islamic world with the Buyids and Seljuks and returned as the crown of Iran under the Safavids {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}. The word runs back through Middle Persian to a Sanskrit root meaning 'to rule' {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}.

## Shahanshah

After Cyrus united the Medes and Persians in 550 BCE, the Achaemenid kings styled themselves 'shah-i shahan' — shah of shahs — which settled into 'shahanshah' {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}. The Achaemenids held kingship to be God's gift: the shah ruled by Ahura Mazda's appointment, second only to God on earth, head of army, government and religion, the 'shadow of God' — zillullah {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}.

## Under the Sasanians

The Sasanians kept the pattern: provincial iqtadars and emirs were called shah, and princes trained as governors bore names like Gilanshah and Kirmanshah; the shahanshah was held free of bodily defect, and the deformed were barred from his sight — he sat behind a curtain at audience and none spoke before him {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}.

## Into Islam

The Buyids adopted the title: Adud al-Dawla first used shahanshah officially in khutba and coinage, and the caliph al-Qadir granted it to Baha' al-Dawla; the Seljuks compounded it — Tughril 'al-sultan al-mu'azzam shahanshah', Alp Arslan 'shahanshah al-mu'azzam', Malikshah likewise {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}. The caliph al-Nasir addressed the Ayyubid al-Malik al-Adil as 'shahinshah malik al-muluk', Hulagu signed himself 'shahanshah of the face of the earth' to the Ayyubid of Aleppo, and even the Mamluk Barquq took 'al-shahinshah al-mu'azzam' {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}. Meanwhile 'shah' fused into royal names — Malikshah, Süleymanshah, Khwarazmshah, Shirvanshah — and into place-titles like 'shah-i jahan' {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}.

## The Safavid crown

Shah Isma'il's founding of the Safavid state brought 'shah' back as the title of the ruler of Iran: he was at once shaykh of the Safavid order and sultan of the state, uniting religious and political power, and under Nadir Shah Afshar the shah again stood as the summit of might {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}. The Zands dropped the title entirely for 'vakil' — the founder called himself 'vakil al-ra'aya', deputy of the flock — before the Qajars restored 'shah' once more {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}.

## In Turkish and Indian usage

Ottoman sultans used 'shah' beside padishah, sultan, khan and khakan; from Selim I to Ahmed III it stood in the tughra between name and 'khan' — Selimşah, Süleymanşah — a pattern born of princes serving in the sancaks under the title {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}. In India, Delhi sultans and Mughals alike carried it in their names — Shah Jahan at the front, Firuz Shah and Tughluq Shah at the end {cite:tdv-sah-hukumdar}.
