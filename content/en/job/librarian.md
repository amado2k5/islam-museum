## Summary

The hafiz-i kutub — 'keeper of the books', also entered in the vakfiyes as emin-i kütüb, hazin or hazin-i kütüb — was the officer of the Ottoman waqf libraries {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. The endowment deeds describe his qualities, duties, appointment and dismissal at length: the earliest to set conditions is Mehmed II's own vakfiye, which requires a man 'acquainted with the names of the authoritative books' who knows which books the professors, teaching assistants and advanced students need {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}.

## What the deeds demanded

Bayezid II's 1488 külliye deed at Edirne asked for a man 'pious, faithful and trustworthy'; Süleyman's deed for Mihrimah Sultan's foundation merged the earlier conditions into 'a knowing, righteous, learned man' {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Feridun Bey's deed of 1559-60 is the most eloquent: a keeper 'trustworthy and devout, upright and dignified, who will not betray the books entrusted to him — informed in knowledge and learning, a keeper of books by instinct, not an ignorant man who scatters the pages like autumn leaves' {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Later deeds refined the profile: some required an alim who would also teach, some barred müderrises, kadis, imams and shaykhs — and Yusuf Ağa even barred craftsmen and merchants — while Halet Efendi required a hard-working bachelor and one Medina foundation required a Naqshbandi initiate {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}.

## The work

The duties centred on custody: the Fatih deed sums them up as not withholding the books from the medrese's members, guarding them with utmost effort, and supervising the lending {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Later deeds added fetching the volumes readers asked for, pledging a deposit against loans, recording titles and borrowers in a register, refusing loans beyond a month, and reporting worn bindings to the mütevelli for repair {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. By the eighteenth century some libraries added teaching — Hacı Selim Ağa required his first two keepers to give lessons to students for extra pay — while the Yusuf Ağa and Raşid Efendi deeds required keepers to watch readers' eyes so pages were not cut or books dirtied, and Kılıç Ali Pasha's deed opened each day with prayers, a Yasin recital and a weekly hatim rota {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}.

## Rank, rota and dismissal

Where several keepers served they were graded first, second and third hafiz-i kutub, the chief also called baş hafiz-i kutub; at Ayasofya the staff reached six, and Toderini reports two on duty each day with the library closed on Fridays {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Deeds from Köprülü onward insisted the keepers be present in person the whole day rather than serve by deputy, softened into a rota where numbers allowed {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Dismissal grounds recorded include three days' unexcused absence, breach of the founder's conditions, leaving the post, lending without pledge or letting books leave the library — and taking a müderrislik or kadılık, which disqualified the holder {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}.

## Pay, food and lodging

Wages tracked the library's size: one to two akçe a day in the foundation era, six at the Fatih külliye, 15 at Turhan Valide Sultan's Yenicami library in 1662-63, then monthly kuruş salaries at Köprülü; Ayasofya's first keeper reached 90 akçe a day by 1752 and Ragıp Pasha set 120 and 110 akçe for his first and second keepers, arguing that a man present six days a week could hold no other work {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Keepers also drew kitchen rations — soup and fodula loaves from the imaret, or a cash ta'amiye — and many deeds gave them a house provided by the founder {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}. Inheritance of the post by a dead keeper's even minor son was an old Ottoman custom the eighteenth-century deeds began to restrict {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}.

## The yamak

A few libraries kept an assistant, the 'hafiz-i kutub yamağı': the first is recorded at Ferruh Kethüda's Balat mosque library in 1565-66 at two akçe a day, and Ragıp Pasha required his yamaks to be capable, careful in handing books to readers, at fifteen akçe daily — at Âşir Efendi's library the title was changed to 'mülâzım-ı hâfız-ı kütüb' {cite:tdv-hafiz-i-kutub}.
