## Summary

The mutavvif — the word first meant "one who makes the tawaf" and came to mean "one who has it made" — guided the pilgrims of Mecca through lodging, food, health and the rites of the hajj; delîl, muallim and shaykh al-hac were also used for the office {cite:tdv-mutavvif}. The fees the pilgrims paid made it an attractive living for the people of Mecca, certain families took it up as their livelihood, and a profession formed {cite:tdv-mutavvif}.

## The closed taifes

The mutavwifs divided into closed sub-groups called taifes, shut even to one another; each had an appointed shaykh al-mutavvifin and the whole body a shaykh al-mashayikh chosen by the shaykhs from among themselves {cite:tdv-mutavvif}. A boy entered the trade as an apprentice in childhood — an early nineteenth-century travelogue describes how children helped the pilgrims — and after a term of experience he was admitted before all the mutavwifs in a ceremony recalling the akhi passage from apprentice to master, closing with the "muallimiye" feast he gave and the reading of the Qur'an {cite:tdv-mutavvif}. Those who abused the office or handled pilgrims roughly were punished by their shaykhs and made to leave the profession {cite:tdv-mutavvif}.

## Dress and duty

To hold their pilgrims' eyes in the crush the mutavwifs wound great yellow turbans, wore broad sashes of yellow, green or red over decorated jackets, and carried coloured cloth banners fixed to a staff; chosen also for strong voices, they walked ahead of their pilgrims at the Ka'ba repeating the prayers of each circuit, and went with them to Mina and Arafat {cite:tdv-mutavvif}. If a pilgrim died they answered for the burial and the keeping of his belongings, and in older times each legal school had its own mutavwifs {cite:tdv-mutavvif}.

## The four servant groups

The hajj service of later centuries divided into four bodies: the mutavwifs at Mecca, the vekils who were their agents at Jeddah — meeting pilgrims at the port, helping with customs and carrying them up to Mecca and back — the delils who accompanied them in Medina, among whom the guides to the visiting-places were called müzevvir, and the zemzemis who served them zemzem water at Mecca, Mina and Arafat {cite:tdv-mutavvif}. A vekil might work for several mutavwifs at once {cite:tdv-mutavvif}.

## Earnings and regulation

A good season could earn a mutavvif his family's yearly keep, though part of it was owed to his shaykh; pilgrims landing at Jeddah paid a landing-fee and a delil fee until recent times, and in later years the government fixed the mutavwifs' charges {cite:tdv-mutavvif}. Under the Saudis new rules followed complaints: a 1965 royal circular asked that hajj servants be chosen for sound body and age, adequate knowledge and kindness to pilgrims {cite:tdv-mutavvif}.

## Today

Pilgrim groups are now chiefly helped by religious officials sent from their own countries, yet the traditional mutavvif institution survives {cite:tdv-mutavvif}.
