## Summary

The sammak sold fish rather than catching it — in Evliya's words, they do not busy themselves with fishing, but only with selling fish {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 210, p. 160}. The hisba manuals police the trade through the fish-fryers' chapter: washed baskets, salted trays, fresh fish known by its red gills {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXI, pp. 34-35; shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, pp. 56-57}.

## The fishmongers' quarter

The fishmongers (Ikiliksatajian) were three thousand men in Evliya's Istanbul; they did not busy themselves with fishing but only with selling fish, and their shops stood at Galata, Fener, Jubbeh Alt, the Flour-hall, the New-gate, Pir Pasha, Kassim Pasha, Khass-koi, Galata, Top-khanah and Beshik-tash {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 210, p. 160}.

## Freshness by the gills

Al-Shayzari orders the fish-fryers to wash daily the baskets and trays with which they carry the fish and to sprinkle ground salt in them every night after washing; likewise their palm-leaf weighing pans, for if they neglect to wash them they give off an offensive smell, and the smell of fresh fish put in them is tainted {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, pp. 56-57}. They must not mix stale fish with fresh; the sign of a fresh fish is that the gills are red, whereas those of a stale fish are not {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, p. 57}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa gives the same mark: the nose and gills of the fresh fish are red {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXI, p. 34}.

## The fry

The fish is thoroughly washed after being sliced open, cleaned out and scraped of its skin and scales, then sprinkled with salt and flour — for every ten ratls of fish, one ratl of flour — then dried and fried {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, p. 56}. When the muhtasib is absent, the 'arif should check the frying pan every hour in case they fry the fish with oil mixed with the fat coming from the fish's stomach; the best thing to fry fish with is sesame oil, and they must not fry it with used oil whose smell has become rancid, nor remove the fish from the frying pan until it is properly cooked, without overcooking or burning {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, p. 57}. Baked fish, Ibn al-Ukhuwwa adds, must be prepared in the presence of an agent trusted by the muhtasib, and the oil used for frying must be saffron-seed oil or fresh sesame-oil {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXI, p. 35}.

## Imported and unsold fish

As for fish imported into the town or remaining unsold in the shops, the scales must not be scraped off; the fish should be protected with salt, especially their heads and gills, where the maggots appear first {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, p. 57}. Whenever fish that has not been sold and is unwanted becomes rotten, it must be thrown on the rubbish heaps outside the town {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 12, p. 57}.

## The fishermen's procession

The fishermen adorned their shops on litters with many thousand fish, amongst which many monsters of the sea were to be seen: they exhibited dolphins in chains, sea-horses, beavers, whales and other fish of great size, which they caught a couple of days before the public procession and loaded on wagons drawn by seventy-eight buffaloes {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 210, p. 160}. A great number of these fishermen were Greeks from Kayseri, Nigde and Manisa; they passed crying 'Mai' and 'Ihu' to the amazement of the beholders, and the enfranchised Karatiaji collected different insects of the sea and carried in their hands halberds, harpoons and artificial trees {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 210, p. 160}.

## The guilds of the catch

Around the fishmongers marched the fishermen's corporations: the fishermen of the Serai, three hundred in number; the fishermen who look out from wooden hustings, the Dalian; the fishermen with large nets, the Ighribjian, three hundred men; the fishermen of the Karatia nets; the fishermen with the common net, the A'ghjian, a thousand men; the harpooners, the Sapkanjian, who know the haunts of fish and watch; the basket fishermen; and the oyster-fishers, eight hundred men {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 200-211, pp. 158-160}.
