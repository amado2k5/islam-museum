## Summary

The gulabji sold rose-water and the kindred distilled waters — of frankincense, amber and jasmine — in a guild of seventy men and forty-one shops in Evliya's Istanbul {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 58, pp. 118-119}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa names the adulteration of Damascene rose-water among the frauds the muhtasib must test {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVI, para 159, p. 40}.

## Streams on the crowd

At the public procession the rose-water merchants pass pouring rose-water and frankincense-water in large streams on the spectators {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 58, p. 119}. Their patron is Atr-ud-din, the Indian, who is buried in India at the town of Div: he presented the Prophet always with scents and perfumes, for the Prophet liked them extremely, and received the girdle from Ali, who created him a pir {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 58, p. 119}.

## The women of Adrianople

Women of Adrianople sell rose-water in large vessels of bronze before the old Beze-stan; others sell water of frankincense, of amber, of jasmine, and many other perfumed waters of this kind {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 58, pp. 118-119}.

## Mokabbil Zerrin's warm sherbets

The sherbet-sellers, three hundred men with no shops, sell sherbet and gulab in the streets {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 184, p. 154}; the salep-merchants cry, "Take salep seasoned with rose-water, rest for the soul, health for the body" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 189, p. 155}. The patron of all these merchants of warm sherbet, jelly, cream, salep and julep is Mokabbil, girded by Hamza, whose intimate friend he was: when Hamza did not go to war, Mokabbil Zerrin shortened the long winter days by cooking for him warm sherbet, cream, jelly, salep and julep; he was cut to pieces with Hamza at the battle of Ohod and is buried near him outside Medina to the north {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 191, p. 155}.

## The sprinkled procession

In the train of the custom-house, boys with faces like the sun sprinkle on both sides rose-water on the spectators out of gulabdars, while others perfume them with aloe and amber burning in censers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 171}.

## The adulterated drop

In Ibn al-Ukhuwwa's chapter on the small traders, Damascene rose-water has its own entry: how it is adulterated, and the tests therefor {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVI, para 159, p. 40}. The same manual lists among the syrup-makers' wares the syrups of roses and lilies, and the julep that takes its name from the rose-water itself {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXIV, para 154, pp. 39-40}.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the guild's numbers, patronage and pouring custom, and the fraud-chapter's tests, but not the stills and their season, the price of a gulabdan, the rose gardens' supply, or the trade outside Istanbul and Adrianople {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 58, pp. 118-119; ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVI, para 159, p. 40}.
