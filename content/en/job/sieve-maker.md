## Summary

The ghirbali made sieves and riddles of horse-hair and of skin, the assistant craft of millers and bakers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 85, p. 125}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa gives the sieve-makers their own chapter of the hisba — the hair washed, the dead-beast's hair barred, the copperas dye exposed as fraud {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, pp. 91-92} — and Evliya marches them three hundred strong in two hundred shops {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 85, p. 125}.

## Washed hair

A trustworthy man experienced in the sieve-makers' deceptions must be appointed over them, Ibn al-Ukhuwwa writes; he must command them to wash all hair before they use it, and they must guard against using the hair of animals which have died of themselves — the criterion of it being that it is coarse and breaks quickly {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, p. 91}.

## The copperas fraud

Hair used must be utilized as it is, without dyeing {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, pp. 91-92}. Some makers take copperas, boil it and leave the hair in it; this weakens the hair so that it snaps in use and holds nothing {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, p. 92}. They sell it without informing the purchaser that it is dyed, which is fraudulent {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, p. 92}.

## The skin sieve

The sieve-makers must be required to swear that they will not use for sieves the skins of animals which have died of themselves {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, p. 92}. They must cleanse the skins before they cut the holes in them to form the sieve, so that they shall not easily show cuts {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, p. 92}.

## The bakers' assistants

In the procession of the trades, the sieve-makers (Ghalburjian) were three hundred men with two hundred shops; they made sieves of horse-hair and were assistants of the bakers, passing on wagons making sieves as they went {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 85, p. 125}. Their patron was Sa'd-ud-din Ibn-en-nassir, who was girded by Selman the Persian; his tomb is at Kara Amid {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 85, p. 125}.

## The sieving guilds around them

The sieve-makers marched in the millers' and bakers' train {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 82-85, pp. 124-125}. The corn-purifiers (Boghdai Chalikjian), three thousand persons attached to the mills and bake-houses and for the most part Egyptian peasants, separated corn, barley, lentils, beans and rice when mingled together in a sieve; some sieved the corn from the dust in so skilful a way that the corn falling on the ground traced out words of praise {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 84, pp. 124-125}. The millers constructed mills on wagons, put in motion by the wheels of the wagon, which ground flour as they went on, and threw flour on the spectators {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 82, p. 124}.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the sieve's materials and frauds and the guild's numbers, but not the frames and drills of the workshop, the sizes of mesh, the prices of sieves, or the sieve trade outside Istanbul and the hisba cities {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXII, pp. 91-92; evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 85, p. 125}.
