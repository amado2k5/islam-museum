## Summary

The tha'ir rises against the order that stands — a tyrant, an occupier or a regime — meaning to change it; the modern tongue pluralises him as 'the revolutionaries' (al-thuwwar). The jurists named the condition baghy: armed rising against a just imam by force, intending his removal, upon a ta'wil {cite:tdv-bagy}. Not an office one is appointed to but a state one is described by — yet one of the oldest roles of public speech: a sermon, a banner and a manifesto.

## Its limits in fiqh

Three conditions for most schools: that it be against a just imam — some hold there is no baghy against a corrupt one; that it be by force of arms — withdrawal or non-violent disobedience does not suffice; and that it intend his removal or the withholding of his obedience upon a ta'wil the riser believes right — one with no ta'wil is a highwayman, not a baghi {cite:tdv-bagy}. The Malikis and Zahiris count mere disobedience, without fighting {cite:tdv-bagy}.

## In history

Islamic history is full of rebels in name and deed: the rising of al-Husayn ibn Ali in 61 AH protesting what he narrated as tyranny; al-Mukhtar ibn Abi Ubayd at Kufa calling for vengeance for the Prophet's house; Ibn al-Ash'ath against al-Hajjaj; the Zanj and the risings of the provinces {cite:tdv-bagy}. In the modern age the anti-colonial rebels carried manifesto with arms: Abd al-Qadir al-Jaza'iri, Abd al-Karim al-Khattabi, Omar al-Mukhtar — each revolution had its sermon, proclamation and crier {cite:tdv-bagy}.

## The rebel's voice

Revolution was never sword alone: word preceded it and manifesto followed — the declaration sermon, the revolution's radio, the movement's paper, and 'the revolution's spokesman' an office of its own {cite:tdv-bagy}. The rebel is always doubled: arms and speech, neither triumphing without the other.

## The jurists and the rebel

The jurists bound the baghi's ruling with humane limits: the fugitive is not pursued, the captive not killed, their property not taken as spoil, and ta'zir lifted after reconciliation {cite:tdv-bagy}. And they distinguished the reformer who calls to rise against corruption from the pretender who merely covets rule {cite:tdv-bagy}.
