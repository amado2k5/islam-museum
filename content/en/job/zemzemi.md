## Summary

The zemzemî served the well of Zemzem in the Masjid al-Haram and carried its water to the pilgrims — a Meccan service corps that grew out of the ancient sikâye, the watering of the pilgrims that Abd al-Muttalib held together with the rifâde and the care of the well he had rediscovered {cite:tdv-zemzem}. In Ottoman times a saka was appointed for the pilgrims of every province, their headman usually a man of the Al-i Zubayr; today the corps called zemzemî distributes the water at Mecca, Mina and Arafat {cite:tdv-zemzem}.

## Abd al-Muttalib's charge

After Abd al-Muttalib dug the lost well open again, the care of Zemzem was added to the sikâye and rifâde offices already in his hands, and his standing in Mecca rose {cite:tdv-zemzem}. He built two basins for it, one for drinking and one for ablution, and when Meccan youths fouled them at night he repaired them with his sons by day; he is also reported to have milked his camels and mixed the milk with honey, serving it to the pilgrims together with the water {cite:tdv-zemzem}. The earliest known vessels were the wooden cups the Companion Abu Rafi' set by the well {cite:tdv-zemzem}.

## The officer by the well

Soon after the conquest of Mecca a special place was built near the well for the sikâye official who looked after Zemzem affairs alone {cite:tdv-zemzem}. The water was drawn from the uncovered well by windlass and poured in buckets into the two basins Abd al-Muttalib had made — the one before the Black Stone for drinking, the one behind the well for ablution {cite:tdv-zemzem}. In al-Walid ibn Abd al-Malik's time the governor Khalid ibn Abdullah al-Qasri set a post opposite the Black Stone with lamps to light the circumambulators and the well in the pilgrimage months; and when a well-worker fell in during al-Mansur's reign, the mouth was ringed with a low stone wall and given its first grille {cite:tdv-zemzem}.

## Domes and cages

Al-Mahdi added a second grille and domed the well over the tree where Hagar had laid Ismail; in 220/835 al-Mu'tasim raised a great gilded dome, hung lamps on its eaves for the pilgrimage season and mosaicked the ceiling over the basins {cite:tdv-zemzem}. The Ottomans rebuilt the whole Zemzem building in 1527-41, roofing the well under a lead-covered dome; Sultan Ahmed I set an iron cage over its mouth in 1612, and four drawers standing on the iron rim hauled the water up continuously with iron pulleys and forked buckets {cite:tdv-zemzem}. Evliya records forty Arabs, hands and feet stained with henna, working these in alternating groups of four {cite:tdv-zemzem}.

## The sakas and the zemzemîs

When the single sikâye officer could no longer meet the pilgrims' needs, sakas were posted at points around the Masjid al-Haram {cite:tdv-zemzem}. Under the Ottomans a saka was assigned to the pilgrims of every province of the empire, and their headship usually went to a man of the Al-i Zubayr {cite:tdv-zemzem}. The water was stored in open basins with taps, then in two closed reservoirs fed by a pump; and the letters announcing the gifts sent to the officials who distributed zemzem to the pilgrims were themselves called zevrak {cite:tdv-zemzem}. At the washing of the Ka'ba's interior it is the zemzem-servers who bring the water inside to be mixed with rose water {cite:tdv-kabe §1}.

## The sebils of today

When the Hijaz passed to the Saudi family the Zemzem building, a cause of crowding, was removed in the enlargement of the mosque and its basement entrance closed; cooled sebils set about the mosque took over the distribution, and the corps called zemzemî was enlarged so that the water now reaches the pilgrims where they are, at Mecca, Mina and Arafat {cite:tdv-zemzem}.
