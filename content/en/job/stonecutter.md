## Summary

The hajjar cut stone at the quarry, dressed it for the builders and carved it for inscriptions and tombs {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 625-626, p. 231}. The hisba manuals know him inside the builders' chapter, bound by the masons' oath over the quality of the lime {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}; Evliya puts the stone-cutters and stone-draggers in the builders' train of Istanbul and the armed stonecutters in the army's van {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 9, p. 107} {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 625-626, p. 231}; and Chalcraft preserves the petition of a Cairean stone mason who refused to surrender half his wage to the guild shaykh {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 83-85}.

## The masons' oath

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa orders that the masons swear not to take bribes or gifts from the lime-burners and makers of gypsum who wish their acquiescence when the lime is insufficiently burnt or of bad quality {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. The workman who does not use angles, weights and lines to keep the building true is liable for any fault {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. The mark of well-burnt lime, which the stoneworker depends on, is that it turns yellow in the kiln before grinding and dries quickly once mixed with water {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}.

## The stone-cutters of the procession

In the builders' train Evliya counts the stone-cutters (Tash-kersen) at one thousand men and the stone-draggers (Tash-keshan) at one thousand, Armenian ass-drivers who drag their loads crying out to each other {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 625-626, p. 231}. The marble workmen (Mermerberan) are one hundred and sixty-one men who cut chronograms and inscriptions in marble and make turbans and pillars for funeral monuments, and the cutters of whetstone pass with wagons full of it, a hundred and fifty-one men {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 614-616, 624, pp. 230-231}.

## The armed stonecutters

Long before the guilds march, the procession opens with the army's nine corps, and among them the miners with hatchets and the stonecutters, all armed, carrying their shovels, hoes, mattocks and hatchets and passing with their war-cries {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 9, p. 107}. Their patron is Kassem the son of Nossair, girded by Selman in Ali's presence, and their business is to level the ground that would stop the march and to blow up walls at sieges {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 9, p. 107}.

## The stone masons of Cairo

In nineteenth-century Cairo stone work stood high in the building economy: imported marble and stone was the second largest category of imports in the 1860s and 1870s and was transformed by Egyptian masons, while stone workers, plasterers and builders also came from Malta, Italy and Greece {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 47}. Yet at Suez in the early 1870s local subjects outnumbered Europeans among the stone masons, and Stanley conceded that Egyptian masons were much helped by the excellent material of the country {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 48}.

## The dues dispute

Chalcraft records that masters in the construction trades paid the shaykh dues known as the ghada and the taba', drawn from builders and unskilled workers, and that the dues taken could amount to half a man's daily wage {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 83-84}. The Cairean stone mason Husayn Muhammad petitioned the Diwan of Public Works when the shaykh attacked him with blows for withholding that half, the building guilds being attached to the diwan's supervision {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 83-84}. In 1869 a master mason complained against Faraj Badawi, the deputy of the masons' guild, for abusing his office {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 85-86}.

## Today

The craft continues wherever stone is dressed and carved, though saws and compressors have changed its tools; the hisba's question, whether the workman built true and the guild dealt fairly, is the one the stone mason's petition put to the modern state {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95} {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 83-85}.
