## Summary

The najjar works the wood: houses, doors, locks, coffers and whatever the town needs of timber {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-96}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa places the carpenters with the sawyers and builders in one chapter of his hisba manual and regulates their wages, their measuring and their honesty {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-96}. Lane watched the ceremony by which a carpenter's son was admitted to the guild in Cairo {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXVII, pp. 609-610}, Evliya counted the joiners and ship-builders of Istanbul by the thousand {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 98, p. 130}, and Chalcraft found the Cairo carpenter's wage among the tightest of the construction trades a century ago {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 128-129}.

## The wage and the day

Most workmen, Ibn al-Ukhuwwa writes, agree a specified daily wage and then come late in the morning and leave before evening, so terms must be made to prevent this {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 94}. Certain carpenters, masons and painters assure a prospective employer that a job is easy and quote him a low price; then, once he is persuaded and the work begun, they require more than he is able to give, and he is sometimes reduced to selling the place before it is completed {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-95}. Timber hired for scaffolding that is cut must be replaced or paid for {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}.

## True work and foul weather rules

A workman who does not use the angles, weights and lines to keep the building true is responsible for any fault {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. The workmen called raqqasin, "dancers," must wear drawers to cover themselves when they go up and down the ladders, and must not leave before sunset {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. The sawyers are bound to work in threes in every workshop: one sharpens the saws and takes the place of whichever of the others tires {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. And they must not combine together against the public {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}.

## The lock-makers

Carpenters who make locks must have over them a man of honesty and experience in the craft, for it is an important one: on it rests the security of wealth and the protection of the women's quarters {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. They may not cut a key to another's pattern unless both are for partners known for their honour {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. The head of the piece holding the movable teeth must not be pierced but morticed, a place for the teeth hollowed out; the teeth are squared above and rounded, filed smooth and flat at the tip below — and the locks themselves are differentiated by their wards so that no key fits another's lock {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 95-96}.

## Entering the guild

In Lane's Cairo the admission of a son into a body of tradesmen was a family feast, and the full ceremony was customary only among carpenters, turners, barbers, tailors, book-binders and a few others {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXVII, p. 609}. The father goes to the sheykh of the trade; the sheykh sends his officer, the nakeeb, who invites the masters by handing each a sprig of green or a flower and saying "For the Prophet, the Fatihah," and inviting them to coffee on a given day {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXVII, pp. 609-610}. When the guests have dined, the nakeeb presents the young man to the sheykh, states his qualifications, calls for the Fatihah, and girds him with a shawl over his outer coat, tying a knot in the girdle {cite:lane-modern-egyptians ch. XXVII, p. 610}. Chalcraft reads such ceremonies as the trace of a guild memory: an unbroken chain of craft skill and virtue leading back to an original patron {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 22}.

## The carpenters of Istanbul

Evliya passes the joiners and ship-builders (Marakozan) at three thousand men: they have no shops but are found in porches at Galata, Top-khanah and Kassim-pasha, Muslims, Greeks and Franks together, and their patron is Noah, who built the ark {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 98, p. 130}. In the great train of the builders' guilds the carpenters march preparing wooden houses alongside the builders raising walls, the wood-cutters, the sawyers sawing, and the masons whitening their shops {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 647, p. 231}.

## Wages of the trade

In early twentieth-century Cairo, an excellent turner could earn fifteen piastres a day while carpenters and masons earned eight to ten {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 129}. Chalcraft cites a family budget for a carpenter with a wife and four children earning fourteen piastres a day — the minimum necessary for existence — living in two rooms, eating bread and vegetables and almost never meat, with debt to the pawnbroker never far away {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 129}.

## Not yet covered

The sources describe the carpenter as wage-earner, guildsman and lock-maker, but not his tools and timber yard in detail, the making of doors, windows and minbars as crafts in their own right, or any named master carpenter {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-96} {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 98, p. 130}.
