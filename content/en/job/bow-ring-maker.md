## Summary

The zehgirci made the archer's thumb-ring — the zehgir that hooks the bowstring in the eastern draw. Evliya places the bow-ring-makers (Zehgirjian) in the arms-makers' procession, their shops adorned with rings of horn and fish-bone {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 339, pp. 199-200}; Elgood explains the ring's work and its honour — a sultan of the Ottomans himself fashioned thumb-rings as gifts {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 79}.

## Horn and fish-bone

The bow-ring-makers (Zehgirjian) adorn their shops with these rings, stuck on the thumb to bend the arrows and hold the sinew with, made of all kinds of horns and fish bones {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 339, p. 199}. Their patron is Abu Taber Selfal, the son of Sa'd Wakkas, girded by Selman Pak, who was torn by a lion near Helleh {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 339, p. 199}.

## The thumb that draws

The ring exists because of how the arrow is loosed. In the eastern technique the archer draws the string with the thumb, not the fingers, and the thumb-ring protects it from the chafe of the string — though, Elgood notes, many of the ornamental thumb-rings that survive were quite unsuitable for practical use and were worn by men of substance simply to show that they were archers {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 79}. The string-makers worked to the same end: the centre of a bowstring was served with thread to protect it against the abrasion of the arrow nock and the thumb ring {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 97}.

## An honourable craft

Archery was taught as a religious duty, and the crafts supplying archers were highly esteemed: sons of Ottoman sultans were apprenticed to bowyers in their early years {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 79}. One sultan, Mehmet II (1595-1603), being skilled in the manufacture of thumb-rings, would himself fashion a ring and present it to the person he specially wished to honour — the zehgir as a royal gift {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 79}.

## Among the arms-makers

In the procession the ring-makers sit inside the archers' quarter: before them the exercise-masters and bowmen, after them the sword-fighters, club-fighters and wrestlers, and the bird-hunters and fowlers beyond {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 336-344, pp. 199-200}. The small guild is the last link of the bow-maker's work: the yayci makes the bow, the okcu the arrow, the zehgirci the ring that lets the archer use both.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the ring's use, materials and honour, but not the workshop — the saws, rasps and polishing of horn — nor prices, nor the craft outside Istanbul {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 339, p. 199; elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 pp. 79, 97}.
