## Summary
In the history of Muslim states the amil was an official in general and a tax collector in particular. From the prophetic period he collected zakat, and also booty, land tax, poll tax and fay, assessing them and dividing them. The word then narrowed or widened. It was used of a governor, of a collector of the land tax, and in the Ottoman codes of a collector, a tax farmer, or a man collecting in a tax farmer's name. The law books treat the zakat amil apart, with conditions and a wage taken from the zakat itself. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## The word
In the dictionary the word is a person who does a piece of work, a workman or a craftsman, and also the upper part of a spear. The entry says it is used in the Qur'an and in the hadith both in that dictionary sense and as a term of office. In the Qur'an, it says, the word is mostly for people who do good or bad deeds, and it points to Al Imran 136 and 195, Hud 121 and al-Muminun 63. It also points to [[passage/quran-9-60|the ninth sura, verse 60]], where the workers on zakat are named among those who have a share of it. In the hadith it is a governor in general and a tax collector in particular. The entry lists words used there in nearly the same sense: arif, ashir, jabi, amin, khazin, sa'i and musaddiq. In a mudaraba partnership the working partner is called a mudarib, and also an amil. This record is the fiscal and administrative office, not that partner. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## What he collected
The entry says that zakat on both visible property and hidden property was collected through amils from the prophetic period. From the practice of that period it takes the amil as charged with more than zakat. He assessed, established, collected and divided the state's other revenues: booty, fay, the poll tax and the land tax. The entry names particular appointments. On the fifth of the booty that belonged to the state: Mahmiye ibn Jaz. On gold and silver mines and on coin: [[person/bilal-ibn-rabah|Bilal ibn Rabah]]. On zakat in general: [[person/zubayr-ibn-al-awwam|al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam]] and Jahm ibn Salt. On the zakat of dates: [[person/abd-al-rahman-ibn-awf|Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf]]. The museum has no person record for Mahmiye ibn Jaz or for Jahm ibn Salt, so no occupation is written for them. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

Beside that collection, amils were appointed over a region with wide administrative and fiscal powers. The entry says [[person/muadh-ibn-jabal|Muadh ibn Jabal]] was sent to Yemen as amil and held a tax collector's powers. In the first period the amil was not only a tax collector. He was an official of the state in general, and the name was even used of the caliph. The entry says the limits of the work and of the power differed by region and by the man sent, and that amils sent outside Medina generally had the wider powers. In later ages those powers narrowed or widened by period and by country. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## Appointment in the first generations
Under the first four caliphs the word still meant both an official in general and a tax collector. The entry says Abu Bakr appointed [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas ibn Malik]] governor of Bahrain and laid the tax collection on him with the governorship. It says Umar sent [[person/arfaja-al-bariqi|Arfaja ibn Harthama]] to Mosul and [[person/mujashi-ibn-masud-al-sulami|Mujashi ibn Masud]] to Basra as amils of the land tax and of zakat, and appointed [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abd Allah ibn Masud]] to Kufa with both tax collection and the judgeship. It says the commander of the Syrian armies under Uthman was called amil, and it does not name him. It says Ali appointed [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Abd Allah ibn Abbas]] amil and governor of Basra, and it points for that to A'yan al-Shi'a, volume 8, page 56, which was not read for this record. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

The entry says Umar gave his amils a diploma that set out their work and their power, stated the rules they were to keep, and added the advice he thought needed. In the season of the pilgrimage he summoned them to Medina and heard them. Where there was a complaint he opened an inquiry, and if he found the man at fault he dismissed him. He asked them for a declaration of their property before they took up the post, and he inquired where a later increase looked suspect. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## In later states
Under the Umayyads and the Abbasids the name was still used of a governor, and more often of a tax collector. Under the Abbasids amils were appointed from the centre, and also by the governors of distant regions who held wide power. Under the Buyids the word is generally a tax collector. The entry reports two safe-conducts of that period, transmitted by al-Qalqashandi, which use the phrase "amils of the land tax", and it points to Subh al-A'sha, volume 13, pages 337–338, which were not read. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

In the Turkic Muslim states the word was generally an official of the finances. Under the Samanids the amil is a tax collector. Under the Qarakhanids, and especially the western line, a finance officer of this name appears from the eleventh century. The entry points to Genç, page 263, which was not read. Under the Ghaznavids and the Seljuks he is both a tax collector and an official of the state in the general sense. The entry points to Nizam al-Mulk's Siyasatnama and to Köymen's history, which were not read. Under the Khwarazmshahs the amils had some administrative power beside the fiscal. Under the Mamluks and the Ottomans the word occurs. The entry points to two books of Uzunçarşılı, which were not read. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

In the Ottoman state, in historical texts and in various codes, the amil is an officer charged with collecting a tax, a tax farmer, or a man collecting in a tax farmer's name. The entry says that an amil collecting for the state also had, under the codes, other important powers. In the sharecropper villages around Istanbul he might, on stated conditions, gather the crop as he decided, and he might not decide the administration of the villages alone. The matters were discussed at a set time in the emin's house, and the decision was taken jointly. An Ottoman code applied in Egypt set out the duties of the amils there and made their order into articles of the code. An emin stood at their head. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

Beside amil, the word ameldar is found in the same sense among the Ottomans, the Ghaznavids and the Crimean Turks. In countries where Persian was the language of the state, ameldar, kardar and kardan are used as equivalents. The entry points to the Turkish Encyclopaedia of Islam, volume 1, page 403, which was not read. Ottoman sources also use iş eri, iş-güzar and maslahat-güzar in this sense. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## In the law books
In the law books the amil is treated mostly as the zakat official, and the rules are given in the chapter on zakat. Al-Mawardi splits the zakat amils into an amil of full discretion and an amil limited to execution. The entry points to al-Ahkam al-Sultaniyya, pages 148–149, which were not read. The first may carry out every transaction that belongs to zakat, and may settle disputes that arise by his own reasoning. The second works only inside the charge he was given, and when a dispute arises he applies the head of state's view, not his own. Some zakat amils both collect and distribute. Others only collect. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

The entry says a zakat amil has to be Muslim, of full legal capacity, trustworthy, knowing the rules of zakat, and free. It points to Abu Yusuf, pages 115–116, for the fuller list, which was not read. It says the condition of being Muslim applies only to the amil of full discretion, and that the others may be dhimmis. It says the view that prevailed among the jurists is that a zakat amil should not be of the Prophet's descendants, because the amils' pay is taken from zakat, and those descendants are not given zakat. Those who hold that the pay is a wage for the work, and not a share of the zakat, hold that one of them may serve as amil. The entry points for this to its article on the family, which was not read. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## The wage
Only the zakat amils are paid from the zakat. Other officials who work under this name in the state are not paid from it. A zakat amil is given a wage in proportion to his work, and the amount can change with the circumstances. The entry says this wage was quite high in the first period. Al-Shafi'i, starting from the view that zakat is divided into eight equal shares among those entitled, holds that the class of amils is also given one eighth, whatever their work, so long as they do not go over that share or fall short of it. Inside that share they may take different amounts. A zakat amil does not have to be poor to earn the wage. The entry reports a hadith, which it places in Abu Dawud, Zakat, section 25, that zakat is not lawful for a rich person except for five classes, and that the zakat official is one of them. It reports another, which it places in Bukhari, Ahkam, section 16: [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] was working on the zakat and did not want to take a share, and he was told to take it and, if he had no need, to give it out as charity. The two books were not read for this record, so the wording here is the entry's report, not the text of the hadith. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}

## Not yet covered
The entry's bibliography was not read, nor Abu Yusuf, al-Mawardi, al-Qalqashandi or Nizam al-Mulk. The entry gives no year for a first appointment, so no first year is written. The museum has no person record for Mahmiye ibn Jaz or for Jahm ibn Salt. The entry does not describe the amil's tools or a picture of him. {cite:tdv-amil p.58-60}
