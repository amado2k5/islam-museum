## Summary

The lubbud matted wool into felt — the material of caps, cloaks, mats, tents and even campaign baths {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 383-385, pp. 206-207; p. 217}. Ibn al-Ukhuwwa polices his wool: none from animals that died of themselves, none from heads, the red felt at four ratls and the blue at a ratl and a half {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIV, p. 93}. In Evliya's Istanbul the felt-makers marched a thousand and five strong in four hundred shops {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 383, p. 206}.

## Honest wool

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa forbids the felt-maker the wool of animals that died of themselves, recognisable by its softness and ill odour, and wool from heads, recognisable by its roughness {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIV, p. 93}. He fixes the honest weight of the piece — a red felt at four ratls and a blue one at a ratl and a half — requires the thread of all felts to be well made, and the gum moistened free of wool-flock {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIV, p. 93}. Because the smell injures people, felt-makers and shoemakers are forbidden to wash felt or leather in the public baths {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XLII, pp. 50-51}.

## The guild and its patron

Evliya gives the felt-makers (Ketshejian) a thousand and five men with four hundred shops; their patron Abu Said Nadi was girded by Selman Pak, killed at Kerbela, and his tomb there is a place of pilgrimage at which Persians swear {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 383, p. 206}. In the procession they worked different kinds of felt in their shops as they passed {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 383, p. 206}.

## Burk caps and Janissary caps

The burk-makers, a hundred men in forty shops at the horse-market under the same patron, made capots and caps of the felt called burk {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 384, p. 207}. A separate guild of sixty men in ten shops, the felt-makers of the Janissaries, had their factory inside the old and new barracks by the mosque of the Janissaries' Orta, and adorned their shops with the Janissary felt cap {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 385, p. 207}. The coarse-felt merchants marched separately, three hundred men in a hundred shops {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 488, p. 224}.

## The famous caps of Kassim Pasha

Writing of the Kassim Pasha district, Evliya singles out the keche caps sold in the market of Pialeh Pasha: nowhere are they worked so neatly, he writes, except at Magnesia and Konia {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 47}.

## Felt under arms

The bath attendants who followed the Ottoman camp made baths of felt heated with coals for the army wherever they found a convenient spot — the felt-maker's product carrying the hammam into the field {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 217}.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the guild's numbers and its honest-wool rules, but not the beating, rolling and steaming of the felting process itself, nor prices, nor the felt industry of Persia and Central Asia that the trade ultimately served {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIV, p. 93; evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 383, p. 206}.
