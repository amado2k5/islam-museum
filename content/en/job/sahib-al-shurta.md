## Summary

The sahib al-shurta (emir al-shurta) commanded the shurta — the uniformed force that kept order, pursued criminals and carried out sentences in the cities of the Islamic states {cite:tdv-surta}. The office grew out of the watchmen (hares) posted around the Prophet, became a named post when Uthman appointed Abdullah ibn Kunfudh at Medina, and broke free of the qadi's department under the Umayyads {cite:tdv-surta}.

## From watch to institution

Under Mu'awiya the shurta became one of the standing parts of the bureaucracy, its chiefs chosen from the ruler's most loyal men and kinsmen; they guarded him, took his place leading the prayer when needed, and fought opposition groups {cite:tdv-surta}. Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik made the shurta independent of the qadi, and outside the great centres the sahih al-ahdas ran a gendarmerie halfway between police and army {cite:tdv-surta}.

## Height under the Abbasids

Al-Mansur counted the shurta among the four pillars of the state; in Baghdad the sahib al-shurta ranked with the emir, walked directly before the caliph in protocol, read official letters to him, reported on the city's security, and often rose to the vizierate — he drew a thousand dinars while his men received at least a hundred {cite:tdv-surta}. The capital's force was divided into the shurtat al-asima and the shurtat al-jisr, then one for each bank of the Tigris {cite:tdv-surta}.

## Judge of the streets

Beyond policing, the shurta's heads gained judicial powers: they heard urgent cases, and under the Mamluks tried theft, illicit wine and unproven accusations, while in the Maghreb they enforced the qadis' orders and in al-Andalus tried wine and adultery cases, sentencing only with the judge's approval {cite:tdv-surta}. Ibn Khaldun called the shurta a religious institution; its chiefs were expected to be learned, just, pious, sharp and imposing {cite:tdv-surta}.

## Men, buildings and uniform

The shurta's personnel ran from guards, jailers, executioners and night watchmen to intelligence men, bridge guards and patrols, all under the indispensable shurta katib who had to know the fiqh of retaliation, hudud and blood-money {cite:tdv-surta}. Its headquarters stood in the great dar al-imara; the chief's investiture dress was the khil'a, mintaqa belt and shashiyya head-cloth, and his men carried swords, spears, studded clubs, whips, axes and the rank-marked dagger {cite:tdv-surta}.

## Egypt and the provinces

In Egypt the shurta was the second office after the governor: Fustat kept two shurta buildings (al-sufla and al-ulya), a five-hundred-man fire service answered to it, and from 100/718 travellers entering or leaving Egypt had to carry identity papers that the shurta checked {cite:tdv-surta}. Even after Ibn Tulun limited its powers the sahib al-shurta remained second in the land until the dynasty's end {cite:tdv-surta}.
