## Summary

The sarraj made saddles, pack-saddles, saddle-cloths and bridles, and because a camp cannot move without him the Ottoman saddlers took rank before every other corporation of craftsmen {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 390-402, pp. 207-209}. Evliya Çelebi counts five thousand saddlers in Istanbul with 1,084 shops, and gathers around them a whole family of satellite guilds — saddle-cloth makers, saddle-tree makers, sumpter-saddle makers, jack-makers and bridle-makers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 386-402, pp. 207-209; sec. 283, p. 191}.

## The first guild of the camp

Evliya writes that the saddlers, being of the greatest necessity in a camp, obtained rank before all other corporations {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 390, p. 208}. Their patron was Abu Nasr Khusaym of Baghdad, girded by Selman Pak, whose tomb stands in the neighbourhood of Baghdad and from whom the lineage of all saddlers is derived {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 390, pp. 207-208}. In the procession they adorned their shops with embroidered saddles, holsters and field-bottles, and the Serrajbashi, chief of the saddlers, closed the train in the greatest pomp {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 390-402, pp. 207-209}.

## The saddler's castle

The saddlers' own officers — kiayas, nakibs, sheikhs, chaushes, dellal, mokadem and watchmen — alone numbered two hundred men, because their factory resembled a castle with four gates, a basin in the court and a mosque {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 402, p. 209}. Evliya declares that no saddler's factory in Arabia or Persia could be compared with it, and dates its building to Mehmed II in the year 859 {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 402, p. 209}.

## The satellite trades

The saddle-cloth makers, a hundred men in fifty shops under the patron Ja'afer Tayar, fitted their shops with saddle-cloths, saddle-bags, halters and pistol-cases and carried their manufactures on poles {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 386, p. 207}. The saddle-tree makers, three hundred men in a hundred shops, adorned their shops with Syrian saddle-trees; the embroidered saddle-cloth makers were a hundred and five men in forty-nine shops; the jack-makers made leathern water-jacks, bottles and pipes in a hundred and five men with fifty shops; and the oil-pot makers produced the leathern pots called debbe {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 391-397, pp. 208}. The bridle-makers, four hundred men in a hundred shops, showed many thousand bridles inlaid with gold and silver, a hundred of them excellent masters attached to the Imperial stables, their craft first taught to Seid Hamai-ud-din by Khidr {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 283, p. 191}.

## The sumptermen

The sumpter-saddle makers (Semerjian or Talanduzan) were a thousand men in five hundred shops; their patron Yussuf Birindi was girded in the Prophet's presence and fell at Kerbela beside Imam Hossein {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 402, pp. 208-209}. They exhibited small sumpter-saddles laid on young asses, mules and goats, and Evliya counts the sumptermen, with the farriers, cooks and harbors, among the most necessary and most esteemed guilds of the Ottoman camp {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 402, p. 209}.

## The twenty-year assembly

In the meadows of Kaghid-khanah, where the goldsmiths' guild pitched four to five thousand tents for its twenty-day season before the Emperor, Evliya records that the saddle-makers assembled in the same way once every twenty years {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 86}.

## Not yet covered

Evliya's account is the procession and the guild's own memory of itself: it gives no prices for saddles, no apprenticeship rules, and no account of the saddler in the Arab cities before the Ottomans {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 386-402, pp. 207-209}.
