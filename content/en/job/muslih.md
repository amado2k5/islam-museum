## Summary

The muslih works to mend people's faith and world — through teaching, guidance, organisation and renewal — without breaking away from the community. The jurists distinguished him from the rebel: baghy is forcible rising against the just imam, while islah changes by word and work, not by arms {cite:tdv-bagy,tdv-islahat}.

## The jurists' definition

Islah stands in fiqh and language opposite fasad: returning a matter to what it was, or to what it ought to be — in soul, community and rule {cite:tdv-islahat}. It requires no office: the reformer works from his place as teacher, preacher, writer and founder, renewal being God's way in His creation, as they say {cite:tdv-islahat}.

## In history

Among the earliest reformers were the founders of schools and associations: Abd al-Hamid ibn Badis laid the rules of the Association of Algerian Ulama to teach and correct belief without the sword {cite:tdv-islahat}; the Ulama and Islah associations of the Maghreb; and the reformist brotherhoods that built schools and papers. Within the state, the Tanzimat were reform from inside the sultanate itself: administrative and scholastic orders and fermans {cite:tdv-islahat}.

## His instruments

The written and spoken word: lesson, sermon, book, newspaper and school {cite:tdv-matbuat}. Organisation: association, waqfs and libraries to preserve what he taught. And example: people see in him what he calls to {cite:tdv-islahat}.

## His boundary from the rebel

The jurists fixed the boundary in instrument and aim: the reformer works by statement and does not dispute the imam's rule, while the rebel disputes by force upon a ta'wil {cite:tdv-bagy}. One life may hold a phase of each — Abd al-Qadir al-Jaza'iri a fighter in Algeria, then a resident of Damascus teaching and protecting Christians — but judgement follows the condition {cite:tdv-bagy,tdv-islahat}.
