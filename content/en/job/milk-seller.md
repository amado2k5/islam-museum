## Summary

The milk-seller kept and poured one of the most perishable goods in the market, and the hisba manuals surround him with shop rules and fraud tests: the whitewashed shop, the palm-fibre stopper, the daily washing, the ban on skimmed or watered milk {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVII, p. 88}. In Istanbul the trade ran to whole guilds — a thousand buffalo milkmen, eight hundred sheep milkmen, and the dawn sellers of hot sweet milk {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 138-139, 190, pp. 141, 155}.

## A shop built against flies

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa's chapter on the milk-sellers is a lesson in dairy hygiene. Their vessels must be covered; the place of trade must be whitewashed and paved and its roof new, "for flies like places where there is milk" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVII, p. 88}. The mouths of the milk-jars take stoppers of clean palm-fibre, and the vats and other containers must be cleaned out daily with new palm-fibre and clean water "so that the milk shall not too soon deteriorate in hot weather" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVII, p. 88}. Everything the rule touches — the cover, the whitewash, the paving, the fibre plug — works on the same enemy: heat and the insects it brings.

## Rich milk, and the ban on water

The milk "must be rich and unskimmed; otherwise its taste and fatness are gone" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVII, p. 88}. Then the central rule: "the sale of milk diluted with water is utterly forbidden", on a tradition related by Rafi'i, and the test for it "is to throw in a sprig of marsh-lentil" {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVII, p. 88}. The chapter treats watered milk not as a lesser product but as a fraud — one the market inspector is expected to catch in the jar.

## The muhtasib's tests

Al-Shayzari gives the inspector two more instruments. The sprig of water-moss, dropped into milk mixed with water, "soaks up the water and leaves the milk" — it separates them {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 20, p. 78}. And the hair test: the muhtasib dips a hair into the milk and draws it out; "if none of the milk sticks to it, it has been adulterated with water, but if the milk sticks to it in droplets, then it is pure" {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 20, p. 78}. The same chapter forbids the dairy dealers to sell rancid or maggot-infested fats — the other end of the milk trade's shelf {cite:shayzari-nihaya-buckley ch. 20, pp. 78-79}.

## The milkmen of Istanbul

Evliya's procession separates the trade by animal and by hour. The milkmen of buffaloes, the Sujian Siuireh, are a thousand men with two hundred shops, their patron Sofian Thun — they milk the buffalo-cows and pass crying "Who buys buffalo-milk", with fine fat cattle {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 138, p. 141}. The milkmen of sheep's milk are eight hundred, with Jethro for patron and, in the Prophet's time, Abder Wornid Lebani, girded by Hamza and buried at the foot of Mount Ohod; they pass milking sheep and goats {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 139, p. 141}. Up in the mountains the two thousand aghil sheep-cotes, worked by four thousand young men, become after the day of Khizr "so many places of pleasure, where all kinds of dairy produce and cheese are to be got" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 135, p. 141}.

## Hot milk at dawn

The smallest entry is the most evocative: the merchants of warm milk, the Sudjian sukhanan, "have no shops, but holding their vessels like the former on the fire, they cry at the dawn of day in the streets, 'Hot and sweet milk'" {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 190, p. 155}. Shop rules, guild ranks and a dawn cry — the milk-seller's trade is visible at every scale, from the fibre plug in the jar to the thousand-man guild {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. XXVII, p. 88; evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 138-139, 190, pp. 141, 155}.
