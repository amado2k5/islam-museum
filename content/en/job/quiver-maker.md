## Summary

The tirkeşci made the quiver — the leather case that rides with the archer. Evliya marches three neighbouring guilds through the saddlers' section of the Istanbul procession: the common quiver-makers, the gudelej-makers and the jab-makers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 392-394, pp. 207-208}. Nicolle's account of the Mamluk horseman's kit shows what they made: thirty arrows in a leather quiver and a pouch with spare strings {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 162}.

## Three guilds, one craft

The quiver-makers (Tirkeshjian) are sixty men, with thirty shops. Their patrons are old Tokhtamish khan of the Jenguiz family, and Hamza, the Prophet's uncle, who received the girdle from the Prophet himself and is buried at the foot of mount Ohod at Medina {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 392, p. 207}.

The makers of a particular kind of quiver called Gudelej are twelve men, with two shops, patronised by a disciple of Hamza, who is buried at Aintab {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 393, pp. 207-208}.

The workmen of the Jab are thirty men, with ten shops, all Albanese. Jab is, like Gudelej, a particular kind of case for bow and arrows {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 394, p. 208}. Three small guilds for three forms of the same work: the open quiver, the gudelej, and the combined bow-and-arrow case.

## A leather case for thirty shafts

What the craft produced is written in the horseman's field kit. Describing the armament of a Mamluk-era cavalryman, Nicolle lists two bows, thirty arrows in a leather quiver, and a pouch with spare strings beside the lance, javelin, sword, dagger, mace or axe {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 162}. The quiver was working equipment, not finery: cut, sewn and stiffened to keep thirty shafts straight at the gallop.

## Local fashion in Kerman

The craft belonged to the armourers' bazaar beyond Istanbul: the armourers of Kerman, Elgood's essay records, forged all kinds of equipment for horsemen — bridle pieces, swords, spurs, arrow heads, quivers and other weapons made after the local fashion {cite:elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 p. 210}.

## Among the saddlers

The quiver guilds march in the saddlers' quarter, not the smiths': before them the felt belt-makers and saddle-cloth makers, after them the sumpter-saddlers and the makers of leather baskets and trunks {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 383-400, pp. 206-208}. The placement is right — the quiver-maker cut and stitched leather like a saddler, even though his case went to war.

## Not yet covered

The sources give the three guilds, their patrons and the quiver's place in the kit, but not the tanning or stitching detail, nor prices, nor how gudelej and jab differed in cut {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 392-394, pp. 207-208; elgood-islamic-arms-armour-1979 pp. 162, 210}.
