## Summary

The banna raised the walls of the city in stone, brick and wood and put up its houses, baths and mosques {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-96}. The hisba manuals place him in one chapter with the carpenters and sawyers and regulate his wage, his instruments and his honesty {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-96}. Evliya Çelebi marches him through Istanbul in the builders' train, three thousand strong behind the architects' establishment {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 603-647, pp. 229-232}, and in nineteenth-century Cairo the building trades were the largest sector of artisanal employment {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 46-47}.

## The agreed wage

Most workmen, Ibn al-Ukhuwwa says, make an agreement about a specified daily wage and then come late in the morning and depart before evening, so terms must be made to prevent it {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-95}. The men known as raqqasin, who run up and down the ladders, must wear drawers to cover themselves and must not leave the work until sunset {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. Timber hired for scaffolding that is cut must be replaced or paid for {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}.

## The true wall

If a workman does not use instruments such as angles, weights and lines to ensure that the building will be true and without departure from the perpendicular, he is responsible for any fault {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}. The masons must swear that they will take no bribes or gifts from the lime-burners and makers of gypsum who want their acquiescence when the lime is underburnt or of bad quality; the mark of well-burnt lime is that it turns yellow in the kiln before grinding and dries quickly once mixed with water, and no old lime may be used {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, p. 95}.

## The low estimate

Ibn al-Ukhuwwa names the trap: some masons, carpenters and painters assure the employer the work is easy and quote a low price, then, once the work has begun, demand more than he can bear, so that he is reduced to poverty and debt and compelled to sell the place before it is finished {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-95}. Six centuries later Chalcraft records the same mechanism inside the Cairo building guild: one member offers to take a thousand-pound job for seven hundred, another for five hundred, then three, until the price falls to two hundred; the builder sells his wife's gold and pawns his house to finish, and the proprietor then rejects the work point by point and accepts it only at a further ten percent off {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 50-51}.

## The builders' train

In Evliya's procession the establishment of the architects holds its divan in the chief's house near Weha, with seventy khalifehs each capable, Evliya says, of building a mosque like Aya Sofiyah or Suleimanieh; seventy kiyayas and chaushes patrol Constantinople daily to stop those who build onto the roads or injure their neighbours by building {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 603, pp. 229-230}. Their ancient patron was Habib-nejar, buried at Antioch whose castle he built, and their patron of the Prophet's time Abul-Kassim Abd-ul-Wahid en-Nejar, who renewed the building of the sacred precinct at Mecca, made two doors to the Ka'bah and lies in the cave of Lokman at Syene {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer sec. 603, p. 230}. Behind them march the carpenters four thousand, the builders three thousand, the wood-cutters, sawyers, masons, glass-cutters, plasterers, mortar-makers, chalk-makers, marble workmen, brick-makers, lead-coverers, paving-men, stone-cutters, whitewashers and the rest, down to the well-diggers and ten thousand day-labourers {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer secs. 604-647, pp. 230-231}. All these guilds pass on wagons or on foot with the instruments of their handicraft: the carpenters prepare wooden houses, the builders raise walls, the sawyers pass sawing {cite:evliya-seyahatname-hammer p. 231}.

## The nineteenth-century city

The census of 1868 suggests that construction and related trades occupied towards one-third of Cairo's artisanal workforce, the largest sector of artisanal activity, fed by Isma'il's projects, the Suez works and the new European-style town houses of the cotton boom {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 46-47}. Imported stone and marble was the second largest category of imports in the 1860s and 1870s, and all of it was transformed by masons in Egypt {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 47}. Chalcraft gives the household of Ahmad Abu Bakr, a master building engineer of Bulaq in 1868, whose brother was a builder (banna) and whose two nephews built while the third was a fiqi {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies p. 49}. The guilds still collected their dues: masters received the ghada and the taba' from builders and unskilled construction workers, and the stone mason Husayn Muhammad petitioned the Diwan of Public Works when his shaykh demanded half his daily wage and struck him {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 83-84}.

## Today

The builder's trade did not vanish: the census of 1897 still counted local subjects outnumbering the European migrants in bricklaying and stone masonry, and Egyptian masons had picked up stone sculpting, marble paving and building in European styles {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 47-48}. What changed was the frame around him: construction law in 1868, the Public Works diwan, the contractors and the labour market now do what the muhtasib's wage terms, plumb-line and oath once did {cite:ibn-ukhuwwa-maalim-levy ch. LXIX, pp. 94-95} {cite:chalcraft-striking-cabbies pp. 46-47, 83-85}.
