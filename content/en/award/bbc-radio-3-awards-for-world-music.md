## Summary
The BBC Radio 3 Awards for World Music were British awards sponsored by the station from 2003 to 2008, conceived by fRoots editor Ian Anderson on the model of the BBC Radio 2 Folk Awards; the critic Charlie Gillett chaired the panel until 2006. {cite:wikipedia-bbc-radio-3-awards-for-world-music-en} {cite:wikidata-bbc-radio-3-awards-for-world-music}

## How they worked
The categories were regional, Africa, Asia/Pacific, the Americas, Europe and the Middle East and North Africa, with others for newcomers, culture crossing, album of the year, the Critics Award and the Audience Award; several thousand industry experts nominated, a twelve-member jury chose the winners in every category but the Audience Award, and winners received a trophy called the "Planet". {cite:wikipedia-bbc-radio-3-awards-for-world-music-en} The BBC decided to axe the awards in March 2009, as The Guardian and The Sunday Times reported. {cite:wikipedia-bbc-radio-3-awards-for-world-music-en}

## In the museum
[[person/youssou-ndour|Youssou N'Dour]] won the Critics Award in 2005, which the article on [[work/egypt-youssou-ndour|Egypt]] describes as album of the year for that album. {cite:wikipedia-bbc-radio-3-awards-for-world-music-en} {cite:wikipedia-egypt-youssou-ndour-en} Middle East and North Africa winners included Kadim Al Sahir (2004), Khaled (2005) and Souad Massi (2006), and the 2006 Asia/Pacific award went to the Pakistani Sufi singer Sain Zahoor. {cite:wikipedia-bbc-radio-3-awards-for-world-music-en}
