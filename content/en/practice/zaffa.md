## Overview
## The zaffa
The zaffa is the bridal procession carrying the bride from her family's home to the groom's: a popular celebration across the Muslim world that has accompanied weddings since the medieval centuries. Its shape changes by country; its heart stays the same — declaring joy and carrying the bride in honour amid songs and ululations.

## Its forms
In Egypt the folk zaffa of drums and mizmar, the decorated horse, the bride on her kosha and the women's zaghrouta; in the Levant the 'arada al-shamiyya with sword and shield in the street procession; in the Gulf women in the nashl dress at women's singing while men walk a separate zaffa; in Morocco the nekkafa carry the bride in the 'ammariyya litter on their shoulders; in South Asia the rukhsati farewell sees the bride off from her parents' home amid tears and prayers; in Turkey the kına gecesi henna night then the decorated horn-blowing car convoy; in Indonesia the extended family procession behind the couple.

## Bounds and sunnah
The zaffa as a procession of celebration is lawful in the accepted view so long as it is free of what is prohibited — mixing, exposure, extravagance, and the music over which fatwas differ; it is in fact the publicising of the marriage God commanded to be publicised, in the Prophet's word "announce the nikah". The Companions had its like in their weddings: Aisha escorted a bride to an Ansari groom and the Prophet asked her, "Did you have no entertainment with you?" — the lawful tambourine and singing of the wedding house being among the marks of those days. {cite:wikipedia-takbir-en}
