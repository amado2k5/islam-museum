## Overview
Tarawih is the plural of tarwiha — the soul's rest, from raha; originally the name of any sitting. The sitting after each four rak'as in the nights of Ramadan was called tarwiha for the rest in it, then each four rak'as took the name by metonymy, and the prayer was called tarawih because they lengthened the standing and sat after every four to rest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10497}

## Definition
It is the qiyam of the month of Ramadan, two by two, with disagreement among the jurists over its number of rak'as and other questions. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10497}

## Sitting between tarwihas
The Hanafis state the worshipper may choose between each two tarwihas: sitting remembering God or silent, or praying a voluntary prayer alone — this sitting a recommended sunna. For the Hanbalis — and as the Maliki and Shafi'i wording suggests — he may rest after every four rak'as with a brief sitting, the practice of the salaf, though leaving it is harmless and the imam does not supplicate in it. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6405}

## The number of rak'as in al-Istidhkar, as its editor sums it up

Abd al-Mu'ti Qal'aji reports that [[person/ibn-abd-al-barr|Ibn Abd al-Barr]] opens the chapter on the night prayer of Ramadan in [[work/al-istidkar|al-Istidhkar]] with [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|Umar ibn al-Khattab]] gathering the people behind a single reciter, then turns to Malik's report of eleven rak'as, which he says was Umar's first practice: they recited suras of a hundred verses and finished near dawn, so Umar lightened the long standing and moved them to twenty-one rak'as with shorter recitation. {cite:istidhkar-qalaji-1993-v1 p.92} {cite:istidhkar-qalaji-1993-v1 p.93} According to the editor, Ibn Abd al-Barr holds that twenty-three is correct, supporting it with a report from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet prayed twenty rak'as and the witr in Ramadan, and says this is the view of the majority of scholars and most jurists; he then reviews the Followers' views and states his own choice of twenty-three rak'as including the witr. {cite:istidhkar-qalaji-1993-v1 p.93} This is the editor's summary of Ibn Abd al-Barr's discussion in volume 5 of his edition.
