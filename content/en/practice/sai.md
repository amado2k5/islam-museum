## Overview
Sa'y is walking or hastening with purpose; in usage, covering the distance between Safa and Marwa seven times back and forth after a tawaf, in the rites of hajj or 'umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9584}

## Its origin
Its prescription rests on Book and Sunna: "Safa and Marwa are among God's rites," and the Prophet's sa'y in his hajj with "do sa'y, for God has prescribed it for you"; the law modelled it on Hajar's running between them seven times seeking water for her son — "that is the people's sa'y between them." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9585}

## Ruling
The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis in their relied-upon view hold sa'y an integral of hajj and 'umra; prior tawaf is a condition of its validity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9585}

## For Explorers
Sa'i in the language is purpose, work, walking or running; technically it is covering the distance between Safa and Marwa seven times back and forth, after tawaf, in the rites of Hajj or Umrah. Its foundation is Book and Sunna: 'Indeed Safa and Marwa are among the rites of God', and the Prophet went between them in his Hajj saying 'Go (sa'u), for God has prescribed sa'i upon you'; the law laid it on the pattern of Hajar's seven-times going between them seeking water for her son — 'that is the people's sa'i between them'. The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis in the relied view hold it a pillar of Hajj and Umrah, the prior tawaf a condition of its validity.
