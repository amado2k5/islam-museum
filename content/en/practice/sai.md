## Overview
Sa'y is walking or hastening with purpose; in usage, covering the distance between Safa and Marwa seven times back and forth after a tawaf, in the rites of hajj or 'umra. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9584}

## Its origin
Its prescription rests on Book and Sunna: "Safa and Marwa are among God's rites," and the Prophet's sa'y in his hajj with "do sa'y, for God has prescribed it for you"; the law modelled it on Hajar's running between them seven times seeking water for her son — "that is the people's sa'y between them." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9585}

## Ruling
The Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis in their relied-upon view hold sa'y an integral of hajj and 'umra; prior tawaf is a condition of its validity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.9585}

## For Scholars
Sa'i in the language is purpose, work, walking or running; technically it is covering the distance between Safa and Marwa seven times back and forth, after tawaf, in the rites of Hajj or Umrah — per the Kuwaiti fiqh encyclopaedia. Its foundation is Book and Sunna: 'Indeed Safa and Marwa are among the rites of God', and the Prophet went between them in his Hajj saying 'Go (sa'u), for God has prescribed sa'i upon you'; the law laid it on the pattern of Hajar's going between them seven times seeking water for her son — 'that is the people's sa'i between them'. On its ruling the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis in the relied view hold it a pillar of Hajj and Umrah, and the prior tawaf a condition of its validity.

## The status of the sa'y in al-Nawawi's al-Majmu'

[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] sets out in [[work/al-majmu-sharh-al-muhadhdhab|al-Majmu']] the scholars' views on the status of the sa'y between Safa and Marwa:

:::perspectives
- **Sunni, Shafi'i and those who agree: a pillar without which hajj and umra are incomplete**: it cannot be made good by a sacrifice, and if even one step of it remains the pilgrimage is incomplete; so held A'isha, Malik, Ishaq, Abu Thawr, Dawud and Ahmad in one report; they cite the hadith of Safiyya bint Shayba that women heard the Prophet say in the course of the sa'y that the sa'y has been prescribed for them, which al-Nawawi says al-Daraqutni and al-Bayhaqi narrated with a good chain. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108}
- **Sunni, Hanafi: Abu Hanifa**: obligatory but not a pillar; leaving it is made good by a sacrifice; the sounder report from Ahmad is the same. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108}
- **Sunni: those who held it voluntary**: al-Nawawi reports this from Ibn Mas'ud, Ubayy ibn Ka'b, Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr, Anas and Ibn Sirin, with a report from Ahmad that nothing is owed for leaving it; they cite the removal of blame in [[passage/quran-2-158|al-Baqara 158]]. Al-Nawawi gives A'isha's answer to Urwa ibn al-Zubayr that the verse came down because the Ansar felt uneasy about going between Safa and Marwa. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.109}
- **Other views he reports from Ibn al-Mundhir**: a sacrifice is owed, from al-Hasan, Qatada and al-Thawri; from Tawus, that leaving four circuits requires a sacrifice and fewer half a sa' for each circuit; and two reports from Ata'. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.108}
:::

He notes that a sa'y before tawaf is not valid for the Shafi'is and the majority, reporting that Ata', some hadith scholars and Dawud held it valid; and that beginning at Safa is a condition for them and the majority, while the well-known view of Abu Hanifa is that it is not. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.109}
