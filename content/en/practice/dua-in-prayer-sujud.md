## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the chapter on the words of prostration: the glorification and supplications reported for it, what is said about asking in prostration, and then the words for the prostration of recitation. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 55}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
He reports from Hudhayfa in Muslim that he said «سبحان ربي الأعلى», Glory to my Lord the Most High, from Aisha in the two Sahihs «سبحانك اللهم ربنا وبحمدك اللهم اغفر لي», and from Ali in Muslim «اللهم لك سجدت وبك آمنت ولك أسلمت سجد وجهي للذي خلقه وصوره» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 55}. He reports from Ibn Abbas in Muslim that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said that in prostration one should strive in supplication, for it is fitting that it be answered, and from Abu Hurayra that a servant is nearest his Lord when prostrating, so he should supplicate much {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 56}. From Abu Hurayra he gives «اللهم اغفر لي ذنبي كله دقه وجله وأوله وآخره وعلانيته وسره», forgive all my sin, small and great, first and last, open and hidden {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 56}. For the prostration of recitation he says one should say what is said in the prayer's prostration, and gives from Aisha in the Sunan «سجد وجهي للذي خلقه وشق سمعه وبصره بحوله وقوته», which al-Tirmidhi called sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 57}. He also gives a supplication asking that the prostration be stored up as reward, a burden be lifted and it be accepted as from Dawud, which al-Tirmidhi reports from Ibn Abbas by a good chain {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 57}.
