## Summary
What al-Nawawi gives at the head of the Book of Comprehensive Supplications in al-Adhkar: supplications recommended at all times and tied to no hour or state, from Sahih Muslim and the two Sahihs, such as the asking for guidance, piety, restraint and sufficiency. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 387}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says the purpose of this book is to give important supplications recommended at all times and tied to no hour or state, and that the subject is too wide to exhaust {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 387}. He begins with the merit of supplication: by sound chains in the Sunan, from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, «الدعاء هو العبادة», supplication is worship, which al-Tirmidhi graded good and sound; and from Sunan Abi Dawud, from Aisha, by a chain he calls strong, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] liked the comprehensive supplications and left the rest {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 387}. From al-Tirmidhi's book, from Abu Hurayra, that one who wishes God to answer him in hardship should supplicate much in ease {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 387}. He then sets out the supplications. From Sahih Muslim, from Ibn Mas'ud: «اللهم إني أسألك الهدى والتقى والعفاف والغنى», O God, I ask You for guidance, piety, restraint and sufficiency. From Tariq ibn Ashyam, what was taught to one who entered Islam: «اللهم اغفر لي، وارحمني، واهدني، وعافني، وارزقني». From Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As: «اللهم يا مصرف القلوب صرف قلوبنا على طاعتك», O God, turner of hearts, turn our hearts to Your obedience {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 388}. From the two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayra, the seeking of refuge from the strain of trial, from being overtaken by misery, from an ill decree and from the gloating of enemies {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 388}. From Sahih Muslim, from Aisha: «اللهم إني أعوذ بك من شر ما عملت ومن شر ما لم أعمل»; from Ibn Umar: «اللهم إني أعوذ بك من زوال نعمتك، وتحول عافيتك، وفجأة نقمتك وجميع سخطك», refuge from the passing of Your favour, the turning of the well-being You give, the suddenness of Your vengeance and all Your displeasure; and from Ali: «اللهم اهدني وسددني» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 389}. Of the supplication taught to Abu Bakr, in the two Sahihs, he says that although it came for use in the prayer it is good and recommended everywhere {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 388}.
