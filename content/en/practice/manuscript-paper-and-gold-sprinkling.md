## Summary
Norah Titley notes that the finest paper in the world was said to come from Samarkand, that [[person/sultan-ali-mashhadi|Sultan Ali Mashhadi]] (d. 1520), master of nasta'liq, wrote of its suitability for fine calligraphy, and that vellum continued to be used for Qur'ans some two hundred years after papermaking became known. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 239} Sultan Ali also said no paper was better than Chinese for the calligrapher's ink and for gold, especially coloured paper. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 239}

## Coloured paper
A British Library example is an anthology copied by Sharaf al-Din Husayn, the royal scribe, at Shamakha in 1468 on Chinese paper in indigo, purple, mauve, apricot, sage green, brown and ochre, sprinkled with large flecks of gold. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 239} In the sixteenth century Persian papermakers produced coloured Bukhara paper as a substitute for Chinese, and the finest paper for Shah Tahmasp's manuscripts was made at Tabriz. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 240}

## Sizing, polishing and sprinkling
Sizing was done with egg-white in Iran and with a starch such as rice-water in India and Kashmir; polishing with crystal in Iran, agate in India and onyx in Turkey; gold had to be applied while the sheets were still wet from size and then burnished. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 240} {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 241} Gold was sprinkled in two ways: through a cloth bag whose mesh set the size of the gold-leaf pieces, or by tapping a brushful of gold paint so as to spatter it. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 241}

## In India
Paper was introduced into India by the Muslims but began to replace palm leaves only in the late fourteenth century; early Mughal paper was probably imported from Tabriz until mills using bamboo, flax, jute or cotton were set up, and Kashmir became famous for its thin white paper. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 241}
