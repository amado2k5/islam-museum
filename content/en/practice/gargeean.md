## Overview
## Gargee'an
Gargee'an is the mid-Ramadan children's festival of the Gulf and Iraq: on the night of the 13th, 14th or 15th of the month children dress in traditional clothes and go door to door in groups singing, and neighbours hand out sweets, nuts and snacks in bags tied round their necks. The word most likely comes from qarqa'a — the clatter of the tins and pots as the sweets are served.

## Names and places
It is qarqī'ān in Bahrain, Kuwait and parts of Saudi Arabia (al-Ahsa and Qatif especially), qarangao or qaranqasho in Basra and Qatar, majeena or kareesh'aan elsewhere, with a twin observance in southern Iran. Sunnis and Shias celebrate it alike; the Shia associate it with the birth of Imam al-Hasan ibn Ali in mid-Ramadan.

## The songs and beyond
Children gather in little choirs before each house singing verses that ask God to keep the family's youngest in health and its mother in happiness — the longer they sing, the more they receive. The custom spreads neighbourliness and gives children a feast in the middle of the fasting month; today supermarkets and municipalities compete over gargee'an promotions, and Gulf cities host official celebrations. {cite:wikipedia-gargeean-en}

## In more detail
The name shifts across the Gulf: gargee'an/qarqi'an in Kuwait and eastern Saudi Arabia, garga'oon in Bahrain and Qatar, haq al-layla in the Emirates (where it falls on 15 Sha'ban instead), qaranqashoh in Oman, and majina in Iraq. Folklore ties it to the birth of al-Hasan ibn Ali on 15 Ramadan, but the earliest textual attestations of the chanting and nut-collecting are late-19th and early-20th century — Lorimer's 1908 Gazetteer of the Persian Gulf and the memoirs of Sayf Marzuq al-Shamlan {cite:wikipedia-gargeean-en}.
