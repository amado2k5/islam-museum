## Summary
The prostrations of forgetfulness are two prostrations a worshipper makes for an addition, omission or doubt in his [[practice/salah|prayer]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 429} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437} [[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] gives them a run of chapters in the Book of Prayer: forgetfulness in the two prostrations; one who prays five units; doubt between two and three; those who say one completes on one's strongest belief; those who say after the salam; one who rises after two units without the testimony; one who forgets the testimony while sitting; and the testimony and salam of the two prostrations. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 506}

## The report of Dhu al-Yadayn
[[person/muhammad-ibn-sirin|Muhammad ibn Sirin]] relates from [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] that the Prophet led one of the two afternoon prayers, the noon or the mid-afternoon, prayed two units and gave the salam, and went to a beam at the front of the mosque; the hasty went out saying, "The prayer has been shortened." Abu Bakr and 'Umar were among the people but held back from speaking to him in awe; a man whom the Prophet called Dhu al-Yadayn said, "Did you forget, or has the prayer been shortened?" He said, "I did not forget, and it has not been shortened." The man said, "Rather, you forgot." He asked the people, "Has Dhu al-Yadayn spoken truly?" They signalled yes; so he prayed the remaining two units and gave the salam, then said "God is greatest" and prostrated as long as his usual prostration or longer, then rose with "God is greatest" and prostrated likewise. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 427-429} Ibn Sirin was asked whether he gave the salam after the forgetfulness prostrations and said, "I did not retain that from Abu Hurayra, but I was told that [[person/imran-ibn-husayn|'Imran ibn Husayn]] said: then he gave the salam." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 429} Abu Dawud notes that only Hammad ibn Zayd gives the words "they signalled". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 429}

## What al-Khattabi draws from the report
- Whoever says "I did not do it", having done it forgetfully, is not a liar. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 428}
- Whoever speaks in his prayer by mistake, or not knowing that he is in prayer, does not invalidate it; Dhu al-Yadayn spoke at a time of abrogation, additions and reductions to the prayer, in a situation where he could suppose the prayer was over. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 428}
- Al-Khattabi rejects the claim that the report was abrogated by the prohibition of speech in prayer: speech was forbidden in Mecca, while this event took place in Medina, since its narrator Abu Hurayra was a late convert and it was also related by 'Imran ibn Husayn, whose emigration was late; and the "speech" of Abu Bakr and 'Umar was a gesture, as in Hammad's version. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 428}
- Whoever forgets several times in one prayer is covered by two prostrations, the view of most jurists; al-Awza'i and al-Majishun, Malik's companion, are reported to have required two prostrations for each lapse. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 428}

## The four reports on which the schools built
Al-Khattabi says that the matter rests on the reports of [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Mas'ud]] (weighing the likelier case and prostrating after the salam), [[person/abu-said-al-khudri|Abu Sa'id al-Khudri]] (discarding the doubt, building on certainty and prostrating before the salam), Dhu al-Yadayn (the salam after two units, then prostrating after the salam) and Ibn Buhayna (rising after two units without the testimony, then prostrating before the salam), and that the jurists' schools branched out from them. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437} Al-Khattabi gives Abu Sa'id's report with a full chain through Sulayman ibn Bilal, from Zayd ibn Aslam, from 'Ata' ibn Yasar, to answer those who weakened it because Malik transmitted it without the Companion, observing that Malik is known to transmit in this way reports he holds with full chains. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 438}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Hanafi): the "people of opinion".** Following Ibn Mas'ud's report, the worshipper weighs the likelier case — for them, the stronger likelihood — and prostrates after the salam; this is for one often beset by doubt, while one doubting for the first time starts the prayer again. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437}

**Sunni (Maliki): Malik ibn Anas.** After the salam for an addition, following Dhu al-Yadayn's report; before it for an omission, following Ibn Buhayna's. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437}

**Sunni (Hanbali): Ahmad ibn Hanbal.** Each report is used in its own case: one who falls back on certainty prostrates before the salam, one who falls back on weighing prostrates after it. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437}

**Sunni (Shafi'i): al-Shafi'i, preferred by al-Khattabi.** One builds on certainty and prostrates before the salam, reading the general reports through Abu Sa'id's explicit one; al-Khattabi relates from al-Zuhri that prostrating before the salam was the later practice. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 437-438}
:::
