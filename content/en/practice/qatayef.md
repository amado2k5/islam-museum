## Overview
## Qatayef
Qatayef is the Ramadan pancake of the Levant and Egypt: a runny batter of flour, semolina and water poured on a hot griddle that bubbles on one face only — never flipped — then filled with crushed nuts in sugar and cinnamon, or white cheese or qashta cream, folded into a half-moon or pouch, fried or baked, and soaked in hot syrup. The bubbly face and the smooth back are what let it hold a filling.

## History and season
Ibn Sirin described it to al-Tabari in the third Islamic century among the sweets of the feast, and it stands in the medieval Baghdad and Andalusian cookbooks; it remains Ramadan's synonym in Damascus, Aleppo, Jerusalem and Cairo: the sweet shops do not open the fasting month without stacking qatayef trays in the window, people queue for it before maghrib, and some is sold raw for home stuffing.

## Its kinds
Qatayef asafiri — the small fried bird's-mouth ones with walnut or hazelnut — the baked cheese-and-cream qatayef, Aleppo's qatayef baladiyya with qashta eaten cold without frying as a sweet iftar course, Egypt's honey-and-cream versions, the Ottoman twin taş kadayıf in Turkey — and in Lebanon and Palestine a neighbourhood's pastry standing is measured by them. {cite:wikipedia-qatayef-en}
