## The practice
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy describes tahnik as chewing a date and then rubbing it on the newborn's palate with a clean finger. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:30}

## Its place
The Sheikh mentions tahnik in the context of aqiqa and the shaving of the newborn's hair, and says it is done on the seventh day after birth (needs_source: the day is spoken once in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:00}

## From the lectures: tahnik on the seventh day
The speaker says a newborn is given tahnik on the seventh day after birth: a date is chewed and then rubbed on the child's palate with a finger {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:00}.
He gives a reason that doctors say teeth have sockets around the gums and that this helps teeth grow quickly; he attributes this to doctors without a source, so it is not adopted here (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:30}.
In the same passage he mentions a claim about Ibrahim's circumcision after eighty; the sentence is garbled and is not written here (needs_source).
