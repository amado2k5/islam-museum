## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar in the chapters on the kunya: addressing people of merit by it, the kunya of a child or of one with no child, the difference of view on taking the kunya Abu al-Qasim, giving a kunya to a non-Muslim, and kunyas of the Abu so-and-so and Umm so-and-so pattern. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 294}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says the chapter on the lawfulness of kunyas is too well known for any report to be cited, and that the manner is to address people of merit by kunya, in speech, in letters and in transmission, and for a man not to mention his own kunya unless he is known by nothing else {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 294}. He says [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] was given the kunya Abu al-Qasim after his son al-Qasim, the eldest of his sons {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 294}. For one with no child he reports in the two Sahihs from Anas that the Prophet used to say to his small brother «يا أبا عمير ما فعل النغير», O Abu Umayr, what became of the little bird {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 294} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 295}; and in Abu Dawud, by sound chains, that Aisha said her companions all had kunyas and he told her «فاكتني بابنك عبد الله», take your kunya from your son Abd Allah, and she was called Umm Abd Allah {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 295}. On the prohibition of the kunya Abu al-Qasim he reports in the two Sahihs «سموا باسمي ولا تكنوا بكنيتي», and sets out three positions: al-Shafi'i that it is lawful for no one, Malik that it is lawful and the prohibition was for the Prophet's lifetime, and a third that it is unlawful for one named Muhammad and lawful for others {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 295}. He then says the people's unbroken practice strengthens Malik's view, since the cause of the prohibition was the Jews' use of that kunya and their calling out to harass, which has gone {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 296}. He says a non-Muslim may be given a kunya when known by it alone or when naming him would cause harm, citing a verse where a man is called by his kunya and Usama ibn Zayd's report in the two Sahihs; otherwise only the name is used, as in the Prophet's letter to Heraclius {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 296}. Kunyas such as Abu so-and-so's father or Umm so-and-so are without restriction, and Uthman ibn Affan had three {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 296}.
