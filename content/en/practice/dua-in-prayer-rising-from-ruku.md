## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the chapter on what is said on lifting the head from bowing and standing upright: the words «سمع الله لمن حمده», the praise and the longer form, with the reports from the two Sahihs. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 54}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says the sunna is for the worshipper to say «سمع الله لمن حمده», God hears whoever praises Him, as the head is lifted, and on standing upright to say «ربنا لك الحمد», Our Lord, to You is the praise, and the praise that follows it. He gives a long form that includes the filling of the heavens, the earth and whatever else God wills {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 54}. He reports from Abu Hurayra in the two Sahihs that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «سمع الله لمن حمده» when he raised his back from bowing and then «ربنا لك الحمد» standing, and that some versions have «ولك الحمد» with the conjunction, both being good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 54}. From Rifa'a ibn Rafi' in al-Bukhari he reports that a man behind the Prophet said «ربنا ولك الحمد حمدا كثيرا طيبا مباركا فيه», and the Prophet said he had seen thirty-odd angels racing to write it down first {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 54}. He says combining these is recommended, and that one who shortens should not go below «سمع الله لمن حمده ربنا لك الحمد» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 55}. He says this dhikr is a sunna and not required, and that leaving it is disliked {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 55}.
