## Summary
What al-Nawawi adds after the hadiths of condolence: reports of patience at the loss of children, the words of early scholars in consoling, and a section on the great plagues in Islamic history. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 150}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi gives from al-Nasa'i's book, by a good chain, from Qurra ibn Iyas, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] consoled a man for his son and asked whether he would rather enjoy him all his life or find him at every gate of Paradise, ahead of him, opening it for him; the man chose the second, and the Prophet told him it was his {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 150} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 151}. He says the reports on this subject are many and that he gives a few so the book does not lack a hint of them: al-Shafi'i's letter to Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, who grieved hard for his son {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 151}; Ibn Umar laughing at his son's grave, to humble Satan {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 151} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 152} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 152}. He then gives a section on the plagues in Islam, there so that one may bear one's own loss patiently by what others suffered. From al-Mada'ini he names five great plagues, the first that of Shirawayh in al-Mada'in in the sixth year of the Hijra, then Amwas in Syria in Umar's time, in which twenty-five thousand died {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 153}; then those of 69, the Plague of the Maidens in 87 and one in 131 {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 153}. He adds from Ibn Qutayba that no plague ever struck Medina or Mecca {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 153}.
