## Overview
## Ma'amoul
Ma'amoul is the Levant's Eid shortbread: semolina or flour worked with ghee and scented with orange-blossom or rose water, mahlab and mastic, filled with dates kneaded with butter and cardamom, or pistachios or walnuts ground with sugar, pressed in carved wooden moulds (qalb) whose patterns differ by filling, baked to pale — not browned — and dusted with powdered sugar.

## Two feasts, one mould
In the Levant Muslims bake ma'amoul for Eid al-Fitr and Christians for Easter — 'id al-nur' — so that neighbours share the mould, the oven and the recipe, until the cookie became the sign of a spring that holds two feasts side by side. Women prepare it in the last two weeks of Ramadan in family gatherings where the exact proportions of semolina to ghee pass from hand to hand.

## Moulds and names
The date ma'amoul is flat and round with crosshatch lines, the walnut oval or crescent, the pistachio a dome or shell — and Damascenes call the pistachio 'al-as' and the walnut 'karkar'. Aleppo's ma'amoul al-karb is pinched with a hand tweezer into pinprick embroidery — an art called 'karb' all its own — carried by pilgrims and merchants into Egypt and the Gulf. {cite:wikipedia-maamoul-en}
