## Overview
## Eid greetings
The exchange of Eid greetings is an inherited custom with no required formula: people say "Eid Mubarak", "Eidkum Mubarak", "taqabbal Allahu minna wa minkum" ("may God accept it from us and you"), "kull 'am wa antum bi-khayr" ("may you be well every year"); the traditionists report the Companions exchanging it. South Asia says "Eid Mubarak"; Turkey "Bayramınız kutlu olsun"; Indonesia "Selamat Idul Fitri"; West Africa "sankey salaa" and "barka da sallah".

## How it is said
The forms tell the customs: handshake and embrace after the Eid prayer among Arabs; hand to brow or heart in South Asia; kissing the elder's hand in Turkey and the Balkans; the triple hug in Egypt and the Gulf; calls and messages for the far. Iraqis say "taqabbal Allahu ta'atakum"; Arabia "asakum min 'awwadah" ("may you be among its returners"); the Levant "ja'alana Allahu wa iyyakum min al-maqbulin".

## After the prayer
The deepest part of the greeting is its timing: leaving the musalla, strangers shake hands, neighbours and the distant embrace, and a year of rancour cools. Then the family visits begin in order — the younger calling first on the elder — hospitality is offered, eidi is given, and the meeting itself becomes worship when the intention is sound. {cite:wikipedia-takbir-en}
