## Summary
Washing, shrouding, praying over, carrying and burying the dead belong to the Muslim's rights over fellow Muslims, detailed by jurists in the books of funerals. Washing the deceased is a communal obligation: if some perform it, it falls from the rest. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6469}

## What precedes the washing
Once death is certain, the jaw is bound and the eyes closed — "when you attend your dead, close the eyes" — the joints are loosened, the whole body covered with a cloth and laid on something raised, and preparation is hastened, for "a Muslim's corpse should not be kept back among his family"; one who dies suddenly is left until death is certain. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6464} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6465} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6466}

## Announcing the death
It is recommended to inform the deceased's neighbours and friends so they may fulfil his right of prayer and supplication; the na'y of the Jahiliyya — a crier proclaiming the death in gatherings — is prohibited by agreement. Ibn al-'Arabi framed three cases: informing family and the righteous is sunna, summoning for vainglory is disliked, and announcing with lamentation is forbidden. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6465} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6466}

## Shrouding
Jurists agreed that shrouding the deceased so as to cover him is a communal obligation; its details belong to the chapter of takfin. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6469}

## Quran recitation beside the body
The Hanafis disliked reciting Quran beside the body before washing; the Shafi'is held nothing is recited before burial lest it distract from hastening the preparation, while after burial it is recommended for them; Ibn Hibban read "recite Ya-Sin over your dead" as referring to the dying person. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6467}
