## Overview
Iftar is entering the time of breaking the fast when it becomes lawful to break it: "when the night advances from here, the day retreats from here, and the sun sets, the faster has broken his fast." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3088}

## Rulings
Its default for one obliged to fast is prohibition; it can be obligatory where an impediment exists — mortal illness, menstruation, lochia, or the forbidden days such as the two Eids; disliked for the traveller per the Malikis and for one who began a voluntary fast without excuse; recommended for an excuse such as honouring a guest; and permissible for the sick who fear worsening. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3088} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3089}

## Effects
Whoever breaks without excuse an obligatory fast requiring consecutiveness — like expiation fasts — interrupts the sequence and must restart; with excuse he builds on what passed. Make-up is agreed for obligatory fasts and disputed for voluntary ones. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.3089}

## In al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar
In the Book of the Remembrances of Fasting al-Nawawi gives a chapter on what is said on breaking the fast {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 190}. From the Sunan of Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i, from Ibn Umar, he reports that when [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] broke his fast he said: «ذهب الظمأ، وابتلت العروق، وثبت الأجر إن شاء الله تعالى», the thirst has gone, the veins are moistened and the reward is assured, God willing; he notes how the word for thirst is spelled and read {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 190}. From Sunan Abi Dawud, from Mu'adh ibn Zuhra, to whom it was reported, that on breaking his fast he said: «اللهم لك صمت، وعلى رزقك أفطرت», O God, for You I fasted and on Your provision I break my fast; al-Nawawi says Abu Dawud relates it in this form, without a Companion in its chain {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 190}. From Ibn al-Sunni's book, from Mu'adh ibn Zuhra: «الحمد لله الذي أعانني فصمت، ورزقني فأفطرت»; and from Ibn Abbas: «اللهم لك صمنا، وعلى رزقك أفطرنا، فتقبل منا إنك أنت السميع العليم» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 190}. From the books of Ibn Majah and Ibn al-Sunni, from Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As, that the fasting person has at his breaking of the fast a supplication that is not turned away, and that he himself used to say then: «اللهم إني أسألك برحمتك التي وسعت كل شيء أن تغفر لي» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 190}. In this chapter al-Nawawi states no judgement on the chains of these reports beyond describing Mu'adh ibn Zuhra's as lacking a Companion {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 190}.
