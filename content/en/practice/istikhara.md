## Overview
Istikhara is asking for the good choice in a matter: directing one's resolve to what is chosen with God and better, through the prescribed prayer or the reported supplication. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2322}

## Related terms
It is distinguished from tiyara (taking a bad omen), fa'l (a good omen), ru'ya (the dream), istiqsam with azlam arrows which is forbidden, and from probing the unseen through the mushaf, geomancy, or lots. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2322} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2323}

## Ruling and form
The scholars agree it is a sunna; the Prophet taught it for all matters like a sura of the Quran: "when one of you resolves on a matter, let him pray two non-obligatory rak'as, then say…" — and "among the felicity of Adam's son is his seeking God's choice." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2323}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy holds that istikhara rests on God's knowledge: in its supplication the servant asks God to choose for him by His knowledge, to decree for him by His power, and to grant from His bounty, so that he leaves his own might and strength for God's. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:13:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:13:30} He says that istikhara has nothing to do with the heart feeling at ease or constricted, nor with the matter becoming easy or hard, and that the one who seeks it is as if saying to his Lord: You choose for me; this is his view. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:14:30} He presents it as what Islam set in place of a pre-Islamic custom in which the Arabs read the flight of birds before a journey, calling a bird that flew to the right sanih and one that flew to the left barih. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep01 00:16:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh explains istikhara for anyone unsure about marrying off a daughter or son, buying a house or car, or selling land: perform ablution and pray two rak'as with that intention, reciting, he says, al-Fatiha and al-Kafirun, then al-Fatiha and al-Ikhlas, then say the supplication and name the matter. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep29 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep29 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep29 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep29 00:06:00} He holds that there is no need to seek guidance thirty times, once is enough, and that it is not tied to a dream: if the heart is at ease and the matter becomes easy, go ahead; if the heart is constricted and the matter difficult, leave it. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep29 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep29 00:06:30}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme "Liqa' al-Iman"
In episode 24 of [[work/likaa-al-iman|Liqa' al-Iman]] [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|al-Sha'rawi]] says istikhara is asking for the better of two, so it is not made between something forbidden and something lawful, but between two things both good and lawful. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep24 00:07:30} He concludes that what matters is that both be good, so that whichever comes about is good. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep24 00:08:30}

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Ish al-Lahza'
In the twenty-seventh episode of his programme [[work/ich-allahda|'Ish al-Lahza']], on the moment of perplexity, [[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] makes the prayer for guidance one side of the 'pyramid' on which a decision rests: seeking the guidance of the All-Knowing, consulting a loving and sincere person, and thinking. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:17:30} He says one should seek guidance in every decision, however small, and recalls that the Companions said the Prophet taught them istikhara in all matters as he taught them a sura of the Qur'an; from this he concludes that one should take no decision without speaking to one's Lord and consulting Him through His knowledge. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:18:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:18:30} He says man is ignorant of the future while God's knowledge encompasses everything, so one must know the istikhara supplication by heart. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:19:00} He adds that the one consulted holds a trust and that solutions are a provision from God, so a sincere friend may be given an answer that the expert lacks. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:19:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep27 00:19:30} He recalls the istikhara supplication again in the twenty-second episode, while explaining God's name al-Qadir. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep22 00:22:00}
