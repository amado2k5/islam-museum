## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in two chapters: the urging to supplicate and seek forgiveness in the later half of every night, and supplication through all the hours of the night in hope of meeting the hour of answer. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 99}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi reports in the two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayra, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said that our Lord comes down each night to the nearest heaven when the last third of the night remains and says: who calls on Me that I may answer him, who asks Me that I may give him, who seeks My forgiveness that I may forgive him {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 99}. He gives a version in Muslim in which this is when the first third of the night has passed, and it continues so until the dawn brightens, and another in which it is when half the night or two thirds have passed {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 99}. In Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, from Amr ibn Abasa, the Prophet said the Lord is nearest to His servant in the last depth of the night, so if you can be among those who remember God at that hour, be so; al-Nawawi adds that al-Tirmidhi called it good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 99}. In the chapter on supplicating through all the hours of the night, he reports in Sahih Muslim that Jabir ibn Abd Allah heard the Prophet say that in the night there is an hour that no Muslim man meets while asking God for any good of this world or the next except that God gives it to him, and that this is every night {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 100}. The chapter's own heading speaks of supplicating through all the hours of the night in hope of meeting that hour {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 100}.
