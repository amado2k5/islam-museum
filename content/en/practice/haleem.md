## Overview
## Haleem and harees
Haleem in Urdu and harees in Arabic is the slow dish of wheat and meat: crushed wheat or jareesh cooked with meat — lamb, beef or chicken — fat and hours over a gentle fire, stirred without pause by a wooden beater until the meat dissolves into the grain into a dense porridge of dough-like body, crowned with fried onion, ginger, lemon and coriander.

## Its history and its lands
Its root is the Arabic 'harees' described in the tenth-century Baghdad cookbook Kitab al-Tabikh of al-Warraq — wheat and meat cooked until they merge — carried by trade, soldiers and migrants into India, where it became 'haleem' proper, and into Turkey as 'keşkek', Iran as 'halim bademjan' with aubergine, and the Caucasus under its own names. Its greatest throne today is Hyderabad Deccan: in Ramadan a whole city stands to the haleem season, the great houses of 'Madina' and 'Shah Ghouse' fire their cauldrons, and the dish itself carries a registered geographical indication as Hyderabadi haleem.

## Its season and customs
It is the faster's dish — filling slowly, feeding strongly: eaten at iftar after the dates and drink, the haleem houses run long queues before maghrib, and great cauldrons of it are given to the poor at mosques, Husayniyyas and quarters — in Hyderabad, and Hyderabad alone among cities, it has become the month's institution as kahk is Cairo's. {cite:wikipedia-haleem-en}
