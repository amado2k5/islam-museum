## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the Book of Comprehensive Supplications on seeking refuge and asking for well-being and steadfastness, from the Sunan of Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i and from the two Sahihs. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 390}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi continues with seeking refuge and asking for well-being. From the two Sahihs, from Ibn Abbas, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «اللهم لك أسلمت، وبك آمنت، وعليك توكلت، وإليك أنبت», I submit to You, believe in You and rely on You {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 390}. From Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah, by sound chains, from Aisha, refuge from «فتنة النار، وعذاب النار، ومن شر الغنى والفقر», the Fire and the evil of wealth and poverty; al-Tirmidhi calls it good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 390}. From Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i, by a sound chain, from Abu Hurayra, refuge from hunger, «فإنه بئس الضجيع», an evil bedfellow, and from betrayal {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 391}. From al-Tirmidhi, from Ali, what the Prophet taught a slave who could not pay his contract: «اللهم اكفني بحلالك عن حرامك، وأغنني بفضلك عمن سواك», sufficiency in the lawful instead of the forbidden; he calls it good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 391}. From Umm Salama, his most frequent supplication, «يا مقلب القلوب ثبت قلبي على دينك», turner of hearts, make my heart firm in Your religion {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 392}. From Anas and from al-Abbas, that the best supplication is to ask God for well-being in this world and the next {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 392}. And from Sa'd, the call of Dhu al-Nun in the whale's belly, by which no Muslim ever called on God but was answered; al-Hakim calls its chain sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 392}.
