## Overview
For al-Sha'rawi, the mahr is a right of the wife and the wage for intimacy in marriage. He reads the word nihla in the verse as a matter of religious conscience rather than a judicial ruling {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2009}.

## The wife's right
He holds that the wife is owed the mahr. Although the marriage gives both spouses enjoyment and a shared purpose, God made the mahr a gift of honour to women {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2009}.

## Waiver given willingly
He holds that a wife may give up part of her mahr if she does so willingly. That strengthens the marriage, and a guardian must not take any of it out of shame {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2010}.

## An account attributed to Ali
He relates that a man complained of an ailment to Ali ibn Abi Talib, who told him to take two dirhams from his wife's mahr, buy honey and dissolve it in rainwater, citing Qur'anic words on blessed water, healing honey and the mahr {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2010}{cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p. 2011}.
