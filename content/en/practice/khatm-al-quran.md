## Overview
Completing a recital of the whole Qur'an and marking the khatma; a Ramadan custom and one practised in memorisation circles across the world. The early generations differed in how quickly they finished: some completed it once a day and night, some twice, some reached three times, and some once a month {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.12687}.

## How quickly to finish
Al-Nawawi held that the right pace differs by person: one whose subtle reflections yield rare insights should confine himself to what secures full understanding; one occupied with spreading knowledge or other pressing affairs of religion and the community should confine himself to what does not detract from his charge; and anyone else may read as much as he can without sliding into boredom or hasty gabble. A group of the early authorities disliked completing it every day and night {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.12687}. Abu'l-Walid al-Baji noted that the Prophet instructed Abd Allah ibn Amr to finish in seven days or three, and Malik, asked about a man finishing every night, said "how excellent that is — the Qur'an leads to every good" {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.12687} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.12688}.

## Supplication after completion
Supplication is recommended upon finishing the Qur'an, on the report — with weakness in its isnad — from al-'Irbad ibn Sariya that "whoever completes the Qur'an has an answered prayer"; and it is recommended to begin a new recital right after finishing, on the hadith "the most beloved of deeds to God is the one who alights and moves on — who traverses the Qur'an from its first to its last, moving on whenever he settles" — whose isnad al-Tirmidhi found weak {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.12688}.

## For young visitors
Completing the Qur'an means a reader reaches the end of the mushaf from beginning to end; the early Muslims differed over how fast to finish, and people grew accustomed to praying at its completion.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy mentions that some people complete the Qur'an on the eve of Friday, and cites the hadith that the deed dearest to God is that of 'the one who arrives and sets off again', which he explains as the one who completes the Qur'an and then returns to its beginning. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep06 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep06 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep06 00:07:30}
