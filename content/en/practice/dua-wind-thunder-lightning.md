## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar of supplications and manners at a rising wind, a falling star, flashing lightning and the sound of thunder, from reports, early sayings and al-Shafi'i. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 178}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi reports in Sahih Muslim from Aisha that when the wind blew hard [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «اللهم إني أسألك خيرها وخير ما فيها وخير ما أرسلت به وأعوذ بك من شرها», I ask You for its good and the good in it, and refuge from its evil {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 178}. In Sunan Abi Dawud and Ibn Majah, by a good chain, from Abu Hurayra: the wind is of God's mercy, coming with mercy and with punishment, so do not curse it but ask God for its good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 178} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 179}. From Ubayy ibn Ka'b in al-Tirmidhi's book, the same prohibition with a similar wording, which al-Tirmidhi calls good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 179}; and by the sound chain in Ibn al-Sunni's book, from Salama ibn al-Akwa, «اللهم لقحا لا عقيما», O God, a fertilising wind and not a barren one {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 179}. From Anas and Jabir in that book, resort to takbir when something great falls or a great wind rises {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 179} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 180}. From Ibn Abbas, in al-Shafi'i's al-Umm, that at the wind he knelt and said «اللهم اجعلها رحمة ولا تجعلها عذابا», make it a mercy and not a punishment {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 180}, and al-Shafi'i says it is God's obedient creature and should not be cursed {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 180}. For a falling star he reports from Ibn Mas'ud, in Ibn al-Sunni, that they were told not to follow it with their eyes but to say «ما شاء الله لا قوة إلا بالله» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 180}. For lightning he gives Urwa ibn al-Zubayr's saying, in al-Shafi'i, not to point at it but to describe it {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 181}. For thunder, from Ibn Umar in al-Tirmidhi, by a weak chain, «اللهم لا تقتلنا بغضبك ولا تهلكنا بعذابك», and by a sound chain in the Muwatta' from Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, who stopped speaking and said «سبحان الذي يسبح الرعد بحمده والملائكة من خيفته» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 181}.
