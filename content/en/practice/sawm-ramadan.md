## Overview
Ramadan is the name of the known month — said to be named for the season it fell in when the month-names were transferred, coinciding with the scorching heat (ramad). The month is established by sighting its crescent, or by completing Sha'ban to thirty when sighting fails. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8986}

## The last ten nights
They begin at the start of the twenty-first night and run to the end of Ramadan, complete or short — the name covers nights and days, as in "and ten nights." The jurists agree it is recommended to redouble effort in them — night prayer, alms, reciting and studying the Quran — following the Prophet who "when the ten entered would keep the night alive, wake his family, strive and tighten his belt"; and to spend freely on dependents and do good to kin and neighbours; and i'tikaf is emphatically recommended in them, the practitioner entering the mosque before sunset of the twenty-first night. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11623}

## For Sprouts
Fasting in Ramadan is a duty. Muslims abstain from food and drink from dawn to sunset.

## For Explorers
In the month of Ramadan Muslims fast, abstaining from food and drink from the first light of dawn until sunset. When they break the fast, they share the iftar meal with their families.

## For Scholars
Fasting through the month of Ramadan, one of the five pillars of Islam. The Qur'an prescribes it and says that whoever witnesses the month should fast it, with a number of other days for the sick and the traveller. The fast means abstaining from food, drink and marital relations from dawn to sunset, and reading the Qur'an and giving charity are encouraged in it. Reports and jurisprudence differ on the details of the sighting and the confirmation of the month, so each detail needs a source. The museum presents the fast as the worship of a whole month and does not rule on questions of sighting or fatwa; it refers to the entry on fasting in law.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy says that in Ramadan people turn to worship, even those who do not pray, and distinguishes the servant of Ramadan, who worships for thirty days and then stops, from the servant of the Lord of Ramadan. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep16 00:10:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep16 00:10:30} In another lesson he answers questions on fasting, among them eye drops. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep09 00:39:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep09 00:39:30}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme al-Munjiyat
Omar Abdelkafy faults the month of fasting becoming a month of eating and banquets, saying the stomach complains to God of people's injustice to it, and counts among the wisdoms of fasting that a person comes to feel the state of the hungry. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-manjeyat/ep08 00:09:00}

## In Yusuf al-Qaradawi's lessons Ta'ammulat Qur'aniyya
[[person/yusuf-al-qaradawi|Yusuf al-Qaradawi]] says fasting was known to earlier nations, that it trains the will by giving up what is lawful, and that no one but God watches the one who fasts ([[passage/quran-2-183|al-Baqara 183]]). {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:14:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:17:30} On the traveller he notes that jurists differ over which is better, and adopts the view of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz that the better is whichever is easier, giving the example of a traveller flying west to America whose day stretches to about twenty hours, who should break the fast. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:22:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:23:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:23:30} On the fidya verse ([[passage/quran-2-184|al-Baqara 184]]) he sets out Ibn Abbas's view about those who fast only with hardship, such as the elderly and the pregnant, and the other view that it was an early stage of gradual legislation, later abrogated. {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:24:30} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:25:00} {cite:assabile-yusuf-al-qaradawi taamolat-qurania-2/ep02 00:26:00}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme In the Courts of the Hadith Qudsi
Episode 22 of [[work/fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah|Fi rihab al-hadith al-qudsi]] explains the hadith qudsi in which God sets fasting apart as His alone, calls it a shield and speaks of the two joys of the one who fasts. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:00:00} The presenter opens with the verse prescribing fasting in [[passage/quran-2-183|al-Baqara 183]] and says its aim is God-consciousness and the purification of the self, not that the faster should feel the hunger of the poor, for then the poor would have been exempted. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:05:30}

[[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Al-Sha'rawi]] says that every deed other than fasting can be directed to someone other than God: a person may tell another "I have none in the world but you", bow to him, or give him money as a bribe; but no one has ever fasted for a human being, because the one fasted for would have to watch the faster all day. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:17:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:19:00} That is why God made its reward unmeasured: every deed has a scale except fasting. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:19:30} He holds that the "shield" (junna) is what protects from deficiency, and that every transgression needs energy, which comes from food and drink, so abstaining cuts off its fuel. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:20:00} He says that since fasting is God's it must not be used as a means of harming people; if someone provokes the faster, he reminds him that he is fasting. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:20:30} He criticises weeping and lamenting at the farewell to Ramadan as contrary to the joy God gave the faster. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy fi-rihab-al-hadith-al-qudsiyyah/ep22 00:22:30}
