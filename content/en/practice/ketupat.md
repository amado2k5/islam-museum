## Overview
## Ketupat
Ketupat is rice boiled inside a pouch woven from young palm leaves — coconut or pandan — until it sets into a cube or diamond: the pouch is first plaited in an interlocking weave with a small gap through which the raw rice goes in, then boiled two hours or more until the rice swells to fill the weave and takes its geometric shape and the leaf's green taste.

## Its feast and its weaving
Ketupat is the dish of Lebaran — Eid al-Fitr — in Indonesia, Malaysia, Brunei, Singapore, the southern Philippines and northern Australia, served with rendang, satay, lontong and opor, and strung in stalls and markets before Eid in rows of green and yellow diamonds. Weaving its pouch is a craft handed from mothers and grandmothers to the girls before Eid, and villages and schools compete over the finest weave.

## Its meaning and story
Popular memory ties it to Sunan Kalijaga, one of the nine saints — wali songo — who spread Islam in Java, said to have introduced it as a celebration to fold the Islamic Eid into the Javanese calendar. To some its cubic form stands for the human heart purified of sins — keto 'the error' and pat 'repentance' — and the interlocking diamonds for the unity Eid brings. {cite:wikipedia-ketupat-en}
