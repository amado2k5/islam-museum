## Summary
Norah Titley says the bookbinder held an important place in producing splendid manuscripts from the earliest times, his skill seen from the Coptic-inspired Arabic covers of the ninth century to the painted lacquer of nineteenth-century Iran. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 245} The decoration of the earliest Qur'ans and their covers was austere, and as Qur'an pages grew more ornate their geometric interlace extended to the covers. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 245}

## The flap
A feature of Islamic bindings is the extra triangular flap attached to the back cover, folded round the leaves and tucked inside the front cover, forming a second spine and protecting the leaves from grit and dust. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 245}

## Iran
In the fifteenth century the binders of Shiraz and Herat developed blind-tooled decoration of animals, birds, plants and arabesques on the outer covers, with gilt stamped designs on the doublures; by the sixteenth century doublures bore filigree cut from silver, gilt or coloured paper pasted on multicoloured grounds, as on the binding of Ahli Shirazi's poems of 1581–82. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 246} Titley notes dated covers for Shahrukh, Baysunghur, Uzun Hasan, Sultan Husayn, Ibrahim Sultan and Pir Budaq in Istanbul libraries, and that lacquer bindings seem to have begun at Herat under [[person/husayn-bayqarah|Sultan Husayn]]. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 247}

## Turkey and India
Persian binders were among the craftsmen taken to Turkey in the early sixteenth century; Ottoman binders developed their own styles — brown leather filigree on deep blue doublures, gold painted directly on covers, and protective cases with matching designs. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 247} Painted lacquer covers multiplied in Qajar Iran, often showing Fath Ali Shah or naturalistic and stylised flowers. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 248} Indian binders learned the art from Persian craftsmen and made much use of lacquer covers. {cite:titley-persian-miniature-painting-1983 p. 248}
