## Summary
Tayammum — linguistically "aiming at" — is, in juristic usage, wiping the face and hands with clean earth in a specified manner, as a substitute for wudu or ghusl when water is unavailable or cannot be used; it is established by Book, Sunna and consensus. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6082} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6083}

## Definitions across the schools
The Hanafis define it as wiping the face and hands from purifying soil; the Malikis as an earth-based purification comprising wiping face and hands with intention; the Shafi'is as conveying earth to face and hands in place of wudu, ghusl, or a limb thereof under specified conditions; the Hanbalis as wiping face and hands with clean earth in a specified way. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6082}

## Occasion of revelation
The verse of tayammum was revealed in the affair of A'isha's lost necklace during the expedition of Banu al-Mustaliq: the prayer time came while the Muslims had no water, the verse descended, and Usayd ibn Hudayr kept saying "How blessed you are, O family of Abu Bakr". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6083}

## A privilege of this community
Jurists count tayammum among this community's distinguishing privileges, citing Jabir's hadith "I was given five things no one before me was given… the earth was made for me a mosque and a means of purification". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6083}

## Concession or strict duty
The majority held tayammum a concession (rukhsa) for traveller and patient; the Hanbalis and some Shafi'is called it a full duty ('azima), and the Malikis differed on it for the traveller. The dispensation lies both in the purifying agent — earth suffices — and in its scope, confined to half the limbs of wudu. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6083}

## Conditions and integrals
Conditions of obligation include majority, ability to use earth, and a nullifying hadath; conditions of validity include Islam, sanity, cessation of menses and postnatal bleeding, and the presence of clean earth. Its integrals are two: the two strikes, and covering face and hands to the elbows with wiping — with disagreement on whether intention is integral or a condition. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6084} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6085}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
In a lesson on law Omar Abdelkafy explains dry ablution: the person strikes clean earth once with both palms and wipes his face and hands with them, and thereby lifts both the minor and the major state of impurity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep16 01:13:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep16 01:14:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep16 01:15:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep16 01:16:00}
