## Overview
Duha, for the jurists, is between the sun's rising and its zenith; salat al-duha is the prayer performed in that time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10583}

## Awwabin and ishraq
Some hold the awwabin prayer is salat al-duha, the two being synonymous; others place the awwabin between maghrib and 'isha'. The ishraq prayer, mentioned by some Shafi'is in the duha discussion, is on the relied-upon view of al-Ramli and al-Ziyadi the same as the duha prayer; a view makes it two rak'as at the sun's rising. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10583} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10494}

## Form
Among the voluntary prayers for which no congregation is prescribed: at least two rak'as, at most twelve, with salam every two. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10494}

## In Zaghloul El-Naggar's series 'al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya'
In [[work/el-hadara-el-nabaouia|al-Hadara al-Nabawiyya]], [[person/zaghloul-el-naggar|Zaghloul El-Naggar]] links the forenoon prayer to the hadith that a human being was created with three hundred and sixty joints and that each joint owes a charity every day, citing Abu Dharr's narration in Muslim that two rak'as of the forenoon suffice for all of it, in place of glorifying, praising and declaring God's oneness for each joint. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:05:00} Many people neglect it, he says; its time runs from when the sun has risen a spear's length until it passes the meridian; and its two "light rak'as" weigh heavy in the scale. {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-zaghloul-el-naggar el-hadara-el-nabaouia/ep28 00:05:30}

## In Abd al-Rahman Mansour's programme 'Ahalina'
[[person/abd-al-rahman-mansour|Abd al-Rahman Mansour]] advises in his programme [[work/ahaleena|'Ahalina']] that a child in late childhood, from seven to ten, be taught some of the sunna prayers, among them the merit of the duha prayer: that whoever keeps four rak'as of it each day, God will spare him what worries him. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:10:30} He cites a hadith qudsi to the effect that God suffices, at the end of the day, whoever prays four rak'as to Him at its start. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:11:00} A child, he says, has a free choice in voluntary prayers, so he is not to be pressed but encouraged gently, and they are begun only once he is steady in the obligatory prayers. {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:11:00} {cite:assabile-abd-al-rahman-mansour ahaleena/ep09 00:13:00}

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Hadaya al-Rahman'
[[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] makes the forenoon prayer the 'gift' of the tenth episode of his programme [[work/hadaya-al-rahman|'Hadaya al-Rahman']]: he cites a hadith that every joint of a person owes a charity each day, that the forms of remembrance and enjoining good and forbidding wrong are charities, and that two rak'as of the forenoon prayer suffice for it all, and says the human body has three hundred and sixty joints. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:01:00} The same question, the joints and the sufficiency of the two rak'as, is discussed from 'Jami' al-'Ulum wa-l-Hikam' in [[concept/sadaqa|charity]]. He says its time begins a third of an hour after sunrise and lasts until about half an hour before the noon prayer, and that it is two rak'as in five minutes, in thanks for the blessing of health. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:02:00} He holds that God encouraged it because the time between dawn and noon is long and God loves His servant to stand before Him, and says the Prophet kept to it and called it the prayer of those who often return to God (salat al-awwabin). {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep10 00:03:30}

In the twenty-fifth episode he cites a hadith that whoever prays the dawn prayer in congregation, stays remembering God until sunrise and then prays two rak'as earns the reward of a complete hajj and umra, and says sunrise comes an hour or a little more after dawn. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:00:30} He points out that hadith scholars differ over its soundness, some authenticating and some weakening it, and that he takes it on the scholars' view that a weak hadith may be acted on in the merits of deeds. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:01:00} Its wisdom, for him, is that God withholds His bounty from no one: whoever cannot afford the pilgrimage, in money or in health, has a hajj and umra written for him by this deed; he advises doing it once a week. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep25 00:03:30}
