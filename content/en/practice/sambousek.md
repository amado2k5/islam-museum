## Overview
## Sambousek
The sambousek — samosa in Urdu — is a triangle or crescent of thin pastry filled with spiced potato and peas, minced meat, cheese or lentils, fried or baked until it is golden and crisp: the queen of the Ramadan iftar appetiser on Arab, Indian, Pakistani, Horn-of-Africa, Turkish (sambusek) and Central Asian (samsa) tables.

## Its journey through the centuries
It began as 'sanbusaj' and 'sanbusaq' in Persia and Central Asia — the tenth-century Baghdad Kitab al-Tabikh knows it — and Ibn Battuta saw it on the table of Muhammad ibn Tughluq, sultan of Delhi, in the fourteenth century, filled with meat; the Persian popular romances mention it, and merchants and caravans carried it from Samarkand to Delhi and from Aden to the East African coast, where it became 'sambusa' for Ethiopians and Somalis.

## Its forms today
In Arabia and the Gulf the sambousek of cheese, potato and meat; in Egypt the sambousek of cheese and mince; in the Levant and Iraq of cheese, chicken and lentils; in India and Pakistan the big samosa of potato and peas served with tamarind and green chutney; in the Horn of Africa the sambusa of meat and onion; in Turkey the sambusek of spinach and cheese; in Central Asia the tandoor-baked samsa — all grandchildren of the same triangle. {cite:wikipedia-samosa-en}
