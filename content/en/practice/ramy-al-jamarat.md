## Overview
## The stoning of the jamarat
The stoning is the rite of the three days at Mina: three sites — the small jamra, the middle one, and the great Jamrat al-Aqaba — where the pilgrim casts pebbles gathered at Muzdalifa, seven at a time, saying the takbir with each. On the day of sacrifice Jamrat al-Aqaba alone is stoned; on the tashriq days all three are stoned after noon — twenty-one pebbles a day.

## Its meaning and story
Jurists and storytellers tie the rite to Ibrahim: as he went out with Ismail for the sacrifice the devil appeared to him at the sites of the three pillars and he drove him off with pebbles — so the stoning stands as a declaration of repudiation and of obedience under trial. The Prophet's own stoning on the day of sacrifice during the farewell hajj is the first recorded instance: he threw from camel-back, pebbles the size of sling-stones, no excess and no neglect.

## The pillars in stone
The jamarat were once simple stone pillars ringed by small basins. As the crowds grew Saudi Arabia rebuilt all of Mina around a multi-storey bridge — the Jamarat Bridge, completed in 2009 with five levels separating entry and exit — where pilgrims cast into long oval concrete basins 26 metres wide holding the three pillars, after the crushes that claimed hundreds of lives in 1990, 1998, 2004 and 2015. {cite:wikipedia-kiswah-en}
