## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar of examples of the Prophet changing names, the permissibility of shortening a name, the prohibition on nicknames their bearer dislikes, and the permissibility of a nickname its bearer likes. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 292}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Here al-Nawawi completes the chapter on changing names. He reports in the two Sahihs from Ibn Umar that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] changed the name Asiya, saying «أنت جميلة», you are Jamila {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 292}. From Usama ibn Akhdari in Sunan Abi Dawud, by a good chain, that a man named Asram was told «بل أنت زرعة», rather you are Zur'a {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 292}. From Abu Shurayh Hani al-Harithi, who was called Abu al-Hakam, the Prophet said «إن الله هو الحكم وإليه الحكم», God is the judge and to Him belongs judgement, and then called him Abu Shurayh after his eldest son {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 292}. He quotes from Abu Dawud a further list of names and places the Prophet changed, among them a land called Aqra which he named Khadra and Shi'b al-Dalala which he named Shi'b al-Huda, Abu Dawud having left out the chains for brevity {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 292}. On shortening a name he reports in the sound collections that the Prophet shortened the names of several Companions, saying to Abu Hurayra «يا أبا هر», to Aisha «يا عائش» and to Anjasha «يا أنجش» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 293}. On nicknames he cites a verse forbidding the trading of nicknames, [[passage/quran-49-11|Qur'an 49:11]], and says scholars agree it is forbidden to give a person a nickname he dislikes, and agree one may be mentioned by it for identification when known by nothing else {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 293}. For a liked nickname he gives Abu Bakr, named Abd Allah ibn Uthman and nicknamed Atiq, and reports from Aisha that the Prophet said «أبو بكر عتيق الله من النار» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 293}. He gives Ali's nickname Abu Turab, when the Prophet found him asleep in the mosque with dust on him, and from Sahl ibn Sa'd that it was the dearest of his names to him {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 294}; and Dhu al-Yadayn, whose name was al-Kharbaq {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 294}.
