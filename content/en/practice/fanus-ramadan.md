## Overview
## The fanous
The fanous is Egypt's folk Ramadan lantern — the symbol the month carries worldwide: a metal-framed lantern with coloured glass panels holding a candle or lamp, hung in streets, homes and mosques in Ramadan, from giants taller than a man to toy-size ones in a child's hand.

## Name and history
The word comes from Greek phanos, 'light, lantern'; ancient Egyptians used torches of the kind in their festivals of the rising of Sirius and the birthdays of Osiris, Horus, Isis, Seth and Nephthys — five days, one illuminated for each. Egyptians tie the Ramadan fanous to the Fatimid caliph al-Mu'izz li-Din Allah's entry into Cairo in Ramadan, when the people came out in multitudes carrying lanterns to greet him, and hanging light in the streets became the month's custom; al-Maqrizi notes in the Khitat that candles and torches were used in Egypt's Christian feasts too.

## From Cairo to the world
The fanous left Cairo's alleys — the copper-beating workshops of Khan al-Khalili and Taht al-Rab' its most famous makers — for the whole Arab world and beyond: hung in the markets of Damascus, Istanbul and Kuala Lumpur in Ramadan, decorating hotels, malls and homes, a visual emblem of the month in advertising, television and art — a purely Arabic language of light gone international. {cite:wikipedia-fanous-en}
