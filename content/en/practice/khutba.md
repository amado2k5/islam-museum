## Overview
A khutba is composed prose addressed by an eloquent speaker to a gathering; technically, composed speech containing exhortation and proclamation in a prescribed form. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7700}

## The prescribed sermons
The sermons of Friday, the two Eids, the two eclipses, the rain prayer, and the hajj sermons — all after the prayer except Friday and the 'Arafa sermon — and the marriage-address khutba. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7700}

## The Friday sermon
It is a condition of the Friday prayer's validity; the two sermons are a condition by agreement except the Hanafis who require one and sunna the second, their proof being the Prophet's practice with "pray as you have seen me pray." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7701}

## Its integrals
Abu Hanifa held its integral a single tahmida, tahlila or tasbiha since "hasten to God's remembrance" is unqualified; his two companions required a long address called khutba; the Malikis the least the Arabs call khutba, even two rhymed clauses. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.7701}
