## Summary
How al-Nawawi ends the manners of recitation: a mistaken custom in the night prayers, the permissibility of naming suras, the dislike of saying one forgot a verse, and the daily portion. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 109}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi condemns a custom of some ignorant leaders of the night prayers: reciting the whole of Sura al-An'am in the last unit on the seventh night, believing it recommended. He counts among its faults misleading the common people and burdening the followers {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 108} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 109}. He holds that one may say Sura al-Baqara and Sura Al Imran without dislike, though some early scholars disliked it, and prefers permission because the hadiths are many {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 109}. It is disliked to say "I forgot such a verse"; one should say it was made to slip. He gives from the two Sahihs, from Ibn Mas'ud, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «لا يقول أحدكم نسيت آية كذا وكذا بل هو نسي», and from Aisha that the Prophet heard a man reciting and said he had reminded him of a verse he had let drop {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 109}. He says the manners of the reciter could not be exhausted in less than volumes, referring to his book al-Tibyan, and that reciting the Qur'an is the most stressed of remembrances, so no day and night should pass without it {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 110}. From Ibn al-Sunni's book, from Anas, one who recites fifty verses is not written among the heedless and one who recites a hundred among the devout {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 110}.
