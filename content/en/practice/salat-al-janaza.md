## Overview
The funeral prayer, in Arabic salat al-janaza or salat ala l-mayyit, the prayer over a dead person, is one of the ritual prayers that the Encyclopaedia of Islam places among the prayers other than the five daily ones. It is a communal duty, fard kifaya, which falls on the community as a whole and not on each individual concerned. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} It differs from the other prayers in one respect that the article stresses: it involves no rak'a, so there is no bowing and no prostration. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} The same article places it last in its survey of the other ritual prayers, after the Friday prayer, the prayer of the two feasts, the witr, the prayer on a journey, the prayers for rain and for eclipses, and the prayer of fear. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997}

## How the prayer is performed
According to the article, the imam stands upright facing the [[concept/qibla|qibla]], with the body of the deceased laid crosswise before him. The others line up in ranks behind him, as for every ritual prayer. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} The imam says four times, in a loud voice, Allahu akbar. After the first he praises God; after the second he says the so-called Abrahamic prayers, the blessing on the Prophet familiar from the ordinary prayer; after the third he makes supplication, du'a, for the deceased; and after the fourth he gives the final salutation, as at the end of other prayers. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997}

## Where the schools differ
The article singles out several points. The first concerns the recitation of the Fatiha after the first takbira. It is obligatory according to [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ibn Hanbal]], and the article adds that the [[group/hanafi-madhhab|Hanafis]] and [[group/maliki-madhhab|Malikis]] allow it to take the place of the praise of God as an option. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} On the [[concept/al-fatiha-umm-al-kitab|Fatiha]] itself the museum's other sections below give the position of each school in the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia.

The supplication for the deceased is not said aloud and has no fixed formula. The article points to some traditional texts, with substantial variants when the deceased is a woman and in the case of a child. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} The article does not give the wording.

## Conditions of the prayer
The prayer is performed only once, and normally in the presence of the corpse. The article names two exceptions to that last condition: when the body has disappeared in a natural disaster such as a flood, or in battle, and when homage is paid in several places to an eminent Muslim who has recently died. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} The stricter and the wider positions of the individual schools on the absent deceased are set out in the later section on conditions.

## Whom the prayer is said for
The article connects this question to the [[concept/munafiq|Hypocrites of Medina]]. It reports that [[person/muhammad|Muhammad]] was told in a Qur'anic verse, [[passage/quran-9-84|9:84]], not to pray over any of them when they died nor to stand at their graves, because they had died unbelieving. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} On this verse and on diversely interpreted prophetic traditions, the article says, jurists have tried to decide which Muslims should be denied the prayer. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997}

It gives an example from the fourth century: the great judge [[person/qadi-abd-al-jabbar|Abd al-Jabbar]], a Mu'tazili who was nevertheless a Shafi'i, declined in 385/995 to pray over the body of the vizier [[person/sahib-ibn-abbad|Ibn Abbad]], because of extortions the vizier had committed and for which he had shown no repentance. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} The article then cites al-Qurtubi, who asserts that the prayer should be said over all Muslims, even those guilty of serious sins, except heretics and declared rebels, ahl al-bida wa-l-bughat. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 997} {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 998}

For a person who has killed himself, the article cites two later authorities, al-Ghawidji and al-Malatawi, as holding that the prayer should still be performed, even though Islam denounces suicide. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 998} A separate EI2 article on the subject says that the question of whether funeral prayers may be given to a person who died by [[concept/intihar|suicide]] was and remains the most debated legal question on that subject, that authorities within the various schools held divergent views and that practice appears to have varied. It suggests that the more charitable view may have prevailed widely. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 1280} As an example it tells of a blind professor in Damascus who took his own life in 602/1206: people refused to pray for him, but another prominent Shafi'i scholar did. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 1280} It adds that the Prophet is said to have refused to say the customary prayers for one who killed himself, and that suicide was generally regarded as a grave sin. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 3 p. 1280}

## The prayer within the funeral
The article on the funeral prayer refers readers to the entry Djanaza for the washing of the dead and the burial. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 8 p. 998} The OCR of that short entry in the museum's copy is broken, but it opens by saying that djanaza means a corpse, a bier, or both, and then a funeral. It notes that it was sunna to whisper the shahada in the ear of a dying man whose face was turned towards Mecca, and it says that the body was washed by persons of the same sex. {cite:encyclopaedia-of-islam-2 vol. 2 p. 466} The washing is treated in the museum's record on [[practice/ghusl-al-janaza|washing the deceased]]. The remainder of that entry could not be read reliably and is not summarised here.

## Summary
The funeral prayer is, for the majority of jurists, a communal obligation — some Malikis said a communal sunna — discharged once some perform it; it consists of takbirs and supplication for the deceased, without bowing or prostration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6476}

## Its integrals
For the Hanafis: the takbirs and standing. For the Malikis, five: intention, four takbirs, supplication between them, one salam, and standing for whoever is able. For the Shafi'is: intention, takbirs, the Fatiha, the blessing on the Prophet, a minimal supplication for the deceased, and the first salam. For the Hanbalis: standing in an obligatory prayer for whoever is able, four takbirs, the Fatiha, the blessing on the Prophet, a minimal supplication specific to the deceased, and salam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6477}

## Its conditions
It requires the conditions of other prayers — purity of body, garment and place, covering the 'awra, facing the qibla, intention — except the time. The Hanafis and Malikis required the body's presence before the worshippers; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis allowed prayer over an absent deceased. For the Hanafis the imam must stand level with a part of the body; for the Hanbalis this is only recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6477} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6478} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6479}

## The salam
For the Hanafis two salams are obligatory after the fourth takbir; for the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis one, on the basis of "its dissolution is the salam" — the second salam is sunna for the Shafi'is and permitted for the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6479}

## Its sunnas
The imam stands level with the chest of the deceased, male or female, for the Hanafis; at the man's head and the woman's pelvis for the Shafi'is. Praise after the first takbir is sunna for the Hanafis, the blessing on the Prophet after the second, and supplication for the deceased, oneself and all Muslims after the third — among the transmitted words: "O God, forgive him, have mercy on him, keep him well and pardon him…". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6480} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6481}

## Funerals in Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lessons (episode 9)
The Sheikh says that Umar ibn al-Khattab asked to be buried quickly, since if he was good he would go to good, and if not he would be removed from people (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:14:00} He holds that walking in a funeral procession and reflecting on it is worth a thousand lessons (his view, needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:14:30} He relates that Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan came to watch a body being washed by Abu Hazim, who answered him (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:15:00} He relates reports on the washing of the Prophet and the disagreement of the Companions about turning him, and says that what was done for him was done only with God's help (needs_source). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:17:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep09 00:18:00}

## For Scholars
The funeral prayer is a communal obligation for most jurists — some Malikis call it a confirmed sunna — discharged once some perform it, done with takbirs and supplication for the dead, no bowing or prostration. Its pillars per school in the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia: Hanafis the takbirs and standing; Malikis five — intention, four takbirs, supplication among them, one taslima, standing for the able; Shafi'is — intention, takbirs, Fatiha, blessing on the Prophet, a minimal supplication, the first taslima; Hanbalis — standing for the able in the obligatory prayer, four takbirs, Fatiha, blessing, a supplication specific to the dead, and salam. Its conditions are those of all prayers — purity of body, garment and place, covering, qibla, intention — save the time; Hanafis and Malikis require the body present before the worshippers while Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow the absent; aligning the imam with part of the body is a condition for Hanafis, recommended for Hanbalis. The salam: twice after the fourth takbir for Hanafis, once for Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis on 'its release is the taslim' — the second a sunna for Shafi'is, permissible for Hanbalis. Among its sunnas: the imam stands opposite the chest per Hanafis, at the man's head and the woman's middle per Shafi'is; the praise after the first takbir, the blessing on the Prophet after the second, and supplication for the dead and the Muslims after the third.

## In Mustafa Hosny's programmes 'Hadaya al-Rahman' and 'Ish al-Lahza'
[[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]] gives the sixth episode of his programme [[work/hadaya-al-rahman|'Hadaya al-Rahman']] to the funeral prayer: he cites a hadith that whoever attends a funeral until the prayer has a qirat of reward, and whoever stays until the burial has two, each qirat like a great mountain, and says some narrations liken it to Mount Uhud. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:01:30} He ties this to the scales of the Day of Resurrection, and says the prayer itself takes a minute or a minute and a half. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:01:00} The wisdom of the reward, for him, is training in sincerity, since the dead can neither thank nor repay, and the mercy of God the Self-Sustaining towards the dead, who cannot ask anyone to pray for them. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:03:30} He relates that [[person/abdullah-ibn-umar|Abd Allah ibn Umar]], on hearing the hadith, said they had let many qirats slip by, and admits that he himself has seen many funerals while in a hurry or lazy. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep06 00:04:00}

In the ninth episode of [[work/ich-allahda|'Ish al-Lahza']] he describes the scenes of the washing, the shroud, the prayer and the burial, and the mourners' prayer that God not deprive them of the deceased's reward nor try them after him; then he explains the prayer: al-Fatiha after the first takbir, the blessing on Muhammad and his family after the second, and the supplication for the deceased after the third, telling the worshipper that he is next in line. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep09 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep09 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny ich-allahda/ep09 00:21:00}
