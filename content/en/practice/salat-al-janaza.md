## Summary
The funeral prayer is, for the majority of jurists, a communal obligation — some Malikis said a communal sunna — discharged once some perform it; it consists of takbirs and supplication for the deceased, without bowing or prostration. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6476}

## Its integrals
For the Hanafis: the takbirs and standing. For the Malikis, five: intention, four takbirs, supplication between them, one salam, and standing for whoever is able. For the Shafi'is: intention, takbirs, the Fatiha, the blessing on the Prophet, a minimal supplication for the deceased, and the first salam. For the Hanbalis: standing in an obligatory prayer for whoever is able, four takbirs, the Fatiha, the blessing on the Prophet, a minimal supplication specific to the deceased, and salam. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6477}

## Its conditions
It requires the conditions of other prayers — purity of body, garment and place, covering the 'awra, facing the qibla, intention — except the time. The Hanafis and Malikis required the body's presence before the worshippers; the Shafi'is and Hanbalis allowed prayer over an absent deceased. For the Hanafis the imam must stand level with a part of the body; for the Hanbalis this is only recommended. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6477} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6478} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6479}

## The salam
For the Hanafis two salams are obligatory after the fourth takbir; for the Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis one, on the basis of "its dissolution is the salam" — the second salam is sunna for the Shafi'is and permitted for the Hanbalis. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6479}

## Its sunnas
The imam stands level with the chest of the deceased, male or female, for the Hanafis; at the man's head and the woman's pelvis for the Shafi'is. Praise after the first takbir is sunna for the Hanafis, the blessing on the Prophet after the second, and supplication for the deceased, oneself and all Muslims after the third — among the transmitted words: "O God, forgive him, have mercy on him, keep him well and pardon him…". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6480} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6481}

## For Scholars
The funeral prayer is a communal obligation for most jurists — some Malikis call it a confirmed sunna — discharged once some perform it, done with takbirs and supplication for the dead, no bowing or prostration. Its pillars per school in the Kuwaiti encyclopaedia: Hanafis the takbirs and standing; Malikis five — intention, four takbirs, supplication among them, one taslima, standing for the able; Shafi'is — intention, takbirs, Fatiha, blessing on the Prophet, a minimal supplication, the first taslima; Hanbalis — standing for the able in the obligatory prayer, four takbirs, Fatiha, blessing, a supplication specific to the dead, and salam. Its conditions are those of all prayers — purity of body, garment and place, covering, qibla, intention — save the time; Hanafis and Malikis require the body present before the worshippers while Shafi'is and Hanbalis allow the absent; aligning the imam with part of the body is a condition for Hanafis, recommended for Hanbalis. The salam: twice after the fourth takbir for Hanafis, once for Malikis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis on 'its release is the taslim' — the second a sunna for Shafi'is, permissible for Hanbalis. Among its sunnas: the imam stands opposite the chest per Hanafis, at the man's head and the woman's middle per Shafi'is; the praise after the first takbir, the blessing on the Prophet after the second, and supplication for the dead and the Muslims after the third.
