## Summary
The etiquette of greeting with peace on meeting. In his commentary on the verse of Surat al-Nisa about greetings, [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|Sha'rawi]] says a greeting is returned with its like or something better, and sets out who begins and who answers. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2501}

## What Sha'rawi says
He reads the verse as ordering a return with its like or better, and reads the words "or return it" as a reassurance to the one who greets that his greeting will be answered. He reports that scholars classed the cases: the walker greets the seated, the rider the walker, the young the old, the sighted the blind, the few the many. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2501} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2502}

## Women
He says women greet women, a husband and close kin; that a young woman does not begin the greeting to men nor answer it unless someone she trusts is with her; and that an elderly woman past desire may begin and answer. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2501} {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2502}

## A greeting from a non-believer
He reports that a non-believer who says "peace" is answered with "and upon you", and that an ambiguous phrase is answered in kind so that the Muslim is not mocked. These are rules he reports; their legal sources have not yet been set out here. {cite:tafsir-sharawi-khawatir-scan p.2502}
