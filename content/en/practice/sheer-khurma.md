## Overview
## Sheer khurma
Sheer khurma — Persian 'milk and dates', sheer milk, khurma dates — is the first dish of Eid al-Fitr morning in South Asia: fine vermicelli toasted in ghee until golden, then simmered in boiling milk with chopped dates, sugar, almonds, pistachios, cashews, cardamom and rose water until it thickens into a sweet cream soup, served warm or cold as the first thing offered to Eid guests.

## Its place and coming
The recipe came with India's Muslim rulers — the Mughal kitchen that carried milk and Persian nuts into Delhi and Lucknow's tables — and became 'Eid' itself for the Muslims of India, Pakistan and Bangladesh: its jugs and bowls are sent round to neighbours before people leave for the musalla, and its taste is tied for them to the arrival of the first guests of the morning.

## Its kinds
Dry sheer khurma — 'sewaiyan kathi' — less milk, more ghee and nuts, keeping a week; a green spinach version in some Lucknow houses; saffron sheer khurma for occasions; Hyderabadi dried-fruit versions; and the children's breakfast favourite — sheer khurma on white bread. {cite:wikipedia-sheer-khurma-en}
