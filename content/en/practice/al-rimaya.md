## Summary
Archery with bow, arrow and lance is among the sports the Muslims practised, and was treated as training in strength for the struggle. Sayyid Sabiq counts it among the praiseworthy sports. {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah p. 47}

## Its standing
In the chapter on contests, Sabiq cites the Qur'an's command to prepare strength and the horses kept ready, and reports the Prophet's words that "strength is shooting", repeated three times. {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah p. 47} He also quotes the saying that every game is forbidden except three: a man's play with his wife, his shooting with his bow, and his training of his horse. {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah p. 47} Abu Nu'aym's Ma'rifat al-Sahaba records the saying "Teach your children swimming and archery", in a chain passing through Sulaym ibn Amr al-Ansari. {cite:marifat-sahaba-abu-nuaym entry 1276}

## Manners of shooting
Sabiq reports Ibn Umar's objection to people who set a live hen as a target, and the Prophet's curse on whoever takes a living creature as a target. {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah p. 47}

## Still needed
The Sabiq passages are drawn from later manuals and cite earlier collections. Abu Nu'aym's chain is not graded in the passage read. No dated shooting event appears on these pages, so archery is not a story here.
