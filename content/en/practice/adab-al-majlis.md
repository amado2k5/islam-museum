## Keeping the secret of the gathering
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy says that secrets spoken in a gathering are a trust, and that only what carries useful knowledge may be repeated. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:07:30}
He says that homes and gatherings have their secrets, and that people should not carry news of gatherings outside. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:23:00}
He recounts a hadith that one of the gravest betrayals is for a spouse to pass on the private matters of the other, and says this applies to husbands as much as to wives. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:22:00}
He mentions a hadith on the disclosure of a secret, and gives the wives of Noah and Lot as examples of one who disclosed a secret (needs_source: the hadith is not named in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:08:00}
He says that people should be left to worship God and to live as they choose, and that extracting their secrets through interrogation pushes them to lie. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:09:30}

## Attending the gathering and listening
The Sheikh criticises those who attend a scholar's gathering only to argue with him and correct him. He recounts that al-Hasan al-Basri listened attentively to a young speaker, and he describes the manners of listening. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:01:30}
He says that the believer in the mosque is like a fish in water and the hypocrite like a bird in a cage, and he attributes the saying to the scholars (needs_source: the saying is attributed to the scholars without a name in the recording). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:03:30}
He cites a hadith that those most beloved to the Prophet are those with the best manners, and draws from it the lesson that a person's standing is set by his character. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep11 00:13:00}
He says that faith is more than speech and must appear in manners and dealings. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep15 00:03:30}

## Speech in the gathering
The Sheikh asks whether our tongues refrain from backbiting and gossip, and whether officials take only lawful money. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep14 00:24:00}

## Visiting and the length of the stay
The Sheikh says that a visitor should not overstay, and he quotes an imam, not named in the recording, who said that he never sat with a heavy-spirited person without feeling the weight of it (needs_source: the imam is not named). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep09 00:23:30}
He says that a visit made to win a patron's favour is not sincere, that a visit should be made for God's sake, and that a right should be claimed without favour. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:23:30}
He says that truth in resolve means keeping it, and that this includes regular visits to kin, calling relatives, and visiting the sick. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-naba-al-safi/ep20 00:26:00}
