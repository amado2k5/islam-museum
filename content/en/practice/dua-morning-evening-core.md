## Summary
The first part of what al-Nawawi gives in the chapter on what is said at morning and evening, the widest chapter of the book: the master of seeking forgiveness, a hundred glorifications, three short surahs, and refuge in God's perfect words. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 76}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says this chapter is the widest in the book, that he gives a number of the shorter reports from it, and that whoever cannot do them all may take as much as he wishes, even a single remembrance {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 76}. In Sahih al-Bukhari, from Shaddad ibn Aws, [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] named «سيد الاستغفار», the master of seeking forgiveness, which begins «اللهم أنت ربي لا إله إلا أنت خلقتني وأنا عبدك»; whoever says it at evening and dies that night enters Paradise or is of its people, and likewise at morning {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 76}. In Sahih Muslim, from Abu Hurayra: whoever says «سبحان الله وبحمده» a hundred times at morning and evening is bettered on the Day of Resurrection only by one who said the same or more {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 76}. From Abd Allah ibn Khubayb, by sound chains in Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i, the Prophet told him to recite the surah of sincerity and the two surahs of refuge three times at evening and morning; al-Tirmidhi called it good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 77}. From Abu Hurayra, «اللهم بك أصبحنا وبك أمسينا وبك نحيا وبك نموت وإليك النشور», which al-Tirmidhi called good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 77}. In Sahih Muslim, to a man stung by a scorpion the Prophet said that had he said at evening «أعوذ بكلمات الله التامات من شر ما خلق», it would not have harmed him {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 78}. From Uthman, in Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, «بسم الله الذي لا يضر مع اسمه شيء في الأرض ولا في السماء» three times; al-Tirmidhi called it good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 78}.
