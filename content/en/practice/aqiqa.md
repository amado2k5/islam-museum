## Summary
'Aqiqa in the language applies to a precious stone, to the hair a child is born with in the womb, and to the animal slaughtered for the newborn when its hair is shaved; juristically, it is what is slaughtered for the newborn in thanks to God, with a specified intention and conditions. Some Shafi'is disliked the name 'aqiqa and preferred calling it nasika. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11783}

## In Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's lesson
The Sheikh says the aqiqa is made on the seventh, fourteenth or twenty-first day: two sheep or rams for a boy and one for a girl (needs_source: the days and numbers are spoken once in the transcript). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:07:00}
He says a boy is held in pledge by his aqiqa, and that the hair is shaved for a boy but not for a girl. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:00}
He describes tahnik, which is done on the seventh day together with the aqiqa. [[practice/tahnik|Tahnik]] {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:30}

## From the lectures: shaving the newborn's hair
The speaker mentions the custom of shaving a newborn's hair on the seventh day, and says the shaving applies to the boy's hair and not the girl's (needs_source: partly garbled) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-hoquq/ep05 00:08:00}.
