## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the chapters on rising from prostration and sitting between the two prostrations and on the words of the second unit: the supplication of the sitting and how the second unit differs from the first. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 57}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says the sunna is to say the takbir as one begins to rise from prostration, lengthening it until sitting upright, and then to supplicate with what he has reported {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 57}. He reports from Hudhayfa, in his account of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]]'s night prayer, that between the two prostrations he said «رب اغفر لي رب اغفر لي», My Lord, forgive me, and sat for as long as his prostration lasted {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 58}. From Ibn Abbas, in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan, in the account of his night with his aunt Maymuna, he reports that on lifting his head from the prostration he said «رب اغفر لي وارحمني واجبرني وارفعني وارزقني واهدني» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 58}. He says the sitting of rest is an established sunna, sound in al-Bukhari and elsewhere as the Prophet's own act, and that it is recommended in his school {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 58}. In the chapter on the second unit he then says that the words given for the first are said in the second as well, with a few exceptions: it has no takbir of entry and no opening supplication {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 58}. He adds that seeking refuge in the second unit is disputed, the sounder view being that one does so, and that the chosen view is that the recitation is shorter than in the first {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 59}.
