## Summary
Sadaqa is what is given seeking nearness to God rather than human favour — covering both zakat and voluntary alms; juristically, a gratuitous transfer in life made for the sake of God. Jurists mostly reserve the word for voluntary alms, and it was said to be named for the sincerity (sidq) of the giver's intention. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10295} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10296}

## Its uses
The word covers zakat — "sadaqat are only for the poor…" — voluntary alms, the endowment, as in Umar's sadaqa of his land "not to be sold, given or inherited, but its yield spent", and every act of kindness — "every good deed is a sadaqa". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10295} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10296}

## Beside hiba, hadiyya and 'ariyya
Gift, present and donation are all gratuitous transfers: for the afterlife's reward it is sadaqa; for affection and fellowship, hiba; for honouring, hadiyya — 'atiyya covering them all. A loan ('ariyya) permits or transfers a usufruct: sadaqa transfers the thing itself and admits no taking back, while the loaned object must return to its owner. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10296}

## Its wisdom and merit
Sadaqa aids the weak, relieves the distressed, gives thanks for God's blessings, and evidences the giver's faith — among the merits transmitted, the seven shaded by God include "a man who gave a sadaqa and hid it so that his left hand did not know what his right hand spent". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10296} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10297}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)
The Sheikh mentions that a soul sends ahead some deeds and leaves behind others, that what it sends ahead is good deeds, and that some people leave behind bad ones, such as one who writes an indecent book {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:45:30}. He says ongoing charity has a counterpart in ongoing sin, so that one who makes an indecent film earns a burden each time people watch it, and he holds that the Messenger urges ongoing charity {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:46:00}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh criticises those who line the poor up in queues or publish their donations in newspapers and on television, and calls for sending charity to their homes in secret, recalling the one among the seven shaded by God who hid his charity; he holds that a respectable employee whose income does not suffice, with children in school, a sick daughter and a loan, is among the most deserving of zakat and charity, because the ignorant think him rich from his restraint and he does not beg insistently. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:08:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep22 00:09:30}

## In Mustafa Hosny's programme 'Hadaya al-Rahman'
Two episodes of [[person/mustafa-hosny|Mustafa Hosny]]'s programme [[work/hadaya-al-rahman|'Hadaya al-Rahman']] are about charity. In the fourth he names, among the seven who will be granted God's shade on the Day of Resurrection, the one who hid his charity, and gives the example of a man asked for charity who puts his hand in his pocket and gives whatever note comes out without looking, in humility. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep04 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep04 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep04 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep04 00:02:00} Its wisdom, for him, is to give with a kind and humble heart that does not remind the poor of the favour, as if telling him that it is the giver who needs him, since because of him the giver is in the shade. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep04 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep04 00:03:00}

In the fourteenth he cites a hadith that charity guards its giver from evil deaths, the least of them dying in worry, and another that secret charity guards against evil ends, and ties them to the teaching that deeds are judged by their endings. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:00:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:01:30} He holds that the giver revives someone 'alive but dead' with sorrow and need, and God then comforts the giver at his weakest moment, the soul's departure. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:02:30} He relates that [[person/ali-ibn-abi-talib|Ali ibn Abi Talib]] had four dirhams and spent one in the morning, one at night, one in secret and one openly, and that a verse praising those who spend at all times and in every state was revealed about him; this is his telling, and he names no source. {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-mustafa-hosny hadaya-al-rahman/ep14 00:03:00}
