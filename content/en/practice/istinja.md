## Summary
Istinja' is removing filth from the private parts after relieving oneself, with water or with stones, and belongs to the chapters of [[concept/tahara|purity]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 19-20} The reports call it *istitaba* ("making clean") because it removes impurity and purifies its place, and cleansing with stones is called *istijmar*, the word behind the stoning of the *jimar* in the pilgrimage. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 30}

## Its chapters in Abu Dawud's Sunan
[[person/abu-dawud-al-sijistani|Abu Dawud]] opens the [[work/sunan-abi-dawud|Sunan]] with the etiquette of relieving oneself, then gives istinja' a run of chapters: {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 17} {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 494}
- **What may not be used**: the report of [[person/ruwayfi-ibn-thabit-al-ansari|Ruwayfi' ibn Thabit]] that Muhammad disowns whoever cleanses himself with animal dung or bone; the report of [[person/jabir-ibn-abdullah|Jabir]] forbidding wiping with bone or droppings; and Ibn Mas'ud's report that a delegation of [[concept/jinn|the jinn]] asked the Prophet to forbid his community cleansing with bone, dung and charcoal, because God had made these their provision. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 31-33}
- **Cleansing with stones**: the report of [[person/aisha|'A'isha]], "let him take three stones with him to clean himself, for they suffice him", and that of [[person/khuzayma-ibn-thabit|Khuzayma ibn Thabit]], "with three stones, without dung among them". {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 33-34}
- **Making sure of the last drops (istibra')**: 'A'isha's report that [[person/umar-ibn-al-khattab|'Umar]] stood behind the Prophet with a jug of water, and he said, "I was not commanded to perform ablution every time I urinate; had I done so it would have become a sunna." {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 34}
- **Cleansing with water**: [[person/anas-ibn-malik|Anas]]'s report that the Prophet entered an orchard with a boy carrying a water vessel and came out having cleansed himself with water, and [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]]'s report that [[passage/quran-9-108|al-Tawba 108]] was revealed about the people of [[place/quba|Quba']] because they cleansed themselves with water. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 34-35}
- **Rubbing the hand on the ground**: Abu Hurayra's report that the Prophet cleansed himself and then wiped his hand on the ground. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 35}

## The etiquette before it
These chapters are preceded by others: going far from people, choosing soft ground for urinating, seeking refuge on entering the privy, the prohibition on [[question/facing-the-qibla-when-relieving-oneself|facing or turning one's back to the qibla]], and the prohibition on cleansing with the right hand. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 17-20} [[person/abu-sulayman-al-khattabi|Al-Khattabi]] holds that for most scholars the prohibition on using the right hand is one of good manners and propriety, since the right hand is kept for eating, drinking, taking and giving. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 19} In the report "whoever uses stones, let him use an odd number; whoever does so has done well, and whoever does not, there is no blame", he reads a choice between water, the original means of purity, and stones, which are a concession. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 30-31}

## The reasons for the prohibitions, as al-Khattabi explains them
- Dung is itself impure; meeting impurity it does not remove it but adds to it. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20}
- "Bone" covers every bone, of carrion or of a slaughtered animal, since the word is unrestricted; it is also said that bone is slippery and does not lift the impurity. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 20}
- Charcoal was made provision for the jinn, so it may not be spoiled for them; and it is soft and crumbles, leaving traces on the place; earth and crumbled clay are in the same category. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 33}

:::perspectives
**Sunni (Shafi'i): al-Khattabi.** Fewer than three stones do not suffice even if fewer have cleaned the place; stones are one of the two means of purity, so if water is not used, stones or their equivalent are needed. He ascribes this to [[person/sufyan-al-thawri|Sufyan al-Thawri]], [[person/malik-ibn-anas|Malik]], [[person/al-shafii|al-Shafi'i]] and [[person/ahmad-ibn-hanbal|Ahmad]]. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 19-20}

**Sunni: a view among the early authorities, as al-Khattabi relates it.** Some early authorities disliked cleansing with water, and a later writer claimed that water is a kind of food; al-Khattabi says the Sunna overrules and refutes this, and that Anas's report shows cleansing with water to be recommended even though stones suffice. {cite:sunan-abi-dawud-ibn-hazm-1997-v1 p. 34}
:::
