## Overview
## Eidiyah
Eidiyah — eidi in Persian and Urdu, bayramlık in Turkish, salami in Bengal — is the gift an elder gives the young on the two Eids: usually money, but extending to greeting cards, sweets, toys and presents. It is a cultural custom, neither obligatory nor sunnah in law, yet it has become the strongest Eid tradition tied to children across the Muslim world, and it has spilled over to the Persian Nowruz and beyond.

## How it is given
The classic form: children line up youngest to eldest before the family's senior member, each receiving a sum that grows with age so the last child takes the most; other families distribute equally or without a queue. Fresh banknotes are preferred — banks in Pakistan and India issue new notes for eidi in the weeks before Eid; in Turkey bayramlık is set aside for the children; in Malaysia and Indonesia the money goes in green envelopes called angpau or sampul hijau.

## What it means
Eidiyah is the child's joy in the day adults otherwise explain in prayers and visits: it tells the child that Eid is their day too, teaches the grown-up giving and the child receiving with thanks. Today its amount and manner have become generational conversation — some families give cash, some substitute the toy or the book, and modern cities have repackaged it in cards and wrapped gift boxes. {cite:wikipedia-eidi-en}
