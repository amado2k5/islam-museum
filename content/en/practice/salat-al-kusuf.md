## Summary
Kusuf is the loss of the light of one of the two luminaries — sun or moon — in whole or part; it is commonly said kusuf for the sun and khusuf for the moon. The eclipse prayer is performed in a specified manner when either luminary darkens. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10614}

## Its ruling
Prayer at a solar eclipse is a confirmed sunna for all jurists — in one Hanafi position, obligatory; prayer at a lunar eclipse is a confirmed sunna for the Shafi'is and Hanbalis, meritorious for the Hanafis, mandub for the Malikis. The basis is the hadith in the two Sahihs: "the sun and moon are two of God's signs; they do not eclipse for anyone's death or life — when you see them, supplicate God and pray until it clears". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10614} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10615}

## Its time
Its time runs from the eclipse's appearance until it clears — "supplicate God and pray until it clears" — since it was legislated to seek the return of the blessing of light. It lapses when the sun fully clears or its time passes. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10615} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10616}

## In the disliked times
The Hanafis, the apparent Hanbali position, and one report from Malik held it is not prayed in the times when prayer is forbidden — tasbih, tahlil and istighfar take its place; the Shafi'is, with another report from Malik and Ahmad, held it is prayed at all times like other prayers that have a cause. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.10615}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
Omar Abdelkafy mentions that the Prophet stretched out his hand during the prayer as if to take something and then drew it back, and said after the prayer that a gate of Paradise had been opened to him. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:42:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep14 00:42:30}
