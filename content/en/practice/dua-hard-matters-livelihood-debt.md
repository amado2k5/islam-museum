## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers for times of strain: what is said when a matter overcomes a person or proves hard, in hard livelihood, to ward off afflictions, in calamity, in debt and in loneliness. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 125}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi gathers chapters for times of strain {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 125}. When a matter overcomes a person, he gives from Sahih Muslim, from Abu Hurayra, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said the strong believer is better and more beloved to God than the weak, and that one should not say "if only I had" but «قدر الله وما شاء فعل» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 125}. From Sunan Abi Dawud, from Awf ibn Malik, a man judged against spoke a phrase of reliance on God, and the Prophet said God blames helplessness; al-Nawawi calls it good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 125}. For a matter that proves hard, from Ibn al-Sunni, from Anas, «اللهم لا سهل إلا ما جعلته سهلا» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 125}. For hard livelihood he gives, from Ibn Umar, a supplication said on leaving the house {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 126}. For calamity he cites [[passage/quran-2-155|Qur'an 2:155]] and [[passage/quran-2-156|Qur'an 2:156]] and, from Abu Hurayra, that one should return to God even over a sandal strap {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 126}. For debt, from al-Tirmidhi, from Ali, «اللهم اكفني بحلالك عن حرامك وأغنني بفضلك عمن سواك», which al-Tirmidhi calls good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 127}. For loneliness, from al-Walid ibn al-Walid, «أعوذ بكلمات الله التامات من غضبه وعقابه وشر عباده» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 127}.
