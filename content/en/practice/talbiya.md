## Summary
What the pilgrim says on entering the sacred state for hajj or umra, as al-Nawawi gives it at the head of his Book of the Remembrances of Hajj: the intention in the heart and on the tongue, the wording of the talbiya, and its rulings and etiquette. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 191}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says the remembrances of hajj are of two kinds: those of the journey, which he leaves to the book on the traveller, and those of the rites, which he gives in the order of the rites, leaving out the evidence for fear of length {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 191}. One who means to enter the sacred state bathes, performs ablution, puts on the two cloths and prays two units, then forms the intention in the heart; it is recommended to help it with the tongue, saying that one intends the hajj and enters its sacred state for God, and then to say the talbiya. The intention in the heart is required and the utterance is recommended {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 191} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 192}. The wording of the talbiya is: «لبيك اللهم لبيك، لبيك لا شريك لك لبيك، إن الحمد والنعمة لك والملك، لا شريك لك», at Your service, O God, at Your service; You have no partner; praise, favour and dominion are Yours. He says this is the talbiya of [[person/muhammad|the Messenger of God]] {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 192}. He records that it is recommended and not a condition of a valid hajj, that it is said three times or more with blessing asked on the Prophet after it, that it is said in every state except the circuits of the Ka'ba and the walk between Safa and Marwa, and that a woman does not raise her voice with it {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 192} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 193}. On seeing something that pleases him the pilgrim says: «لبيك إن العيش عيش الآخرة», at Your service; the true life is the life to come {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 193}.
