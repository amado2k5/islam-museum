## Summary
The teacher sitting with students gathered around him in the mosque courtyard to recite the Quran and explain Hadith and jurisprudence. Muhammad Rajab al-Bayyumi considers the mosque to be, at its core, the school of Islam, and states that mosques served as institutes of learning, exposition, and exegesis. {cite:al-masjid-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 13}

## The Prophet's Mosque
Study circles for the Quran and Prophetic instruction were held in the courtyard of the Prophet's Mosque for men and women together. Later, the women complained of crowding by the men and requested a separate day from the men's day, which the Prophet granted. {cite:al-masjid-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 17}

## Mecca
After the conquest of Mecca, Mu'adh ibn Jabal remained, according to the book, teaching people the principles of their religion in the Sacred Mosque, and Ibn 'Abbas took the lead in issuing legal opinions and teaching there. {cite:al-masjid-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 30} The book cites this account without a second source, so it remains subject to verification.

## The Hajj Season
The Hajj season was an opportunity for students of knowledge to meet scholars in the Sacred Mosque, where study circles continued with the reading of biographical dictionaries and tabaqat works. {cite:al-masjid-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 31}

## Note
The book quotes Ibn Jubayr's description of the nights of completing the Quran recitation in Ramadan at the Sacred Mosque, and states that al-Shafi'i first received his education in its study circles, without a second source for this statement. {cite:al-masjid-fi-al-islam-azhar p. 30}
