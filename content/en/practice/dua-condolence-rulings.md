## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the chapter on condolence: its merit, its time, whom it includes, the dislike of sitting for it, its wording and the best words with which to console. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 148}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
In the chapter on condolence al-Nawawi gives, from al-Tirmidhi and al-Bayhaqi, from Ibn Mas'ud, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «من عزى مصابا فله مثل أجره», whoever consoles a stricken person has a reward like his, and he says its chain is weak {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 148}. From Abu Barza, in al-Tirmidhi, one who consoles a bereaved mother is clothed in a mantle in Paradise, and al-Tirmidhi says its chain is not strong {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 148}. He explains that condolence is encouraging patience and mentioning what eases the grief, and that it is recommended {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 148}, citing [[passage/quran-5-2|Qur'an 5:2]] as the best proof {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 149}. Its time runs from the death until about three days after the burial {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 149}. He reports that al-Shafi'i and his companions mildly disliked sitting for condolence {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 149}. The wording is wide, and he recommends «أعظم الله أجرك وأحسن عزاءك وغفر لميتك» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 150}. The best words, he says, are the Prophet's to a daughter's messenger, from Usama ibn Zayd in the two Sahihs: to God belongs what He took and what He gave, and all things with Him have a fixed term {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 150}.
