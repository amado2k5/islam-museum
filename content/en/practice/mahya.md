## Overview
## Mahya
Mahya are strings of lamps stretched between the minarets of Turkey's great mosques that write luminous messages in the night sky — 'welcome Ramadan', 'O month of fasting', 'welcome month of the Qur'an' — or draw patterns and figures in the air. They are raised especially in Ramadan, on the two Eids and on the blessed nights.

## Origin and craft
The custom is Ottoman, from the seventeenth century, credited to Süleyman Ağla, muezzin of the Sultanahmet mosque. The lamps were oil-lights on wires run between two minarets, the mahya-master calculating the letter-spans from the ground; a mahya mosque needed at least two minarets — which is why the art belongs to the imperial mosques of Istanbul: Sultanahmet, Ayasofya, Süleymaniye, Yeni Cami.

## Today
Oil lamps gave way to electric lights and the messages grew longer and finer, but the form endures: Istanbul's people come out on Ramadan nights to the mosque squares to watch the mahya lit after maghrib, its lighting times are announced on television and in the press as part of the month's season, mahya-masters compete — and the craft stands on Turkey's intangible-heritage lists, taught and tended. {cite:wikipedia-fanous-en}
