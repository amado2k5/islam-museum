## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers on announcing a death and the forbidden pre-Islamic announcing, what is done while washing and shrouding, and the first rules and words of the funeral prayer. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 154}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
On announcing a death al-Nawawi gives from Hudhayfa, in al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, that he asked that no one be told of his death, fearing it would be the forbidden announcing, and al-Tirmidhi calls it good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 154}. He notes that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] announced the death of the Negus to his companions {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 154}, and says the verifying scholars and most of his companions hold it recommended to inform the dead man's family and friends, the forbidden thing being the announcing of the pre-Islamic age {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 154}. In washing the dead it is recommended to remember God often and pray for him; the washer who sees something pleasing may tell of it, but is forbidden to tell of what he dislikes {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 154}. From Abu Rafi in al-Bayhaqi, one who washes the dead and conceals what he sees is forgiven forty times, and he adds al-Hakim's grading of it as sound on Muslim's condition {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 155}. The prayer is a communal duty with four takbirs, the opening sura after the first, a blessing on the Prophet after the second and prayer for the dead after the third {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 155}. From Ibn Abbas in al-Bukhari, he recited the opening sura at a funeral and said it was to show it is the sunna {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 156}.
