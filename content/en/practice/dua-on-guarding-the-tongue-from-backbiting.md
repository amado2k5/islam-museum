## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers at the close of the chapter on silence, from the early scholars, and in the chapter on the prohibition of backbiting and tale-bearing: their definitions, their ruling and the reports on them. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 335}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi ends the chapter on silence with sayings. He says that «من صمت نجا», whoever is silent is saved, has a weak chain and that he mentions it only to point this out, since it is well known {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 335}. He gives the account of Qass ibn Sa'ida and Aktham ibn Sayfi counting human faults, and the one quality that covers them all, guarding the tongue {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 335}; and al-Qushayri's report from Abu Ali al-Daqqaq that whoever is silent about the truth is a mute devil {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 335}. The next chapter, on forbidding backbiting and tale-bearing, defines backbiting as mentioning a person with what he dislikes, in body, religion, worldly matters or otherwise, by word, writing or gesture, and tale-bearing as carrying people's words to one another to cause corruption; he says both are forbidden by the consensus of Muslims {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 336}. He cites [[passage/quran-49-12|Qur'an 49:12]], [[passage/quran-104-1|104:1]] and [[passage/quran-68-11|68:11]] {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 337}; from the two Sahihs, from Hudhayfa, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «لا يدخل الجنة نمام», no tale-bearer enters Paradise {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 337}; and from Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i, from Abu Hurayra, the definition «ذكرك أخاك بما يكره», mentioning your brother with what he dislikes, which al-Tirmidhi calls good and sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 337}.
