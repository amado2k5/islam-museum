## Overview
## Jashn-e taklif
Jashn-e taklif is the Shia celebration of religious majority: a party held for the boy or girl reaching the age of legal responsibility — nine lunar years for a girl and fifteen for a boy in the Imami view — at which the child is dressed in their best, given a Qur'an and gifts, has verses recited and is told they are now mukallaf — bound by prayer, fasting and the law — so that puberty becomes a feast, not a secret.

## Its meaning and places
It is current in Iran, Pakistan and India and among Iraqi, Bahraini and Afghan Shias, held at homes, Husayniyyas and schools as a joint ceremony for a whole class of children; its idea tells the child that entering the world of worship is a celebration, not a burden — the new heart is congratulated on its first prayer and first fast. A part of it is reciting to the child a passage of the Makarim al-Akhlaq supplication or memorising the verse of accountability.

## Its Sunni parallels
Sunnis have no celebration of this name, but the meaning of taklif exists with them from puberty and its signs — and the famous hadith bids a boy be told to pray at seven and disciplined for it at ten. Some modern Sunni families have begun marking a child's first full fast or first mosque prayer under the loose name 'first taklif' — a new custom with no old name. {cite:wikipedia-jashn-e-taklif-en}
