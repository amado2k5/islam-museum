## Overview
Laylat al-qadr is a noble, blessed, exalted, preferred night. It was so named, it is said, because what will be in that year — good and provision — is decreed on it; or because qadr here means constriction, for it is concealed; or judgment and decree, for what the angels write on it of provisions and lifespans. The majority hold the "blessed night" of "We sent it down on a blessed night… on it every wise matter is resolved" is laylat al-qadr, not mid-Sha'ban. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13731} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13732}

## Its merit
The jurists hold it the best of nights: "laylat al-qadr is better than a thousand months." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.13732}

## In al-Sha'rawi's programme "Liqa' al-Iman"
In episode 28 of [[work/likaa-al-iman|Liqa' al-Iman]] [[person/muhammad-mutawalli-al-shaarawi|al-Sha'rawi]] says qadr means honour and rank, and that rank varies with the one who raises: if God raised this night, its rank has no rank beyond it. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:03:00} He holds that its honour lies in the descent of the Qur'an in it, the way of life that secures for a person a standing not cut off by death and a bliss that does not perish, as a maker sets the purpose of his product before making it. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:05:00} He says its being in Ramadan is beyond doubt; the narrations then narrow it to the last ten nights, once to their odd nights and once to their even ones. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:05:30} He sees the wisdom of its concealment in its being sought across ten nights rather than one. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:06:00} He reconciles the odd and even narrations by noting that the last ten begin on the twenty-first if the month has thirty days; if it has twenty-nine and the nights are counted from the end of the month, the odd become even and the even odd. {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-muhammad-metwally-al-shaarawy likaa-al-iman/ep28 00:07:00}

## In Abu Ishaq al-Heweny's lessons "Madrasat al-Hayat"
[[person/abu-ishaq-al-heweny|Abu Ishaq al-Heweny]] opens one lesson of [[work/madrassat-al-hayat|Madrasat al-Hayat]] by saying that it is the night of 27 Ramadan 1432; he says many people believe it to be Laylat al-Qadr, and that because the moon-sightings differ between Muslim countries its date is not certain, Egypt and Saudi Arabia differing by a day every year. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:03:00} He cites Ibn Umar's hadith that the Companions' dreams agreed on the last seven nights, the hadiths on seeking it in the odd nights of the last ten, and al-Shafi'i's preference for the night of the twenty-first. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:04:00}

He narrates the hadith of Zirr ibn Hubaysh in Muslim, which he says al-Tirmidhi and others graded sound: Zirr asked Ubayy ibn Ka'b about Ibn Mas'ud's saying that whoever stands the whole year will find Laylat al-Qadr, and Ubayy said Ibn Mas'ud meant that people should not rely on one night, and that he knew it to be the twenty-seventh; and he gave its sign, that the sun rises the next morning white and without rays, like the moon. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:06:30} Al-Heweny says the sign does not change even if the night moves, that some sound hadiths mention the last night of Ramadan, which may be the thirtieth, and he advises whoever wishes to find it to stand all ten nights, quoting al-Tirmidhi that most hadiths point to the odd nights. {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-abu-ishaq-alheweny madrassat-al-hayat/ep03 00:08:30}
