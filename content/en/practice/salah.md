## Summary
"The prayer" is an obligatory worship, one of [[concept/five-pillars|Islam's five pillars]] {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.192}. In the hadith of Talha ibn Ubayd Allah about the Bedouin the obligation is "five prayers in the day and night", and what exceeds them is voluntary {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.529}.

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an commands establishing the prayer coupled with the zakat: [[passage/quran-2-43|al-Baqara 43]]. It describes it as "a timed ordinance" upon the believers: [[passage/quran-4-103|al-Nisa' 103]]. The verse of al-Isra' mentions prayer times: [[passage/quran-17-78|al-Isra' 78]].

## In the hadith
Ibn Khuzayma gives a chapter on the proof that establishing the prayer is part of Islam, and in it reports Umar's account of Gabriel's question about [[concept/islam-din|Islam]] {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.191}.

Ibn Khuzayma narrates the hadith of Talha ibn Ubayd Allah about the Bedouin with the words "five prayers in the day and night"; the man asked whether anything else was required, and the Prophet said: "No, unless you do something voluntarily" {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.529}.

## What is not covered here
The detail of prayer times, its form, its rulings and the schools' disagreements on it will be added in a later batch from the fiqh books, with each position's school identified.

## For Sprouts
Prayer is performed five times a day. It is one of the pillars of Islam, and in it the Muslim stands before God with the whole heart.

## For Explorers
Prayer is performed five times a day and night, and it is one of the pillars of Islam. In it the Muslim stands before God, recites, bows and prostrates, and prays in the mosque or at home.

## For Scholars
The prescribed prayer, five times in the day and night, one of the five pillars of Islam. The Qur'an commands its establishment and calls it a timed obligation on the believers, and the hadith adds that other prayers are voluntary. It consists of standing, recitation, bowing and prostration, and the jurists fixed its times from the Book and the Sunna. The jurists differ on the details of its pillars, conditions and recommended acts, so this entry gives its basis without weighing the schools against each other. Wudu and the mosque are linked to prayer, and each has its own entry.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 02)

The Sheikh explains that the person praying says, when he gives the closing salam, the plural form 'peace be upon you and God's mercy', that in the tashahhud he says 'peace be upon us and upon the righteous servants of God', and that the singular form of address is used only to the Prophet in 'peace be upon you, O Prophet' {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:01:30}.

He mentions that Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali used to sit before the Prophet and greet him, and that the person praying has his teacher, as it were, in front of him, so he says 'peace be upon you, O Prophet' so that his mind does not wander; he begins with the greetings, prayers and good things belonging to God, then greets the Prophet, then himself (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:02:00}.

The Sheikh takes up the case of someone who begins to pray after years of not praying, giving the example of a man who starts at twenty with some seven or eight years of prayers owed, and he reports of the Hanbalis that once a person has begun to pray nothing is owed for what has passed, noting that Imam Ahmad regarded a person who does not pray as a disbeliever outside the community (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:12:30}.

He says that the majority hold that a person who owes days of prayers should make them up, taking the more cautious course, and mentions that there is an easier view, that he need not make them up but should pray many voluntary prayers, and he thinks that scholarly honesty requires presenting both views {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:13:00}.

He says he leans to the view of the majority, so that a person prays what is owed until he is fairly sure he has made up everything he missed, and if it turns out on the Day of Resurrection that nothing was owed, he hopes that God will count them in his favour {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:13:30}.

He speaks of women's prayers lost because of bleeding, such as the forty days after childbirth, mentions that a mother may tell her daughter to wait forty days without praying or fasting even though the bleeding may stop after a week, and suggests, as the more cautious course, that a make-up prayer be prayed before the obligatory prayer (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:14:00}.

He urges making up missed prayer, fasting and zakat because they are a debt owed to God, then mentions the rights of people, the nearest being the right of one's wife and children not to be fed from what is unlawful or doubtful, and then a person's repentance with himself and his heart {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep02 01:14:30}.

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 07)

The Sheikh says that an imam's error in recitation does not invalidate the prayer and that he should not be blamed for it, and he mentions that sins fall away as the leaves of a tree fall (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:24:30}.

He holds that error in recitation is to be expected, because the Qur'an is precious and anyone may slip over a vowel or a shortening, so one should not be strict about it, and he says that the follower has no prostration of forgetfulness of his own but follows his imam {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:25:00}.

He says that the imam is a guarantor and the muezzin is trusted, that the imam bears responsibility for the prayer of those behind him, and that the follower follows him in the prostration of forgetfulness even if he himself did not forget (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep07 00:25:30}.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series on the Hikam of Ibn Ata Allah
In a lesson on law Omar Abdelkafy explains the conditions for a valid prayer, comparing them to the conditions for putting up a building, and lists among them purity of body, clothes and place; he says that one with a standing excuse, such as continuous incontinence, prays and his prayer is valid, and that the urine of a nursing boy who has not yet eaten food is sprinkled with water. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 01:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 01:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 01:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 01:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 01:12:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy sharh-al-hekam-al-ataeya/ep15 01:13:30}
