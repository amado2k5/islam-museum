## Summary
"The prayer" is an obligatory worship, one of [[concept/five-pillars|Islam's five pillars]] {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.192}. In the hadith of Talha ibn Ubayd Allah about the Bedouin the obligation is "five prayers in the day and night", and what exceeds them is voluntary {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.529}.

## In the Qur'an
The Qur'an commands establishing the prayer coupled with the zakat: [[passage/quran-2-43|al-Baqara 43]]. It describes it as "a timed ordinance" upon the believers: [[passage/quran-4-103|al-Nisa' 103]]. The verse of al-Isra' mentions prayer times: [[passage/quran-17-78|al-Isra' 78]].

## In the hadith
Ibn Khuzayma gives a chapter on the proof that establishing the prayer is part of Islam, and in it reports Umar's account of Gabriel's question about [[concept/islam-din|Islam]] {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.191}.

Ibn Khuzayma narrates the hadith of Talha ibn Ubayd Allah about the Bedouin with the words "five prayers in the day and night"; the man asked whether anything else was required, and the Prophet said: "No, unless you do something voluntarily" {cite:sahih-ibn-khuzayma p.529}.

## What is not covered here
The detail of prayer times, its form, its rulings and the schools' disagreements on it will be added in a later batch from the fiqh books, with each position's school identified.

## For Sprouts
Prayer is performed five times a day. It is one of the pillars of Islam, and in it the Muslim stands before God with the whole heart.

## For Explorers
Prayer is performed five times a day and night, and it is one of the pillars of Islam. In it the Muslim stands before God, recites, bows and prostrates, and prays in the mosque or at home.

## For Scholars
The prescribed prayer, five times in the day and night, one of the five pillars of Islam. The Qur'an commands its establishment and calls it a timed obligation on the believers, and the hadith adds that other prayers are voluntary. It consists of standing, recitation, bowing and prostration, and the jurists fixed its times from the Book and the Sunna. The jurists differ on the details of its pillars, conditions and recommended acts, so this entry gives its basis without weighing the schools against each other. Wudu and the mosque are linked to prayer, and each has its own entry.
