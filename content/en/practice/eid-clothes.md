## Overview
## Eid clothes
Wearing the new or the best on Eid is both sunnah-in-spirit and universal custom: the jurists like a Muslim to adorn himself on Eid in his finest and cleanest clothes; Ibn Umar is reported to have worn his best clothes on the Eid day, and the Prophet is reported with a red burd he wore on the feasts. Buying 'Eid clothes' for children in the last days of Ramadan became a whole commercial season by which the month's end is measured.

## Its forms across countries
In Arabia the new white thawb with the shemagh and agal and the bisht over it for men; in Egypt a new jalabiyya or modern clothes for the boys; in the Levant the new 'set' for the little ones; in Morocco the embroidered jellaba and balgha slippers; in South Asia fresh shalwar kameez and kurta and the white prayer cap; in Indonesia the baju koko and sarong; in West Africa the embroidered agbada; in Turkey new clean clothes called bayramlık.

## Its meaning
Adornment here is thanks, not pride, in the accepted view: displaying God's bounty on the day of assembly, teaching children that days have shapes to be worn as well as prayed, honouring host and guest. Newness is not required — the clean old garment fulfils the sunnah when the new is beyond reach, and an expensive new garment adds nothing to the reward. {cite:wikipedia-takbir-en}
