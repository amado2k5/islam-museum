## Summary
Nikah in the language applies both to the marital act and to the contract apart from it; juristically it is the contract from which lawful mutual enjoyment between the spouses results — its precise definition and the word's true reference being matters of juristic disagreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}

## Definitions across the schools
For the Hanafis: a contract conferring ownership of licit enjoyment of a woman by intent. For the Malikis: a contract rendering enjoyment of a lawful woman licit, by formula. For the Shafi'is: a contract comprising the licitness of intercourse through the words inkah, tazwij or their translation. For the Hanbalis: the marriage contract in which the word nikah, tazwij or their equivalent is observed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}

## The word's true reference
Three views: nikah properly denotes the act and metaphorically the contract — the sound Hanafi position; properly the contract and metaphorically the act — the preponderant Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali position; or properly both — on a third view differing between homonymy and univocity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16035}

## A consequence of the difference
On the disagreement turns, among other rulings, affinity: one who commits zina with a woman finds her forbidden to his ascendants and descendants for the Hanafis and in the better-known Hanbali view, while the Shafi'is and the relied-upon Maliki position hold that zina establishes no affinity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16036}

## In Abdul Hamid Kishk's sermons
[[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Abdul Hamid Kishk]] says the aim of marriage is tranquillity in the widest sense: tranquillity of the heart, which finds rest from the troubles of life, and of the body. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:45:00} He comments on a report in Akhbar al-Yawm about a wife who took her husband to court, asks why the courts have entered homes, and says the Prophet's Medina had not a single court or policeman; this is his view. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:43:30}
