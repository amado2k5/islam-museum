## Summary
Nikah in the language applies both to the marital act and to the contract apart from it; juristically it is the contract from which lawful mutual enjoyment between the spouses results — its precise definition and the word's true reference being matters of juristic disagreement. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}

## Definitions across the schools
For the Hanafis: a contract conferring ownership of licit enjoyment of a woman by intent. For the Malikis: a contract rendering enjoyment of a lawful woman licit, by formula. For the Shafi'is: a contract comprising the licitness of intercourse through the words inkah, tazwij or their translation. For the Hanbalis: the marriage contract in which the word nikah, tazwij or their equivalent is observed. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034}

## The word's true reference
Three views: nikah properly denotes the act and metaphorically the contract — the sound Hanafi position; properly the contract and metaphorically the act — the preponderant Maliki, Shafi'i and Hanbali position; or properly both — on a third view differing between homonymy and univocity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16034} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16035}

## A consequence of the difference
On the disagreement turns, among other rulings, affinity: one who commits zina with a woman finds her forbidden to his ascendants and descendants for the Hanafis and in the better-known Hanbali view, while the Shafi'is and the relied-upon Maliki position hold that zina establishes no affinity. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.16036}

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Bustan al-Amal
The Sheikh takes up mothers' anxiety over their daughters' delayed marriage, holding that parents set conditions on a righteous suitor like treaties, and that 'we made the lawful difficult so the unlawful became easy', whereas the early generations made it easy so the unlawful was hard; he mentions the hadith 'seek even an iron ring' and marrying for what one knows of the Qur'an, and criticises wedding costs that load the husband with debt. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:03:30} He notes the difference between 'khutba' with a u-vowel, delivered from the pulpit, and 'khitba' with an i-vowel, the marriage proposal, which carries humility and submission, saying it is the only time a young man humbles himself before his father-in-law. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:05:00}
He holds that marriage is provision that comes when God wills, likening it to a foetus that, born before its time, is born incomplete, and tells of a calm man on a storm-tossed ship whose wife asked about his calm; he drew a dagger and said she did not fear it because it was in the hand of one who loved her, and the ship was in the hand of One who loved them, God (needs_source: the story as the Sheikh tells it). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:08:30} He ends with the hadith 'if there comes to you one whose religion and character please you, marry him', and urges young men to choose a religious wife because beauty becomes familiar while character remains. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy bustan-el-amal/ep19 00:11:00}

## In Abdul Hamid Kishk's sermons
[[person/abdul-hamid-kishk|Abdul Hamid Kishk]] says the aim of marriage is tranquillity in the widest sense: tranquillity of the heart, which finds rest from the troubles of life, and of the body. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:45:00} He comments on a report in Akhbar al-Yawm about a wife who took her husband to court, asks why the courts have entered homes, and says the Prophet's Medina had not a single court or policeman; this is his view. {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:43:00} {cite:assabile-abdul-hamid-kishk shorut-kobol-assalat/ep02 00:43:30}

## In al-Nawawi's al-Adhkar
In the Book of the Remembrances of Marriage al-Nawawi says a suitor is recommended to begin with praise of God and blessing on His Messenger and then to state his wish, citing Abu Hurayra's report in the Sunan of Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah that speech not begun with praise of God is maimed, which he calls a good report {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 281}. At the contract it is recommended to give an address before it; by sound chains, in the Sunan of Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, he gives the address of need, which opens «الحمد لله نستعينه ونستغفره ونعوذ به من شرور أنفسنا، من يهد الله فلا مضل له», praise to God, whose help and forgiveness we seek, and in whom we take refuge from the evil of ourselves; then come the two testimonies and three verses. He quotes al-Tirmidhi's grading of it as good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 282}. He says its least form is «الحمد لله، والصلاة على رسول الله، أوصي بتقوى الله», that it is a recommended practice without which the marriage is valid by agreement of the scholars, and that what is reported from Dawud al-Zahiri to the contrary is not counted {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 282} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 283}. After the contract the groom is told: «بارك الله لك، وبارك عليك، وجمع بينكما في خير», may God bless you and bless what comes to you, and join you both in good, which he reports by sound chains in the Sunan of Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, from Abu Hurayra, and which al-Tirmidhi graded good and sound; from the two Sahihs he gives the words of [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] to Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, «بارك الله لك» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 283}. He says it is disliked to wish the groom harmony and sons in the old formula {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 283}. When his wife is brought to him he names God and says what al-Nawawi reports by sound chains, in the Sunan of Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah and Ibn al-Sunni's book, from Amr ibn Shu'ayb, from his father, from his grandfather: «اللهم إني أسألك خيرها وخير ما جبلتها عليه، وأعوذ بك من شرها وشر ما جبلتها عليه» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 284}.
