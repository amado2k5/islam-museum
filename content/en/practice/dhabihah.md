## Overview
In fiqh, dhabh contrasts with nahr: nahr is stabbing the upper chest — recommended for camels — while dhabh is for sheep and the like; 'aqr is a fatal wound to an animal wherever it cannot be reached for slaughter, by arrow or by beasts and birds of prey. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8380}

## Tadhkiya
The verbal noun of dakkaytu — slaughtering or nahr; in usage, the means that permits eating a wild animal at will, and for the Hanafis the lawful way of preserving the animal's purity and permitting its flesh if edible, or its hide and hair if not. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8381}

## Its effect
Tadhkiya affects both the edible and the inedible animal; the conditions of the slaughterer and the slaughtered are detailed in the schools' treatments of dhaba'ih. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8381} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8412}
