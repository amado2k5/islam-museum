## Overview
## Chaand raat
Chaand raat — Hindustani 'night of the moon' — is Eid eve among the Muslims of the South Asian subcontinent: on the evening of the last day of Ramadan families and friends gather on rooftops and in open squares to spot the Shawwal crescent. If it is seen, Eid is declared and the 'Eid Mubarak' greetings begin; if not, the fast runs a thirtieth day.

## How it is spent
Once the moon is sighted, mosques, radio and television announce it and the festivities start at once and run to the dawn prayer. Bazaars and malls stay open deep into the night strung with lights, streets and government buildings and mosques are lit up, families rush out for last-minute shopping — shalwar kameez, bangles, jewellery, bags, shoes, children's toys — barbershops and beauty parlours fill in the evening, and at home women and girls have their hands patterned with mehndi while tomorrow's dishes are prepared — sheer khurma and sweets — and food and sweets are sent to friends and neighbours.

## Its social weight
Its place in subcontinental culture is often compared to Christmas Eve in the West: a night of gathering before the feast, when trade overflows and homes open. It is more festive than the night before Eid al-Adha precisely because its date is not known in advance — it turns on that night's crescent — so the anticipation is keener. Officially the moon is announced by Pakistan's Central Ruet-e-Hilal Committee and India's Central Moon Sighting Committee, but the joy itself is wholly popular. {cite:wikipedia-chaand-raat-en}

## In more detail
The custom crystallized in Mughal India (16th-18th centuries) — Chandni Chowk in Delhi, Lahore, Lucknow — as an all-night bazaar where women bought bangles and applied mehndi; Mughal miniatures and the shahr-ashob poetry of Mir and Nazir Akbarabadi describe the illuminated Meena Bazaars. Outside the subcontinent it lives mainly in the diaspora; parallel nights exist elsewhere — laylat al-ja'iza in the Arab world, malam takbiran in Indonesia, arefe gecesi in Turkey — without the bazaar's name {cite:wikipedia-chaand-raat-en}.
