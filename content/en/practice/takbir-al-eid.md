## Overview
## The Eid takbir
The takbir of the Eids — "Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, la ilaha illa Allah; Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, wa lillahi'l-hamd" — is the voice of Eid itself: recited on Eid al-Fitr from the sunset of the last night of Ramadan until the Eid prayer in the morning, and on Eid al-Adha from the dawn of the day of Arafat or the morning of the sacrifice day until the afternoon of the last tashriq day, in the preferred view. The unbound takbir is at all times; the bound form follows each obligatory prayer.

## Its form and meaning
Raising the voice in takbir through markets, streets and mosques from the last night of Ramadan breaks the ordinary day with remembrance: a declaration that the coming day is God's, and thanks for completing the fast or being granted the rite. Ibn al-Qayyim counts the day of sacrifice the best of days with God — the day of the greater hajj, in which slaughter, stoning, shaving, tawaf, sa'y and takbir were all legislated; the verse ties it to completing the count: "that you complete the number and magnify God for having guided you, and that you may give thanks."

## Its wording
Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas had: "Allahu akbar kabira, wa'l-hamdu lillahi kathira, wa subhan Allahi bukratan wa asila"; other salaf had the doubled form "Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, la ilaha illa Allah; Allahu akbar, Allahu akbar, wa lillahi'l-hamd." In the age of broadcast the takbir is relayed from mosque loudspeakers, television and radio, so that whole cities intone a single melody on Eid eve. {cite:wikipedia-takbir-en}
