## Overview
## Arba'in
Arba'in — chehelom in Persian, chaliswan in Urdu, erbain in Turkish — is the fortieth-day memorial of a death or martyrdom. In Sunni and Shia cultures alike the forty days mark the formal mourning period, but for the Shia Arba'in became the great observance of 20 Safar — forty days after the killing of al-Husayn ibn Ali at Karbala in 61 AH (680 CE).

## The Arba'in pilgrimage
The Arba'in walk to Karbala is today the largest regular gathering in the Muslim world, perhaps in the world: crowds estimated in the millions walk on foot from Najaf, Basra, Baghdad and Iran to Karbala over tens and hundreds of kilometres, and along the routes stand the mawakeb — Husayni service tents — offering food, drink, lodging and treatment free to every passer-by, until the walk itself became a seasonal obligation for Husayn's devotees and a lesson in hospitality and solidarity.

## Its meaning and the debate
The fortieth day ends the formal mourning in many Muslim cultures — except the widow's 'idda of four months and ten — and it is marked with recitation, prayer and alms for the dead. The Shia have kept it since the first centuries, relating that the Ahl al-Bayt visited Husayn's grave on the fortieth day; Sunnis do not count it a binding religious observance and differ between disapproval and a studied view of the visit itself — though all agree on the standing of al-Husayn, which the museum describes with the difference stated, not settled. {cite:wikipedia-arbaeen-en}
