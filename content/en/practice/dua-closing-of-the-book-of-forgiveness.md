## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers at the end of the Book of Forgiveness: sayings on asking forgiveness, the prohibition of a whole day's silence, and the hadiths on which Islam turns, which he appends to the book. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 405}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Near the end of the Book of Forgiveness al-Nawawi reports the saying of al-Adawiyya that our asking of forgiveness needs much asking of forgiveness itself, and a Bedouin who clung to the curtains of the Kaaba saying «اللهم إن استغفاري مع إصراري لؤم، وإن تركي الاستغفار مع علمي بسعة عفوك لعجز», O God, my asking forgiveness while I persist is baseness, and my leaving it, knowing the breadth of Your pardon, is incapacity {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 405}. Then comes the chapter forbidding a whole day's silence: from Sunan Abi Dawud, by a good chain, from Ali, that he memorised from [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] «ولا صمات يوم إلى الليل» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 405}; and from al-Bukhari, from Qays ibn Abi Hazim, that Abu Bakr told a woman who had made the pilgrimage in silence to speak, since this was not lawful and was a deed of the pre-Islamic age {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 405}. At the close of what he intended for the book he appends the hadiths on which Islam turns, thirty in all with those he added {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 405}: first Umar's «إنما الأعمال بالنيات» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 405}; second, from Aisha in the two Sahihs, «من أحدث في أمرنا هذا ما ليس منه فهو رد» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 406}; fifth, from al-Hasan ibn Ali, «دع ما يريبك إلى ما لا يريبك», which al-Tirmidhi calls sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 406}; and tenth, Tamim al-Dari's «الدين النصيحة» in Sahih Muslim {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 407}.
