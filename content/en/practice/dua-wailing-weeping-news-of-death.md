## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers on the prohibition of wailing and the cries of the pre-Islamic age, the allowance of tearful weeping, and what was said on the news of Abu Jahl's death. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 146}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi gives from Ibn al-Sunni's book, from Ibn Mas'ud, that he told [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] Abu Jahl had been killed, and the Prophet said «الحمد لله الذي نصر عبده وأعز دينه», praise be to God who helped His servant and made His religion mighty {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 146}. He then says the community agreed that wailing over the dead and the cries of the pre-Islamic age are forbidden {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 146}. From the two Sahihs, from Ibn Mas'ud, he gives «ليس منا من لطم الخدود وشق الجيوب ودعا بدعوى الجاهلية», and from Abu Musa that the Prophet disowned the woman who raises her voice in wailing, the one who shaves her head and the one who tears her clothes {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 146}. He distinguishes weeping without lament: from Ibn Umar, in the two Sahihs, the Prophet wept at Sa'd ibn Ubada's bedside and said God does not punish for the tears of the eye or the grief of the heart; from Usama ibn Zayd, «هذه رحمة جعلها الله في قلوب عباده» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 147} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 147}. The hadiths on the dead punished by their families' weeping are to be interpreted, most plausibly as concerning one who caused it, and weeping is allowed before and after death, though before is better {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 148}.
