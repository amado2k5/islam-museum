## Summary
I'tikaf — linguistically, adhering and holding fast — is, in the law, remaining in the mosque in a specified manner with intention. It is sunna and becomes binding only by vow. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2996} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2997}

## Related terms
It differs from khalwa in that it may be performed with others in the place set for it; from ribat in that it is not confined to frontier posts; and from jiwar, the broader term, since i'tikaf is itself called jiwar in A'isha's report "while he was in retreat (mujawir) in the mosque". {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2997}

## Its wisdom
In i'tikaf the retreatant surrenders himself wholly to the worship of God seeking nearness, withdraws from worldly distraction, and immerses his hours in prayer in fact or in effect — its original purpose being to await the congregational prayer — likening himself to the angels who glorify night and day without flagging. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2997}
