## Overview
Aqiqah is the animal slaughtered for the newborn; Fiqh al-Sunna defines it and affirms it is a confirmed sunna that the Prophet and his Companions performed {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah}.

## In the sources
Fiqh al-Sunna details the aqiqa's definition and ruling, noting it is a confirmed sunna even if the father is in straitened means {cite:sabiq-fiqh-al-sunnah}.

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam lists the aqiqah under birth rites and describes it as a ritual that includes shaving the child's head, giving money to the poor, sacrificing an animal and naming the child, and says the adhan is spoken into the child's right ear and the iqama into the left. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e177}

This is a general description for a non-specialist that puts in one line matters the jurists treat separately: the Dictionary counts the money given as part of the rite, while the juristic definition of the aqiqah as the slaughtered animal, and its ruling, are as Fiqh al-Sunna gives them above. It is recorded here as the Dictionary's wording, not as a statement of the rulings on the aqiqah.
