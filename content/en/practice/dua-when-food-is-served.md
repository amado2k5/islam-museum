## Summary
What is said when food is brought, as al-Nawawi gives it at the head of his Book of the Remembrances of Eating and Drinking: Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As's report from Ibn al-Sunni's book, and the recommendation that a host invite his guests to eat. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 228}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi opens the Book of the Remembrances of Eating and Drinking with the chapter on what is said when food is brought near. In it he reports from Ibn al-Sunni's book, from Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said over food set before him: «اللهم بارك لنا فيما رزقتنا، وقنا عذاب النار، بسم الله», O God, bless for us what You have provided us and keep us from the punishment of the Fire; in God's name {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 228}. He states no judgement on its chain at this point {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 228}. He then gives a chapter recommending that the host say to his guests when he serves them «كلوا», eat, or «بسم الله», or words to that effect, as a plain permission to begin {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 229}. He says this is not required, that setting out the food is permission enough, that the view of those of his colleagues who required an utterance is not the correct one, and that the wording of permission found in the sound reports is to be read as a recommendation {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 229}. The chapter on naming God at eating and drinking follows {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 229}.
