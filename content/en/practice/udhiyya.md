## Summary
The udhiyya (sacrificial animal) — also called dahiyya, plural adahi/dahaya — is, in the law, what is slaughtered seeking nearness to God on the days of nahr; the Day of Sacrifice is named for it. What is slaughtered for sale or food is not of it, nor what is slaughtered outside those days, nor the 'aqiqa or the compensations of pilgrimage. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2864}

## Beside qurban and hady
Qurban is broader — anything offered for nearness, slaughtered or not. The hady is what is slaughtered of livestock in the Sanctuary on the days of nahr for tamattu', qiran, or to redress a rite; when intended purely for nearness it resembles the udhiyya so closely that only intention separates them: the hady's vower intends an offering conveyed to the Sanctuary, the udhiyya's intends slaughter specific to the excellent days. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2864} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2865}

## 'Aqiqa, far' and 'atira
The 'aqiqa is slaughtered in thanks for a newborn; the udhiyya thanks for the blessing of life itself. The far' — the first-born of the flock which the Jahiliyya slaughtered for their idols — came to be slaughtered for God, and the 'atira — a Rajab sacrifice to their deities — likewise, without obligation or fixed time. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2865}

## The status of the sacrifice in al-Nawawi's al-Majmu'

[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] sets out in [[work/al-majmu-sharh-al-muhadhdhab|al-Majmu']] the scholars' views on the sacrifice:

:::perspectives
- **Sunni, Shafi'i and the majority: an emphasised sunna for one who can afford it, not obligatory**: the view of most scholars, among them Abu Bakr, Umar, Bilal, Abu Mas'ud al-Badri, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, Ata', Alqama, al-Aswad, Malik, Ahmad, Abu Yusuf, Ishaq, Abu Thawr, al-Muzani, Dawud and Ibn al-Mundhir. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.358}
- **Sunni, Hanafi and those who agree: obligatory for one who can afford it**: held by Rabi'a, al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Abu Hanifa and al-Awza'i, except for the pilgrim at Mina; Muhammad ibn al-Hasan held it obligatory for town residents; the well-known view of Abu Hanifa is that it is obligatory for a resident who owns the zakat threshold. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.359}
:::

Al-Nawawi gives the evidence of those who hold it obligatory: the Prophet's own sacrifice together with the command to follow his example in [[passage/quran-33-21|al-Ahzab 21]]; the hadith of Abu Ramla ibn Mikhnaf that "every household owes each year a sacrifice and an atira", reporting al-Tirmidhi's grading of it as good and al-Khattabi's remark that its source is weak because Abu Ramla is unknown; Jundab's hadith ordering one who slaughtered before the prayer to slaughter again; and Abu Hurayra's hadith "whoever has the means and does not sacrifice, let him not come near our prayer-place", reporting from al-Bayhaqi, citing al-Tirmidhi, that it is soundly attributed to Abu Hurayra himself. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.359}
