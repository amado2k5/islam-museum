## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the chapter on the qunut in the dawn prayer: its standing and place, the supplication of al-Hasan ibn Ali reported for it, the qunut attributed to Umar, and the rulings for imam and follower. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 59}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi says the qunut in the dawn prayer is an emphasised sunna in his school, and he cites a report from Anas that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] kept to the qunut in the dawn prayer until he left this world, which al-Hakim gives in his Forty and calls sound {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 59}. He then reports from al-Hasan ibn Ali, by a sound chain in the Sunan, that the Prophet taught him words to say in the witr, beginning «اللهم اهدني فيمن هديت وعافني فيمن عافيت وتولني فيمن توليت», O God, guide me among those You have guided; al-Tirmidhi called it good and said nothing better is known for the qunut {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 60}. He says his companions held that the qunut reported from Umar is also good, a supplication of asking help and forgiveness and professing faith, then asking forgiveness for believing men and women and good relations among them {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 60}. He says an imam says «اللهم اهدنا» in the plural, and reports from Thawban in Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi that one who leads a people and singles himself out for a supplication has betrayed them {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 61}. He says a follower who hears the imam's loud qunut says amin to his supplication {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 62}.
