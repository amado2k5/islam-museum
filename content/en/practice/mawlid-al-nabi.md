## Overview
The celebration of the birthday of the Prophet on the twelfth of Rabi' al-Awwal in most of the Islamic world; processions, praise poems of the Prophet, markets and charitable projects. {cite:wikidata}

## Description
Most scholars approve it as a good custom, while some object that it is an innovation; its crowds in Egypt, Morocco and Sudan are among the largest popular celebrations. {cite:wikidata}

## In The Oxford Dictionary of Islam
The Oxford Dictionary of Islam describes Mawlid al-Nabi as the Prophet Muhammad's birthday, observed on the twelfth of Rabi al-Awwal. It is a popular celebration with Qur'an readings, recited poetry, songs about Muhammad's virtues, and food made in his name and handed out to the poor. The entry adds that, apart from Saudi Arabia, it likewise serves as a time for state ceremonies. The Dictionary notes that some conservatives condemn the celebration because it was not kept in Muhammad's lifetime and focuses on a human being instead of God. {cite:oxford-dictionary-of-islam e1483}
