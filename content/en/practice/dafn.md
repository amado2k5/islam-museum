## Summary
Burial — concealing the dead in the earth — is a communal obligation by consensus where possible. People have handed it down since Adam with censure of whoever neglects it; the first to perform it was Qabil, shown by the crow how to cover his brother's shame in Sura al-Ma'ida. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8216} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8217}

## When impossible
If one dies on a ship far from land, he is washed, shrouded and prayed over, then consigned to the sea weighted to sink; al-Shafi'i said he is weighted if near a shore, in case he reaches people who will bury him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8217}

## Its place
The cemetery is the best place — for following precedent and for the prayers of passers-by; the Prophet was buried in his house since prophets are buried where they die. Burial in a dwelling is disliked; the Malikis stated it is disliked in a purpose-built mosque, and the Hanbalis forbid it in mosque, madrasa and ribat. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8217}

## Moving the dead
The Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis held exhuming and moving a buried body absolutely impermissible — a later Hanafi licence was rejected by Ibn 'Abidin. Before burial, the Hanafis allowed moving the body without restriction; the majority of Shafi'is and Hanbalis forbade it except for a valid purpose, the position of al-Awza'i and Ibn al-Mundhir. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8217} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8218}

## For Scholars
Dafn, in the language covering and concealing, is in the technical sense laying the dead to rest in the earth; it is a communal obligation by consensus when possible, handed down since Adam with blame on whoever abandons it — first done by Qabil when God guided him to bury his brother by the crow's story in al-Ma'ida. The Kuwaiti fiqh encyclopaedia details: one who dies at sea far from land is washed, shrouded, prayed over and committed to the sea weighted — al-Shafi'i says weighted if near the coast so he may reach people who bury him. The cemetery is the best place, for following custom and the passers-by's prayers; the Prophet was buried in his house, prophets being buried where they die; burial in a house is disliked, the Malikis declaring it so in a mosque built for prayer and the Hanbalis forbidding it in mosque, school and ribat. On transfer: Hanafis, Shafi'is and Hanbalis hold it unlawful after burial absolutely — a later Hanafi fatwa allowing it rebutted by Ibn Abidin — while before burial the Hanafis allow it absolutely and most Shafi'is and Hanbalis forbid it save for a sound purpose, the view also of al-Awza'i and Ibn al-Mundhir.
