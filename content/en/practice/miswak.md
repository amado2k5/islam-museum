## Overview
Istiyak — using the siwak — is lexically the act of cleaning the mouth and teeth with the tooth-stick (from saka, "to rub"); the word siwak denotes both the act and the stick itself, also called miswak, and the juristic usage adds nothing beyond this {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2589} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2590}. A sunna in the Prophet's practice, it remains an everyday hygiene tool kept in many homes today {{cite:sahih-al-bukhari}}.

## Its wisdom and ruling
The siwak purifies the mouth and pleases the Lord, on Aisha's report that the Prophet said "the siwak is a purification for the mouth and a means of the Lord's pleasure" {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2590}. Its baseline ruling across the four schools is recommendation — al-Nawawi even reported a consensus of the accredited scholars on this — on the hadith of Abu Hurayra "were it not that I would burden my community, I would have ordered them to use the siwak at every prayer", and on the Prophet's constant use of it, even in his final illness, and its listing among the traits of fitra {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2590}. Ishaq ibn Rahwayh held it obligatory, taking the command in the hadith at face value; the Shafi'is — with a Hanbali report and the positions of Abu Thawr and Ata' — dislike it for a fasting person after noon, while the Hanafis, Malikis and the other Hanbali report treat its ruling as the same whether fasting or not {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2591}.

## Its place in purification
The four schools agree the siwak is a sunna at wudu, then differ on whether it belongs to wudu's own sunnas: the Hanafis, Malikis and one Shafi'i view count it among them, while the Hanbalis and the weightier Shafi'i view make it a sunna outside and prior to wudu; it is also recommended at tayammum and ghusl {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.2591}.

## For young visitors
The miswak is a small twig from the arak tree used to clean the mouth and teeth; the Prophet kept using it, so it stayed among his enduring practices.
