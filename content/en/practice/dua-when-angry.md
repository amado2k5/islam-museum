## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers in the Book of Miscellaneous Remembrances in the chapter on what is said when angry: the two verses he cites, the reports on self-control in anger, the seeking of refuge, a supplication and ablution. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 300}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi opens the chapter on what is said when angry with two verses, [[passage/quran-3-134|Qur'an 3:134]] and [[passage/quran-7-199|7:199]] {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 300}. He reports from the two Sahihs, from Abu Hurayra, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said the strong one is not the wrestler but the one who controls himself in anger {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 300}. From Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, from Mu'adh ibn Anas, that God will call before all creatures, one who swallowed his anger while able to act on it and let him choose among the houris; al-Tirmidhi calls it good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 301}. From the two Sahihs, from Sulayman ibn Surad, that when two men reviled each other the Prophet said he knew a word that would take the feeling away, «أعوذ بالله من الشيطان الرجيم», I seek refuge in God from the accursed Satan {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 301}; its sense from Mu'adh ibn Jabal in Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi is called mursal by al-Tirmidhi {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 301}. From Ibn al-Sunni, from Aisha, that the Prophet told her to say «اللهم اغفر لي ذنبي، وأذهب غيظ قلبي، وأجرني من الشيطان», O God, forgive my sin, remove the rage of my heart and protect me from Satan {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 301}. From Abu Dawud, from Atiyya al-Sa'di, that anger is from Satan, who was made of fire, which water quenches, so one who is angry should perform ablution {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 301}.
