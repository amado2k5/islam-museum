## Summary
What al-Nawawi gathers on cheering the sick, reminding the recovered of their promises, what a person says who has given up hope of life, and what he asks of his family. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 139}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi gathers here what concerns the sick and then the dying. From al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, by a weak chain, from Abu Said al-Khudri, he gives that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] told those who visit a sick person to speak of a long life for him, because that cheers him {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 139}. He recalls Ibn Abbas consoling Umar when he was stabbed, and Aisha in her illness {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 139}. From Uqba ibn Amir comes the prohibition on forcing food on the sick, which al-Tirmidhi calls good {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 140}. From Ibn Majah and Ibn al-Sunni, from Umar, the visitor should ask the sick to pray for him, since his prayer is like the angels' {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 140}. For one who has despaired of life he gives from Aisha «اللهم أعني على غمرات الموت وسكرات الموت», and from the two Sahihs «اللهم اغفر لي وارحمني وألحقني بالرفيق الأعلى» {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 141}. He says it is recommended for him to recite much Qur'an and remembrance, to return wrongs and to think well of God {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 141} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 142}. He should ask his family for patience and not to weep over him, and al-Nawawi says it is sound that the dead are punished by their families' weeping {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 142}.
