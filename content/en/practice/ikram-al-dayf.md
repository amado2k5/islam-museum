## Summary
Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy presents honouring the guest as an old Arab custom, and joins it to sayings and stories about generosity without pretence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:15:30}
He tells of a poor man who had nothing for his guest but bread and oil, of a man who insisted on sharing his food with his guest, and he gives a saying attributed to Abdullah ibn Masud about the guest's place with his host. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:16:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:18:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:19:30}

## Welcoming the guest without asking
He says that Arabs used to host a guest without asking his name or the reason for his coming. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:15:30}
He gives a saying of an Arab sage on welcoming the guest; the note does not name the speaker of it (needs_source: unattributed-saying). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:15:30}
The Sheikh affirms that generosity does not rest on pretence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:15:30}

## Contentment and the guest
The Sheikh tells of a poor man from his village who had only bread and oil for his guest; when the guest asked for thyme, the man sold his waterskin to buy it (needs_source: anecdote-unattributed). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:16:30}
He comments that contentment with what a person has is better than pretence. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:16:30}

## Ibn Masud's saying on the guest
The Sheikh gives a saying attributed to [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Abdullah ibn Masud]] that the guest is the person most beloved to his host, because his provision is with God (needs_source: saying-attribution-garbled; hadith-ref: Ibn-Masud). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:18:30}
He says that a generous person serves his guest his food without pretence, and points to [[person/hatim-al-tai|Hatim al-Tai]] as an example of generosity. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:18:30}

## The man who would not eat alone
The Sheikh tells of an Arab man who insisted that his guest share his food, and would not eat alone while the guest was with him (needs_source: anecdote-unattributed). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:19:30}
He links this to the description of the Prophet as generous and to the generosity of his lineage, and refers to a hadith whose name is not given in the note (needs_source: hadith-unnamed). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy al-fawzu-al-moubeen/ep13 00:19:30}
