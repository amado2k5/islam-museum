## Overview
Du'a is supplicatory speech expressing request with humility — also called su'al. Al-Khattabi: its essence is the servant's invoking his Lord's care and drawing on His aid, the display of neediness and disavowal of power and strength — the mark of servanthood — together with praise of God and the ascription of generosity to Him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8102}

## Its senses in the Quran
It occurs as seeking rescue, as worship, as calling out, and as petitioning God — the sense here, as in "when My servants ask you of Me — I am near; I answer the call of the caller when he calls" and "your Lord said: call on Me, I will answer you." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8102} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8103}

## Istighfar and dhikr
Every istighfar is du'a, not every du'a istighfar; dhikr in its widest sense includes du'a and more. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8103}

## Ruling
Al-Nawawi: the chosen doctrine of the jurists, hadith scholars and the mass of the learned is that du'a is recommended; it can be obligatory, like the supplication inside al-Fatiha in prayer and the janaza supplication. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8103}
