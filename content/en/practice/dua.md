## Overview
Du'a is supplicatory speech expressing request with humility — also called su'al. Al-Khattabi: its essence is the servant's invoking his Lord's care and drawing on His aid, the display of neediness and disavowal of power and strength — the mark of servanthood — together with praise of God and the ascription of generosity to Him. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8102}

## Its senses in the Quran
It occurs as seeking rescue, as worship, as calling out, and as petitioning God — the sense here, as in "when My servants ask you of Me — I am near; I answer the call of the caller when he calls" and "your Lord said: call on Me, I will answer you." {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8102} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8103}

## Istighfar and dhikr
Every istighfar is du'a, not every du'a istighfar; dhikr in its widest sense includes du'a and more. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8103}

## Ruling
Al-Nawawi: the chosen doctrine of the jurists, hadith scholars and the mass of the learned is that du'a is recommended; it can be obligatory, like the supplication inside al-Fatiha in prayer and the janaza supplication. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.8103}

## From Sheikh Omar Abdelkafy's Tafsir lectures (episode 4)

The Sheikh says that the prayer of a transgressor may be answered before that of an upright person for one of two reasons: either God wants to draw him towards prayer, or He does not want him to keep standing at His door {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:23:30}. He mentions a saying attributed to God to the effect of hastening the need of a servant because He dislikes hearing his voice (needs_source) {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:23:30}. The Sheikh describes mothers whose child falls ill and whose illness worsens, who say, Lord, take what he has and give it to us, and he counts this as emotion in its right place, and then asks God for well-being {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:51:00}.

The Sheikh advises that whoever goes to the doctor, himself or his child, make three supplications: that God guide the doctor to a sound diagnosis, since an unsound diagnosis can lead the patient to harm, and that He guide him to prescribe the suitable medicine {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:51:30}. The third supplication is that God not remove the secret of healing from that medicine, and the Sheikh says that God has created no disease without creating a cure for it, except old age {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy tafsir-al-quran-al-qareem/ep04 00:52:00}.

## For Sprouts
Du'a is asking God with our hearts: we raise our hands, ask Him for what we need, thank Him and ask His forgiveness. A Muslim may make du'a at any time and place, loves to pray for parents and Muslims, and children learn short easy supplications from the Sunna.

## For Explorers
Du'a in Islam is turning to God in petition and praise: asking Him for religious and worldly needs, seeking forgiveness and giving thanks. The hadith books record that du'a is 'the essence of worship', and that God loves a servant's insistence in du'a without boredom or haste. It has manners: facing the qibla, raising the hands, beginning with praise and blessing on the Prophet, insistence with good opinion of God, and times when answer is hoped — the last third of the night, between the adhan and iqama, Friday, travel and fasting, and the worshipper's prostration. Muslims have learned transmitted supplications from the Qur'an and Sunna for morning, evening, eating and travel.

## For Scholars
Du'a, in the scholars' view, is the heart of inward worship: the Muslim turning to God in petition, humility and praise, divided in fiqh into du'a of worship, du'a of request and du'a of glorification. The hadith books record that du'a is 'the essence of worship' and 'worship' itself, that God loves a servant's insistence without boredom or haste, and the texts warn against praying for sin or severing kinship. Its transmitted manners: facing the qibla, raising the hands, beginning with praise of God and blessing on the Prophet, insistence with good opinion and certainty, and times and places where answer is hoped — the last third of the night, between adhan and iqama, Friday, fasting and breaking fast, travel, prostration, and days like Arafa. The jurists wove the Prophetic supplications into the prayers and daily remembrances, and the adab literature urges taking the transmitted and comprehensive supplications and leaving affectation. Scholars gathered du'as in separate works, and congregational qunut in mosques is among the matters jurists disagreed over.

## From Omar Abdelkafy's series Nafahat Ramadaniyya (the faults of the tongue)
The Sheikh counts turning in supplication to other than God as a fault of the tongue and a form of associating partners with God, and says some people fall into it without realising. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:00:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:12:00} He mentions Ibn Abbas's hadith on asking God alone and seeking help from Him, and says that supplication is worship, so it must not be directed to other than God. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:01:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:02:00}
He observes that the questions put to the Prophet in the Qur'an are answered with the word 'Say', except the question about God's nearness, whose answer comes directly that He is near; he sees in this that there is no intermediary between the servant and his Lord in supplication. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:03:00} Hence he criticises those who go to a shrine and ask the one buried there for their needs, holding that he is a human being with no power of benefit or harm over himself. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:03:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:03:30}
He quotes Ibn Abbas's warning against saying 'but for the dogs, thieves would have robbed us' and the like, and teaches saying instead 'but for God's grace through so-and-so'; he mentions the Bedouin who said 'I rely on God and on you' and was corrected by the Prophet. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:04:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:04:30} He gives the example of someone who hires a skilled lawyer and sleeps at ease, and says that one who takes God as his trustee has more reason for peace. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:06:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:07:00}
Among the examples of certainty he tells: a Companion he calls 'al-Bara'' who swore by God that He would open to the Muslims a city that held out against them in the war with Persia, and was answered (needs_source: the name and the place are unclear in the recording); {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:05:30} [[person/musab-ibn-umayr|Mus'ab ibn Umayr]], who he says brought Islam into every house of Medina before the Hijra, and for whom no shroud long enough was found; {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:09:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:09:30} the words of [[person/ribi-ibn-amir|Rib'i ibn Amir]] to Rustam about bringing people out of the worship of people; {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:10:00} and the story of [[person/abu-hazim-al-araj|Abu Hazim]] with [[person/harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]]. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:10:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:11:00}
He closes by saying one should pray without impatience, and mentions the hadith that a prayer is answered so long as the one praying does not say 'I prayed and was not answered'. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:12:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy nafahat-ramadaneya-1436-h/ep02 00:12:30} See also: [[concept/al-dua-ala-al-nafs-wal-awlad|praying against oneself and one's children]].

## From Omar Abdelkafy's programme Wa Nafsin wa ma Sawwaha
The Sheikh holds that the distance between heaven and earth is one answered prayer, and observes that the Qur'an's questions to the Prophet are answered with 'Say', except the question about God's nearness, whose answer is that He is near and answers the caller; he concludes that there is no veil between God and His servants, and no 'confessional' where sins must be admitted. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:01:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:02:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:02:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:03:00}
He criticises those who deal with God like a trade and pray only in crises, and urges praying in ease and hardship, health and illness, wealth and poverty, victory and defeat. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:03:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:04:00} To one who has grown weary after praying five years for a son's guidance or a daughter's or wife's hijab without answer, he recalls that [[person/nuh|Nuh]] called his people for a thousand years less fifty and did not stop until told that none of his people would believe except those who already had. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:04:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:05:00}
He holds that a prayer not fulfilled as the servant wished is also an answer, because God knows what sets him right and what corrupts him: given money he might spend it on the unlawful; childlessness may be a gift, as a son or a daughter is a gift, since the child might have been like Nuh's son or the boy al-Khidr killed. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:05:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:05:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:06:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:06:30} Among the manners of prayer he names beginning and ending with blessings on the Prophet, with certainty of answer, and says a prayer is either granted quickly, or averts an affliction, or is stored as reward for the Day of Resurrection; he urges praying before dawn. {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:07:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:07:30} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:08:00} {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:08:30} He ends with the story of a righteous man he calls Abu Ayyub, who prayed when the waves tossed the ship, and the sea grew calm (needs_source: the name and story are the Sheikh's narration). {cite:assabile-omar-abdelkafy wa-nafsun-ma-sawaha/ep03 00:09:30}
