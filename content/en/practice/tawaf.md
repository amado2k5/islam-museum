## Overview
Tawaf is circling a thing; in usage, circling the Sacred House; the mataf is its place. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11248}

## Its kinds
The Hanafis, Malikis and Hanbalis count seven: arrival tawaf, visitation tawaf, farewell tawaf, 'umra tawaf, votive tawaf, greeting tawaf of the Sacred Mosque, and voluntary tawaf; the Shafi'is count six, including rukn tawaf covering the integrals of hajj and 'umra and the tawaf of release after missing hajj. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11249}

## Arrival tawaf
Called the comer's, arrival's, greeting's and meeting tawaf, prescribed for one arriving from outside Mecca to greet the House; a sunna for the outsider. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.11249}

## For Sprouts
Tawaf is walking seven times around the sacred Kaaba in Mecca with prayers, remembrance, and peace in the heart.

## For Explorers
Tawaf is a sacred act of worship where Muslims circumambulate the Kaaba seven times counter-clockwise, keeping the Sacred House on their left. Beginning each circuit at the Black Stone with prayers and praise, believers from all lands walk together in devotion, reflecting unity and shared faith in God.

## For Scholars
The liturgical circumambulation of the Kaaba, constituting an essential pillar in both the Hajj and Umrah pilgrimages and an esteemed independent voluntary devotion. The rite requires seven continuous counter-clockwise circuits performed in a state of ritual purity, keeping the Kaaba on the worshiper's left. Commencing and concluding aligned with the Black Stone with praises, it includes Sunna practices such as brisk walking (ramal) during early circuits for men and praying two units behind the Station of Abraham (Maqam Ibrahim). Encompassing the arrival, effusion (ifadah), and farewell circumambulations, Tawaf symbolizes the cosmic unity of believers focused upon monotheism.

## Purity for tawaf in al-Nawawi's al-Majmu'

[[person/al-nawawi|Al-Nawawi]] states in [[work/al-majmu-sharh-al-muhadhdhab|al-Majmu']] that for the Shafi'is tawaf is valid only in a state of purity, for every kind of tawaf, and reports a weak view from Abu Ya'qub al-Abiwardi that the farewell tawaf is valid without purity and made good by a sacrifice, which the Imam al-Haramayn called an error. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27} He then sets out the scholars' views:

:::perspectives
- **Sunni, Shafi'i and Maliki: purity from ritual impurity and filth is a condition**: the Shafi'i view, also Malik's, ascribed by al-Mawardi to most scholars and by Ibn al-Mundhir, for ritual impurity, to scholars generally; they cite A'isha's report that on arriving in Mecca the Prophet first performed ablution and then circumambulated, the hadith "take your rites from me", his telling A'isha when she menstruated not to circumambulate until she had bathed, and Ibn Abbas's saying "circumambulation of the House is prayer", as his own words. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27} {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.28}
- **Sunni, Hanafi: Abu Hanifa as al-Nawawi reports him**: purity is not a condition of tawaf, so one who circumambulates in minor or major impurity has a valid tawaf; his followers differ on whether purity is obligatory, and those who say so hold that a sheep is owed for tawaf in minor impurity and a camel in major impurity, and that it should be repeated while still in Mecca; they cite the general wording of [[passage/quran-22-29|al-Hajj 29]] and analogy with standing at Arafa and the other pillars. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27}
- **Sunni, Hanbali: two reports from Ahmad**: one as the Shafi'is; the other, that he repeats it if he stays in Mecca and makes it good with a sacrifice if he has returned home. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27}
- **Sunni, Zahiri**: Dawud held purity obligatory for tawaf, but a tawaf in minor impurity still counts except for a menstruating woman; al-Mansuri of his school held it a condition. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.27}
:::

The Shafi'is answer that the verse is general and specified by the hadiths, that tawaf without purity is disapproved even for Abu Hanifa so the verse cannot be read as commanding it, and, against the analogy, that purity is not required for the other pillars. {cite:majmu-irshad-v8 p.28}
