## Summary
The hadiths with which al-Nawawi closes the merit of the Qur'an, warning against forgetting it, then his sections on the reciter's manners: sincerity, the toothstick, humility, the written copy, reciting aloud and beautifying the voice. {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 106}

## What al-Nawawi gathers in al-Adhkar
Al-Nawawi closes the merit of the Qur'an with three hadiths warning against forgetting it. From the two Sahihs, from Ibn Umar, that [[person/muhammad|the Prophet]] said «إنما مثل صاحب القرآن كمثل الإبل المعقلة», the keeper of the Qur'an is like hobbled camels, kept if he tends them and gone if he lets them loose {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 106}. From Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, from Anas, that the greatest sin shown to him was a sura or verse given to a man who then forgot it, and he notes that al-Tirmidhi spoke against it {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 106}. From Sunan Abi Dawud and the Musnad of al-Darimi, from Sa'd ibn Ubada, that one who recited the Qur'an and forgot it will meet God on the Day of Resurrection leprous {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 106}. He then opens sections on the reciter's manners, given in outline without their proofs: first sincerity, reciting as one who sees God {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 106}, then cleaning the mouth with the toothstick {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 106}. He urges humility, reflection and weeping, citing [[passage/quran-17-109|Qur'an 17:109]] {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 107}. Aloud is better if showing off is not feared and no one is disturbed {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 107} {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 108}, and a beautiful voice is recommended without drawling {cite:al-adhkar-nawawi-arnaut-1994 p. 108}.
