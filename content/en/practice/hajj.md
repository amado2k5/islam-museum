## Overview
In Arabic hajj means heading for something, or, some lexicographers say, for something honoured; in legal usage it is heading for a specific place, the Sacred House and [[place/arafat|Arafa]], at a specific time, the months of hajj, to perform specific acts, which for most scholars are the standing at Arafa, the [[practice/tawaf|tawaf]] and the sa'y, under specific conditions {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836}. Umra is heading for the Sacred House for tawaf and sa'y {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836}. Hajj is an individual duty, once in a lifetime, on every legally responsible person who is able, and one of the [[concept/five-pillars|pillars of Islam]], established by the Book, the Sunna and consensus {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836}. Its proof from the Book is [[passage/quran-3-97|Al Imran 97]], which the Kuwaiti Encyclopaedia of Fiqh reads as an explicit text for the obligation {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836}.

The jurists differ on whether it falls due at once when its conditions are met: Abu Hanifa (in the sounder of two reports from him), Abu Yusuf, Malik (in the preferred report from him) and Ahmad held that it does; al-Shafi'i and Muhammad ibn al-Hasan held that it may be deferred {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6837}. The Shafi'is argue that the Prophet took [[place/mecca|Mecca]] in year 8 of the Hijra and did not perform the hajj until year 10 {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6838}. Five conditions, agreed among the scholars, make it obligatory: Islam, sanity, maturity, freedom and ability {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6840}. Among its purposes the encyclopaedia names the gathering of Muslims from all over the earth, where they come to know one another and differences of wealth and poverty, race, colour and language fall away {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6839} {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6840}.

## Its relation to 'umra
'Umra is the purpose of the House for tawaf and sa'y; hajj contains its acts and adds to them. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836}

## Ruling
Hajj is an individual obligation on every legally capable person once in a lifetime, a pillar of Islam established by Book, Sunna and consensus — "owed to God by the people is hajj to the House, for whoever can make his way to it; and whoever disbelieves — God needs nothing of the worlds" — the phrasing of obligation itself, and disbelief placed opposite the command, proves the obligation. {cite:fiqh-mawsua-kuwaitiyya-noor-v3 p.6836}

## For Sprouts
A journey to Mecca to perform special rites. A Muslim makes it once in a lifetime if able.

## For Explorers
The Hajj is a journey to Mecca, where Muslims perform known rites, such as circling the Kaaba and standing at Arafat. Whoever is able to make the way makes it once in a lifetime.

## For Scholars
The journey to the Sacred House at Mecca to perform prescribed rites, one of the five pillars of Islam. The Qur'an makes it a duty for those able to make the way there, and tells of the call to proclaim it to the people. The Hajj includes the state of ihram, circling the Kaaba, running between Safa and Marwa, standing at Arafat, spending the night at Muzdalifa and at Mina, and throwing the pebbles. The books of sira tell of the Prophet's pilgrimage and the Farewell Sermon. This entry presents the Hajj as a global journey where Muslims gather from many countries, and refers the details of the rites to the entry on the Hajj in law.
