## Summary
Verse 136 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/sacred-livestock-jahiliyya|the sacred livestock of the Jahiliyya (bahira, sa’iba, wasila, ham)]], and [[concept/ibadat-al-asnam-jahiliyya|idol worship in the Jahiliyya and its rites]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 136 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "who will have the final abode; wrongdoers do not prosper", al-Tabari says: you will come to know, unbelievers, when you see the punishment, which of us or of you will have the outcome of the abode, that is, whose world is followed by what is better or worse for him through the good or bad deeds he sent ahead. Then God starts a fresh report, "wrongdoers do not prosper", meaning that whoever acts contrary to what God commanded him in the world will not succeed or attain his need with God, which is the meaning of the wrongdoer's wrong here. He says "man" has two possible cases: nominative as a subject, or accusative governed by "you will know"; the nominative is better, because the sense is "you will know which of us has the final abode", and the editor refers to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 130}

On verse 136, "they assign God a share of the crops and cattle He created, saying, this is for God by their claim and this is for our partners; what is for their partners does not reach God, while what is for God reaches their partners; evil is their judgement", al-Tabari says: these who set idols and statues equal to their Lord assigned to their Lord something of what its Creator made, namely crops and cattle (one says "dhara'a Allah al-khalq" when He created them), and "nasib" means a portion and part. The commentators then differed on the description of the portion they assigned to God and the one they assigned to their partners among the idols and Satan. Some said it was a part of their crops and cattle which they set aside for one and another part for the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 131}

He relates (13899) from Ibn Abbas that when they brought in the grain and made it into bundles, they made a share of it for God and a share for their gods; if the wind blew from what they had assigned to their gods toward what they had assigned to God, they returned it to the gods' share, and if it blew from God's share toward the gods' share, they let it stay and did not return it; that is "evil is their judgement". And (13900) from him that they assigned a portion of their fruits to God and a portion to Satan and the idols; if some fruit from God's portion fell into Satan's portion they left it, and if some from Satan's fell into God's they picked it up, kept it and returned it to Satan's; if a channel from the irrigation of God's portion burst into Satan's they left it, and if one from Satan's portion burst into God's they blocked it. As for what they assigned to Satan from cattle, it is the subject of "God did not appoint any bahira, sa'iba, wasila or hami" (5:103). The editor's notes explain "faraz", the setting apart, and the origin of "dhar'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 131}

Report 13901, also from Ibn Abbas, says much the same: God's enemies, when they ploughed a field or had fruit, assigned a part of it to God and a part to the idol; what fell from the part named for God they returned to the idol, and if water reached the idol's part first and watered something assigned to God, they counted that for the idol; if the idol's share mixed with God's they said this one is poor and did not return it to God's; and of their livestock they forbade the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and hami, giving them to the idols while claiming they forbade them for God. From Mujahid (13902): they named a part of the crops for God and a part for their partners and idols; what the wind carried from what they named for God to their idols' part they left, and what went from the idols' part to God's they returned, saying God is free of need of this; and 13903 is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 132}

From Qatada (13904): some misguided people divided their crops and livestock, a part for God and a part for their partners; if some of what they had assigned to God mixed with what they assigned to their partners they left it, and if some of the partners' mixed into God's they returned it to the partners; when a lean year struck they used what they had assigned to God and left what they assigned to their partners. Report 13905 is similar from him. From al-Suddi (13906): they divided their wealth, a share for God, sowed a crop for God and made the same for their gods; what came from the gods' portion they spent on the gods and what came from God's they gave in charity; if what belonged to the partners was lost and God's share was plentiful they said our gods must have some spending and took God's share and spent it on their gods, but if God's share was barren and the gods' share abundant they said, had He willed, He would have made His own share thrive, and returned nothing of the gods' to Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 133}

God said, had they been truthful in what they divided, evil would be their judgement in taking from Me while giving Me nothing; that is "evil is their judgement". Others said that the portion they assigned to God from which something reached their partners meant that they would not eat what they slaughtered for God until they named the gods, while they ate what they slaughtered for the gods without mentioning God's name over it; Ibn Zayd's report (13907) says so. Al-Tabari then says the better of the two interpretations is that of Ibn Abbas and those who said the same, because God reported that they assigned to God a fixed portion of their crops and cattle and said this is God's, and made the like for their partners, who are their idols by the agreement of the commentators, saying this is for our partners, and that the partners' portion does not reach God's, while what was God's reached the partners' portion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 134}

Al-Tabari adds that if what they assigned to God reached their partners through naming or not naming, then what God said did not reach might have reached, and what He said reached might not have, which is contrary to the apparent sense, because of two animals, one slaughtered for God and the other for the gods, their meats might have been mixed and they mixed them, since what they disliked was naming God over what was slaughtered for the gods, not the mingling of the actual things. As for "evil is their judgement", it is God's report of the deed of these idolaters: they judged badly, taking from My portion for their partners and not giving Me from their partners' portion; it points to their ignorance, their error and their departure from the way of truth, in that they were not content to set up as equal to Him who created and fed them and gave them countless blessings what neither harms nor benefits them, but preferred it in their own allotments. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 135}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
