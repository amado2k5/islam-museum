**Commentary on verse 139 (pp. 83-84)**

Al-Tabari says this is God's report of what Moses said to his people of the Children of Israel: these who are devoted to these idols, God will destroy what they are engaged in, spoil it and cause them loss in it, by repaying them with a humbling punishment for it; "and vain is what they used to do" of worshipping them, so it comes to nothing, because it will not benefit them when God's command comes and descends on their courtyard, nor will it ward off God's might from them when it falls on them, nor save them from His punishment when He punishes them on the resurrection day; it is as though it had never been. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "vain". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 83}

Report 15059, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Musa ibn Harun, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat: "destroyed is what they are in" means that what they are in will be destroyed. Report 15060, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: loss. Report 15061, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: all of this is the same, like "forgiving, merciful" and "pardoning, forgiving"; and the Arabs say "he is wretched, destroyed" and "he is wretched, a loser". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 84}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
