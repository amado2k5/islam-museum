## Summary
Verse 177 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 177 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 177 (pp. 419-420, reports 8265-8266).** Al-Tabari says the verse means the hypocrites about whom God earlier told His Prophet not to grieve at their hastening toward disbelief: those who exchanged disbelief for their faith, turned away from it after entering it, and were content with disbelief in God and His Messenger in place of faith; they will do God no harm at all by their disbelief, but only harm themselves, by bringing on themselves a punishment from God they cannot withstand. The note refers to earlier comment on the word for "purchase" (the numbers are garbled in the extracted text). He then says that God, by these verses from [[passage/quran-3-166|3:166]] to this one, urged His believing servants to sincerity of certainty, to devote themselves to Him in their affairs and to be content with Him alone as helper, apart from the rest of creation; He encouraged them to fight His enemies and the enemies of His religion and strengthened their hearts, and told them that whoever He supports with victory will not be forsaken even if all who oppose him gather against him, while whoever He forsakes will have no helper whose help avails, however many his supporters (the note: the printed text reads "or his helpers" and the manuscript's "and" is right). Ibn Ishaq (8265), through Ibn Humayd from Salama: the hypocrites, and the punishment described as painful means agonising; the note gives Ibn Hisham (3: 128) as completing the reports ending at 8263, without that word's explanation. Mujahid (8266), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: they are the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 419} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 420}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 420}
