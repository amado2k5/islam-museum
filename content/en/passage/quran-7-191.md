**Commentary on verse 191 (pp. 318-319)**

Al-Tabari says: do they associate in the worship of God, worshipping beside Him "what creates nothing", when God creates and brings them forth? Pure worship is for the creator, not the created. Ibn Zayd used to say of it, in report 15532: a child was born to Adam and Eve and they named him Abd Allah, and Iblis came to them and said what did you name your son, Adam and Eve? A child had been born to them before and they had named him Abd Allah and he died. They said we named him Abd Allah; Iblis said do you think God will leave His servant with you? By God no, He will take him as He took the other, but I shall show you a name that will stay with you as long as you stay, so name him Abd Shams. That is God's saying "do they associate what creates nothing and is itself created?"; does the sun create anything so that it has a servant? It is created. And he said the Messenger of God said he deceived them twice, he deceived them in Paradise and he deceived them on earth. The editor says he did not find the report from the Messenger and that in al-Durr al-Manthur it is Zayd's own saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 318}

It is said "and they are created", so their pronoun came out in the form of the pronoun for the children of Adam, though He said "do they associate what", giving them with "what" and not "who", the form of speech about what is not of the children of Adam, since what they worshipped was stone, wood, copper or some of the things spoken of with "what" and not "who"; and then "they" was said, their pronoun coming out as the pronoun for the children of Adam because the report of the polytheists' glorifying them is like the report of people's glorifying one another. The editor points out that the eleventh part of the manuscript begins here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 319}
