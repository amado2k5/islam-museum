## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "do you not know that God has power over all things", he says: do you not know, Muhammad, that I am able to compensate you for what I abrogate of my rulings and change of my obligations, with what I will that is better for you and my believing servants, now in this world or later in the Hereafter, or to put in its place its like; know then that I have power over this and over all things. "Qadir" here means strong, and he records the usage "qadartu alayhi", that Banu Murra of Ghatafan kasra the dal, and that "taqdir" from "qadartu al-shay'" has a different verbal noun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 484}

On "do you not know that to God belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth", al-Tabari takes up a question: did the Prophet not already know this? He reports that some said the speech took the form of affirmation, as the Arabs say "have I not honoured you?" meaning "I have honoured you"; he rejects this, since in Arabic an interrogative before a particle of negation does not carry the sense of affirming. He holds that although the address appears to be to the Prophet, it is meant for his Companions, whom God told "do not say ra'ina but say unzurna and listen", and he argues that the end of the verse, "you have no protector or helper besides God", returns the address to all of them. He says it is fluent Arabic for a speaker to cast speech as addressed to some while meaning others, or to one while meaning many, or to many while meaning one, giving as examples Surat al-Ahzab (address to the Prophet, then to the group), the lines of al-Kumayt ibn Zayd praising the Prophet while meaning his family, a verse of Jamil ibn Ma'mar, "alaa inna jiranii al-'ashiyyata ra'ih", and another of his describing a woman with a masculine word. So this verse, though apparently addressed to the Prophet, is meant for his Companions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 484} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 485} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 486} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 487}

On "to Him belongs the kingdom of the heavens and the earth" al-Tabari distinguishes "mulk" in the sense of sovereignty and dominion from "milk" in the sense of ownership. He says the verse means: do you not know, Muhammad, that to Me belongs the dominion and sovereignty of the heavens and the earth to the exclusion of any other, where I judge as I will, command and forbid, and abrogate, replace and change my rulings among my servants as I will. He holds that although the report is an address to the Prophet about God's greatness, it is a denial of the Jews who rejected the abrogation of the rulings of the Torah and denied the prophethood of Jesus and Muhammad; God told them that the dominion is His and that He may command, forbid, abrogate, confirm and cause to be forgotten, and then told the Prophet and the believers to submit to His command and not be alarmed by any opponent, for there is no one to see to their affairs but Him and no helper for them but Him. "Wali" is a "fa'il" form from "waliytu amr fulan", when you become the one in charge of it, "nasir" is from "nasartuka", the one who supports, and "min dun Allah" means other than God and after God, for which he cites a verse of Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 487} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 488} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 489}
