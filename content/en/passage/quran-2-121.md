## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "those to whom We gave the Book" the interpreters differed over whom God means. Some said the believers in the Messenger of God among his Companions, who believed in God's Book and held it true, from Qatada. Others said the learned of the Children of Israel who believed in God and held His messengers true and affirmed the ruling of the Torah, acting by what God ordered in it of following Muhammad and believing in him, from Ibn Zayd (whoever of the Jews disbelieved in the Prophet, those are the losers). Al-Tabari holds this view better than Qatada's, because the verses before it passed over the reports of the two peoples of the Book and the alteration by those who altered God's Book, no mention of the Companions of Muhammad ran in the preceding verse nor in the following one, and no report comes which must be accepted that this is about them; so it is better that it be a report about those whose stories God told in the verse before and after, the two peoples of the Book, and the verse means: those to whom We gave the Book you have known, Muhammad, the Torah, and who read it and followed what is in it, so believed you and what you came with, those recite it as it ought to be recited; "al-kitab" is definite, and the Prophet and his Companions knew which book was meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 564} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 565} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 566}

On "they recite it as it ought to be recited" they differed. Some said it means they follow it as it ought to be followed, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas; and from Ibn Abbas by the route of al-Suddi and Abu Malik, they make lawful what it makes lawful and forbidden what it forbids and do not alter it from its places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 566}

On "they recite it as it ought to be recited" al-Tabari gives many reports on the sense of following: from Ibn Mas'ud (that he make lawful what it makes lawful and forbidden what it forbids and not alter it from its places), from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, Ata, Abu Razin, Mujahid and Qays ibn Sa'd (they follow it as it ought to be followed, Qays citing God's words on the sun, "and the moon when it follows it"), from Mujahid (they act by it as it ought to be acted on), from al-Hasan (they act by its clear verses, believe in its ambiguous ones, and leave what is unclear to them to One who knows it), and from Qatada (they made lawful its lawful and forbidden its forbidden and acted by what is in it, and he mentions from Ibn Mas'ud that its due recitation is to read it as God sent it down and not alter it). Others said they read it as it ought to be read. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 566} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 567} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 568} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 569}

Al-Tabari holds the right sense is that they follow it as it ought to be followed, from "I kept following his trail", for the authorities among the interpreters agree on it. So the sense is: those to whom We gave the Book, Muhammad, from the people of the Torah who believed in you and what you brought them from Me, follow My Book which I sent down upon Moses, believing in it and affirming what it holds of your description and that you are My messenger, acting on what I made lawful to them and avoiding what I forbade, and neither altering it from its places nor replacing it as it was sent down by interpretation or otherwise. "Haqqa tilawatihi" intensifies the description of their following the Book and keeping to action by it, as one says "so-and-so is a scholar truly a scholar". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 569} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 570}

On adding "haqq" to a definite noun the grammarians differed. Some Kufan grammarians said it may not be added to a definite noun, since it is like "ayy" and "af'al" is not added to a single definite noun, and it is allowed here because the Arabs count the pronoun, when it refers to an indefinite, as indefinite, as in "I passed a man unique in his mother", so it means "they recite it as due recitation". Some Basran grammarians said "haqq" may be added to indefinites with indefinites and to definites with definites, like "I passed the man, the man's slave". Al-Tabari prefers the first view because "haqqa tilawatihi" means "what a recitation" in praise of it, "ayy" may not be added to a single definite noun in anyone's view, nor may "haqq", and it was added to a word with the pronoun for the cause given (the editor says the right text is "haqqa tilawat al-kitab"). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 570} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 571}

On "those believe in it" he says: those who recite what they were given of the Book as it ought to be recited hold it true, the pronoun in "bihi" returning to that in "tilawatihi", both being of the Book mentioned; so God reported that the believer in the Torah is the one who follows what is in it of its lawful and forbidden and acts by God's obligations in it, and that its people are those with that description, not one who alters it or replaces its meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 571}

Al-Tabari says God described those who follow the Torah as He did, and praised them, because following it is following Muhammad and holding him true: the Torah orders its people so, tells them of his prophethood and the duty of obeying him laid on all God's creation of the children of Adam, and denying Muhammad is denying it; so the followers of the Torah are those who believe in Muhammad and act by what is in it, from Ibn Zayd (whoever of the Children of Israel believed in the Messenger and the Torah, and the disbeliever in Muhammad is the disbeliever in it). On "whoever disbelieves in it, those are the losers" he says: whoever disbelieves in the Book of which He reported that believers who were given it recite it as due, that is denies what is in it of God's obligations and Muhammad's prophethood and his being held true, and alters it, changing its meaning, those are the ones who lost their knowledge and learning, wronged themselves of their share of God's mercy and took in its place His wrath and anger; and from Ibn Zayd, whoever of the Jews disbelieved in the Prophet, those are the losers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 571} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 572} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 573}
