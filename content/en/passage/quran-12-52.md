**Commentary on verse 52 (pp. 140-142)**

On "that is so that he may know that I did not betray him in his absence, and that God does not guide the plot of the betrayers", al-Tabari says it refers to what Joseph had done: sending back the king's messenger, not answering him or going out to him, and asking him to question the women who cut their hands about their affair. He did it so that he would know that he had not betrayed him with his wife "in his absence", that is, had not committed an indecency with her while he was away; and if he did not do it in his absence, he was all the more far from it in his presence. Report 19421, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Joseph says "that he may know", Itfir his master, "that I did not betray him in his absence", that I would not have gone against him with his household where he did not know. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 140}

Reports 19422 to 19424, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa, all from Ibn Abi Najih: Joseph says it, I did not betray my master. Report 19425, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: this is Joseph's saying. Report 19426, from Abu Salih, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Isma'il ibn Salim: it is Joseph, who did not betray the Aziz regarding his wife. Report 19427, from al-Dahhak: it is Joseph saying I did not betray the king in his absence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 141}

On "and that God does not guide the plot of the betrayers", al-Tabari says: I did that so my master would know I did not betray him in his absence, and that God does not direct one who betrays trusts nor guide their deeds in their betrayal. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "huda" and "kayd". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 141}

Al-Tabari then explains that the words "that is so that he may know that I did not betray him" connect with the Aziz's wife's words "I sought him, and he is among the truthful" because the hearers understand the meaning, as God's words "and thus they do" connect with the woman's words in Sura al-Naml, "they make the noblest of its people the lowest", being a fresh statement; and likewise Pharaoh's words to his people in Sura al-A'raf, "what do you command", are connected to the chiefs' words "he wants to drive you out of your land". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 142}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
