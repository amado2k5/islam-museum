**Commentary on verse 51 (pp. 17-18)**

Al-Tabari says: God reports what the angels say to these idolaters killed at Badr as they strike their faces and backs: taste the punishment of God that burns you. This punishment is for you "for what your hands have sent ahead", meaning what your hands earned of sins and burdens and the disobedience to God you committed during your lives; so taste the punishment today, and in your return the punishment of burning. That is because God is "not unjust to His servants": He punishes none of His creatures except for a crime they committed and punishes only for disobedience to Him, since injustice cannot belong to Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 17}

There are two grammatical readings of the "that" in "and that God": one is the accusative, joined to the "what" in "for what", so "that is for what your hands sent ahead and because God is not unjust to His servants", and according to some the genitive; the other is the nominative, "that is for what you sent ahead, and that is because God". The editor refers to al-Farra and to the earlier explanation of "what your hands sent ahead". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 18}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
