**Commentary on verse 141: the hypocrites who wait for the believers (pp. 323-328)**

Report 10711, from Ibn Jurayj, says the hypocrites wait for the Muslims: if the Muslims gain spoils from their enemy, the hypocrites say, were we not with you? We were with you, so give us a share of the spoils like what you take; and if the unbelievers get a share from the Muslims, the hypocrites say to the unbelievers: did we not gain the upper hand over you and keep you from the believers? We were holding them back from you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 324}

Al-Tabari explains the verse: those who wait on you, believers, so that if God gives you a victory, that is a conquest and spoils from your enemy, they say: were we not with you fighting your enemy, so give us a share of the spoils? If the unbelievers have a share of you, the hypocrites say to them: did we not prevail over you, keeping the believers from you by holding them back? God will judge between them on the Day of Resurrection, admitting the believers to His garden and the hypocrites, with their unbelieving allies, to His fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 324}

In the closing words of the verse there is a promise from God that He will not admit the hypocrites to the believers' place in the garden, nor the believers to the hypocrites' place, so the unbelievers have no proof against the believers on the Day of Resurrection that they were enemies in this world while the hypocrites were their allies, and that God gathered them together with their allies. That is the 'path' the promise refers to, which the verse calls proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 324}

Al-Tabari gives two senses of 'did we not gain mastery over you': the first, did we not prevail over you, the view of al-Suddi; the second, did we not make clear to you that we are with you in what you are doing, the view of Ibn Jurayj. He holds the two are close in meaning. The root sense of istahwadha in Arabic is mastery, as when God says that Satan mastered them. He cites verse to show the dialect forms of the root. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 326}

Al-Tabari notes that by analogy the form should be istahaddha, but the Arabs sometimes keep the root's original form, and the Qur'an comes in this dialect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 327}

On the closing words, about the Day of Resurrection, there is no disagreement among the commentators that the sense is that the unbelievers will not prevail over the believers that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 327}

Report 10714, from Yusay' al-Hadrami, says a man asked Ali how God could promise the believers protection when they fight us and win and kill us; Ali told him to come closer, then recited the words about judgement on the Day of Resurrection. Reports 10715 and 10716 give the same through Dharr and Yusay'; report 10717, through Dharr and a man, has Ali say it is in the Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 327}

Report 10718, from Abu Malik, says the Day of Resurrection; report 10719, from Ibn Abbas through Ibn Jurayj and Ata al-Khurasani, says the same. Al-Tabari says 'path' here means proof, and report 10720, from al-Suddi, says the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 328}

The reports on verse 141 end with report 10720 at p. 328; verse 142 begins on p. 329. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 328}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
