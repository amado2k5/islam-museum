## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 28 to 34 as a single section. He interprets the phrase "Then let man look at his food" as meaning: Let this disbelieving man, who denies God's oneness, look at his food and how God has provided it. He also cites a narration from Sufyan, on the authority of Mansur, on the authority of Mujahid, regarding "Then let man look at his food" and his drink, saying: at his food and drink. And on the authority of Ibn Abi Najih, on the authority of Mujahid, regarding the phrase "Then let man look at his food": a sign for them. The reciters differed in their reading of this phrase, and he quotes statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, Al-Hasan, and Sufyan in this section.
