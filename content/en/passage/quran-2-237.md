## Summary
Verse 237 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 237 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-237|2:237]]) begins on p. 140. Al-Tabari says this ruling explains the one before: there is no blame in divorcing before touching when a dower was fixed, and she has half of what was settled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 140} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 141} His reason is that the earlier mention of the women with no fixed dower shows that those joined to them by the conjunction have a different ruling, and God repeated the women with a fixed dower to remove doubt and confusion, so that hearers would not think them other than the women first mentioned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 141} The exception for forgiveness means that the women owed half may leave it to the husbands out of grace, if they are of those whose judgement over their property is valid, adult and sound-minded; then their waiver stands and it falls from the husband. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 141} Interpreters said the same: Ibn 'Abbas, she has half her dower and no more (5246); Mujahid, half unless they forgo (5247, 5248); Qatada, this verse abrogated what came before for one with a named dower not consummated, so she has half and no gift (5249); al-Rabi', she has half and also the gift and no waiting period (5250). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 142} The difference between Qatada and al-Rabi' on the gift is set out here without a verdict; al-Tabari had already chosen a gift for every divorced woman.

More reports on ([[passage/quran-2-237|2:237]]) concerning the half that falls due after divorce: from Ibn Shihab, if she is divorced before touching and a dower was fixed, she has half her dower and no waiting period (5251). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 142} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 143} Al-Tabari then gives those who said that forgiveness means the woman leaving what is due to her: 'Ikrima (5252); al-Dahhak (5253); Ibn 'Abbas, that it is the woman, previously married or a virgin, whom someone other than her father married off, and God made forgiveness hers: if she wishes she leaves it, if she wishes she takes the half (5254); Mujahid (5255, 5256, 5269); al-Rabi' (5257); Shurayh, that she may forgive and leave the dower (5258, 5259, 5265) or leave half (5267); Nafi' (5260); al-Suddi, that the previously married woman leaves part of her dower or all of it (5261); Ibn Shihab, that forgiveness is hers, the previously married woman especially, and no guardian holds that over her since she has taken charge of her own affairs; if she wants to forgo her half it stands, and if she wants to take it she has the greater right (5262); that it means the women (5263), the previously married (5268) or the woman (5272); Abu Salih, the previously married woman leaves her dower (5264); Sa'id b. al-Musayyab, she may forgive her dower (5266); Ibn 'Abbas in another report, the women (5270); Ibn Zayd, if previously married she forgives (5271); Sufyan, the woman not yet consummated leaves the dower to him and takes nothing (5273). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 144} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 145} Al-Tabari says he never heard anyone name one narrator in that form except his teacher Abu Hisham; the editor corrects the narrator's name and says what Abu Hisham gave is not found in the biographical works. These are the editor's notes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 145}

Al-Tabari turns to the second part of the exception and says interpreters disagreed over whom God meant by the one who holds the marriage tie. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 146} Some said it is the guardian of the virgin; the sense is that the guardian leaves to the husband the half owed to the divorced woman before consummation and overlooks it, where the girl is not one whose dealings in her property are valid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 146} Those who said it: Ibn 'Abbas, that God permitted and ordered forgiveness, and if she forgives it stands, and if she is stingy and her guardian forgives it is valid even if she refuses (5274); and that it is the father of the virgin girl, to whom God gave the forgiveness, and she has no say beside him while in his care (5275); then 'Alqama, the guardian (5276 to 5279, 5281, 5285, 5304); the companions of 'Abd Allah b. Mas'ud (5280); al-Aswad b. Yazid (5282). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 146} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 147} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 148} Tawus and Mujahid are reported to have said the guardian and then to have returned and said the husband (5283, 5284). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 147} Al-Sha'bi relates that a man married off his sister, her husband divorced her before consummation, her brother forgave the dower and Shurayh allowed it; al-Sha'bi condemned that strongly; Shurayh later said it is the husband, who may forgive the whole dower and hand it to her in full, or she may forgo the half named for her, and if both are stubborn she takes half her dower (5286). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 147} The editor says al-Bayhaqi relates it in different wording which corrects the manuscript, and that he did not know who the family of Murra meant; these are his remarks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 147} It is reported that 'Ali asked Shurayh, who said the guardian (5287), and that Shurayh gave up that view and said the husband (5288, 5289). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 148} Then al-Hasan, the guardian (5290 to 5293) or the one who married her off (5294); Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (5295, 5296, 5297); al-Sha'bi (5297); 'Ata' (5298); Abu Salih, the guardian of the virgin (5299); al-Zuhri, the guardian of the virgin (5300) and in one report the father (5303); Ibn 'Abbas, the guardian (5301); Tawus, 'Ikrima and al-Hasan (5302); Mujahid (5305); al-Suddi, the virgin's guardian (5306). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 148} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 149} These are the views of Companions and Followers under their names; al-Tabari then sets out the other view and his argument, which follows in its place.

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-237|2:237]]) who said the holder of the contract is the guardian: Ibn Zayd, the father (5307); Zayd b. Aslam and Rabi'a, the father for his virgin daughter and the master for his slave woman, found in the Muwatta' (5308); Malik, that he may forgive half the dower so long as no divorce has occurred, where the editor says the source has a blank and he could not find Malik's wording (5309); Ibn Shihab, the virgin whose guardian forgives, which stands, while her own forgiveness does not (5310); 'Ikrima, that the woman forgives half the dower, and if she is stingy then she and the guardian who married her off, uncle, brother or father, may forgive the half even if she dislikes it (5311, 5312); Ibrahim, the guardian (5313). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 150} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 151} The editor says he found no biography for one of al-Tabari's teachers in chain 5312 and that Ibn Kathir transmits the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 150} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 151}

Al-Tabari then gives the others, who said the holder of the contract is the husband, so that the sense is that he forgives by giving her the dower in full. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 151} Among them: 'Ali b. Abi Talib (5314); it is reported that 'Ali asked Shurayh, who said the guardian, and 'Ali said no, it is the husband (5315, 5316); Ibn 'Abbas (5317 to 5320); it is reported that Jubayr b. Mut'im divorced his wife before consummation, completed the dower for her and said he had more right to forgive (5321, 5323), and in the version of Salih b. Kaysan (5322) the editor thinks the chain is broken and the right reading is in 'Abd al-Razzaq's Musannaf, that it was Nafi' b. Jubayr, and that the slip may be al-Tabari's or his teacher's; this is the editor's estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 152} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 153} Shurayh, after changing his view: if the husband wishes he gives her the dower in full (5324 to 5337), with a report that his ruling was sent back to him and that Ibrahim said what does he know of Shurayh (5327, 5328); Sa'id b. al-Musayyab (5338, 5339); Mujahid (5340 to 5344); Sa'id b. Jubayr (5345 to 5349), in whose reports Mujahid and Tawus had said the guardian, and he argued with them, asking whether it would be allowed if the guardian forgave and the woman refused, and they went back on their view and followed Sa'id (5346, 5348, 5349); Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi, the husband, who gives what he has as a favor (5350); al-Sha'bi (5351); Nafi', the woman forgives half to her husband, or the husband forgives and completes her dower (5352). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 153} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 154} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 155} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 156} The editor adds biographical notes on some narrators, corrections to names, and a parallel version from Ibn Hazm of Sa'id's report; these are his, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 155} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 156} These are Companions and Followers under their own names; the museum does not decide between them.

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-237|2:237]]) who said it is the husband: al-Rabi' (5353); Shurayh, who argued hard against opponents (5354); a mursal report from 'Amr b. Shu'ayb from the Prophet that the husband is meant, who forgives or she forgives (5355); al-Dahhak, that the woman may leave her half if she wishes or take it (5356, 5358); Sufyan (5357); Sa'id b. 'Abd al-'Aziz (5359); Shurayh, explaining both exceptions (5360). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 157} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 158} The editor gives what Ibn Kathir and al-Bayhaqi said of report 5355, including that al-Bayhaqi called it not preserved and Ibn Lahi'a not relied on; that is al-Bayhaqi quoted in the editor's note, not al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 157}

Al-Tabari then makes his choice: the better of the two views is that the husband is meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 158} His grounds: first, all agree that a guardian of a girl, virgin or previously married, young or grown, if he released her husband from her dower before the divorce or gave it to him, that release is void and her dower stays due, so what he releases after the divorce is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 158} Second, all agree that if a woman's guardian gave her divorced husband a dirham of her property other than as a forgiveness of her dower, the gift is rejected and void, and her dower is her own property. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 158} Third, all agree that if a cousin or brother of a virgin among her guardians forgave her property to her husband, the forgiveness is void and her right stands, so the same holds for every guardian, father, grandfather or maternal uncle, since God did not single out some of those who hold the contract for the validity of their forgiveness, provided they are persons whose own judgement over self and property is valid. The editor reconstructs a gap in the wording here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 158} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 159}

Al-Tabari also gives a dialogue argument. To one who says it is the guardian: is it every guardian entitled to marry her off, or some? If every one, may a man who freed a slave woman marry her off with her consent and then forgive her dower? If yes, he leaves the consensus; if no, why; if some only, proof of the restriction is asked and who is meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 159} To one who thinks the contract leaves the husband's hand once she is parted, so the guardian is meant, al-Tabari replies that the sense is that the one who holds the contract of his own marriage forgives, the article standing in for the possessive, and he cites a verse in Surat al-Nazi'at (79:41) and a line of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani; the editor gives the line's source and wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 160} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 161} So for him the husband is the one holding the contract of his own marriage in every state, before and after divorce, not the guardian, who has no power over a woman's contract without her consent except in her childhood, and God did not restrict the guardians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 161} He adds that the exception refers to the women already mentioned, and girls are not called women in Arabic, so all women are meant, and forgiveness by one under guardianship of her property is void; if the guardian were meant, guardians of grown, mature, previously married women would have the same power of forgiveness as guardians of children, which his opponents deny, and that shows their reading is wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 161} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 162} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument; the opposing views are given under their own names.

Al-Tabari then turns to the last clause of ([[passage/quran-2-237|2:237]]), which says forgiveness is nearer to piety. Interpreters differed over who is addressed. Some said men and women: Ibn 'Abbas, the one of the two who forgives is the closer to God-fearing (5361); Sa'id b. 'Abd al-'Aziz, they all forgive (5362); others said the husbands of the divorced women: al-Sha'bi (5363). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 162} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 163} Al-Tabari chose Ibn 'Abbas's view: that some of you, husbands and wives, forgive one another after parting what is due, leaving it if it remains, or paying it in full if not, is nearer to piety. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 163} The editor says he restored an incomplete sentence on the strength of the earlier reports on completing the dower. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 163}
