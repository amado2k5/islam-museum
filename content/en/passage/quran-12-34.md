**Commentary on verse 34 (pp. 90)**

Al-Tabari raises a difficulty over "so his Lord answered him and turned their scheming away": how can it be said He answered, when Joseph had made no request nor prayer to turn away their scheming, but only told his Lord that prison was dearer to him than disobeying Him? He replies that his telling was a complaint to his Lord of what he met from them, and the words "unless You turn away their scheming" carry the sense of a prayer and request for it, so God said "He answered"; it is like a man saying to another, if you do not visit me I will shun you, and the other replying, then I will visit you, since the first words carry a call to visit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 90}

The meaning: God answered Joseph's prayer and turned away from him what the Aziz's wife and her companions wanted of him by way of disobedience to God. Report 19252, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: He saved him from committing the disobedience with them, though some of what he feared from them did befall him. On "He is the Hearing, the Knowing": the Hearing of Joseph's prayer when he asked to be spared the women's scheming and of the prayer of every caller among His creatures, the Knowing of his need, what he seeks and what is good for him, and of the need of all His creatures. The editor refers to the earlier indexes for "the Hearing" and "the Knowing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 90}
