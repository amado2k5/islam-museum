## Summary
Verse 33 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 33 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 33 (pp. 509-519)**

Then al-Tabari says on "God would not punish them while you were among them, nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness" [[passage/quran-8-33|8:33]] and "what is there for them that God should not punish them while they bar from the Sacred Mosque" [[passage/quran-8-34|8:34]]: the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said: "while you were among them" means while you stay among them, and this came down on the Prophet while he stayed at Mecca; then he left them, the Muslims there sought forgiveness, and after his leaving it was sent down "nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness"; then those remaining Muslims left, and the unbelievers were punished. Report 15990, from Ibn Abza, is like this, adding that God gave him leave for the conquest of Mecca, and that is the punishment He promised them; reports 15991 and 15992, from Abu Malik: "while you were among them" means the Prophet, "while they sought forgiveness" the Muslims there, "what is there for them" means Mecca with the unbelievers in it; report 15993, from Ibn Abza, those Muslims who stayed; report 15994, from Abu Malik: the people of Mecca; reports 15995 and 15996, from al-Dahhak: the believers of Mecca and the polytheists of Mecca; report 15997, from Ibn Abbas: those who believed with you seek forgiveness at Mecca until He brought you and those who believed with you out; report 15998, from Ibn Abbas: He did not punish a town until the Prophet and those who believed with him had left it and joined him where he was told to go; report 15999, from Ibn Zayd: the people of Mecca. The editor corrects the manuscript's reading in 15991 and identifies a narrator of 15993. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 509} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 510} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 511}

Others said its sense is rather: God would not punish these polytheists of Quraysh at Mecca while you were among them, Muhammad, until He took you out from them, nor would God punish them while they said, Lord, Your forgiveness, and the like of forgiveness-seeking by words, and "what is there for them" is about the hereafter. Report 16000, from Ibn Abbas: the polytheists used to circle the House saying the call of response, and the Prophet would say, enough, enough, and they would add a saying that made a partner for God whom He owns together with what he owns, and say, Your forgiveness, Your forgiveness, so God sent down the verse; Ibn Abbas said there were two securities in them, the Prophet of God and the seeking of forgiveness, the Prophet went and the seeking of forgiveness remained, and "what is there for them" is the punishment of the hereafter while that was the punishment of this world. The editor explains "enough, enough", corrects the printed edition and identifies Abu Zumayl Simak ibn al-Walid. Report 16001, from Yazid ibn Ruman and Muhammad ibn Qays: Quraysh said to each other, Muhammad is honoured by God from among us, then said what they said in 8:32, and when evening came they regretted it and said, Your forgiveness, God, and God sent down the verse. Report 16002, from Ibn Ishaq: they used to say God will not punish us while we seek forgiveness and does not punish a community while its prophet is with it until he is taken out, and that was their saying with the Prophet among them; the editor says the words in brackets dropped out of the text and he restored them from Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 511} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 512} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 513}

Report 16003, from Abu Musa: there were two securities before; as for the Prophet he has gone, and as for forgiveness-seeking it goes on among you until the day of resurrection. The editor says al-Tirmidhi gave the like as a saying of the Prophet in the commentary of this sura and called it strange, with Isma'il ibn Ibrahim weak in hadith, and that al-Tabari's report is without doubt stopped at Abu Musa al-Ash'ari. Report 16004, from Abu al-Ala: the community of Muhammad had two securities, one went and the other remained; the editor found no mention of Amir Abu al-Khattab al-Thawri and fears his name is corrupted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 513} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 514}

Others said its sense is: God would not punish them while you were among them, Muhammad, and God would not punish the polytheists while they sought forgiveness, that is, if they had sought it; they said they did not seek it, so God said, since they did not seek forgiveness, "what is there for them that God should not punish them". Report 16005, from Qatada: the people did not seek forgiveness, and had they sought it they would not have been punished; and some of the people of knowledge used to say they are two securities God sent down, one of which passed, the Prophet of God, and the other God left as a mercy among you, forgiveness-seeking and repentance; report 16006, from al-Suddi: I would not have punished them had they sought forgiveness, and had they sought forgiveness and admitted their sins they would have been believers, and how should I not punish them when they do not seek it; report 16007, from Ibn Zayd: had they sought forgiveness I would not have punished them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 514}

Others said its sense is: God would not punish them while they entered Islam, saying their "seeking forgiveness" here is their becoming Muslim. Report 16008, from Ikrima: they asked for punishment, and He said He would not punish them while you were among them, nor while they were entering Islam; reports 16009, 16010 and 16011, from Mujahid: "while you were among them" means in their midst, "while they sought forgiveness" means becoming Muslim, and "they bar" means Quraysh barring from the Sacred Mosque. The editor corrects the manuscript's and printed edition's reading in 16010. Others said its sense is rather that among them were those for whom entering Islam had already been decreed by God; report 16012, from Ibn Abbas: God did not punish a people with their prophets in their midst until He brought them out, "while they sought forgiveness" meaning among them were those for whom entering belief was already decreed, which is the seeking of forgiveness, and "what is there for them" He punished them at Badr by the sword. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 515} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 516}

Others said its sense is rather: God would not punish them while they pray. Report 16013, from Ibn Abbas: it means "they bar", meaning by this the people of Mecca; report 16014, from Mujahid: they pray; report 16015, from al-Dahhak: it means the people of Mecca, I would not punish you with Muhammad among you, "while they sought forgiveness" meaning they believe and pray; report 16016, from Mujahid: while they pray. Others said its sense is that God would not punish the polytheists while they sought forgiveness, and this was then abrogated by "what is there for them that God should not punish them"; report 16017, from Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri: the following verse abrogated it, so they were fought at Mecca and hunger and want struck them there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 516} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 517}

Abu Ja'far said: the best of these views, to my mind, is that of those who said its interpretation is: God would not punish them while you, Muhammad, stay among them until He takes you out from among them, for I do not destroy a town with its prophet in it; "nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness" for their sins and unbelief, but they do not seek forgiveness for that, rather they persist in it, so they deserve punishment; as one says, I would not do you good while you do me wrong, meaning I will not do you good if you wrong me, and had you wronged me I would not do you good, but I do you good because you do not wrong me. Then it is said "what is there for them that God should not punish them while they bar from the Sacred Mosque" in the sense: what is their case and what stops God punishing them when they do not seek forgiveness for their unbelief so as to believe in Him, and they bar the believers in God and His Messenger from the Sacred Mosque? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 517} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 518}

He says we called this the best view because the people, meaning the polytheists of Mecca, had hurried the punishment on and said what they said in [[passage/quran-8-32|8:32]]; God said to His prophet, I would not punish them while you are among them, nor would I punish them had they sought forgiveness, and how would I not punish them after your leaving them when they bar from the Sacred Mosque? So He told him that the punishment they hurried for was coming upon them and would descend, and told them when it would descend, after his leaving from among them. There is no ground for threatening them with punishment in the hereafter when they are hurrying it in this world, though they are without doubt bound for punishment in the hereafter; rather in God's hastening it for them at Badr is the clear proof that the saying is as we said. Likewise there is no ground for the view of those who took "nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness" to mean the believers, since it is in the course of the report about the unbelievers and what God will do with them; and likewise none for the saying that it was abrogated by "what is there for them", because "nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness" is a report, and a report cannot be abrogated, only a command or prohibition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 517} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 518}

The editor cites Abu Ja'far al-Nahhas in his book on the abrogating and abrogated, who gave the view of those who made the two pronouns different, calling it a good saying though Muhammad ibn Jarir rejected it, claiming no mention of the believers had gone before to be referred to, which al-Nahhas calls a mistake since the believers were mentioned in several places in the sura; he holds that the sense itself shows them, because the unbeliever said it only mockingly and obstinately, and had he sought the truth he would have said, guide us to it, so it is as if he said, destroy the group of unbelievers and Muslims together, and the meaning is how would God destroy the Muslims? So "nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness" means the believers and "what is there for them" means the unbelievers. The editor corrects readings of the printed edition and suspects a gap in al-Tabari's wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 518}

Then he says the people of Arabic differed on why "that" enters "what is there for them that God should not punish them". Some Basran grammarians said it is an extra here and has acted as "not" acts when extra, and a verse of al-Farazdaq has this. Some people of Arabic rejected that, saying "that" enters only for a sound sense, since "what is there for them" means what stops them from being punished, so "that" entered for this, and "not" was added to show it carries the sense of denial, because preventing is denial, and the "not" in the verse has a sound sense, since a denial applied to a denial becomes an affirmation, as when you say, Zayd is not not standing, you have affirmed standing. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani and to the verse's transmission in the earlier place, and explains "sins". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 519}
