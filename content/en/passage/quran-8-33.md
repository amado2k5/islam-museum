## Summary
Verse 33 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 33 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 33 (pp. 509-514)**

Then al-Tabari says on "God would not punish them while you were among them, nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness" [[passage/quran-8-33|8:33]] and "what is there for them that God should not punish them while they bar from the Sacred Mosque" [[passage/quran-8-34|8:34]]: the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said: "while you were among them" means while you stay among them, and this came down on the Prophet while he stayed at Mecca; then he left them, the Muslims there sought forgiveness, and after his leaving it was sent down "nor would God punish them while they sought forgiveness"; then those remaining Muslims left, and the unbelievers were punished. Report 15990, from Ibn Abza, is like this, adding that God gave him leave for the conquest of Mecca, and that is the punishment He promised them; reports 15991 and 15992, from Abu Malik: "while you were among them" means the Prophet, "while they sought forgiveness" the Muslims there, "what is there for them" means Mecca with the unbelievers in it; report 15993, from Ibn Abza, those Muslims who stayed; report 15994, from Abu Malik: the people of Mecca; reports 15995 and 15996, from al-Dahhak: the believers of Mecca and the polytheists of Mecca; report 15997, from Ibn Abbas: those who believed with you seek forgiveness at Mecca until He brought you and those who believed with you out; report 15998, from Ibn Abbas: He did not punish a town until the Prophet and those who believed with him had left it and joined him where he was told to go; report 15999, from Ibn Zayd: the people of Mecca. The editor corrects the manuscript's reading in 15991 and identifies a narrator of 15993. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 509} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 510} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 511}

Others said its sense is rather: God would not punish these polytheists of Quraysh at Mecca while you were among them, Muhammad, until He took you out from them, nor would God punish them while they said, Lord, Your forgiveness, and the like of forgiveness-seeking by words, and "what is there for them" is about the hereafter. Report 16000, from Ibn Abbas: the polytheists used to circle the House saying the call of response, and the Prophet would say, enough, enough, and they would add a saying that made a partner for God whom He owns together with what he owns, and say, Your forgiveness, Your forgiveness, so God sent down the verse; Ibn Abbas said there were two securities in them, the Prophet of God and the seeking of forgiveness, the Prophet went and the seeking of forgiveness remained, and "what is there for them" is the punishment of the hereafter while that was the punishment of this world. The editor explains "enough, enough", corrects the printed edition and identifies Abu Zumayl Simak ibn al-Walid. Report 16001, from Yazid ibn Ruman and Muhammad ibn Qays: Quraysh said to each other, Muhammad is honoured by God from among us, then said what they said in 8:32, and when evening came they regretted it and said, Your forgiveness, God, and God sent down the verse. Report 16002, from Ibn Ishaq: they used to say God will not punish us while we seek forgiveness and does not punish a community while its prophet is with it until he is taken out, and that was their saying with the Prophet among them; the editor says the words in brackets dropped out of the text and he restored them from Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 511} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 512} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 513}

Report 16003, from Abu Musa: there were two securities before; as for the Prophet he has gone, and as for forgiveness-seeking it goes on among you until the day of resurrection. The editor says al-Tirmidhi gave the like as a saying of the Prophet in the commentary of this sura and called it strange, with Isma'il ibn Ibrahim weak in hadith, and that al-Tabari's report is without doubt stopped at Abu Musa al-Ash'ari. Report 16004, from Abu al-Ala: the community of Muhammad had two securities, one went and the other remained; the editor found no mention of Amir Abu al-Khattab al-Thawri and fears his name is corrupted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 513} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 514}

Others said its sense is: God would not punish them while you were among them, Muhammad, and God would not punish the polytheists while they sought forgiveness, that is, if they had sought it; they said they did not seek it, so God said, since they did not seek forgiveness, "what is there for them that God should not punish them". Report 16005, from Qatada: the people did not seek forgiveness, and had they sought it they would not have been punished; and some of the people of knowledge used to say they are two securities God sent down, one of which passed, the Prophet of God, and the other God left as a mercy among you, forgiveness-seeking and repentance; report 16006, from al-Suddi: I would not have punished them had they sought forgiveness, and had they sought forgiveness and admitted their sins they would have been believers, and how should I not punish them when they do not seek it; report 16007, from Ibn Zayd: had they sought forgiveness I would not have punished them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 514}

We stop here at the foot of p. 514, after report 16007 in the commentary on 8:33 and 8:34; the next batch begins at p. 515. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 514}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
