**Commentary on verse 66 (pp. 336-337)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet: say to these whose description I gave you, do not make excuses with falsehood by saying we were chatting and playing; "you have disbelieved", that is, you have denied the truth by what you said about the Messenger of God and the believers in him, "after your belief", after your affirming him and acknowledging him; "if We pardon one party of you, We shall punish another party". It is mentioned that "party" in this place meant one man. Ibn Ishaq used to say, which is report 16919, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the one who was pardoned, as it reached me, was Makhshi ibn Humayyir al-Ashja'i, an ally of Banu Salima, because he disapproved of some of what he heard. The editor notes that Ibn Hisham's Sira has Makhshin ibn Humayyir and that he kept the manuscript's form. Report 16920, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, through Ibn Waki', Zayd ibn Hibban and Musa ibn Ubayda: the party was one man. The editor refers to earlier explanations of pardon and party. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 336}

The interpreters differed over the meaning. Some said it means: if We pardon one party of you for disapproving what was disapproved before the unbelief, We shall punish a party for its unbelief and its mockery of the verses of God and His Messenger. Report 16922, from Ma'mar, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Muhammad ibn Thawr: some said one man among them did not join them in the talk but travelled apart, and then came down "if We pardon one party of you, We shall punish a party", and he was called a party though he was one. Others said rather it means: if a party of you repents and God pardons him, God will punish a party of you for leaving repentance. As for "they were criminals", it means We shall punish a party of them for their earning of crime, which is disbelief in God and their slander of the Messenger of God. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of criminality. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 337}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
