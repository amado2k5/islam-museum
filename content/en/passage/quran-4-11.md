## Summary
Verse 11 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 11 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Start of the commentary on verse 11 (p. 30).** Abu Jafar says that the opening verb here means God entrusts you (the editor refers to the earlier commentary on the word), that is, your Lord charges you when one of you dies and leaves male and female children, then his male and female children take his whole inheritance among them, the male receiving twice the share of the female, if he has no other heir, the same for his young and grown children and his daughters, in that all of it is divided among them in that proportion. In the editor's note the manuscript has a form "and his grown ones" and the printing's is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 30}

**Grammar of the clause and the occasion of the verse (p. 31, report 8725).** Abu Jafar says the word for the matching measure in the sentence is in the nominative as a description; the editor explains that "description" here means the preposition (referring to earlier notes), and that it is not made accusative by the first verb because the enjoining in this place is a charge and an informing in the sense of saying, and saying does not govern the nouns it reports (the editor glosses "falling upon" as transitive governing). It is as if God were saying to you, regarding your children, that a son has twice a daughter's portion. Abu Jafar then says it was mentioned that the verse came down to the Prophet to make clear the binding rule for the estate of one who died leaving heirs, because the people of the Jahiliyya gave nothing from the estate to one who did not face the enemy or fight, whether small sons or women, reserving it for the fighters and not the offspring; so God informed them that what the deceased left is shared among those named and assigned shares in this verse and at the end of the sura, small and grown children and females, and that they take their father's estate if he has no other heir, the male receiving twice the female. Al-Suddi (8725, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat) reports that in the Jahiliyya they would not give daughters or small boys a share, a man inheriting from his son only if he could bear fighting; Abd al-Rahman, brother of the poet Hassan, died leaving a wife called Umm Kujja and five sisters, and the heirs came to take his property; Umm Kujja complained to the Prophet, and this verse came down. Al-Suddi then recites the daughters' shares (two-thirds for more than two, a half for one) and what is said of Umm Kujja concerning the wives' portion with and without a child. In his note the editor gives the name as Umm Kujja (referring to his note on report 8656), says the printing and the manuscript have Umm Kuhha with a ha, and identifies Abd al-Rahman, brother of Hassan, as a brother of Hassan ibn Thabit al-Ansari, the Prophet's poet; he cites Ibn Hajar's al-Isaba, which gives al-Suddi's report and adds that Ibn Hajar did not see it from anyone else and that the genealogists name no brother of Hassan with that name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 31} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 32}

**Ibn Abbas (8726) and the view that the bequest was abrogated (pp. 32-33, reports 8726-8729).** Ibn Abbas (8726, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father from his uncle from his father from his father) says that when the fixed shares came down for the male and female child and the parents, people, or some of them, disliked them: a woman is given a quarter or an eighth, a daughter a half, and a small boy is given, though none of these fights the enemy or takes the spoils; keep silent about this matter, perhaps the Messenger of God will forget it, or we will speak to him and he will change it. One of them said: Messenger of God, do we give a girl half of what her father left though she does not ride a horse or fight, and the boy though he does nothing of use? They had done so in the Jahiliyya, giving only to the fighter, the eldest first (the editor notes that the printing added a waw that has no place and that he kept the manuscript). Others said that it came down because before it the property went to the child and the parents had the bequest, and God abrogated that by this verse. For this Muhammad ibn Amr (8727, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid or Ata from Ibn Abbas) reports that the property was for the child and the bequest for parents and kin, and God abrogated of it what He wished, making the male's portion twice the female's, a sixth each for the parents when there is a child, a half or a quarter for the husband and a quarter or an eighth for the wife; al-Muthanna (8728, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid) reports Ibn Abbas saying the same, and al-Qasim (8729, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid from Ibn Abbas) the like. The editor traces 8727 in al-Bukhari through Muhammad ibn Yusuf from Warqa from Ibn Abi Najih from Ata from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 32} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 33}

**The report of Jabir ibn Abd Allah (pp. 33-34, reports 8730-8731).** It was also reported from Jabir, as Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna relates (8730, from Wahb ibn Jarir from Shu'ba from Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir): the Messenger of God came to me when I was ill, performed ablution and sprinkled some of his ablution water on me, and I recovered; I said, Messenger of God, only a kalala will inherit from me, so what of inheritance? and the verse of the fixed shares came down. Al-Qasim (8731, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj from Ibn al-Munkadir from Jabir) relates that the Prophet and Abu Bakr visited him among the Banu Salima on foot and found him unconscious; the Prophet called for water, made ablution and sprinkled him, and when he woke he asked, how should I deal with my property? and this verse came down. The editor traces 8730 in al-Bukhari through Shu'ba, and elsewhere in his Sahih through Ibn Jurayj and through Sufyan from Ibn al-Munkadir; he quotes Ibn Kathir that Muslim and al-Nasa'i relate it from Hajjaj ibn Muhammad al-A'war from Ibn Jurayj and that the whole group relate it from Sufyan ibn Uyayna from Ibn al-Munkadir from Jabir, and that al-Suyuti added attribution to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi in his Sunan. On 8731 he says it repeats the previous report; the printing has "he called for his ablution water and made ablution," the manuscript "he called and made ablution," and al-Bukhari by this route has "he called for water," so he judges that "for water" dropped from the scribe's copy by oversight; he also says the printing did not complete the verse at the start of the report and he kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 33} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 34}

**The clause on daughters beyond two (pp. 34-35).** Abu Jafar says the left-behind females meant here are the deceased's daughters and that "beyond two" means more than two in number; they take two-thirds of what he left after him, apart from his other heirs, if he leaves no son with them. The scholars of Arabic differed over what the left-behind females in the wording stand for: some Basran grammarians, and some Kufan ones with them, said what Abu Jafar says; others of the Kufans said the sense is that the children are women, God having mentioned the children and then divided the charge across their cases. The editor has a note that the text at this point is unclear in both the printing and the manuscript, that he restored a form between brackets from the sense of the verse, and that he thinks the word for "one" was brought in from the next verse. Abu Jafar prefers the Basran view because, had "the children" been meant, the masculine plural would have been used, since "children" covers males and females. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 34} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 35}

**A single daughter and the parents (pp. 35-37).** Abu Jafar says the clause means that the one left is a single daughter, who takes a half of what the deceased left if no other child of his, male or female, is with her (the editor notes that the text does not stand without the bracketed addition, made on the pattern of its sister clauses). If someone asks where the share of two daughters is, since this gives the share of one and of more than two, he answers that theirs is established by the transmitted sunna, passed down as inheritance, in which there is no doubt. The editor remarks that he seems to mean Jabir's report on the death of Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' and the Prophet's giving his two daughters two-thirds (in al-Bayhaqi, and found in Ahmad, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah) and Zayd ibn Thabit's report on a daughter and two daughters (in al-Bukhari); he finds it strange that Abu Jafar leaves out the chains of reports for his argument here, fears something fell out from the early copyists or that Abu Jafar meant to give the reports and overlooked them, and says this is the first time Abu Jafar departs from his method in this commentary. On the parents, Abu Jafar says the deceased's father and mother each take a sixth of what he left, mother and father alike, neither exceeding a sixth when the deceased has a child, male or female, one or several. If someone objects that this would mean the father should not exceed a sixth beside a single daughter, contrary to the agreement of the community that the remainder after her share goes wholly to the father, he replies that it is not as supposed: each has a fixed sixth from God, and the father is then given more from the rest of the half because of the nearness of the deceased's agnatic kin, for the rule on what the fixed shares leave over is that it belongs to the nearest agnates by the ruling on the Prophet's tongue, and the father is the nearest agnate of his son when the dead man has no son. The editor identifies this with the report of the two Shaykhs from Ibn Abbas on giving the fixed shares to their holders, the remainder going to the nearest male (also transmitted with "nearest man," meaning nearest agnate), calls it strange that Abu Jafar omits his hadith proof as he has throughout, and suggests he abbreviates because he must have covered it elsewhere in his books; in further notes he records where the printing and the manuscript differ in this passage and which he chose. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 36} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 37}

**When there is no child and the parents inherit (pp. 37-38).** Abu Jafar says the clause means that if the deceased has no child, male or female, and his parents inherit from him with no other heir, the mother takes a third of the whole estate. If asked who takes the other two-thirds, he says the father; and why, because he is the nearest of the deceased's kin (the editor notes that the manuscript has "nearest child of the deceased," a scribal error, and that the printing is right). That is why the verse leaves out who holds the other two-thirds, God having made known through His Messenger that the nearest agnate of every deceased person has the first claim on the inheritance once the holders of fixed shares have received theirs. He adds that this same reasoning explains why the mother's share is named when the deceased leaves no heir besides his parents, since a mother is never an agnate; God stated her share and left out who has the rest because He had taught them in His general statement who gets what remains of the estate after the fixed-share holders, which spares repeating the ruling with each person assigned a share. Abu Jafar then raises a question: why is the parents' rule given with several brothers and not with a single brother? He answers that their rule differs between a group of brothers and one brother, and the text breaks off at the end of the page in the words that it was in the clarification of... (the editor notes that the word "I said" is not in the manuscript but the context requires it, so he supplied it). In the notes to these pages the editor also remarks that Abu Jafar abbreviates his hadith argument and omits its chains, an observation he makes in his own name and does not attribute to al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 37} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 38}

**The mother's share alongside brothers, continued (p. 39).** Abu Jafar completes the argument: in stating the parents' ruling on what they inherit from their dead child when he has brothers, God made it unnecessary to say that their ruling on what they inherit stays unchanged from what it was when the deceased has no brother and no heir but them, since it is known to them that whoever has a right by God's decree does not lose it except by God's own transfer of it. So God's fixing for the mother what He fixed (a third of her dead child's property if he has no heir besides her and his father and no brother) is a plain sign that it is her binding right until the one who fixed it changes it; and since He changed it with brothers in a group, and left it unchanged with a single brother, it is known that her share changes only in the case where He changed it. The editor has two notes on the wording here: the printing has what Abu Jafar would not write, the manuscript is to be read as "and no brother" joined to what precedes, and the "and" in "which is a third" must be kept or the sentence breaks down. Abu Jafar then reports the disagreement on the number of brothers meant: a group of the Companions of the Messenger of God, the Followers, and the scholars of Islam after them in every age said that two or more are meant, whether females, males, or one of each; many who held this argued that the community transmitted it from God's declaration on His Messenger's tongue, so widely that it cuts off excuse and doubt. The editor remarks that Abu Jafar again leaves out the line of reports for his proof, as in the two earlier places (pp. 36 and 37), and refers to al-Bayhaqi's Sunan al-Kubra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 39} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 40}

**The view of Ibn Abbas (p. 40, report 8732).** It is reported from Ibn Abbas that he said a group of at least three is meant, and he denied that God blocked the mother from her third beside the father by fewer than three brothers; so for parents with two brothers he said the mother takes a third and the remainder goes to the father, as scholars say for parents with one brother. Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam (8732, from Ibn Abi Fudayk from Ibn Abi Dhi'b from Shu'ba, client of Ibn Abbas) relates that Ibn Abbas went in to Uthman and asked why two brothers return the mother to a sixth when God said "brothers" and two are not brothers in the speech of Uthman's people; Uthman answered: can I undo a matter that was in force before me, which people inherited and which has spread through the cities? The editor says the printing added a formula of blessing after Uthman's name and he kept the manuscript. He traces the report in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan al-Kubra through Ishaq ibn Ibrahim from Shababa from Ibn Abi Dhi'b from Shu'ba; Ibn Kathir quotes it in his commentary and comments that its soundness is open to question because Malik ibn Anas spoke of this Shu'ba, that had it been sound from Ibn Abbas his close companions would have held it whereas the transmission from them is the opposite, and that Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi al-Zinad relates from Kharija ibn Zayd from his father that two brothers are called brothers; Ibn Kathir wrote a separate part on this question. The editor identifies Shu'ba, client of Ibn Abbas, as read from the copy (the reading is uncertain in the scan) and cites Ibn Hibban's remark that he related from Ibn Abbas what has no basis, and that most scholars left off using him as proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 40}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the argument from Arabic usage (pp. 41-44).** Abu Jafar says the sound view, in his judgement, is that two or more of the deceased's brothers are meant, as the Companions said and not as Ibn Abbas said, because the community transmitted the soundness of what the Companions said and rejected what Ibn Abbas said (the editor again remarks that this is a place where the proof should have been given). If someone asks how the plural is used of two brothers when the dual has a form in Arabic unlike the plural, he answers that it is the way of the Arabs to join two expressions whose meanings are close even if they differ in some respects: it is common to say "I struck of Abd Allah and Amr their heads" and "I hurt of them their backs," which is more common than the dual, though the dual is also said. He cites a line of al-Farazdaq on two hearts that long and love (the editor traces it in the poet's diwan, the Naqa'id, Sibawayh and al-Shajari's Amali, says it belongs to the poem from which a line was quoted on p. 27, in which the poet speaks of his sport, lying and trifling and mentions his beloved; he explains that in the neighbouring lines the poet says he prayed to God to afflict her husband with a lasting illness that stupefies him so that he does not watch over her, that God answered and sent water into his eyes, that physicians were sought and the poet was let in to treat him for two years while she was near; he glosses "one whose heart is broken" as one broken by grief, from the breaking of a bone after it has set, and the next word as one whom love has inflamed until it burned his heart, notes that the manuscript and printing have another form with a different letter that may also be right, and calls the reading of the diwan and the Naqa'id poor, Abu Ubayda having explained it as one in wooden splints, which the editor judges worthless). More eloquent still, Abu Jafar says, is the plural for what is in the hearts, as in the verse of the sura of Banning, which he cites here [[passage/quran-66-4|66:4]]. So when anything single in a person is joined to a single one in another person and they become two of two, it is put in the plural form, and since two brothers are two persons from two different selves, their meaning resembles the single organs of a person, so the two are given the form for two organs: "brothers" is said for two brothers as "backs" is said for two backs, "mouths" for two mouths and "hearts" for two hearts. Some grammarians said "brothers" is used because the least plural is two. Abu Jafar replies that even if this is so in sense, it is no ground for permitting what usage has fixed among the Arabs in one form to be given the form of three or more: one who says "your two brothers stood" knows each is a single joined to the other, yet the Arabs do not allow a plural verb for them, since whatever speech has run with a known form, when someone changes it from what they know, they reject it (in a note the editor cites the verse in the sura of Hud on the two verbs for rejecting [[passage/quran-11-70|11:70]]). So the two brothers have a form distinct from three or more and cannot be shifted to the other except by an understood meaning, and no view is sounder than his. The editor's notes on pp. 42-44 correct a number of passages where the printing and the manuscript differ, and he states that the first publisher misread and altered some sentences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 41} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 42} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 43} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 44}

**Why was the mother reduced from a third? (pp. 44-45, reports 8733-8735).** Abu Jafar asks why the mother is reduced from her third when two or more of the deceased's brothers are with her, and says scholars differed. Some said she is reduced and not the father because the father bears their upkeep and their mother does not; Bishr ibn Mu'adh (8733, from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Sa'id from Qatada) says they harm the mother without inheriting, a single brother does not block her from the third and more than one does, and the scholars thought they blocked their mother because their father arranges their marriages and spends on them and their mother does not (the editor records the two texts' difference over the first verb and that he chose a reading following al-Durr al-Manthur and Ibn Kathir; he traces the report to Ibn Kathir, who says "this is a fine statement," and to al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur). Others said the mother is reduced by a sixth and limited to one sixth to help the deceased's brothers with the sixth they blocked her from; al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8734, from Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar from Ibn Tawus from his father from Ibn Abbas) relates that the sixth the brothers block the mother from, they block her so that it is theirs and not their mother's. A report from Ibn Abbas on another question, the kalala, is placed beside it: Yunus (8735, from Ibn Uyayna from Amr ibn Dinar from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad) relates that Ibn Abbas said the kalala is one who has neither child nor parent. Abu Jafar says the soundest is that God fixed a sixth for the mother with brothers because He knows best the welfare of His creation; it may be because of what He charged fathers with for their children, and it may be for another reason, and this is not what we are charged to know; we are told to act on what we know. As for what is reported from Tawus from Ibn Abbas, it contradicts what the community holds, since all agree that a brother of the deceased has no inheritance beside the deceased's father, and their agreement against it suffices as witness to its invalidity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 44} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 45}

**Debt before the bequest (p. 46, reports 8736-8737).** Abu Jafar then explains the words of the verse on debt and bequest: what God apportioned to the dead man's sons and daughters and parents from his estate is divided as the verse sets out after paying the deceased's debt from his estate and after carrying out his bequest in its place once the whole debt is paid, so that God gave no heir or legatee anything except after the debt is met from the whole estate, even if it exceeds it. Then the legatees become partners with the heirs, after the debt, in what remains, up to what was bequeathed to them, provided it does not exceed a third; if it does, the heirs may approve or return the excess over a third, and what is up to the third stands. He says the community agrees on all this and that a report from the Prophet is related on it: Muhammad ibn Bashshar (8736, from Yazid ibn Harun from Sufyan from Abu Ishaq from al-Harith al-A'war from Ali) relates that Ali said you recite this verse yet the Messenger of God ruled that the debt comes before the bequest; Ibn Bashshar (8737, from Yazid ibn Harun from Zakariyya ibn Abi Za'ida from Abu Ishaq from al-Harith from Ali from the Prophet) relates it likewise. The editor notes that the word for "in its place" is so in the printing and undotted in the manuscript and is strange here, that if given the choice he would have chosen "to its people" but left it as it is lest it be right in some other way, and that the printing dropped a conjunction before "the Messenger of God" which he restored from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 46}

**The rest of the report of Ali and its tracing (p. 47, reports 8738-8739).** Abu al-Sa'ib (8738, from Hafs ibn Ghiyath from Ash'ath from Abu Ishaq from al-Harith from Ali from the Prophet) relates it likewise, and Ibn Humayd (8739, from Harun ibn al-Mughira from Ibn Mujahid from his father) relates that the debt is begun with before the bequest. The editor says reports 8736, 8737 and 8738 are weak because of al-Harith al-A'war, that is al-Harith ibn Abd Allah al-Hamdani, who is very weak, and that al-Sha'bi and others called him a liar; he refers to what his brother Ahmad wrote at an earlier place and in the Musnad, and says the three chains revolve around him. He notes that Ahmad related it at length in his Musnad, and that it is given by al-Bayhaqi in the Sunan al-Kubra, al-Hakim in the Mustadrak, Ibn Kathir in his commentary (who says Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah and the commentators related it) and al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur, who attributes it to a group of compilers; al-Shafi'i gave it in al-Umm in an abridged form like al-Tabari's and said that a report from the Prophet exists on beginning with the debt before the bequest which the hadith scholars do not establish, then gave it through Sufyan from Abu Ishaq. He quotes al-Bayhaqi that the hadith scholars declined to establish it because al-Harith stood alone in relating it from Ali and his report is not used as proof given what the memorizers knew of him; al-Hakim, who mentioned the same defect and said that for this reason the two Shaykhs did not include it, adding that he had heard this ruling from Zayd ibn Thabit and giving it with its chain; and Ibn Kathir's report that al-Tirmidhi said "we know it only from the hadith of al-Harith al-A'war, and some scholars have spoken about him," to which Ibn Kathir adds that he was nonetheless a memorizer of the fixed shares who took care over them and over arithmetic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 47}

**The difference in recitation and al-Tabari's preference (pp. 47-48).** Abu Jafar says the reciters differed over the verb of the bequest here: most reciters of Medina and Iraq read it with the named agent (active voice), and some of Mecca, Syria and Kufa read it in the passive. Abu Jafar prefers the active reading because the whole verse speaks of someone whose agent is named, so what follows runs on like what precedes it; and he explains the sentence as the parents' share coming after what is paid for the deceased by way of bequest or debt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 47} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 48}

**"Your fathers and your sons" and what nearness in benefit means (pp. 48-50, reports 8740-8744).** Abu Jafar says the fathers and sons meant are those concerning whom God gave His charge, the division of your dead's inheritance among them as He named and explained in the verse, and the sense is: give them their rights from the heritage of their dead as He commanded you to give them, for it is not within your knowledge which of them is nearer and more useful to you in your present life and your life to come. In a note the editor explains that the wording calls for treating the two nouns as the predicate of an omitted subject, that none of the commentators pointed to this construction, that al-Qurtubi said it is nominative as an inchoative with a hidden predicate meaning "they are those among whom it is divided and who are given," that al-Alusi said the address is to the heirs, "your fathers" is the inchoative, "your sons" is joined to it and the clause after is its predicate, and that al-Ukbari said likewise; the editor judges the best statement to be Abu Jafar's. The interpreters differed about nearness in benefit. Some said it means nearest in benefit in the next world, and for this al-Muthanna (8740, from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports Ibn Abbas that the most obedient to God among fathers and sons will be highest in rank on the Day of Resurrection, because God lets believers intercede for one another. Others said it means in this world: Muhammad ibn Amr (8741, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid) gave this, al-Muthanna (8742, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid) the same, and Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8743, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat from al-Suddi) said that some held it of benefit in the next world and some of benefit in this. Others held what Abu Jafar holds, and Yunus (8744, from Ibn Wahb from Ibn Zayd) reports that the sense is which of them is better for you in religion and in this world, the father or the son who inherit from you, in that God let in no one else, fixed the inheritances for them and brought no others to share your wealth (the editor notes that a word in this report is corrupted in the printing and the manuscript and that he corrected it). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 48} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 49} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 50}

**The meaning of the obligation and the close of the verse (pp. 50-51).** Abu Jafar says the word for obligation means known, timed shares which God has set out for them (the editor explains "timed" as limited and measured by a bound, and refers to the explanation in volume 7, the glossary and the earlier commentary on the root words for fixing a share). He says its accusative is as a verbal noun for the charge at the start of the verse, so that it is taken out of the sense of the sentence because its meaning is what he has described, and it could also be accusative as a "taking out" from the clause about brothers, as one says "it is yours as a gift, yours as a charity from me upon you" (the editor says "taking out" seems to mean the confirming circumstantial clause and refers to volume 7). On the closing words that describe God as knowing and wise, Abu Jafar says the sense is that God has always known what makes His creatures right, so people should stop at what He commands, which sets their affairs right, and that He has always been wise in His ordering, including in how they divide inheritance among each other and in the rulings He decrees between them, with no flaw or slip entering His judgement, since it is the decree of one from whom the places of benefit, at the start and in the outcome, are not hidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 50} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 51} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 51}
