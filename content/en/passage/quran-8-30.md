## Summary
Verse 30 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/hijra-departure|Hijra Departure]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 30 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 30 (pp. 491-502)**

Then al-Tabari says on "when those who disbelieved plotted against you to confine you, or kill you, or drive you out; they plot and God plots, and God is the best of plotters" [[passage/quran-8-30|8:30]]: he says to His prophet Muhammad, reminding him of His favours to him: remember, Muhammad, when those who disbelieved among the polytheists of your people plotted against you to confine you. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "plotting". The interpreters differed over the interpretation of "to confine you". Some said its meaning is to bind you; report 15956, from Ibn Abbas: to tie you; 15957, from Mujahid; 15958, from Qatada: to bind you firmly, meaning by that the Prophet of God, who was then in Mecca; 15959, from Qatada and Miqsam: they said bind him with a bond; and 15960, from al-Suddi: confining is imprisonment and binding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 491} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 492}

Others said its meaning is imprisonment; report 15961, from Ata: to jail you, and Abd Allah ibn Kathir said it; report 15962, from Ibn Zayd: they said jail him. Others said its meaning is rather to bewitch you; report 15963, from al-Muttalib ibn Abi Wada'a: Abu Talib said to the Messenger of God, what is your people plotting against you? He said, they want to bewitch me, kill me and drive me out; he said, who told you this? He said, my Lord; he said, a good Lord is your Lord, so take good care of Him; the Messenger of God said, I take care of Him? Rather He takes good care of me; and the verse came down. Report 15964, from Ubayd ibn Umayr, is like it: when they plotted against the Prophet to kill him, confine him or drive him out, Abu Talib said to him, do you know what they plotted against you? He said yes; he said, tell me; he said who told you? He said, my Lord; he said, a good Lord is your Lord, take good care of Him; he said, do I take care of Him, or does He take care of me? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 492} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 493}

In his note on 15963 the editor identifies Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Basri, known as al-Wasawisi, inclining to his being Ibn Abi Dirar al-Razi, the truthful, and Abd al-Majid ibn Abi Rawwad, whom Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in held trustworthy and Abu Hatim and Ibn Sa'd weakened, corrects the name Ubayd ibn Umayr, and says al-Muttalib ibn Abi Wada'a al-Sahmi was a Companion. He cites Ibn Kathir on this report: the mention of Abu Talib in it is very strange, indeed rejected, because the verse is Medinan and Quraysh's gathering over confining, banishing or killing was on the night of the migration, about three years after Abu Talib's death; and the editor says he takes a different view of the report from Ibn Kathir, and points out that Ibn Jurayj will say in 15976 that this verse is Meccan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 493}

Al-Tabari says: the sense of his people's plotting against the Messenger of God to confine him seems to be what is in report 15965, from Ibn Abbas: a group of Quraysh, nobles of every tribe, gathered to enter the Dar al-Nadwa, and Iblis met them in the form of a venerable old man. When they saw him they said, who are you? He said, an old man of Najd, I heard you had gathered and wished to be present, and you will not lack opinion and counsel from me. They said, yes, come in, and he entered with them and said, look at this man's affair, for he will soon leap upon you in your affairs by his command. One said, hold him in bonds and wait for the stroke of fate until he perishes as the poets before him perished, Zuhayr and al-Nabigha. The Najdi old man, God's enemy, cried out: by God this is not a good opinion for you, his Lord will bring him out from his confinement to his companions and they will soon leap on him until they take him from your hands and keep him from you. They said, look for something other than this. One said, expel him from among you and rest from him, for once he is out it will not harm you what he does or where he lands. The Najdi old man said, this is not an opinion for you; have you not seen the sweetness of his speech, the ease of his tongue and how he takes hearts with what you hear of his talk? If you did it and he went among the Arabs, they would gather to him and he would come to you until he drove you from your land and killed your nobles. They said, true, look for another opinion. Abu Jahl said, I will advise you with an opinion I do not think you have yet seen: we take from each tribe a noble young man of standing and give each a sharp sword, and they strike him as one man, so when they have killed him his blood is spread among all the tribes, and I do not think this clan of Banu Hashim can fight all Quraysh, for when they see that they will accept the blood price and we shall be at rest. The Najdi old man said, this, by God, is the opinion. They dispersed on that, agreed on it, and Gabriel came to the Prophet and told him not to spend that night in his bed, and God gave him leave to leave, and after his arrival in Medina sent down the sura of the Spoils, reminding him of His favours to him, and sent down about their words "wait for the stroke of fate" [[passage/quran-52-30|52:30]]; that day was called the Day of the Press, for the opinion they gathered on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 494} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 495} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 496}

The editor comments on 15965: it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira with a chain that includes someone Ibn Ishaq did not name, he corrects readings of the manuscript in several places, and explains "of standing", "noble young man" and "the blood price". He gives the objection made to the report, that the verse of al-Tur is Meccan and came down some time before the migration while Ibn Ishaq's placing of the verse after the report suggests it came down on the night of the migration. On p. 494 he comments on Ibn Kathir's saying cited above: he thinks the first two reports may concern another matter and another night, and almost decides that Ibn Jurayj's report has no link with the migration and that Ibn Kathir followed al-Tabari in what he supposed; he brings from Ibn Hisham's Sira the gathering of the nobles of Quraysh in the Hijr, their taunting the Prophet as he circled, his words to them and Abu Bakr standing before him, and says that was a long time before the migration, in Abu Talib's lifetime, so that it may be the report where Ubayd ibn Umayr in his report from al-Muttalib ibn Abi Wada'a said it was his people's plotting against him; if that were sound Ibn Kathir's saying would have no ground. That is the editor's opinion, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 494} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 496}

Report 15966, from Qatada and Miqsam: they took counsel about him one night while at Mecca; some said, when morning comes bind him with bonds; some said rather kill him; some said rather expel him; when morning came they saw Ali, and God turned back their plot. Report 15967, from Ikrima: when the Prophet and Abu Bakr left for the cave, Ali ibn Abi Talib was told to sleep in his place, and the polytheists spent the night watching him, and seeing him asleep thought he was the Prophet and left him; in the morning they rose against him and found Ali, and said, where is your companion? He said, I do not know, and they rode hard and soft mounts in search of him. Report 15968, from Ibn Abbas, is like it, adding that the Prophet reached the cave and that when the polytheists reached the mountain and passed the cave they saw a spider's web on its mouth and said, had he entered here there would be no web on its mouth; and he stayed in it three days. The editor weakens Uthman al-Jazari in the chain of 15968 and points to Ahmad's giving it in the Musnad and to al-Haythami's remark in Majma' al-Zawa'id. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 496} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 497}

Report 15969, from al-Suddi: the elders of Quraysh gathered to take counsel about the Prophet after the Ansar became Muslims, afraid his cause would rise once he found a refuge to turn to, and Iblis came in the form of a man of Najd and entered with them in the Dar al-Nadwa; they were shy and let him be. One said, take Muhammad when he lies on his bed and put him in a house and wait for the stroke of fate; Iblis said, what an evil thing you said, you put him in a house and his companions come and take him out and there is fighting among you. They said, the old man is right. He said, expel him from your town; Iblis said, an evil thing, you expel him from your town when he has corrupted your fools, and he comes to another town and corrupts their fools, then comes to you with horses and men. Abu Jahl said, rather we go to every clan of Quraysh and take a man from each and give them weapons and they fall on Muhammad together and strike him as one man, and the sons of Abd al-Muttalib cannot kill Quraysh, so they have only the blood price. Iblis said, true, and this young man has the best opinion of you. God told His Messenger, and Ali slept on the bed while he and Abu Bakr went to the cave, and that is God's saying "to confine you or kill you or drive you out", confining being imprisonment and binding, and it is His saying in [[passage/quran-17-76|17:76]]. When he migrated, Umar met him and asked what happened to the people, thinking they were destroyed when he left from among them, as was done with nations, and the Prophet said, they were commanded to fight. The editor explains "were afraid". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 498} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 499}

Reports 15970, 15971 and 15972, from Mujahid: the unbelievers of Quraysh wanted that for Muhammad before he left Mecca; report 15973, from Ibn Abbas: it is the Prophet, whom they plotted against while he was at Mecca. Report 15974, from Ibn Zayd: they gathered and took counsel about the Messenger of God and said, kill this man; one said, no man will kill him but will be killed for him; they said, take him and jail him and put irons on him; they said, his household will not leave you; they said, expel him; they said, then he will gather people against you; and Iblis was with them in the form of a man of Najd. They agreed that when he came to circle the House and touch the stone they would gather on him, crowd him and kill him, so his people would not know who killed him and would accept the blood price. When he came to circle the House they gathered on him and crowded him; Abu Bakr came, was told, and found no way in; he said [[passage/quran-40-28|40:28]], and then God relieved it from him. When night fell Gabriel came to him and said, who are your companions? He said, so-and-so and so-and-so and so-and-so; he said, no, we know them better than you, Muhammad, they are night-gnats. Those men were seized from their beds while standing and brought to the Prophet; one was brought forward to Gabriel who put kohl in his eyes and let him go; he said, what is his form, Gabriel? He said, you are spared him, Prophet of God. Then another was brought forward and struck on the head once with a staff and let go; then another struck on the knee; then another given a drink of watered milk; and a fifth was brought and, when he went out from his house, passed an arrow-maker and an arrowhead caught in his cloak, he twisted and cut the vein of his leg. The one with kohl put in his eyes woke blind, the one given watered milk woke with dropsy, the one struck on the head was taken by the naqba, a great sore, and the one struck on the knee woke crippled; and that is God's saying in the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 499} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 501}

In his notes on 15974 the editor says "he will incite people" is better read "he will call them out", and that "crowded him" is with the dotted letter, meaning they rose over him and covered him until no gap was left, citing a verse of al-Namir ibn Tawlab describing the massing of fighters as evidence; he explains "night fell", "night-gnat", "watered milk", "arrowhead", "the vein" and "the naqba", and points to a possible gap in the report since it does not say what Gabriel did to the fifth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 499} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 501}

Report 15975, from Ibn Ishaq on "they plot and God plots": I plotted against them with My firm stratagem until He delivered you from them, as in the Sira. Report 15976, from Ikrima: this is Meccan, and Ibn Jurayj said Mujahid said this is Meccan; the editor says to decide that this verse or those that follow it came down at Mecca is hard and objection to it has many sides not for here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 502}

Abu Ja'far said: so the sense of the words is: remember, Muhammad, My favour to you in My plotting against whoever tried to plot against you among the polytheists of your people by confining, killing or expelling you from your homeland until I rescued you from them and destroyed them; so go on with My command in fighting those who fight you among the polytheists and turning from answering what I sent you with of the right religion, and let their many numbers not frighten you, for your Lord is the best of plotters against whoever disbelieves in Him, worships another and goes against His command and prohibition. We have shown the meaning of "plotting" earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 502}

We stop here at the foot of p. 502, with the commentary on 8:30 complete; the next batch begins with the commentary on 8:31, which starts on this page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 502}
