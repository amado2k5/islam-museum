## Summary
Verse 101 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 101 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the question (p. 61).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: how can you believers disbelieve and turn back on your heels after believing in God and His messenger, when the proofs God sent down in His book to His Prophet are read out in your hearing and His messenger stands in your midst as a further proof beside the verses of His book, all of it calling you to the truth, showing you guidance and forbidding error? He asks what excuse they could have with their Lord in denying the prophethood of their Prophet and returning to the affair of their pre-Islamic period, with these clear proofs before them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 61}

**Qatada (7533, p. 61).** From Bishr, from Yazid ibn Zurayʿ, from Sa'id, from Qatada: two clear signs, the presence of God's Prophet and God's book; as for the Prophet, he has passed, and as for the book, God left it among them as mercy and favour, with its permitted and forbidden, obedience and disobedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 61}

**Holding fast and guidance (pp. 61-62).** Al-Tabari explains that whoever clings to God clings to God's means and keeps to His religion and obedience, and that being guided means being granted success on a clear, straight and uncrooked road leading to God's good pleasure, deliverance from His punishment and the winning of His garden. He relates from al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj that holding fast means believing in God (7534). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 61} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 62}

**The root meaning in the language (pp. 62-63).** He says the root of the verb is prevention, so whatever prevents something is its "protector" and one who takes refuge in it is said to hold fast to it. He cites a line of al-Farazdaq in boasting (the editor notes it is in his Diwan and the Naqa'id, the opening of a poem answering Jarir's satire). Hence a rope is called by a related noun, as is the means by which a man reaches his need, and he cites a line of al-A'sha in praise of Qays ibn Ma'dikarib al-Kindi, explaining the plural as means of covenant and safety; the editor adds that Qays's awe among the tribes gave him a pledge with each tribe to pass through its land safely. The Arabs say both "I held fast by a rope of so-and-so" and "I held fast to it" and "I held it fast", and the more eloquent usage adds the preposition, as in the Qur'an at [[passage/quran-3-103|3:103]]; the transitive use occurs in a line by an unknown poet (the editor refers to al-Farra'), which he pairs with similar cases such as "I took the fodder" and "I took by the rein", and "I hung on to her" in two forms, citing another anonymous line (the editor explains from al-Farra' the description of the girl in it). Al-Tabari says he already explained guidance and the straight path as Islam earlier and does not repeat it; the editor refers to the earlier places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 62} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 63}

**The reported occasion: the Aws and Khazraj (7535, pp. 63-64).** Al-Tabari notes that what was revealed over the quarrel of the two tribes came from this verse. He relates from Abu Kurayb, Hasan ibn Atiyya, Qays ibn al-Rabi', al-Agharr ibn al-Sabbah, Khalifa ibn Husayn, Abu Nasr, Ibn Abbas that the Aws and Khazraj had war between them in the pre-Islamic period; as they sat they recalled what had been between them until they grew angry and rose against one another with arms, and this verse and the next were revealed, followed by [[passage/quran-3-103|3:103]]. The editor notes that the manuscript reads "every month" while other works such as al-Durr al-Manthur and al-Qurtubi's commentary say only evil or fighting, and he suspects a copyist's slip. He gives the transmitters: Hasan ibn Atiyya was treated earlier; Qays ibn al-Rabi' al-Asadi, Abu Muhammad al-Kufi, was declared reliable by al-Thawri, Shu'ba and others while others weakened him, saying he is not strong, his hadith is written but not argued from; al-Agharr ibn al-Sabbah al-Tamimi al-Minqari was called reliable by Ibn Ma'in and al-Nasa'i; of Abu Nasr al-Asadi, al-Bukhari said his hearing from Ibn Abbas is not known, while Abu Zur'a called him reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 63} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 64}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 64}
