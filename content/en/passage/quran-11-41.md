**Commentary on verse 41 (pp. 327-330)**

On [[passage/quran-11-41|11:41]] al-Tabari says: Noah said, ride in the ark, 'in the name of God be its course and its mooring'. A phrase is left out, which the report makes unnecessary: 'We said, carry in it two of every pair and your family' and so on; so Noah carried them in it and said to them, ride in it. The readers differed on 'majraha wa mursaha'. Most readers of Medina and Basra and some Kufans read 'mujraha wa mursaha' with damma on the mim in both words, from 'ajra' and 'arsa', with two possible constructions: the nominative, meaning in God's name is its causing to run and its mooring, so both are nominative through the ba'; or the accusative, meaning in God's name at its running and mooring, or at the time of its running and mooring, so 'in God's name' is complete in itself, like what one says when starting a work, and the two are accusative as the Arabs put in the accusative their saying 'praise be to God for your new moon and your last night of it', meaning the beginning and end of the moon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 328}

Most Kufan readers read 'bismillahi majraha wa mursaha' with fatha on the mim of 'majraha' and damma on 'mursaha', making 'majraha' a verbal noun from 'jara, yajri, majran' and 'mursaha' from 'arsa, yursi, irsa'', with the same two constructions. It is reported of Abu Raja' al-Utaridi that he read 'mujriha wa mursiha' with damma on the mim in both, making them attributes of God, with two constructions, the commoner the genitive, since the sense on this reading is 'in the name of God, who makes the ark run and moors it'; the accusative as a circumstantial is possible if the article is dropped. It is related that a certain Kufan read 'majraha wa marsaha' with fatha in both, from 'jara' and 'rasa', meaning in the state of its running and its mooring, and he cites a line of Antara on the soul's steadfastness in hardship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 329}

Al-Tabari says the reading he chooses is that of those who read 'bismillahi majraha' with fatha on the mim 'wa mursaha' with damma, meaning in God's name when it runs and when it moors. He chose the fatha in 'majraha' because it is close to 'and it ran with them in waves like mountains', which does not say 'was made to run with them'; whoever reads 'mujraha' ought to read 'and it is made to run with them', and their agreement on reading 'tajri' with fatha on the ta is clear evidence that the form in 'majraha' is with fatha on the mim. He chose the damma in 'mursaha' because the readers who are authority agreed on it. 'Its course' means its going, 'its mooring' its stopping, as God stopped it and moored it. Mujahid used to read both with damma. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 329}

Reports 18182 to 18185, from Mujahid: in the name of God when they ride, sail and moor; report 18186, from al-Dahhak: when he wanted it to moor he said 'in the name of God' and it moored, and when he wanted it to run he said 'in the name of God' and it ran. His words 'my Lord is Forgiving, Merciful' mean my Lord covers the sins of whoever repents and turns to Him and is merciful to them in not punishing them after repentance. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'mooring', 'Forgiving' and 'Merciful'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 330}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
