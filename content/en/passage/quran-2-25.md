## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari explains "and give good news" as "tell them"; glad tidings are news of what pleases. It is God's command to His Prophet to announce to the believers who confirm their faith with good works that they have gardens with rivers flowing beneath them, and to them alone, not to those who denied him or who professed belief in words, denied it inwardly and never made it real in deeds; for these there is the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 383} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 384}
He says "jannat" is the plural of "janna", a garden, and that what is meant is its trees, fruit and plantings, not its ground, hence "rivers flowing beneath them", since water running under the ground would not be seen without removing a covering. He reports from Masruq that the date palms of Paradise are heaped with fruit from root to branch, the fruit like large jars, and when one is picked another takes its place, while its water flows without a channel; al-Tabari holds this a better description than rivers beneath the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 384} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 385}
He reads the verse as God encouraging His servants to believe, as the previous verse warned them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 385}
On "whenever they are provided with fruit from it they say: this is what we were provided with before" he says the pronoun returns to the gardens, meaning their trees. He gives two readings. The first, "what we were given before in the world", comes from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions, Qatada and Ibn Zayd, with Mujahid's "how like it is". The second is "what we were given from Paradise's fruits before this", because of how closely they resemble one another in colour and taste, reasoning that when anything is picked the like of it returns in its place, reported from Abu Ubayda through Masruq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 385} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 386}
