**Commentary on verse 21: the command to enter the Holy Land (pp. 167-171)**

On 'my people, enter the Holy Land which God has written for you', al-Tabari says this reports Moses' words to his people of the Children of Israel and his command, at God's command, to enter the Holy Land. The commentators differ over which land: some said Mount Sinai and its surroundings, as reports 11644 and 11645 from Mujahid and 11646 from Ibn Abbas say; others said it is Syria, as report 11647 from Qatada says. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 167}

Others said the land of Jericho, as reports 11648 from Ibn Zayd, 11649 from al-Suddi and 11650 from Ibn Abbas say. It is also said to be Damascus, Palestine and part of Jordan. 'Holy' means purified and blessed, and reports 11651 and 11652 from Mujahid say 'blessed'. Al-Tabari judges the right course is to say it is the Holy Land as Moses said, since saying it is one land and not another cannot be known as true except by a report, and there is none that allows a firm testimony; but it is surely within the land between the Euphrates and the border of Egypt, by the agreement of the commentators, historians and scholars of reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 168}

'Which God has written for you' means which He has set down in the Preserved Tablet as dwellings and homes for you rather than for the giants in it. If asked how He said so when they did not enter it, as 'it is forbidden to them' shows, so how could it be set down as their dwelling yet forbidden to them, the answer is that it was written for the Children of Israel as a home and dwellings, and they did settle it afterward and it became theirs. Moses meant that God wrote it for the Children of Israel, not for those very people he commanded to enter. If someone said it was written for some of them and the wording is general with a particular sense, since Joshua and Caleb entered and were among those addressed, that too would be sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 169}

Report 11653, from Ibn Ishaq, says 'written' means gave; al-Suddi said it means commanded, and report 11654 from him gives the sense 'which God commanded you'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 169}

Then 'and do not turn back on your heels, for then you will return as losers': Moses tells his people, when he commands them at God's command to enter, to go forward at God's command and not retreat backward, but press on to enter against the people God commanded them to fight and attack in their land, for He has written it for them as a home and settlement; 'return as losers' means go back disappointed and ruined. He explained the meaning of loss elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 170}

If asked whether one who does not enter a land assigned to him deserves loss, the answer is that God commanded them to fight its disbelieving inhabitants and made entering it an obligation, so they deserved loss for abandoning what was made obligatory in two ways: wasting the duty of fighting, and opposing God's command by refusing to enter and telling their prophet that they would not enter until the people left. Report 11655, from Qatada, says they were commanded it as they were commanded prayer, almsgiving, pilgrimage and the lesser pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 171}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
