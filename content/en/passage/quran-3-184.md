## Summary
Verse 184 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 184 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Consoling the Prophet (p. 450).** Al-Tabari says this is a consolation from God to His Prophet Muhammad for the harm he met from the Jews and the polytheists among the other communities. God says to him: do not be grieved by the belying of those who ascribed need to Him and claimed He had covenanted with them not to believe a messenger until he brought them an offering, and by their fabrication against their Lord, deceived by His giving them respite; let their belying you and their claims of false covenants not weigh heavily on you, for if they belie you, their forebears belied God's messengers before you, who came with proofs that cut off excuse, evidence that dazzled the intellect and signs that staggered creation, which is what the word for clear proofs means. The note refers to the explanation of this word in earlier places in volumes 2 to 5 and the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 450}

**The scriptures, the Book and the illuminating (pp. 450-451).** Al-Tabari says the word for scriptures is the plural of zabur, which is a book, every book being a zabur, and he cites the opening of a poem of Imru' al-Qays in which a ruin is likened to the trace of faded writing. The note, from the Diwan (p. 186), gives al-Shantamari's explanation: the poet says he looked at this ruin and it grieved him, and compares what remains of it to writing in its faintness and thinness; the people of Yemen wrote their covenants and deeds on palm stalks, and a variant puts the phrase in the construct. Al-Tabari says "the Book" means the Torah and the Gospel: the Jews belied 'Isa and what he brought, distorted what Musa brought of the description of Muhammad and altered His covenant with them about it, and the Christians denied what the Gospel says of his description and changed what they were commanded about him. The illuminating is that which gives light and makes the truth clear to one who finds it confused and explains it, from light and brightening; one says "this matter has been made bright for you", meaning it shone and became clear. The second note says the manuscript and printed text have "and the illuminating thing", but the language explanation calls for what the editor printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 451}

**Reports 8312-8313 (p. 451).** Al-Dahhak (8312), through al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Abu Zuhayr from Juwaybir: it consoles His Prophet. Ibn Jurayj (8313), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: it consoles His Prophet. Al-Tabari then notes that this word is written without the preposition in the copies of the Qur'an of the Hijaz and Iraq and with it in those of Syria, as in [[passage/quran-35-25|35:25]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 451}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 451}
