**Commentary on verse 43 (pp. 116-117)**

By "and the king said: I see seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones" he means: the king of Egypt said, I see in sleep seven fat cows eaten by seven lean cattle. He said "I see" without saying in his sleep because of the Arab usage that when one of them says "I see that I do such and such" it is an account of seeing it in sleep even if sleep is not named. "And seven green ears" means I see seven green ears in my sleep, "and others" means seven other ears, "dry"; "O chiefs" means O nobles among my men and companions, "give me a ruling on my vision", interpret it, "if you can interpret dreams". The editor notes that "lean" is not explained here but later in the following verses, that "'abara" is the plural of an interpreter of dreams, and refers to the earlier commentary on "ear of grain" and "the chiefs". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 116}

Report 19330, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: God showed the king in his sleep a dream that alarmed him, seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones and seven green ears and others dry; he gathered the sorcerers, soothsayers, diviners and trackers and told them, and they said it was confused dreams and that they did not know the interpretation of dreams. Report 19331, from Ibn Ishaq: then King al-Rayyan ibn al-Walid saw his dream and was alarmed, knew it was a dream that would come true, and did not know its interpretation, and said to the chiefs around him of his realm what he said. The editor explains the diviners as soothsayers who reckon things by conjecture and look to the stars, the trackers as those who follow traces and know a man's likeness to his father and brother, and notes that al-Tabari gave it in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 117}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
