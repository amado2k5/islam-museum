**Commentary on verse 137: turning from belief to unbelief (p. 314)**

Al-Tabari gives the views on those who believed, then disbelieved, then believed, then disbelieved, then increased in disbelief. One view is that these are the Christians, who believed in Moses then disbelieved in him, then believed in Jesus and disbelieved in him, then increased in disbelief toward Muhammad; God will not forgive them nor guide them to the way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 314}

Report 10697, from Qatada through Sa'id and Bishr ibn Mu'adh, says those who believed, then disbelieved, then believed, then disbelieved, then increased in disbelief, are the Jews and the Christians: the Jews believed in the Torah and then disbelieved, the Christians believed in the Gospel and then disbelieved, and then increased in disbelief toward the Criterion and Muhammad. Report 10698, from Qatada through Ma'mar, is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 315}

Others say they are the hypocrites, who believed, then apostatised, then believed, then apostatised, and then increased in disbelief by dying in unbelief. Report 10699, from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj, says they used to be thought hypocrites, and anyone like them is included; the increase is persisting in disbelief until death. Reports 10700 and 10701, from Mujahid, say until they died. Report 10702, from Ibn Zayd, says they are hypocrites who believed twice and disbelieved twice, and then increased in disbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 316}

Others say they are the People of the Two Books, who sinned in their disbelief and repented, but whose repentance was not accepted while they persisted in unbelief. Report 10703, from Abu al-Aliya through Dawud ibn Abi Hind, says they are the Jews and Christians who sinned in their shirk and repented, and their repentance was not accepted; had they repented of shirk, it would have been accepted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 316}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that they are the People of the Book who acknowledged the judgement of the Torah and then denied it by opposing it, then some of them acknowledged Jesus and the Gospel and then denied them, and then denied Muhammad and the Criterion, increasing in disbelief by their denial. He argues that the verse before concerns the two Books and nothing shows that 'those who believed, then disbelieved' is cut off from it, so joining it to what precedes is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 317}

The sense of 'God would not forgive them' is that He would not cover their disbelief and sins by pardoning them the punishment, but would expose them before all witnesses; 'nor guide them to a way' means He would not direct them to the right road and grant them success, but would abandon them to it as punishment for their great crime and their boldness towards their Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 317}

Some, drawing on this verse, hold that an apostate is given three chances to repent; others disagree. Al-Tabari gives reports for three: from Ali through al-Sha'bi and Amir, that an apostate is asked to repent three times; from Ibn Umar through Abd al-Karim and another man, the same; and others say he is asked each time he apostatises, which is reported from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i through Amr ibn Qays. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 317}

Al-Tabari holds that the case for asking the apostate to repent the first time is established, so the ruling for each time he apostatises is the ruling of the first: his repentance is accepted and his Islam protects his blood. The reason that protected his blood the first time is his Islam, so it is not lawful for the reason to exist and his blood to be made licit while it exists, unless a text separates the first time from the others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 318}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
