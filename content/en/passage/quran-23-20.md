## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And a tree that grows from Mount Sinai, growing with oil and dye for the eaters": We also made for you a tree that grows from Mount Sinai, and a 'tree' is in the accusative conjunction with "gardens," meaning the olive tree. And His saying: "It emerges from Mount Sinai" means: it grows from a mountain where trees grow. I have explained the meaning of Mount Sinai in the past with its evidences and the differences among those who differ, which makes it unnecessary to repeat it here. As for the phrase "Sinai," the reciters differed in its reading; the majority of the reciters of Medina and Basra read it as "Sinai" with a kasra on the 'sin'. The majority of Kufa's reciters read it as "Sinai" with a fatha on the 'sin,' and they are all unanimous on its length. The correct view is that they are two well-known readings in the recitation of the cities with the same meaning, so whichever one reads the Qur'an, he is correct. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Zayd, and Ata.
