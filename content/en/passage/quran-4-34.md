## Summary
Verse 34 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 34 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of standing over (p. 290).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that men are the people who stand over their wives, in disciplining them and taking them by the hand in what is due from them to God and to their husbands, and that God's favouring them means He preferred them over their wives by the dowers they bring to them, the money they spend on them and the cost of their needs they cover; for this they became guardians over them, with authority in the matters God placed in their hands, and the interpreters said the same. The editor's note says the print and manuscript open the sentence with a different form and the context requires the one he printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 290}

**Reports 9300-9303 (pp. 290-291).** Al-Muthanna (9300, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that men are commanders over women and she should obey him in what God ordered of obedience to him, which is that she be good to his family and guard his property, and his favour over her is by his spending and effort. Al-Muthanna (9301, from Ishaq, from Abu Zuhayr, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) reports that the man stands over the woman and bids her obey God; if she refuses, he may strike her with a blow that does not injure, and the favour is his by spending and effort. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9302, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says they take hold of their hands and discipline them; the editor notes that the print and manuscript give a different verb form, a copyist's slip, and that the manuscript margin carries the letter ta as a mark of error, or the source it was copied from had it so, itself an error. Al-Muthanna (9303, from Habban b. Musa, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says the sense is God's preferring men over women. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 291}

**Reports on the occasion (p. 291, reports 9304-9306).** It was mentioned that the verse came down about a man who struck his wife on the face and was brought to the Prophet, who ruled retaliation for her. Ibn Bashshar (9304, from Abd al-A'la, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from al-Hasan) reports that a man struck his wife, she came to the Prophet, and he wished to let her retaliate; then God sent down the verse, and the Prophet called the man, recited it to him and said: I wanted one thing and God wanted another. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9305, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says it was mentioned to them that a man struck his wife and she came to the Prophet, then he gives something similar (the verb is garbled in the scan; the sense is striking). Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9306, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says a man struck his wife, she came to the Prophet, he wished to allow her retaliation, and the verse came down about it. These reports are given as transmitted and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 291}

**Reports 9307-9310 (p. 292).** Ibn Waki (9307, from his father, from Jarir b. Hazim, from al-Hasan) reports that a man of the Ansar struck his wife and she came seeking retaliation; the Prophet set retaliation between them; then a verse of sura 20, at [[passage/quran-20-114|20:114]], came down and then the verse of this place. Al-Qasim (9308, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports that a man struck his wife and the Prophet wished retaliation, and while they were so the verse came down. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9309, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) reports that an Ansari had words with his wife and struck her; her family went and told the Prophet, who told them what had come down. Al-Zuhri used to say there is no retaliation between a man and his wife short of life; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9310, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri) reports that if a man split his wife's head or wounded her there is no retaliation on him but he owes the blood-price, unless he attacks and kills her, in which case he is killed for her. The editor's notes say sura Ta Ha is Meccan by agreement, so al-Hasan's saying that it came down about the Ansari woman at Medina is open to question; that the words for retaliation and blood-price are glossed; and he adds that it suffices a Muslim to keep from the sound reports what al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated on a man beating his wife and lying with her at the end of the day, and what Ibn Maja narrated that the best are the best to their women (this is the editor's remark). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 292}

**What they spend (pp. 292-293, reports 9311-9313).** Al-Tabari says the spending means the dower they send to them and what they spend on them. Al-Muthanna (9311, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that his favour over her is by his spending and effort; likewise (9312, from Ishaq, from Abu Zuhayr, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak); and (9313, from Habban b. Musa, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) that it is the dower they sent. Al-Tabari then says the sense of the sentence is: men stand over their wives by God's preferring them over women and by their spending on them from their wealth, the particle in both places being of the nature of a verbal noun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 293}

**The righteous and the devout (pp. 293-294, reports 9314-9322).** Al-Tabari says the righteous women are those upright in religion who do good; al-Muthanna (9314, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says they do good, and the editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word. The devout he takes as obedient to God and to their husbands. Muhammad b. Amr (9315, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the obedient; al-Muthanna gives the same (9316 and 9317, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid), and Ali b. Dawud (9318, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas); al-Hasan b. Mu'adh as printed (9319, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says obedient to God and their husbands; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9320, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada), Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9321, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) and al-Muthanna (9322, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan: obedient to their husbands). Al-Tabari says he explained earlier that the root word means obedience. The editor notes that he added report 9317 from the manuscript; the print left it out as a repetition, but he kept it lest the copyist's eye had skipped and the chain differed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 294}

**What guarding the unseen means (pp. 295-296, reports 9323-9328).** Al-Tabari says it means they guard themselves when their husbands are away, in their chastity and their husbands' property, and in what is due from them to God. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9323, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says they guard what God entrusted of His right and guard their husbands' absence; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9324, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says the woman keeps her husband's property and her chastity until he returns, as God commanded; al-Qasim (9325, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) and Zakariyya b. Yahya (9326, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) report that Ibn Jurayj asked Ata and he said they are keepers for the husband; al-Muthanna (9327, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says keepers for their husbands in what of their affairs is absent. Al-Muthanna (9328, from Abu Salih, from Abu Ma'shar, from Sa'id b. Abi Sa'id al-Maqburi, from Abu Hurayra) reports that the Prophet said: the best of women is she who pleases you when you look at her, obeys you when you command her, and keeps herself and your property when you are away; then he recited the verse. The editor's note says the print and manuscript read 'Sa'id from Abu Sa'id al-Maqburi', an obvious slip; Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi gave it in his Musnad (306) from Abu Ma'shar, from Sa'id b. Abi Sa'id al-Maqburi; Ibn Kathir said Ibn Abi Hatim narrated it from Yunus b. Habib, from al-Tayalisi, from Ibn Abi Dhi'b, from al-Maqburi, and if so the editor thinks Ibn Abi Hatim erred, since the Musnad has it from Abu Ma'shar, perhaps because the reports before it there come by Ibn Abi Dhi'b; al-Suyuti, in al-Durr al-Manthur, ascribes it to Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi; the editor found in al-Hakim's Mustadrak a similar report by Ibn Ajlan, from Sa'id b. Abi Sa'id, from Abu Hurayra, abridged and in other words, which al-Hakim called sound on Muslim's condition though the two did not give it, and he did not find it in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan. Al-Tabari says this report supports his view of the meaning: righteous in their religion, obedient to their husbands, keeping for them themselves and their property. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 296}

**The reading of the last clause of this part (pp. 296-297, reports 9329-9334).** Al-Tabari says the readers differed. The general run of readers in all the Muslim regions read the divine name in the nominative, meaning: by God's keeping of them, since He made them so. Zakariyya b. Yahya (9329, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ata that it means God kept them; al-Muthanna (9330, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says by God's keeping them since He made them so. Abu Jafar Yazid b. al-Qa'qa' of Medina read the name in the accusative, meaning that they kept God in obeying Him and rendering His right in the guarding of their husbands' absence, as one man says to another that he has not kept God in such a matter, meaning he neither watched nor feared Him. Al-Tabari says the sound reading is what the Muslim readers brought in a way that cuts off excuse and fixes the proof, not what Abu Jafar alone held and so stood apart; that is the nominative, because it is sound in Arabic and the accusative is ugly there, outside the known speech of the Arabs, who do not drop the doer with verbal nouns since the verb would then have no known subject. He adds that something is left unsaid, the apparent sense standing for it: so treat them well and put things right, as is also reported in the reading of Ibn Mas'ud. Al-Muthanna (9331, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Rahman b. Abi Hammad, from Isa the blind, from Talha b. Musarrif) reports that Abd Allah's reading has an added command to put things right toward them; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9332, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says treat them well; Ali b. Dawud (9333 and 9334, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says set things right toward them, and in the last, if they are so, set things right toward them. The editor's notes: the manuscript and print differ in the phrase on watching and fearing and he printed the manuscript's reading with the added negative; the note on Abu Jafar Yazid b. al-Qa'qa' al-Makhzumi says he was a freedman of Abd Allah b. Ayyash b. Abi Rabi'a, one of the ten readers, a famous Follower of high rank, brought to Umm Salama as a small boy, who stroked his head and prayed a blessing for him; he prayed behind Ibn Umar, was imam of Medina in recitation, called 'the reader', and Ibn Ma'in said he was trustworthy with few hadith. On p. 298 he notes that the manuscript and print leave out the word for admonishing at the head of the next section although al-Tabari explains it afterwards. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 297}

**What the word for fear means in the third clause (pp. 298-299).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the sense of fear here. Some said it means knowing of their rebellion; the turning of fear to knowledge is like turning thinking to knowledge, their meanings being close, thinking being doubt and fear being joined with hope, both acts of the heart; he cites a line of Abu Mihjan al-Thaqafi, asking not to be buried in the open desert, and a line of Abu al-Ghul al-Tuhawi addressed to Sallam (described, not given). The editor says the first line was treated earlier with its sources in vol. 3, adds al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, and that the print had a corrupt word in the second while the manuscript is unpointed. A group said fear here is the opposite of hope: if you see from them what makes you fear that they will rebel, such as looking at what is not fitting, going in and out, and you suspect their affair, then counsel and leave them; Muhammad b. Ka'b said so (the editor refers to report 9342). Al-Tabari says 'rebellion' is their rising above their husbands and rising from their beds in disobedience and opposition in what obedience is due, out of hatred and aversion; the root is elevation, whence a raised place is called by the same root. Admonishing them is reminding them of God and warning them of His threat for doing what He forbade, disobeying the husband in what obedience to him is due; the interpreters said the same. Then he begins listing those who said rebellion is hatred and disobedience of the husband with report 9335 (Muhammad b. al-Husayn, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from ...), and the chain is cut off at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 299}

**More on the meaning of rebellion (p. 300, reports 9335-9338).** Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9335, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says it is their hatred; Yunus (9336, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says it is the woman whose disobedience is feared, and rebellion is disobedience to him and opposition; al-Muthanna (9337, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says she is the woman who rebels, belittles her husband's right and does not obey his order; al-Muthanna (9338, from Ishaq, from Rawh, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ata that rebellion is that she wishes to leave him, and the man likewise. The editor's notes say the print and manuscript are very corrupt in 9337 and the right reading is from al-Durr al-Manthur and al-Bayhaqi's al-Sunan al-Kubra, that al-Bayhaqi gave the report at greater length by Uthman b. Sa'id from Abd Allah b. Salih, and that al-Tabari will give part of it at 9339 and then 9356. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 300}

**The meaning of exhorting (pp. 300-301, reports 9339-9346).** Al-Muthanna (9339, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that it means exhorting with God's book, and that God commanded the husband, when she rebels, to exhort her, remind her of God and magnify His right over her; al-Muthanna (9340, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) reports that when a wife rebels against her husband's bed he says to her fear God and return to your bed, and if she obeys he has no way against her; al-Muthanna (9341, from Amr b. Awn, from Hushaym, from Yunus, from al-Hasan) that he exhorts with his tongue, bidding her fear God and obey; Ibn Waki (9342, from his father, from Musa b. Ubayda, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi) that if the man sees lightness in her gaze and her going in and out, he says to her with his tongue that he has seen such and such from her and she should stop; if she gives satisfaction, he has no way against her, and if she refuses he abandons her bed; al-Muthanna (9343, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Shibl) gives Mujahid's saying; Ibn Waki (9344, from his father, from Isra'il, from Jabir) from Ata that it is with speech; al-Qasim (9345, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) that it is with tongues; Ibn Humayd (9346, from Hakkam, from Amr b. Abi Qays, from Ata, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) that it is with the tongue. The editor's notes on 9342: the print altered the wording, the right reading is the manuscript's, which agrees with what al-Tabari ascribed to Muhammad b. Ka'b on p. 299; lightness of gaze means her eyes reaching for other men; the end of the report comes at 9364; and the word for giving satisfaction is explained as leaving what you blamed her for and returning to what pleases you. On 9346 he says Amr b. Abi Qays al-Razi was treated at 8611. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 301}

**Forsaking, first view: leaving intercourse (pp. 302-303, reports 9347-9352).** Al-Tabari sets out the interpreters' dispute on forsaking in the bed. Some said: exhort them in their rebellion, and if they refuse to return to the right and what is due from them, leave intercourse with them in your lying together. Al-Muthanna (9347, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that they exhort them, and if they obey, well, else they forsake them; Muhammad b. Sa'd (9348, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) that forsaking is that the man and his wife are on one bed and he does not have intercourse with her; Ibn Humayd (9349, from Jarir, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib) from Sa'id b. Jubayr that it is forsaking intercourse; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9350, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) that the husband must exhort her and if she does not accept, forsake her in the bed: he lies down beside her, turns his back, and has intercourse with her without speaking to her, and al-Tabari adds 'so it is in my book'; al-Muthanna (9351, from Amr b. Awn, from Hushaym, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) that he lies with her but forsakes her speech and turns his back; al-Muthanna (9352, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sharik, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) that he does not have intercourse with her. The editor's note on 9350 says the words 'so it is in my book' are al-Tabari's own, an extreme care and trustworthiness, though the sense may be spoiled in several ways; he kept it as he found it, lest the meaning be that the forsaking is of speech alone and not of intercourse, otherwise he would have joined it to the next view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 303}

**Second view: leaving speech (pp. 303-304, reports 9353-9357).** Others said it means forsake their speech while they leave lying with you, until they return to it. Abu Kurayb and Abu al-Sa'ib (9353, from Ibn Idris, from al-Hasan b. Ubayd Allah, from Abu al-Duha, from Ibn Abbas) report that she is not left alone in speech, the forsaking being in the matter of the bed (as printed; the report seems to run against the heading of the view); Ibn Humayd (9354, from Yahya b. Wadih, from Abu Hamza, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) that it is until they come to your beds; Ibn Humayd (9355, from Hakkam, from Amr, from Ata, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) that it is in intercourse; al-Muthanna (9356, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) that he exhorts her, and if she accepts, well, else he forsakes her in the bed and does not speak to her, without leaving her marriage, which is hard on her; al-Muthanna (9357, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sharik, from Khusayf, from Ikrima) that it is speech and conversation. The editor's notes: the print reads a doubled verb where the manuscript's reading is right; report 9354 is repeated in the manuscript in the same words a second time; and the note on the end of 9356 (printed with the number 9357) says it completes report 9337. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 304}

**Third view: leaving the bedding (pp. 304-305, reports 9358-9366).** Others said, as the editor supplied in brackets from the sense of the following reports because the heading is missing from the manuscript and print (it is the third of four views): do not approach them in their bedding until they return to what you love. Al-Hasan b. Zurayq al-Tuhawi (9358, from Abu Bakr b. Ayyash, from Mansur, from Mujahid) reports do not lie with them; Ibn Humayd (9359, from Jarir, from Mughira, from al-Sha'bi) that forsaking is that he does not lie with her; the same (9360) from Amir and Ibrahim; Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9361, from Hushaym, from Mughira) from Ibrahim and al-Sha'bi that he forsakes lying with her until she returns to what he loves; Muhammad b. al-Muthanna (9362, from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Mughira) from the same two that he forsakes her in the bed; al-Muthanna (9363, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sharik, from Khusayf) from Miqsam that his forsaking of her in her bed is that he not come near her bedding; Ibn Waki (9364, from his father, from Musa b. Mu'adh (9366, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada that he begins by exhorting her and if she refuses forsakes her, meaning her bedding. The editor's notes: al-Hasan b. **Fourth view: harshness in speech (pp. 305-306, reports 9367-9371).** Others said: say to them harsh words in their leaving lying with you. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9367, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from a man, from Abu Salih, from Ibn Abbas) reports that he forsakes her with his tongue and speaks harshly to her and does not leave intercourse; the same (9368, from al-Thawri, from Khusayf) from Ikrima, that forsaking is by speech, to be harsh, not by intercourse; Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9369, from Hushaym, from Mughira) from Abu al-Duha, that he forsakes with speech and does not forsake lying with her until she returns to what he wants; al-Muthanna (9370, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Abd al-Warith b. Sa'id, from a man) from al-Hasan, that he forsakes her only in the sleeping place, and has no right to forsake in speech or anything but the bed (this report stands under this view in the print but runs against it); al-Muthanna (9371, from Ishaq, from Ya'la) from Sufyan, that it is in her intercourse but he says to her words with some harshness, and when she does so he should not burden her to love him, for her heart is not in her hands. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 306}

**Al-Tabari's argument on the meaning of forsaking (pp. 306-307).** Al-Tabari says the word has no meaning in Arabic except one of three. First, a man's abandoning another's speech and conversation, that is refusing and leaving it; one says that so-and-so forsook his family. The second is much repetitive speech like a mocker's, said of one who raves and drawls; he cites a line of Dhu al-Rumma about a hunter who missed and kept calling woe on himself (described, not given). The third is the camel tied by its owner with the rope on its flanks and fetlock; he cites a line of Imru al-Qays on a camel that nearly broke her rope (described, not given). The word for harshness and hurt is a different form, said of one who speaks obscene talk. He then argues that since the word has only these three senses, and the woman whose rebellion is feared is ordered to be exhorted by her husband so that she returns to obedience in what is due to him, namely meeting him when he calls her to his bed, it cannot be that his exhorting is until the wife returns to God's command and her husband's obedience in that, and then the husband is ordered ... and the sentence breaks off at the foot of the page, finishing on p. 308. The editor's notes: Dhu al-Rumma's line is from his poem on wild asses and a hunter of the Jalal tribe, and he records this sense of the word, rarely found in dictionaries, there; Imru al-Qays's line is one of two he said in his need, and the editor explains the words for fatigue, vigour, shying, chasm, plain and raised ground, and that 'cut' means to break her rope; he also notes the vowel difference between the word for obscene talk and the three-sense word; and the footnote on the unfinished sentence continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 307}

**End of al-Tabari's argument on forsaking (pp. 308-309, with the editor's notes).** Al-Tabari finishes the broken sentence: the husband would be ordered to forsake her in the very matter about which he had exhorted her. He says that if so, the view that forsaking means leaving intercourse is void; and likewise void for him is the sense of forsaking their speech because they forsake the bed, since it has no intelligible ground, for God told, through His Prophet, that it is not lawful for a Muslim to forsake his brother beyond three days, and because a wife who has turned away from her husband in rebellion is pleased that he not speak to her or see her, so how is the man in her hatred of him ordered to leave what pleases her, intercourse and talk, while he is ordered to strike her to bring her back from leaving obedience when he calls her to his bed and in other matters in which obedience is due. A third possibility, that the sense is to repeat their words to them with harshness, he rejects because the verb would then be intransitive and cannot govern the pronoun of the women, as one says that so-and-so raved in his speech and does not say that so-and-so forsook so-and-so. Since all these senses have the flaw described, the soundest for him is that the word is directed to binding with the camel's rope, so the sense is: exhort them in their rebellion; if they take the exhortation, you have no recourse against them; and if they refuse to return from it, make them fast by a bond in their bedchambers, meaning their dwellings and houses where they lie and where they lie with their husbands. This is al-Tabari's choice, on which the editor comments. The editor's notes: the copyist could not read a word in the manuscript and the right form is as printed; the conjunction in the print is wrong; the report of Anas in al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari 10:413) says it is not lawful for a Muslim to forsake his brother beyond three days, and the report of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari adds that the better of the two is the one who begins with the greeting. In note 4 he says this argument is very good if one limits oneself to one form of rebellion and one cause, the one al-Tabari mentioned, but rebellion has many forms and various causes, and this verse is a general discipline a Muslim applies when needed on fearing rebellion or knowing it and its causes; he thinks al-Tabari dropped all the views to reach his own interpretation, and promises to give Abu Bakr b. al-Arabi's reply in a note at the end of al-Tabari's discussion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 309}

**Reports 9372-9374 (pp. 309-311).** Abbas b. Abi Talib (9372, from Yahya b. Abi Bukayr, from Shibl, from Abu Qaza'a, from Amr b. Dinar, from Hakim b. Mu'awiya, from his father) reports that he came to the Prophet and asked what a wife's right over one of them is, and he said: he feeds her, clothes her, does not strike the face, does not revile, and does not forsake except in the house. Al-Hasan b. Arafa (9373, from Yazid b. Harun, from Shu'ba b. al-Hajjaj, from Abu Qaza'a, from Hakim, from his father) gives something similar. Al-Muthanna (9374, from Habban b. Musa, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Bahz b. Hakim, from his grandfather) reports that he said: Messenger of God, our women, what do we come to of them and what leave? He said: your tilth, so come to your tilth as you wish, except do not strike the face, do not revile, do not forsake except in the house, feed when you eat and clothe when you are clothed; how, when you have been intimate with each other? except for what is due on her. The editor's notes on 9372: Abbas b. Abi Talib is Abbas b. Ja'far b. Abd Allah (treated at 880); Shibl is Ibn Abbad al-Makki the reader; Abu Qaza'a is Suwayd b. Hujayr b. Bayan; he thinks 'narrates from Amr b. Dinar' is a copyist's addition of the word 'from' and that Abu Qaza'a was narrating to Amr b. Dinar, confirmed by Ahmad's Musnad (4:446-447), where the Riyadh manuscript has it right; Hakim b. Mu'awiya b. Haydah al-Qushayri was treated at 873 with his father. On 9373: al-Hasan b. Arafa al-Abdi of Baghdad, al-Tabari's teacher, is trustworthy and died in 257 over 110 years old; Ahmad narrated it from Yazid b. Harun, from Shu'ba. On 9374: Habban b. Musa b. Sawwar al-Sulami is trustworthy and a teacher of al-Bukhari and Muslim; the report repeats the two before in longer form and there are longer sound chains; Hakim's two sons Bahz and Sa'id narrated it; Ahmad, Abu Dawud (2142-2144), Ibn Maja (1850) and al-Bayhaqi gave it; he quotes al-Mundhiri that the authorities differed on relying on the copy 'Bahz b. Hakim from his father from his grandfather', some relying on it and some refusing, and that al-Tirmidhi took something from it and graded it sound; the editor says the truth is that it is sound if the chain to Bahz is sound; Ibn Kathir gave it abridged without chain; and 'except for what is due on her' (in the Musnad version, on them) means what is lawful to husbands against them of the striking God permitted on fearing rebellion, which God spelled out in this verse, without passing the limit God allowed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 311}

**Reports 9375-9377 (pp. 311-312).** Al-Tabari says a number of the interpreters spoke as he did. Al-Muthanna (9375, from Amr b. Awn, from Hushaym) reports from al-Hasan that when a wife rebels against her husband he exhorts her with his tongue; if she accepts, well, else he strikes her a non-severe blow; if she returns, well, else it is lawful for him to take from her (a release for payment) and let her go. Ibn Humayd (9376, from Jarir, from al-Hasan b. Ubayd Allah, from Abu al-Duha) reports from Ibn Abbas that he does that to her and strikes her until she obeys him in the bed, and when she obeys him in it he has no recourse against her if she lies with him. Al-Muthanna (9377, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Yahya b. Bishr) heard Ikrima say, on the verse, a non-severe blow, and say that the Prophet said: strike them, if they disobey you in what is right, a non-severe blow. The editor says the report Ikrima narrated, and which al-Tabari argues from afterwards, is a mursal one, and that al-Suyuti gave it in al-Durr al-Manthur ascribing it to Ibn Jarir only. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 312}

**What al-Tabari draws from them (p. 312).** Al-Tabari says all those whose sayings he gave did not make forsaking mean anything but striking, and did not require forsaking, as it is one of the postures of the woman who is beaten, given the report Ikrima narrated from the Prophet that he ordered striking them if they disobeyed their husbands in what is right without ordering the husbands to forsake them. He adds that if someone thought that the Prophet's not ordering the man to forsake his wife when she disobeys him in what is right, and ordering striking before forsaking, proved his own view of the word, then God's command to the husband to exhort her when she rebels would have no meaning, since exhortation is not mentioned in Ikrima's report. He answers that the matter is the opposite: the Prophet's words 'if they disobey you in what is right' clearly show that he did not allow a man to strike his wife except after exhorting her about the rebellion, since she is not disobedient to him unless there has gone before a command or exhortation in what is right, as God commanded. The editor's notes explain the sentence structure, and say that the beaten woman is beaten only because she forsook her husband's bed, so forsaking is one of her states. His note 5 begins at the foot of the page, saying al-Tabari's reading of forsaking as the binding with the rope and making them fast in their dwellings is a very unusual interpretation, standing apart from all that the early authorities held; it continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 312}

**The editor's reply to al-Tabari's choice (p. 313, note 5).** The editor says later scholars corrected al-Tabari, and among the best who said so was Abu Bakr b. al-Arabi in Ahkam al-Qur'an, who called it a slip from a scholar of the Qur'an and the sunna, and said that what emboldened him, though al-Tabari did not wish to say he took it from it, was a strange report Ibn Wahb narrated from Malik, about Asma daughter of Abu Bakr, wife of al-Zubayr b. al-Awwam: the story of al-Zubayr beating Asma and her co-wife, knotting the hair of one to the other, Asma's appeal to Abu Bakr, and his advice to be patient, since al-Zubayr is a righteous man who may be her husband in paradise. Ibn al-Arabi said that al-Tabari saw binding and knotting, with the word's possibility and al-Zubayr's act, and so ventured the interpretation, and he wondered how, with his learning, the right view was far from him. The editor says Abu Bakr's drawing out of al-Tabari's thought does not impress him, and had al-Tabari wanted it he would have mentioned it as usual; he thinks rather that Abu Jafar was led into this interpretation by the reasons given after his discussion of the word, and that had the wording not had the exhortation before it he would not have needed it; what led him was the succession of the two statements, and he found no way to join the senses of rebellion and forsaking, as the editor said at p. 308, note 4. These are the editor's views; the reading of forsaking as binding is al-Tabari's choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 313}

**The command to strike (pp. 313-315, reports 9378-9394).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: exhort them, men, in their rebellion; if they refuse to return to what is due to you, make them fast in their dwellings and strike them, so that they return to what is due from them, obedience to God in what is due of your rights; and the interpreters said the kind of striking God allowed the husband of a rebellious wife is the non-severe blow. Ibn Humayd (9378, from Hakkam, from Amr, from Ata) reports it from Sa'id b. Jubayr, and the same (9379, from Yahya b. Wadih, from Abu Hamza, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib) from Sa'id; at 9380 (from Jarir, from Mughira) from al-Sha'bi; al-Muthanna (9381, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sharik, from Ata b. al-Sa'ib, from Sa'id) from Ibn Abbas. Al-Muthanna (9382, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that he forsakes her in the bed, and if she comes round, well, else God has allowed him to strike her a non-severe blow and not break a bone, and if she comes round, well, else the ransom from her is lawful for him. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9383, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) reports a non-severe blow from al-Hasan and Qatada, and (9384, from Ibn Jurayj) that he asked Ata and he said a non-severe blow; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9385, from Yazid b. Zurayʿ, from Sa'id) from Qatada that he forsakes her in the bed, and if she refuses he strikes her a non-severe blow, meaning one that does not disfigure. Al-Muthanna (9386, from Ishaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ata that he asked Ibn Abbas what a non-severe blow is, and he said the toothstick and the like; Ibrahim b. Sa'id al-Jawhari (9387, from Ibn Uyayna) the same; al-Muthanna (9388, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Ibn Uyayna) from Ata that the Prophet said in his sermon a non-severe blow, and he said, the toothstick and the like (the note says the sermon is that of the Farewell Pilgrimage, treated at 8905). Al-Qasim (9389, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) reports that the Prophet said: do not forsake women except in the beds, and strike them a non-severe blow, meaning one that leaves no mark; Ibn Waki (9390, from his father, from Isra'il, from Jabir) from Ata: a non-severe blow; al-Muthanna (9391, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Yahya b. Bishr) from Ikrima likewise; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9392, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi that if she comes round in the forsaking, well, else he strikes her a non-severe blow; Ibn Waki (9393, from his father, from Musa b. Ubayda) from Muhammad b. Ka'b that he forsakes her bed as long as he thinks she will desist (the note: give up her rebellion), and if she does not, strikes her a non-severe blow; al-Muthanna (9394, from Amr b. Awn, from Hushaym, from Yunus) from al-Hasan: a non-severe blow. These statements and reports are given as transmitted and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 315}

**Report 9395 (p. 316).** Al-Muthanna (9395, from Hibban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Abd al-Warith b. Sa'id, from an unnamed man, from al-Hasan) reports that the striking meant is a non-severe blow, that is, one that leaves no effect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 316}

**The closing clause about obedience (pp. 316-317, reports 9396-9402, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: if your wives, whose rebellion you fear when you exhort them, obey you, do not forsake them in the beds; if they do not obey, forsake and strike them; and once they return to obedience and to what is due from them, do not look for any way to harm or compel them, nor use pretexts to reach what is not lawful for you of their persons and wealth, as when one of you tells an obedient wife that she does not love him and hates him, and so strikes or hurts her. God tells the men not to wrong them over their dislike of them and not to burden them with loving them, for that is not in their hands. He glosses the verb of seeking as looking for and asking, as one says of looking for a strayed animal, and cites a line of Suhaym, the slave of the Banu al-Hashas, about death (described, not reproduced); the editor notes that the verb and the line were treated earlier (the cross-reference numbers are unreadable in the scan), and that al-Tabari glosses the line as 'your seeking and what you seek'. The reports: al-Muthanna (9396, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas): if she obeys you, do not wrong her with pretexts; Ibn Humayd (9397, from Jarir, from al-Hasan b. Ubayd Allah, from Abu al-Duha, from Ibn Abbas): if she obeys him he has no way against her once she lies with him; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9398, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Jurayj): the pretexts; Abd al-Razzaq (9399) from al-Thawri: if she comes to the bed though she dislikes him; al-Muthanna (9400, from Ishaq, from Ya'la, from Sufyan): then he does not burden her to love him, for her heart is not in her hand; al-Muthanna (9401, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid): if she obeys and lies with him this clause applies; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9402, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): if she obeys you, do not seek pretexts against her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 317}

**The close of the verse (p. 318, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says God is above all things, so the men should not look for any means against obedient wives in what He made due to them, on account of the superiority of their hands over the women's hands; God is above them and above everything, and greater, and they are in His hand and grasp, so let them fear Him lest they wrong wives who obey, for their Lord would then support them. In note 1 the editor says the printed edition and the manuscript are both confused here and that he corrected the passage from the sense of the context (judging the first publisher's repair mistaken); in note 2 he refers to the earlier treatment of the word for loftiness (volume 5, as printed). These statements are given as transmitted; the choices are al-Tabari's, the notes are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 318}
