## Summary
Verse 34 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 34 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of standing over (p. 290).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that men are the people who stand over their wives, in disciplining them and taking them by the hand in what is due from them to God and to their husbands, and that God's favouring them means He preferred them over their wives by the dowers they bring to them, the money they spend on them and the cost of their needs they cover; for this they became guardians over them, with authority in the matters God placed in their hands, and the interpreters said the same. The editor's note says the print and manuscript open the sentence with a different form and the context requires the one he printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 290}

**Reports 9300-9303 (pp. 290-291).** Al-Muthanna (9300, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that men are commanders over women and she should obey him in what God ordered of obedience to him, which is that she be good to his family and guard his property, and his favour over her is by his spending and effort. Al-Muthanna (9301, from Ishaq, from Abu Zuhayr, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) reports that the man stands over the woman and bids her obey God; if she refuses, he may strike her with a blow that does not injure, and the favour is his by spending and effort. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9302, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says they take hold of their hands and discipline them; the editor notes that the print and manuscript give a different verb form, a copyist's slip, and that the manuscript margin carries the letter ta as a mark of error, or the source it was copied from had it so, itself an error. Al-Muthanna (9303, from Habban b. Musa, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says the sense is God's preferring men over women. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 291}

**Reports on the occasion (p. 291, reports 9304-9306).** It was mentioned that the verse came down about a man who struck his wife on the face and was brought to the Prophet, who ruled retaliation for her. Ibn Bashshar (9304, from Abd al-A'la, from Sa'id, from Qatada, from al-Hasan) reports that a man struck his wife, she came to the Prophet, and he wished to let her retaliate; then God sent down the verse, and the Prophet called the man, recited it to him and said: I wanted one thing and God wanted another. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9305, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says it was mentioned to them that a man struck his wife and she came to the Prophet, then he gives something similar (the verb is garbled in the scan; the sense is striking). Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9306, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says a man struck his wife, she came to the Prophet, he wished to allow her retaliation, and the verse came down about it. These reports are given as transmitted and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 291}

**Reports 9307-9310 (p. 292).** Ibn Waki (9307, from his father, from Jarir b. Hazim, from al-Hasan) reports that a man of the Ansar struck his wife and she came seeking retaliation; the Prophet set retaliation between them; then a verse of sura 20, at [[passage/quran-20-114|20:114]], came down and then the verse of this place. Al-Qasim (9308, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports that a man struck his wife and the Prophet wished retaliation, and while they were so the verse came down. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9309, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) reports that an Ansari had words with his wife and struck her; her family went and told the Prophet, who told them what had come down. Al-Zuhri used to say there is no retaliation between a man and his wife short of life; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9310, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri) reports that if a man split his wife's head or wounded her there is no retaliation on him but he owes the blood-price, unless he attacks and kills her, in which case he is killed for her. The editor's notes say sura Ta Ha is Meccan by agreement, so al-Hasan's saying that it came down about the Ansari woman at Medina is open to question; that the words for retaliation and blood-price are glossed; and he adds that it suffices a Muslim to keep from the sound reports what al-Bukhari and Muslim narrated on a man beating his wife and lying with her at the end of the day, and what Ibn Maja narrated that the best are the best to their women (this is the editor's remark). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 292}

**What they spend (pp. 292-293, reports 9311-9313).** Al-Tabari says the spending means the dower they send to them and what they spend on them. Al-Muthanna (9311, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) reports that his favour over her is by his spending and effort; likewise (9312, from Ishaq, from Abu Zuhayr, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak); and (9313, from Habban b. Musa, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) that it is the dower they sent. Al-Tabari then says the sense of the sentence is: men stand over their wives by God's preferring them over women and by their spending on them from their wealth, the particle in both places being of the nature of a verbal noun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 293}

**The righteous and the devout (pp. 293-294, reports 9314-9322).** Al-Tabari says the righteous women are those upright in religion who do good; al-Muthanna (9314, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says they do good, and the editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word. The devout he takes as obedient to God and to their husbands. Muhammad b. Amr (9315, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the obedient; al-Muthanna gives the same (9316 and 9317, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid), and Ali b. Dawud (9318, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas); al-Hasan b. Mu'adh as printed (9319, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says obedient to God and their husbands; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9320, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada), Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9321, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) and al-Muthanna (9322, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan: obedient to their husbands). Al-Tabari says he explained earlier that the root word means obedience. The editor notes that he added report 9317 from the manuscript; the print left it out as a repetition, but he kept it lest the copyist's eye had skipped and the chain differed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 294}

**What guarding the unseen means (pp. 295-296, reports 9323-9328).** Al-Tabari says it means they guard themselves when their husbands are away, in their chastity and their husbands' property, and in what is due from them to God. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9323, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says they guard what God entrusted of His right and guard their husbands' absence; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9324, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says the woman keeps her husband's property and her chastity until he returns, as God commanded; al-Qasim (9325, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) and Zakariyya b. Yahya (9326, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) report that Ibn Jurayj asked Ata and he said they are keepers for the husband; al-Muthanna (9327, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says keepers for their husbands in what of their affairs is absent. Al-Muthanna (9328, from Abu Salih, from Abu Ma'shar, from Sa'id b. Abi Sa'id al-Maqburi, from Abu Hurayra) reports that the Prophet said: the best of women is she who pleases you when you look at her, obeys you when you command her, and keeps herself and your property when you are away; then he recited the verse. The editor's note says the print and manuscript read 'Sa'id from Abu Sa'id al-Maqburi', an obvious slip; Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi gave it in his Musnad (306) from Abu Ma'shar, from Sa'id b. Abi Sa'id al-Maqburi; Ibn Kathir said Ibn Abi Hatim narrated it from Yunus b. Habib, from al-Tayalisi, from Ibn Abi Dhi'b, from al-Maqburi, and if so the editor thinks Ibn Abi Hatim erred, since the Musnad has it from Abu Ma'shar, perhaps because the reports before it there come by Ibn Abi Dhi'b; al-Suyuti, in al-Durr al-Manthur, ascribes it to Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi; the editor found in al-Hakim's Mustadrak a similar report by Ibn Ajlan, from Sa'id b. Abi Sa'id, from Abu Hurayra, abridged and in other words, which al-Hakim called sound on Muslim's condition though the two did not give it, and he did not find it in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan. Al-Tabari says this report supports his view of the meaning: righteous in their religion, obedient to their husbands, keeping for them themselves and their property. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 296}

**The reading of the last clause of this part (pp. 296-297, reports 9329-9334).** Al-Tabari says the readers differed. The general run of readers in all the Muslim regions read the divine name in the nominative, meaning: by God's keeping of them, since He made them so. Zakariyya b. Yahya (9329, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ata that it means God kept them; al-Muthanna (9330, from Habban, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Sufyan) says by God's keeping them since He made them so. Abu Jafar Yazid b. al-Qa'qa' of Medina read the name in the accusative, meaning that they kept God in obeying Him and rendering His right in the guarding of their husbands' absence, as one man says to another that he has not kept God in such a matter, meaning he neither watched nor feared Him. Al-Tabari says the sound reading is what the Muslim readers brought in a way that cuts off excuse and fixes the proof, not what Abu Jafar alone held and so stood apart; that is the nominative, because it is sound in Arabic and the accusative is ugly there, outside the known speech of the Arabs, who do not drop the doer with verbal nouns since the verb would then have no known subject. He adds that something is left unsaid, the apparent sense standing for it: so treat them well and put things right, as is also reported in the reading of Ibn Mas'ud. Al-Muthanna (9331, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Rahman b. Abi Hammad, from Isa the blind, from Talha b. Musarrif) reports that Abd Allah's reading has an added command to put things right toward them; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9332, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says treat them well; Ali b. Dawud (9333 and 9334, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says set things right toward them, and in the last, if they are so, set things right toward them. The editor's notes: the manuscript and print differ in the phrase on watching and fearing and he printed the manuscript's reading with the added negative; the note on Abu Jafar Yazid b. al-Qa'qa' al-Makhzumi says he was a freedman of Abd Allah b. Ayyash b. Abi Rabi'a, one of the ten readers, a famous Follower of high rank, brought to Umm Salama as a small boy, who stroked his head and prayed a blessing for him; he prayed behind Ibn Umar, was imam of Medina in recitation, called 'the reader', and Ibn Ma'in said he was trustworthy with few hadith. On p. 298 he notes that the manuscript and print leave out the word for admonishing at the head of the next section although al-Tabari explains it afterwards. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 297}

**What the word for fear means in the third clause (pp. 298-299).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the sense of fear here. Some said it means knowing of their rebellion; the turning of fear to knowledge is like turning thinking to knowledge, their meanings being close, thinking being doubt and fear being joined with hope, both acts of the heart; he cites a line of Abu Mihjan al-Thaqafi, asking not to be buried in the open desert, and a line of Abu al-Ghul al-Tuhawi addressed to Sallam (described, not given). The editor says the first line was treated earlier with its sources in vol. 3, adds al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, and that the print had a corrupt word in the second while the manuscript is unpointed. A group said fear here is the opposite of hope: if you see from them what makes you fear that they will rebel, such as looking at what is not fitting, going in and out, and you suspect their affair, then counsel and leave them; Muhammad b. Ka'b said so (the editor refers to report 9342). Al-Tabari says 'rebellion' is their rising above their husbands and rising from their beds in disobedience and opposition in what obedience is due, out of hatred and aversion; the root is elevation, whence a raised place is called by the same root. Admonishing them is reminding them of God and warning them of His threat for doing what He forbade, disobeying the husband in what obedience to him is due; the interpreters said the same. Then he begins listing those who said rebellion is hatred and disobedience of the husband with report 9335 (Muhammad b. al-Husayn, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from ...), and the chain is cut off at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 299}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
