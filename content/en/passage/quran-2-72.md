## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when you killed a soul", al-Tabari says the sense is: remember, Children of Israel, your killing of the soul whose story he gave on the previous verse. "Fa-iddara'tum" is originally "tadara'tum", from "dar'", meaning you differed and disputed; he cites Abu al-Najm and Ru'ba, and a report from al-Sa'ib with the words "you neither contended nor disputed", meaning you did not oppose your partner or quarrel with him; the editor notes the chain is unsteady and two names in the text are garbled, so no argument stands on it. He explains the assimilation of the letters in the word, and that some said it means "you pushed it off", from "dara'tu the matter from myself". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 223} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 224}

Al-Tabari explains the assimilation of letters in "fa-iddara'tum", with an alif brought in to join the word. From Mujahid, Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Zayd he reports that it means you differed and disputed, one side saying "you killed him" and the other "no". Three accounts of the dispute are given: Mujahid says the cow's owner was killed by a man who left him at the door of other people, whose kin claimed the blood from them, and they denied it; Qatada says each tribe accused another until the matter was brought to Moses, and that the kinsman seeking blood-money was himself the killer, over an inheritance; Ibn Abbas says the nephews of a rich childless old man, urged by Satan, killed him and threw him at the gate of another city so its people would pay the blood-money. Mujahid, Muhammad ibn Ka'b and Muhammad ibn Qays tell of a tribe who built a walled town to avoid people's evils. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 224} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 225} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 226} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 227}
Al-Tabari concludes that their quarrel and dispute over the slain man is the "dar'" God mentions. On "God brings out what you were hiding", he says God makes public the killing they kept secret; "bringing out" here is making known, and "hiding" is concealing, as reported from Mujahid.{cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 228}
