## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when you killed a soul", al-Tabari says the sense is: remember, Children of Israel, your killing of the soul whose story he gave on the previous verse. "Fa-iddara'tum" is originally "tadara'tum", from "dar'", meaning you differed and disputed; he cites Abu al-Najm and Ru'ba, and a report from al-Sa'ib with the words "you neither contended nor disputed", meaning you did not oppose your partner or quarrel with him; the editor notes the chain is unsteady and two names in the text are garbled, so no argument stands on it. He explains the assimilation of the letters in the word, and that some said it means "you pushed it off", from "dara'tu the matter from myself". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 223} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 224}
