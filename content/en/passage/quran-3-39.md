## Summary
Verse 39 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 39 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The difference in reading the verb of the call.** Al-Tabari says the readers differed: most of the people of Medina and some of Kufa and Basra read it with the feminine ending, meaning the plural of angels, as the Arabs make verbs feminine for male groups when they precede, especially nouns that have a feminine form, as in 'the Talhas came'. A group of Kufans read it with the masculine ending, meaning that the caller was Gabriel; the editor notes this is the reading of Hamza and al-Kisai. They made it masculine for the sense, as others made a male's verb feminine for the form. In al-Tabari's view they took support from a reading reported from Abdullah ibn Masud: report 6945 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq ibn al-Hajjaj, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hammad) says Ibn Masud's reading has Gabriel as the caller. A group of commentators interpreted it so: report 6946 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says the caller was Gabriel, or that the angels said it and it was Gabriel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 363} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 364}

**The question of plural and singular.** Al-Tabari raises the objection: how can it be said, on this interpretation, that the angels called, when 'angels' is a plural and not one? He replies that it is allowed in Arabic to speak of one in the manner of a plural, as one says 'so-and-so went out on the post-mules' when he rode one mule, 'he rode the ships' when he rode one ship, or when asked 'from whom did you hear this report?' one answers 'from the people' though he heard it from one man. Some said that [[passage/quran-3-173|3:173]], where the speaker was, as reported, one man, and [[passage/quran-30-33|30:33]] are of this kind, which is allowed where no single person is aimed at. The editor refers to earlier passages. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 365}

**Al-Tabari's choice on reading and sense.** Al-Tabari says the sound view with him is that both readings, with the feminine and the masculine ending, are known and eloquent and do not differ in meaning, so whichever the reader recites he is right: if 'angels' means Gabriel, as reported from Abdullah, the feminine verb is eloquent for the word's form when the verb comes first, and the masculine is allowed for the sense; if it means the plural of angels, the feminine is allowed when the verb precedes. As for the sense of the verse, he holds that God reports that the angels called him, and the apparent sense is a group of angels and not one, Gabriel being one; the interpretation of the Qur'an may only be carried to the more apparent and more frequent usage of the Arabs, not the rarer, so long as a way exists, and no need forces turning it to the singular. A group of scholars said as he did, among them Qatada, al-Rabi ibn Anas, Ikrima and Mujahid, and al-Tabari says he has mentioned what they said earlier; the editor says nothing of the sort has passed in the report of Zechariah and Mary and fears the manuscripts are abridged here. This is al-Tabari's choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 365} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 366}

**The state of standing, the sanctuary and the reading of the next particle.** Al-Tabari says the clause on standing means the angels called him while he stood praying, so it reports the time of the call, and the verb of praying is in the accusative of state from the standing; the sanctuary he has already defined as the front of the mosque. He then says the readers differed on the particle after the call: most read it with fatha, the call acting upon it, meaning 'they called him with this'; some Kufans read it with kasra, meaning 'the angels said', since a call is speech, and they report that Abdullah's reading has a vocative to Zechariah before it, arguing that if the call cannot act on the vocative it cannot act on the particle either. Al-Tabari prefers the fatha and says the argument from Abdullah's reading is no proof for them: if Abdullah read so, the vocative to Zechariah was interposed between the particle and the verb, and when it is interposed the Arabs make the call act on the particle and also set it aside; in the reading he follows there is no interposed vocative, and in fluent Arabic when they put the call on the person called by an accusative they put it likewise on the particle that follows, though setting its action aside is allowed. The editor notes the differences between the manuscript and the printed text, that he added two words in brackets because the predicate had fallen from the copyist, defines the 'effective verb' as the transitive, and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 366} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 367}

**The widespread reading.** Al-Tabari closes his case on the particle after the call by saying that fatha is the widespread reading in the readings of the lands of Islam, and an isolated reading is not set against the community whose reading carries the weight of proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 368}

**The readings of the verb of glad tidings.** Al-Tabari says the readers differed on the verb. Most of the people of Medina and Basra read it with damma on the first letter and the doubled middle letter, in the sense of God giving Zechariah good news of a child, as people say 'the bearers of good news gave so-and-so glad tidings'. A group of Kufans and others read it with fatha and the light middle letter, meaning God will gladden him with a child He gives him; he cites a line of a poet, which al-Farra heard from some Arabs, in which the poet gladdened his family on seeing a letter from al-Hajjaj being read to them. It was also said to be the dialect of the people of Yamama, of Kinana and others of Quraysh, who say 'I gladdened so-and-so, so I am his gladdener' and 'are you glad of this?', and a line of Abd Qays ibn Khafaf al-Burjumi was recited for them, from his counsel to his son Jubayl: help the generous whose hands are dusty from the drought and giving, be glad of what they are glad of, and when they alight in hardship, alight with them. When they come to the command, the sound usage is without the extra letter, and they hardly say the doubled or the extended form of the command. It is also reported from Humayd ibn Qays that he read with damma, kasra on the middle letter and the light form. The editor notes that the word for 'bearers of good news' is written differently in both witnesses and the right form is the one he gave, that the first poet is unknown, refers to the sources of the second (the Asma'iyyat, the Mufaddaliyyat, Lisan al-Arab, al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an), explains its words and refers to earlier treatments of the two nouns for glad tidings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 368} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 369}

**Muadh al-Kufi's report and al-Tabari's choice.** Report 6947 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hammad, from Muadh al-Kufi) says that whoever reads it heavy takes it from glad tidings, and whoever reads it light with fatha takes it from gladness. Al-Tabari chooses damma and the doubled letter, in the sense of announcing, since it is the current language and the well-known usage among people, and all the readers of the lands agree on the doubled form of the verb at [[passage/quran-15-54|15:54]], so the rest of its like in the Qur'an should be the same. As for the distinction attributed to Muadh between the light and heavy forms, he did not find the people of knowledge of Arab speech know it from a sound route, so what is reported of him has no sense. He cites a line of Jarir to the governor Bishr that his face deserved cheer, meaning beauty, brightness and gladness, using the heavy noun and not the plain one, which shows the light and heavy forms mean the same. The editor gives the background of that line: Jarir's poem to Bishr ibn Marwan, when Suraqa al-Bariqi, whom Bishr used to set against poets, pushed Jarir to satirize him; Jarir left Bishr and even praised him and reproached him as one who feigns ignorance of what Bishr knew; and the editor says the printed text garbled the line through a slip of the publisher. Report 6948 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) says the angels gave him the news. This is al-Tabari's choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 369} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 370}

**The name John.** Al-Tabari says 'Yahya' is a name of the pattern 'yaf'al', from the saying 'so-and-so lived', and it is said that God named him so because He gave him life by faith. Report 6949 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says he was a servant God gave life by faith, and 6950 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from Qatada) says he was named so because God gave him life by faith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 370} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 371}

**Confirming the Word.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God gives Zechariah good news of John as a son who will confirm the Word from God, meaning Jesus son of Mary, and the word for 'confirming' is accusative as a state from John, because it is an indefinite attribute while John is definite; the editor refers to earlier notes on the term for the state. The commentators said much the same. Report 6951 (Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad al-Tufawi, from Muhammad ibn Rabi'a, from al-Nadr ibn Arabi, from Mujahid) says Zechariah's wife told Mary she found what was in her womb moving toward what was in Mary's; she bore John and Mary bore Jesus, so John confirms Jesus. Report 6952 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from al-Raqashi) says he confirms Jesus son of Mary, and the same come from Mujahid (6953, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl), from Qatada (6954, through Ibn Bashshar, Sulayman and Abu Hilal), 6955 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada: confirming Jesus and following his way and course; the editor says the printed text differs from the manuscript and he kept the manuscript), 6956 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar) and 6957 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far). Report 6958 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says he was the first man to confirm Jesus, who is the Word and a spirit from God, and 6959 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says he confirms Jesus. Report 6960, narrated to the compiler without naming the source (from al-Husayn, from Abu Muadh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman, from al-Dahhak), says John was the first to confirm Jesus and bear witness that he is God's Word, and that John was the son of Jesus' maternal aunt and older than he. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 371} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 372}

**Reports of Ibn Abbas and al-Hasan.** Report 6961 (Ibn Waki, from his father, from Isra'il, from Simak, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) says the Word is Jesus son of Mary, whose name is the Messiah. Report 6962 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abbas) says Jesus and John were sons of two aunts, that John's mother would tell Mary she found what was in her womb prostrating to what was in Mary's, which is his confirming of Jesus, and that he was the first to confirm him and older than Jesus. The editor explains that prostration here means submission, lowering and humility, not the prostration of prayer, which is a figurative use of this root, and refers to earlier pages. Report 6963 (Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) says the Word he confirmed is Jesus. Report 6964 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says John's mother met Jesus' mother, each pregnant; Zechariah's wife said she sensed herself with child, Mary said she did too, and Zechariah's wife said she found what was in her womb prostrating to what was in Mary's, which is that confirming. Report 6965 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says he confirms Jesus son of Mary. These reports, including the account of the unborn child's prostration, are given as transmitted, with no verdict from the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 372} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 373}

**Al-Tabari's reply to a Basran.** Al-Tabari says one of the Basran scholars of Arabic claimed that 'word from God' here means a book from God, from the Arab saying 'so-and-so recited me a word of such a poet', meaning a poem; he judges this ignorance of the sense of 'word' and boldness in translating the Qur'an by opinion. The editor says the scholar is Abu Ubayda Mamar ibn al-Muthanna in Majaz al-Qur'an, explains that 'translating' in their usage means explaining, and adds a personal comment wondering, if al-Tabari counts this boldness, what he would say of some in the editor's own time who set themselves up to assail the Book of God, beside whom Abu Ubayda's remark would count as glorifying and seeking forgiveness. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 373} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 374}

**The sense of 'master'.** Al-Tabari says the word means one noble in knowledge and worship, is accusative by conjunction with the word for 'confirming' before it, so the sense is that God gives Zechariah good news of John confirming this and being a master, and that 'sayyid' is of the pattern 'fay'il' from 'he led, he leads'. Report 6966 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says he was a master in worship, forbearance, knowledge and scrupulousness; 6967 (Ibn Bashshar, from Muslim, from Abu Hilal, from Qatada) says in knowledge and worship; 6968 (from Ammar, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from Qatada) says the forbearing. Report 6969 (Ibn Waki, from his father, from Sharik, from Salim al-Aftas, from Said ibn Jubayr) says the forbearing, and 6970 (al-Muthanna, from al-Himmani, from Sharik, from Salim, from Said ibn Jubayr) says the pious. Report 6971 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the one honoured with God, and 6972 the same from al-Raqashi (through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl). Report 6973 (al-Muthanna, from Amr ibn Awn, from Hushaym, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) says the forbearing and pious, and 6974 is similar from al-Dahhak; 6975 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, from Sufyan) says the forbearing and pious. Report 6976 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says the noble; 6977 (Said ibn Amr al-Sakuni, from Baqiyya ibn al-Walid, from Abd al-Malik, from Yahya ibn Said, from Said ibn al-Musayyib) says the jurist and scholar; 6978 (Muhammad ibn Sad through his family chain, from Ibn Abbas) says forbearing and pious; and 6979 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abu Bakr, from Ikrima) says the one anger does not overcome. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 374} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 375} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 376}

**The start of the word for one who abstains.** Al-Tabari turns to the next word of the verse, saying it means one who holds back from intercourse with women, from the saying 'I held back from such a thing', and the like 'so-and-so was hindered in his recitation' when he could not recite, and 'the siege of the enemy' when they confine the people and block their movement. For this reason one who contributes nothing to his drinking companions is called by the same word, and he cites a line of al-Akhtal about a drinking companion who was neither stingy nor quarrelsome in the cup (with a variant reading), and one who keeps his secret and does not disclose it is also so called. The editor says the line is from a poem al-Akhtal addressed to Yazid ibn Muawiya when he held him back after his satire on the Ansar, that the manuscript has a corrupt word, and he explains the terms for the one who pays up, the quarrelsome and the dregs, with references. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 376}

**The rest of the sense of abstaining.** Al-Tabari says one who holds back his secret from being revealed is also called by this word, citing a line of Jarir in which the slanderers sought his slips and found a man sparing of his beloved's secret, and the root of all these uses is one: prevention and confinement. The editor gives the variants of a verb in that line (the printed text differs from the manuscript), prefers the manuscript, cites a line of Suwayd ibn Abi Kahil, and notes that the copyist dropped the name of the woman addressed and left a blank. The commentators spoke as al-Tabari does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 377}

**The report of Abdullah ibn Masud and the report of Ibn al-As.** Report 6980 (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Khalaf, from Hammad ibn Shu'ayb, from Asim, from Zirr, from Abdullah) says it means one who does not approach women. Report 6981 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Yahya ibn Said, from Said ibn al-Musayyib) says Ibn al-As told him he heard the Messenger of God say that every son of Adam comes on the Day of Resurrection with a sin, except John son of Zechariah; then he reached his hand to the ground, took a small twig and said that John had of what men have only the like of this twig, and for this God called him master and one who abstains. Report 6982 (Yunus, from Anas ibn Iyad, from Yahya ibn Said, from Said ibn al-Musayyib) says that everyone meets God on the Day of Resurrection with a sin except John son of Zechariah, who abstained, having only the like of a fringe. Report 6983 (Ahmad ibn al-Walid al-Qurashi, from Umar ibn Jafar, from Shu'ba, from Yahya ibn Said, from Said ibn al-Musayyib) says Ibn al-As, either Abdullah or his father, said that no one meets God except with a sin save John son of Zechariah, and Said ibn al-Musayyib said that the abstainer does not go near women and had only the like of the fringe of a garment. The editor notes that al-Tabari gave this at 6981 from Said from Ibn al-As as a report traced to the Messenger of God, at length, and gives it here as stopping at the Companion, with doubt whether the Companion was Abdullah ibn Amr or his father; that Ibn Kathir quotes it from Ibn Abi Hatim with this doubt, as traced to the Messenger, and then from Ibn Abi Hatim from Abdullah ibn Amr ibn al-As as stopping at the Companion; that Ibn Kathir calls the traced version 'very strange' and says the stopped version is sounder in chain; and that al-Suyuti likewise gives both and calls the stopped version stronger in chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 378}

**Reports of Said and others.** Report 6984 (Said ibn Amr al-Sakuni, from Baqiyya ibn al-Walid, from Abd al-Malik, from Yahya ibn Said, from Said ibn al-Musayyib) says the abstainer is one who has no desire for women, and that Said struck his hand on the ground, took a date-stone and said he had only the like of this. Reports 6985-6987 (Said ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and Ata ibn al-Sa'ib; and through Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Hakkam and Amr) say he does not approach women. Report 6988 (Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad, from Muhammad ibn Rabi'a, from al-Nadr ibn Arabi, from Mujahid) says the same, as does 6989 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) and 6990 (al-Raqashi, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl). Reports 6991 and 6992 (al-Dahhak) say he is one who begets no child and has no seed, and 6993 (Qatada) says 'we were told' he does not go near women, as do 6994-6996 (Qatada, through Ibn Bashshar, Sulayman and Abu Hilal; Ammar ibn al-Hasan; and al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Mamar). Report 6997 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Qabus, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) says he is one who does not ejaculate, 6998 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) that he does not approach women, 6999 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) that he has no wish for women, and 7000 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) that he does not go near women. These are reports given as transmitted; the hadith and its grading belong to the editor, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 378} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 380}

**The sense of 'prophet' and 'of the righteous', and the division of the manuscript.** Al-Tabari says 'prophet' here means a messenger of his Lord to his people who informs them from Him of His command and prohibition, the lawful and the unlawful, and conveys what he was sent with; he has already explained the meaning and origin of prophethood with its evidence, so there is no need to repeat it, and 'of the righteous' means among His righteous prophets. The editor refers to the earlier treatment of 'righteous' and of the relative pronoun. He notes that at this place a part of the old division of the work from which the manuscript was copied ended, and that the copyist wrote here the opening invocation, the name Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari, praise to God alone for His kindness and a blessing on Muhammad, his family and companions, and then the heading of what follows, the interpretation of the next verse. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 380}
