## Summary
Verse 71 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 71 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 71 (first half).** Al-Tabari says the address is to the people of the Torah and the Gospel, and the meaning is: why do you confound what is right with what does not belong to it? He explains the confounding as their showing with their tongues belief in Muhammad and in what he brought from God, while in their hearts they held something else, their Judaism or Christianity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 504}

**Report 7223 (Ibn Abbas).** Al-Tabari gives (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, from Ikrima or Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) that Abdallah ibn al-Sayf, Adi ibn Zayd and al-Harith ibn Awf said to one another: come, let us profess belief in what was sent down to Muhammad and his companions at the start of the day and reject it at its end, so as to unsettle their religion; perhaps they will do as we do and turn back from it. The verses of this passage were sent down about them, up to the point the report marks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 504} The editor says the report is in the Sira of Ibn Hisham (2: 202) and follows the earlier report 7202. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 504}

**Reports 7224-7226.** In report 7224 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) the meaning is: why do you mingle Judaism and Christianity with Islam when you know that the religion of God which He accepts and rewards through alone is Islam. Report 7225 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same, except that he says Islam is what is accepted from no one besides, and does not add the clause about reward. In report 7226 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) the meaning is the mingling of Islam with Judaism and Christianity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 504}

**The editor on report 7225.** He says the printed text had a different wording which the publisher read so because of the copyist's faulty hand; that the sound reading is the one he established; and that the manuscript has a truncated phrase which he calls a bad corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 504}

**Another view (report 7227).** Others said, as in the report of Ibn Zayd (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb), that the right is the Torah God sent down to Moses and the false is what they wrote with their own hands. Al-Tabari says he explained the sense of the confounding earlier, which spares repetition, and the editor refers to volume 1 (pp. 567-568). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 505}

**The second half of verse 71: hiding the matter of Muhammad.** Al-Tabari says: and why do you hide, People of the Book, what is right? What they hid is what their books contain of the description of Muhammad, his mission and his prophethood. The editor points to the commentary on the parallel verse and the one before it in volume 1 (pp. 566-572) and notes that the reports given here were given there in their places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 505}

**Reports 7228-7230.** In report 7228 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) they hid the matter of Muhammad though they find it established in their books, where he orders them to do good and forbids them wrong. Report 7229 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) has the same sense. In report 7230 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) what was hidden is Islam and the matter of Muhammad, and their knowledge is the knowledge that Muhammad is the messenger of God and the religion is Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 505}

**Al-Tabari's close on the verse.** He says their knowing means they know that what they hide is right and from God. He takes this as God's report of the deliberate disbelief of the People of the Book and of their hiding what they knew of Muhammad's prophethood, found in their books and brought to them by their prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 506}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
