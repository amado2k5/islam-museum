## Summary
Verse 11 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/assassination-attempt-of-amir-and-arbad|Assassination Attempt of Amir and Arbad]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 11 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 11 (pp. 369-375)**

On "each has attendants before him and behind him, guarding him by God's command; God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves; and when God wills evil for a people there is no repelling it, and they have no protector besides Him", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its meaning. Some said it means that God, mighty be His praise, has attendants, the "ha" in "lahu" referring to the name of God, and the "mu'aqqibat" being those who succeed one another over the servant: when the night angels ascend by day, the day angels follow them, and when the day is over the day angels ascend and the night angels follow. They said it was "mu'aqqibat" because "angels" is the plural of "malak", a masculine, not feminine; the singular of "al-mala'ika" is "mu'aqqib", its plural "mu'aqqiba", and then the plural of that plural, that is of "mu'aqqib" after it was pluralised as "mu'aqqiba", and "mu'aqqibat" was said as one says "sadat Sa'd" and "rijalat of such-and-such", a plural of "rijal". "Before him and behind him" means before this man who hides by night and goes forth by day, and behind his back. Report 20210, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Mansur ibn Zadhan: "each has attendants before him and behind him", he said, the angels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 369}

Report 20211, from Kinana al-Adawi, through al-Muthanna, Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Salam al-Qushayri, Ali ibn Jarir, Hammad ibn Salama and Abd al-Hamid ibn Ja'far: Uthman ibn Affan went in to the Messenger of God and asked him how many angels are with a servant. He said: an angel on your right over your good deeds, who is trusted over the one on the left; when you do a good deed it is written ten times, and when you do an evil deed the one on the left says to the one on the right, write, and he says no, perhaps he will seek God's forgiveness and repent; when he has said it three times he says yes, write, God give us rest from him, what a bad companion, how little his watchfulness of God and how little his shame before us; God says "he utters no word but has a watcher ready by him" [[passage/quran-50-18|50:18]]; and two angels before you and behind you, of whom God says "each has attendants before him and behind him, guarding him by God's command"; an angel holding your forelock, who raises you if you humble yourself before God and breaks you if you are arrogant against God; two angels on your lips, who record against you only the prayer for the Prophet; an angel standing at your mouth who does not let a snake enter it; and two angels on your eyes. These are ten angels over every human being, the night angels descending on the day angels, since the night angels are other than the day angels, so twenty angels over each human, and Iblis by day and his offspring by night. In a note the editor says this chain is problematic and rejected, that Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Salam and Ali ibn Jarir are not found in the books on narrators, that Kinana al-Adawi is a reliable Follower not recorded as having met Uthman or narrated from him, that the report has an objectionable element and severe weakness and al-Tabari alone transmits it from al-Muthanna, and that Ibn Kathir called it a very strange report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 370}

Report 20212, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "each has attendants before him and behind him", the angels, "guarding him by God's command". Report 20213, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: the same. Report 20214, from Mujahid, through Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, Abd al-Malik and Qays: with every person are guardians who guard him by God's command. Report 20215, from Ibn Abbas, through Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the attendants are of God's command, and they are the angels. Reports 20216 and 20217, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', his father, Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima, and al-Harith and Abd al-Aziz: angels who guard him before him and behind him, and when his appointed measure comes they leave him. Report 20218, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mansur: the guardians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 371}

Report 20219, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Waki', his father, Sufyan and Mansur: angels. Report 20220, from Abu Salih, through Ahmad ibn Jazim, Ya'la and Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: "attendants" are the night angels succeeding the day angels. Report 20221, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: these are the night angels who succeed one another among you by night and day, and it was mentioned to us that they gather at the afternoon prayer and the dawn prayer; in the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b it is "attendants before him and an observer behind him, guarding him by God's command". Report 20222, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: angels who succeed one another over him. Report 20223, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the angels; Ibn Jurayj said the angels succeed one another by night and day, and it reached us that the Prophet said they gather among you at the afternoon and dawn prayers; of "guarding him" he said it is like [[passage/quran-50-17|50:17]], good deeds before him and evil deeds behind him, the one on his right writing the good and the one on his left the evil. Report 20224, from Mujahid, through Suwar ibn Abd Allah, al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman and Layth: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 372}

there is no servant without an angel assigned to him who guards him in his sleep and waking from jinn, humans and vermin, and none of them comes at him wanting him without his saying "behind you", except what God permits to strike him. Report 20225, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: he means the angels. Others said that "al-mu'aqqibat" in this place are the guards who succeed one another over the prince. Report 20226, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan, Habib ibn Abi Thabit and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: that is a king of the kings of this world, who has guards beneath whom are guards. Report 20227, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: he means the ruler's ally, over whom are guards; the editor notes the printed text has "the ruler's ally", which is correct, the meaning being the princes who guard themselves. Report 20228, from Ikrima, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Sharqi: these are the princes. Report 20229, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Umar ibn Nafi': {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 373}

the processions before him and behind him; the editor notes that Umar ibn Nafi' al-Thaqafi was called "nothing" by Ibn Ma'in, listed among the reliable by Ibn Hibban and among the weak by al-Saji and Ibn al-Jarud. Report 20230, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it is the sultan guarding himself against God, and they are the people of polytheism. Al-Tabari says: the more correct of the two interpretations is that of those who said the "ha" in "lahu mu'aqqibat" refers to "man" in "and he who hides by night", and that the "attendants before him and behind him" are his guards and officers, as those whose words we cited said. His reason is that "he has attendants" is nearer to "he who hides by night" than to "Knower of the unseen", so by its nearness it is likelier to refer to him, together with the indication in "when God wills evil for a people there is no repelling it" that they are the ones meant. For He, mighty be His praise, mentioned a people of disobedience and suspicion who hide by night and show themselves by day and consider themselves protected by guards and a force that keeps the people of obedience from stopping what they do in disobedience to God; then He told that when God wills evil for them their guard will not help them nor their protection ward it off. The editor explains "jalawiza" as the plural of "jilwaz", the policeman who hurries before the prince and takes his orders. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 374}

On "guarding him by God's command", the interpreters differed as they did over "he has attendants": whoever said the attendants are angels said that those who guard him are also angels, and whoever said they are the guards and officers from the children of Adam said that those who guard him by God's command are those guards. They also differed over the meaning of "min amr Allah": some said their guarding him is from His command, others said "they guard him by God's command". Among those who said that those who guard him are the angels and took "from God's command" to mean that their guarding him is from God's command: report 20231, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: he says, by God's permission, so the attendants are of God's command and they are the angels. Report 20232, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib: the guardian angels, and their guarding him is from God's command. Report 20233, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ubayd and Abd al-Malik: are they guards from God's command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 375}

We stop here at the foot of p. 375, in the middle of the commentary on verse 11 (after report 20233, on the sense of "min amr Allah"); the next batch begins with report 20234, which starts at the foot of p. 375 and continues from p. 376. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 375}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
