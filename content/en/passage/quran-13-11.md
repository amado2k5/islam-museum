## Summary
Verse 11 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/assassination-attempt-of-amir-and-arbad|Assassination Attempt of Amir and Arbad]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 11 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 11 (pp. 369-385)**

On "each has attendants before him and behind him, guarding him by God's command; God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves; and when God wills evil for a people there is no repelling it, and they have no protector besides Him", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its meaning. Some said it means that God, mighty be His praise, has attendants, the "ha" in "lahu" referring to the name of God, and the "mu'aqqibat" being those who succeed one another over the servant: when the night angels ascend by day, the day angels follow them, and when the day is over the day angels ascend and the night angels follow. They said it was "mu'aqqibat" because "angels" is the plural of "malak", a masculine, not feminine; the singular of "al-mala'ika" is "mu'aqqib", its plural "mu'aqqiba", and then the plural of that plural, that is of "mu'aqqib" after it was pluralised as "mu'aqqiba", and "mu'aqqibat" was said as one says "sadat Sa'd" and "rijalat of such-and-such", a plural of "rijal". "Before him and behind him" means before this man who hides by night and goes forth by day, and behind his back. Report 20210, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Mansur ibn Zadhan: "each has attendants before him and behind him", he said, the angels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 369}

Report 20211, from Kinana al-Adawi, through al-Muthanna, Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Salam al-Qushayri, Ali ibn Jarir, Hammad ibn Salama and Abd al-Hamid ibn Ja'far: Uthman ibn Affan went in to the Messenger of God and asked him how many angels are with a servant. He said: an angel on your right over your good deeds, who is trusted over the one on the left; when you do a good deed it is written ten times, and when you do an evil deed the one on the left says to the one on the right, write, and he says no, perhaps he will seek God's forgiveness and repent; when he has said it three times he says yes, write, God give us rest from him, what a bad companion, how little his watchfulness of God and how little his shame before us; God says "he utters no word but has a watcher ready by him" [[passage/quran-50-18|50:18]]; and two angels before you and behind you, of whom God says "each has attendants before him and behind him, guarding him by God's command"; an angel holding your forelock, who raises you if you humble yourself before God and breaks you if you are arrogant against God; two angels on your lips, who record against you only the prayer for the Prophet; an angel standing at your mouth who does not let a snake enter it; and two angels on your eyes. These are ten angels over every human being, the night angels descending on the day angels, since the night angels are other than the day angels, so twenty angels over each human, and Iblis by day and his offspring by night. In a note the editor says this chain is problematic and rejected, that Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Salam and Ali ibn Jarir are not found in the books on narrators, that Kinana al-Adawi is a reliable Follower not recorded as having met Uthman or narrated from him, that the report has an objectionable element and severe weakness and al-Tabari alone transmits it from al-Muthanna, and that Ibn Kathir called it a very strange report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 370}

Report 20212, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "each has attendants before him and behind him", the angels, "guarding him by God's command". Report 20213, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: the same. Report 20214, from Mujahid, through Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, Abd al-Malik and Qays: with every person are guardians who guard him by God's command. Report 20215, from Ibn Abbas, through Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the attendants are of God's command, and they are the angels. Reports 20216 and 20217, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', his father, Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima, and al-Harith and Abd al-Aziz: angels who guard him before him and behind him, and when his appointed measure comes they leave him. Report 20218, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mansur: the guardians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 371}

Report 20219, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Waki', his father, Sufyan and Mansur: angels. Report 20220, from Abu Salih, through Ahmad ibn Jazim, Ya'la and Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: "attendants" are the night angels succeeding the day angels. Report 20221, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: these are the night angels who succeed one another among you by night and day, and it was mentioned to us that they gather at the afternoon prayer and the dawn prayer; in the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b it is "attendants before him and an observer behind him, guarding him by God's command". Report 20222, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: angels who succeed one another over him. Report 20223, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the angels; Ibn Jurayj said the angels succeed one another by night and day, and it reached us that the Prophet said they gather among you at the afternoon and dawn prayers; of "guarding him" he said it is like [[passage/quran-50-17|50:17]], good deeds before him and evil deeds behind him, the one on his right writing the good and the one on his left the evil. Report 20224, from Mujahid, through Suwar ibn Abd Allah, al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman and Layth: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 372}

there is no servant without an angel assigned to him who guards him in his sleep and waking from jinn, humans and vermin, and none of them comes at him wanting him without his saying "behind you", except what God permits to strike him. Report 20225, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: he means the angels. Others said that "al-mu'aqqibat" in this place are the guards who succeed one another over the prince. Report 20226, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan, Habib ibn Abi Thabit and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: that is a king of the kings of this world, who has guards beneath whom are guards. Report 20227, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: he means the ruler's ally, over whom are guards; the editor notes the printed text has "the ruler's ally", which is correct, the meaning being the princes who guard themselves. Report 20228, from Ikrima, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Sharqi: these are the princes. Report 20229, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Umar ibn Nafi': {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 373}

the processions before him and behind him; the editor notes that Umar ibn Nafi' al-Thaqafi was called "nothing" by Ibn Ma'in, listed among the reliable by Ibn Hibban and among the weak by al-Saji and Ibn al-Jarud. Report 20230, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it is the sultan guarding himself against God, and they are the people of polytheism. Al-Tabari says: the more correct of the two interpretations is that of those who said the "ha" in "lahu mu'aqqibat" refers to "man" in "and he who hides by night", and that the "attendants before him and behind him" are his guards and officers, as those whose words we cited said. His reason is that "he has attendants" is nearer to "he who hides by night" than to "Knower of the unseen", so by its nearness it is likelier to refer to him, together with the indication in "when God wills evil for a people there is no repelling it" that they are the ones meant. For He, mighty be His praise, mentioned a people of disobedience and suspicion who hide by night and show themselves by day and consider themselves protected by guards and a force that keeps the people of obedience from stopping what they do in disobedience to God; then He told that when God wills evil for them their guard will not help them nor their protection ward it off. The editor explains "jalawiza" as the plural of "jilwaz", the policeman who hurries before the prince and takes his orders. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 374}

On "guarding him by God's command", the interpreters differed as they did over "he has attendants": whoever said the attendants are angels said that those who guard him are also angels, and whoever said they are the guards and officers from the children of Adam said that those who guard him by God's command are those guards. They also differed over the meaning of "min amr Allah": some said their guarding him is from His command, others said "they guard him by God's command". Among those who said that those who guard him are the angels and took "from God's command" to mean that their guarding him is from God's command: report 20231, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: he says, by God's permission, so the attendants are of God's command and they are the angels. Report 20232, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib: the guardian angels, and their guarding him is from God's command. Report 20233, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ubayd and Abd al-Malik: are they guards from God's command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 375}

Report 20234 (which begins at the foot of p. 375), from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Madini, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and Amr ibn Dinar: "attendants before him", observers, "and behind him", by God's command, guarding him; the editor identifies the narrators as well-known reliable men. Report 20235, from Ibn Abbas, through Abd al-Wahhab, Sa'id, Qatada and al-Jarud: "before him" an observer, "and behind him". Report 20236, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Isra'il and Khusayf: the angels, by God's command. Report 20237, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the angels, by God's command. Report 20238, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mansur: the guardians. Among those who said the meaning is "they guard him by God's command": report 20239, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: that is, by God's command. Report 20240, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: and in some readings it is "bi-amr Allah". Report 20241, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym, Abd al-Malik and Qays: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 376}

with every person are guardians who guard him by God's command. Among those who said that the guards from the children of Adam are the ones who guard him: report 20242, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: he means the ruler's ally, who has guards guarding him before and behind; God, mighty and exalted, says, they guard him from My command, for when I will evil for a people there is no repelling it, and they have no protector besides Him. Report 20243, from Ikrima, through Abu Hurayra al-Dab'i, Abu Qutayba, Shu'ba and Sharqi: the officers. The editor identifies Abu Hurayra al-Dab'i as Muhammad ibn Firas al-Sayrafi, al-Tabari's reliable and truthful teacher, and corrects the name Shu'ba in the chain. Others said: the meaning is that they guard him from God's command, "God's command" being the jinn and whoever wishes him harm and ill before God's decree comes, and when His decree comes they leave him to it. Report 20244, from Ibrahim, through Abu Hurayra al-Dab'i, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, Waraqa, Mansur and Talha: from the jinn. Report 20245, from Mujahid, through Suwar ibn Abd Allah, al-Mu'tamir and Layth: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 377}

there is no servant without an angel assigned to him who guards him in his sleep and waking from jinn, humans and vermin, and none of them comes to him wanting him without his saying "behind you", except what God permits to strike him. Report 20246, from Ka'b al-Ahbar, through al-Hasan ibn Arafa, Isma'il ibn Ayyash, Muhammad ibn Ziyad al-Alhani and Yazid ibn Shurayh: if every plain and rough ground were uncovered to the son of Adam he would see devils on all of it, and were it not that God has set angels over you to drive them off from your food, your drink and your private parts, you would be snatched away. Report 20247, from Abu Mijlaz, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Umara ibn Abi Hafsa: a man of Murad came to Ali while he was praying and said, take care, some men of Murad want to kill you; he said, with every man are two angels who guard him from what is not decreed, and when the decree comes they leave him to it, and the appointed term is a strong shield. Report 20248, from Abu Umama, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Wahhab, al-Hasan ibn Dhakwan and Abu Ghalib: there is no human without an assigned angel who drives things from him until he hands him over to what was decreed for him. Others said: the meaning is that they keep a record against him from God. Report 20249, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: they keep a record against him from God. Al-Tabari says Ibn Jurayj means by "they keep a record against him" the angels assigned to the son of Adam to record his good and evil deeds, and these are the "attendants" in our view, who record the son of Adam's good and evil deeds by God's command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 378}

Al-Tabari says: on this view the meaning of "min amr Allah" must be that the recording angels are by God's command or record by God's command, and the pronoun in "yahfazunahu" must be singular and masculine though it stands for the good and evil deeds, because it refers to "man", the one who hides by night and goes forth by day, whose mention is put in place of the report about his evil and good deeds, as in "ask the town" [[passage/quran-12-82|12:82]]. Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd said something different from all these views. Report 20250, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: Amir ibn al-Tufayl and Arbad ibn Rabi'a came to the Messenger of God, and Amir said, what will you give me if I follow you? He said, you are a horseman, I will give you the reins of the horses. He said, no. He said, then what do you want? He said, the east for me and the west for you. He said, no. He said, then the tent-dwellers for me and the house-dwellers for you. He said, no. He said, then I will fill it against you with horses and men. He said, God will prevent you, and the two sons of Qayla, meaning the Aws and the Khazraj. They left, and Amir said to Arbad, this man could be dealt with; if we killed him two goats would not butt over it, they would accept that we pay blood money for him, and they love peace and hate war when they see a thing has happened. The other said, if you wish. They conferred, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 379}

and he said, go back and I will keep him busy arguing while you stand behind him and strike him once with the sword. So they did, one behind the Prophet and the other saying, tell us your story. He said, what do you mean? He said, your Qur'an. He kept arguing with him and looking to see why his companion was slow, until afterwards he said, what is the matter with you, you are exhausted? He said, I put my hand on the hilt of my sword and it stiffened, and I could neither loosen it nor draw it nor move it. They left, and when they were at the lava field Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Usayd ibn Hudayr heard of it and went out to them, each in his armour with his spear in his hand and his sword slung, and they addressed Amir ibn al-Tufayl harshly and said, were it not that you are under the Messenger of God's protection you would not leave this place before we struck off your head. The harder of the two on him was Usayd ibn Hudayr, who said, who is this? They said, Usayd ibn Hudayr, and he said, if his father were alive he would not have done this to him. Then he said to Arbad: the editor notes that Arbad is the son of Qays, Lubayd's half-brother on his mother's side, that Ibn Zayd said ibn Rabi'a because of his brotherhood with Lubayd ibn Rabi'a, that the text is disordered and perhaps has a lacuna, that Qayla is the mother of the Aws and Khazraj, and that he printed words in the manuscript he could not decipher as they stand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 380}

you, Arbad, go out toward Adana and I will go out to Najd, and we will gather men and meet against him. Arbad went out until, when he was at al-Raqam, God sent a summer cloud with a thunderbolt in it and it burned him. Amir went out until, when he was in a valley called al-Jarir, God sent the plague upon him, and he began to cry, O family of Amir, a gland like a young camel's gland is killing me, and dying too in the house of a woman of Salul, she being a woman of Qays. That is the saying of God "equal among you, one who conceals speech and one who proclaims it"; he recited until he reached "they guard him", those being the attendants by God's command, this being an inversion of order: the Messenger of God has attendants guarding him before him and behind him, and those are the attendants by God's command. And of these two He said "God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves"; he recited until he reached "He sends the thunderbolts and strikes with them whom He wills", the verse, and recited until he reached "the prayer of the disbelievers is only in error". Lubayd, weeping for his brother Arbad, said that he feared every kind of death for him but did not fear the rain-stars and summer rain. The editor notes the places Adana and al-Raqam and explains the rain-stars in the lines as the heavy summer rains, adding that Lubayd feared for his brother every death he knew except this sudden death from summer thunderbolts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 381}

The report concludes with Lubayd saying that thunder and thunderbolts bereaved him of the horseman on the day of fierce fighting, and the editor explains "najd" as the brave man of great might who answers quickly to whatever he is called to, good or evil. Al-Tabari says: this view of Ibn Zayd's on the interpretation of this verse is far from the verse's interpretation, besides going against the views of the interpreters we have cited, for he made the "ha" in "lahu mu'aqqibat" refer to the Messenger of God, who has not been mentioned in the verse before it or the one before that, unless he meant to link it to "you are only a warner, and for every people a guide", which would be far-fetched given the verses between that carry no report about the Messenger. That being so, its returning to "man" in "and he who hides by night" is nearer, since it comes before it and the report after it concerns him. So the meaning of the speech is: equal among you, people, in your Lord's sight, are the one who conceals speech and the one who proclaims it, and the one who hides his depravity and doubtful dealings in the darkness of the night and goes to and fro in the light of day, protected by his troops and guards who succeed one another, from among the people of obedience to God, so that they cannot stand between him and what he does or apply God's limit to him; that is His saying "guarding him by God's command". On "God does not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves" he says: God does not change the well-being and favour a people have, removing it from them and destroying them, until they change what is in themselves by wronging one another and transgressing against one another, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 382}

whereupon His punishment and change come upon them. On "when God wills evil for a people there is no repelling it" he says: when God wills for these who hide by night and go forth by day, who have troops and protection before and behind guarding them from God's command, perdition and disgrace in the present world, "there is no repelling it", none but God can turn it from them. "And they have no protector besides Him" means that these people, the "hum" in "lahum" referring to the people in "for a people evil", have no protector besides God who takes charge of them, their affair and their punishment. One of the scholars of Arab speech said that "su'" is perdition, and that every leprosy, vitiligo, blindness and great affliction is "su'" with a damma on the first letter, while with a fatha it is the verbal noun of "su'tu", as in "rajul saw'"; the editor refers to Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an. The grammarians differed over "and he who hides by night and goes forth by day". One of the Basran grammarians said the meaning of "he who hides by night" is he who is apparent by night, from "khafaytu al-shay'" when I made it apparent, and cited a verse of Imru' al-Qays saying that if they hide the sickness they will not expose it, and if they stir up war they will not sit out; he said it has also been read "akadu ukhfiha" [[passage/quran-20-15|20:15]] meaning I almost make it apparent. Of "goes forth by day" he said the "sarib" is the one who is hidden, as if he took it to mean that he has become hidden in the burrow by day, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 383}

hidden. Another group of Basran and Kufan grammarians said the meaning of "he who hides" is concealed by night, from "istikhfa'", and "sarib by day" is one going by day, from "sarabat al-ibil ila al-ra'y", which is the camels' going out to the pastures; it is said "surub" is in the evening and "suruh" in the morning. They also differed over the feminine "mu'aqqibat", an attribute of non-females. One of the Basran grammarians said it was made feminine for the abundance of such among them, like "nassaba" and "allama", and then made masculine because the sense is masculine, so "yahfazunahu". One of the Kufan grammarians said it is "mala'ika mu'aqqiba", then pluralised "mu'aqqibat", a plural of a plural, and then "yahfazunahu" because it is for the angels. Al-Tabari says his view on the meaning of "he who hides by night and goes forth by day" has already been given. As for what we mentioned from the Basran grammarians, it is a saying which, though it has a ground in Arab speech, goes against the interpreters, and its going outside all their sayings is proof enough of its error. As for "al-mu'aqqibat", "ta'qib" in Arab speech is returning after beginning and going back to a thing after leaving it, from God's saying "he turned his back and did not look back" [[passage/quran-27-10|27:10]], that is, did not return, and as Salama ibn Jandal said in a verse describing how their horses charged again on the tracks of their enemies after their hooves were worn by the first raid and the return, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 384}

meaning that they returned in a second raid; and as Tarafa said in a verse reproaching his people, who then returned, that is, came back with goodwill after his reproach. Al-Tabari says: the feminine came to it, in our view, because it is an attribute of the guards who guard the one who hides by night and goes forth by day, since by it He meant "guards, mu'aqqiba", then "al-mu'aqqiba" was pluralised as "mu'aqqibat", which is a plural of a plural of "mu'aqqib", "mu'aqqib" being the singular of "mu'aqqiba", as Labid said in a verse describing a wild ass hurrying to water like a wronged man who comes back to press his claim again and again. "Al-mu'aqqibat" is its plural; then He said "yahfazunahu", returning the report to the masculine of the guards and troops. As for "yahfazunahu min amr Allah", the Arabic scholars differed over its meaning. One of the Kufan grammarians said: it means "he has attendants from God's command who guard him", not that they guard him from His command; it is an inversion; or that they guard with that guarding from God's command and by His leave, as you say to a man "I answered you from your calling me" and "by your calling me". One of the Basran grammarians said the meaning is "they guard him against God's command", as they say "he fed me from hunger and against hunger" and "he clothed me from nakedness and against nakedness". Al-Tabari says he has shown earlier that the better view is that "guarding him from God's command" describes the guards of this one who hides by night, who guard him in the belief that they ward God's command off him; so God, mighty be His praise, told that his guard will avail him nothing when His command comes, and said "when God wills evil for a people there is no repelling it, and they have no protector besides Him". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 385}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
