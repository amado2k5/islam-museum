**Commentary on verse 122: the true promise of God (pp. 226-228, note 3 on p. 226 and notes 1-5 on p. 227).** Al-Tabari says that those who believed in God and His Messenger, affirming God's oneness and the prophethood of His Messenger, and who did righteous deeds, meaning performed the obligations God laid on them, are promised that on the Day of Resurrection, when they come to God, He will bring them into gardens, as a recompense for what they did in the world, abiding there for ever. The word "gardens" means orchards, and "for ever" means without end. God's promise is true: it is a certain and honest promise, not like the devil's false promise, which is a delusion to those of his allies who are promised it, but a promise from One who does not lie, cannot lie and does not break His word. God described His promise as truthful in this verse, al-Tabari says, because of what He had earlier reported of Satan's saying (the determined portion, the leading astray, the desires, the command to slit the cattle's ears, see [[passage/quran-4-119|4:119]]) and of his promising and arousing desires ([[passage/quran-4-120|4:120]]). So God described the two promises and the two promisers and stated the verdict on the people of each, to alert His creatures to what is for their good and their rescue from ruin, so that they would be deterred from disobeying Him, act in obedience and win the reward He has prepared in His gardens. Then God asks, rhetorically, whether anyone's speech is truer than His, meaning that no one's is, so how could they leave the work for which their Lord promised the gardens, disbelieve in Him and oppose His command, knowing no one is more truthful, and do what the devil commands in hope of the false promises he makes, when they know that his promises are a delusion with no truth in them, and take him as an ally rather than obeying God? The two words for speech used in the verse and in the explanation have one meaning. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the phrase for doing righteous deeds (p. 226, note 3), of "gardens" and of "abiding" in the lexical indexes, and of the word for truth, and notes the manuscript reading against the print in two places, including a noun for ruin which the manuscript gives in a different but correct form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 228}
