**Commentary on verse 4 (pp. 132-133)**

Al-Tabari says: an announcement from God and His messenger to the people that God and His messenger are free of the polytheists, except of the covenant of those you covenanted with, believers, who then did not diminish anything of your covenant and did not back anyone against you among your enemies, helping them with their persons or bodies, with weapons, horses or men: complete their covenant to its term, that is keep faith with them in the covenant you made and do not wage war on them until its term ends; God loves those who fear Him by obeying Him, performing His obligations and avoiding disobedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 132}

Report 16471, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: to their term means to their appointed time. Report 16472, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the particular covenant to a stated term. Report 16473, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they are the polytheists of Quraysh with whom the Messenger covenanted at the time of al-Hudaybiyya, who had four months of their term left after the day of sacrifice; God told His prophet to fulfil their covenant to its term, and for those without one to the end of Muharram, every covenant holder's covenant was thrown back, and he was told to fight them until they testify that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His messenger, accepting nothing else from them. Report 16474, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: the term of one who held a covenant before the sura came down was four months, from the day of the proclamation to the tenth of Rabi' al-Akhir; if they broke their covenant and backed an enemy they had none, but if they kept it and backed no enemy against him, he was told to pay them their covenant and keep it. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of covenanting, backing, completing and fearing God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 133}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
