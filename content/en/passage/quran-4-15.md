## Summary
Verse 15 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 15 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of the indecency and the period of confinement (pp. 73-74).** Abu Jafar says the women who commit the indecency are the women among yours who fornicate, whether married or unmarried; the sense is: call as witnesses against them, to what they have done of the indecency, four male witnesses from among the Muslims; if they testify against them, keep them at home until they die, or until God provides for them an exit and a path to deliverance from what they did of the indecency. In his notes the editor says that al-Tabari's gloss using the preposition "with" before the word for fornication, unlike the verse, will be clarified at p. 81, and that Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud's reading has the preposition; for the indecency, the holding, the death-taking and the way he refers to the earlier places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 73}

**The early authorities on the "way" (pp. 74-75, reports 8795-8800).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters spoke along these lines. Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i Muhammad ibn Yazid (8795, from Yahya ibn Abi Za'ida from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Mujahid that confinement in the home until death was ordered, and that the way is the hadd penalty; Muhammad ibn Amr (8796, from Abu Asim from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that the indecency is fornication, that confinement was ordered once four had testified against them until they die, and that the way is the hadd. Al-Muthanna (8797, from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that a woman who fornicated used to be confined in the house until she died, and that God afterwards sent down the ruling on the fornicatress and fornicator in [[passage/quran-24-2|sura 24]], a flogging of each of a hundred, and stoning if both were married, so that this is the way God made for them. Muhammad ibn Sa'd (8798, from his father from his uncle from his father from his father) reports from Ibn Abbas that God provided a path for them, which is flogging and stoning. Bishr ibn Mu'adh (8799, from Yazid from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that this was before the hadd penalties, when the two were harmed by words together and the woman confined; then God made a way, which for a married person was a hundred lashes then stoning, and for one not married a hundred lashes and a year's banishment. Al-Qasim (8800, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj) reports that Ata ibn Abi Rabah and Abd Allah ibn Kathir said the indecency is fornication and the way is the hadd, stoning and flogging. The editor notes that the printing dropped the word "the hadd" in the last report and he restored it from the manuscript, and that Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i Muhammad ibn Yazid was introduced at no. 2739 and elsewhere, the printing's "from Muhammad ibn Yazid" being a plain error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 74} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 75}

**Al-Suddi on the dower of the fornicatress (pp. 75-76, report 8801).** Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8801, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that these are women who have married and menstruated; that a woman who fornicated was confined in the house and her husband took her dower, which became his, this being what is in [[passage/quran-4-19|verse 19]] of this sura; then the hadd penalties came and abrogated it, so she was flogged and stoned and her dower became an inheritance, and the way was flogging. The editor's note says that in the citation both the printing and the manuscript run in the text of another verse, of sura 2 ([[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]), which he judges a copyist's slip and not Abu Jafar's; he wonders at al-Suyuti, who has it in al-Durr al-Manthur ascribed to Ibn Jarir alone and gives it as in the manuscript and printing without pausing at the merged verse; the editor therefore printed the text of the verse that is the object of the citation, and records that the publisher dropped after "clear indecency" the word "fornication", which he restored from the manuscript and al-Durr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 75} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 76}

**Reports of al-Dahhak and Mujahid, and the hadith of Ubada ibn al-Samit (pp. 76-78, reports 8802-8810).** A report said to be related to al-Tabari (8802, "it was related to me" from al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj from Abu Mu'adh from Ubayd ibn Sulayman) has al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim say that the way is the hadd and that the hadd abrogated this verse. Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i (8803, from Yahya from Isra'il from Khusayf) reports from Mujahid that the way is a flogging of a hundred, for the male and the female doer, and al-Rifa'i (8804, from Yahya from Warqa from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid that it is flogging. Muhammad ibn Bashshar (8805, from Mu'adh ibn Hisham from his father from Qatada from al-Hasan from Hittan ibn Abd Allah al-Raqashi) reports from Ubada ibn al-Samit that when revelation came down to the Prophet he would lower his head and his Companions would lower theirs; when it lifted from him he raised his head and said: God has set a path for them, the previously married with the previously married and the unmarried with the unmarried; the previously married is flogged then stoned, and the unmarried is flogged then banished. Ibn Bashshar (8806, from Abd al-A'la from Sa'id from Qatada from al-Hasan from Hittan) reports from Ubada that the Prophet said: take from me, God has set a path for them, the previously married with the previously married flogged a hundred and stoned with stones, and the unmarried a hundred lashes and a year's banishment. Bishr (8807, from Yazid from Sa'id from Qatada from al-Hasan from Hittan ibn Abd Allah, brother of the Banu Raqash) reports from Ubada that when revelation came the Messenger of God was distressed by it and his face turned dark; one day it came to him and he met that, and when it lifted from him he said: take from me, God has set a path for them, the previously married with the previously married a hundred lashes then stoning with stones, the unmarried with the unmarried a hundred lashes then a year's banishment. Yunus (8808, from Ibn Wahb) reports Ibn Zayd on the verse: the sense is not to marry them until they die, and that did not put them out of Islam; then this was abrogated and the way was made, so that if she fornicated while married she was stoned and brought out, and for the unmarried the way was a hundred lashes. Yahya ibn Abi Talib (8809, from Yazid from Juwaybir) reports from al-Dahhak that the way is flogging and stoning. Ibn al-Muthanna (8810, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba from Qatada from al-Hasan from Hittan) reports from Ubada that the Messenger of God said: take from me, God has set a path for them, the previously married with the previously married and the unmarried with the unmarried; the previously married are flogged and stoned, the unmarried flogged and banished. These reports are given as transmitted; the choice and argument are al-Tabari's, and the museum gives no verdict on them. The editor's notes: al-Tabari gives this hadith with five chains (8805-8807 and 8810-8811), all sound and connected except the last as will be shown, and Muslim narrated it from Muhammad ibn Bashshar, al-Tabari's teacher, by this chain and by others; Hittan ibn Abd Allah al-Raqashi al-Basri is a reliable, firm Follower and a reciter, listed in the Tahdhib, al-Bukhari's Kabir, Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Abi Hatim and the Tabaqat of the reciters. In 8806 Sa'id is Ibn Abi Aruba, and "from al-Hasan" has fallen from the chain in the manuscript and printing through the copyists' error, since Muslim narrated it from Ibn Bashshar and from Ibn al-Muthanna, both from Abd al-A'la, in the right form, so he restored it; all the reports he saw "from Qatada" have this addition, and likewise Ahmad in his Musnad, Abu Dawud (no. 4415) and al-Bayhaqi. In 8807 the manuscript has a different wording for "distressed by it" and the right one is from the versions of the hadith; he explains that "distressed" means the matter grieved and weighed on him, that "his face turned dark" means its colour changed toward dust-grey, and that "it lifted from him" means what was on him was removed. In 8808 the printing added "which he mentioned"; the manuscript is somewhat sound if the verb is read as a passive, so he left it for fear it may be right, though he is now in doubt. In 8809 the printing dropped "Yazid" from the chain, who is in the manuscript, a chain that recurs throughout the commentary. In 8810 the word "Ibn" fell from the printing before al-Muthanna, and "Muhammad ibn Ja'far" is Ghundar, companion of Shu'ba, the printing's "Muhammad ibn Abi Ja'far" being an evident error; Ahmad narrated the hadith by this route in the Musnad from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba, Muslim from Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna and Ibn Bashshar both from Shu'ba, Ahmad also from Yahya from Hajjaj from Shu'ba, al-Tahawi in Sharh Ma'ani al-Athar through Asad ibn Musa from Shu'ba, and Hammad ibn Salama from Qatada from al-Hasan from Hittan from Ubada in al-Darimi's Sunan. His note then begins: most of the narrators who narrated this hadith from al-Hasan al-Basri stated that it is from al-Hasan, from ... and breaks off at the end of the page, to continue on the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 76} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 77} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 78} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
