**Commentary on verse 27 (pp. 295-297)**

On [[passage/quran-11-27|11:27]] al-Tabari says: the great men of Noah's people and their nobles, the chiefs, who disbelieved in God and denied the prophethood of their prophet Noah, said: 'we see you, Noah, as only a mortal like us', meaning a human like them in make, form and kind, as if they denied that God sends a mortal as messenger to His creation. His words 'and we see that none follows you but those who are the lowest of us, in the first thought' mean we see none follow you but our lowly people, not the great and noble, as it seems and appears to us. The readers differed on 'badi al-ra'y': most readers of Medina and Iraq read 'badiya' without hamza and 'ra'y' with hamza, in the sense of the outward view, from 'the thing appeared', and he cites a rajaz of Abu Nukhayla al-Sa'di with 'badi badi' without hamza, and a similar line; the editor spends long tracing the rajaz. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 295}

Some of the people of Basra read 'badi'a al-ra'y' with hamza also, in the sense of the beginning of the opinion, from 'I began with this matter' when one begins with it before others. Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two readings is that of those who read 'badiya al-ra'y' without hamza on 'badi' and with hamza on 'ra'y', since the sense is: only those who are our lowliest in outward view and as it appears to us. His words 'and we see in you no merit over us' mean we do not see any merit you have over us that you gained by differing from us, going from worshipping idols to worshipping God and making servitude pure for Him, so that we should follow you seeking that merit, 'rather we think you liars'. This is their address to Noah, for they denied Noah and not his followers, since his followers were not messengers; the address is to one but made like the address to many, as in [[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]]. Report 18105, from Ibn Abbas: 'in the first thought' means as it appeared to us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 297}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
