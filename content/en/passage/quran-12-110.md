## Summary
Verse 110 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 110 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 110 (pp. 296-309)**

On "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied, Our help came to them, and whom We willed was saved, and Our might is not turned back from the criminal people", al-Tabari says: We sent before you only men whom We inspired, from the people of the towns, and they called those to whom We sent them, who denied them and rejected what they brought from God, "until, when the messengers despaired", those We sent to them, of their believing in God and confirming them in what they brought from God, and the denying nations to whom We sent them thought that the messengers We sent had lied to them in what they told them from God of His promise to help them against them, "Our help came to them". That is the saying of a group of interpreters. Report 19987, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu al-Sa'ib Salm ibn Junada, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Muslim: "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied", he said, when the messengers despaired that their people would respond to them, and their people thought the messengers had been denied, help came to them thereupon, and whom He willed was saved. Report 19988, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abu Mu'awiya the blind, al-A'mash and Muslim, is similar except that he said "the messengers despaired" and not "when the messengers despaired". The editor draws attention in a note to the care taken in transmitting reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 296}

Report 19989, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal, Sufyan and Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib: "until, when the messengers despaired" that their people would submit, and the messengers' people thought the messengers had been denied, Our help came. Report 19990, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal, Sufyan, al-A'mash and Abu al-Duha, is the same. Report 19991, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Imran ibn Uyayna, Ata' and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: until the messengers despaired of their people and their people thought the messengers had been denied, "Our help came to them". Report 19992, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Husayn and Imran al-Sulami: the messengers despaired of their people confirming them and their people thought the messengers had lied to them. The editor says Imran al-Sulami is Imran ibn al-Harith al-Sulami, Abu al-Hakam, a reliable Kufan Successor who narrated from Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr and Ibn Umar. Report 19993, from Ibn Abbas, through Amr ibn Abd al-Hamid, Jarir, Husayn and Imran ibn al-Harith al-Sulami: the messengers despaired of their people responding to them, "and thought they had been denied", he said, their people thought they had come with a lie. Report 19994, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Idris, Husayn and Imran ibn al-Harith: until the messengers despaired of their people responding to them and their people thought they had denied them, "Our help came to them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 297}

Report 19995, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Husayn Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Yunus, Abthar, Husayn and Imran ibn al-Harith: the messengers despaired of their people believing, and their people thought the messengers had been lied to in what they were promised, "Our help came to them". The editor says Abu Husayn is Abd Allah ibn Ahmad ibn Abd Allah ibn Yunus al-Yarbu'i, a teacher of al-Tabari, and that Abthar ibn al-Qasim al-Zubaydi is reliable. Report 19996, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Ibn Abi Adi, Shu'ba, Husayn and Imran ibn al-Harith: "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people's help, "and thought they had been denied", their people thought they had denied them. Report 19997, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah, Hushaym, Husayn and Imran ibn al-Harith: of their people believing in them and responding to them, and their people thought the messengers had denied them, "Our help came to them", meaning the messengers. Report 19998, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym, is exactly the same. Report 19999, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata', Harun, Abbad al-Qurashi and Abd al-Rahman ibn Mu'awiya: "and thought they had been denied" read with the light form, whose sense according to him is that the people thought the messengers had lied. The editor gives notes on the narrators of these chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 298}

Report 20000, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Bakr, Talq ibn Ghannam, Za'ida, al-A'mash and Muslim: "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people confirming them, and their people thought that their messengers had lied to them, "Our help came to them"; the editor thinks "Abu Bakr" is a miscopying of "Abu Kurayb". Report 20001, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the messengers despaired of their people following them and their people thought the messengers had been lied to, so God helps the messengers and sends punishment. Report 20002, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: until the messengers despaired of their people obeying and following them and their people thought their messengers had lied to them, "Our help came to them". Report 20003, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Muhammad ibn Fudayl, Husayn and Imran ibn al-Harith: "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people, "and thought they had been denied", he said: the delay came upon them only from one who thought they had been lied to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 299}

Report 20004, from Ibn Abbas, through Adam al-Asqalani, Shu'ba, Husayn ibn Abd al-Rahman and Imran ibn al-Harith: "and thought they had been denied" in the light form, and Ibn Abbas said: the people thought the messengers had lied to them, in the light form. Report 20005, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Jarir and Ata': "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people, and their people thought the messengers had lied to them. Report 20006, from Khusayf: I asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr about God's words "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people, and the unbelievers thought that they had themselves lied. Report 20007, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ya'qub, al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Isma'il ibn Ulayya and Kulthum ibn Jabr: of their people believing, and their people thought the messengers had lied. Report 20008, from Ibrahim ibn Abi Hurra al-Jazari, through al-Muthanna, Arim, Hammad ibn Zayd and Shu'ayb: a young man of Quraysh asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Abu Abd Allah, how do you read this word, for when I come to it I wish I did not read this sura, "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied"? He said: yes, until the messengers despaired of their people confirming them, and those to whom they were sent thought that the messengers had lied. Al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim said: I have never seen the like of today, a man called to knowledge who hesitates! Had you travelled to Yemen for this it would have been little! The editor gives notes on the narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 300}

Report 20009, from Rabi'a ibn Kulthum from his father, through al-Muthanna and al-Hajjaj: Muslim ibn Yasar asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr and said: Abu Abd Allah, a verse has reached me to the utmost, "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied"; this is death, that the messengers should think they had been lied to, or think it in the light form! Sa'id ibn Jubayr said: Abu Abd al-Rahman, until the messengers despaired of their people responding and their people thought the messengers had lied to them, "Our help came to them, and whom We willed was saved, and Our might is not turned back from the criminal people". Muslim rose to Sa'id, embraced him and said: may God relieve you as you have relieved me! The editor notes that some words are missing from the manuscript and were supplied by the editor of the first printing of al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur, and gives notes on the narrators, among them Muslim ibn Yasar al-Basri, Abu Abd Allah the jurist, a reliable Successor. Report 20010, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Yahya ibn Abbad, Wuhayb and Abu al-Mu'alla al-Attar: the messengers despaired of their people's faith and their people thought the messengers had lied about what they told them and threatened them with. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 301}

Reports 20011 and 20012, from Mujahid, through Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih, and Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa: "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people confirming them, and their people thought the messengers had lied, help came to the messengers. Report 20013, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hammad and Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib: of their people, and their people thought the messengers had lied. Report 20014, from Kulthum ibn Jabr: Sa'id ibn Jubayr said to me, a lord among you asked me about this verse and I said, the messengers despaired of their people and their people thought the messengers had lied. Report 20015, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the messengers despaired of their people believing in them, and their polytheist people thought the messengers had been lied to about what God promised them of help against them and that they had been betrayed; he recited "Our help came to them", and said: help came to the messengers then. He said: and my father used to read it "kudhibu". Report 20016, from Abd Allah ibn al-Harith, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata', Sa'id, Abu al-Mutawakkil and Ayyub ibn Abi Safwan: "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people's belief, "and thought they had been denied", and the people thought they had lied to them in what they brought. In a long note the editor says the chain of this report is problematic because of the obscurity of some of its men and that he has no way of settling it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 302}

Report 20017, from al-Dahhak, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Wahhab and Juwaybir: their people thought their messengers had lied to them in what they promised them. Report 20018, from Ibn Mas'ud, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Muhammad ibn Fudayl, Jahsh ibn Ziyad al-Dabbi and Tamim ibn Hadhlam: "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied", the messengers despaired of their people believing in them, and their people, when the matter was delayed, thought they had lied, in the light form! The editor says no criticism is recorded of Jahsh ibn Ziyad al-Dabbi and that Tamim ibn Hadhlam al-Dabbi is a Companion-circle man of Ibn Mas'ud, reliable and of few reports. Report 20019 begins at the end of the page, through Abu al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far and Shu'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 303}

Report 20019 concludes, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Abu al-Mu'alla: "until, when the messengers despaired", he said, the messengers despaired of their people's help and the messengers' people thought the messengers had lied to them; the editor says Abu al-Mu'alla is al-Attar, Yahya ibn Maymun. Report 20020, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Amr ibn Thabit and his father: until the messengers despaired of their people confirming them and their people thought the messengers had lied to them; the editor says Amr ibn Thabit ibn Hurmuz al-Bakri is very weak and not reliable, while his father Thabit ibn Hurmuz al-Haddad Abu al-Miqdam is reliable. Report 20021, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Ahmad, Isra'il, Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: of their people confirming them, and their people thought the messengers had lied to them. Report 20022, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: they despaired of their people responding to them and believing in them, "and thought", he says, the messengers' people thought the messengers had lied to them about the promise. Al-Tabari says: the reading on this interpretation we have given is "kudhibu" with a damma on the kaf and a light dhal, which is also the reading of some Medinan readers and most Kufan readers. We chose this interpretation and this reading because it follows God's words "We sent before you only men whom We inspired, from the people of the towns. Have they not travelled through the land and seen how was the end of those before them?", {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 304}

which was evidence that the messengers' despair was of the faith of their peoples who were destroyed, and that the implied subject in "and thought they had been denied" is the mention of those before them, the destroyed nations. This was made clearer by God's following, in the course of the report about the messengers and their nations, with "and whom We willed was saved", since those destroyed were those who thought the messengers had lied to them and so denied them, thinking they had been lied to. Some who read this reading went to an interpretation other than the one we chose, taking the sense as: until the messengers despaired of their people's faith and the messengers thought they had been denied in what they were promised of help. Report 20023, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Uthman ibn Umar and Ibn Jurayj: Ibn Abbas read "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied" and said: they were human, they grew weak and despaired. Report 20024, from Ibn Jurayj, through Hajjaj ibn Muhammad: Ibn Abi Mulayka told me that Ibn Abbas read "and thought they had been denied" in the light form; Ibn Jurayj said, I say as he says: they were betrayed. Abd Allah said: Ibn Abbas said to me, they were human; and Ibn Abbas recited [[passage/quran-2-214|2:214]]; Ibn Jurayj said Ibn Abi Mulayka said: he took it to mean that they grew weak and thought they had been betrayed. Report 20025, from Masruq from Abd Allah, through Ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal, Sufyan, al-A'mash and Abu al-Duha: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 305}

that he read "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied" in the light form. Abd Allah said: it is what you dislike. Report 20026, from Masruq, through Abu Amir, Sufyan, Sulayman and Abu al-Duha: a man asked Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, and he said: it is what you dislike, in the light form. Report 20027, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Abu Bishr: I said, "kudhibu"! He said: yes, were they not human? Report 20028, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima: they were human, they thought it. Al-Tabari says: this is an interpretation and saying, and another interpretation is more correct in my view, while the opposite saying better suits the attributes of the prophets. If it were allowed that messengers should doubt God's promise to them and have uncertainty about the truth of His report, while they see of God's proofs and signs what those sent to do not see, so that they would be excused in that, then those sent to would be even more worthy of excuse in it; and that is a saying whose problem is plain if anyone says it. The editor explains what he corrected in the wording of the printed and manuscript text. This last interpretation was reported from Ibn Abbas to A'isha, and as we were told she denied it in the strongest terms. Report 20029, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Uthman ibn Umar and Ibn Jurayj: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 306}

Ibn Abbas read "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied" and said: they were human, they grew weak and despaired. Ibn Abi Mulayka said: I mentioned this to Urwa, and he said: A'isha said: God forbid! God never promised His messenger anything but he knew it would come to pass before he died, but trial did not cease for the messengers until the prophets thought that those who followed them had denied them. She used to read it "kudhdhibu", with the doubled dhal. The editor says this chain is sound and that al-Bukhari reports it in his Sahih through Ibrahim ibn Musa, from Hisham, from Ibn Jurayj. Report 20030, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, through Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: Ibn Abbas read "and thought they had been denied" in the light form; Abd Allah said, then Ibn Abbas said to me, they were human! and recited [[passage/quran-2-214|2:214]]; Ibn Abi Mulayka said he took it to mean that they grew weak and thought they had been betrayed. And Urwa told me that A'isha opposed that and refused it, saying: God never promised Muhammad anything but he knew it would come to pass until he died, but trial did not cease for the messengers until they thought that the believers with them had denied them. A'isha used to read "and thought they had been denied" with the doubled dhal, for denial. This repeats the previous report and its chain is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 307}

Report 20031, from Urwa from A'isha, through Sulayman ibn Dawud al-Hashimi, Ibrahim ibn Sa'd, Salih ibn Kaysan and Ibn Shihab: I said to her, God's words "until, when the messengers despaired and thought they had been denied"; A'isha said: they were certain that they had been denied. I said, "kudhibu"? She said: God forbid, the messengers never thought that of their Lord; it is only the followers of the messengers: when revelation was delayed from them and trial grew heavy on them, the messengers thought their followers had denied them, "Our help came to them". The editor says al-Bukhari reports it at length in his Sahih, that al-Tabari's wording is very much abridged, and that Ibn Hajar wrote a good full discussion in explaining this hadith. Report 20032, from A'isha, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, al-Zuhri and Urwa: until the messengers despaired of those of their people who denied them confirming them, and the messengers thought that those of their people who had believed had denied them, God's help came to them then; its chain to A'isha is sound. Al-Tabari says: this is what was related on the matter from A'isha, but she read "kudhdhibu" with the doubled dhal and a damma on the kaf, in the sense we reported from her: that the messengers thought of their followers who had believed in them that they had denied them and turned back from their religion, being impatient for help. We have shown that the reading and interpretation we choose for this word specifically are otherwise. Others who read it "kudhdhibu", with a damma on the kaf and a doubled dhal, said the sense is: until the messengers despaired of their people believing in them and confirming them, and the messengers thought, meaning were certain, that their peoples had denied them, Our help came to the messengers. They said: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 308}

"zann" here means knowledge, as in the verse of the poet Duraid ibn al-Simma in which he says they thought of two thousand armored horsemen. Report 20033, from al-Hasan and Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: until the messengers despaired of their people's faith and "thought they had been denied", that is, were certain that their people had no good and no faith, "Our help came to them". Report 20034, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "until, when the messengers despaired" of their people, "and thought they had been denied", he said, and knew they had been denied, "Our help came to them". Al-Tabari says: most readers of Medina, Basra and Syria read this reading, that is with a doubled dhal and a damma on the kaf of "kudhdhibu". This interpretation of al-Hasan and Qatada, if read with the doubled dhal and damma on the kaf, goes against the sayings of all the Companions whose words we have reported, since none of them took "zann" in this place to mean knowledge and certainty; moreover the Arabs use "zann" for knowledge only where the knowledge was gained through report or not through witnessing and seeing, whereas where knowledge was gained through witnessing and seeing they do not use "zann": one hardly says "I think myself alive" or "I think myself a human" for "I know myself a human" or "I know myself alive". The messengers whose nations denied them certainly saw their nations and heard their denial, so it is said of them that they thought of their nations that they had denied them. A saying was reported from Mujahid on this which differs from all the earlier sayings we have mentioned, and it continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 309}

We stop here at the foot of p. 309, in the middle of the commentary on verse 110 (at the introduction of Mujahid's differing report); the next batch begins at p. 310. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 309}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
