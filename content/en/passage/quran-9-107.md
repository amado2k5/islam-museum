## Summary
Verse 107 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/demolition-of-masjid-al-dirar|Demolition of Masjid al Dirar]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 107 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 107 (pp. 468-475)**

Al-Tabari says: those who built a mosque for harm, who are, as it is mentioned, twelve persons of the Helpers. Report 17186, from al-Zuhri, Yazid ibn Ruman, Abdallah ibn Abi Bakr, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada and others, through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq: the Messenger of God came back, that is from Tabuk, until he halted at Dhu Awan, a town an hour's journey from Medina; the people of the mosque of harm had come to him while he was preparing for Tabuk and said "Messenger of God, we have built a mosque for the sick and needy and for rainy and winter nights, and we should like you to come and pray for us in it", and he said "I am on the wing of a journey and in a state of preoccupation", or as the Messenger of God said it, "and when we have come back we shall come to you, God willing, and pray for you in it". When he halted at Dhu Awan the news of the mosque reached him, and the Messenger of God called Malik ibn al-Dukhshum, brother of Banu Salim ibn Awf, and Ma'n ibn Adi, or his brother Asim ibn Adi, brother of Banu al-Ajlan, and said "go to this mosque whose people are wrongdoers, demolish it and burn it". They set out quickly until they came to Banu Salim ibn Awf, who are Malik ibn al-Dukhshum's clan, and Malik said to Ma'n "wait for me until I bring you fire from my family", and went in to his family and took a palm frond and lit a fire in it, and they went out running until they entered the mosque with its people in it, burned it and demolished it, and the people scattered from it; and what came down about them of the Qur'an came down, "those who took a mosque for harm and unbelief" to the end of the story. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 468}

Those who built it were twelve men: Khidham ibn Khalid of Banu Ubayd ibn Zayd, one of Banu Amr ibn Awf, from whose house the mosque of discord was brought out; Tha'laba ibn Hatib of Banu Ubayd, who is attached to Banu Umayya ibn Zayd; Mu'attib ibn Qushayr of Banu Dubay'a ibn Zayd; Abu Habiba ibn al-Az'ar of Banu Dubay'a ibn Zayd; Abbad ibn Hunayf, brother of Sahl ibn Hunayf, of Banu Amr ibn Awf; Jariya ibn Amir and his two sons Mujammi' ibn Jariya and Zayd ibn Jariya; Nabtal ibn al-Harith, of Banu Dubay'a; Bahzaj, who is attached to Banu Dubay'a; Bijad ibn Uthman, of Banu Dubay'a; and Wadi'a ibn Thabit, who is attached to Banu Umayya, the clan of Abu Lubaba ibn Abd al-Mundhir. The editor notes that these names are from Ibn Hisham's Sira and that the manuscript and the printed text differ in some. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 469}

Al-Tabari says: the sense of the passage is: those who built a mosque in harm to the mosque of the Messenger of God and in unbelief in God, through their opposing the Messenger of God by it, and to divide the believers so that some would pray in it apart from the mosque of the Messenger of God and some in his mosque, and so they would differ and split because of it; "and an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger before" means preparing it for Abu Amir the unbeliever, who opposed God and His Messenger, disbelieved in them and fought the Messenger of God, "before", meaning before their building that mosque. That is because Abu Amir was the one who rallied the confederates to fight the Messenger of God; when God forsook him he joined the Romans, seeking help from their king against the Prophet of God, and wrote to the people of the mosque of harm telling them to build the mosque they built, as is reported of him, so that he might pray in it, as he claimed, when he returned to them, and they did so. That is the meaning of God's words "and an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger before". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 469}

As for "and they will surely swear that we meant only the best", the Almighty says its builders will swear that "we meant by building it only kindness to the Muslims, benefit and ease for the weak and sick and those unable to get to the mosque of the Messenger of God to pray in it", and that is the good act; "and God bears witness that they are liars" in their oath and in their saying "we built it only wishing the best", but they built it wanting by building it evil, harm to the mosque of the Messenger of God, unbelief in God, division among the believers and an ambush for Abu Amir the transgressor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 470}

What the interpreters said agrees with what we said. Report 17187, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah and Mu'awiya: "those who took a mosque for harm" are some of the Helpers who built a mosque, and Abu Amir said to them "build your mosque and prepare what you can of strength and weapons, for I am going to Caesar, king of the Romans, and will come with an army of the Romans and drive out Muhammad and his Companions"; and when they had finished their mosque they came to the Prophet and said "we have finished building our mosque and should like you to pray in it and ask for blessing for us", and God sent down about it "never stand in it; a mosque founded on piety from the first day is more worthy of your standing in it" to the words "God does not guide the wrongdoing people", which is [[passage/quran-9-108|9:108]]. Report 17188, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "those who took a mosque for harm, unbelief and dividing the believers", he said, when the Messenger of God built the mosque of Quba, some men of the Helpers went out, among them Bahzaj, Wadi'a ibn Hizam and Mujammi' ibn Jariya al-Ansari, and built the mosque of hypocrisy, and the Messenger of God said to Bahzaj "woe to you, what did you intend by what I see?", and he said "Messenger of God, by God I intended only the best", and he was lying, and the Messenger of God believed him and wished to excuse him, and God sent down "those who took a mosque for harm, unbelief and dividing the believers, and an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger", meaning a man of them called Abu Amir who had been a fighter against the Messenger of God and had gone to Heraclius; they were lying in wait, that when Abu Amir came he might pray in it, he having left Medina fighting God and His Messenger; "and they will surely swear we meant only the best, and God bears witness that they are liars". The editor says he could not verify the words "grandfather of Abdallah ibn Hunayf" in this report and fears something dropped from it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 471}

Report 17189, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "and an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger before", he said, Abu Amir the monk went to Caesar, and they said "when he comes he will pray in it", thinking he would prevail over Muhammad. Reports 17190, 17191, 17192 and 17193, from Mujahid, by the routes of Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ishaq, Abdallah, Warqa', al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "those who took a mosque for harm and unbelief", he said, the hypocrites; "for whoever fought God and His Messenger", for Abu Amir the monk. Reports 17194, 17195 and 17196, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, by the routes of Ibn Waki', Suwayd ibn Amr, Hammad ibn Zayd, Ayyub, Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, al-Hasan ibn Yahya and Abd al-Razzaq: they are Banu Ghanm ibn Awf, a clan called Banu Ghanm; and in report 17196 from Aisha: "an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger", Abu Amir the monk went to al-Sham, and those who built the mosque of harm said "we built it only so that Abu Amir might pray in it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 472}

Report 17197, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: some of the people of hypocrisy set out and built a mosque at Quba to rival the mosque of the Messenger of God, then sent to the Messenger of God that he might pray in it; it was mentioned to us that he called for his shirt to go to them, until God informed him of it; as for "an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger", he was a man called Abu Amir who fled from the Muslims and joined the polytheists, and they killed him for his Islam; he said "when he comes he will pray in it", and God sent down "never stand in it; a mosque founded on piety", the verse. The editor notes that Abu Amir the monk died among the Romans in the year nine or ten. Report 17198, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd: they are some of the hypocrites who built a mosque at Quba to harm the Prophet of God and the Muslims, "and an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger", they used to say "when Abu Amir returns from Caesar of the Romans he will pray in it", and they used to say "when he comes he will overcome the Prophet of God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 473}

Report 17199, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the mosque of Quba, in which all of them used to pray; and there was a man among the chiefs of the hypocrites called Abu Amir, father of Hanzala "washed by the angels", and of Sayfi and his brother, and these three were among the best of the Muslims; Abu Amir fled, he and Ibn Abd Yalil of Thaqif and Alqama ibn Ulatha of Qays, from the Messenger of God until they joined the king of the Romans; as for Alqama and Ibn Abd Yalil, they returned and pledged to the Prophet and became Muslims, but Abu Amir became a Christian and stayed. He said, and some of the hypocrites built the mosque of harm for Abu Amir, saying "until Abu Amir comes and prays in it", "and to divide the believers", by which they divided their congregation, since they used to pray together in the mosque of Quba; and they came to deceive the Prophet, saying "Messenger of God, sometimes the flood comes and cuts us off from the valley and from the people, and we pray in our mosque, and when the flood goes we pray with them", and he said they built it on hypocrisy; he said, and their mosque collapsed in the time of the Messenger of God. The editor notes that the names in this report are not readable in the manuscript, and that Ibn Abd al-Barr mentions that Abu Amir the transgressor joined Heraclius and died an unbeliever with him, Kinana ibn Abd Yalil and Alqama ibn Ulatha disputing his inheritance before Heraclius. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 474}

He said, and people threw straw and refuse on it, and God sent down "those who took a mosque for harm, unbelief and dividing the believers", so that the believers should not all pray in the mosque of Quba, "and an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger before", before Abu Amir, "and they will surely swear we meant only the best, and God bears witness that they are liars". Report 17200, from Layth, through Ibn Humayd, Harun and Abu Ja'far: Shaqiq had not caught the prayer in the mosque of Banu Amir, and was told "the mosque of Banu so-and-so has not yet prayed", and he said "I do not like to pray in it, for it was built in harm, and every mosque built in harm, show or repute traces its origin to the mosque built in harm". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 475}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
