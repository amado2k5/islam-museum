## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "Moses said to them, 'Woe to you, do not invent falsehood against Allah, lest He seize you with punishment, and He has failed in his fabrication.'" He says: "Moses said to the magicians when Pharaoh brought them: 'Woe to you, do not invent lies against Allah.' He means: Do not dispute against Allah lying, and do not say 'He will seize you with punishment,' so He will destroy you and destroy you. The Arabs have two dialects for it: 'sahat', 'asahat', and 'sahat', more than 'asahat'; from it, it is said: 'sahat al-dhir' (the time has set), and 'asahah' means so-and-so's wealth is destroyed: if he destroys him, he is 'yasahat' sahtan, and 'asahtuhu yasahthu isahat' isahat. Among the sayings of Al-Farazdaq: 'And a time ago, O son of Marwan, he left nothing but wiped or wiped.' It is also narrated: except 'masahat' or 'majallaf'. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, and Ibn Zayd.
