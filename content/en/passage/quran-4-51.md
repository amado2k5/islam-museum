## Summary
Verse 51 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 51 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Those addressed and what they accept (p. 461).** Al-Tabari says: have you not seen with your heart, Muhammad, those who were handed part of God's Book and learned it, who accept the Jibt and the Taghut as true while disbelieving in God, though they know that belief in the two is disbelief and accepting them is association of partners with God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 461}

**The Jibt and the Taghut: two idols, or the idols and their interpreters (p. 461, reports 9764-9765, note 1).** The interpreters differed over the two terms. Some said they are two idols the polytheists worshipped besides God; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9764, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Ayyub) reports from Ikrima that they are two idols. Others said the Jibt is the idols and the Taghut their interpreters; Muhammad b. Sa'd (9765, by his chain) reports from Ibn Abbas that the Jibt is the idols and the Taghut those who stand before the idols and speak for them falsehood to lead people astray. The editor explains that the interpreters of the idols are the diviners who speak in their name, as if telling people in their own tongue what the idols had said. Al-Tabari adds that some men claimed the Jibt is the diviner and the Taghut a man of the Jews called Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, chief of the Jews (pp. 461-462), without naming who said so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 461}

**The view that the Jibt is sorcery and the Taghut Satan (pp. 462-463, reports 9766-9772, notes 1-2 on p. 462).** Others said the Jibt is sorcery and the Taghut Satan. Muhammad b. al-Muthanna (9766, from Muhammad b. Abi Adi, from Shu'ba, from Abu Ishaq, from Hassan b. Fa'id) and Ibn Waki' (9767, from his father, from Sufyan, from Abu Ishaq, from Hassan b. Fa'id al-Absi) report the same from Umar; Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9768, from Hushaym, from Abd al-Malik, from someone who told him) from Mujahid; Ya'qub (9769, from Hushaym, from Zakariyya) from al-Sha'bi; Muhammad b. Amr (9770, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid that the Taghut is Satan in the form of a man to whom they take their disputes and who is the master of their affair; and Ibn Humayd (9771, from Jarir, from Abd al-Malik, from Qays) from Mujahid that the Taghut is Satan and the diviner. Others said the Jibt is the sorcerer and the Taghut Satan; Yunus (9772, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that his father used to say so (p. 463). The editor notes that Hassan b. Fa'id al-Absi was treated at 5834, that the print here and in the next report reads "Hassan b. Qa'id al-Ansi", that the chain occurs at 5835 and that report 9767 occurs at 5834. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 463}

**The view that the Jibt is the sorcerer and the Taghut the diviner (p. 463, reports 9773-9776).** Others said the Jibt is the sorcerer and the Taghut the diviner. Ibn Bashshar (9773, from Muhammad b. Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Abu Bishr) reports from Sa'id b. Jubayr that the Jibt is the sorcerer in the Abyssinian tongue and the Taghut the diviner; Ibn al-Muthanna (9774, from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud) from Rufay' the same; Ibn al-Muthanna (9775, from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud) from Abu al-Aliya the reverse, the Taghut being the sorcerer and the Jibt the diviner; and al-Muthanna (9776, from Amr b. Awn, from Hushaym, from Dawud) from Abu al-Aliya that one of them is sorcery and the other Satan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 463}

**The view that the Jibt is Satan and the Taghut the diviner (p. 463, report 9777, number inferred).** Others said the Jibt is Satan and the Taghut the diviner. Bishr b. Mu'adh (from Yazid b. Zuray', from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that they used to be told the Jibt is a satan and the Taghut the diviner. The number of this report is unreadable on the page and is inferred from the sequence as 9777. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 463}

**Continuation: the Jibt as Satan and the Taghut as diviner (p. 464, reports 9778-9779).** Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9778, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) reports the same as before from Qatada, and Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9779, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi that the one term means Satan and the other the diviner. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 464}

**The view that the Jibt is the diviner and the Taghut the sorcerer (p. 464, reports 9780-9781, note 1).** Ibn Waki' (9780, from his father, from Sufyan, from a man) reports this from Sa'id b. Jubayr; Ibn Bashshar (9781, from Hammad b. Mas'ada, from Awf) from Muhammad (named no further) that one is the diviner and the other the sorcerer. The editor notes that the print and the manuscript both have a corrupt word in the preceding sentence and that he put the reading the context requires. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 464}

**The view that they are Huyayy b. Akhtab and Ka'b b. al-Ashraf (pp. 464-465, reports 9782-9784).** Others said one is Huyayy b. Akhtab and the other Ka'b b. al-Ashraf. Al-Muthanna (9782, from Abdullah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali) reports from Ibn Abbas that the Taghut is Ka'b b. al-Ashraf and the Jibt Huyayy b. Akhtab; al-Muthanna (9783, from Ishaq, from Abu Zuhayr, from Juwaybir) and Yahya b. Abi Talib (9784, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) from al-Dahhak that the Jibt is Huyayy and the Taghut Ka'b. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 465}

**The view that the Jibt is Ka'b and the Taghut Satan (p. 465, report 9785).** Others said the Jibt is Ka'b b. al-Ashraf and the Taghut Satan. Ibn Humayd (9785, from Jarir, from Layth) reports from Mujahid that the Satan was in the form of a man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 465}

**Al-Tabari's choice and argument (p. 465, note 1).** Abu Ja'far says the right sense in his view is: they accept as true two objects of worship besides God, worship them and take them as two gods; the two terms are names for anything magnified by worship, obedience or submission apart from God, whatever the magnified thing, stone, human being or satan. So the idols of the pre-Islamic age were jibts and taghuts, as were the satans the unbelievers obeyed in disobeying God, the sorcerer and the diviner whose statements about the polytheists were accepted, and Huyayy b. Akhtab and Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, who were obeyed by their Jewish community in disobedience to God and disbelief in Him and His messenger. He says he explained earlier the origin of the name Taghut and does not repeat it. The note refers to vol. 5 (p. 419) and reports 5834-5845 as printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 465}

**The Jews' saying that the unbelievers are on a straighter path (p. 466, note 1).** Al-Tabari says they say to those who denied God's oneness and the mission of His messenger Muhammad, these being those described as unbelievers, that they are straighter and more just than those who believed God and His messenger and acknowledged what their prophet brought; "path" means road. He says this is a figure: the sense is that God described the Jews handed part of God's Book as saying, in their veneration of others than God by worship and obedience while disbelieving in God and His messenger, that the people of disbelief in God have more right to the truth than the people of belief and that the religion of the deniers is more just and correct than that of the affirmers. The editor notes that print and manuscript have a stray conjunction fused to the alif in the manuscript, that the right reading is the one he gave, and that it depends on the phrase "God described". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 466}

**The view that this is the description of Ka'b b. al-Ashraf (pp. 466-468, reports 9786-9791, notes).** It is reported that this describes Ka'b b. al-Ashraf and that he said it. Muhammad b. al-Muthanna (9786, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Dawud, from Ikrima) reports from Ibn Abbas that when Ka'b came to Mecca Quraysh said to him: you are the scholar of the people of Medina and their chief? He said yes. They said: do you not see this cut-off palm shoot among his people, who claims to be better than us, while we are the people of pilgrimage, of the Ka'ba's keeping and of watering the pilgrims? He said: you are better than he. Then God sent down the verse on the one cut off ([[passage/quran-108-3|108:3]]) and the verse concerning these men. Ibn al-Muthanna (9787, from Abd al-Wahhab, from Dawud, from Ikrima) reports similarly; Ishaq b. Shahin (9788, from Khalid al-Wasiti, from Dawud, from Ikrima) that the polytheists told Ka'b: judge between us and this cut-off palm shoot, for you are our master and your people's, and he said: by God you are better than he. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9789, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Ayyub) reports from Ikrima that Ka'b went to the unbelievers of Quraysh, urged them to raise an army against the Prophet, told them to fight him and said he was with them; they said: you are people of a book and he is a man of a book, and we do not feel safe that this is not a ruse from you; if you want us to go out with you, prostrate to these two idols and believe in them; and he did. Then they asked: are we on a straighter path, or Muhammad, when we slaughter the camel, give milk with water to drink, join kin, host the guest and circle this House, while Muhammad cut his kin and left his town? He said: you are better and better guided, and the verse came down about him. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9790, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that after what happened between the Prophet and the Jews of Banu al-Nadir, when he came to them seeking help with the blood-money of the two men of Amir and they plotted against him and his companions and God informed His messenger, and he returned to Medina, Ka'b fled to Mecca and made a pact with them against Muhammad; Abu Sufyan said to him: Abu Sa'd, you are a people who read the Book and know and we do not, so tell us, is our religion better or Muhammad's? Ka'b said: show me your religion. Abu Sufyan listed slaughtering the camel, watering the pilgrims, hosting guests, tending the House of their Lord and worshipping the gods of their fathers, and that Muhammad commanded them to leave this; Ka'b said: your religion is better than Muhammad's, hold to it; do you not see that Muhammad claims he was sent with humility yet marries as many women as he likes, and we know no greater kingship than kingship over women? Then the verse came down. Al-Qasim (9791, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Mujahid that it came down about Ka'b and the unbelievers of Quraysh, and Ibn Jurayj said that Ka'b came and Quraysh asked him about Muhammad; he belittled and played down his affair and told them he was astray; they then adjured him by God, are we better guided or he, since you know we slaughter camels, water the pilgrims, tend the House and feed as long as the wind blows? He said: you are better guided. In the editor's notes: the manuscript has "scholar" where the print has "best", and he preferred "scholar" because they asked him about religion; the word for the palm shoot means the offshoots that grow on a palm trunk unrooted in the ground, then used of a lone, weak man with no family, offspring or helper, and he explains that Quraysh meant that when the Prophet died he would have no line, while "cut off" means one cut off with no descendants; that he found no biography for Ishaq b. Shahin al-Wasiti and that Khalid al-Wasiti is Khalid b. Abdullah b. Abd al-Rahman (cited at an earlier report whose number is garbled in the scan); that "to rally" a people means asking them to raise an army; that the high-humped she-camel is the best, fattest and dearest of their camels; that the print has a reading he replaced with the manuscript's; that the events of Banu al-Nadir were in the fourth year after the hijra, when they wanted to betray the Prophet, plotting to drop a stone on him from above the wall of the house by which he sat, God informed him, he rose and returned to Medina, then ordered preparation for war on them, besieged them and exiled them, and all of sura al-Hashr came down about them, with a reference to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3:199-213); and that the phrase about feeding while the wind blows conveys continuity. On Ka'b's remark about women the editor adds a polemical note directed at critics of the Prophet including Orientalists; it is his, and the museum records it without adopting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 468}

**The view that this describes a group of Jews (pp. 469-470, reports 9792-9793, notes 3-4 on p. 469 and 1-3 on p. 470).** Others said this describes a group of Jews, among them Huyayy b. Akhtab. Ibn Humayd (9792, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from someone, from Muhammad b. Abi Muhammad, from Ikrima or Sa'id b. Jubayr) reports from Ibn Abbas that those who rallied the factions from Quraysh, Ghatafan and Banu Qurayza were Huyayy b. Akhtab, Sallam b. Abi al-Huqayq Abu Rafi', al-Rabi' b. al-Rabi' b. Abi al-Huqayq, Abu Ammar, Wahwah b. Amir and Hawdha b. Qays, Wahwah, Abu Ammar and Hawdha being of Banu Wa'il and the rest of Banu al-Nadir. When they came to Quraysh, Quraysh said: these are the rabbis of the Jews and people of knowledge of the earlier books, so ask them whether your religion is better or Muhammad's. They asked, and the rabbis said: your religion is better than his and you are better guided than he and his followers. God then sent down the verses about them as far as the mention of the great kingdom ([[passage/quran-4-54|4:54]]). Bishr b. Mu'adh (9793, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that it was mentioned to them that the verse came down about Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, Huyayy b. Akhtab and two Jewish men of Banu al-Nadir who met Quraysh at a season; the polytheists said: are we better guided or Muhammad and his companions, we being the people of the keeping and the watering and of the Haram? They said: you are better guided than Muhammad and his companions, though they knew they were lying and were moved by envy of Muhammad and his companions. In the editor's notes, Abu Rafi' is the epithet of Sallam b. Abi al-Huqayq, the added conjunction in the print is an error, the print dropped "b. al-Rabi'", the print's "Abu Amir" is an error for Abu Ammar from the manuscript, all agreeing with Ibn Hisham's Sira; report 9792 is in the Sira (2:210) following the reports up to 9724; and a season is a gathering place of people in a market or pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 470}

**The view that this describes Huyayy b. Akhtab alone (pp. 470-471, report 9794).** Others said it describes Huyayy alone. Yunus (9794, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that Huyayy b. Akhtab came to the polytheists who said: Huyayy, you are people of books, are we better or Muhammad and his companions? He said: we and you are better than they. Then the verses came down as far as the mention of God's cursing ([[passage/quran-4-52|4:52]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 471}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 471, note 1).** Abu Ja'far says the soundest view is that of those who said this is a report from God about a group of the Jewish People of the Book; it may have been the group Ibn Abbas named in the report from Muhammad b. Abi Muhammad from Ikrima or Sa'id, or Huyayy and one other with him, either Ka'b or someone else. The editor notes that the print has a wording he calls an error beyond doubt and that he put the right one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 471}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 471}
