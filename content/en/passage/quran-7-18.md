## Summary
Verse 18 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 18 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "He said: get out of it, disgraced and driven away", he says: this is a report from God of what He inflicted on the wicked one of His vengeance and curse and of driving him from His Paradise when he disobeyed Him, went against His command and answered back with what he should not have answered; God then said to him, get out of it, that is, from Paradise, "disgraced and driven away", meaning faulted. "Dha'm" is fault; of it one says "he faulted him, faults him, a fault, he is faulted", and they drop the hamza and say "I censured him, I censure, censure"; "dha'm" and "dhaym" are stronger in blame than "dhamm", and someone cited a line of al-Harith ibn Khalid al-Makhzumi on this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 342}

Most narrators recite al-Harith's line with "I blame her" instead of "I censure her", and the note refers to its earlier place. As for "madhur", it means driven far away; one says "dahara-hu, yadharuhu, duhur" when he drove him far away and expelled him, and from it the saying "drive the devil away from you"; the note refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. The commentators said the like of what he said: he gives (14384) from Qatada that it means get out of it cursed and banished; (14385) from Ibn Abbas that "disgraced" means loathed; (14386) from him that it means small, banished; (14387) from al-Suddi that "disgraced" is banished and "driven away" is expelled; and (14388) from Mujahid the like, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 343}

and (14389) from al-Rabi' that "disgraced" is banished and "driven away" is belittled; (14390) from Ibn Abbas that it is banished; and (14391) that al-Tamimi asked Ibn Abbas about "disgraced and driven away" and he said hated, and the note says it found no biography for Uthman ibn Yahya al-Qarqasani and discusses what is in al-Tabari's Tarikh; and (14392) from Ibn Zayd that he said we know no difference between "madh'um" and "madhmum", but letters may be reduced, as the poet said "O Amir" to Amir and "O Har" to Harith, and the Qur'an was sent down in the speech of the Arabs; the note gives two lines as evidence of such shortening of names, one on "Amir" and one by Zuhayr on "al-Harith", and corrects the wording of the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 344}

Then, on "as for those of them who follow you, I shall fill Hell with all of you", he says: this is an oath from God, who swore that whoever of the children of Adam follows the enemy of God, Iblis, obeys him and proves his opinion of him right, He will fill with all of them, meaning with the disbelievers among the children of Adam who followed Iblis, and with Iblis and his offspring, Hell. May God have mercy on one who proved the enemy of God's opinion of him false, frustrated his hope and wish regarding him, did not allow what his enemy coveted of him, deemed him a deceiver and did not take him as an adviser; for God alerted His servants through these verses to the old enmity of His enemy and theirs, Iblis, toward them, to the earlier envy he bore their father and his aggression against him and against them, and made known to them where His blessings fell on them anciently in themselves and in their father, so that they ponder His verses and the people of understanding remember, desist from obeying His enemy and theirs and turn to obeying Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 345}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
