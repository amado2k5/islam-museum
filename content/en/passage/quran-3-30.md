## Verse 30 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 319-321)

**The day each soul finds its work.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-30|3:30]]) says the meaning is that God warns of His punishment on a day when every soul finds the good it did present and complete, and what it did of evil it wishes there were a far goal between it and that evil; the return that day is to Him, so guard yourselves against your sins. Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada (6840), glosses the word for 'present' as 'in full'; the editor says this sense is rarely found in the lexica and so he recorded it. Al-Tabari adds that some grammarians held the implied wording to be 'remember the day', because the Qur'an came for command and remembrance, and similar commands to fear a given day occur elsewhere; the editor notes that the word 'some' is an addition required by the context. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 319}

**The relative pronoun.** Al-Tabari says the 'ma' attached to the first verb means 'that which', and cannot be conditional because the verb 'find' acts upon it; the second 'ma' is joined to the first and the clause is in a nominative position because of what follows. The editor says both the manuscript and printed text have a sentence that does not stand, which he reconstructed from al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:206), that the clause is a second object of 'find' as 'present' was, and that 'wuqu'' here means transitive effect, explained in the index of terms. In al-Tabari's reading, the sense is: on the day when each soul finds what it did of good present, and what it did of evil, it wishes there were a goal between it and that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 320}

**The word for 'term', and the verse of al-Tirimmah.** Al-Tabari says the 'amad' is the goal to which a thing proceeds, and cites a line by al-Tirimmah to the effect that every living being completes the count of its life and perishes when its term ends, meaning the end of its appointed time. The editor says the line is in the poet's diwan (p. 112) in a form that differs from al-Tabari's, notes that al-Tabari transmitted al-Tirimmah's diwan, read it in the congregational mosque in Egypt, dictated it to people and explained its rare words, and says he cannot tell whether this is a slip or another version known to al-Tabari; he then sets the line in the poem, which describes the abode of a beloved after her people's departure, and explains words such as 'shu'ab' (scattered), 'la yalithan' (do not delay) and 'mud' (perishing). Musa ibn Harun, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi (6841), gives the far term as a far place; al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj (6842), as a term of life. Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan (6843), says one of them would be glad never to meet that deed, and that would be his wish, whereas in the world it was a sin he enjoyed; the editor notes that the printed text and manuscript differ on the last word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 321}

**The warning joined to compassion.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that God warns them against incurring His displeasure through what they commit, so that they would meet Him on the day each soul finds its work while He is displeased with them and they meet a painful punishment they cannot bear. Then He declares that He is compassionate and merciful to His servants, and that part of His compassion is warning them, making them fear His punishment and forbidding them His disobediences. Al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Amr, from al-Hasan (6844), says it is of His compassion that He warned them of Himself. The editor says al-Hasan is undoubtedly al-Hasan al-Basri, that the report is attributed to him in Ibn Kathir's tafsir and al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur, that the manuscript and printed text have 'Amr ibn al-Hasan', which is a mistake, and that he could not determine who 'Amr' is. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 321}
