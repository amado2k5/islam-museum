## Summary
Verse 44 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum

Then, on "the companions of Paradise called to the companions of the Fire: we have found what our Lord promised us true, so have you found what your Lord promised true? They said: yes. Then a caller announced between them: God's curse is on the wrongdoers": the people of Paradise called to the people of the Fire after entering it: people of the Fire, we found true what our Lord promised us in the world on the tongues of His messengers, of reward for belief in Him and them and obedience to Him; so did you find what your Lord promised on their tongues for disbelief in Him and for disobedience, of punishment? The people of the Fire answered yes. He gives (14670) from al-Suddi that the people of Paradise found what they were promised of reward and the people of the Fire what they were promised of punishment; and (14671) from Ibn Abbas that God promised the people of Paradise bliss, honour and every good that people did or did not know, and the people of the Fire all disgrace and punishment that people did or did not know. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 446}

The readers differed over "they said: yes". Most readers of Medina, Kufa and Basra read "na'am" with fatha on the ayn, and a reading "ni'm" with kasra on the ayn is reported from some Kufans, for which a line of the Banu Kalb is cited. Al-Tabari says the right reading to him is fatha, because it is the widespread reading among the readers of the lands and the well-known usage among the Arabs. As for "a caller announced between them", it means a caller called and an announcer informed them that God's curse, His wrath, anger and punishment, is on whoever disbelieved in Him. He adds that he has already explained that "an", when it accompanies speech resembling quotation, is sometimes heavy and sometimes light, so whether it is read heavy or light is the same, the meaning being one and both being well-known readings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 447}
