## Summary
Verse 144 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/death-of-the-prophet|Death of the Prophet]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 144 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of the verse (pp. 251-252).** Abu Ja'far says the verse means that Muhammad is a messenger like the other messengers God sent to His creation, calling to Him and to obedience to Him, who died and whom God took when their terms ended; so what God does in taking Muhammad at the end of his term is like His taking of the messengers before him. God then rebukes the Companions of Muhammad for the panic and distress they showed when at Uhud they were told that Muhammad had been killed, and condemns the turning away of those who turned from the enemy and fled: if Muhammad dies at the end of his term or an enemy kills him, will you leave the religion to which he was sent and return to unbelief after faith and after the truth of what he brought was clear to you? Whoever among you leaves his religion and returns to unbelief does no harm to God by it: it does not weaken God's might and authority or diminish His dominion; he harms himself by apostasy and lessens his own share by unbelief. And God will reward whoever thanks Him for granting success and guidance by holding firm to what Muhammad brought, whether he dies or is killed, keeping to his path and his religion after him. The editor notes that the clause about the terms qualifies the messengers, that the copyist added a word which spoiled the sentence and he dropped it, that he kept the manuscript reading elsewhere, and he refers to the earlier explanation of this reversal (vol. 3: 163). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 251}

**Reports on the thankful (p. 252).**
- Al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Abd Allah ibn Hashim from Sayf ibn Umar from Abu Ruq from Abu Ayyub from Ali (7938): the thankful are those firm in their religion, Abu Bakr and his companions; Ali used to say that Abu Bakr was the trustee of the thankful and of God's friends, the most thankful and the most beloved to God. The editor says the printed text has Sayf ibn Amr, an error, and that the right form is Sayf ibn Umar al-Tamimi, author of the books on the apostasy wars and the conquests, whose reports Abu Ja'far often gives in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 252}
- Ibn Humayd from Jarir from Mughira from al-Ala' ibn Badr (7939): Abu Bakr is the trustee of the thankful, and he recited the verse. The editor identifies al-Ala' as al-Ala' ibn Abd Allah ibn Badr al-Ghanawi, named for his grandfather, who narrated mursal from Ali and is reliable in his view (in al-Tahdhib). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 252}
- Ibn Ishaq (7940): those who obeyed Him and acted on His command; Ibn Hisham (3: 118), continuing 7937. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 252}

**The occasion (pp. 252-254).** It is mentioned that the verse was sent to the Messenger about those of his Companions who fled from him at Uhud.
- Qatada (7941): that was Uhud, when wounds and killing struck them, and they then announced the Prophet's death to one another; some said that if he were a prophet he would not have been killed, and some of the ordinary Companions said to fight for what Muhammad fought for until God gives victory or they join him. The editor explains the verb for announcing a death to one another (the Arabs announced their dead in war to rouse each other to fight and avenge) and the phrase for "in the wake of that", and notes that al-Suyuti (al-Durr al-manthur 2: 80) words it differently. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 253}
- Al-Rabi' (7942): like that, adding that an emigrant passed a man of the Ansar who was writhing in his blood and asked him whether he knew Muhammad had been killed; the Ansari said that if he had been killed he had delivered the message, so fight for your religion; then God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 253}
- Al-Suddi (7943): when the Messenger came out to the polytheists at Uhud he posted the archers at the foot of the mountain facing the polytheists' cavalry and told them not to leave their place if they saw him defeat them, with Abd Allah ibn Jubayr, brother of Khawwat ibn Jubayr, as their commander. Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam and al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad charged and routed the polytheists, and the Prophet and his Companions attacked and routed Abu Sufyan. When Khalid ibn al-Walid, in command of the polytheists' cavalry, saw it, he wheeled back and the archers shot at him and he drew back; but when the archers saw the Messenger and his Companions in the midst of the polytheist camp plundering it, they hurried for the spoil. Some said they would not leave the Messenger's order, but most went and joined the camp. Khalid, seeing how few archers remained, shouted to his cavalry and charged, killing the archers and attacking the Companions; the polytheists, seeing their cavalry fighting, called to one another, charged the Muslims, routed them and killed them. Ibn Qami'a al-Harithi, of the Banu al-Harith ibn Abd Manat ibn Kinana, came and shot... and the report continues on the next page. The editor says al-Tabari's History (3: 14-15) gives the report to this point and that his notes treat wording differences, including the Prophet's words to the archers in the History, the sense of the words for wheeling, drawing back and calling to one another, and the correction of Abd Manat, and refers to the tracing at report 8004. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 254}

**Close of al-Suddi's account of Uhud (pp. 255-256).** (7943) The report continues: Ibn Qami'a struck the Messenger with a stone, breaking his nose and incisor (the tooth next to the front teeth) and wounding his face so that he was weighed down; his Companions scattered, some entering Medina and some going up the mountain to the rock. The Messenger called to the people, and thirty men gathered to him, but only Talha and Sahl ibn Hunayf stood firm; Talha shielded him and was hit by an arrow in the hand, which withered. Ubayy ibn Khalaf al-Jumahi, who had sworn to kill the Prophet, came on; the Prophet said that he would be the one to kill Ubayy, and stabbed him at the neck opening of his mail, a light wound, and Ubayy fell bellowing like a bull. When they carried him and said he had no real wound, he answered that the Prophet had said he would kill him, and that were all Rabi'a and Mudar to be struck so he would have been killed; he lived a day and part of a day and died of that wound. The word spread that the Messenger had been killed, and some of those at the rock said they wished for an envoy to Abd Allah ibn Ubayy to get them safe-conduct from Abu Sufyan, and that since Muhammad was killed they should go back to their people before they were killed. Anas ibn al-Nadr said that if Muhammad was killed, Muhammad's Lord was not, so they should fight for what he fought for; he then excused himself to God for what these men said, disowned what they brought, and charged with his sword until he was killed. The Messenger went calling to the people until he reached the men at the rock; one of them set an arrow to his bow to shoot him until he said he was the Messenger of God; they rejoiced to find him alive, and he rejoiced to see that he had Companions able to defend him with him. Their grief left them, and they recalled the victory and what they had missed of it, and their dead companions. God then spoke to those who had said Muhammad was killed in the verse before us. In notes the editor explains the word for the incisor; says "rather I will kill him" was the Prophet's answer when he heard of Ubayy's oath made in his absence; corrects some wording (the neck opening of the mail, the bellowing of the bull) from the History; explains the word for safe-conduct; and says al-Tabari's History (3: 14-15, then 3: 20) gives the report, referring to report 8004. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 255}

**Other reports (pp. 256-258).**
- Muhammad ibn Amr through Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid (7944): "turns back" means apostatises. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 256}
- From Ibn Abi Najih's father, by two chains (7945): the account of the emigrant and the wounded Ansari already given at 7942. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 256}
- Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from al-Qasim ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Rafi' of the Banu Adi ibn al-Najjar (7946): Anas ibn al-Nadr, uncle of Anas ibn Malik, came to Umar and Talha ibn Ubayd Allah among men of the Emigrants and Ansar who had given up, asked what kept them sitting, and was told that Muhammad the Messenger had been killed; he said, what will you do with life after him, rise and die for what the Messenger died for. He faced the enemy and fought until killed, and Anas ibn Malik was named for him. The editor explains the phrase for giving up and cites the Sira (3: 88) and the History (3: 19). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 257}
- Al-Dahhak (7947): a caller cried at Uhud, when the Companions were routed, that Muhammad had been killed and they should return to their first religion; then the verse was sent down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 257}
- Mujahid (7948): it was put into the Muslims' mouths at Uhud that the Prophet was killed, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 257}
- Ibn Abbas (7949): the Prophet withdrew with a band to a hillock while people fled, and a man standing on the road asked those passing what had become of the Messenger and heard that they did not know; he said that if the Prophet were killed they would give themselves up to the enemy, since these were their clans and brothers. Others said that if Muhammad were alive he would not have been routed, so he must be dead, and they excused themselves for fleeing; God then sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 257}
- Al-Dahhak (7950): people of doubt, sickness and hypocrisy, when the people scattered from the Prophet, his brow was cut and his incisor broken, said that Muhammad was killed and they should rejoin their first religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 258}
- Ibn Zayd (7951): nothing stands between you and leaving Islam except Muhammad's dying or being killed, and one of the two will happen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 258}
- Ibn Ishaq (7952): it concerns what people said, that Muhammad was killed, and their rout and turning from the enemy: if your Prophet dies or is killed, will you return to unbelief and abandon fighting your enemy, the Book of God and what his Prophet left of the religion among you, when it was made clear to you that he will die and leave you? Whoever leaves his religion does not lessen God's might, dominion or authority. The report is in Ibn Hisham (3: 117-118), continuing 7937, with its remainder at 7940. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 258}
- Ibn Jurayj (7953): the people of sickness, doubt and hypocrisy said, when people fled from the Prophet, that Muhammad was killed and they should rejoin their first religion; the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 258}

**Grammar: the question placed on a condition (pp. 259-260).** Abu Ja'far says the sense is: will you turn from your religion if Muhammad dies or is killed? The question is placed on the conditional particle but belongs to the answer; every question entering a conditional has this sense, because the answer is a statement standing by itself and the condition is its proviso, and the answer is then put in the jussive though its sense is indicative, since it follows the condition. He cites a line of al-Ra'i (a threat that a line of his verse would travel ahead of the one addressed; the editor traces it to al-Farra' and others and notes Ibn Qutayba's variant reading), and then [[passage/quran-21-34|21:34]] and [[passage/quran-73-17|73:17]]. He adds that if a present-tense verb stood in the answer, either the indicative or the jussive would be allowed, and that the question is not repeated with the second verb because the question at the start suffices. He notes that some reciters, in the places where the question about the resurrection is repeated ([[passage/quran-17-49|17:49]], [[passage/quran-37-16|37:16]], [[passage/quran-56-47|56:47]]), chose not to repeat the question with the second clause, and reasons from the reciters' unanimity on not repeating it here; he promises to give the right view when he reaches those places. The editor corrects wording where the printed text and manuscript misquoted the recitation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 259}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
