## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 19 to 21 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "So he cast it away, and behold, it was a serpent running": "When he saw it trembling, it was as if it were a jinn, and he turned away and did not follow," so he called: "O Moses, do not fear; indeed I do not fear the messengers." From Ibn Ishaq, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, who said: "Cast it, O Moses, and cast it away, and behold, it is a serpent running," trembling, with fangs and a form as God willed it to be. He saw something terrible, turned away, and did not hesitate, so his Lord called him: "O Moses, come forward and do not fear; We will restore it to its original history." And His saying: "He said, 'Take it and do not fear,' Allah Almighty says to Moses: 'Take the serpent,' and the 'ha' and 'alif' are from the mention of the serpent. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Ishaq.
