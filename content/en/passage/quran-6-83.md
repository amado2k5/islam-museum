## Summary
Verse 83 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 83 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "that was our argument that we gave Abraham against his people; we raise in degrees whom we will; your Lord is wise, knowing" al-Tabari says: by "that was our argument" he means Abraham's saying to his disputants among his polytheist people "which of the two parties has better right to security?", the one who worships one lord, making religion and worship pure for him, or the one who worships many lords? and their answer to him by saying that the one who worships one lord has better right to security, and their judgement for him against themselves; in that was the cutting off of their excuse and the failing of their argument and the rising of Abraham's argument over them, and that is the argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 504}

The editor comments at length on this place, saying that Abu Jafar, for all his greatness of rank, memory, accuracy and care, has contradicted himself and introduced a large inconsistency into his speech: at p. 494 he held that the verse is God's report of which party has better right to security and his final judgement between Abraham and his people, and said that if it were the saying of Abraham's people they would have admitted the oneness; then after a few pages he came back and interpreted this verse, claiming it is from the answer of Abraham's people to Abraham, the very view he had refuted. He thinks that such contradiction appears in the books of scholars as a proof that no one has infallibility in anything, infallibility being God's alone.

That is the argument God gave Abraham against his people. 13513, from Mujahid: "that was our argument that we gave Abraham against his people" is "those who believe and do not mix their belief with wrong"; 13514, from him: Abraham's saying when he asked "which of the two parties has better right to security" is Abraham's argument, and "we gave it to Abraham against his people" says we taught it to Abraham, showed it to him and made it known to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 505}

The readers differed in "we raise in degrees whom we will". Most readers of the Hijaz and Basra read it with "degrees" annexed to "whom", meaning we raise the degrees of whom we will, and most readers of Kufa with tanwin on "degrees", meaning we raise whom we will in degrees. "Darajat" is the plural of "daraja", rank, the root being the steps of a ladder, then used for the rising of stations and ranks. Abu Jafar said: the right view for me is that these are two readings, each read by leading readers, close in sense, since one whose degree is raised is raised in degrees and one raised in degrees has his degree raised; so whichever a reader reads he is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 505}

The editor refers for "degree" to earlier passages and says his explanation here is clearer than before.

So the sense is: that was our argument which we gave Abraham against his people, and by it we raised his degree above them and honoured him over them in this world and the next; in this world we gave him his reward in it, and in the next he is of the righteous; "we raise in degrees whom we will" for what he did of that and else. As for "your Lord is wise, knowing" it means: your Lord, Muhammad, is wise in his governing of his creatures and teaching his prophets the arguments against the communities that deny them and reject their Lord's oneness, and in the rest of his ordering, knowing what the matter of his messengers and those they are sent to comes to, of the communities persisting in denying them and perishing in it, or turning back and repenting by affirming God's oneness, believing his messengers and returning to obedience to him. He says to his prophet Muhammad: so take Abraham, your father and my friend, as your model in yourself and your people who deny you and the polytheists, and bear what befalls you from them as he bore it, for I know what your matter and theirs will come to and am wise in the ordering of you and them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 506}

The editor says "ta'assa bihi" means he took him as a model in himself and his way, that print has another form and he kept the manuscript's, and that "in the ordering" lacks the joining "and" in print and manuscript, which should be restored.

Verse 83 closes here on p. 506, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-84|6:84]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
