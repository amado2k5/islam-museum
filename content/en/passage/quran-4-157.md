## Summary
Verse 157 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 157 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says that their claim 'we killed the Messiah' is one God refuted, saying: they did not kill him nor crucify him, but a likeness was made for them; that is, they neither killed nor crucified Jesus, but it was made to seem so. Commentators differ on the form of the likeness. Some say that when the Jews surrounded Jesus and his companions, all of them were changed into Jesus' form, so that the men who wanted to kill him could not tell him apart, and one of those in the house went out to them, and they killed him thinking him Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 367}

Report 10779, from Wahb ibn Munabbih: Jesus was in a house with seventeen of the disciples when they were surrounded; God made them all in his form, and they said: bring out Jesus to us or we will kill you all. Jesus asked his disciples who would buy himself with paradise today; a man went out and said: I am Jesus. They seized him, killed him and crucified him; hence the likeness was cast on them, and the Christians thought it was Jesus, and God raised Jesus that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 368}

Report 10780, another version from Wahb through Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil: when God told Jesus that he was going out of the world, he was grieved at death and invited the disciples to a meal; then he washed their hands with his own and wiped them on his clothes; he asked them to pray that his term be postponed. They slept until they could not pray, and he said: can you not bear with me one night? He told them one of them would disbelieve in him before the cock crowed three times, and another would sell him for a small sum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 368}

In the same report, the Jews sought him and seized Simon, one of the disciples, who denied him; then he heard the crowing of a cock and wept. One disciple guided the Jews to Jesus for thirty dirhams; they seized and bound him, spat on him and put thorns on him, and brought him to the wood to crucify him, but God raised him up, and they crucified the likeness, and it remained seven days. His mother and the woman he had healed came weeping at the crucified figure; Jesus came to them and said: I have been raised to God, and nothing has befallen me but good; this was a thing made to appear to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 369}

The disciple who had sold Jesus was missing; it was said that he repented, hanged himself. Jesus said: had he repented, God would have accepted his repentance. He told the disciples to meet him at a place, and eleven came. He asked for a young follower called Yuhanna, and told them he was with them; go, for each of you will wake speaking the language of a people, to warn them and call them. The editor notes that al-Tabari gives this in his history, with small differences in wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 370}

Others say that Jesus asked those with him in the house to put his likeness on one of them, and a man volunteered; his likeness was cast on him and he was killed, and God raised Jesus. Report 10781, from Qatada through Sa'id and Bishr ibn Mu'adh, says the enemies of God, the Jews, plotted to kill Jesus and claimed they had killed and crucified him; Jesus asked his disciples who would have his likeness cast on him and be killed, and a man answered: I, prophet of God! So that man was killed, and God protected His prophet and raised him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 370}

Report 10782, from Qatada through Ma'mar, says that the likeness was cast on a disciple and he was killed, and Jesus had put this to them: who will take my likeness and have paradise? A man said: I. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 370}

Report 10783, from al-Suddi, says that the Children of Israel besieged Jesus and nineteen of the disciples in a house. Jesus asked: who will take my form and be killed, and have paradise? A man took it, and Jesus was raised to heaven. When the disciples came out they found nineteen, and they missed one from the count and doubted, so they killed the man and crucified him, thinking he was Jesus. Report 10784, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza, says Jesus asked who would have his likeness cast on him and die in his place; a man said: I, prophet of God; the likeness was cast on him, and they killed him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 371}

Report 10785, from Ibn Ishaq, says the king of the Children of Israel who was sent to kill Jesus was a man called David; when they resolved to act, Jesus told the disciples, who were twelve, and gives their names as they appear in Christian books; among them was a man called Sergius. The editor notes that the names are taken from the tenth chapter of the Gospel of Matthew, and that one of the disciples, Judas Iscariot, sold Jesus for thirty dirhams and then hanged himself in remorse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 372}

Ibn Ishaq also reports, from a man who had been a Christian and became Muslim, that Jesus said: O company of disciples, which of you wishes to be my companion in paradise, on condition that he be made to look like me to the people and be killed in my place? Sergius said: I. He sat in Jesus' place, and Jesus was raised. The people entered, seized him and crucified him; they had known the number of those in the house, but found the count incomplete, so the matter was confused for them, and Judas showed them the way for thirty dirhams, saying: I will kiss him, and you take him. They saw Sergius in Jesus' form and did not doubt it, and Judas kissed him, and they seized and crucified him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 373}

Ibn Ishaq says that Judas repented of his deed and hanged himself with a rope, and that some Christians claim that Judas was the one made to resemble Jesus, so that they crucified him, while he said: I am not your companion, I am the one who guided you to him. Al-Tabari says: God knows which of these was the case. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 373}

Report 10786, from Ibn Jurayj, says we have been told that Jesus asked his disciples who would volunteer to have his likeness cast on him and be killed; a man said, I, prophet of God; his likeness was cast on him and he was killed, and God raised His prophet to Himself. Report 10787, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih, says they crucified a man other than Jesus, supposing him to be him; report 10788, through Shibl, is the same; report 10789, from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj, says they crucified a man they took for Jesus, and God raised Jesus alive to Himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 374}

Al-Tabari prefers one of the two accounts from Wahb ibn Munabbih: the likeness was cast on all who were in the house with Jesus when he was surrounded, without his asking them, so that God would humble the Jews, save His prophet from the harm they intended, and test those among His servants whom He would test. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 374}

Al-Tabari argues that if the disciples who witnessed Jesus had been there at his raising and the casting of the likeness, they would have seen his raising and kept the one who was cast upon distinct from him; the matter would not have been confused for them. He also says the account that Jesus asked, who would have my likeness and be my companion in paradise, does not fit with their seeing the one who answered transformed into Jesus' form. He suggests either that all the people in the house were changed into Jesus' form, so that none could be distinguished from him, or that the people left him before the Jews entered, and his likeness fell on one companion who remained, and the companions and the Jews thought the one killed was Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 375}

Al-Tabari holds that those who reported that Jesus was killed do not deserve to be called liars, since they reported what they held to be true outwardly, though the matter with God was in reality different from what they related. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 376}

On the closing words, that those who differed about him were in doubt, having no knowledge, but only following conjecture, and did not kill him with certainty: he means the Jews who surrounded Jesus and his companions; they had identified the number of those in the house before they entered; when they entered they found one missing, so the matter of Jesus was confused, and they killed some in doubt. The pronoun in 'they did not kill him' refers to the conjecture they followed. Reports 10790 and 10791, from Ibn Abbas and Juwaybir, say they did not kill their conjecture with certainty; report 10792, from al-Suddi, says they did not kill his affair with certainty that the man was Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 377}
