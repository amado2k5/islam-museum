**Commentary on verse 35 (pp. 91-94)**

On "then it appeared to them, after they had seen the signs, that they should imprison him for a time" al-Tabari says it appeared to the Aziz, husband of the woman who had solicited Joseph. The plural "to them" was used though he is one, since he is not named or pointed at, like "those to whom the people said" in [[passage/quran-3-173|3:173]], where the speaker was said to be one. Others say the meaning is in the opinion they held, of leaving Joseph free, which changed: they decided to jail him after seeing the signs of his innocence of what the Aziz's wife charged him with. Those signs were the shirt torn from behind, the scratching of the face, and the cutting of the women's hands. Reports 19253 and 19254, from Ibn Abbas through Ikrima, Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki' and Ibn Numayr: among the signs were a tear in the shirt and a scratch on the face. The editor holds "Nasr ibn Awf" in the chain to be a corruption of Nadr ibn Arabi al-Bahili, who narrates from Ikrima. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 91}

Reports 19255 to 19262 name the signs: the shirt torn from behind, from Mujahid through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa, Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far, al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj; from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar, the women's cutting of their hands and the torn shirt; from Ibn Ishaq, his innocence of what he was charged with, in that his shirt was torn from behind; from al-Suddi, the shirt and the cutting of hands. On "for a time" he says: until the time at which they decide what to do; the editor refers to the commentary on "hin". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 92}

It is said that God made that prison for Joseph a penalty for his intent toward the woman and an expiation of his fault. Report 19263, from Ibn Abbas, through Ikrima, Khusayf, Isra'il and Yahya ibn Abi Za'ida: Joseph stumbled three times: when he intended her and was jailed; when he said to the one he thought would be freed, mention me to your lord, and stayed several years in prison as Satan made him forget to mention his Lord; and when he told his brothers, you are thieves, and they replied, if he steals, a brother of his stole before. The reason for his jailing is said to have been the Aziz's wife's complaint to her husband; report 19264, from al-Suddi: she said to her husband, this Hebrew slave has shamed me among the people, excusing himself to them and telling them I solicited him, and I cannot offer my excuse; either let me go out and give it, or imprison him as you imprisoned me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 93}

The grammarians differed over the lam in "layasjununnahu". One Basran said it enters because this is a place where "ayy" can fall, and since the interrogative particle can enter there the nun came with it, as in "it appeared to them which of them would take", meaning it became clear to them. Another grammarian denied this, saying it is an oath, and "hal taqumanna" is not an oath, whereas "la-taqumanna" can only be one. A Kufan grammarian said "it appeared to them" has the sense of "saying", and saying takes any speech, oath or question, so one may say "it appeared to them: Zayd stood" and "it appeared to them: let him stand". It is also said that "hin" here means seven years; report 19265, from Ikrima, through Ibn Waki', al-Muharibi and Dawud: seven years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 94}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
