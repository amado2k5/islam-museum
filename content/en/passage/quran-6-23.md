## Summary
Verse 23 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 23 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "then their trial was nothing but to say: by God, our Lord, we were not polytheists" al-Tabari says: then their saying, when we said to them "where are your partners whom you claimed?", was not an answer to our question when we tried them and tested them, "but to say: by God our Lord, we were not polytheists", lying in their oaths in saying so. The readers differed on this. A group of readers of Medina and Basra and some Kufans read "thumma lam takun fitnatuhum" with ta and the raised noun, which the grammarians hold odd and not eloquent; a line of Labid in his famous ode is related like it, describing a wild ass and his mares, where he made "kanat" feminine because of its neighbour "habit". A group of Kufan readers read "thumma lam yakun fitnatahum" with ya and the accusative, "but that they said", with the same sense as the others meant, except that they made "yakun" masculine since "an" is masculine. Al-Tabari holds this the better of the two readings because "an" is more fixed in definiteness than "fitna". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 298}

The editor notes that the printed text dropped "with ta", that the manuscript has "their saying", which is eloquent, refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an and Ibn al-Shajari's Amali, explains Labid's line, where the pronoun in "went on" is the wild ass and in "put them ahead" his mares and "'arradat" means they fled and turned from the path, and says Abu Jafar passed over the raised reading of "fitnatuhum", which is Hafs's reading in our copies, perhaps on purpose, since he treated this verse and its like fully earlier.

The interpreters differed on "then their trial was nothing": some said it means "then their saying was not"; report 13134, from Qatada: their statement, and Mamar said: I heard another than Qatada say, their excuse. 13135, from Ibn Abbas: their saying; 13136, from him: their words; 13137, from al-Dahhak: their speech. Others said it means their excuse: 13138 and 13139, from Qatada: their excuse, excusing themselves with falsehood and lies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 299}

Al-Tabari holds the right view to be that it means: then their saying, at our testing them, was not an excuse for the idolatry they had practised, except that they said "by God our Lord, we were not polytheists"; "trial" was put in place of "saying" because the hearers know the sense, for fitna is testing and trying, but since the people's answer would not occur there except upon testing, the trial, which is the test, was put in place of the report of their answer and excuse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 300}

The readers also differed on "by God our Lord": most readers of Medina and some Kufans and Basrans read "wa-llahi rabbina" in the genitive, "Lord" being an epithet of God, and a group of Followers and most readers of Kufa read "rabbana" in the accusative, meaning "by God, our Lord". Al-Tabari holds the accusative the better, because this is the answer of those asked "where are your partners", and the answer of the people to their Lord was "by God, our Lord, we were not polytheists", so they denied having said it in the world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 300}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

By "we were not polytheists" he means we did not call on any partner for you and called on none but you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 301}

Verse 23 closes here at p. 301, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-24|6:24]].
