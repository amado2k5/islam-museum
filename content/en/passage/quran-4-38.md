## Summary
Verse 38 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 38 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Those who give out money for display (pp. 356-357, notes 1-6).** Al-Tabari says God prepared for the unbelieving Jews He described a degrading punishment, and for those who give out their money for display before people, not in obedience to God or His path but in the path of Satan, and who do not affirm God's oneness or the return to Him on the day of resurrection, when deeds are repaid; the relative is in the genitive, linked to the word for the unbelievers. The notes refer to the earlier treatment of the word for display (page numbers unclear as printed) and say the printed edition reads a different word for the return. Al-Tabari says Mujahid (in report 9495) held this to be a description of the Jews, but in his own view it fits better the hypocrites, who had been polytheists and showed Islam out of caution before the Messenger and the believers while staying in unbelief, because the Jews affirmed God's oneness and believed in the resurrection and their unbelief was denying Muhammad's prophethood (his choice). Notes 5-6: manuscript and printed edition share a sentence that does not parse, which the editor corrected by the sense, and the printed edition's publisher made it worse by changing another word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 357}

**What the separating conjunction shows (p. 357, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says that the conjunction coming between this description and that of the other group in the verse before, for whom a degrading punishment was announced, indicates that these are descriptions of two kinds of people with different meanings, although all of them are unbelievers; had both descriptions been of one kind, there would have been no conjunction between them. And if someone supposes that a conjunction joining one attribute to another of a single subject is not unknown in Arabic, he holds that the most eloquent usage, when the same subject is meant, is to omit it and to include it when another description is meant, and that directing God's speech to the most eloquent and best-known usage of those in whose tongue the Book came down is better than directing it to the rarer (his argument and choice). The notes on p. 357 are textual: the printed edition misread an unpointed word, and its publisher tried to repair a place where the copyist ran one letter into another and spoiled the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 357}

**Whoever makes Satan his intimate (p. 358, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: whoever has Satan as a close friend and companion, acting in obedience to him, following his order and abandoning God's order in his spending of his money for display, not in obedience, and in his denial of God's oneness and of the resurrection, for him God has condemned Satan as a bad companion. The accusative of the word for companion is because the verb of condemnation carries a pronoun for Satan, as in another verse ([[passage/quran-18-50|18:50]]); so the Arabs do with this verb and its like. He cites a line of Adi b. Zayd (described, not reproduced) saying that one does not ask about a man but looks at his companion, for the companion follows the one he accompanies, and he glosses the word as companion and friend. Note 1 refers to the earlier treatment of the verb (numbers unclear), to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:267-269) and Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1:127); note 2 places the line in the Diwan of Adi among the poets of the Jahiliyya (p. 466) and the Majmu'at al-Ma'ani (p. 14) and elsewhere, says the editor kept it as Abu Ja'far narrated it and as in the manuscript, and that the printed edition changed it to the usual narration, which he calls undoubtedly poor handling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 358}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 358}
