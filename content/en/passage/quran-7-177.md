**Commentary on verse 177 (p. 275)**

Al-Tabari says: evil is the likeness of the people who denied God's proofs and evidences and rejected them, and it was their own selves whose shares they cut short and whose benefits they diminished by their denial of them, not others'. It is said that "evil as a likeness" is from the word for evil, meaning "how bad a likeness", that is the likeness of the people, with "the people" put in the place of "the likeness" and the likeness dropped as the sense is understood, as in [[passage/quran-2-177|2:177]] whose sense is "but righteousness is the righteousness of him who believes in God"; he has given parallels elsewhere. The editor notes that the grammarians treat this verb as a fixed one that works like "how excellent" and "how bad", and that the bracketed words are needed to complete the sentence since the copyist confused it badly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 275}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
