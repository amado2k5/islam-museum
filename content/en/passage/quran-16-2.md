## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "The angels descend by the Spirit by His command upon whomever He wills among His servants: to warn that there is no deity except Me, so fear [Allah]": The reciters differed in the interpretation of His saying: "The angels descend," and the majority of the reciters of Medina and Kufa read it as: "He descends the angels" with a ya, a stressed zāy, and the angels accusative, meaning that God sends down the angels with the Spirit. Some Basranis and some Meccans read this: 'yanazl al-malaika' with a ya, a lightened zay, and the accusative accusative of angels. It is narrated from some Kufans that he used to recite it: 'tanazal al-malaika' with a ta' and a stressed zay and al-malailika in the nominative, although he differs from this in this regard. It is narrated from him in agreement with all the reciters of his country. The most correct readings in this regard are those who recited: 'yanazl al-malaika' meaning: Allah descends angels. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Ibn Jurayj.
