**Commentary on verse 38 (pp. 251-252)**

Al-Tabari says this verse is an urging from God to the believers in Him among the Messenger's Companions to fight the Romans, that being the Messenger's campaign of Tabuk. He says: believers who affirmed God and His messenger, what is the matter with you, what is your affair, that when it is said to you "go forth in God's way", that is when God's messenger Muhammad says to you go forth, leave your homes for your place of fighting, "you sink down to the earth"? The origin of the word for going forth is leaving one place for another for a reason that stirred one to it, hence the shying of an animal, but for going out to battle one says a man went out to such a frontier with a verbal noun of a different form, which he thinks is one of the distinctions made when what is spoken of differs even though the sense is the same; the editor says the language books record the other verbal noun as well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 251}

So the sense is: what is the matter with you, believers, that when you are told to go out to fight in God's way, that is in struggle against God's enemies, you sink down to the earth, that is you grow heavy about staying on your land and in your homes and sitting in them. The form was said with the assimilation of the "ta" into the "th", an alif being added to begin with, since if the alif were dropped and the word begun it would have to start with a vowelled letter, as at [[passage/quran-7-38|7:38]] "when they have all overtaken one another in it", and as a poet said, in a line whose author is unknown. The editor says its sense is the pattern "you made yourselves heavy" from heaviness, citing Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. And "are you pleased with the life of this world instead of the Hereafter?" is God's question whether they are pleased with a share of the world and ease in it in exchange for the bliss of the Hereafter and what is with God for the pious in His gardens; "so what"; the sentence continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 252}
