## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 64 to 66 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "And those who spend the night prostrating and standing before their Lord": those who spend the night with their Lord praying to Allah, oscillating between prostration in their prayer and standing. And His saying: "and standing" is the plural of standing, just as fasting is the plural of fasting. "And those who say, 'Our Lord, avert from us the punishment of Hell'" means the Exalted: And those who call upon Allah to avert from them His punishment and punishment out of fear and reverence for Him. And His saying: "Indeed, its punishment was a love," it means: the punishment of Hell was a constant, persistent, constant insult that never separated from those who were punished by it among the disbelievers, and it destroyed him. From this is their saying: a man in debt, from debt and indebtedness. From this, it is said that the debtor is called a debtor because he demands his right and insists on his owner regarding it. In the passage, he quotes from al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd.
