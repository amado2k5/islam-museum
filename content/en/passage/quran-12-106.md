**Commentary on verse 106 (pp. 286-289)**

On "most of them do not believe in God except while associating others", al-Tabari says: most of those whom God described by "how many a sign in the heavens and the earth they pass by while turning away" do not acknowledge God as their Creator and Provider and the Creator of everything "except while associating" in their worship of idols and statues, their taking lords besides Him and their claim that He has a child, exalted be God above what they say. The interpreters said the same: report 19954, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Imran ibn Uyayna, Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "most of them do not believe in God", the verse, he said, their belief is that when they are asked who created the heaven, who created the earth and who created the mountains they say "God", while associating. Report 19955, from Ikrima, through Hannad, Abu al-Ahwas and Simak: you ask them who created them and who created the heavens and the earth and they say "God", and that is their belief in God while they worship others. Report 19956, from Amir and Ikrima, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Isra'il and Jabir: they know that He is their Lord and that He created them, and they associate others with Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 286}

Report 19957, from Amir and Ikrima, is similar, through Ibn Waki', his father, Isra'il and Jabir. Report 19958, from Ikrima, through Ibn Numayr and Nadr: part of their belief is that when asked who created the heavens they say God, and when asked who created them they say God, and yet they associate others with Him. Report 19959, from Ikrima, through Abu Nu'aym and al-Fadl ibn Yazid al-Thumali: it is God's word at [[passage/quran-31-25|31:25]] and [[passage/quran-39-38|39:38]] that they say "God" when asked who created the heavens and the earth; when they are asked about God and His attributes they describe Him otherwise than as He is, give Him a child and associate others with Him. The editor says al-Fadl ibn Yazid al-Thumali al-Bajali is a reliable Kufan and that the diminutive "al-Fudayl" in the manuscript and printed text is an error. Reports 19960 to 19963, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa, all from Ibn Abi Najih: their belief is their saying "God created us, provides for us and causes us to die"; in one of them: this is belief together with the shirk of worshipping others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 287}

Report 19964, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Hani ibn Sa'id, Abu Ma'wiya, Hajjaj and al-Qasim: they say "God is our Lord and He provides for us", and yet they associate others with Him. Report 19965, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: their belief is their saying "God created us, provides for us and causes us to die". Report 19966, from Ikrima, Mujahid and Amir, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Abu Tumayla, Abu Hamza and Jabir: there is no one who does not know that God created him and created the heavens and the earth; this is their belief, and they disbelieve in everything besides. Report 19967, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: in this belief of theirs, you meet none of them but he tells you that God is his Lord who created him and provided for him, and he is a polytheist in his worship. Report 19968, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: you do not ask any of the polytheists who his Lord is but he says "my Lord is God", and he associates others in that. Report 19969, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "most of them do not believe in God except while associating" means the Christians; and he cites the verses in which they say "God" when asked who created the heavens and the earth, who created them and who provides for them from heaven and earth, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 288}

while they nevertheless associate others with Him, worship others and prostrate to rivals besides Him. Report 19970, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Juwaybir: they used to associate others with Him in their talbiya. Report 19971, from Ata', through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr and Abd al-Malik: they know that God is their Lord and yet they associate others with Him. Report 19972, from Ata', through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Abd al-Malik: they know that God is their Creator and Provider, and they associate others with Him. Report 19973, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: no one worships another with God but he believes in God and knows that God is his Lord, his Creator and Provider, and yet he associates others; do you not see how Ibrahim spoke in Sura al-Shu'ara' [[passage/quran-26-75|26:75-77]], which shows he knew they worshipped the Lord of the worlds along with what they worshipped. He said: no one associates others with Him but he believes in Him. Do you not see how the Arabs gave their talbiya, saying that He has no partner except a partner who is His, whom He owns together with what he owns? The polytheists used to say this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 289}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
