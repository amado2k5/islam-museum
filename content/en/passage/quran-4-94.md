## Summary
Verse 94 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/raid-of-ibn-abi-hadrad-on-batn-idam|Raid of ibn Abi Hadrad on Batn Idam]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 94 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The opening of the verse (p. 70, notes 1-5).** Al-Tabari begins his commentary on [[passage/quran-4-94|4:94]]: the addressees are those who believe God and His messenger in what came to them from their Lord; striking in God's way means setting out to fight their enemies for God; investigating means taking care before killing someone whose case is unclear, whose Islam or unbelief is not known in truth, so they must not rush or proceed to kill anyone but those known with certainty to be at war with them, with God and with the Messenger; and the prohibition on denying faith is aimed at the one who surrendered to them and did not fight, showing himself to be of their community and call. The editor says the printed text had the reading found in our copies today, whereas al-Tabari's explanation follows the other reading, which he chose and which means surrender and submission, so he replaced it; that the manuscript has errors in two places; and he refers to earlier volumes for "God's way" and for the word of surrender. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 70}

**The rest of the explanation of the verse (p. 71, notes 1-5).** Al-Tabari continues on [[passage/quran-4-94|4:94]]: the prohibition is on killing one who offered submission in order to seek the goods of this world, since with God there is of His provision and abundant favours what is better for them if they obey Him in what He commanded and forbade, and He rewards them for it, so they should seek it from Him; his sense of the clause recalling their past is that they once concealed their religion, before God gave His religion strength through its followers and helpers, as the man they killed and robbed of his property concealed his religion from his people, fearing for his life; it is also said that the sense is that they were unbelievers like them, and that God's favour to them is giving His religion strength through many followers, or else His favour of repentance for having killed a man who had submitted to them. Then he commands them to verify, not to rush to kill one whose Islam is unclear, for God may have favoured him with Islam as He favoured them; and God is aware of their killing and of their holding back, a knowing observer, keeping it for them and for others until He repays all on the Day of Resurrection, the doer of good with his good and the wrongdoer with his wrong (this is al-Tabari's explanation). The editor refers the words for seeking, favouring and being aware to earlier volumes, notes that the printed text gave the reading found in our copies in two places, and that a sentence in the printed text is faulty and corrected from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 71}

**Occasion of revelation: the slain man who said he was Muslim (pp. 72-73, notes 1-7).** Al-Tabari reports that the verse came down about a man killed by a detachment of the Prophet after he said he was Muslim, or declared the testimony, or greeted them, for the sake of a flock or other property he had, which they took from him. Report 10211 (Ibn Waki', from Jarir, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from Nafi, from Ibn Umar): the Prophet sent Muhallim ibn Jaththama with a detachment; Amir ibn al-Adbat met them and greeted them with the greeting of Islam, there being an old grudge between them from the pre-Islamic period, and Muhallim shot him with an arrow and killed him. The news reached the Prophet, and Uyayna and al-Aqra' spoke about it; al-Aqra' said: set a custom today and change it tomorrow! Uyayna said: no, by God, until his women taste the bereavement my women tasted. Muhallim came in two cloaks to sit before the Prophet and ask forgiveness, and the Prophet said to him: may God not forgive you. He rose wiping his tears with his cloaks, and within seven days died; they buried him and the earth cast him out. They told the Prophet, who said that the earth takes in worse than your companion, but God wished to admonish you; they then threw him between two sides of a mountain and piled stones on him, and the verse came down. The editor says the printed text and the manuscript differ in several words here and he chose between them; that the grudge word is explained as rancour in the chest, the manuscript's variant coming from a root with the same sense, and recalls that at report 2195 in part 3 he noted al-Asma'i denying it and al-Azhari claiming it is not Arabic, which he takes as one more proof of its soundness, calling al-Azhari's claim worthless; that Ibn Kathir (2: 546) gives al-Aqra's saying in a plainly wrong form; that the manuscript has a copyist's corruption in Uyayna's words, corrected from context and Ibn Kathir; that the "side of a mountain" word means the flank facing you; and that Ibn Kathir (2: 546) and al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 200) give the report abridged. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 73}

**Report 10212 from Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad (pp. 73-75, notes 3-7 and the editor's long note).** Report 10212 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from Yazid ibn Abd Allah ibn Qusayt, from Abu al-Qa'qa' ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad al-Aslami, from his father): the Messenger of God sent us to Idam, and I went out with a group of Muslims including Abu Qatada al-Harith ibn Rib'i and Muhallim ibn Jaththama ibn Qays al-Laythi. When we were at the bottom of Idam, Amir ibn al-Adbat al-Ashja'i passed us on a young camel with some goods and a skin of milk, and greeted us with the greeting of Islam; we held back from him, but Muhallim, because of something between them, attacked him, killed him and took his camel and goods. When we came to the Messenger of God and told him, the Qur'an came down about us on this matter (see [[event/raid-of-ibn-abi-hadrad-on-batn-idam|the raid of Ibn Abi Hadrad on Batn Idam]]). The editor says Idam is a valley that runs across the Hijaz from Medina to the sea, belonging to Ashja' and Juhayna; the young camel is one whose back can bear riding; the goods word is a diminutive; the skin is a milk skin; and the printed text has an error in the chain, corrected from the manuscript. He traces the report to Ibn Hisham's Sira (4: 275), Ahmad's Musnad (6: 11), Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat, al-Tabari's History (3: 106), the Isti'ab, Usd al-Ghaba, Ibn Kathir (2: 545), Ibn Hajar's Isaba and al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 199), where al-Suyuti adds further collections. He then writes a long note on the confusion in its chain, gathering the forms in Ibn Hisham, Ahmad, al-Tabari's commentary and History, and Ibn Sa'd: Ibn Hisham and Ahmad have al-Qa'qa' ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad from his father; the commentary here has Abu al-Qa'qa'; the History adds "from his father" and Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad, with some saying "ibn al-Qa'qa'"; Ibn Sa'd has Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad in that place, whom he did not find in the biographical works; Ibn Abd al-Barr gives yet another name; Ibn Abi Hatim's al-Jarh wa al-Ta'dil has an Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hadrad who heard Abu Hurayra. He cites al-Bukhari's entry in al-Tarikh al-Kabir for al-Qa'qa' ibn Abi Hadrad, brother of Abd Allah, with the remark that "al-Qa'qa' ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad" is said but not sound; says Ibn Hajar erred in Ta'jil al-Manfa'a and corrected it in the Isaba; says he kept "Abu al-Qa'qa'" in the chain here though unattested in the books, because it agrees with the History and al-Tabari's remark "or ibn al-Qa'qa'" rules out a copyist's slip; and gives another chain in the History (3: 125) through Ibn Ishaq, Ya'qub ibn Utba and al-Zuhri to a son of Abd Allah ibn Abi Hadrad without naming him. He says he gathered all this because he found no one who covered it, and signs it with his name, Mahmud Muhammad Shakir (this is the editor's note; the museum gives no verdict on it). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 75}

**Reports 10213-10218 (pp. 74-76, notes).** Report 10213 (Harun ibn Idris al-Asamm, from al-Muharibi Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from Yazid ibn Abd Allah ibn Qusayt, from Ibn Abi Hadrad al-Aslami, from his father) is like the above, and the editor refers to his note on it; Harun and al-Muharibi are teachers of al-Tabari who appeared earlier. Report 10214 (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Uyayna, from Amr, from Ata) from Ibn Abbas: some Muslims caught up with a man with his small flock, who said "peace be upon you", and they killed him and took the flock, and the verse came down, the goods of this world being that flock. Report 10215 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Ata) is similar, and report 10216 (Sa'id ibn al-Rabi', from Sufyan, from Amr, from Ata) the same, absent from the manuscript. The editor explains the small flock as a diminutive of flock and refers to al-Tabari's remarks on the added letter in an earlier volume. Report 10217 (Abu Kurayb, from Abd al-Rahim ibn Sulayman, from Isra'il, from Simak, from Ikrima) from Ibn Abbas: a man of Banu Sulaym, with his flock, passed some companions of the Prophet and greeted them; they said he greeted only to seek refuge from them, killed him, took his flock and brought it to the Prophet, and the verse came down. Report 10218 (Ibn Waki', from Ubayd Allah, from Isra'il, from Simak, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet) is the same. The editor says Ahmad narrated it in the Musnad through several routes from Isra'il with a sound chain, and quotes Ibn Kathir (2: 544) that al-Tabari said in some of his books other than the commentary that the report has a sound chain in his view but might be weak in the view of others for reasons: no route from Simak is known but this one, there is a question about Ikrima's narrations, and the occasion of the verse is disputed, some saying Muhallim ibn Jaththama and some Usama ibn Zayd. Ibn Kathir replies that it is firmly reported from Simak by many leading scholars, that Ikrima is used as proof in the Sahih, and that it is narrated by another route from Ibn Abbas. The editor judges that the passage Ibn Kathir quotes comes from al-Tabari's Tahdhib al-Athar and notes it as the first such quotation he had seen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 76}

**Reports 10219-10221: Mirdas (pp. 76-78, notes).** Report 10219 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) from Ibn Abbas: a man would profess Islam, believe in God and the Messenger, and stay among his people; when a detachment of the Prophet came his clan was warned and fled, while he stayed unafraid of the believers because he shared their religion, until he met them and greeted them, and the believers denied his faith and killed him. The verse concerns their killing him to make his property lawful, which is seeking the goods of this world. He was a man named Mirdas, whose people fled from cavalry the Prophet had sent, led by a man of Banu Layth named Qulayb; Mirdas did not flee with them, and when he met them and greeted them they killed him. The Prophet ordered his blood-money for his family, returned his property and forbade the believers to do the like. The editor notes the disagreement over the spelling of Qulayb in the Isaba, and a copyist's corruption in both printed text and manuscript, corrected. Report 10220 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): this report concerns Mirdas, a man of Ghatafan; we were told the Prophet sent an army under Ghalib al-Laythi against the people of Fadak, among whom were some Ghatafan, Mirdas among them; his companions fled and Mirdas said: I am a believer and am not following you. The cavalry attacked at dawn, and when they met him Mirdas greeted them; some of the Prophet's companions killed him and took his goods, and God sent the verse about him, since the greeting of peace is the Muslims' greeting by which they recognise one another. The editor traces it to al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 200) and corrects readings in the manuscript and printed text. Report 10221 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): the Prophet sent a detachment under Usama ibn Zayd to Banu Damra, and they met a man of theirs called Mirdas ibn Nahik with his small flock and a red camel. Seeing them he took refuge in a mountain cave and Usama followed; Mirdas put his flock in it, came out to them, greeted them and made the declaration of faith, but Usama charged him and killed him for his camel and flock. When the Prophet sent Usama he liked to hear him praised and to ask his companions about him; on their return he did not ask, so the men told him that Usama had met a man who made the declaration and killed him, while he turned away from them; when they pressed him he raised his head to Usama and asked how he would answer for the declaration. Usama said: Messenger of God, he only said it to seek refuge (the report continues on the next page). The editor says the copyist dropped a clause of the verse as quoted in this report, and that the printed text and manuscript differ in two words in the preceding report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 78}


These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
