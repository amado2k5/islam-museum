## Summary
Verse 11 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 11 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 11 (pp. 418-424)**

Al-Tabari says, on "when He covered you with drowsiness as a security from Him, and sent down water on you from the sky to purify you with it and take from you the uncleanness of Satan, to strengthen your hearts and make your feet firm" [[passage/quran-8-11|8:11]] and "when your Lord revealed to the angels, I am with you, so make firm those who believe" [[passage/quran-8-12|8:12]]: "so that your hearts might be at rest, when He covered you with drowsiness"; by "He covered you with drowsiness" he means He cast drowsiness on you, "security" meaning security from God for you against your enemy overcoming you, and so drowsiness in war is a security from God. Report 15758, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud: drowsiness in fighting is a security from God, and in prayer it is from Satan; reports 15759 and 15760 are in the same sense. "Amana" is a verbal noun of "I was secure from such", "amana", "aman" and "amn", all of one sense. The interpreters said the like: reports 15761 and 15762, from Mujahid: security from God; report 15763, from Ibn Zayd: God sent down drowsiness as a security from the fear that struck them on the day of Uhud, and he recited [[passage/quran-3-154|3:154]]. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "covers", "drowsiness" and "security". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 418} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 419} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 420}

The reciters differed over the reading of "when He covered you with drowsiness": most reciters of Medina read it with damma on the first letter, a light shin and the accusative of "drowsiness", from "God covered them with drowsiness, He covers them"; most Kufan reciters read it with damma and a doubled shin, from "God overspread them with drowsiness"; some Meccans and Basrans read it with fatha on the first letter and "drowsiness" in the nominative, in the sense "drowsiness covered them", citing in support of their reading His words in Al Imran, "covered a party". Al-Tabari chooses: the better reading is the doubled shin of the Kufans, because all the reciters agree in reading "and He sent down water on you from the sky" as God's own act, so "He covered you" too ought to be, "He sent down" being joined to "covers", so that the speech runs on one pattern. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 421}

As for "and sent down water on you from the sky to purify you with it", it is rain God sent down from the sky on the day of Badr to purify the believers for their prayer, since they had woken that day in a state of major impurity without water; when God sent water on them they washed and purified themselves, and Satan had whispered to them what grieved them in their waking in impurity without water, and God took that from their hearts with the rain. That is His strengthening of their hearts, His fortifying their means, and His making their feet firm by that rain, since they had met their enemy on a soft sandy tract, which the rain packed until their feet stood firm on it and did not sink, a preparation by God for His prophet and his friends of the means of overcoming their enemy and winning against them. Reports to this effect followed one another from the companions of the Messenger of God and other people of knowledge. The editor says the printed edition had a word for the sand that has no root in the language, and the "maytha'" sand is the soft, easy kind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 421}

Report 15764, from Ali: a light rain fell on us in the night, that is the night before the morning of the battle of Badr, and we went under the trees and the shields, sheltering from the rain; the Messenger of God spent the night praying to his Lord, God, if this band is destroyed You will not be worshipped on earth; when dawn broke he called, prayer, servants of God, and the people came from under the trees and shields and the Messenger of God prayed with us and urged us to fight; reports 15765 to 15768, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and al-Sha'bi: a light rain on the day of Badr by which God made the feet firm; report 15769, from Qatada: it was mentioned to us that they were rained on that day until the valley ran with water and they fought on a reddish dune which God packed with water, and the Muslims drank and made ablution and watered their beasts, and God took from them Satan's whispering. The editor explains "tashsh" as a light rain above a drizzle and "hajaf" as the plural of a shield of hide, and identifies Harun ibn Ishaq al-Hamdani and Mus'ab ibn al-Miqdam as trustworthy and Isra'il, Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i and Haritha ibn Mudarrib al-Abdi as trustworthy Followers, saying the report is sound in chain and al-Suyuti traced it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 422} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 423}

Report 15770, from Ibn Abbas: the Prophet halted, when he marched to Badr, with a deep sandy tract between the Muslims and the water; the Muslims suffered severe weakness and Satan cast anger into their hearts, whispering among them, you claim you are friends of God and His Messenger is among you, and the polytheists have overcome you for the water while you pray in impurity; so God sent a heavy rain on them and the Muslims drank and purified themselves, God took Satan's uncleanness from them, and the sand held firm when the rain fell on it, and the people and beasts walked on it and marched to the enemy; God reinforced His prophet with a thousand angels, Gabriel with five hundred angels on one wing and Michael with five hundred on the other. Report 15771, from Ibn Abbas: when the polytheists of Quraysh went out to help the caravan and fight for it they halted at the water on the day of Badr and overcame the believers for it, so thirst struck the believers and they prayed in impurity and without ablution until it weighed heavily in the hearts of the companions of the Messenger of God; God sent water from the sky until the valley ran, and the Muslims drank, filled their skins, watered their mounts and washed from impurity, God making it a purification and making their feet firm, since there was a sandy tract between them and the people, on which God sent rain that beat it until it hardened and feet stood firm on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 423} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 424}

Report 15772, from al-Suddi: while the Messenger of God and the Muslims were marching, the polytheists got ahead of them to the water of Badr and halted by it, and Abu Sufyan and his companions turned toward the sea; they halted at the upper part of the valley and Muhammad at its lower part; a man of Muhammad's companions would become impure and be unable to find water and pray in impurity, and Satan cast into their hearts, saying, how do you hope to overcome them when one of you stands to pray impure and without ablution? So God sent rain on them and they washed, made ablution and drank, and the ground hardened for them, having been a pebbly bed in which their feet sank, and it hardened for them by the rain and they hardened on it. The editor notes that "da'sa" is a description in the reports not found in the dictionaries and that some reports have "dahsa", explains "mujannaba" as the squadron that takes one side of the army, and "bat'ha'" as soft earth carried down by floods. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 424}

We stop here at the foot of p. 424, after report 15772 in the commentary on 8:11; the next batch begins at p. 425. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 424}
