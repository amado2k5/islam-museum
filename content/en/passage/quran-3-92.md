## Summary
Verse 92 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 92 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 92: the sense of birr (p. 587).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: believers cannot reach birr, which is the birr from God that they seek from Him by obeying Him and worshipping Him and hope for from Him, namely His favour of admitting them to His garden and turning His punishment from them. For this reason many of the interpreters said birr is Paradise, because the Lord's birr to His servant in the hereafter is honouring him by admitting him to Paradise. Abu Kurayb relays from Waki, from Sharik, from Abu Ishaq, from Amr ibn Maymun that birr is Paradise (7386); al-Muthanna relays it from al-Himmani, from Sharik, from Abu Ishaq, from Amr ibn Maymun (7387); and Muhammad ibn al-Husayn relays from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that birr is Paradise (7388). The editor refers to the earlier commentary on birr in previous volumes and in a note removes a conjunction the printed text added without need. Al-Tabari says the interpretation is: believers cannot reach their Lord's Paradise before they give in charity of what they cherish and wish to keep, of your precious wealth. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that the sense is that you cannot reach your Lord's birr before you spend of what pleases you and what you desire of your wealth (7389); and Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr, from Abbad, from al-Hasan that it means from wealth (7390).

**God's knowledge of what is spent (p. 588).** Al-Tabari says the sense of the second half is: whatever you spend and give in charity from your wealth, God knows what the giver among you gives, spending it from what he loves of his wealth in God's way and otherwise, and has knowledge of all that, nothing of it hidden from Him, so that He repays its giver his recompense in the hereafter. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that it is kept safe for you and God knows it and is thankful for it (7391). The editor refers to the nearby discussion of the particle used in the sense of 'whatever'.

**What came from the Companions and the Successors (7392-7398).** Al-Tabari says that a group of the Companions and Successors interpreted this verse along the lines he gave. Muhammad ibn Amr relays from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that Umar ibn al-Khattab wrote to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari to buy him a slave girl from Jalula on the day the cities of Khosrow were conquered in the fighting under Sad ibn Abi Waqqas; Umar called for her and recited the verse [[passage/quran-3-92|3:92]] and set her free; and it is like what is in the sura of al-Insan [[passage/quran-76-8|76:8]] and the sura of al-Hashr [[passage/quran-59-9|59:9]] (7392), and al-Muthanna relays it from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid the same (7393). Ibn Bashshar relays from Ibn Abi Adi, from Humayd, from Anas ibn Malik that when this verse came down, or the verse of the goodly loan [[passage/quran-2-245|2:245]] and [[passage/quran-57-11|57:11]], Abu Talha said: Messenger of God, my garden in such and such a place is charity, and if I could keep it secret I would not make it public; and the Prophet said: place it among the poor of your family (7394). In a note the editor says that Humayd is Ibn Abi Humayd al-Tawil; that the report was transmitted by Ahmad in the Musnad (12170 from Yahya ibn Said al-Qattan, 12809 from Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Ansari and 12803 from Abd Allah ibn Bakr, all three from Humayd from Anas); that al-Tirmidhi has it by the route of Abd Allah ibn Bakr from Humayd and called it a good sound report; that al-Suyuti mentions it and adds Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Mardawayh; that it is an abridgement of a long report which Malik relayed in the Muwatta from Ishaq ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Talha from Anas, and which Ahmad, al-Bukhari and Muslim also have by the route of Malik; and that the 'garden' is a palm orchard when it has a wall. Al-Muthanna relays from al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, from Hammad, from Thabit, from Anas that when it came down Abu Talha said: Messenger of God, God asks us for our wealth; I call you to witness that I have made my land at Ariha God's; and the Prophet said: put it among your kin; and he divided it between Hassan ibn Thabit and Ubayy ibn Ka'b (7395).

**The editor's note on the place name.** In a long note the editor says that Hammad is Ibn Salama; that Ahmad, Muslim (by the route of Bahz) and Abu Dawud transmitted the report and al-Suyuti, who mentions it, adds al-Nasa'i; that the word 'at Ariha' stands so in al-Tabari's report and is neither a misreading nor a copyist's slip, since it is established in Abu Dawud's report, and al-Hafiz says in al-Fath that it is established with this spelling in Abu Dawud's version through Hammad ibn Salama, whereas Muslim's version has another name over whose vowelling there is much disagreement. He quotes the text of al-Qadi Iyad in Mashariq al-Anwar and then that of al-Hafiz in al-Fath, which say that the narrators differed over this name and its vowelling, with the open, closed and lowered vowels and the long and short endings; that the Andalusian and Maghribi transmission has a closed vowel on the r; that they said it is a compound of 'well' added to 'Ha', and Abu Ubayd al-Bakri said that 'Ha' is a place in Medina facing the mosque to which the name is ascribed; that Abu al-Walid al-Baji rejected the closed vowel and said the r is open in every case; that its place is south of the mosque and is known as the fort of Banu Hudayla; that Hammad ibn Salama's transmission has another form fixed by the teachers; that al-Hafiz quotes Ibn al-Athir in al-Nihaya for eight forms of the name and says that al-Baji judged the most eloquent to be with an open b, a silent y and an open r with a short ending, which al-Saffani affirmed, saying it is of the pattern fay'ala from baraah; and that whoever thought it a well among the wells of Medina has misread it, and that Abu Dawud's report 'I made my land at Ariha' shows it is not a well. He refers to al-Fath and to Mu'jam al-Buldan.

**The report of Abu Dharr and the report of Zayd (7396-7398).** Imran ibn Musa relays from Abd al-Warith, from Layth, from Maymun ibn Mihran that a man asked Abu Dharr which deeds are best, and he said: prayer is the pillar of Islam, jihad is the summit of deed, and charity is something wondrous; the man said: you have left something that is the most trusted of my deeds in my own mind, and I do not see that you mentioned it; he said: what is it; he said: fasting; he said: it is a means of nearness, but it is not that; and he recited this verse (7396). The editor says the report has a broken chain because Maymun ibn Mihran did not meet Abu Dharr: Abu Dharr died in the year 32 and Maymun was born in 40 and died in 118, as in the histories of al-Bukhari and in Tahdhib al-Kamal; that al-Suyuti mentions it without attributing it to anyone other than al-Tabari; and that he kept 'ajab' from the manuscript, where the printed text and al-Durr al-Manthur have 'ajib'. Yunus relays from Ibn Wahb, from Dawud ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Makki, from Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Husayn, from Amr ibn Dinar that when this verse came down Zayd brought a horse of his called Sabal to the Prophet and said: give this in charity, Messenger of God; the Prophet gave it to Zayd's son Usama ibn Zayd ibn Haritha, and Zayd said: I only wanted to give it in charity; and the Prophet said: your charity has been accepted (7397). The editor says it is a mursal report, since Amr ibn Dinar is a Successor; that Dawud ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Attar al-Makki is trustworthy, one of al-Shafi'i's teachers, whom Ibn Main, Abu Dawud and others held reliable; that Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Husayn al-Makki al-Nawfali is trustworthy and the compilers of the six books relayed from him; that al-Suyuti alluded to the report without attributing it to anyone but al-Tabari and mentioned before it a similar report from Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir, also mursal, attributed to Said ibn Mansur, Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim; and that the name of the horse has an open s and b and was not pointed in the manuscript. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Ayyub and another that when it came down Zayd ibn Haritha brought a horse he loved and said: Messenger of God, this is for God's way; the Prophet mounted Usama ibn Zayd on it, and Zayd seemed to feel something in himself; when the Prophet saw that from him he said: God has accepted it (7398). The editor says it is a mursal report like the one before and that al-Suyuti mentions it and attributes it to Abd al-Razzaq and al-Tabari. (These are reports al-Tabari transmits, and the museum presents them as they were reported, without a verdict.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 592}
