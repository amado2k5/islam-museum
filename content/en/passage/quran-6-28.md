## Summary
Verse 28 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 28 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "no, what they used to hide before has become clear to them; and if they were sent back they would return to what they were forbidden, and they are liars" al-Tabari says: it is not sorrow and regret at leaving belief in God and trust in you, Muhammad, that is in these who hold others equal to their Lord and reject your prophethood, in their saying when made to stand before the Fire "if only we were sent back and not deny the signs of our Lord and be among the believers", but fear of what is coming to them of God's punishment and painful torment for the sins they used to hide from people's eyes and conceal from them, which God brought out of them on the day of resurrection and showed before the witnesses, exposing them by them and then repaying them their due. He says: what they used to hide of their bad deeds, hidden before in the world, has become clear; "and if they were sent back" to the world and given respite "they would return to what they were forbidden", that is, go back to the like of the deeds they did in the world before, rejecting God's signs, disbelieving in him and doing what angers their Lord, "and they are liars" in their saying "if we were sent back we would not deny the signs of our Lord and would be among the believers", for they said it when they said it for fear of the punishment, not from belief in God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 321}

The interpreters said so: report 13181, from al-Suddi: their deeds in the next world, which they hid in the world, became plain to them. 13182, from Qatada: "what they used to hide before" is some of their deeds. 13183, from Qatada: had God joined them a world like theirs, they would return to their deeds, deeds of evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 322}

The editor notes that the printed text has "what these intended", which is nothing, while the manuscript has "what these who hold equal", so he inferred the right reading from what follows, "but fear is in them", and the sense: it is not in these who hold equal... sorrow and regret.

Verse 28 closes here at p. 322, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-29|6:29]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
