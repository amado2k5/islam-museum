**Commentary on verse 71 (pp. 347-348)**

Al-Tabari says: as for the believers, men and women, who affirm God, His Messenger and the verses of His book, their description is that they are supporters and helpers of one another; "they command the right" means they command people to believe in God, His Messenger and what he brought from God; "and forbid the wrong"; "and establish prayer" means they perform the obligatory prayer; "and give the zakat" means they give the obligatory zakat to those entitled to it; "and obey God and His Messenger", so they follow the command of God and His Messenger and abstain from what they forbade; "those, God will have mercy on them" means these, with this description, are the ones on whom God will have mercy, rescuing them from His punishment and admitting them to His Garden, not the people of hypocrisy and of denial of God and His Messenger, who forbid the right and command the wrong and close their hands from paying God's due from their wealth; "God is mighty, wise" means He is possessed of might in His vengeance on whoever He takes vengeance on among His creatures for disobedience and unbelief, no one preventing Him from it or helping the person against Him, and wise in His vengeance on them and in all His acts. The editor notes that the bracketed words are the rest of the verse, which he added as the copyist had left them out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 347}

Report 16938, from Abu al-Aliya, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father and al-Rabi' ibn Anas: wherever God mentions in the Qur'an commanding the right and forbidding the wrong, commanding the right is calling from polytheism to Islam, and forbidding the wrong is forbidding the worship of idols and devils. Report 16939, from Ibn Abbas, through Abdallah, Mu'awiya and Ali: "they establish prayer" he said the five prayers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 348}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
