## Summary
Verse 256 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 256 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens ([[passage/quran-2-256|2:256]]) on p. 407 by saying the interpreters differed on its meaning. Some said it came down about some of the Ansar, or one of them, whose children they had made Jews or Christians; when Islam came they wished to force them to it and God forbade it, so that the children themselves choose to enter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 407} From Ibn Abbas (5812), via Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a woman whose children did not live would vow that if a child lived she would make him a Jew; when the Banu al-Nadir were expelled there were among them sons of the Ansar, who said we will not leave our sons, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 408} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr (5813), to like effect, adding that whoever wished to stay stayed and whoever wished to go went; in the note the editor says Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Hibban and Ibn Abi Hatim transmitted it and that this last sentence is Sa'id's, as in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 408} From Amir al-Sha'bi (5814): a woman of the Ansar whose children did not live would vow that if hers lived she would place him with the People of the Book in their religion; when Islam came some Ansar sons were still in their religion and their families said they would force them, and the verse came down; what decided between those who chose Judaism and those who chose Islam was that whoever joined them chose Judaism and whoever stayed chose Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 409} From him (5815, 5816) to like effect: the dividing event was the Prophet's expulsion of the Banu al-Nadir, those who were Jews and had not accepted Islam joined them and those who had accepted stayed; in one version they went to Khaybar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 409}

From Ibn Abbas (5817): it came down about a man of the Ansar of the Banu Salim ibn Awf called al-Husayn, a Muslim with two Christian sons, who asked the Prophet whether he should force them since they refused anything but Christianity, and the verse was sent down; the editor refers to Ibn Hajar's inquiry into the name of this Companion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 409} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr (5818), when Abu Bishr asked him: it came down about the Ansar specifically; a woman in the Jahiliyya would vow that if she bore a child she would place him among the Jews; when the Nadir were expelled they said, our sons and brothers are among them, and the Prophet was silent, then the verse came down, and the Prophet said, your companions have been given the choice: if they choose you they are yours and if they choose them they are theirs; so they were expelled with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 409} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 410}

From al-Suddi (5819): it came down about a man of the Ansar called Abu al-Husayn with two sons; traders came from Syria to Medina carrying olive oil, and when they had sold and wished to return the two sons called them to Christianity, became Christian and went back to Syria with them; their father came to the Prophet and asked whether he should pursue them; the Prophet said the matter stood on the rule against compulsion in the religion, since fighting the People of the Book had not yet been commanded, and said, God keep them far, they are the first to disbelieve; Abu al-Husayn felt resentment against the Prophet for not sending after them, and the passage in ([[passage/quran-4-65|4:65]]) was sent down. Then, in al-Suddi's account, the ruling was abrogated and fighting the People of the Book was commanded in the chapter of Repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 410} In the note the editor says Abu Ja'far did not mention this report in his commentary on the chapter of Women, and that this shows the commentary was abridged, as they transmitted from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 410} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 411} This is al-Suddi's account, with the claim of abrogation reported as transmitted, and the museum does not rule on it.

From Mujahid (5820): the Jews of the Banu al-Nadir had suckled men of the Aws; when the Prophet ordered their expulsion, their foster-sons of the Aws said we will go with them and follow their religion, and their families prevented them and forced them to Islam, and the verse came down about them. From him (5821): some of the Ansar were put out to nurse among the Banu Qurayza and their families wished to force them to Islam, and the verse came down. From Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid (5822), like the report of 5820, with Abd al-Karim reporting from Mujahid that the sons of the Aws had taken the religion of the Nadir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 411} In the notes the editor discusses readings of wording in the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 411} We stop before report 5823, which is cut at the page break.
