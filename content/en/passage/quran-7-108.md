**Commentary on verse 108 (pp. 17-18)**

Al-Tabari says "he drew out his hand and it was white to the beholders" means he brought out his hand, and it was white, shining to whoever looked at it. Moses was, it is reported, a dark-skinned man, and God made his hand turn white without leprosy as a sign for him and a proof of the truth of his saying that he was a messenger from the Lord of the worlds. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "drew out". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 17}

Report 14918, from Ibn Abbas through al-Abbas, Yazid, al-Asbagh ibn Zayd, al-Qasim ibn Abi Ayyub and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he took out his hand from his collar and saw it white, without harm, meaning without leprosy; he put it back in his sleeve and it returned to its first colour. Report 14919, from Ibn Abbas through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: without leprosy. Reports 14920 and 14921, from Mujahid: he drew his hand from his collar white without leprosy. Report 14922, from al-Suddi: he took it out of his collar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 17}

Report 14923, from Mujahid through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Sa'd: Moses was a dark man, and he took out his hand and it was whiter than milk, without leprosy, a sign for Pharaoh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 18}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
