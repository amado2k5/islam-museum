## Summary
Verse 203 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 203 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and remember God in counted days": al-Tabari says it means remember God with affirmation of His oneness and glorification in numbered days, which are the days of throwing the pebbles at the jamras, when He commanded His servants to say the takbir after the prayers and at the throwing with each pebble thrown at a jamra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 208} The interpreters said the same. From Ibn 'Abbas by several routes: the days of tashriq, which are three days after the sacrifice; in 'Amr ibn Dinar's report he heard him on the day of departure after leaving saying the takbir in the mosque, interpreting the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 208} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 209} From 'Ata', Mujahid (the days of tashriq at Mina), Ibrahim, Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, Qatada (we used to be told they were the days of tashriq), al-Suddi and al-Rabi': they are the days of tashriq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 209} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 210} From al-Hasan: the days after the sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 210} From Malik: the counted days are three days after the sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 210} The editor identifies Muhammad ibn Nafi' al-Basri and Ghundar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 208}

From al-Dahhak: the three days of tashriq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 210} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 211} From 'Amr ibn Abi Salama: I asked Ibn Zayd about the counted days and the known days, and he said the counted days are the days of tashriq, and the known days are the day of 'Arafa, the day of sacrifice and the days of tashriq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 211}

Al-Tabari says he holds that the counted days are the days of Mina and of throwing the pebbles because of the many reports from the Messenger of God that he said of them that they are days of remembering God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 211} From Abu Hurayra from the Messenger of God: the days of tashriq are days of eating and remembrance; and from him that the Messenger of God sent 'Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa to go about Mina saying, do not fast these days, for they are days of eating, drinking and remembrance of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 211} From 'A'isha from the Messenger of God: these days are days of eating, drinking and remembrance of God; and from her that he forbade fasting on the days of tashriq and said they are days of eating, drinking and remembrance of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 212} From 'Amr ibn Dinar that the Messenger of God sent Bishr ibn Suhaym, who proclaimed the same in the days of tashriq; from al-Zuhri that he sent 'Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa ibn Qays, who proclaimed that these are days of eating, drinking and remembrance of God, except for whoever owes a fast in place of a sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 212} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 213} From the mother of Mas'ud ibn al-Hakam al-Zuraqi: it is as if I see 'Ali on the Messenger of God's white mule when he stood at the Ansar's pass saying, people, these are not days of fasting, they are days of eating, drinking and remembrance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 213} The editor sets out the standing of the narrators and the sources for these reports in Ahmad, al-Tahawi, Ibn Majah and al-Hakim, and notes that 'Amr ibn Dinar's report is mursal but comes connected by other routes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 211} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 212} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 213}

Al-Tabari then raises an objection: the Prophet did not say that the days of Mina are the counted days that God mentioned, so why not say that by "remembrance of God" he meant the known days? He answers, as his argument, that this is not possible, because God did not make obligatory in the known days the remembrance He made obligatory in the counted days; He described the known days as days in which God's name is pronounced over the grazing livestock, in the surah of al-Hajj (22:28); so when the Prophet said "remembrance of God" without condition or link to pronouncing it over livestock, it is known that he meant the remembrance God made unconditionally obligatory in the counted days. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 214} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 215}

On "whoever hastens in two days, there is no sin on him, and whoever delays, there is no sin on him, for whoever fears God": al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said it means whoever hastens in two of the days of tashriq and departs on the second day, there is no sin in his departing, and whoever delays to the third day, there is no sin in his delaying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 215} From 'Ata': no sin in his hastening and no sin in his delaying; al-Hasan and 'Ikrima the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 215}

From Mujahid: "whoever hastens in two days" is the day of departure, "no sin on him" means no hardship on him; from al-Suddi: whoever departs in two days there is no blame on him, and whoever delays and departs on the third there is no blame on him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 216} From Qatada: whoever is overtaken by night at Mina on the second day before departing may not depart until the sun passes noon the next day; God allowed them to depart in two of the days if they wish, and whoever delays to the third day has no sin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 216} From Ibrahim: no sin on whoever hastens and none on whoever delays, and this is about hastening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 216} From Ibn 'Umar: departure in two days is lawful for whoever fears God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 216} From Ibn 'Abbas: no sin in his hastening and no sin in his delaying; in the report of 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: whoever departs from Mina in two days after the sacrifice has no sin, and whoever delays has no hardship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 217} From 'Ata', when Ibn Jurayj asked whether a Meccan may depart in the first departure: yes, God said whoever hastens in two days, there is no sin on him, and it is for all people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 217} The editor corrects the chain of report 3931. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 217}

Others said the meaning is that whoever hastens in two days is forgiven and has no sin, and whoever delays likewise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 217} From 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud: he has no sin; in another report: that is, he is forgiven, and whoever delays is forgiven; in another: he is free of sin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 217} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 218} From Ibn 'Umar: he returned forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 218} From Mujahid: he has been forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 219} From Ibn 'Abbas: he has been forgiven; they interpret it other than its meaning, for 'umra expiates the sins that go with it, so how much more the hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 219} From Ibrahim and 'Amir al-Sha'bi: he is forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 219} From Ibn Mas'ud: he has come out of all sin, and whoever delays is free of all sin, this being at the return from the hajj; and from 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, relayed by Ibn Jurayj from a man from 'Ata': he is forgiven, and whoever delays is forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 219} From Mu'awiya ibn Qurra: he comes out of his sins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 219} The editor doubts the name Aswad ibn Sawada al-Qattan and thinks it is Sawada ibn Abi al-Aswad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 219}

Others said the meaning is no sin between him and the year after. From Mujahid, when Ishaq ibn Yahya ibn Talha asked him: for the one in the hajj, there is no sin on him until the hajj of the next year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 220} Others said the meaning is no sin on him if he fears God in the rest of his life; from Abu al-'Aliya: all his sin is gone if he fears God in what remains, and Ibrahim the same; from Ibn Zayd: for whoever fears God, as a condition; from al-Suddi: no blame on him for whoever fears God, and Ibn 'Abbas used to say I wish I were among those who earn the name of God-fearing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 220} From Ibn Jurayj: in Ibn Mas'ud's codex it reads "for whoever fears God"; from Ibn 'Abbas: for whoever avoids God's disobediences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 221}

Others said the meaning is that whoever hastens in two of the days of tashriq has no hardship in hastening his departure if he avoids killing game until the third day passes, and whoever delays to the third has no hardship; from Muhammad ibn Abi Salih: for whoever avoids taking any game until the third day passes; from Ibn 'Abbas: it is not lawful for him to kill game until the days of tashriq are over. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 221} Others said the meaning is that there is no sin on him, that is, he is forgiven, if he fears God in his hajj by avoiding what God forbade; from Qatada: for whoever fears God in his hajj, and it is said that Ibn Mas'ud used to say, whoever fears God in his hajj is forgiven his earlier sin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 221} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 222}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: the soundest of these views is that of those who said the verse means: whoever hastens in two of the three days of Mina and departs on the second day has no sin, because God has removed his sins if he feared God in his hajj, avoiding what God ordered him to avoid and doing what God ordered him to do, and whoever delays to the third day has no sin, because God has expiated his past sins, if he feared God in his hajj by performing it within its limits. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 222} His argument is the many reports from the Messenger of God that whoever performs hajj to this House without rafath or fusuq comes out of his sins as on the day his mother bore him, and that he said, follow the hajj with the 'umra, for they drive out sins as the bellows drives out the dross of iron, gold and silver. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 222} From Ibn Mas'ud from the Prophet: follow the hajj with the 'umra, for they drive out poverty and sins as the bellows drives out the dross of iron, gold and silver, and an accepted hajj has no reward short of Paradise; Zirr ibn Hubaysh reports it from him too. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 223} From 'Umar to the same effect, with 'Asim ibn 'Ubayd Allah, weak in the editor's view, in its chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 223} From Ibn 'Abbas from the Prophet: when you complete your hajj you are as your mother bore you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 223} The editor explains the bellows as the skin the smith blows into and the dross as what the bellows drives out of iron, and notes that Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i reported the hadith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 223}

Al-Tabari says these and similar reports, too long to cite, show that whoever performs hajj and fulfils it within its limits comes out of his sins, and they make clear that "no sin on him" means he has come out of his sins, his wrongs removed, and that there is no sense in the view that it means no hardship in departing on the second day and none in staying to the third. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 224} For hardship is lifted from a doer only where leaving the act was required and he is allowed to do it, or where doing it was required and he is allowed to leave it; but for what the doer must do, there is no point in lifting hardship from him if he does it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 224} The editor comments on al-Tabari's use of "harijan" in the sense of sinful, which the lexicographers reject in favour of "harij". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 224}
