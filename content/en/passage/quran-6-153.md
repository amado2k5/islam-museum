## Summary
Verse 153 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 153 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then al-Tabari says on "this is My straight path, so follow it, and do not follow other ways": God says that what your Lord enjoined on you, people, in the two preceding verses and commanded you to fulfil is "His path", meaning His way and the religion He approved for His servants; "straight" means upright, with no deviation from the truth; "follow it" means act on it and make it a course for yourselves to walk; and "do not follow the ways" means do not walk any road but this, do not ride any other course, and do not seek any other religion, whether Judaism, Christianity, Magianism, idol worship or other creeds, for they are innovations and errors. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "the straight path" and "following", and notes that the manuscript's reading "a religion other than it" is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 228}

"And you be scattered from His path" means that following the newly made ways, which are not God's ways, roads or religions, would scatter you away from His way and the religion He prescribed and approved, which is Islam, with which the prophets were charged and which the nations before you were commanded. "That is what He has enjoined on you, that you may be mindful of God" means: that you fear God regarding your own selves so you do not destroy them, and beware your Lord about them so you do not anger Him against them and bring on yourselves His vengeance and punishment. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "way", "enjoining" and "fearing God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 229}

He gives (14163), (14164) and (14165) from Mujahid that "the ways" are innovations and doubtful matters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 229}

He gives (14166) from Ibn Abbas, on this verse and on [[passage/quran-42-13|42:13]], that God told believers to keep to the community, forbade them dispute and division, and told them that those before them perished only through argument and quarrels in God's religion; (14167) from him that it means do not follow the misguidances; (14168) from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud that the Prophet drew a line for them one day and said this is God's path, then drew lines to its right and left and said these are ways, on each of which is a devil calling to it, then recited this verse, and the editor notes that the chain is sound, that Ahmad gave it in his Musnad and that Ibn Kathir discusses its chain at length; and (14169) from Ibn Zayd that His path is Islam and His way is Islam, and that He forbade following other ways. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 230}

He gives (14170) that a man asked Ibn Mas'ud what the straight path is, and he said: Muhammad left us at its lowest end and its other end is in Paradise; to its right and left are tracks, and there are men calling those who pass; whoever takes those tracks, they end in the Fire, and whoever takes the path, it ends in Paradise; then he recited the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 231}

Then he says the readers differ in reading "and that this is My path". Most readers of Medina and Basra and some Kufans read "wa anna" with fatha on the alif and doubled nun, joined to what precedes: say, come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you, that you set up no partner with Him, and that this is My straight path. Most Kufan readers read "wa inna" with kasra, as a fresh beginning cut off from the first, since the speech had ended, in their view, with the report of the bequest God gave His servants. Al-Tabari chooses that both are widespread readings in the lands and among the Muslims at large, with both meanings sound, so whichever one reads is right; for God commanded following His path just as He commanded His prophets; and if someone includes this in what God told His Prophet to say to the idolaters, he opens "anna" accordingly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 231}

If the reader opens "anna" with fatha because recitation is a kind of saying even without the word "say", given its distance from "I will recite" which he intends to govern it, he is right; and if he breaks it with kasra as a fresh start cut off from the first, since what the Prophet was commanded to recite to those he was commanded to recite it to has ended before this, he is right. Abdullah ibn Abi Ishaq al-Basri read "wa an" with fatha on the alif and a light nun, meaning: say, come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you, that you set up no partner, and that this is My path, lightening it because the "an" in "that you set up no partner" is light and this one is joined to it. Al-Tabari says that although this is a defensible way, he does not like reading it so, because it is anomalous against the reading of the readers of the lands and against what they hold in their cities. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 232}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
