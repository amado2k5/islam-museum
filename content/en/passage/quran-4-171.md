**Commentary on verse 171: exaggeration in religion, and Jesus as God's messenger and word (pp. 415-419)**

Al-Tabari says that 'O People of the Book' means the People of the Gospel, the Christians: do not exceed the limits in your religion, that is, do not go beyond the truth in it and do not fall short; do not say about Jesus other than the truth. Your saying that he is God's son is saying about God what is untrue, since God has not taken a son. The root sense of exaggeration is passing a limit in anything. Report 10853, from al-Rabi': they became two groups: one exaggerated in the religion, and their exaggeration was doubt in it and turning away from it; another fell short of it and so transgressed the command of their Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 417}

Al-Tabari says that 'the Messiah Jesus son of Mary is only God's messenger' means that the Messiah, O exaggerators among the People of the Book, is not the son of God as you claim, but Jesus son of Mary, who has no lineage other than that; God described him as His messenger sent with the truth to those to whom He sent him. The root of 'Messiah' is the one wiped, changed from the passive form to the form fa'il; God named him so for cleansing him from sins and blemishes; hence Mujahid said the Messiah is the upright one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 417}

Al-Tabari rejects the view that the word is Hebrew or Syriac and was Arabized like the names Isma'il, Isaac, Moses and Jesus, since those are names and not attributes, while 'Messiah' is an attribute, and the Arabs are not addressed in the description of a thing except in terms they understand. He adds that 'the false Messiah' too means the one wiped, of the eye: the Messiah as applied to Jesus is the one wiped of body from blemishes and sins, while as applied to the Dajjal it is the one wiped of the right or left eye, as reported from the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 418}

On 'His word which He cast to Mary', al-Tabari says the word is the message that God commanded His angels to bring to Mary as good news from Him, as in the verse that tells of the angels' good news to Mary. Report 10854, from Qatada: it is His saying 'be', and it was. 'Cast to' means informed her of it and told her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 419}

On 'and a spirit from Him', the learned differ: some say it is a breath from Him, since it is related that Gabriel breathed into Mary's garment by God's command, so he is ascribed to be a spirit from God because it was by His command; and the breath is called a spirit because it is wind that comes out of the spirit; they cite a line of Dhu al-Rumma describing a fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 419}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
