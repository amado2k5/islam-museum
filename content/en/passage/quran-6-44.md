## Summary
Verse 44 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 44 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

By "when they forgot what they were reminded of, we opened to them the gates of everything, until when they rejoiced in what they were given, we seized them suddenly, and they despaired" al-Tabari means: when they left off acting on what we commanded them through our messengers' tongues. Report 13226, from Ibn Abbas: "they forgot what they were reminded of" means left off what they were reminded of. 13227, from Ibn Jurayj: what God and his messengers called them to, they refused and threw back at them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 357}

The editor refers for "forgetting" and "reminding" to earlier volumes 6, 5, 2 and 10.

"We opened to them the gates of everything" means we changed hardship to ease and breadth of livelihood, and affliction to health and soundness of body, as a gradual leading on of them. 13228, from Mujahid: the ease and comfort of the world on the earlier generations. 13229, from Qatada: ease and breadth of provision. 13230, from al-Suddi: of provision. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 358}

If someone asks how it was said "we opened to them the gates of everything", when you know the door of mercy and the door of repentance were not opened to them, nor many other doors, the answer is that the sense is not as you thought, but: we opened to them, by way of leading them on, the gates of all that we had shut on them when we seized them with hardship and affliction that they might humble themselves, since they did not and left God's command, for the end of this saying turns back on its beginning. That is as he says in [[passage/quran-7-94|7:94]] and [[passage/quran-7-95|95]], "we sent to no town a prophet but we seized its people with hardship and affliction that they might humble themselves; then we changed the bad to good until they multiplied and said: hardship and ease touched our fathers too; so we seized them suddenly while they did not perceive". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 358}

The editor notes that the printed text and manuscript have "that the door of mercy and the door of repentance were not opened to them and many other doors" but the manuscript has only "other doors" without the "and", so he judged that what he supplied had fallen out.

God's opening to the people of whom he said in this verse that they forgot what they were reminded, in "when they forgot what they were reminded of, we opened to them the gates of everything", is his changing for them the bad state they were in while he tried them, from narrow living to ease and breadth, and from bodily harm to health and well-being; it is the opening of the gates of everything whose gate had been shut on them, of what was mentioned before "we opened to them the gates of everything". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 359}

By "until when they rejoiced in what they were given" he means until these who denied their messengers rejoiced in our opening to them the doors of breadth in living and health in bodies. 13231, from al-Suddi: "in what they were given" of provision. 13232, from Hammad ibn Zayd: a man used to invoke mercy on whoever recited this verse and then reflected on what is meant by it, "until when they rejoiced in what they were given, we seized them suddenly". 13233, from Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak, from Muhammad ibn al-Nadr al-Harithi, on... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 359}

The rest of 13233: on "we seized them suddenly" he said: they were given respite twenty years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 360}

The editor says he did not know "Ibn Abi Raja'", that the printed text has "of the people of the frontier" and dropped "a man", and that he kept the manuscript; he identifies Muhammad ibn al-Nadr al-Harithi, Abu Abd al-Rahman, the devout, and says Abu Nu'aym related it in the Hilya through Muhammad ibn Munabbih, Ibn al-Mubarak's sister's son, so that he fears "Ibn Abi Raja'" is Muhammad ibn Munabbih.

By "we seized them suddenly" he means we brought them punishment all at once while they were off guard, not perceiving that it would come or was upon them. Report 13234, from Ibn Jurayj: "until when they rejoiced in what they were given, we seized them suddenly", he said: at the time it was most pleasing to them and most deceiving. 13235, from al-Suddi: the punishment seized them suddenly. 13236, from Mujahid: all at once, while secure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 360}

As for "and they were despairing", they are perishing, their pleas cut off, regretting their earlier denial of their messengers. 13237, from al-Suddi: they are destroyed and their state changed. 13238, from Mujahid: dejection. 13239, from Ibn Zayd: the "mublis" is the one on whom has come an evil he cannot ward off, and he is worse than the submissive, and he recited [[passage/quran-23-76|23:76]], "they did not submit to their Lord nor humble themselves"; the first time there was reproach and sparing, and he recited "we seized them with hardship and affliction that they might humble themselves", "why did they not humble themselves when our might came on them?" up to "Satan made what they did seem fair to them", then there came a command with no sparing in it, and he recited "until when they rejoiced in what they were given we seized them suddenly and they despaired", a command with no sparing; had they humbled themselves at first it would have been lifted from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 361}

The editor notes that the printed text has "they are destroyed" in 13238 and he does not know whence it came, the manuscript being undotted so he preferred his reading, that "iblas" means grief and regret, and corrects "baqiyya" from "laqiyya"/"taqiyya", its sense being sparing them.

Reports 13240 and 13241, from Uqba ibn Amir, from the Prophet: when you see God giving his servant in his world what he asks despite his disobeying him, that is only a leading on from him; then he recited "when they forgot what they were reminded of, we opened to them the gates of everything" to "praise be to God, Lord of the worlds". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 361}

In his notes the editor identifies Sa'id ibn Amr al-Sakuni; Baqiyya ibn al-Walid al-Himsi, trusted but criticised for concealment; Dubara ibn Malik, Abu Shurayh al-Himsi, with what Ibn Hibban and Ibn Adi said of him; Abu al-Salt al-Shami, whom he found no mention of; Harmala; Uqba ibn Muslim al-Tujibi al-Misri, a trusted Follower; and Uqba ibn Amir al-Juhani, the Companion of early emigration, a jurist and scholar, one of those who collected the Qur'an; and notes that Ahmad related it in the Musnad by another route, and that al-Haythami, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti traced it.

The root of "iblas" in Arab speech is, for some, grief and regret over a thing, for others the cutting off of an argument and silence when it is cut off, and for others submission; they said it is the one abandoned and left, from which comes al-Ajjaj's line in which one asks about Rustam and is told he knows him and he was "ublisa". So the sense of "ublisa" for those who said "iblas" is the cutting off of an argument and silence is that he gave no answer; others took it as submission and his people leaving him fixed in his place; others as grief and regret. One says "ablasa al-rajul iblasan", and from it Iblis was called "Iblis". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 362}

Verse 44 closes here at p. 363, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-45|6:45]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
