## Summary
Verse 68 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/badr-prisoners|Badr Prisoners]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 68 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 68 (pp. 64-69)**

Al-Tabari says: God says to the people of Badr who took booty and took ransom from the captives, "were it not for a writing from God that had gone before", meaning were it not for a decree of God that had gone before for you, people of Badr, in the Preserved Tablet, that God would make booty lawful for you, and that God had decreed among His decrees that He does not lead a people astray after guiding them until He makes clear to them what to guard against, and that He does not punish anyone who attended the gathering you attended at Badr with the Messenger of God, helping God's religion, "there would have touched you" from God for your taking of booty and ransom "a great punishment". The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "writing" and "touching". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 64}

Report 16295, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Ibn Abi Adi and Awf: God was going to give this community the booty, and they took ransom from the captives of Badr before being commanded to; God faulted that in them, then made it lawful. Report 16296, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Buzay', Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal and Awf: the companions of the Prophet took booty and captives before being commanded to, and God had written in the Mother of the Book that booty and captives are lawful to Muhammad and his community, which He had not made lawful to any community before them; "were it not for a writing from God that had gone before" means in the first Book that booty and captives are lawful to you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 65}

Report 16297, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father and his father: before the Prophet was sent, when nations took booty they made it an offering and God forbade them to eat any of it, little or much, as forbidden to every prophet and his community; He did not make it lawful to any prophet except Muhammad, and it had gone before in God's decree that booty is lawful to him and his community; that is His word on the day of Badr about taking ransom from the captives. Report 16298, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki, Abu Usama and Urwa: God was going to give this community the booty, and they did what they did before booty was made lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 65}

Report 16299, from al-A'mash, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it had gone before from God that He made booty lawful to them. Report 16300, from Abu Hurayra, through Abu Kurayb, Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla, Bashir ibn Maymun and Sa'id: were it not that it had gone before in My knowledge that I would make booty lawful, a great punishment would have touched you for what you took of the captives; the editor says Bashir ibn Maymun is weak, accused of fabrication, and that they agreed to discard his reports. Report 16301, from Abu Hurayra, through Abu Kurayb, Jabir ibn Nuh, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Abu Salih, from the Messenger of God: booty was not made lawful to any of the black-headed before you; a fire would come down from the sky and eat it, until the day of Badr, when people fell on the booty, and God sent down "were it not for a writing from God that had gone before", up to "lawful and good". Report 16302 is like it, through Ibn Waki and Abu Mu'awiya. The editor says the chain is sound except for Jabir ibn Nuh al-Himmani, who is weak, so Abu Mu'awiya's report is the sound one; al-Tirmidhi gave it, calling it good and sound, al-Bayhaqi gave it, and al-Suyuti ascribed it also to al-Nasa'i, Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn Abi Hatim, Abu al-Shaykh and Ibn Mardawayh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 66}

Report 16303, from Ubayda, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Fudayl, Ash'ath ibn Sawwar and Ibn Sirin: the Muslims took seventy of the idolaters captive and killed seventy; the Messenger of God said, choose: you may take ransom from them and gain strength by it against your enemy, but if you accept it seventy of you will be killed, or you may kill them; they said, rather we take ransom from them, and seventy of us be killed; Ubayda said, and they sought both the choices. Report 16304, from Ubayda: the ransom of the captives of Badr was a hundred ounces, the ounce being forty dirhams, or six dinars. Report 16305, from Ubayda on the captives of Badr, through Abu Kurayb, Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya, Ibn Awn and Ibn Sirin: the Messenger of God said, if you wish you may kill them, and if you wish ransom them and as many of you will be martyred; they said, we take the ransom and enjoy it, and as many of us will be martyred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 67}

Report 16306, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Tusi, Abd al-Samad ibn Abd al-Warith, Hammam ibn Yahya, Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Abu Wa'il: Umar commanded the killing of the captives, and God sent down "were it not for a writing from God that had gone before, a great punishment would have touched you for what you took"; the editor judges the chain sound. Report 16307, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: booty was forbidden to every prophet and his community, who when they took booty made it an offering to God that fire consumed; it had gone before in God's decree and knowledge that booty would be lawful to this community to eat in their bellies. Report 16308, from Ata, through Ibn Humayd and Jarir: it was in God's knowledge that booty would be lawful to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 68}

Others said the sense is: were it not for a writing from God that had gone before for the people of Badr, that He would not punish them, a great punishment would have touched them. Report 16309, from Sa'id, through Ibn Waki, Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, Sharik and Salim: for the people of Badr, of happiness; report 16310, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Numayr, Warqa and Ibn Abi Najih: for the people of Badr, their attendance; report 16311, from al-Hasan: good had gone before from God for the people of Badr; report 16312, from Qatada: good had gone before for them from God and He made booty lawful to them; report 16313, from al-Hasan: it had gone before that He would not punish any of the people of Badr; report 16314, from Mujahid: for the people of Badr and their attending it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 69}

Report 16315, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: it would have touched you for what you took of booty on the day of Badr before I made it lawful to you; pardon of them and mercy to them had gone before, and that He does not punish the believers, for He does not punish His messenger and those who believed in him, emigrated with him and helped him. Others said the sense is: were it not for a writing from God that had gone before, that He does not hold anyone to account for an act done in ignorance, "a great punishment would have touched you for what you took"; report 16316 begins from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 69}

We stop here at the foot of p. 69, in the middle of the commentary on verse 68 (at the start of report 16316, on the view that ignorance is not held to account); the next batch begins at p. 70. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 69}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
