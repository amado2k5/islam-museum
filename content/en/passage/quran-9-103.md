**Commentary on verse 103 (pp. 454-458)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet Muhammad: Muhammad, take from the wealth of these who confessed their sins and repented of them "a charity to purify them" of the filth of their sins, "and cleanse them by it", meaning raise them and lift them up from the base ranks of the people of hypocrisy to the ranks of the people of sincerity; "and pray for them" means call for forgiveness of their sins for them and ask forgiveness for them; "your prayer is a calm for them" means your supplication and asking forgiveness is reassurance for them that God has pardoned them and accepted their repentance; "and God is hearing, knowing" means God hears your supplication when you pray for them and anything else of His creatures' words, and knows what you ask for them in praying to your Lord for them and the rest of His servants' affairs. The editor refers to earlier explanations of purifying, cleansing, prayer and calm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 454}

Report 17152, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: they came with their wealth, meaning Abu Lubaba and his companions, when they were freed, and said "Messenger of God, this is our wealth, so give it in charity for us and ask forgiveness for us", and he said "I was not ordered to take anything of your wealth", and God sent down "take from their wealth a charity to purify them and cleanse them by it", meaning by "zakat" obedience to God and sincerity; "and pray for them", meaning ask forgiveness for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 454}

Report 17153, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: when the Messenger of God freed Abu Lubaba and his two companions, Abu Lubaba and his companions went with their wealth and brought it to the Messenger of God and said "take of our wealth and give it in charity for us, pray over us", meaning ask forgiveness for us, "and purify us", and the Messenger of God said "I shall take nothing of it until I am ordered", and God sent down "take from their wealth a charity to purify them and cleanse them by it and pray for them, your prayer is a calm for them", meaning ask forgiveness for them for the sins they had committed; and when this verse came down the Messenger of God took a part of their wealth and gave it in charity for them. Report 17154, from Zayd ibn Aslam, through Ibn Humayd and Ya'qub: when the Prophet freed Abu Lubaba and those who had tied themselves to the pillars they said "Messenger of God, take from our wealth a charity by which you purify us", and God sent down "take from their wealth a charity to purify them", the verse. Report 17155, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Jarir, Ya'qub and Ja'far: those who tied themselves to the pillars said, when He pardoned them, "Prophet of God, purify our wealth", and God sent down "take from their wealth a charity to purify them and cleanse them by it"; and the three, when one of them complained, the other two complained the same, and two of them went blind, and the other kept praying until he went blind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 455}

Report 17156, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: the four, al-Jadd ibn Qays, Abu Lubaba, Haram and Aws, are those about whom it was said "take from their wealth a charity to purify them and cleanse them by it and pray for them, your prayer is a calm for them", that is, a dignity for them, and they had promised of themselves to spend, strive and give charity. Report 17157, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: when the Prophet of God freed Abu Lubaba and his companions they came to the Prophet of God with their wealth and said "Prophet of God, take of our wealth and give it in charity for us, purify us and pray over us", saying ask forgiveness for us, and the Prophet of God said "I take nothing of your wealth until I am ordered about it", and God, mighty and exalted, sent down "take from their wealth a charity to purify them" of the sins they committed, "and pray for them", saying ask forgiveness for them, and the Prophet of God did what God had ordered. Report 17158, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "take from their wealth a charity", Abu Lubaba and his companions, "and pray for them", meaning ask forgiveness for them for the sins they had committed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 456}

Report 17159, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: these are people of the hypocrites who stayed behind from the Prophet in the Tabuk campaign and confessed hypocrisy, saying "Messenger of God, we doubted and were hypocrites and had misgivings, but here is a new repentance and a charity we bring out of our wealth", and God said to His Prophet "take from their wealth a charity to purify them and cleanse them by it", after He had said "never pray over any of them who dies", which is [[passage/quran-9-84|9:84]]. The people of Arabic differed over the reason for the nominative of "cleanse them". One of the Basran grammarians said "cleanse them by it" is nominative as a beginning, or, if you wish, you made it an attribute of "charity" and then brought it as emphasis, and likewise "purify them". One of the Kufan grammarians said: if the words "purify them" are of the Prophet, the preferred form is the jussive, since nothing refers back to the charity, and "cleanse them" is a new statement; and if the charity purifies them and you cleanse them with it, both verbs may be jussive or both nominative. Al-Tabari holds that "purify them" is part of the relative description of the charity, since the readers are agreed on its nominative, which shows it is part of the description of "the charity", while "and cleanse them by it" is a new statement meaning "and you cleanse them by it", which is why it is in the nominative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 457}

The interpreters differed over the meaning of "your prayer is a calm for them". Some said it is mercy for them, which is report 17160, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali. Others said rather that the meaning is that your prayer is a dignity for them, which is report 17161, from Qatada, through Bishr and Sa'id. The reciters differed in reading it. The reciters of Medina read it in the plural, "your prayers are a calm for them", meaning your supplications, and the reciters of Iraq and some Meccans read it in the singular, "your prayer", meaning your supplication. It seems that those who read it in the singular thought the singular reading sounder, since the singular has a sense of plurality and large number that the plural does not, the plural of prayer being for a number from three to ten and not more. Al-Tabari says that the reading in the singular is his, but not for that reason, since in number it is more than the plural, but because what is intended is the report about the supplication and prayers of the Prophet being a calm for these people and not a report of number, and since that is so, the singular for "prayer" is preferable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 458}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
