## Summary
Verse 113 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 113 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "and so that the hearts of those who do not believe in the hereafter incline to it, and be pleased with it, and earn what they are earning", al-Tabari says: some of these devils inspire others with speech adorned with falsehood, to deceive with it the believers among the followers of the prophets and tempt them from their religion, "and so that the hearts of those who do not believe in the hereafter incline to it", that is, lean to it. It is from "saghawtu, tasgha and tasghu"; the revelation has "tasgha", with verbal nouns "saghw" and "sughuww"; some Arabs say "saghaytu" with ya, reported of some of Banu Asad ("I inclined to his talk, I incline"), when you lean; one says "my inclination is with you" when your desire and leaning are with someone; one says "I tilted the vessel" when one tips it so its contents gather; a line by a poet the editor could not identify is cited (the footnote traces it to the Lisan, Abu Hayyan and al-Qurtubi and explains its sense); and the moon is said to "sagha" and "asgha" when it leans toward setting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 57}

The interpreters said the like. Report 13781, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha: hearts swerve toward it; 13782, from him: that they lean; 13783, from al-Suddi: the hearts of the unbelievers lean to it, love it and are pleased with it; 13784, from Ibn Zayd: that they love that and be pleased with it, as a man says of a woman "I inclined to her", meaning he loved her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 58}

As for "and earn what they are earning", al-Tabari says it means: that they acquire of deeds what they are acquiring. It is reported from the Arabs, as heard from them, "he went out yaqtarifu for his family", meaning earning for them, and hence "so-and-so qarafa this affair" when he fell into it and did it. Some said it is accusation and claim: one says to a man "you qarafta me", meaning you accused me, and "evil is what you have earned for yourself", with a line of Ru'ba cited (the footnote says it is not in his diwan but in Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an). Report 13785, from Ibn Abbas: that they earn what they are earning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 59}

Report 13786, from al-Suddi: that they do what they are doing; 13787, from Ibn Zayd: the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 60}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
