## Summary
Verse 227 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 227 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reports the interpreters' differences over the meaning of the resolve to divorce in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-227|2:227]]). Some said it means that those who swear off, if they return to what God made binding for women of good companionship during the four months, then God is forgiving and merciful, and if they leave the return until the four months end, their wives are divorced from them by their passing, the passing being for these people the sign of the husband's resolve to divorce his wife. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 477} They then differed over the divorce which falls on her when the four months pass. Some said it is a final single divorce. From 'Ali: when four months pass it is a final divorce (4557, 4559); that 'Ali and Ibn Mas'ud made it one divorce, after which she is more entitled to herself (4558), a view Qatada preferred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 478} From Abu Salama, that 'Uthman ibn 'Affan and Zayd ibn Thabit said that when the four months pass it is one final divorce (4560 to 4563). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 478} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 479} From Ibn Mas'ud in the story of 'Abdallah ibn Unays, who swore off from his wife and she waited six months; Ibn Mas'ud told him to let her know she now has charge of her affair, and he told her and gave her a pound of silver as dowry (4564, 4567, 4569); and that he used to say when four months pass it is a final divorce (4565, 4566, 4568), and she observes three periods (4570). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 479} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 480} In another report 'Abdallah ibn Unays had intercourse with her after four months, forgetting, and Ibn Mas'ud said she has become separate from him, so propose to her again (4571); and Ibn Mas'ud struck the thigh of al-Nu'man ibn Bashir when he swore off from his wife and said, when four months pass, admit one divorce (4572); and if they pass without his return, she has become separate by one divorce and he is a suitor (4573). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 480} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 481} From Ibn 'Abbas: the resolve to divorce is the passing of the four months (4574, 4575), and it is a single final divorce (4576). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 481} The editor lists where other books record the Companions' and Successors' sayings on swearing off, saying no one gave them as fully as al-Tabari, and gives some narrators' biographies and explains the word for silver. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 478} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 480} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 481}

To finish the view that the divorce falling on the passing of four months is a final single divorce: from 'Ikrima from Ibn 'Abbas (4577), and from Ibn 'Abbas that the resolve to divorce is the passing of the four months (4578, 4579); from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, that the governor of Mecca asked him about one who has sworn off and he said Ibn 'Umar used to say that when four months pass she is given charge of her affair, as Ibn 'Abbas also said (4580); from Ibn 'Abbas (4581) and Ibn al-Hanafiyya (4582) that it is a final divorce; and from Qabisa ibn Dhu'ayb, a final divorce, with the waiting period starting afresh and she more entitled to her own affair (4583). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 481} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 482} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 483} From al-Sha'bi, a man came to Shurayh and said he had sworn off from his wife and four months had passed before he returned, and Shurayh recited the verse and added nothing; he went to Masruq, who said, may God have mercy on Abu Umayya, if all people said this who would ease the case of such a man; then he said, it is a final divorce and you are one of the suitors (4584, 4585). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 483} From Salim ibn 'Abdallah and Abu Salama (4586), and from 'Ata': a final divorce, and he may propose to her during the waiting period (4587). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 483} From 'Ali, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas and al-Hasan, about one who swears never to approach her and four months pass without his return: a final divorce and he is a suitor (4588). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 483} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 484} From al-Hasan about one who told his wife "if I approach you, you are divorced three times": when four months pass it is one final divorce and that falls away (4589); from al-Hasan and Muhammad ibn Sirin (4590, 4591) and Ibrahim (4592); and from al-Nakha'i, about one who said "you are divorced three times if I approach you for a year": if he approaches before the four months she is separate by three, and if he leaves her she is separate by swearing off (4593). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 484} Qatada reported that 'Ubaydallah ibn Ziyad stayed late at Hind's, whose maids locked the doors against him, so he swore not to come to her until she came to him, and he was told that if four months passed he would lose her (4594), and from 'Awf (4595). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 484} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 485} From Ibn 'Abbas: if the four months pass she is forbidden to him, observes the waiting period of a divorced woman and he is one of the suitors (4596), and from Qabisa (4597). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 485} From Qatada, the people of the Jahiliyya counted it as divorce, and God set them four months; if he returns within them he expiates his oath and she is his wife, and if they pass without return it is a final divorce (4598, 4599). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 485} From al-Suddi, that Ibn Mas'ud and 'Umar ibn al-Khattab said when four months pass she is divorced and final (4600); and from al-Dahhak, if she returns to him it is with a new dowry, a marriage before witnesses and the guardian's consent (4601). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 486}

Others said what falls on her with the passing of the four months is a divorce in which the husband may take her back. From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith ibn Hisham: it is one and he has more right to take her back (4602, 4603); from Makhul (4604); and from Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, al-Zuhri giving rulings by his word (4605). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 486} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 487} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, he has more right to her while she is in her waiting period (4606), and he said to an Iraqi questioner, perhaps you are one who says that when four months pass she is separate; no, even if four years pass (4607). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 487} From Rabi'a: one divorce, she starts her waiting period and her husband has more right to take her back (4608). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 487} The editor gives biographies of some narrators of these reports and explains some words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 481} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 482} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 483} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 485} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 486} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 487}

From al-Awza'i, that the view of his companions al-Zuhri and Makhul is that it is a divorce once four months pass and he has more right to her in her waiting period (4610); and it is mentioned that Ibn Shubruma used to say that when four months pass he may take her back, arguing from the Qur'an and drawing on the following verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]) (4609). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 487} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 488} The editor gives biographies of Abu Yunus al-Qawi, Hajjaj ibn Rishdin and 'Abd al-Jabbar ibn 'Umar, and explains the verb for drawing on a verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 487} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 488}

Others said the sense of the verse ([[passage/quran-2-227|2:227]]) is that those who swear off keep apart from their wives for four months, waiting on his affair and hers; if after the four months they return, going back to good companionship and coming to them, God is forgiving and merciful, and if they resolve on divorce and pronounce a divorce after the four months, God hears their divorce and knows what they did to them of good and ill. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 488} Those who held this said the passing of four months gives the wife a claim on her husband who swore off to return or divorce, and the ruler must make the husband stand for it; if he returns or divorces, well, and otherwise the ruler divorces for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 488} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, 'Umar said of swearing off that nothing is on him until he is made to stand, then he divorces or keeps (4611, 4612); and from Sa'id ibn Jubayr from 'Umar, that when four months pass it does not make it anything (4613). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 488} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 489} From 'Ali, that he made the one who swore off stand after the four months until he returns or divorces (4614 to 4620), and Ibn Idris said it is the view of the people of Medina (4618). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 490} From Tawus, that 'Uthman made him stand, by the view of the people of Medina (4621, 4622). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 490} From Abu al-Darda', he has no term and it is a sin; he is made to stand, either to keep her or to divorce (4623, 4624, 4625); from him and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, that he stays in sin until he returns or divorces (4626, 4628); and from Abu al-Darda' and 'A'isha (4627). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 490} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 491} From Ibn Abi Mulayka from 'A'isha (4629 to 4631), and from al-Qasim from 'A'isha, when four months pass he either keeps her as God commanded or divorces her, and what he did does not itself require divorce or anything else (4632). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 491} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 492} Al-Qasim ibn Muhammad reported that Khalid ibn al-'As al-Makhzumi used to swear about the daughter of Abu Sa'id not to approach her for a long time, and 'A'isha said to him, do you not fear God, as though she counted it a sin to him and did not think he had left his wife (4633). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 492} From Ibn 'Umar, only what God made lawful is lawful to him, either to return or to divorce (4634 to 4636, 4638, 4639, 4641), and from 'Umar likewise (4637), with his remark to a questioner that the rulers decide this (4640). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 492} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 493} From Suhayl ibn Abi Salih, from his father, that he asked twelve Companions of the Prophet about a man who swears off from his wife and all said nothing is on him until the four months pass, then he is made to stand, and if he returns, well, otherwise he divorces (4642). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 493} The editor gives narrators' biographies, corrections to the chains and cross-references to earlier notes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 488} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 491} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 492}

To finish the view of making him stand after four months ([[passage/quran-2-227|2:227]]): from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, he did not think separation befell him until he divorced (4643), that God made it a time he may not pass until he returns or divorces, and if he passes it he has sinned but his wife is not forbidden to him (4644), and that he is made to stand when the four months end (4645 to 4648). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 493} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 494} The editor says another division of the copy from which the manuscript was transcribed ends here, with a note of a hearing in Sha'ban 408. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 494} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 495} From Malik from al-Zuhri from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, as 'Umar said, nothing is on him until he is made to stand, then he divorces or keeps (4649); from Mujahid, he is made to stand (4650, 4651); that Marwan made him stand after six months (4652); and from 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz (4653). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 495} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 496} From Ibn 'Abbas, he is a man who swears to his wife by God not to marry her, so he waits four months; if he marries her he expiates his oath, and if the term passes before he does, the ruler compels him either to return or to resolve and divorce (4654); from 'Ali and Ibn 'Abbas (4655); and from Ibn Zayd, if you take him to the imam a term of four months is set for him and if he returns, well, otherwise he divorces for him; if she does not take him there it is her right which she has left (4656). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 496} From Malik: no divorce falls on the one who swore off until he is made to stand, and he is not one who swore off until he swears for more than four months (4657). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 496} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 497} From Ibn 'Umar, until it is taken to the ruler (4658); from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, even if a man swore off for four years we would not separate her from him until we bring them together, and if he returns, he returns, and if he resolves on divorce, he resolves (4659); and from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, he is made to stand when the four months pass (4660). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 497}

Others said swearing off is nothing. From Ibn al-Musayyab (4661); from Maymun ibn Mihran, who asked Ibn 'Umar about a man who swore off and four months passed, and he recited the verse (4662); and from 'Ata': I have no knowledge of it (4663). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 497} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 498} Others of this group said the resolve to divorce means their refusal to return after the imam makes them stand for return or divorce; from Ibrahim, the one who swore off is made to stand when the four months end, and if he does not return she is a final divorce (4664, 4665). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 498}

Al-Tabari's choice: the view most like the apparent sense of the Book of God for him is that of 'Umar, 'Uthman and 'Ali, and those who followed them on divorce, which means that if they return after the imam makes them stand when the four months end, going back to doing their duty to God by their wives, then God is forgiving and merciful, and if they resolve to divorce and divorce them, God hears their divorce and knows what they did to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 498} His reason is that God closed the mention of resolving to divorce with "hearing, knowing", and the passing of the four months is not heard but known, so if the resolve to divorce were the end of the four months, the verse would not close with hearing; just as He did not close the mention of returning with "severe in punishment", since it is not a place of threat but of promise to the one who turns back, but with "forgiving, merciful", so He closed what contains speech with "hearing" and what contains action with "knowing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 498} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499} He says he fully set out the evidence for this in his "Book of the Subtle" and disliked repeating it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499} This is al-Tabari's own choice; the editor explains why he divides the verse in two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 498} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499}
