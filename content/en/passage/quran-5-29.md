**Commentary on verse 29: "I want you to bear my sin and your sin", pp. 215-219**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said it means I want you to bear the sin of killing me and your sin of disobeying God and your other sins. Report 11730, from al-Suddi, from several Companions, says the sin of killing him added to the sin already on his neck, so that he would be among the people of the Fire; reports 11731 and 11732, from Qatada, say it is your killing me and your sin before it; reports 11733 and 11734, from Mujahid, say the same; report 11735, from al-Dahhak, says "your sin" is what he did before killing his brother and "his sin" is killing his brother. Those who said this took the sense to be the sin of my killing, the word for killing being dropped as understood.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 215}

Others said it means that you bear my fault and take on its burden together with your sin in killing me; al-Tabari found this from Mujahid and fears it is a mistake, since the sound report from him is the earlier one; report 11736 gives it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 216}

His choice is that it means I want you to turn back with your sin of killing me, and "your sin" is his sin apart from the killing, his disobedience of God in other deeds. He reasons from the agreement of the interpreters and from God's report that each doer's reward is for or against him, so a killer cannot be taken for the slain man's sins but only for the forbidden killing and his own other sins.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 217}

He raises an objection: was the killing not disobedience by the killer, so how could the slain man want it and say "I want you to bear"? He answers that it means I want you to bear the sin of killing me if you kill me, since I will not kill you; if the killer kills him he bears it by God's judgement regardless, and wanting it does not put the speaker in error. He explains "so you will be among the companions of the Fire, and that is the reward of the wrongdoers" as being, through killing him, among the Fire's dwellers who remain in it, the Fire being the recompense of those who leave the path of truth and overstep what was assigned them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 217}

He says this shows God had commanded and forbidden Adam after sending him down to earth, with promise and warning, else the slain one would not have said it. He relays from Mujahid that one of the killer's legs was bound from calf to thigh from then until the Day of Resurrection, with his face to the sun wherever it turns, as report 11737 says, and from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr that the killer shares in the Fire's punishment equally and has half of it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 218}

Reports 11738 and 11739, from Ibn Mas'ud, give the Prophet's saying that no soul is killed unjustly but the first son of Adam bears a share of it, because he was the first to establish killing; the editor says it is sound, given by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and others, and that the share means a portion of the burden. Report 11740, from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, says the same with the devil also named; report 11741, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr, says the most wretched of people is the son of Adam who killed his brother, since no blood is shed on earth until the Day of Resurrection without some of it reaching him, as he first established killing.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 219}

Al-Tabari holds that this report from the Prophet shows al-Hasan's view, that the two were not Adam's sons from his loins but men of the Children of Israel, and what was reported from him that Adam was first to die and that sacrifice was in Israel's time, to be wrong.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 219}

We stop here at the foot of p. 219, in al-Tabari's argument that the Prophetic report shows al-Hasan's view wrong; the rest of verse 29 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
