**Commentary on verse 51 (pp. 137-140)**

On "he said, what was your affair when you sought Joseph?" and what follows, al-Tabari says something is left out of the speech, spared by what is mentioned: the messenger returned to the king from Joseph with his message, and the king summoned the women who had cut their hands and the Aziz's wife and said to them "what was your affair". Report 19406, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when the messenger came to the king from Joseph with what he had sent, he gathered the women and said what was their affair when they sought Joseph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 137}

By "what was your affair" he means what your matter and condition was when you sought Joseph; they answered "God forbid, we know no evil of him", and the Aziz's wife said "now the truth has become clear", that is, now the truth has become plain, been uncovered and shown: "I sought him, and he is among the truthful" in what he said, "she sought me". The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "muradawa" and "hasha lillah". The interpreters said the same of the meaning: report 19407, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: it became clear. Reports 19408 to 19411, from Mujahid: it became clear, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr and Waraqa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Shababa; al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far, all from Ibn Abi Najih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 138}

Report 19412, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: now the truth has become clear. Report 19413, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the same. Report 19414, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: it became clear. Reports 19415 and 19416, from al-Suddi, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat, and through Ibn Waki': it became clear. Report 19417, from al-Dahhak, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hushaym and Juwaybir: the same. Report 19418, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Ra'il, the wife of Itfir the Aziz, said "now the truth has become clear", that is, it has come out and become plain, "I sought him, and he is among the truthful" in what Joseph said of what she had claimed against him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 139}

Report 19419, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: the king said bring them to me and asked what their affair was; they said "God forbid, we know no evil of him", but the Aziz's wife told us that she sought him, that he entered the house with her and loosened his trousers and then tied them again, so we do not know what occurred to him; and the Aziz's wife said "now the truth has become clear". Report 19420, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: it became clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 139}

Al-Tabari says the root of "hashhasa" is "hassa", but the doubled form was used as "kubkibu" was said for "kubbu", "kafkafa" for "kaffa" and "dhardhara" for "dharra"; the root "hass" means uprooting a thing, as one says "he cut his hair off at the root". What is meant here is that falsehood and lying went away and ceased, and the truth became clear and showed itself. The editor notes the difference between the manuscript and the printed text on the form "dhardhara" and that the word means scattering and dispersing a thing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 140}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
