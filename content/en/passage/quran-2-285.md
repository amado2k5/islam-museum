## Summary
Verse 285 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 285 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-285|2:285]]), pp. 124 and 125. Al-Tabari explains the opening as saying that the Messenger, meaning the Messenger of God, affirmed and acknowledged what was sent down to him from his Lord of the Book, with its lawful and unlawful, promise and threat, command and prohibition and the other meanings it holds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 124} He mentions that when this verse came down to the Prophet he said that it was right for him (to believe). In 6499, Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said): we were told that the Prophet of God, when it came down, said: it is right for him to believe. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 124} The editor traces it to al-Hakim's Mustadrak (through Khallad ibn Yahya, Abu Aqil, Yahya ibn Abi Kathir and Anas, in close wording); al-Hakim called it sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs though they did not take it, and al-Dhahabi added the correction that it is broken in transmission; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 124}

Al-Tabari says it was also said that it came down after the previous verse, because the believing Companions of the Prophet were burdened by God's threat of reckoning them for what their selves hid and complained of it to him, and he said to them: perhaps you will say as the Children of Israel said, declaring hearing with disobedience; they said: rather we declare hearing and obedience; so God sent down the verse for this, from the Prophet's saying and his Companions' saying, meaning that the believers also affirmed, together with their prophet, God, the angels, the books and the messengers; he says he has mentioned those who said this earlier, and the editor refers back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 124} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 125}

Then he gives the readers' difference over the word for the books. Most of the readers of Medina and some of Iraq read the plural, meaning that the believers each believed in God, His angels and all the books He sent to His prophets and messengers. A group of the readers of Kufa read the singular, meaning the Qur'an He sent down to His prophet Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 125} It is reported that Ibn Abbas read the singular and said that the book is more than the books, as though he took it in the sense of the generic use found in [[passage/quran-103-1|103:1]] and [[passage/quran-103-2|103:2]], the class of people and the class of book, as one says how many are so-and-so's dirhams and dinars, meaning the class of coins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 125} Al-Tabari says that is a known approach, but he finds it more pleasing to read the plural, because what comes before and after is plural, so joining the books to the plural in wording is more pleasing to him than making it singular, so that it fits the preceding and following words in both form and sense; this is his choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 125}

These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices and arguments are his, and the editor's notes and references are his, with no museum verdict.

On p. 126 al-Tabari explains the clause that the believers make no distinction between any of the messengers: God reports it of the believers as something they say. In the reading with the letter nun in the verb for distinguishing, a word is omitted and understood from the context, namely 'they say', so the sense is: the believers, each of them, affirm God, the angels, the books and the messengers, saying that they make no distinction between any of His messengers; and the saying is dropped here as it is dropped in the description of the angels entering upon them from every gate in [[passage/quran-13-23|13:23]] and [[passage/quran-13-24|13:24]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 126} A group of the early readers read it with ya, meaning that each of them makes no distinction between any of His messengers, believing some and denying others, but affirms them all and acknowledges that what they brought was from God and that they called to God and to obedience to Him; in this they differ from the Jews, who affirmed Moses and denied Jesus, and the Christians, who affirmed Moses and Jesus and denied Muhammad and rejected his prophethood, and from those like them among the nations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 126} In 6500, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): not distinguishing as that people did, meaning the Children of Israel, who said so-and-so is a prophet and so-and-so is not, this one we believe in and that one we do not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 126}

Abu Jafar says that the only reading he allows is the nun reading, because it is the one whose proof is established by widespread transmission, which rules out collusion, oversight and error, and an odd reading is not to be set against what the proof has brought by transmission and inheritance; this is his choice and argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 127} In the notes to p. 127 the editor says the print has one form of a word where the manuscript has another and that he kept the right one; that the manuscript's word for mutual collusion is correct and the print has a wrong letter, the verb meaning that they made it known among themselves, from a root for knowing, a word rare in the dictionaries but common in al-Tabari's books and those of his generation, and he refers to al-Jahiz and to a later page (155); and that the manuscript's phrase 'by transmission and inheritance' is right where the print has 'by transmission and report', noting that al-Tabari often used the word for inheritance earlier; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 127}

Then al-Tabari explains the believers' words of hearing, obeying and seeking forgiveness: all the believers said that they heard their Lord's word and His command to them of what He commanded and His prohibition of what He forbade, and obeyed Him in the obligations He laid on them and the obedience He bound them to, and submitted to Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 127} He explains the plea for forgiveness as 'forgive us, our Lord, with Your forgiveness', as one says a word of glorification meaning 'we glorify You with glorification', and says he has shown earlier that forgiveness is God's covering of the sins of whom He forgave, His holding back from exposing His cover over them in this world and the next, and His pardon from punishment; the editor refers to part 2. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 127} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 128} The last clause he takes to mean that they said: to You, our Lord, is our return and our resurrection, so forgive us our sins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 128}

He raises a grammatical question: what puts the word for forgiveness in the accusative? He answers that it is a verbal noun standing in the place of an imperative, and the Arabs put verbal nouns and nouns in the accusative when they take the place of a command and give its sense, saying thanks to God, praise to Him, meaning thank and praise, and, of nouns, God, God, my people; if it were in the nominative, on the sense 'it is God' or 'this is God', with the predicate having the sense of a command, it would be allowed, and he cites two lines by a poet, which describe a people among whom are Umayr, the father of Thumayr and al-Tuffah, as worthy of loyalty when the man of valour cries out for weapons; and if the word for forgiveness had come in the nominative in a reading, it would not be wrong, but right as he has described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 128} The editor says he does not know who the poet is, that the lines are in al-Farra's Maani al-Quran and al-Ayni's Shawahid, that he could not identify the Umayr and al-Tuffah named since so many bear those names, and that most of this discussion is from al-Farra; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 128}

Al-Tabari then says it is related that when this verse came down to the Prophet as God's praise of him and his community, Gabriel said to him that God had praised him and his community well, so he should ask his Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 128} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 129} In 6501, Hakim ibn Jabir (through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Bayan): when this verse came down to the Prophet, Gabriel said God had praised him and his community well, ask and it will be given; so he asked what is in [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] to the end of the sura. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 129} The editor notes: Bayan is Ibn Bishr al-Ahmasi; Hakim ibn Jabir ibn Tariq ibn Awf al-Ahmasi is an eminent reliable tabii who narrated mursal from the Prophet, and from his father, Umar, Uthman, Ibn Masud, Talha and Ubada ibn al-Samit, and Ismail ibn Abi Khalid and Bayan narrated from him; he died at the end of al-Hajjaj's governorship, said to be 82 or 95, and he stated that he heard Umar; so this report is mursal. He adds al-Suyuti's attribution also to Said ibn Mansur and Ibn Abi Hatim, and says Ibn Kathir copied it from here but with a corrupted chain (from Sinan, from Hakim, from Jabir) that makes it seem connected through the Companion Jabir ibn Abd Allah, which this place corrects; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 129}
