## Summary
Verse 120 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 120 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 120: the promises of the devil and what they come to (pp. 224-225, notes 4-5 on p. 224 and notes 1-3 on p. 225).** Al-Tabari says that the devil's relationship to his allies lasts as long as the person lives and is given respite from punishment, as God described it: the rebellious devil promises his allies, who are his determined portion, that he will help them against anyone who wishes them ill, defend them and give them the upper hand over whoever tries to harm them, and he arouses in them hopes of victory. The word for delusion means falsehood, and he calls his promise a delusion because they thought, in taking him as ally, that they stood on the truth of his false promises and empty hopes, until the truth was laid bare and they came to need him, and then the enemy of God disowned them. He gives three illustrations from the Qur'an: the devil's address to his followers on the Day of Judgement, [[passage/quran-14-22|14:22]]; his conduct toward the polytheists at Badr, after he made their deeds look fair to them, when he said that no one would overcome them and that he was their protector but, on seeing the two hosts, turned on his heels and said he was free of them, [[passage/quran-8-48|8:48]]; and the simile of the mirage, [[passage/quran-24-39|24:39]], that the thirsty man takes for water until he reaches it and finds nothing. The editor's notes: that the word for victory (falaj) means success and rising above an opponent; a reference to the earlier explanation of the word for delusion; on p. 225, that he prefers the manuscript's wordings over the print's in two places, that both witnesses read the word for the severity of the matter with one letter where he reads the word for its earnestness, and judges it also right and preferable, and a grammatical note on the case of the phrase "the enemy of God", placed between the verbal noun and its object. The commentary then turns, in the middle of p. 225, to the next verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 225}
