## Summary
Verse 174 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 174 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 174 (pp. 414-415, reports 8251-8255).** Al-Tabari explains the turning back as the return of those who had answered the call of God and the Messenger after the wounds from their journey, namely their going after their enemy to Hamra' al-Asad; the blessing as well-being from their Lord, since they met no enemy; the favour as the profits they made from the trade they carried on and the reward they earned; the untouched state as meaning that no harm or injury from their enemy reached them; the following of God's pleasure as their pleasing God by what they did, following His Messenger in pursuing the enemy and obeying him; and the breadth of bounty as God's beneficence and grace to them by turning away their enemy, who had meant to charge back on them, and by His other favours to them and to others, great in the view of the creature who receives it. The notes refer to earlier passages on the words for "turned back", "blessing", "favour" (p. 299, note 2) and "touch" (reference numbers garbled in the extracted text), and say that the printed text reads "ittajaru" where the manuscript has "tajaru", which the editor adopts, both meaning to buy and sell. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 414}

Mujahid (8251), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: the favour is what they gained of trade and reward. Mujahid (8252), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj: they found the market and bought, and the favour is what they gained of trade and reward; Ibn Jurayj said that what they gained from selling was a blessing and favour from God, that they came upon the market unawares with no one contesting it, that the "harm" is killing, and the following of God's pleasure is obedience to the Prophet. The note says the printed text has a meaningless word where the manuscript is undotted, and that "ghirra" is heedlessness, meaning the market was empty of rivals as though they came while people were unaware of it, a figure like "a ghirr life", an easy life whose people are not alarmed (the extracted text is partly garbled here). Ibn Ishaq (8253), through Ibn Humayd from Salama: God's bounty is great because He turned away from them a meeting with their enemy; the note gives Ibn Hisham (3: 128) as completing the reports ending at 8244. Ibn 'Abbas (8254), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather: they obeyed God and went about their business and no one harmed them. Al-Suddi (8255), through Muhammad from Ahmad from Asbat: when the Prophet set out for the expedition of Badr al-Sughra he gave them dirhams at Badr with which they bought in the Badr season and made a profit; the blessing is well-being, the favour trade, and the harm killing. The note says the word before "dirhams" differs in the printed text and al-Durr al-Manthur from the manuscript, where it is undotted; the editor fears it is a corrupted word he could not resolve, and has read it as "nathr dirhams", not knowing whether that is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 414} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 415}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 415}
