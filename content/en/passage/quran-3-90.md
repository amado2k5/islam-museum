## Summary
Verse 90 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 90 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 90: increase in disbelief.** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said that God meant by His words about those who disbelieved in some of His prophets sent before Muhammad, after their faith, and then increased in disbelief by disbelieving in Muhammad, that their repentance is not accepted at the presence of death and the rattle in the throat. The editor says that the phrase 'that is, in some of His prophets' in the printed text is an addition that is not in the manuscript. Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan that they are the Jews and Christians, whose repentance is not accepted at death (7372). Report 7373 then begins, from Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, and breaks off at the end of p. 578. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 578}

**The end of report 7373 and the other views (7373-7375).** Report 7373, from Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, concludes that the ones meant are the Jews, whom Qatada called enemies of God (a judgement the museum reports as his); they disbelieved in the Gospel and in Jesus and then added to their disbelief with Muhammad and the Furqan (7373). Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada that the sense is that they persisted in disbelief until death came to them, so their turning back was not received when death came, and Mamar said that Ata al-Khurasani said the same (7374). Al-Muthanna relays from Ishaq, from Abd Allah ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Qatada that they are the Jews, who disbelieved in the Gospel and then added to disbelief when God sent Muhammad, denying him and rejecting him (7375).

**Second view: the addition is sins (7376-7381).** Others said the sense is that those of the People of the Book who disbelieved in Muhammad after belief in their prophets, and then added sins, will not have their turning back from their sins received while they remain in disbelief. Al-Muthanna relays from Abd al-Wahhab, from Dawud, from Rufay that they added sins while unbelievers, so their turning from those sins is not received as long as they were in their disbelief and error (7376). Ibn al-Muthanna relays from Ibn Abi Adi, from Dawud, who said he asked Abu al-Aliya, who said that they are only these Christians and Jews who disbelieved and then added to their disbelief by sins they committed, repenting of them while in their disbelief (7377). Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan al-Sukkari relays from Ibn Abi Adi, from Dawud that he asked Abu al-Aliya about those who believed and then disbelieved, and he gave something similar (7378); the editor says the printed text has 'al-Shukri', which is an error, the manuscript's 'al-Sukkari' being correct, and that he is sometimes called 'al-Qannad' after qand, which is sugar. Ibn al-Muthanna relays from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud, who said he asked Abu al-Aliya about the verse and he said that they are the Jews, Christians and Magians who committed sins in their disbelief and wished to repent of them and to repent of disbelief, and 'do you not see that God calls them astray' (7379); the editor says he fears that the correct reading is 'and did not repent of disbelief'. Ibn Bashshar relays from Abu Asim, from Sufyan, from Dawud, from Abu al-Aliya that they repented of some and did not repent of the root (7380). Ammar relays from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Dawud ibn Abi Hind, from Abu al-Aliya that they are the Jews and Christians who commit sins and say 'we repent' while being polytheists, and that God does not receive repentance in error (7381).

**Third view: persisting in disbelief (7382).** Others said the sense of the addition is their persisting in disbelief until they perished remaining in it, and that the first faith does not benefit them because of the last disbelief and death. Al-Qasim relays from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ikrima that they persisted in their disbelief, and Ibn Jurayj said that their first faith will not benefit them (7382). The editor says that to persist in a thing is to remain steadfast in it, that the manuscript's reading is correct, and that the printed text has a poor sentence.

**Fourth view: dying in disbelief (7383).** Others said the sense of the addition is that they died unbelievers, which was their addition to their disbelief, and the sense of their turning back not being received is that it is not received at their death. Muhammad relays from Ahmad, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that they died as unbelievers, and that if he repents at his death his repentance is not received (7383).

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the most correct of these views is that of those who said the Jews are meant, and the interpretation is: those of the Jews who disbelieved in Muhammad at his sending after their belief in him before his sending, and then added to disbelief through the sins they committed in their disbelief and persistence in error, will not have their turning from the sins they committed in their disbelief received until they repent of their disbelief in Muhammad and return in repentance by confirming him in what he brought from God. He says he preferred this because the verses before it and after it came down about them, so it is better that it have the same sense as what precedes and follows, being in one context. He holds that the addition is the transgressions they committed, because God said it is not received, so it is known that the sense is that their turning from what they added to their disbelief after belief is not received, not from their disbelief itself. For God promised to receive repentance from His servants, and he cites the verse of al-Shura [[passage/quran-42-25|42:25]], so it is impossible that He say 'I receive' and 'I do not receive' of one thing. Since it is God's rule for His servants that He receives the repentance of every repentant from every sin, and disbelief after faith is one of the sins whose repentance He promised to receive [[passage/quran-3-89|3:89]], it is known that the sense for which He does not receive repentance differs from the sense for which He does; and that for which He does not receive it is the adding of disbelief to disbelief, the repentance of whose doer God does not receive as long as he remains in his disbelief, because God receives no deed from a polytheist while he remains in his polytheism and error. But if he repents of his polytheism and disbelief and mends his ways, then God, as He has described Himself, is forgiving and merciful. The editor adds in the notes that the copyist dropped the words 'after that' from the preceding verse, that the correct form is the one of the manuscript with the third-person or second-person letter as it stands, that the plural of 'their repentance' is a mistake and the singular correct, and that the following objection is an answer to those who held the views in reports 7382 and 7383 and those before them.

**An objection and its answer (pp. 582-583).** Al-Tabari raises an objection: why do you deny that the meaning is as those said who said that his repentance from his disbelief at the arrival of his term, and his first repentance, will not be received? He answers that he denies it because repentance by a servant exists only in his lifetime, and after his death there is no repentance, and God promised His servants the reception of repentance as long as their spirits are in their bodies. He says there is no disagreement among all the authorities that an unbeliever who became Muslim a blink of an eye before his soul left has the ruling of the Muslims regarding the prayer over him, inheritance and the other rulings, so it is known that if his repentance in that state were not received, his ruling would not move from that of the unbelievers to that of the people of Islam, and there is no station between death and life in which it could be said that God does not receive the repentance of the unbeliever; so if it is sound that it is received in his lifetime and there is no way to it after death, the saying of the one who claimed that it is not received at the arrival of the term is invalid. (This is al-Tabari's argument, presented by the museum as his, without a verdict.) As for the saying of the one who claimed that the sense is the repentance that came before the disbelief, he says it has no meaning, because God did not describe the people as having a faith that came after disbelief and then a disbelief after faith; He described them only with disbelief after faith, so no disbelief preceded the faith for which the faith would be a repentance, and the interpretation of the Quran by what is found in the plain recitation, when no proof points to a particular hidden sense, is better than another reading even if it could be directed to it.

**The sense of those who have gone astray.** Al-Tabari says that those who disbelieved after having believed and then added to their disbelief are the ones who lost the way of truth, erred from its path and left the middle of the way and the guidance of religion, out of perplexity and a wilful blindness to it. He refers to the earlier account of the sense of error. In a long note the editor says that the printed text has a sentence Abu Jafar never wrote, that the manuscript is unpointed so the publisher could not read it, that the correct reading is the one he put, that he would have liked to read 'the aim of the way' but judged that Abu Jafar was rendering the sense of the phrase for the middle of the way, which he explained earlier in volume 2, p. 497, and that people say 'the middle of the road', which is sound in sense and fine as a figure in Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 584}
