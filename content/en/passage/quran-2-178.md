## Summary
Verse 178 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "O you who believe, retaliation is prescribed for you in the matter of the slain: the free for the free, the slave for the slave, the female for the female" al-Tabari says "kutiba" means "was made obligatory", and asks whether retaliation was made obligatory on the slain man's guardian, answering that for him it is permitted, as are pardon and taking the blood-money; the sense is that when a free man kills a free man the killer's blood is equal to the slain's and retaliation is on him and no other, so do not go beyond the killer; the obligation is to refrain from exceeding, not that retaliation is obligatory like prayer and fasting, for if it were, "whoever is pardoned something by his brother" would have no meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 357} It is also said the sense of qisas is offsetting some blood-money against others, as the verse was revealed about two clans who fought in the Prophet's time and he ordered a settlement by cancelling women's blood-money against women's, men's against men's, slaves' against slaves'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 357} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 358} Asked whether retaliation for a free man is only from a free man, he says we may retaliate for a free man from a slave and for a female from a male, by the verse "whoever is killed wrongfully, We have given his guardian authority" and what is widely reported from the Prophet, "the blood of Muslims is equal". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 358}

He reports the interpreters' differences on the verse. Some said it was revealed about people who, when their slave killed another people's slave, would not accept the killer slave but killed his master, and when a woman killed a man would not be satisfied until they killed a man of her clan; God told them retaliation is killing the killer and no other. From al-Sha'bi, it was revealed about two tribes who fought a blind fight, and from Qatada much the same, adding that God afterwards sent down in al-Ma'ida "a life for a life", and that those before us had no blood-money, only killing or pardon; from Mujahid, "the free for the free" includes man for woman and woman for man, and from 'Ata' there is no difference between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 359} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 360} Others said it was revealed about two parties who fought in the Prophet's time and he ordered the blood-money of women, men and slaves to be set off one against the other, from al-Suddi, Abu Malik and al-Sha'bi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 360} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 361} Others said it is an order to set off the blood-money of free and slave, male and female, in deliberate killing if retaliation is taken from the killer, with the difference paid back between the two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 361}

From al-Rabi' on the authority of 'Ali ibn Abi Talib on setting off blood-money: if a free man kills a slave he is liable to retaliation, and if the slave's masters wish they may kill him, setting off the slave's price against the free man's blood-money and paying the rest of it; if a slave kills a free man, the free man's guardians may kill the slave and take the rest of the blood-money, or take it all and spare the slave; if a free man kills a woman he is liable to retaliation and if her guardians wish they kill him and pay half the blood-money; and in a report from 'Ali, al-Hasan and al-Sha'bi about a man who killed his wife, if they wish they kill him and return half the blood-money, or the difference between a man's and a woman's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 361} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 362} Others said it was revealed when people did not kill a man for a woman but a man for a man and a woman for a woman, until God made the rule the same for all by "a life for a life" in al-Ma'ida, from Ibn 'Abbas, so that free persons are equal in retaliation for life and below it, and likewise slaves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 362} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 363}

Al-Tabari then says that since the occasion is disputed, the verse must be applied to the ruling it shows by the report that cuts excuse, and the reports from the Prophet are widely transmitted that a free man's life is retaliation for a free woman's; he holds the view of retaliation with repayment of the difference in blood-money to be mistaken, since Muslims agree it is forbidden for a man to destroy a limb of his body for a price, so a man's life must be retaliation for a woman's; so the verse does not mean a slave is not killed for a free man, nor a male for a female; it means either that retaliation must not go beyond the killer, or that it was revealed about certain people ordered to set off the blood-money of their dead, as al-Suddi said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 363} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 364} He holds that setting off rights is, by agreement, not obligatory and that God did not decree it and then abrogate it, and "prescribed for you" indicates an obligation, which invalidates the second view and sustains the first. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 364}

Asked what "kutiba" means, he answers that in Arabic speech and poetry it means "was made obligatory", citing lines, and says it is taken from the "book" as drawn writing, since God wrote what He made obligatory in the Preserved Tablet; so the sense is that retaliation in the matter of the slain was written for you in the Preserved Tablet as an obligation not to kill anyone but the killer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 365} "Qisas" is from "I settled my right with so-and-so", killing the killer for the one he killed is qisas because the same is done to him, and "qatla" is the plural of "qatil"; so the sense is: retaliation is prescribed for you, believers, in the slain, that the free be retaliated against for the free, the slave for the slave, the female for the female. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 365} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 366}

On "but whoever is pardoned something by his brother, then let there be a reasonable pursuit and a payment to him in good manner" he reports the interpreters' differences. Some said: for the one to whom some retaliation is waived, the pardoner pursues the killer for the blood-money due, and the pardoned pays him well; from Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid, pardon is to accept the blood-money in deliberate killing, a reasonable pursuit is for the claimant to ask fairly and the one claimed against to pay well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 366} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 367}

Reports continue from Mujahid, al-Hasan, al-Sha'bi, Qatada, al-Rabi', 'Ata', Ibn Zayd and Ibn 'Abbas that the sense is: one who killed deliberately is pardoned and the blood-money is accepted from him, the claimant pursuing fairly and the one claimed against paying well; and from Qatada, deliberate killing is retaliation with no blood-money unless they agree to it, and if they agree it is as they settle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 367} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 368} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 369} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 370}

Others said "pardoned" means something is left over, some remainder, of his brother's blood-money or the price of his wound, which he pursues fairly and the killer pays well; this is the view of those who said the verse was revealed about those who fought in the Prophet's time and were ordered to set off their blood-moneys; al-Tabari supposes they took "pardon" as abundance, as in "until they increased", and from al-Suddi, something remains to him of his brother's blood-money or wound price, let him pursue fairly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 370} He adds that on the view of 'Ali and al-Hasan about offsetting the sense would be: whoever is relieved of what is due to his brother, to the point of accepting the slain man's blood-money, then the guardian pursues fairly and the killer pays well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 370} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 371}

Al-Tabari chooses that the sense is: whoever is forgiven something of what was due to his brother from retaliation, for blood-money taken from him, then a reasonable pursuit by the one who pardons the blood and accepts the blood-money, and payment by the killer in good manner, because retaliation in the verse is retaliation from the persons who killed, wounded or struck deliberately, and so is pardon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 371} "Reasonable pursuit" is according to what God made due to him from the killer, without adding what is not his; from Qatada, reported of the Prophet, whoever adds or increases a camel in the blood-money camels is of the way of the pre-Islamic time; good payment is paying what he owes without shortchanging or forcing a demand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 371} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 372} He explains that the nominative in "ittiba'" means the matter in it is a reasonable pursuit, which is more eloquent than the accusative, comparing it with verses in al-Ma'ida and al-Baqara, "either retaining honourably or releasing with kindness", and distinguishing it from "strike the necks", accusative because it is an exhortation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 372}

On "that is an alleviation from your Lord and a mercy" he says the ruling that pardon of retaliation for blood-money owned like other property is allowed, which was forbidden to earlier nations, is an alleviation and mercy; from Ibn 'Abbas, among the Children of Israel there was retaliation and no blood-money, and God lightened it for this community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 373}

Reports continue on "that is an alleviation from your Lord and a mercy": from Ibn 'Abbas, those before us killed the killer for the slain and the blood-money was not accepted from them, and the Children of Israel had retaliation for life and wound with no blood-money; from Qatada and al-Rabi', God showed this community mercy by making blood-money lawful, which was lawful for none before; the people of the Torah had only retaliation or pardon and the people of the Gospel pardon, and this community has retaliation, pardon and the blood-money. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 373} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 374} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 375} On al-Suddi's view of setting off blood-moneys the sense would be: what I did to you in setting off the blood-money of some of you against others and not requiring retaliation is an alleviation and mercy from Me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 375}

On "whoever transgresses after that will have a painful punishment" al-Tabari says: whoever goes beyond what God allowed him after taking the blood-money, to kill the killer of his kin, will have a painful punishment, from Mujahid, Qatada, al-Rabi', Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn Zayd and al-Suddi; from Qatada, it was mentioned that the Prophet used to say "I will not excuse a man who kills after taking the blood-money"; and from al-Hasan, in pre-Islamic times a man who killed fled to his people, who made peace with blood-money, and he came out safe and was killed, and that was the transgression. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 375} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 376} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 378} He reports their difference on the painful punishment: some said it is being killed for the one he killed after taking the blood-money, from al-Dahhak, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and 'Ikrima; some said it is a penalty the ruler imposes as he sees fit, and he cites from Ibn Jurayj a report from the Prophet that he made it binding that a man who pardoned the blood and took the blood-money, then attacked and killed, is not to be excused, and that judgment on him belongs to the ruler. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 378} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 379}
