## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "O you who believe, retaliation is prescribed for you in the matter of the slain: the free for the free, the slave for the slave, the female for the female" al-Tabari says "kutiba" means "was made obligatory", and asks whether retaliation was made obligatory on the slain man's guardian, answering that for him it is permitted, as are pardon and taking the blood-money; the sense is that when a free man kills a free man the killer's blood is equal to the slain's and retaliation is on him and no other, so do not go beyond the killer; the obligation is to refrain from exceeding, not that retaliation is obligatory like prayer and fasting, for if it were, "whoever is pardoned something by his brother" would have no meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 357} It is also said the sense of qisas is offsetting some blood-money against others, as the verse was revealed about two clans who fought in the Prophet's time and he ordered a settlement by cancelling women's blood-money against women's, men's against men's, slaves' against slaves'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 357} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 358} Asked whether retaliation for a free man is only from a free man, he says we may retaliate for a free man from a slave and for a female from a male, by the verse "whoever is killed wrongfully, We have given his guardian authority" and what is widely reported from the Prophet, "the blood of Muslims is equal". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 358}

He reports the interpreters' differences on the verse. Some said it was revealed about people who, when their slave killed another people's slave, would not accept the killer slave but killed his master, and when a woman killed a man would not be satisfied until they killed a man of her clan; God told them retaliation is killing the killer and no other. From al-Sha'bi, it was revealed about two tribes who fought a blind fight, and from Qatada much the same, adding that God afterwards sent down in al-Ma'ida "a life for a life", and that those before us had no blood-money, only killing or pardon; from Mujahid, "the free for the free" includes man for woman and woman for man, and from 'Ata' there is no difference between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 359} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 360} Others said it was revealed about two parties who fought in the Prophet's time and he ordered the blood-money of women, men and slaves to be set off one against the other, from al-Suddi, Abu Malik and al-Sha'bi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 360} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 361} Others said it is an order to set off the blood-money of free and slave, male and female, in deliberate killing if retaliation is taken from the killer, with the difference paid back between the two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 361}
