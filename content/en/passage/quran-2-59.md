## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "but those who did wrong changed the word to other than what had been said to them" al-Tabari says "baddala" means changed, "those who did wrong" are those who did what they had no right to do, and their changing was saying other than what they had been told to say. He gives a report from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet: God told the Children of Israel to enter the gate prostrating and say "hitta", but they changed it and entered the gate crawling on their backsides, saying "a grain in a barley-corn". The editor notes it is in Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Tirmidhi, with a variation between "shi'ra" and "sha'ira" in its wording, and that of the two chains al-Tabari gives from Ibn Ishaq one is sound and connected and the other has an unnamed narrator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 112} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 113}
The reports give differing forms of what they said: from Ibn Mas'ud "a red wheat with a barley-corn in it"; from Ibn Abbas that they were told to enter bowing through a small gate and entered backsides first saying "wheat" in mockery; from Qatada and al-Hasan that they came in crawling on their haunches; from Mujahid that the gate was lowered so they would prostrate, but they did not and entered on their backs; from al-Suddi, through Ibn Mas'ud, that they said a non-Arabic phrase meaning a pierced red grain of wheat with a black barley-corn in it; from al-Rabi' that each one's prostration was on his cheek. Ibn Zayd says they mocked Moses saying "hitta, hitta, what is hitta?", and said to one another "wheat"; Ikrima that they entered with their heads covered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 113} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 114} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 115}
On "so We sent down upon those who did wrong a scourge from heaven for their transgression" he holds that those who did wrong are those who changed the word and disobeyed, and that "rijz" in Arabic is punishment, distinct from "rujz" with damma, as in surah al-Muddaththir. He cites the report of Usama ibn Zayd from the Prophet about plague, that it is a scourge with which some nations before you were punished; the editor notes its origin in the two Sahihs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 116}
