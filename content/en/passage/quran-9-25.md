## Summary
Verse 25 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-hunayn|Battle of Hunayn]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 25 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 25 (pp. 178-187)**

Al-Tabari says: God has helped you, believers, in many places of battle where you steeled yourselves to meet your enemy and many scenes where you met them, and on the day of Hunayn too He helped you. Hunayn is a valley, it is reported, between Mecca and al-Ta'if; the name is declined because it is a masculine name for a masculine thing, though it may be left undeclined when meant for the town it is in, and he cites a line of Hassan ibn Thabit praising the Prophet's Helpers for backing him and bracing his strength at Hunayn on the day when the heroes left one another to fend for themselves. The editor refers to al-Farra and explains that the Prophet forbade leaving one another to fend for themselves, each man abandoning his companion to himself instead of helping him, which leads to weakness, estrangement and the ruin of affairs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 178}

Report 16573, from Urwa, through Abd al-Warith ibn Abd al-Samad, his father, Aban al-Attar and Hisham ibn Urwa: Hunayn is a valley beside Dhu al-Majaz. "When your great number pleased you": they were that day, it is reported, twelve thousand; it is related that the Prophet said that day that they would not be overcome for want of numbers, or, it is said, a Muslim man said it. "It availed you nothing" means your numbers did not; "the earth was narrowed for you for all its breadth" means it narrowed on you in its vastness, the preposition standing for "in"; "then you turned back, retreating" from your enemy in rout. God informs them that victory is in His hand and from Him, not by greatness of numbers and strength, and that He helps the few against the many when He wills and leaves many and few alone so that the many are routed. The editor notes that the printed text dropped words and spoiled the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 180}

Report 16574, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: Hunayn lies between Mecca and al-Ta'if, where God's prophet fought Hawazin and Thaqif, Hawazin under Malik ibn Awf of Banu Nasr and Thaqif under Abd Yalil ibn Amr al-Thaqafi; twelve thousand went out with the Messenger, ten thousand of the Emigrants and Helpers and two thousand of the freed Meccans, and it was said that a man declared they would not be overcome that day by want of numbers; the freed Meccans bolted, scattering the people, and the people turned from God's prophet until he dismounted from his grey mule and said "Lord, give me what You promised"; al-Abbas held his mule's bridle, and he told him to call out to the Helpers and Emigrants, which he did clan by clan, and then to call the companions of Sura al-Baqara, and the people came as one stream. He turned to a band of the Helpers and asked if anyone else was with them, and they said by God if he went to Birk al-Ghimad in Yemen they would be with him; then God sent down His help, routed their enemy and the Muslims rallied. He took a handful of earth or pebbles and threw them at the faces of the unbelievers, saying "shame on the faces", and they fled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 181}

When he gathered the spoils and came to al-Ji'rana he divided the spoils of Hunayn, winning over certain people including Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, al-Harith ibn Hisham, Suhayl ibn Amr and al-Aqra ibn Habis, and the Helpers said the man had found safety and preferred his own people; this reached the Messenger in his leather tent and he asked what had reached him, saying were they not astray and God guided them, and humbled and God exalted them. Sa'd ibn Ubada asked leave to speak and said as to being astray they had been so, and as to being humbled and exalted the Arabs knew no clan better at protecting what lay behind them; Umar asked whether he knew whom he was addressing and he said he was addressing the Messenger of God. The Messenger said that if the Helpers took one valley and the people another he would take the Helpers' valley, and that but for the emigration he would be one of the Helpers; it was related that he said the Helpers were his inner circle and his bag, so accept from their good-doers and overlook their wrong-doers. Then he asked whether they were not content that the people should go home with camels and sheep while they went home with God's messenger; the Helpers said they were content with God and His messenger and had said it only from concern for the Messenger, and he said God and His messenger believe you and excuse you. The editor explains Birk al-Ghimad, a correction to the printed text, and the terms for the inner circle and the bag. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 182}

Report 16575, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was told that the Messenger's foster mother of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr came and asked him for captives of Hunayn, and he said he did not own them and had only his share, but to come the next day and ask him with the people present, since when he gave her his share the people would give theirs; she came, he spread a cloth for her and she sat on it, he gave her his share, and the people gave theirs on seeing that. Report 16576, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: a man among the Companions said at Hunayn they would not be overcome that day for want of numbers, liking the great number, twelve thousand; they were left to that man's word and fled from the Messenger except al-Abbas, Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith and Ayman ibn Umm Ayman, who was killed before him that day; the Messenger called out where the Helpers were and where those who pledged under the tree were, the people returned and God sent down angels with victory, routing the polytheists, which is what is at [[passage/quran-9-26|9:26]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 182}

Report 16577, from al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, al-Zuhri and Kathir ibn al-Abbas: at Hunayn the Muslims and polytheists met and the Muslims turned back; I saw the Prophet with no one but Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Muttalib, holding his stirrup and sparing no effort; he said: I went and took hold of his bridle, he being on his grey mule, and he said "Abbas, call the companions of the acacia", and I was a loud-voiced man, so I called out with my highest voice "where are the companions of the acacia?"; they turned like camels being gathered to their young, saying "at your service" three times, and the polytheists came on and met the Muslims; the Helpers called one another, then the call narrowed to Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj, and the Messenger, on his mule, looking toward their fighting as if craning to see, said "now the furnace is hot"; then he took a handful of pebbles in his hand, threw them at the enemy and said "they are defeated, by the Lord of the Kaaba", twice; their affair kept turning back and their edge dulled until God defeated them, and I seem to see the Messenger spurring behind them on his mule. The editor explains the words for stirrup, loud-voiced and furnace, and says Kathir ibn al-Abbas was a reliable Successor with few reports, and that the report is given by Ahmad in his Musnad, Muslim in his Sahih, al-Hakim in the Mustadrak and Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 183}

Report 16578, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, through Ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, Qatada and al-Zuhri: they took six thousand captives that day, and then their people came as Muslims and said Messenger of God, you are the best and most dutiful of people, and you have taken our sons, wives and property; he said that he had with him those they saw, that the best speech is the most truthful, and to choose either their children and women or their property; they said they set nothing against lineage; so he stood and said these had come to him as Muslims and had been given the choice between children and property and set nothing against lineage, so whoever held any of them and was content to return them should do so, and whoever was not should give them to the Muslims as a loan against him until something was gained and given in return; they said they were content and had submitted, and he said he did not know whether some of them were not content, so they should tell their chiefs to refer it back to him, and the chiefs reported that they had agreed and submitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 184}

Report 16579, from Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Fihri, through Ali ibn Sahl, Mu'ammal, Hammad ibn Salama, Ya'la ibn Ata and Abu Hammam: I was with the Prophet in the campaign of Hunayn, and when the sun stood at its height I put on my armour, mounted my horse and came to the Prophet in the shade of a tree, saying "Messenger of God, the time to set out has come", and he said yes and called Bilal, who rose from under an acacia, his shadow like a bird's, and said "at your service, my life be your ransom"; he told him to saddle his horse, and he brought out a saddle padded with palm fibre, with neither arrogance nor insolence in it; he mounted and we drew up in ranks facing them that day and night, and when the two armies met the Muslims turned back, as God said; the Messenger called out "servants of God, band of Emigrants", leaned from his horse, took a handful of earth, threw it in their faces and they turned back; Ya'la ibn Ata said their sons told him from their fathers that every one of them had his eyes filled with that dust. The editor gives the narrators and the sources of Ahmad, Ibn Sa'd, Abu Dawud, Ibn Abd al-Barr, Ibn al-Athir and al-Haythami. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 185}

Report 16580, from al-Bara', through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Abu Ishaq: a man of Qays asked whether they had fled from the Messenger on the day of Hunayn, and al-Bara' said the Messenger did not flee; Hawazin were archers, and when we charged them they gave way, we fell on the booty and they met us with arrows; I saw the Messenger on his white mule with Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith holding its bridle, saying that he was the Prophet, no lie, and the son of Abd al-Muttalib. Report 16581, from al-Bara', through Ibn Waki, his father, Isra'il and Abu Ishaq: asked whether he had turned back at Hunayn, he said he bore witness that the Messenger did not turn his back that day while Abu Sufyan led his mule, and when the polytheists pressed on him he dismounted and said the same declaration; none was seen that day fiercer than he. The editor notes that Muslim and al-Bukhari give this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 186}

Report 16582, from a man who was a polytheist at Hunayn, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Ja'far ibn Sulayman, Awf al-A'rabi and Abd al-Rahman, freedman of Umm Burthun: when we met Muhammad's companions they did not stand against us for the time of milking a sheep before we broke them, and as we drove them we came to the master of the grey mule, and men, white and handsome of face, met us and said "shame on the faces, go back"; we went back, and the people rode us down, and that was that. The editor explains Abd al-Rahman's name and the two figurative expressions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 186}

Report 16583, from Sa'id, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Ya'qub and Ja'far: God reinforced His prophet at Hunayn with five thousand marked angels, and that day God named the Helpers "believers", and sent down His tranquillity on His messenger and on the believers and sent down hosts they did not see. Report 16584, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: they were twelve thousand. Report 16585, from Yazid ibn Amir, through Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Adami, Ma'n ibn Isa, Sa'id ibn al-Sa'ib al-Ta'ifi and his father: when the Muslims gave way at Hunayn, the Prophet struck his hand to the ground, took a handful of earth, turned to the polytheists pursuing the Muslims and flung it in their faces, saying "go back, shame on the faces"; we turned back, and no one met another without wiping the grit from his eyes. The editor notes that al-Bukhari gives the report in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 187}
