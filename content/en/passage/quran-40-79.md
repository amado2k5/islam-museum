## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 79 to 81 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Allah who made for you cattle that you may ride from and from which you may eat": "Allah" is the only one suitable for divinity, O polytheists of Quraysh, "who made for you livestock" of camels, cattle, sheep, horses, and other livestock that the people of Islam purchase for a boat or a restaurant, "so you may ride from them," meaning horses and donkeys, "and from them you may eat," meaning camels, cattle, and sheep. And he said: 'So that you may ride from it,' meaning: to ride some of it, and some of it to eat, so it was omitted as self-sufficiency as indicated by what was omitted. And His saying: 'And you will have benefits therein,' is because He made for you from its skins houses that you would take for granted on the day you were hiding, on the day you dwelled, and from its wool, its wool, its wool, and its poetry as furniture and goods for a while. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Qatadah.
