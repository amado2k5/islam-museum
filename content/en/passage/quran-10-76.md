**Commentary on verse 76 (pp. 155-156)**

On [[passage/quran-10-76|10:76]] al-Tabari says: 'when the truth came to them from Us' means when the explanation of what Moses and Aaron called them to came to them, the proofs they brought, which are the truth from God; 'they said, this is clear magic', meaning that to one who sees and beholds it, it is plainly magic with no reality; 'Moses said' to them, 'do you say of the truth when it has come to you' from God, 'is this magic?' The people of Arabic differed on the reason for the question alif in 'is this magic?': some of the Basran grammarians said it was added as a quotation of their words, for they said 'is this magic?' and he said 'do you say: is this magic?' {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 155}

Some of the Kufan grammarians said they said 'this is magic' without the alif, since most readings have none; if one asks why the alif was added, it may be from their saying while they knew it was magic, as a man says to a prize that comes to him 'is this real?' knowing it is real, or it may be astonishment on their part: magic, how great it is! The editor refers to al-Farra'. Al-Tabari says the soundest view for him is that the object is dropped, and 'is this magic?' is Moses' saying, rejecting Pharaoh's and his chiefs' calling the truth 'magic' when it came to them. The meaning is then: do you say of the truth when it came to you, the signs he brought as proof of his truthfulness, 'magic'? Is this truth that you see magic? The first 'magic' is dropped because Moses' words show it is meant. He gives for this the lines of Dhu al-Rumma in which the verb 'came on' is dropped because the sense shows it, and a similar omission in [[passage/quran-17-7|17:7]], and many like it that would be wearying to count. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 156}
