## Summary
Verse 56 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 56 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The threat for those who persist in denial (p. 484, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says this is a threat from God to those among the Jews of Israel and other unbelievers who persisted in denying what was sent down to Muhammad; God's signs are the indications of His revelation, the inspiration of His book and its proofs of Muhammad's truthfulness. To roast them means to cook them in a fire in which they are grilled; the skins' being done means being scorched and burned, and the exchange of skins means other than the skins that were done. The editor refers to earlier explanations of roasting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 484}

**Reports on the exchanged skins (pp. 484-485, reports 9833-9837, notes).** Ibn Humayd (9833, from Jarir, from al-A'mash, from Thuwayr) reports from Ibn Umar that when their skins burn they are given white skins like sheets of paper; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9834, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada that whenever their skins burn they are given others; al-Muthanna (9835, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father) from al-Rabi' that they heard it written in the first book that one of them has a skin forty cubits thick, a molar seventy cubits and a belly that would hold a mountain placed in it, and when the fire eats their skins they are given others; al-Muthanna (9836, from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak) from al-Hasan, as a report that reached him, that they are cooked seventy thousand times a day; al-Qasim (9837, from al-Husayn, from Abu Ubayda al-Haddad, from Hisham b. Hassan) from al-Hasan that the fire cooks seventy thousand skins a day and an unbeliever's skin is forty cubits thick, followed in the report by the remark that God knows best by which cubit. In his notes the editor says Thuwayr is Thuwayr b. Abi Fakhita Sa'id b. Ulaqa al-Hashimi, whose entry has appeared earlier; that the print has a corrupted name and the manuscript is unpointed; that the print has a wrong word in report 9833 and the manuscript is right; that a qirtas is a white sheet for writing; that in report 9835 he put the manuscript's wording over the print's in two places; and that Abu Ubayda al-Haddad is Abd al-Wahid b. Wasil al-Sadusi and the entry for Hisham b. Hassan al-Qardusi appeared earlier under a number the scan blurs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 485}

**The questioner (p. 485).** Al-Tabari puts a question: what is the sense of the exchange of skins, and may they be given skins other than those they had in the world and be tormented in them? If so, then bodies and spirits might be exchanged too, the ones tormented in the hereafter would not be those God threatened for their disbelief, and the punishment would have been lifted from the unbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 485}

**First view: the torment reaches the person, not the skin (p. 486, note 1).** Al-Tabari says people differed. Some said the torment reaches the human being, who is other than skin and flesh; the skin is burned only so the pain reaches the person, while skin and flesh feel nothing; so it is the same whether the unbeliever's original skin is restored or another, since what suffers is the self that feels, and it is not impossible that countless skins be created for each unbeliever at every moment and burned on him. The editor notes that the manuscript lacks the word "other than" here and that the first edition's publisher rightly added it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 486}

**Second view: skins feel, and the sense is unburnt skins (pp. 486-487, note 2).** Others said the skins do feel, as do the flesh and all the parts of the body; the exchange means they are restored new and unburnt after the first burned, and are called "other" because they differ from the skins they had in the world. They compared the Arabs' saying to a goldsmith who is asked to make another ring from a ring: he breaks it and makes another, and the ring made the second time is the first, yet since it was restored after breaking it is called another. The editor notes that this verb form is not recorded in the dictionaries, but is genuine Arabic and sound analogy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 487}

**Third view: the tar garments (p. 487, notes 1-5).** Others said the skins here are garments of tar, called skins for being peculiar to them, as one says of something close to a person that it is the skin between his eyes and face; they cite the verse of Abraham on tar garments and fire covering faces ([[passage/quran-14-50|14:50]]); each time the tar blazes on their bodies and burns, they are given other garments of tar. They said the unbelievers' skins in the fire do not burn, because burning until restoration would be their annihilation, and in annihilation lies relief; God has said they will not die nor be lightened; the skin is one of their bodily parts, and if one part could burn, perish and be restored, all could, so perishing then restoration and death then revival would be possible for them, yet God said they do not die. In his notes the editor records where print and manuscript differ (the form of the burning, a phrase "others said", a bracketed addition he calls indispensable, the form of "burn"), and glosses their word for annihilation as meaning that they perish until restored and that perishing implies an interval of lighter torment, which he calls false, as the following arguments will show. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 487}

**The purpose of the exchange (p. 487).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: We did that to them so that they feel the pain of the torment, its distress and severity, because they denied God's signs in the world and rejected them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 487}

**The close of the verse (p. 488, note 2).** Al-Tabari says God has always been mighty in taking vengeance on whom He takes it from among His creatures, none able to withstand Him if He wills harm or to gain victory over Him if He brings punishment on them, and wise in His ordering and decree. The editor refers to the language indexes for earlier explanations of the two names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 488}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 488}
