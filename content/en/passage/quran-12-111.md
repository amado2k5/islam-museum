**Commentary on verse 111 (pp. 312-315)**

On "in their stories there is a lesson for people of understanding; it was not a narration invented, but a confirmation of what came before it, a detailed explanation of everything, and a guidance and mercy for a people who believe", al-Tabari says: in the stories of Joseph and his brothers there was a lesson for people of sense and intellect, by which they take heed, and an admonition by which they are warned. For after God had cast Joseph into the well to perish, and he was sold as slaves are sold for a paltry price, and after captivity and a long imprisonment, He made him king of Egypt, established him in the land, raised him over his brothers who had wished him evil, and by His power united him with his parents and brothers after the long period, bringing them to him from a far distant journey; so God, mighty be His praise, said to the polytheists of Quraysh, the people of His prophet Muhammad: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 312}

there was for you, O people, in their stories a lesson, if you took it, that the One who did that for Joseph and his brothers is not prevented from doing the like for Muhammad, bringing him out from among you, then making him prevail over you, establishing him in the lands and supporting him with troops and men of followers and companions, though hardships pass over him and days, nights, ages and times come between. Mujahid used to say that the meaning is: in their stories there was a lesson for Joseph and his brothers. Reports 20038 to 20041, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj, all from Ibn Abi Najih: "in their stories there was a lesson" for Joseph and his brothers. Al-Tabari says: this saying of Mujahid, though it has a possible face, is less fitting than what we have said, because the verse comes right after the report about our Prophet and his polytheist people and after the threat and warning to them over disbelief in God and His messenger Muhammad, and is detached from the report of Joseph and his brothers; moreover {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 313}

it is a general report about all people of understanding that their stories are a lesson for them, not specific to some and not others; since the matter is as we have described, it is more like a report that it is a lesson for others. The report we mentioned from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj is more likely to be Ibn Jurayj's own words, since it agrees with what we have said. The editor notes the words "ashbah" and "min" which he added to the text so that it reads correctly. On "it was not a narration invented" he says: this speech was not a story made up, falsely claimed or conjectured. Report 20042, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "it was not a narration invented", and "firya" is lying. "But a confirmation of what came before it" means a confirmation of what lies before it of God's books which He sent down before it to His prophets, like the Torah, the Gospel and the Psalms, confirming all of that and witnessing that all of it is true from God. Report 20043, from Qatada: "but a confirmation of what came before it", the Furqan confirms the books before it and witnesses over them. On "a detailed explanation of everything" he says: it is also a detailing of all that servants need of the explanation of God's command and prohibition, His lawful and unlawful, obedience and disobedience. On "and a guidance and mercy for a people who believe" he says: it is the explanation of His command {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 314}

and His guidance for whoever is ignorant of the path of truth and blind to it, when he follows it and is guided by it out of his misguidance; "and mercy" for whoever believes in it and acts on what is in it, saving him from God's anger and painful punishment and bequeathing him in the Hereafter His gardens and eternal abiding in lasting bliss; "for a people who believe" means a people who confirm the Qur'an and what is in it of God's promise and warning, command and prohibition, and so act on what it commands and refrain from what it forbids. With this the commentary on Sura Yusuf ends in this volume; the manuscript adds after it that what follows is the commentary on the sura in which al-Ra'd is mentioned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 315}

Here al-Tabari's commentary on Sura Yusuf ends in volume 16 of Shakir's edition; the next batch begins the commentary on Sura al-Ra'd at p. 319. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 315}
