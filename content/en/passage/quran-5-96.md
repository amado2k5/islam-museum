## Summary
Verse 96 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 96 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food": lawful to you, believers, is sea game, meaning what is caught fresh. Report 12667, from Abu Hurayra, from Umar ibn al-Khattab: its game is what is caught of it (the editor notes the weakness of Umar ibn Abi Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf according to Ibn Sa'd and Shu'ba, and that this report is an abridgement of 12687). Report 12668, from Ibn Abbas: Abu Bakr preached to the people and said of the verse, its game is what is taken. Report 12669, from Ibn Abbas: its game is what is caught of it (the editor cites al-Bayhaqi). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 57}

Report 12670, from Ibn Abbas: its game is the fresh. Report 12671: its game is what is caught (a note describes Hudhayl ibn Bilal, called weak by Ibn Ma'in and others, though Abu Hatim said he is truthful). Report 12672, from Ibn Abbas: the fresh. Report 12673, from Ikrima: Ibn Abbas used to say sea game is what he caught (the editor records al-Tabari's doubt about the transmitter's name, al-Hasan or al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Hanafi). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 58}

Report 12674, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the fresh. Report 12675, from Abu Salama: sea game is what is caught. Reports 12676 to 12678, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: fresh fish. Report 12679, from al-Suddi: sea game is fresh fish, that is the fishes. Report 12680, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: its game is what you catch fresh; Qatada said what you catch. Report 12681, from Mujahid: its fishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 59}

Report 12682, from Zayd ibn Thabit: its game is what you catch. Report 12683, from Mujahid on "and its food, a provision for you and for travellers": the pilgrim and the non-pilgrim may fish from the sea and eat its game. Report 12684, from Ikrima: Abu Bakr said the food of the sea is everything in it; Jabir ibn Abd Allah said, eat what it has receded from. Report 12685, from Abu Bakr on "and its food": it is everything in it. Al-Tabari says the "sea" here means all rivers, since the Arabs call rivers "seas", as in "corruption has appeared on land and sea". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 60}

So the sense, for al-Tabari: lawful to you, believers, is the fresh fish of the rivers that you caught in your lawful state or in ihram, and of its food what you did not catch, which it killed and threw onto its shore. The interpreters differ over "its food". Some said it means what it casts dead onto its shore, as he said. Report 12686, from Ibn Abbas: Abu Bakr preached and said, its food is what it casts up. Report 12687, from Abu Hurayra: I was in Bahrain and they asked me about what the sea cast up and I gave them the opinion that they might eat; when I came to Umar ibn al-Khattab and told him, he asked: on what basis did you give them the opinion? I said, that they eat; he said: had you told them otherwise I would have struck you with the whip! Then he said, God says in His Book "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you", so its game is what is caught of it and its food is what it casts up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 61}

Reports 12688, 12689 and 12690, from Ibn Abbas: its food is what it casts up. Report 12691, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas: its food is all that the sea throws out. Report 12692: what it casts out of its dead. Report 12693: its food is what is found dead on the shore (the editor identifies Husayn ibn Ali al-Ju'fi and notes that the transmitter in 12692 is a different man). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 62}

Report 12694, from Ibn Abbas: its food is what it throws up. Report 12695, from Abu Bakr: its food is everything in it. Report 12696, from Ikrima: Abu Bakr said its food is its dead; Amr ibn Dinar said, I heard Abu al-Sha'tha say, I thought its food was only the salted. Report 12697, Ibn Abbas: its dead. Report 12698, Ikrima: what it casts. Report 12699, from Nafi: Abd al-Rahman (ibn Abi Hurayra) came to Abd Allah (ibn Umar) and said, the sea has thrown up many fish; he forbade him to eat them, then said: Nafi, bring the mushaf; he read "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you"; I said, its food is what it threw up; he said: go after him and tell him to eat it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 63}

Report 12700, from Nafi, to the same effect: Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hurayra asked Ibn Umar about many dead fish the sea threw up, may we eat them? He said, do not eat them; when he went back to his family he took the mushaf and read Sura al-Ma'ida, and when he reached "and its food, a provision for you and for travellers" he said: go and tell him to eat it, for it is its food (the editor notes that Malik relates it in the Muwatta and that al-Bayhaqi and al-Suyuti cite it). Report 12701, from Ibn Umar, is similar; 12702, from Ikrima, repeats 12696. Report 12703, from Nafi: Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hurayra asked Ibn Umar about many fish the sea cast up, are they carrion? He said yes, and forbade them; then he went into the house, called for the mushaf and read the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 64}

He said: its food is anything taken out of it, eat it, there is no harm, and anything in it is eaten, dead or on its shores. Report 12704, Qatada: its food is what is cast out of it. Reports 12705 and 12706, from Abu Ayyub: what the sea casts up is its food, even if dead. Others said "its food" means the salted fish, so that the verse means: lawful to you is fish of the sea and its salted in every state, in your lawful state and in ihram. Report 12707, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas: its food is the salted of it (the editor discusses the objection to the phrase "salted fish" and gives Ibn Barri's justification of it). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 65}

Report 12708, from Ibn Abbas: by its food He means its salted, and what the sea throws up of it, salted; 12709: it is the salted; 12710, from Ikrima on "a provision for you": the salted; 12711, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the salted; 12712, from Ibrahim: the salted, and what it cast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 66}

Report 12713, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a man comes to the people of the sea and says feed me; if he says fresh, they cast their net and catch for him, and if he says feed me of your food, they feed him of their salted fish. Report 12714, from him: the "manbudh" is the salted fish; 12715: the salted; 12716, from Ibrahim: it is its salted, then he said, what it casts; 12717, from Qatada: salted fish; 12718, from Ibrahim, he used to say salted fish, then later said what it casts up; 12719, from Sa'id: the salted; 12720, from Mujahid: salted fish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 67}

Reports 12721 and 12722, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: its food is "al-sir", meaning the salted (the editor explains "sir" and quotes two lines of Jarir against the family of al-Muhallab). Report 12723, from al-Suddi: its food is the salted. Report 12724, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: its food is what you carry salted for your journey. Report 12725, from Jabir ibn Zayd: we were told its food is its salted, and we dislike the floating fish of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 68}

Others said its food is what is in it. Report 12726, Ikrima: the food of the sea is what is in it. Report 12727, from him: what the sea brings with its waves. Report 12728, from Mujahid: its food is everything caught from it. Al-Tabari chooses the view that its food is what the sea casts up, or what it recedes from and is found dead on its shore, because God mentioned before it the game of what is caught, "lawful to you is the game of the sea", so what should be joined to it in sense is what is not caught of it, as if He said: lawful to you is what you catch from the sea and what you do not catch. As for the salted, what is salted after being caught is already included in "game of the sea", so there is no reason to repeat it, as there is no benefit in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 69}

We stop here at the foot of p. 69, in the middle of the commentary on verse 96, in al-Tabari's choice for "its food" and his reply to those who say it is the salted fish.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
