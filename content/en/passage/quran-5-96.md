## Summary
Verse 96 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 96 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food": lawful to you, believers, is sea game, meaning what is caught fresh. Report 12667, from Abu Hurayra, from Umar ibn al-Khattab: its game is what is caught of it (the editor notes the weakness of Umar ibn Abi Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf according to Ibn Sa'd and Shu'ba, and that this report is an abridgement of 12687). Report 12668, from Ibn Abbas: Abu Bakr preached to the people and said of the verse, its game is what is taken. Report 12669, from Ibn Abbas: its game is what is caught of it (the editor cites al-Bayhaqi). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 57}

Report 12670, from Ibn Abbas: its game is the fresh. Report 12671: its game is what is caught (a note describes Hudhayl ibn Bilal, called weak by Ibn Ma'in and others, though Abu Hatim said he is truthful). Report 12672, from Ibn Abbas: the fresh. Report 12673, from Ikrima: Ibn Abbas used to say sea game is what he caught (the editor records al-Tabari's doubt about the transmitter's name, al-Hasan or al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Hanafi). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 58}

Report 12674, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the fresh. Report 12675, from Abu Salama: sea game is what is caught. Reports 12676 to 12678, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: fresh fish. Report 12679, from al-Suddi: sea game is fresh fish, that is the fishes. Report 12680, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: its game is what you catch fresh; Qatada said what you catch. Report 12681, from Mujahid: its fishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 59}

Report 12682, from Zayd ibn Thabit: its game is what you catch. Report 12683, from Mujahid on "and its food, a provision for you and for travellers": the pilgrim and the non-pilgrim may fish from the sea and eat its game. Report 12684, from Ikrima: Abu Bakr said the food of the sea is everything in it; Jabir ibn Abd Allah said, eat what it has receded from. Report 12685, from Abu Bakr on "and its food": it is everything in it. Al-Tabari says the "sea" here means all rivers, since the Arabs call rivers "seas", as in "corruption has appeared on land and sea". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 60}

So the sense, for al-Tabari: lawful to you, believers, is the fresh fish of the rivers that you caught in your lawful state or in ihram, and of its food what you did not catch, which it killed and threw onto its shore. The interpreters differ over "its food". Some said it means what it casts dead onto its shore, as he said. Report 12686, from Ibn Abbas: Abu Bakr preached and said, its food is what it casts up. Report 12687, from Abu Hurayra: I was in Bahrain and they asked me about what the sea cast up and I gave them the opinion that they might eat; when I came to Umar ibn al-Khattab and told him, he asked: on what basis did you give them the opinion? I said, that they eat; he said: had you told them otherwise I would have struck you with the whip! Then he said, God says in His Book "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you", so its game is what is caught of it and its food is what it casts up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 61}

Reports 12688, 12689 and 12690, from Ibn Abbas: its food is what it casts up. Report 12691, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas: its food is all that the sea throws out. Report 12692: what it casts out of its dead. Report 12693: its food is what is found dead on the shore (the editor identifies Husayn ibn Ali al-Ju'fi and notes that the transmitter in 12692 is a different man). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 62}

Report 12694, from Ibn Abbas: its food is what it throws up. Report 12695, from Abu Bakr: its food is everything in it. Report 12696, from Ikrima: Abu Bakr said its food is its dead; Amr ibn Dinar said, I heard Abu al-Sha'tha say, I thought its food was only the salted. Report 12697, Ibn Abbas: its dead. Report 12698, Ikrima: what it casts. Report 12699, from Nafi: Abd al-Rahman (ibn Abi Hurayra) came to Abd Allah (ibn Umar) and said, the sea has thrown up many fish; he forbade him to eat them, then said: Nafi, bring the mushaf; he read "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you"; I said, its food is what it threw up; he said: go after him and tell him to eat it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 63}

Report 12700, from Nafi, to the same effect: Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hurayra asked Ibn Umar about many dead fish the sea threw up, may we eat them? He said, do not eat them; when he went back to his family he took the mushaf and read Sura al-Ma'ida, and when he reached "and its food, a provision for you and for travellers" he said: go and tell him to eat it, for it is its food (the editor notes that Malik relates it in the Muwatta and that al-Bayhaqi and al-Suyuti cite it). Report 12701, from Ibn Umar, is similar; 12702, from Ikrima, repeats 12696. Report 12703, from Nafi: Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hurayra asked Ibn Umar about many fish the sea cast up, are they carrion? He said yes, and forbade them; then he went into the house, called for the mushaf and read the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 64}

He said: its food is anything taken out of it, eat it, there is no harm, and anything in it is eaten, dead or on its shores. Report 12704, Qatada: its food is what is cast out of it. Reports 12705 and 12706, from Abu Ayyub: what the sea casts up is its food, even if dead. Others said "its food" means the salted fish, so that the verse means: lawful to you is fish of the sea and its salted in every state, in your lawful state and in ihram. Report 12707, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas: its food is the salted of it (the editor discusses the objection to the phrase "salted fish" and gives Ibn Barri's justification of it). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 65}

Report 12708, from Ibn Abbas: by its food He means its salted, and what the sea throws up of it, salted; 12709: it is the salted; 12710, from Ikrima on "a provision for you": the salted; 12711, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the salted; 12712, from Ibrahim: the salted, and what it cast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 66}

Report 12713, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a man comes to the people of the sea and says feed me; if he says fresh, they cast their net and catch for him, and if he says feed me of your food, they feed him of their salted fish. Report 12714, from him: the "manbudh" is the salted fish; 12715: the salted; 12716, from Ibrahim: it is its salted, then he said, what it casts; 12717, from Qatada: salted fish; 12718, from Ibrahim, he used to say salted fish, then later said what it casts up; 12719, from Sa'id: the salted; 12720, from Mujahid: salted fish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 67}

Reports 12721 and 12722, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: its food is "al-sir", meaning the salted (the editor explains "sir" and quotes two lines of Jarir against the family of al-Muhallab). Report 12723, from al-Suddi: its food is the salted. Report 12724, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: its food is what you carry salted for your journey. Report 12725, from Jabir ibn Zayd: we were told its food is its salted, and we dislike the floating fish of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 68}

Others said its food is what is in it. Report 12726, Ikrima: the food of the sea is what is in it. Report 12727, from him: what the sea brings with its waves. Report 12728, from Mujahid: its food is everything caught from it. Al-Tabari chooses the view that its food is what the sea casts up, or what it recedes from and is found dead on its shore, because God mentioned before it the game of what is caught, "lawful to you is the game of the sea", so what should be joined to it in sense is what is not caught of it, as if He said: lawful to you is what you catch from the sea and what you do not catch. As for the salted, what is salted after being caught is already included in "game of the sea", so there is no reason to repeat it, as there is no benefit in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 69}

Al-Tabari completes his argument: God has already told His servants that what is caught from the sea is lawful by "lawful to you is the game of the sea", so there is no benefit in saying to them afterwards "and its salted, which was caught, is lawful to you", since what is caught of it, fresh or salted, has been declared lawful, and God is exalted above addressing His servants with what gives them no benefit. He says a report has come from the Prophet in line with what he says, though some transmitters stop it at the Companion: report 12729, from Abu Hurayra, the messenger of God said of "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food": its food is what it casts up dead (the editor says the chain is sound, and that only al-Suyuti mentions it, attributing it to al-Tabari alone). Report 12730, from Abu Hurayra as his own saying: its food is what it casts up dead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 70}

He then explains "a provision for you and for travellers": a benefit to whoever of you is resident in his land, enjoying eating it, and a benefit and provision for those going from land to land, travellers who take it salted as supplies; "sayyara" is the plural of "sayyar". Report 12731, from Ikrima: for whoever is near the sea, and "for travellers" are those who journey. Reports 12732 and 12733, from Qatada: what the sea casts up, and this salted fish they take as provisions on their journeys. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 71}

Report 12734, from al-Hasan on "and for travellers": they are the pilgrims in ihram (the editor could not find a transmitter named Abd al-Salam ibn Habib al-Najjari and suggests Abd al-Salam ibn Abi al-Junub, who is discarded). Report 12735, from al-Suddi: its food is the salted, a sufficiency that travellers eat on journeys. Report 12736, from Ibn Abbas: its food is its salted and what the sea casts up, which the traveller takes as provisions; 12737: meaning the salted that he takes as provisions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 72}

Reports 12738 and 12739, from Mujahid: "a provision for you" for the people of villages and "for travellers" the people of cities, and the fish is for all people. Al-Tabari comments that Mujahid's saying that the travellers are the people of cities has no intelligible sense, unless he means the travellers among the people of cities, which would have to include every traveller, whether from cities or villages; travellers cannot be understood to be those resident in their cities. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 73}

He then explains "and the game of the land is forbidden to you as long as you are in ihram": God forbade you, believers, game of the land for as long as you are in ihram and have not left it. The scholars differ on the meaning. Some said it means that every sense of land game is forbidden to us: hunting, eating, killing, selling, buying, keeping and owning. Report 12740, from Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal from his father: Uthman ibn Affan made the pilgrimage with Ali; Uthman was brought the meat of game hunted by a man not in ihram, and he ate of it and Ali did not; Uthman said, by God we did not hunt it or order it or point to it! Ali recited "and the game of the land is forbidden to you as long as you are in ihram" (a note gives the narrators). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 74}

Report 12741, from Subayh ibn Abd Allah al-Absi: Uthman sent Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith to al-Arud, and he stopped at Qudayd; a man from Syria passed with a falcon and a hawk and he borrowed them and hunted partridges with them, putting them in an enclosure. When Uthman passed, he cooked them and served him, and Uthman said, eat! Some said, wait until Ali ibn Abi Talib comes; when he came and saw what was before them he said: we will not eat of it! Uthman said, why do you not eat? He said, it is game and it is not lawful to eat while I am in ihram. Uthman said, explain to us! Ali recited "O you who believe, do not kill game while you are in ihram", and Uthman said, did we kill it? So he recited to him "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you and for travellers, and the game of the land is forbidden to you as long as you are in ihram". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 75}

Report 12742 is another version of this account, with additions: Uthman stayed as long as God willed, then was told at Mecca, would you like to see Ibn Abi Talib, dried donkey meat has been given to him and he is eating it! Uthman sent for him and asked about eating the dried meat, saying: you eat and you forbid us? Ali said it was game of last year, when I was not in ihram, so there is no harm in eating it, whereas that one (the partridges) was hunted while I was in ihram and slaughtered while I was in ihram. Report 12743, from al-Hasan: Umar saw no harm in game meat for a pilgrim, and Ali disliked it. Report 12744, from Ibn al-Musayyab: Ali disliked game meat for the pilgrim in every case. Report 12745, from Abd Allah ibn al-Harith: he saw Uthman and Ali brought meat; Uthman ate and Ali did not, and Uthman said, did we hunt or (it finishes on p. 77). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 76}

The rest of report 12745: Uthman said, did we hunt it, or was it hunted for us? And Ali recited to him "lawful to you is the game of the sea and its food, a provision for you and for travellers, and the game of the land is forbidden to you as long as you are in ihram." Report 12746, from Abu Salama: Uthman ibn Affan made the pilgrimage with Ali; game meat hunted by a man not in ihram was brought; Uthman ate of it in ihram and Ali did not; Uthman said, it was hunted before we entered ihram! Ali said to him: we had encamped and it was lawful to us (the editor says one word is unclear in the manuscript), does it become lawful for us today? Report 12747, from Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal: Ali was brought half a donkey's rump while in ihram and said, I am in ihram. Report 12748, from Ibn Abbas: he disliked it in every case while a person is in ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 77}

Report 12749, from Nafi: Ibn Umar disliked any game while he was in ihram, whether it was taken for him or not, whether wasiqa or anything else (the editor defines wasiqa as meat boiled once in salted water and made into provisions for journeys). Report 12750, from Nafi: Ibn Umar did not eat game while in ihram, even if a non-pilgrim hunted it. Report 12751: Tawus forbade the pilgrim to eat game and its wasiqa and the like, whether hunted for him or not. Report 12752, from al-Hasan: if a man hunts game and then enters ihram he does not eat its meat until he is released; if he eats of it while in ihram, al-Hasan saw nothing owed. In 12753 Salim asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr about game hunted by a non-pilgrim, may the pilgrim eat of it? He cited the verses on the prohibition of killing, the compensation for the deliberate, and the lawfulness of sea game and its food, then "the game of the land is forbidden to you as long as you are in ihram", and it is forbidden to you, whether you hunted it or a non-pilgrim did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 78}

Others said that by "the game of the land is forbidden to you as long as you are in ihram" God meant only what the pilgrim newly hunts or slaughters in ihram, or what is newly done for him in that state; what a non-pilgrim slaughtered for non-pilgrims is no harm for the pilgrim to eat, and what was in his possession before ihram is not forbidden for him to keep. Report 12754, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: Abu Hurayra was asked about game hunted by a non-pilgrim, may the pilgrim eat it? He gave the opinion that he may; then he met Umar ibn al-Khattab and told him, and Umar said: had you given them any other opinion I would have hurt your head (the editor says the chain is sound). Report 12755, from Abu Salama: Uthman ibn Affan stopped at al-Arj in ihram and the man of al-Arj gave him sandgrouse; he said to his companions, eat, it was hunted only in my name (that is, for me); they ate and he did not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 79}

Report 12756, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: Abu Hurayra was at al-Rabadha and they asked him about game meat hunted by a non-pilgrim; then he gave an account like 12754; and report 12757, from Abu Hurayra from Umar, is similar. Report 12758, from Abu al-Sha'tha: I asked Ibn Umar about game meat that a non-pilgrim gives a pilgrim; he said Umar ate it and saw no harm; I said, do you eat it? He said, Umar is better than I (the editor has a long note identifying Abu al-Sha'tha al-Kindi, Yazid ibn Muhasir, and distinguishing him from Jabir ibn Zayd). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 80}

Report 12759 is the same, from Abu al-Sha'tha. Report 12760, from Abu Hurayra: a man from Syria asked me for an opinion on game meat he got while in ihram, and I told him to eat it; I went to Umar and told him; he said, what opinion did you give? I said, that he eat it; he said: by Him in whose hand is my soul, had you given him other than that I would have struck you with the whip! And Umar said: you were forbidden only to hunt it. Report 12761, from Ka'b: I was coming with some pilgrims and we got wild ass meat; people asked me about eating it and I gave them the opinion that they eat it while in ihram; we came to Umar and they told him that I had given the opinion that they eat wild ass in ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 81}

Umar said: I have made him your commander until you return. Report 12762, from Abu Hurayra: I passed al-Rabadha and its people asked me about a pilgrim eating what a non-pilgrim hunted, and I gave them the opinion that they eat; I met Umar and told him; he said, what opinion did you give them? I said, that they eat; he said, had you given them any other I would have opposed you. Report 12763, from Abu al-Sha'tha al-Kindi: I said to Ibn Umar, what do you think of a party of pilgrims who met a party of non-pilgrims carrying game meat, who either sold it to them or fed them? He said, lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 82}

Report 12764, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Hatib: he made umra with Uthman ibn Affan in a company including Amr ibn al-As, until they stopped at al-Rawha, and birds were brought to them while they were in ihram; Uthman said to them, eat, for I am not eating it! Amr said: do you order us to do what you will not eat? Uthman said: were it not that I think it was hunted for me, I would eat it! And the party ate. Report 12765, from Urwa: al-Zubayr used to take wild meat as provisions while in ihram (the editor notes Malik's version, dried gazelle meat). Report 12766, from Ibn Abbas: what is hunted or slaughtered while you are not in ihram is lawful to you, and what is hunted or slaughtered while you are in ihram is forbidden to you; report 12767 from him says the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 83}

We stop here at the foot of p. 83, in the middle of the commentary on verse 96, after report 12767 on a pilgrim eating what a non-pilgrim hunted.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
