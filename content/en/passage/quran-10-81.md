**Commentary on verse 81 (pp. 160-162)**

On [[passage/quran-10-81|10:81]] al-Tabari says: when they had thrown what they threw, Moses said to them 'what you have brought is magic'. The readers differed. Most of the readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read it as a statement from Moses about what Pharaoh's magicians had brought, that it is magic, so the meaning is: what you have brought, magicians, is the magic. Mujahid and some Medinans and Basrans read it as a question from Moses to the magicians about what they brought: is it magic or something else? The editor refers to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 160}

Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two readings for him is the one that reads it as a statement and not a question, because Moses was not in doubt about what the magicians brought, that it was magic with no reality, so as to need to ask them about it, and because he knew that Pharaoh had brought them only to contend against him over the truth God had given him. It was not hidden from him that they would not believe him in the report of what they had brought of falsehood, so he did not ask them or think it right to ask; rather he told them that he knew the falsity of what they brought through the truth that had come to him, and that he would frustrate their scheming by his strength; and this fits the Messenger's description better than the other. The editor says the manuscript has a gap in the sentence and the printed text has 'jadd' with jim, whereas the right is 'hadd' with ha, meaning force and might. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 161}

If someone asks why the article is in 'magic', when the Arabs in the like of this say 'what Amr brought me is a dirham', the reply is that it is the Arabs' way to put the article in the predicate of 'what' and 'that which' when the report is about something known to speaker and hearer; indeed it is only permitted with the article then, since the report is about a particular thing known to both parties, and it comes without the article only when the report is about something unknown. Moses's report was about something known to him and to the magicians, because they had ascribed to magic what Moses brought of signs God made a proof of his truth and prophecy. So Moses said to them: the magic with which you described the signs I brought is what you have brought, magicians, and not what I brought. The editor notes what Ibn Hisham wrote on the predicate of 'kada' joined with 'an'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 161}

Then he told them God would bring it to nothing: 'God will make it void' means will carry it away, and He carried it away by setting on it the staff of Moses, which He had turned into a serpent that swallowed it until nothing remained. 'God does not set right the work of the corrupters' means He does not set right the work of one who strives in God's land by what He dislikes and acts in it by disobedience. It is reported that in Ubayy b. Ka'b's reading it is 'what you have brought is magic' and in Ibn Mas'ud's likewise, which supports the reading al-Tabari chose. The editor refers to al-Farra' and to the earlier commentary on 'corruption'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 162}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
