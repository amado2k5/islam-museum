## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "abiding in it; the punishment will not be lightened for them, nor will they be given respite" al-Tabari says "abiding in it" is in the accusative as a circumstantial of the "hum" in "on them"; he notes that al-Hasan read the preceding clause with "the angels and all people" in the nominative, which he holds permissible in Arabic but not permitted as a reading, since it differs from the Muslims' codices. The pronoun in "in it" returns to "the curse", meaning what the unbeliever came to through it, which is the Fire of Hell; from Abu al-'Aliya, abiding in Hell, in the curse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 264} "The punishment will not be lightened for them" is a report that the punishment is everlasting with no limit or lightening, and "nor will they be given respite" means they will not be given a delay with an excuse they could offer, from Abu al-'Aliya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 264}
