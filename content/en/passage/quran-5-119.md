## Summary
Verse 119 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 119 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The readers differed on "this is a day when the truthful profit from their truth": some of Hijaz and Medina read "yawma" in the accusative; others of Hijaz and Medina and most of Iraq read "yawmu" in the nominative; those who raised it did so as the predicate of "this", making "day" a noun though its annexation is not a pure one since it has become as if qualified. A grammarian claimed that the Arabs treat the case of times such as "day" and "night" as they treat what follows them: if what follows is raised they raise it, as in "this is the day the emir rides", and if accusative they put it in the accusative, as in "this is the day the army went out", though in both cases the sense is "idh" or "idha". Those who read it raised took it to be God's word on the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 241}

So al-Suddi said too: in 13039 he says of "this is a day when the truthful profit from their truth" that it is a break from Jesus' words and this is the day of resurrection, meaning that "glory to you" to "you are the mighty, the wise" is God's report of what Jesus said in the world after God had raised him, and what follows is God's word to his servants on the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 242}

The accusative can be taken two ways. One is that "yawm", when annexed to something other than a noun, takes the accusative, since the annexation is not pure; annexation is pure only when to a sound noun, and like "day" in this are "hin" and "zaman" and other times, as in al-Nabigha's line in which he reproaches himself for his youth after turning grey. The other is that the sense is: this matter and this thing is on the day when the truthful profit from their truth, so "day" is accusative as a time and attribute, meaning this matter is on a day when the truthful profit from their truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 242}

The editor traces al-Nabigha's line to his Diwan, al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, Sibawayh and the Khizana, notes that Abu Jafar's reading "alamma tasihhu" matches al-Farra's while other sources have "alamma asihhu", both sound, and that the line comes from the poem in which al-Nabigha excused himself to al-Nu'man ibn al-Mundhir for what Murra ibn Rabi'a had charged him with.

Al-Tabari holds the better of the two readings to be "hadha yawma yanfa'u al-sadiqin", with the accusative of "day" as time and attribute, because the sense is that God answered Jesus when he said "glory to you" to "you are the mighty, the wise", saying to him: this useful saying, or this useful truth, is on the day when the truthful profit from their truth; so the day is the time of the saying and the useful truth. If one asks what is the case of "this", it is nominative; and what raises it? An omitted word: as if he said, God said, "this is" a day when the truthful profit from their truth, as the poet says in a line by a rajaz poet the editor could not identify: do you not see how the clouds run, this, not your horses, son of Bishr, meaning this, this and not your horses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 243}

On his view the sense is: God said to Jesus, this useful saying is on a day when their truth in the world profits the truthful in the next world with God; "for them are gardens beneath which rivers flow" means for those truthful in the world, gardens beneath which rivers flow in the next, as reward from God for the truth with which they were true to God in what they promised him and kept faith with God, so God kept faith with them in the reward he promised; "abiding in them forever" means remaining in the gardens he gave them, always, with delight that is not removed or ends. He has already explained that "abiding" means permanence and remaining. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 244}

The editor refers for "forever" to volumes 10 and 9 and for "abiding" to the language indexes.

Verse 119 closes here at p. 244, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-5-120|5:120]].
