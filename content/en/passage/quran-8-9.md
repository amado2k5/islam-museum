## Summary
Verse 9 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 9 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 9 (pp. 408-411)**

Al-Tabari says, on "when you sought help from your Lord and He answered you, I shall reinforce you with a thousand angels following one another": "and brings falsehood to naught" when you seek help from your Lord, so "when" is attached to "brings to naught". The sense of "you seek help from your Lord" is that you seek refuge with Him from your enemy and call on Him for help against them, "and He answered you" meaning He answered your call, saying I shall reinforce you with a thousand angels, one following another. The interpreters said the like, and the report came from the companions of the Messenger of God. Report 15734, from Umar ibn al-Khattab: on the day of Badr the Messenger of God looked at the polytheists and their number and at his companions, a little over three hundred; he faced the qibla and began to pray, saying, God, fulfil for me what You promised me, God, if this band of the people of Islam is destroyed You will not be worshipped on earth; he went on so until his cloak fell, and Abu Bakr the Truthful took it and put his cloak on him and embraced him from behind and said, enough, Prophet of God, may my father and mother be your ransom, your entreating your Lord, for He will fulfil for you what He promised you; and God sent down the verse [[passage/quran-8-9|8:9]]. The editor identifies Ikrima ibn Ammar al-Yamami al-Ijli as trustworthy and Simak al-Hanafi, Abu Zumayl, as trustworthy, and says Muslim gave it in his Sahih at length, and Ahmad in the Musnad, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi who called it good, sound and strange, and Abu Ja'far in his History by this same route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 409}

Report 15735, from Ibn Abbas: when the people drew up in ranks Abu Jahl said, God, whichever of us is nearer the truth, help him; and the Messenger of God raised his hand and said, Lord, if this band is destroyed You will never be worshipped on earth; report 15736, from Ibn Abbas: the Prophet stood and said, God, our Lord, You sent down the Book to me and commanded me to fight and promised me help, and You do not break the promise; Gabriel came to him and God sent down [[passage/quran-3-124|3:124]] and [[passage/quran-3-125|3:125]], the editor noting that Abu Ja'far did not give this report in his commentary on the verse in Al Imran; report 15737, from Zayd ibn Yuthay': Abu Bakr was with the Messenger of God in the shelter and the Prophet began to pray, God, help this band, for if You do not they will not be worshipped on earth; Abu Bakr said, part of your entreaty, He will fulfil for you what He promised you. The editor identifies Zayd ibn Yuthay' al-Hamdani, who narrated from Abu Bakr, Ali, Hudhayfa and Abu Dharr, and from whom only Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i narrated, listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 410}

Report 15738, from al-Suddi: the Prophet turned, praying to God, seeking His help and victory, and God sent down the angels on him; report 15739, from Ibn Jurayj: the Prophet prayed; report 15740, from Ibn Ishaq: "when you sought help from your Lord", that is by your prayer, when they looked at the great number of their enemy and the fewness of their own, "and He answered you" by the prayer of the Messenger of God and your prayer with him; report 15741, from Abu Salih: on the day of Badr the Prophet began to entreat his Lord most earnestly in prayer, and Umar came and said, Messenger of God, part of your entreaty, for by God God will fulfil for you what He promised. The editor explains "nishda" as the verbal noun of "I adjured you by God". As for "I shall reinforce you with a thousand angels following one another", he has already shown its sense. The interpreters said the like, and the list of those who said so begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 411}

We stop here at the foot of p. 411, in the commentary on 8:9 at the list of those who said so on the sense of "a thousand angels"; the next batch begins at p. 412. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 411}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
