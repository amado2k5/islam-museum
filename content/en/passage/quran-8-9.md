## Summary
Verse 9 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 9 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 9 (pp. 408-417)**

Al-Tabari says, on "when you sought help from your Lord and He answered you, I shall reinforce you with a thousand angels following one another": "and brings falsehood to naught" when you seek help from your Lord, so "when" is attached to "brings to naught". The sense of "you seek help from your Lord" is that you seek refuge with Him from your enemy and call on Him for help against them, "and He answered you" meaning He answered your call, saying I shall reinforce you with a thousand angels, one following another. The interpreters said the like, and the report came from the companions of the Messenger of God. Report 15734, from Umar ibn al-Khattab: on the day of Badr the Messenger of God looked at the polytheists and their number and at his companions, a little over three hundred; he faced the qibla and began to pray, saying, God, fulfil for me what You promised me, God, if this band of the people of Islam is destroyed You will not be worshipped on earth; he went on so until his cloak fell, and Abu Bakr the Truthful took it and put his cloak on him and embraced him from behind and said, enough, Prophet of God, may my father and mother be your ransom, your entreating your Lord, for He will fulfil for you what He promised you; and God sent down the verse [[passage/quran-8-9|8:9]]. The editor identifies Ikrima ibn Ammar al-Yamami al-Ijli as trustworthy and Simak al-Hanafi, Abu Zumayl, as trustworthy, and says Muslim gave it in his Sahih at length, and Ahmad in the Musnad, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi who called it good, sound and strange, and Abu Ja'far in his History by this same route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 409}

Report 15735, from Ibn Abbas: when the people drew up in ranks Abu Jahl said, God, whichever of us is nearer the truth, help him; and the Messenger of God raised his hand and said, Lord, if this band is destroyed You will never be worshipped on earth; report 15736, from Ibn Abbas: the Prophet stood and said, God, our Lord, You sent down the Book to me and commanded me to fight and promised me help, and You do not break the promise; Gabriel came to him and God sent down [[passage/quran-3-124|3:124]] and [[passage/quran-3-125|3:125]], the editor noting that Abu Ja'far did not give this report in his commentary on the verse in Al Imran; report 15737, from Zayd ibn Yuthay': Abu Bakr was with the Messenger of God in the shelter and the Prophet began to pray, God, help this band, for if You do not they will not be worshipped on earth; Abu Bakr said, part of your entreaty, He will fulfil for you what He promised you. The editor identifies Zayd ibn Yuthay' al-Hamdani, who narrated from Abu Bakr, Ali, Hudhayfa and Abu Dharr, and from whom only Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i narrated, listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 410}

Report 15738, from al-Suddi: the Prophet turned, praying to God, seeking His help and victory, and God sent down the angels on him; report 15739, from Ibn Jurayj: the Prophet prayed; report 15740, from Ibn Ishaq: "when you sought help from your Lord", that is by your prayer, when they looked at the great number of their enemy and the fewness of their own, "and He answered you" by the prayer of the Messenger of God and your prayer with him; report 15741, from Abu Salih: on the day of Badr the Prophet began to entreat his Lord most earnestly in prayer, and Umar came and said, Messenger of God, part of your entreaty, for by God God will fulfil for you what He promised. The editor explains "nishda" as the verbal noun of "I adjured you by God". As for "I shall reinforce you with a thousand angels following one another", he has already shown its sense. The interpreters said the like, and the list of those who said so begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 411}

Report 15742, from Ibn Abbas: "murdifin" is the added, as you say "go to the man and add so much to him"; report 15743, from Ibn Abbas: following one another, report 15744 the same; report 15745, from Ibn Abbas: behind each angel an angel; report 15746: following one another; report 15747, from Abu Zabyan: the angels, one on the heels of another; report 15748, from al-Dahhak, and 15749, from Mujahid: one on the heels of another; report 15750, from Mujahid: reinforcing, and from Abd Allah ibn Kathir: "irdaf" is reinforcing with them; report 15751, from Qatada: following one another; report 15752, from al-Suddi: one following another; report 15753, from Ibn Zayd: one on the heels of another; report 15754, from al-Dahhak: following one another on the day of Badr. In the notes the editor identifies the men of the chains: Ahmad ibn Bashir al-Kufi, Harun ibn Antara and his father Antara ibn Abd al-Rahman, Sulayman ibn Abd al-Jabbar the tailor, Muhammad ibn al-Salt al-Asadi, Abu Kudayna Yahya ibn al-Muhallab al-Bajali, Qabus ibn Abi Zabyan al-Janbi and his father Husayn ibn Jundab, and Hani ibn Sa'id al-Nakha'i; and explains that the chain of 15752 is confused in the manuscript, being two chains, the end of Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la's chain having dropped out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 412} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 413} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 414}
The reciters differed over its reading: most reciters of Medina read "murdafin" with fatha on the dal, and some Meccans and most Kufan and Basran reciters read "murdifin" with kasra; Abu Amr read it so and, it is reported, said it is from "they added one to another"; some people of knowledge of Arab speech denied this saying of Abu Amr, saying "irdaf" is only a man's carrying his companion behind him, and that it was not heard in a description of the angels on the day of Badr. The people of knowledge of Arab speech differed over the sense when read with fatha or kasra: some Basrans and Kufans said when read with kasra it means that the angels came one following another, in the dialect of those who say "ardaftuhu", the Arabs saying "ardaftuhu" and "radiftuhu" for "I followed him" and "I made follow", citing the poet's line "when Gemini followed the Pleiades", meaning "radifat", came after it, since Gemini comes after the Pleiades. In a long note the editor names the poet, Juzayma or Khuzayma ibn Nahd, one of the early poets of the pre-Islamic age, and tells the story of the line and the strife it caused between Quda'a and Rabi'a, and gives Ibn al-Sarraj's explanation of the line. They said that when read with fatha it is passive, as if the sense were "with a thousand angels, God making them follow one another". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 414} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 415} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 416}
Others said the sense when the dal has kasra is that the angels made one another follow, and when read with fatha that God made the Muslims follow with them. Al-Tabari chooses: the right reading in my view is that of those who read it with kasra on the dal, because of the agreement of the interpreters that its sense is that they follow one another and are in succession; in their agreement on that interpretation is the clear proof that the sound reading is the kasra, meaning some angels followed others, and it is heard from the Arabs, "I came following so-and-so", meaning after him. As for the saying of those who said that when read with fatha it means God made the Muslims follow with them, it is a saying with no sense, since the reference in "murdafin" is to the angels and not the believers, the sense being rather that He reinforces you with a thousand angels some of whom are made to follow others, the doer then being dropped. Were it as those say, the reference in "murdafin" would have to be to the Muslims and not to the angels, which is contrary to the apparent sense of the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 416}
Another reading is also mentioned, and report 15755, from Abd Allah ibn Yazid: "muraddifin", "muriddifin" and "murudifin", doubled, in the sense of "murtadifin", the editor noting that al-Qurtubi vocalised it. Report 15756, from Ali: Gabriel came down with a thousand angels on the right of the Prophet, Abu Bakr among them, and Michael came down with a thousand angels on the Prophet's left, I being among them. The editor says its chain is very weak: Abd al-Aziz ibn Imran al-Zuhri al-A'raj is disowned in hadith, al-Bukhari saying his hadith is not written down, al-Zam'i Musa ibn Ya'qub is trustworthy but spoken of, Abu al-Huwayrith Abd al-Rahman ibn Mu'awiya is spoken of, and Muhammad ibn Jubayr ibn Mut'im is a trustworthy Follower. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 417}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
