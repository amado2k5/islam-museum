## Summary
Verse 54 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 54 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The interpreters differed on whom God meant in "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you; your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself: whoever of you does evil in ignorance and then repents after it and mends, he is forgiving and merciful". Some said he meant those he forbade his prophet to drive away, and the reports from those who said so have passed. Others said he meant a people who asked the Prophet for a ruling on great sins they had committed, and God did not make them despair of repentance. Report 13291, from Mahan: a people came to the Prophet who had committed great sins; Mahan said: I do not think he replied to them with anything; so God revealed this verse, "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you". 13292, from Mahan: a people came to the Prophet and said, Muhammad, we have committed great sins! I do not think he replied to them with anything, and they left, and God revealed "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you; your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself"; he said: he called them and recited it to them. 13293, from Mahan, the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 390}

At 13291 the editor refers to report 13258 and those after it, and at 13293 says Sufyan is Ibn Uyayna and that Mahan appeared at report 12710.

Others said: no, it means a people of the believers who had advised the Prophet to drive away the people he was forbidden to drive away, and that was a fault of theirs which God forgave and pardoned, and he ordered his prophet, when they came to him, to give them good news that he had forgiven the fault they had made through their advice to drive away the people they advised him to drive away. That is the saying of Ikrima and Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd, whose reports have been given before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 391}

Al-Tabari holds the soundest of these views on the verse to be that of those who said: those meant by "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you" are not those whom God forbade the Prophet to drive away, since "when those who believe in our signs come to you" is a fresh report after the report about those God forbade his prophet to drive away was finished; had they been the same it would have been said "when they come to you, say: peace upon you", and God's beginning the report of the story of these and not joining it to the report of the former shows that they are others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 391}

So the sense, as the matter is as we described, is: when those come to you, Muhammad, who trust our revelation, proofs and arguments and acknowledge that in word and deed, asking you about their earlier sins between me and them, whether there is repentance for them, do not make them despair of it, and say to them "peace upon you", God's security for you that he will not punish you for your sins after you repent; "your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself" says: your Lord decreed mercy for his creatures; "whoever of you does evil in ignorance, then repents after it and mends, he is forgiving and merciful". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 392}

The editor refers for "peace" to volume 10 and the language indexes and for "prescribed" to p. 273.

The readers differed on this. Most readers of Medina read "annahu man amila minkum su'an" with fatha, making "anna" accusative as a statement explaining "mercy", and "fa-innahu ghafur rahim" with kasra, beginning afresh after the fa, as a particle with no place, meaning "he is forgiving and merciful to him", or "he has forgiveness and mercy". Some Kufans read both with fatha on the alif, meaning "your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself", then explaining mercy by "that whoever of you does evil in ignorance", "and that he is forgiving, merciful", joining the second "anna" to the first, both being accusative nouns as explained. Some Meccans and most readers of Iraq, Kufa and Basra read both "inna" with kasra, as a beginning and as particles with no place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 393}

Al-Tabari holds the best reading to be that of those who read both with kasra, "kataba rabbukum ala nafsihi al-rahmata innahu", beginning a sentence, the report having ended at "your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself", then renewing the report of what he does with whoever did evil in ignorance, then repented and mended. The sense of "whoever of you does evil in ignorance" is: whoever of you commits a sin and is ignorant of committing it, then repents and mends, "he is forgiving" to him when he repents, turns back and returns to obedience to God, leaving the like of it with regret for what he did, "merciful" to the repentant in not punishing him for his sin after his repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 393}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and to what Abu Jafar said earlier on this reading at pp. 278 to 280, and for "ignorance" to pp. 89 to 93 of volume 8, a very good explanation.

A group of interpreters said as we said: report 13294, from Mujahid: "whoever of you does evil in ignorance" is one who does not know lawful from forbidden and by his ignorance commits the act. 13295, from al-Dahhak, the like. 13296, from Mujahid... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 393}

The rest of 13296: "do evil in ignorance", he said: whoever acts in disobedience to God, that is ignorance in him until he returns. 13297, from Mujahid: everyone who does a sin is ignorant in it. 13298, from Khalid ibn Dinar Abu Khalda: when we went in to Abu al-Aliya he would say "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you; your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 394}

At 13297 the editor identifies Bakr ibn Khunays al-Kufi, the devout, who related from Layth ibn Abi Sulaym, Abd al-Rahman ibn Ziyad, Ismail ibn Abi Khalid and Ata ibn Abi Rabah, and gives Ibn Adi's remark that he is a righteous man but righteous men have hadith made to seem so to them, his hadith counting among the weak and not to be used as proof; and at 13298 Khalid ibn Dinar al-Tamimi al-Sa'di, Abu Khalda, trusted.

Verse 54 closes here at p. 394, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-55|6:55]].
