## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 72 (pp. 348-357)**

Al-Tabari says: God promised those who affirmed God and His Messenger and acknowledged Him and what came from Him, men and women, gardens beneath which rivers flow, meaning orchards under whose trees rivers run, "abiding in them", staying in them forever, their bliss never ceasing or perishing, "and good dwellings", meaning pleasant homes in which they live. The editor refers to earlier explanations of garden, abiding and goodness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 348}

Their goodness, as has been mentioned to us, is as in report 16940, from al-Hasan, through Abu Kurayb, Ishaq ibn Sulayman and Jasr: I asked Imran ibn Husayn and Abu Hurayra about the verse "good dwellings in gardens of Eden", and they said you have fallen on one who knows; we asked the Messenger of God and he said a palace in Paradise of pearl, in which are seventy houses of red ruby, in each house seventy rooms of green emerald, in each room seventy couches. Report 16941, from al-Hasan, through Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Jawhari, Qurra ibn Habib and Jasr ibn Farqad, from Imran ibn Husayn and Abu Hurayra: the Messenger of God was asked about this verse, and he said a palace of a single pearl, in which are seventy houses of red ruby, in each house seventy rooms of green chrysolite, in each room seventy couches, on each couch seventy bedcovers of every colour, on each bedcover a wife of the wide-eyed houris, in each room seventy tables, on each table seventy kinds of food, in each room seventy maidservants, and the believer is given strength in a single morning to attend to all of that. The editor notes that Jasr ibn Farqad was a righteous man but nothing in hadith, that al-Haythami records the report in Majma' al-Zawa'id and calls its chain weak, and that the chain is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 349}

As for "in gardens of Eden", it means that these good dwellings the Almighty described are in the gardens of Eden, the preposition belonging to "dwellings". They were called gardens of Eden because they are gardens of permanence and dwelling from which no one departs. It is also said they were called gardens of Eden because they are God's abode, which He chose for Himself and for whom He wishes among His creatures, from the Arabs' saying that someone "took up residence" in such a land when he stayed and settled there, from which comes the word for a mine or source, and one says "he is in a source of truth", meaning in a firm origin. Some of the reciters recited the line of al-A'sha, about a patron to whom people turn for hospitality and find a weighty, settled judgement. The editor explains that to seek hospitality means to take refuge with him in need and that both transmitted forms of the word give a sound sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 350}

Ibn Abbas and a group with him, as is mentioned, interpreted it so. Report 16942, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, through Ishaq ibn Ibrahim, Attab ibn Bashir and Khusayf: "gardens of Eden", he said, a man's source in which he is. Report 16943, from Abu al-Darda, through Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn Askar, Ibn Abi Maryam, al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Ziyada ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Fadala ibn Ubayd: the Messenger of God said God opens the remembrance in the last three hours of the night; in the first of them He looks in the Book into which no one else looks, effacing what He wishes and confirming; then in the second hour He comes down to the Garden of Eden, which is in His abode that no eye has seen and that has not occurred to the heart of any human, and it is His dwelling, and none of the children of Adam dwell with Him but three, the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs; then He says, blessed is whoever enters you; and he mentioned the third hour. The editor says Ziyada ibn Muhammad al-Ansari is rejected in hadith, that al-Dhahabi in the Mizan gave the report in full and said its wording is objectionable and that only Ziyada brought it, and that al-Haythami calls it weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 351}

Report 16944, from Abu al-Darda, through Musa ibn Sahl, Adam, al-Layth, Ziyada, Muhammad ibn Ka'b and Fadala: Eden is His abode, meaning God's abode, which no eye has seen and has not occurred to the heart of any human, and it is His dwelling, and none of the children of Adam dwell in it with Him but three, the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs; God, blessed and exalted, says blessed is whoever enters you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 352}

Others said the meaning of "gardens of Eden" is gardens of vines and grapes. Report 16945, from Abdallah ibn al-Harith, through Ahmad ibn Abi Surayj, Zakariyya ibn Adi, Ubaydallah ibn Amr, Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa and Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad: Ibn Abbas asked Ka'b about the gardens of Eden and he said they are the vines and grapes, in Syriac. The editor vouches for the men of the chain, noting that Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad's hadith is held weak. Others said it is the name for the middle and heart of Paradise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 352}

Reports 16946 to 16952, from Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, by the routes of Humayd ibn Mas'ada, Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, Shu'ba, al-A'mash, Abdallah ibn Murra, Masruq, Ibn Bashshar, Ibn al-Muthanna, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Sufyan, Abu al-Duha, Jarir and Mansur: gardens of Eden are the heart of Paradise; in report 16947 al-A'mash, asked what the heart of Paradise is, said its middle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 353}

Others said Eden is the name of a palace. Reports 16953 and 16954, from al-Hasan, through Ali ibn Sa'id al-Kindi, Abda Abu Ghassan and Awn ibn Musa al-Kinani, and through Ahmad ibn Abi Surayj and Abdallah ibn Asim: gardens of Eden, and what will make you know what the gardens of Eden are? A palace of gold which none enters except a prophet, a truthful one, a martyr or a just ruler; and al-Hasan raised his voice as he said it. Report 16955, from Abdallah ibn Amr, through Ahmad, Yazid, Hammad ibn Salama, Ya'la ibn Ata and Nafi' ibn Asim: in Paradise is a palace called Eden around which are towers and fragrance, with fifty thousand doors, on each door a patterned cloth, which none enters but a prophet or a truthful one; report 16956 from him, through al-Hasan ibn Nasih, Abu Dawud, Shu'ba, Ya'la ibn Ata and Ya'qub ibn Asim: in Paradise is a palace called Eden with five thousand doors, on each door five thousand patterned cloths, which none enters but a prophet, a truthful one or a martyr. The editor says he did not know Abda Abu Ghassan, that Awn ibn Musa is reliable and Abdallah ibn Asim al-Himmani truthful, and explains the patterned cloth as a kind of striped Yemeni garment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 354}

It is also said that it is the city of Paradise. Report 16957, from al-Dahhak, through Abd al-Rahman al-Muharibi and Juwaybir: it is the city of Paradise, in which are the messengers, the prophets, the martyrs and the leaders of guidance, with the people around them and the gardens around it. It is also said to be the name of a river. Report 16958, from Ata, through al-Muharibi and Wasil ibn al-Sa'ib al-Raqashi: Eden is a river in Paradise with its gardens on both banks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 355}

As for "and pleasure from God is greater", it means God's being pleased with them is greater than all that, as the report came from the Messenger of God. Report 16959, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Malik ibn Anas, Zayd ibn Aslam and Ata ibn Yasar: the Messenger of God said God says to the people of Paradise "people of Paradise", and they say "here we are, our Lord, at Your service", and He says "are you satisfied?", and they say "why should we not be, when You have given us what You gave none of Your creation?", and He says "I shall give you better than that", and they say "Lord, what is better than that?", and He says "I bestow My pleasure on you, and I shall never be angry with you after it". The editor says the report is sound, related by al-Bukhari with this same chain and wording and by Muslim, and that Ibn Kathir mentions it and attributes it to al-Bazzar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 356}

Report 16960, from Shamir, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Hafs: the Qur'an comes on the Day of Resurrection in the form of a pale man to a man when his grave splits open and says "good news of God's honour, good news of God's pleasure", and he says "one like you gives good news? who are you?", and it says "I am the Qur'an that kept you awake at night and thirsty by day"; it carries him on its neck until it brings him to his Lord and he stands before Him, and it says "Lord, this servant of Yours, reward him well for me, for I kept him awake at night and thirsty by day, and I commanded him and he obeyed me, and forbade him and he obeyed me"; and the Lord, blessed and exalted, says "then his is the robe of honour", and it says "Lord, give him more, for he deserves it", and He says "then his is My pleasure"; he said, "and pleasure from God is greater". The editor says Ya'qub and Hafs are reliable, that Shamir ibn Atiyya is reliable, and that he did not find the report with a chain in this wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 356}

The statement about God's pleasure for the believers, men and women, was begun anew, that it is greater than everything the Almighty mentioned, and put in the nominative though pleasure is among what He promised them, not joined in the grammar to the gardens and the good dwellings, so as to make known God's preferring His pleasure with the believers over all else He apportioned them of His bounty and gave them of His honour, like one saying to another "I gave you and joined you with this and honoured you, and my pleasure with you besides is better for you". Then "that is the great triumph": these things promised to the believers, men and women, are the great triumph, meaning the great winning and the weighty deliverance, for they won eternal honour and were delivered from humiliation in Saqar. The editor notes that something fell out of the text at the copyist's hand so he restored the sense and separated the two statements, and refers to al-Farra and to earlier explanations of triumph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 357}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
