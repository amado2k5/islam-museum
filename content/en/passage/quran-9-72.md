**Commentary on verse 72 (pp. 348-352)**

Al-Tabari says: God promised those who affirmed God and His Messenger and acknowledged Him and what came from Him, men and women, gardens beneath which rivers flow, meaning orchards under whose trees rivers run, "abiding in them", staying in them forever, their bliss never ceasing or perishing, "and good dwellings", meaning pleasant homes in which they live. The editor refers to earlier explanations of garden, abiding and goodness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 348}

Their goodness, as has been mentioned to us, is as in report 16940, from al-Hasan, through Abu Kurayb, Ishaq ibn Sulayman and Jasr: I asked Imran ibn Husayn and Abu Hurayra about the verse "good dwellings in gardens of Eden", and they said you have fallen on one who knows; we asked the Messenger of God and he said a palace in Paradise of pearl, in which are seventy houses of red ruby, in each house seventy rooms of green emerald, in each room seventy couches. Report 16941, from al-Hasan, through Ibrahim ibn Sa'id al-Jawhari, Qurra ibn Habib and Jasr ibn Farqad, from Imran ibn Husayn and Abu Hurayra: the Messenger of God was asked about this verse, and he said a palace of a single pearl, in which are seventy houses of red ruby, in each house seventy rooms of green chrysolite, in each room seventy couches, on each couch seventy bedcovers of every colour, on each bedcover a wife of the wide-eyed houris, in each room seventy tables, on each table seventy kinds of food, in each room seventy maidservants, and the believer is given strength in a single morning to attend to all of that. The editor notes that Jasr ibn Farqad was a righteous man but nothing in hadith, that al-Haythami records the report in Majma' al-Zawa'id and calls its chain weak, and that the chain is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 349}

As for "in gardens of Eden", it means that these good dwellings the Almighty described are in the gardens of Eden, the preposition belonging to "dwellings". They were called gardens of Eden because they are gardens of permanence and dwelling from which no one departs. It is also said they were called gardens of Eden because they are God's abode, which He chose for Himself and for whom He wishes among His creatures, from the Arabs' saying that someone "took up residence" in such a land when he stayed and settled there, from which comes the word for a mine or source, and one says "he is in a source of truth", meaning in a firm origin. Some of the reciters recited the line of al-A'sha, about a patron to whom people turn for hospitality and find a weighty, settled judgement. The editor explains that to seek hospitality means to take refuge with him in need and that both transmitted forms of the word give a sound sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 350}

Ibn Abbas and a group with him, as is mentioned, interpreted it so. Report 16942, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, through Ishaq ibn Ibrahim, Attab ibn Bashir and Khusayf: "gardens of Eden", he said, a man's source in which he is. Report 16943, from Abu al-Darda, through Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn Askar, Ibn Abi Maryam, al-Layth ibn Sa'd, Ziyada ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Fadala ibn Ubayd: the Messenger of God said God opens the remembrance in the last three hours of the night; in the first of them He looks in the Book into which no one else looks, effacing what He wishes and confirming; then in the second hour He comes down to the Garden of Eden, which is in His abode that no eye has seen and that has not occurred to the heart of any human, and it is His dwelling, and none of the children of Adam dwell with Him but three, the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs; then He says, blessed is whoever enters you; and he mentioned the third hour. The editor says Ziyada ibn Muhammad al-Ansari is rejected in hadith, that al-Dhahabi in the Mizan gave the report in full and said its wording is objectionable and that only Ziyada brought it, and that al-Haythami calls it weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 351}

Report 16944, from Abu al-Darda, through Musa ibn Sahl, Adam, al-Layth, Ziyada, Muhammad ibn Ka'b and Fadala: Eden is His abode, meaning God's abode, which no eye has seen and has not occurred to the heart of any human, and it is His dwelling, and none of the children of Adam dwell in it with Him but three, the prophets, the truthful and the martyrs; God, blessed and exalted, says blessed is whoever enters you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 352}

Others said the meaning of "gardens of Eden" is gardens of vines and grapes. Report 16945, from Abdallah ibn al-Harith, through Ahmad ibn Abi Surayj, Zakariyya ibn Adi, Ubaydallah ibn Amr, Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa and Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad: Ibn Abbas asked Ka'b about the gardens of Eden and he said they are the vines and grapes, in Syriac. The editor vouches for the men of the chain, noting that Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad's hadith is held weak. Others said it is the name for the middle and heart of Paradise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 352}

We stop here at the foot of p. 352, in the middle of the commentary on verse 72 (at the view that it names the middle of Paradise); the next batch begins at p. 353. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 352}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
