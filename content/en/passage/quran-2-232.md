## Summary
Verse 232 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 232 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-232|2:232]]) by saying that it was reported to have been revealed about a man who had a sister he married to a cousin of hers; the cousin divorced her and left her until her waiting period ended, then asked to marry her again, and her brother refused to give her to him and stopped her, though she wanted him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 17} The interpreters differed over who the man was, and some said he was Ma'qil ibn Yasar al-Muzani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 17} From al-Hasan, from Ma'qil, his sister was married to a man who divorced her and left her until her waiting period ended, then asked for her; Ma'qil took offence and said, he left her when he could have had her, and he stood between them, and the verse was revealed (4927). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 17} The editor explains that the verb for leaving something is good Arabic although rarely clear in dictionaries, that "hamiyya" is pride and jealousy, and traces the report to al-Bukhari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 17} From al-Hasan, from him, his sister's husband divorced her and wanted to take her back, and Ma'qil prevented it, and the verse was revealed (4928); the editor says it is in al-Hakim and that al-Dhahabi faulted him for the weakness of al-Fadl ibn Dalham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 18} In another narration al-Hasan said that Ma'qil ibn Yasar told him that he had a sister whom he kept from suitors until a cousin asked for her and he married her to him; they lived together as long as God willed, then he divorced her with a divorce that allowed return and left her until her term ended, then came asking for her among the suitors; Ma'qil said he would never marry her to him, and the verse was revealed about him; he said, I atoned for my oath and married her to him (4929). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 18} The editor identifies al-Makhrami, one of the narrators, and notes that al-Bukhari and Abu Dawud reported it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 18}

From Qatada, it was mentioned to them that a man divorced his wife once and left her until her term ended, then asked for her, she being the sister of Ma'qil ibn Yasar; Ma'qil took offence and said, he left her while she was in her term and, had he wished, he could have taken her back, and now he wants to take her back after she is separated from him; so he refused to marry her to him; and it was mentioned that when the verse was revealed the Prophet called him and recited it to him, and he gave up his pride and submitted to God's command (4930). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 18} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 19} The editor says this is al-Tabari's usual chain for Qatada's commentary and that "submitted" means he yielded and obeyed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 19} From al-Hasan, the verse was revealed about Ma'qil ibn Yasar, who said, I married a sister of mine to a man who divorced her, and when her term ended he came asking for her, and I said to him, I married you, made her your bed and honoured you, then you divorced her and now come asking for her; she will never return to you; he was a man of integrity with no fault, and the woman wished to return to him; then the verse was revealed and I said, now I will do it, Messenger of God, and married her to him (4931). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 19} The editor says al-Bukhari reported it, as did al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 19}

The reports on ([[passage/quran-2-232|2:232]]) continue. From Bakr ibn 'Abd Allah al-Muzani, the sister of Ma'qil ibn Yasar was married to a man who divorced her and then asked for her, and her brother stopped her, and the verse was revealed (4932). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 20} From Mujahid, it was revealed about a woman of Muzayna whose husband divorced her and she separated from him; another man married her, and her brother Ma'qil ibn Yasar then prevented her, to harm her, for fear that she would return to her first husband (4933); Ibn Jurayj reports from 'Ikrima that it was revealed about Ma'qil, and that his sister's name was Jumal bint Yasar, who was married to Abu al-Baddah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 20} Two more narrations from Mujahid say the same (4934, 4935), {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 20} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 21} and in the report of Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani her name is Fatima bint Yasar and Ma'qil said, we married you to her and you divorced her (4936). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 21} The editor notes that the copies of al-Tabari differ on how her name is read, the manuscript giving Jumal and the printed text Jamil, refers to Fath al-Bari and al-Isaba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 20} From al-Hasan and Qatada, it was revealed about Ma'qil and is addressed to guardians (4937); from a man who heard Ma'qil, his sister's husband divorced her with a final divorce and asked for her, and he refused to marry her to him (4938). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 21}

Al-Tabari then gives the view of those who said the man was Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah al-Ansari. From al-Suddi, he had a cousin whose husband divorced her once and her term ended, and then he wished to take her back; Jabir said, we divorced our cousin and now you want to marry her a second time, while the woman wanted her husband (4939). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 21} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 22} Then the view that it was revealed to show that a man must not harm a woman under his guardianship by preventing her from marrying. From Ibn 'Abbas, in the report of 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, a man divorces his wife once or twice, her term ends, and then he wants to marry her again and she wants it, but her guardians stop her, and God forbade them (4940); another narration from him says the same (4941); and so do Masruq (4942) and Ibrahim (4943); Ibn Shihab said, where the guardian is the one who divorced her, he may not prevent her so as to inherit from her and keep her from chastity through a husband (4944); and al-Dahhak said God was addressing the woman's guardians, not to stop her returning to her husband in a new marriage if she agrees (4945). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 23}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the verse was sent down to show that God forbids guardians to harm women under their guardianship by preventing them from marrying husbands from whom they were separated by divorce or annulment, though it may have been revealed about Ma'qil and his sister or about Jabir and his cousin; and whichever it was, the verse shows what he says. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 23} He explains the prohibition as making things narrow for the women by stopping them from going back to their husbands in a new marriage, in order to harm them. He notes the verb pattern, says one tribe of the Arabs is reported to use another vowel for it, and that the reading has the damma; the editor remarks that this explanation is not found in dictionaries. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 24} The root meaning of the word is narrowness, and he cites a saying of 'Umar about the people of Iraq, the incurable disease, a line of Dhu al-Rumma, the saying that the open space was too narrow for the army by its numbers, the saying of a woman in difficult labour, and a line of Aws ibn Hajar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 24} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 25} He says the particle in the clause is in the accusative governed by the verb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 25}

For him, the agreement between husbands and wives means agreeing on what is lawful as a return for the marriage, the dower and a fresh new marriage; he reports from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Baylamani that the Prophet said, marry off the unmarried, and when a man asked what the marriage gifts were, he said, whatever their families agree on (4946). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 25} The editor judges it weak because it is mursal, and the report from Ibn 'Umar (4947) weaker still because of Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Baylamani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 25} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 26}

Al-Tabari then says the verse is clear evidence for the view that there is no marriage without a guardian from the paternal relatives, because God forbids the guardian to prevent the woman if she wants to marry; if she could marry herself or appoint whom she wished, the prohibition would have no meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 26} This is al-Tabari's argument; what other jurists say on guardianship in marriage is a matter of difference that the museum does not settle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 26}
