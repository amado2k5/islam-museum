## Summary
Verse 49 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/al-jadd-ibn-qays-asks-to-stay-behind|Al Jadd ibn Qays Asks to Stay Behind]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 49 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 49 (pp. 286-289)**

Al-Tabari says it is reported that this verse came down about al-Jadd ibn Qays. By "and among them" God means among the hypocrites, "who says give me leave", that is let me stay and not go out with you, "and do not tempt me", that is do not try me with the sight of the women of Banu al-Asfar and their daughters, for I am infatuated with women and would go out and sin by it. The reports agree on this explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 286}

Report 16785, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the Messenger said fight at Tabuk and you will take the daughters of Banu al-Asfar and the women of Rome, and al-Jadd said give us leave and do not tempt us with women; report 16786 is the same through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. Report 16787, from Ibn Abbas: "give me leave and do not tempt me", he said it is al-Jadd ibn Qays, who said the Helpers know that when I see women I cannot restrain myself until I am tempted, but I will help you with my wealth. Report 16788, from Ibn Ishaq, from al-Zuhri, Yazid ibn Ruman, Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada and others: one day, while he was making his preparations, the Messenger said to al-Jadd ibn Qays, brother of Banu Salima, "would you this year fight Banu al-Asfar?", and he said Messenger of God, will you give me leave and not tempt me, for by God my people know no man more taken with women than I, and I fear that if I see the women of Banu al-Asfar I will not restrain myself; the Messenger turned away from him and said "I have given you leave"; and about al-Jadd ibn Qays this verse came down, "among them is one who says give me leave and do not tempt me", that is, if he fears temptation only from the women of Banu al-Asfar, which is not his case, the temptation into which he has fallen by staying behind from the Messenger and preferring himself to the Messenger's self is greater. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. Report 16789, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "among them is one who says give me leave and do not tempt me", he said it is a man of the hypocrites called Jadd ibn Qays, to whom the Messenger said "this year we raid Banu al-Asfar and take from them concubines and young servants", and he said Messenger of God, give me leave and do not tempt me, if you do not give me leave I shall be tempted and stay behind; the Messenger was angry, and God said they have fallen into the very temptation, and hell encircles the unbelievers. He was of Banu Salima, and the Prophet asked them who is your chief, Banu Salima; they said Jadd ibn Qays, except that he is miserly and cowardly, and he said "what disease is worse than miserliness? Your chief is the fair, curly-haired youth, Bishr ibn al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur". The editor notes that the printed text misspelled the word for servants, that the plural there means young male servants, that the manuscript has "stay behind" rather than "fall", meaning to stay behind from the campaign, that the words "the Messenger was angry" lack their subject in the printed text and he restored it in brackets, that "curly" here means the compact, well-knit build that marks a noble man rather than curliness of hair, and that al-Bara' ibn Ma'rur, Bishr's father, was among the first to pledge at the First Aqaba, the first to face the qibla, the first to bequeath a third of his property and one of the chiefs, and died before the Prophet arrived at Medina, his grave being turned toward the qibla when they buried him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 288}

Report 16790, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "give me leave and do not tempt me" means do not put me in difficulty, and "into temptation they have fallen" means into difficulty they have fallen. Report 16791, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "do not tempt me" means do not make me sin, and into sin they have fallen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 288}

Al-Tabari then explains that "hell encircles the unbelievers" means the Fire surrounds whoever disbelieved in God, denied His signs and called His messengers liars, enclosing them and gathering them all on the Day of Resurrection, so that it will be enough for Jadd ibn Qays and his like among the hypocrites to burn in it in disgrace. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "encircling" in volume 13. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 289}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
