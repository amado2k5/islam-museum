## Summary

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On God's words "the hajj is in known months", al-Tabari says: it means the time of the hajj is known months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 114} "The months" are in the nominative with "the hajj", though it is a time and not a description or attribute, since they are not restricted by definition through annexation to a definite thing or a known referent; so the nominative here is like the Arabs' saying "the Muslims a side, the disbelievers a side", with the nominative "side" that is not restricted to a known limit, while had one said "a side of their land or their country" the accusative would be the speech; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 114} The interpreters then differed over "the hajj is in known months". Some said that by the known months He means Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and ten days of Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 114} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 115}

Among those who said it is 'Abd Allah (ibn Mas'ud), in the report of Abu al-Ahwas: Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and the ten of Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 115} From Ibn 'Abbas through several routes the same; and from him, they are Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and ten of Dhu al-Hijja, which God made for the hajj, and the other months for the 'umra, so it is not right for anyone to assume ihram for hajj except in the months of hajj, while ihram for 'umra is assumed in every month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 115} The editor notes that no. 3522 dropped out of their numbering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 115} From Ibrahim, al-Sha'bi, 'Amir, al-Suddi, Mujahid, al-Hasan, al-Dahhak and 'Ata' the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 115} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 116} From Ibn 'Umar: Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and the ten of Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 116} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 117} From al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 117}

Others said it means Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and all of Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 117} From Ibn Jurayj: I asked Nafi', did 'Abd Allah (ibn 'Umar) name the months of hajj? He said yes, Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 117} From Mujahid, from Ibn 'Umar the same; and from 'Ata', al-Rabi', Qatada (who sometimes added "and ten of Dhu al-Hijja"), Mujahid, Tawus and Ibn Shihab: the months of hajj are Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and Dhu al-Hijja. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 117} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 118}

Al-Tabari then raises a question: if someone asks us, what is the ground of those who held this, when you know that the work of the hajj is not done after the days of Mina have ended? He answers that the meaning is not what he imagined; by saying the hajj is three full months they meant only that these are the months of hajj and not the months of 'umra, and that the months of 'umra are the other months of the year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 118} What indicates that this is their meaning is Ibn 'Umar's saying: that you separate the months of hajj from those of 'umra, making the 'umra outside the months of hajj, is fuller for the hajj of any of you and fuller for his 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 118}

From Shu'ba: Ayyub never met me without asking about the hadith of Qays ibn Muslim from Tariq ibn Shihab, who said I said to 'Abd Allah (ibn Mas'ud): a woman of ours has performed hajj, or wants to; may she add an 'umra to her hajj? He said, I see these only as months of hajj; in Sufyan's report from him: I hear God say "the hajj is in known months", and I see them only as months of hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 118} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 119} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: an 'umra in the months of hajj is not complete; when he was asked about 'umra in Muharram he said they used to consider it complete; and Ibn Sirin used to like 'umra in Muharram and said they did not consider it complete in the months of hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 119} From Ibn 'Umar, when he said to al-Hakam ibn al-A'raj or another: if you obey me you will wait until Muharram begins, then go out to Dhat 'Irq and assume ihram for 'umra from there; and from him: to perform 'umra in the ten of Dhu al-Hijja is dearer to me than to perform it in the twenty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 119} From Ibn Sirin: none of the scholars doubts that an 'umra outside the months of hajj is better than an 'umra in the months of hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 119}

Al-Tabari says that these and parallels too long to cite indicate that the meaning of those who said the time of hajj is three full months is that they are other than the months of 'umra, and that they are months for the work of the hajj and not of the 'umra, even though the work of the hajj is done in some of them and not all. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 120} As for those who said Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and ten of Dhu al-Hijja, they said that by "the hajj is in known months" God intended to inform His creatures of the time of their hajj and not to report the time of 'umra; the whole year is the time for 'umra, because of the many reports that the Messenger of God performed 'umra in some months of hajj and no contrary report from him is sound; and since the time of the hajj work ends with the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja, the verse means the time of the hajj is two months and part of the third. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 120}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the right view in his opinion is that of those who said it means the hajj is two months and ten days of the third, because it is God's report of the time of hajj and no hajj work is done after the days of Mina, so it is known that the whole third month is not meant, and since it is not meant in full, the view "and ten of Dhu al-Hijja" is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 120} If it is asked how "months" is said when it is two months and part of a third, he answers that the Arabs, especially with times, do not avoid such usage; they say "it has been two days since I saw him" meaning a day and part of another, as in the verse on hastening in two days, where one in fact hastens in a day and a half; and one of them may do an act in an hour and then speak of it generally of the year or month, saying "I visited him this year" or "I came to him today", not meaning that his act took from the start of the period to its end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 120} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 121} So the meaning of the verse is: the time of your hajj, people, is two months and part of the third, namely Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and ten days of Dhu al-Hijja; the editor refers to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 121}

On God's words "whoever makes the hajj binding on himself in them", al-Tabari says it means whoever makes the hajj obligatory on himself and binds himself to it in them, that is, in the known months he has explained; and his making it obligatory on himself is the resolve to do all that God has made incumbent on the pilgrim and to leave all that He has commanded him to leave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 121} The interpreters differed over the meaning by which a man makes the hajj binding, after all agreed that "fard" means making obligatory and binding. Some said making the hajj binding is the ihlal (raising the voice in the talbiya). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 121} From Ibn 'Umar: whoever raised the call for hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 121} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 122} From 'Ata', Mujahid and Tawus: the talbiya; from al-Thawri: the obligation is ihram and ihram is the talbiya; from Ibrahim: the obligation is the talbiya, and he may turn back if he wishes as long as he is not in ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 122} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: when you have bathed, put on your garment and said the talbiya, you have made the hajj binding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 122} The editor identifies the narrators of these reports, corrects the printed "Abu 'Amr" to "Abu 'Umar al-Darir", who is Hafs ibn 'Umar al-Basri, reliable, died 220, and notes that Jabr ibn Habib is reliable and an authority in language. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 122}

Others said making the hajj binding is its ihram. From Ibn 'Abbas: whoever assumed ihram for hajj or 'umra; from Ibrahim, 'Ata', al-Hasan, Qatada and al-Dahhak from Ibn 'Abbas: the obligation is ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 123}

From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Ali: "whoever makes the hajj binding on himself in them" means whoever assumed ihram for hajj or 'umra; from Ibrahim: whoever assumed ihram; from 'Ata': the obligation is ihram; from al-Hasan: the obligation of the hajj is ihram; from Qatada: this is at ihram; from al-Dahhak from Ibn 'Abbas the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 123} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 124}

Al-Tabari gives his own preference: this second view may mean that ihram, for its holder, is making obligatory by resolve, or may mean resolve together with the talbiya, as the first group said. He says the obligation of the hajj is ihram because all agree on it, and that ihram is a man's making binding on himself what binds the muhrim, since there are only three possibilities. First, that he is not muhrim except by the talbiya and doing everything incumbent (which the consensus refutes, since he may be muhrim without stripping off his clothes, and may be muhrim while omitting some rites). Second, that he is muhrim without the talbiya, stripping or resolve (refuted by the consensus that one who has not resolved on ihram and bound himself to it is not muhrim). Hence the third is sound: he is muhrim by making ihram binding through his resolve, even if it does not show in stripping, the talbiya and some rites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 124} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 125} The editor corrects a printed reading ("the obligation passed by resolve") to "the obligation accompanied by resolve", on the strength of the context. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 125}

On "no rafath": al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the meaning of rafath here. Some said it is coarse speech to a woman, such as saying "when we are out of ihram I will do such and such to you" without allusion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 125} From Tawus: I asked Ibn 'Abbas about rafath and he said it is alluding to intercourse, which is the Arabic "'araba", and it is the lowest degree of rafath. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 125} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 126} The editor explains that i'raba is foul or explicit indecent speech, and that the better gloss is allusion to sexual relations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 125} From Husayn ibn Qays: I travelled with Ibn 'Abbas on hajj, and after we assumed ihram I took hold of his camel's tail while he chanted a verse of rajaz mentioning a woman; I said, do you commit rafath while in ihram? He said, rafath is only what is said in the presence of women; Abu al-'Aliya reports the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 127} From Ibn 'Umar: rafath is approaching women and speaking of it to men and women when they mention it aloud; Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 126}

From 'Ata', when Ibn Jurayj asked whether a muhrim may say to his wife "when I am out of ihram I will have you": no, that is rafath; he also said rafath is what falls short of intercourse, and in another report that it is intercourse and the indecent speech short of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 127} Ibn al-Zubayr said i'raba is not lawful for the muhrim; when this was told to Ibn 'Abbas he said he spoke truly, and i'rab is allusion; Tawus said i'raba is saying in ihram "when I am out of ihram I will have you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 127} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 128} From 'Ata': they disliked i'raba, meaning alluding to intercourse while in ihram, and he disliked it for the muhrim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 128} From Ibn 'Abbas through Ibn Tawus: the rafath mentioned here is not the rafath of the verse on the night of fasting (2:187), and part of it is alluding to intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 128} The editor notes that "Ibn al-Zubayr al-Saba'i" is unidentified and that the printed text has "Qitr" for Fitr ibn Khalifa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 127} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 128}

From Ibn Tawus from his father: rafath is i'raba, what one expresses allusively about women, and i'raba is making explicit reference to intercourse; the editor corrects the printed "mimma rawahu" to "mimma warrahu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 128} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 129} From 'Ali ibn Abi Talha from Ibn 'Abbas: rafath is approaching women, kissing, nudging, and alluding to them with indecent speech and the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 129} From Mujahid: Ibn 'Umar used to say to the camel-driver: do not allude to women. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 129} From Ibn 'Abbas: rafath in the fast is intercourse, and rafath in the hajj is i'raba; he used to say that "entering" and "touching" mean intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 129}

Others said that rafath here is intercourse itself. From Miqsam, and from Ibn 'Abbas by several routes: rafath is intercourse, and is approaching women; from Ibn 'Abbas through Bakr ibn 'Abd Allah: rafath is intercourse, but God is generous and uses indirect expression for what He wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 129} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 130} In Abu al-'Aliya's report Ibn 'Abbas chanted rajaz in ihram with a verse mentioning a woman, and when asked whether that was not rafath he said no, rafath is only approaching women and having intercourse; the editor notes that Sharik recited the verse, broke off, and did not pronounce the last word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 130} From Ibn Mas'ud: rafath is approaching women. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 130}

In this sense, intercourse or approaching women, there are reports from al-Hasan; 'Amr ibn Dinar (intercourse and what falls short of it in matters of women); 'Ata'; Mujahid; Qatada; Ibn 'Abbas through al-Dahhak and Abu al-Duha; Sa'id ibn Jubayr; al-Suddi; al-Rabi'; Ibrahim; Ibn 'Umar (through Nafi' and Thuwayr); 'Ikrima; al-Dahhak; and Ibn Zayd, who cited the verse on the night of the fast (2:187). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 131} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 132} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 133} In one report from Ibn 'Abbas: rafath is marriage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 133} The editor notes that al-Bukhari records Yahya ibn Bishr al-Khurasani as having heard 'Ikrima. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 132}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the right view in his opinion is that God forbade rafath to whoever made the hajj binding on himself in the months of hajj. The root of rafath in Arabic is coarse speech, which is then used allusively for intercourse; and since the scholars differed on whether the prohibition covers some senses of rafath or all, it must cover all of them unless a report that must be accepted limits it to speech in the presence of women, because the apparent ruling of a verse may not be moved to a hidden interpretation without firm proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 133} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 134} To the objection that all agree rafath in the absence of women is not forbidden to the muhrim, so the verse concerns only some rafath, he answers that what has been singled out of the verse and permitted lies outside the prohibition, while what proof has not singled out stays under the general ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 134} The editor corrects the printed "if that is so" to "since that is so" and explains the construction of "since no report came". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 134}

On "nor fusuq": al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the meaning of the fusuq forbidden here. Some said it is all acts of disobedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 135} This is from Ibn 'Abbas by several routes (including: disobeying God, and all God's disobediences), from 'Ata', who cited 2:282, from al-Hasan, Tawus, Mujahid, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, Qatada, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibrahim, al-Rabi', 'Ikrima (disobedience to God, with no disobedience to God being small), and al-Zuhri. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 135} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 136} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 137}

Others said that fusuq here is what is done in disobedience to God during ihram of the things forbidden in it, such as killing game, removing hair and clipping nails and the like, which God singled out for ihram and commanded to be avoided. From Ibn 'Umar: fusuq is committing God's disobediences in the Haram; in another report, whatever disobedience is committed there, game or otherwise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 137} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 138}

Others said that fusuq here is abuse. From Ibn 'Umar by several routes, Ibn 'Abbas through al-Dahhak, Mujahid, al-Suddi, Ibrahim and 'Ata' ibn Yasar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 138}

From al-Hasan, Ibrahim, Miqsam from Ibn 'Abbas, and Mujahid: fusuq is abuse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 138} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 139}

Others said that fusuq is slaughtering for idols. From Ibn Zayd: fusuq is slaughtering for stone altars; he cited the verse in al-An'am (6:145) on what is consecrated to other than God, and said this was cut off, as rafath was, by the Prophet when he performed hajj and taught his community the rites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 139} Others said that fusuq is calling one another by offensive nicknames, reported from al-Dahhak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 139}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the best of the views for the verse is that of those who said that "nor fusuq" means the prohibition of disobeying God by taking game and doing what God forbade the muhrim to do while in ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 139} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 140} His argument is that God forbade His disobediences to everyone, muhrim or not, and forbade offensive nicknames in ihram and outside it by the verse in al-Hujurat (49:11), and forbade a Muslim to abuse his brother in every state, so there is no reason to single out the state of ihram for what is forbidden always; the fusuq forbidden to the muhrim specifically because of his ihram is therefore what was otherwise permitted to him before it, such as perfume, clothing, shaving, clipping nails, killing game and the rest that God singled out for the muhrim, just as the rafath forbidden is what was otherwise permitted to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 140} The verse then means, in his reading: whoever makes the hajj binding in the months of hajj and enters ihram in them, let him not commit rafath in the presence of women by speaking explicitly to them of intercourse, nor have intercourse, nor commit fusuq by doing what God forbade in his ihram, such as killing game, taking hair and clipping nails. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 141}

On "no disputing in the hajj": al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said it is the prohibition of the muhrim's disputing with anyone, and they then differed: some said he is forbidden to dispute with his companion until he angers him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 141} From Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas by several routes, 'Ata', Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Mujahid, 'Amr ibn Dinar, al-Dahhak and al-Rabi': jidal is that you wrangle with your companion until you anger him; from al-Hasan, Ibrahim, 'Ata' ibn Yasar and Mujahid: it is wrangling (mira'); from Sa'id ibn Jubayr in one report: that you shout at your companion; from 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah: that they wrangle with one another until they are angry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 142} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 143} The editor doubts the printed "tamhan", prefers "tamhak", adds "'ala" to the shouting report, and identifies Waqid al-Khalqani, recorded by al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 142} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 143}

From 'Ikrima: jidal is anger, that you anger a Muslim, except that you may rebuke a slave and admonish him without angering him, and there is no blame on you in that, God willing; in another report: that you wrangle with your companion until he angers you or you anger him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 144} From al-Zuhri and Qatada: it is clamour and wrangling while you are in ihram; from 'Ata': whatever angers your companion; from Ibn 'Abbas: wrangling and quarrelling until you anger your brother and companion, and God forbade this; from Ibrahim: wrangling, and they disliked disputation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 144} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 145} The editor notes that Ibn Kathir's text reads "no blame on you" and that "ista'tabahu" means to turn him back from wrongdoing, that is, to discipline him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 144}

Others said that jidal here is abuse. From Ibn 'Umar: abuse, wrangling and quarrels, and abuse and contention; from Ibn 'Abbas and Qatada: abuse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 145} Others said that a particular kind of disputing is meant, namely disagreement over which pilgrims have the more complete hajj; from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: when Quraysh gathered at Mina, one party said our hajj is more complete than yours, and the other said the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 145} Others said it is the disagreement among them over the day on which the hajj falls, which they were forbidden; from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: that some say the hajj is today and others say it is tomorrow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 146} Others said it is their disagreement over the standing places of the hajj, each claiming to stand where Ibrahim stood; from Ibn Zayd: they stood in different places and disputed, each claiming his place was Ibrahim's, and God cut it off when He informed His Prophet of their rites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 146}

Others said that "no disputing in the hajj" is God's report that the time of the hajj is settled on one fixed time, neither advanced nor delayed, and that the practice of postponement (nasi') is void. From Mujahid: the hajj has become settled and there is no dispute in it; no month is postponed and there is no doubt about the hajj, which has been made clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 146} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 147} Mujahid describes the nasi': they dropped Muharram and then gave the months names that were not theirs, so they performed hajj in Muharram, then began the count anew and performed hajj twice each year, dropping another month; the one who did the postponing for them was Abu Thumama, a man of Banu Kinana. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 147} Also from Mujahid: there is no uncertainty about the hajj and God has made its matter clear; from al-Suddi: the matter of the hajj is settled, so do not dispute it; from Mujahid: the time of the hajj is known, so there is no dispute or doubt, it being a known month about which there is no contention. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 147} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 148} From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Ata': wrangling about the hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 148} Mujahid also says that they used to perform hajj in Dhu al-Hijja for two years, then in Muharram for two, then in Safar for two, and so on through every month, until the hajj of Abu Bakr fell in Dhu al-Qa'da of that cycle a year before the Prophet's hajj; the Prophet then performed hajj the next year in Dhu al-Hijja, and that is when the Messenger of God said that time has come round to its form on the day God created the heavens and the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 148} From Mujahid: God made clear the matter of the hajj and its landmarks, so there is no talk about it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 148}
