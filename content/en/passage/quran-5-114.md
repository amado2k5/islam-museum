## Summary
Verse 114 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 114 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

This is God's report of his prophet Jesus, that he answered the people by asking his Lord for what they sought, a table sent down to them from heaven. The interpreters then differed on "that it be a feast for the first of us and the last of us". Some said: we shall take the day it came down as a feast that we and those after us honour; report 12997, from al-Suddi. Report 12998, from Qatada: they wanted it to be for their descendants after them. Report 12999, from Ibn Jurayj: "the first of us" are those of them then alive and "the last of us" those after them. Report 13000, from Sufyan: "a feast", they said, we shall pray on it; and he said it came down twice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 225}

Others said: it means we shall all eat of it; report 13001, from Ibn Abbas: the last of the people ate of it as the first had, when it was set before them. Others said "feast" means a return, a favour that comes back to us from God, and a proof and evidence. Al-Tabari holds the soundest to be that of those who said it means: let it be for us a feast on which we worship our Lord on the day it comes down and pray to him on it, as people worship on their feasts, since that is the known and current use of the word among people, not the saying that it means a return of favour from God; and to take the sense of God's words from the known speech of those addressed is better than to take it from the unknown, as far as a way is found. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 226}

As for "for the first of us and the last of us", the soundest interpretation is: for those of us alive today and those who come after us, for the same reason. "And a sign from you" means a mark and proof from you, Lord, to your servants of your oneness and of my truth that I am a messenger to them with what you sent me; "and provide for us, you are the best of providers" means give us of your giving, for you are the best of givers and the most generous of benefactors, since no reproach or grudge enters your giving. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 226}

The editor refers for "sign" and "provision" to the language indexes.

The interpreters then differed on whether the table was sent down to them and what it was. Some said it came down and was a fish and food, and the people ate of it, but it was taken up after it had come down because of things they did between themselves and God. Report 13002, from Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami: the table came down as bread and fish. Reports 13003 and 13004, from Atiyya: the table was a fish with the taste of every food in it. Report 13005, from Abu Abd al-Rahman, as 13002. Report 13006, from Ibn Abbas: a dining board came down to Jesus and the disciples with bread and fish on it, from which they ate wherever they alighted, whenever they wished. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 227}

Report 13007, from Wahb ibn Munabbih: a barley loaf and some fish came down on them; asked what that could do for them, he said nothing, but God scattered blessing among its parts, so that a group ate and left and others came and ate and left until all had eaten, with some left over. Report 13008, from Mujahid: it was food that came down to them wherever they alighted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 227}

The editor says that Abu Bakr in the chain of 13007 is Abd al-Razzaq ibn Hammam al-Himyari.

Report 13009, from Mujahid: a table with food on it, brought to them when punishment was put to them if they disbelieved, kinds of food sent down to them. Report 13010, from Ishaq ibn Abd Allah: the table came down to Jesus with seven loaves and seven fish, and they ate of it as they wished; one of them stole from it, saying perhaps it will not come down tomorrow, and it was taken up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 228}

Report 13011, from a man of Banu Ijl: I prayed beside Ammar ibn Yasir, and when he finished he said, do you know how it was with the table of the children of Israel? They asked Jesus for a table with food on it that would not run out, and were told it would stay with them as long as they did not hoard, betray or take it up, and if they did he would punish them as he punished no one in the worlds; before the day was out they hoarded, took it up and betrayed, and were punished as none in the worlds had been. Ammar then turns to the Arabs, who had followed the tails of camels and sheep until God sent among them a messenger of their own, told them through their prophet that they would prevail over the Arabs, forbade them to hoard gold and silver, and swears that night and day would not pass before they hoarded them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 228}

The editor notes that the printed text changed the wording of 13009 wholesale and destroyed its sense, that he restored the manuscript, and that the sense the first editor corrected it to is wholly contrary, since it belongs to those who said no table came down, a view reported from Mujahid further on at 13021.

Report 13012, from Ammar ibn Yasir, traced to the Prophet: the table came down as bread and meat, and they were commanded not to betray, store up or put aside for the next day, but they betrayed, stored and put aside, and were turned into apes and swine. Report 13013, from Ibn Abbas: it was food that came down on them from heaven wherever they alighted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 229}

Others said: the table came down with fruit of the fruits of Paradise on it. Report 13014, from Ammar: the table came down with fruit of Paradise on it, and they were commanded not to hide, betray or store, and they betrayed, hid and stored, so God turned them into apes and swine. Report 13015, from Qatada: we were told that it was a table that fruit of Paradise came down on, and they were commanded not to hide, betray or store for tomorrow, a trial with which God tried them; whenever they did any of it Jesus told them, and the people betrayed in it, hid and stored for tomorrow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 230}

The editor identifies al-Hasan ibn Qaza'a, Sufyan ibn Habib and Khallas ibn Amr al-Hajari, corrects the printed "Jallas", quotes al-Tirmidhi on the report, that Abu Asim and others related it as the Companion's own words, that he knows it traced to the Prophet only through al-Hasan ibn Qaza'a, and that the Companion's version is sounder; and he corrects "abla-hum" to "ibtala-hum" in manuscript and print.

Others said it had every food on it except meat. Report 13016, from Maysara: when the table was set for the children of Israel hands reached to it for every food. Reports 13017 and 13018, from Maysara and Zadhan, the same, adding that they saw hands reaching for everything on it but meat. Others said God sent down no table on the children of Israel; and these then differed, some saying it is only a parable God set for his creatures, forbidding them thereby to ask God's prophet for signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 230}

We stop here at the foot of p. 230, where those who say no table came down and that it was a parable begin their reports, before report 13019, which continues on p. 231.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
