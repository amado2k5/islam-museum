## Summary
Verse 89 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 89 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "those are they to whom we gave the book, the judgement and prophethood" al-Tabari says: by "those" he means these whom we named of his prophets and messengers, Noah and his offspring whom he guided to the religion of Islam and chose for his message to his creatures; they are "those to whom we gave the book", meaning the scrolls of Abraham and Moses, the Psalms of David and the Gospel of Jesus, "and the judgement" meaning understanding of the book and knowing the rulings in it. 13518, from Mujahid: "the judgement and prophethood": the judgement is the core. Mujahid meant by it, God willing, what I said, since "lubb" is the intellect, as if he meant that God gave them the intellect through the book, which is the sense we gave of understanding by it. Al-Tabari has already explained "prophethood" and "judgement" with their evidences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 514}

The editor identifies, in 13518, Muslim ibn Ibrahim al-Azdi al-Farahidi and Aban ibn Yazid al-Attar, and says "Malik ibn Shaddad" is so in print and manuscript but he found no mention of him in the books and perhaps it is corrupted from something he does not know; and refers for "prophethood" and "judgement" to earlier passages.

On "if these disbelieve in it, we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it" he says: if, Muhammad, these polytheists who hold others equal to their Lord disbelieve in the signs of my book that I sent down to you and reject it. 13519, from Ibn Abbas: "if these disbelieve in it" says if they disbelieve in the Qur'an. The interpreters then differed over who is meant by "these". Some said the unbelievers of Quraysh are meant, and by "we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it" the Ansar. 13520, from Qatada: "if these disbelieve in it" is the people of Mecca, "we have entrusted it" the people of Medina; 13521, from al-Dahhak: the Ansar; 13522, from him: if the people of Mecca disbelieve in it, we have entrusted it to the people of Medina, the Ansar; 13523, from al-Suddi: if Quraysh disbelieve in it, we have entrusted it to the Ansar; 13524, from Ibn Jurayj: the people of Mecca, and the people of Medina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 515}

13525, from Ibn Abbas: the people of Medina had taken up the abode and faith before God's messenger came to them, and when God sent down the signs to them the people of Mecca rejected them; and God said "if these disbelieve in it, we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it". Atiyya said: I did not hear this from Ibn Abbas but heard it from another. 13526, from Ibn Abbas: "these" are the people of Mecca, says if they disbelieve in the Qur'an, "we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it", meaning the people of Medina and the Ansar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 516}

The editor says that "Atiyya" in 13525 is Atiyya ibn Sa'd al-Awfi, grandfather of Muhammad ibn Sa'd the elder.

Others said the sense is: if the people of Mecca disbelieve in it, we have entrusted it to the angels, and 13527 and 13528, from Abu Raja: they are the angels. Others said "if these disbelieve in it" means Quraysh and "we have entrusted it to a people" the prophets whom he named in the verses before this one; 13529, from Qatada: "if these disbelieve in it" the people of Mecca, "we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it", they being the eighteen prophets of whom God said "those are they whom God guided, so follow their guidance"; 13530, from Qatada: he means the people of Muhammad, then said "we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it", meaning the prophets whose stories he told before this verse, then said "those are they whom God guided, so follow their guidance". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 517}

Abu Jafar said: the best of these views for being right is that of those who said that "if these disbelieve in it" means the unbelievers of Quraysh and "we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it" means the eighteen prophets God named in the verses before this one, because the report in the verses before it has gone on about them and in those after it mention of them follows, so what lies between being a report about them is fitter and truer than being a report about others. So the sense is: if your people of Quraysh, Muhammad, disbelieve in our signs and reject and deny their truth, we have charged our messengers and prophets before you with keeping them and tending them, who do not deny their truth or reject them but believe in them and affirm their soundness. Some said that "we have entrusted it to a people" means we have given it as provision to a people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 518}

The editor says the printed text has "so in what lies between it", the manuscript "so what lies between them", the right being as he set; and that the printed "if your people of Quraysh disbelieve" and the manuscript's "if your people disbelieve in it" cannot be sound without dropping "in it", and the sentence among the successive conjoined clauses is only sound if it is "if your people disbelieved" in the past tense like the one joined to it.

Verse 89 closes here on p. 518, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-90|6:90]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
