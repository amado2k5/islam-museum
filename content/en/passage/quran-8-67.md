## Summary
Verse 67 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/badr-prisoners|Badr Prisoners]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 67 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 67 (pp. 58-62)**

Al-Tabari says: it was not for a prophet to hold back an unbeliever he had overpowered and who had come into his hand, of the idol-worshippers, for ransom or favour; "taking captive" in Arabic speech means holding, and one says "captive" for held. God said this to His Prophet to tell him that killing the idolaters he took captive at Badr and then ransomed would have been closer to what is right than taking ransom from them and letting them go. The editor in the note gives the correct reading of an expression in a prayer and refers to the earlier explanation of "captive". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 58}

On "until he has made slaughter in the land" he says: until he has gone far in killing the idolaters in it and subdued them by force and compulsion; one says "so-and-so made slaughter in this matter" when he went far in it. On "you desire" he says to the believers among the companions of the Messenger of God, you desire, believers, "the goods of this world" by taking the idolaters captive, which is what comes to a man of it of wealth and possessions, you desire by taking ransom from the idolaters the enjoyment of the world; "God desires the next world" means God wants for you the ornament of the next world and what He prepared for the believers and His friends in His gardens, by your killing them and making slaughter in the land; so seek what God wants for you and work for it, not what your own desires call you to, the longing for this world; "God is mighty", so if you want the next world no enemy of yours will overcome you, and "wise" in ordering the affair of His creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 59}

Report 16286, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: that was on the day of Badr when the Muslims were few; when they grew many and their authority firm, God sent down after this on the captives "either grace afterwards or ransom" ([[passage/quran-47-4|47:4]]), leaving the Prophet and the believers a choice about captives: they could kill them, enslave them or ransom them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 59}

Report 16287, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: the companions of the Prophet of God wanted ransom on the day of Badr and ransomed them at four thousand each, though the Messenger of God had not made slaughter that day, it being the first fight he fought against the idolaters. Report 16288, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Fudayl and Habib ibn Abi Amra: making slaughter is killing. Report 16289, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sharik and al-A'mash: when you take them captive do not ransom them until you have made slaughter among them. Report 16290, from Mujahid: the permission came down afterwards, if you wish grant favour and if you wish ransom. Report 16291, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it means those taken captive at Badr. The editor notes that the publisher dropped the second "four thousand" and gives the biography of Habib ibn Abi Amra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 60}

Report 16292, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: a prophet is not to have captives of his enemy until he has made slaughter in the land, that is until he has overcome his enemy and driven them from the land; "you desire the goods of the world" means goods and ransom by taking men, "and God desires the next world" by killing them, for the triumph of the religion they wish to put out, by which the next world is reached. The editor notes it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira with some difference of wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 61}

Report 16293, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Abu al-Sa'ib, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash, Amr ibn Murra and Abu Ubayda: when it was the day of Badr and the captives were brought, the Messenger of God said, what do you say about these captives? Abu Bakr said, Messenger of God, they are your people and family, spare them and be patient with them, perhaps God will turn to them; Umar said, they denied you and drove you out, bring them forward and strike their necks; Abd Allah ibn Rawaha said, look for a valley with much firewood, put them in it and set it alight; al-Abbas said to him, you have cut your kinship. The Messenger of God was silent and did not answer them, then went in. Some said he would take Abu Bakr's view, some Umar's, some Abd Allah ibn Rawaha's. Then he came out and said: God softens the hearts of men until they are softer than milk, and God hardens the hearts of men until they are harder than stones; you, Abu Bakr, are like Abraham who said "whoever follows me is of me, and whoever disobeys me, You are forgiving, merciful" ([[passage/quran-14-36|14:36]]), and like Jesus who said "if You punish them they are Your servants" ([[passage/quran-5-118|5:118]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 61}

It continues: and you, Umar, are like Noah who said "my Lord, leave not on the earth any dwelling of the unbelievers" ([[passage/quran-71-26|71:26]]), and like Moses who said "our Lord, obliterate their wealth and harden their hearts so they do not believe until they see the painful punishment" ([[passage/quran-10-88|10:88]]). The Messenger of God said: you are in need today, so none of them is to escape except by ransom or beheading. Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud said: except Suhayl ibn Bayda, for I heard him mention Islam; the Messenger of God was silent, and never did I fear more in a day that stones would fall on me from the sky than on that day, until the Messenger of God said, except Suhayl ibn Bayda. He said: and God sent down "it is not for a prophet to have captives until he has made slaughter in the land", to the end of the three verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 62}

The editor notes in the margin that the publisher added a phrase about Ibn Rawaha that is neither in the manuscript nor in the sources and that the saying is addressed to Umar; he explains "in need" as poor. He says the chain of the report is broken because Abu Ubayda ibn Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud did not hear from his father, that Ahmad gave it in his Musnad by the same route, that al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak, calling the chain sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing, that al-Tabari gave it in his History, that al-Haythami gave it in Majma' al-Zawa'id saying its men are reliable, and that al-Wahidi gave it in Asbab al-Nuzul; and he holds "Suhayl ibn Bayda" to be an error of some narrators for Sahl ibn Bayda, Suhayl's brother, who became a Muslim in Mecca and hid it, and whom Quraysh took out with them in the expedition to Badr, where he was captured. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 62}

Report 16294, from Ibn Abbas from Umar, through Ibn Bashshar, Umar ibn Yunus al-Yamami, Ikrima ibn Ammar and Abu Zumayl: when they had taken the captives on the day of Badr the Messenger of God said, where are Abu Bakr, Umar and Ali? and then, what do you think of the captives? Abu Bakr said, Messenger of God, they are cousins and kin, and I think you should take ransom from them, to be a strength for us against the unbelievers, and perhaps God will guide them to Islam. He said, what do you think, son of al-Khattab? Umar said, no, by Him than whom there is no god, I do not hold what Abu Bakr holds, but I think you should give us power over them: give Ali power over Aqil that he strike his neck, Hamza power over al-Abbas that he strike his neck, and me power over so-and-so, a relative of Umar, that I strike his neck; for these are the leaders and chiefs of unbelief. The Messenger of God inclined to what Abu Bakr said and not to what I said. Umar said: the next day I came to the Messenger of God and found him and Abu Bakr sitting weeping. I said, Messenger of God, tell me what you and your companion are weeping for, and if I find cause I will weep, and if not I will make myself weep. He said: I weep for what has befallen my companions in their taking ransom; your punishment was shown to me nearer than this tree, a tree near the Messenger of God. Then God sent down "it is not for a prophet to have captives until he has made slaughter in the land", up to "lawful and good", and God made booty lawful for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 63}

The editor notes in the margin that the report is from Ibn Abbas from Umar and that the chain has a gap which he completed from al-Tirmidhi's version; that Ahmad gave it at length in his Musnad and Muslim at length in his Sahih, and al-Tabari in his History and al-Wahidi in Asbab al-Nuzul; he judges it sound and says it is known only through Ikrima ibn Ammar. He adds that whoever told the story about Suhayl ibn Bayda erred: Suhayl ibn Bayda became a Muslim before Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, emigrated to Medina and was at Badr as a Muslim, and the narrator confused him with his brother Sahl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 63}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
