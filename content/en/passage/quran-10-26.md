**Commentary on verse 26 (pp. 62-72)**

He begins "For those who do good is the best reward and more": for those among His creatures who did well in the worship of God in the world and obeyed Him in what He commanded and forbade, is "the best reward". Then the interpreters differed over the meaning of "the best reward" and "more", which He promised to the doers of good among His creatures. Some said the best reward is Paradise, which God made a repayment for the doers of good, and the "more" over it is looking upon God; the list of those who said this then begins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 62}

To set out the view that the "more" is looking upon God, al-Tabari gives reports: 17610, from Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Isra'il, Abu Ishaq and Amir ibn Sa'd), that the "more" is looking at the face of their Lord, and 17611, from Abu Bakr to the same effect through Amir ibn Sa'd from Sa'id ibn Nimran. In the note the editor says Amir ibn Sa'd al-Bajali is a trusted follower whose report from Abu Bakr is mursal, and that al-Ajurri gave the report in mursal form; he discusses at length Sa'id ibn Nimran al-Hamdani, from whom Amir narrated, citing what al-Bukhari, Ibn Hajar and al-Dhahabi said of him (including a description as unknown) and what al-Tabari's history says of his presence at the Yarmuk, his service as Ali's scribe and his judgeship at Kufa under Ibn al-Zubayr, and concludes that the chain needs scrutiny, referring to al-Suyuti's tracing in al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 63}

Reports 17612 and 17613, from Amir ibn Sa'd, give the same sense (looking at the face of the Merciful); 17614 is from Hudhayfa (through Muslim ibn Nudhayr) and 17615 from Abu Ishaq; the editor identifies Muslim ibn Nudhayr al-Sa'di of Kufa. Report 17616, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari: on the Day of Resurrection God sends the people of Paradise a caller who asks whether God has fulfilled what He promised them; they look at the honour God has prepared and say yes; then He says that the "more" is looking at the face of the Merciful. The editor says Abu Bakr al-Hudhali is very weak, so the chain is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 64}

Report 17617, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari preaching from the pulpit of Basra: God sends on the Day of Resurrection an angel to the people of Paradise who asks if God has fulfilled what He promised; they look and see the jewels, garments, fruit, rivers and purified spouses, and say yes; the angel repeats the question three times and they miss nothing of what was promised; then he says something remains, for God says "for those who do good is the best reward and more", and the best reward is Paradise and the more is looking at the face of God. The editor says it repeats the previous report at length, is weak for the same reason, and records where the printed text and the manuscript differ in the wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 65}

Report 17618, from Abu Tamima al-Hujaymi, that he heard Abu Musa al-Ash'ari narrate from the messenger of God that on the Day of Resurrection God sends a caller who calls the people of Paradise in a voice heard by the first and last of them: God promised you the best reward and more; the best reward is Paradise and the more is looking at the face of the Merciful. The editor says Shabib ibn Sa'id al-Tamimi's reports are sound but Ibn Wahb narrated unsound ones from him, and that Aban ibn Abi Ayyash Fayruz was a pious man with heedlessness who erred in tradition so that scholars dropped his reports, citing a harsh remark by Shu'ba; he judges the report to have a ruined chain. These are the editor's judgements. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 65}

Reports 17619 and 17620, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: the "more" is looking at the face of their Lord, and he recited "neither dust nor humiliation will cover their faces", saying this is after looking at the face of their Lord; in 17620 he was asked about "and more" and said that when the people of Paradise enter and receive the honour and delight they receive, they are called: people of Paradise, God has promised you the "more", and He appears to them; Ibn Abi Layla said, what do you think of them, when their scales are heavy, the records are in their right hands, they have crossed the bridge over hell and entered Paradise and received what they received? All that was as nothing in what they saw. Reports 17621, 17622 and 17623 are of the same kind from Ibn Abi Layla, and in 17622: the best reward is Paradise and the more is looking at the face of their Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 66}

The editor says al-Suyuti traced Abu Musa's report in al-Durr al-Manthur, adding Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Daraqutni in his book on the vision, and Ibn Mardawayh, and that reports 17619 to 17623 are to be seen together with the end of the next note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 66}

Report 17624, from al-Hasan: looking at the Lord. Report 17625, from Abd al-Rahman (ibn Mahdi), from the Prophet on the verse: when the people of Paradise enter Paradise and the people of the Fire the Fire, they are called: people of Paradise, you have an appointment with God; they ask what it is, has He not whitened our faces, made our scales heavy, admitted us to Paradise and saved us from the Fire? Then the veil is lifted and He appears to them, and by God He gave them nothing dearer to them than looking at Him. Report 17626, from Suhayb: the messenger of God recited this verse and then gave the report in much the same way: people of Paradise, you have an appointment with God that He wishes to fulfil. The editor says report 17626 is sound, narrated by Muslim in his Sahih through Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi from Hammad ibn Salama and through Yazid ibn Harun, and by al-Tayalisi, Ahmad, Ibn Maja, al-Tirmidhi and al-Ajurri; al-Tirmidhi notes that Sulayman ibn al-Mughira narrated it from Thabit from Ibn Abi Layla as his own saying without Suhayb or the Prophet, which is what al-Tabari gave in reports 17619 to 17623. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 67}

Reports 17627 and 17628, from Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (through Sharik and Abu Ishaq, from Sa'id ibn Nimran and then from Amir ibn Sa'd): looking at the face of God; the editor says it is not stated that Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i heard from Sa'id ibn Nimran and that Amir ibn Sa'd is evidently between them. Reports 17629 and 17630, from Qatada: it reached us that when the believers entered Paradise a caller called out that God promised them the best reward, which is Paradise, and the "more" is looking at the face of the Merciful. Report 17631, from Ka'b ibn Ujra, from the Prophet: the "more" is looking at the face of the Merciful. The editor says Ibrahim ibn al-Mukhtar al-Tamimi was described by Ibn Ma'in as not strong and by al-Bukhari as open to criticism, and that Ata al-Khurasani narrated from Companions in mursal form, so the chain is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 68}

Report 17632, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Sabit: the best reward is radiance and the "more" is looking at the face of God. Report 17633, from Abu al-Aliya: Ubayy ibn Ka'b told us that he asked the messenger of God about God's words "for those who do good is the best reward and more", and was told the best reward is Paradise and the more is looking at the face of God; the editor says the chain is weak because the one who narrated from Abu al-Aliya is unknown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 69}

Others said something else about the "more". Report 17634, from Ali: the "more" is a chamber of a single pearl with four doors, through Yahya ibn Talha, Fudayl ibn Iyad, Mansur and al-Hakam; report 17635, from him, is the same except that it has "four doors" (with a variant wording), and report 17636, from him, is the same as Yahya ibn Talha's. The editor says al-Hakam ibn Utayba al-Kindi was born in the year 50 and died in 113 and had a leaning toward Shi'ism that did not show itself, and that the report is weak because it is mursal from Ali. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 69}

Others said the "best reward" is one good deed for one, and the "more" is the multiplying up to ten. Report 17637, from Ibn Abbas: it is like "and with Us there is more", He repays them for their deed and adds from His bounty, and He said "whoever brings a good deed will have ten like it"; report 17638, from Alqama ibn Qays: I said this is the best reward, so what is the more? He said, have you not seen that God says "whoever brings a good deed will have ten like it"? Report 17639, from Qatada, that al-Hasan used to say: the more is that a good deed gets ten like it up to seven hundredfold. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 70}

Others said the "best reward" is a good deed matching a good deed, and the "more" is an added forgiveness and good pleasure from God; report 17640, from Mujahid: its like in goodness and more, forgiveness and good pleasure. Others said the "more" is what they were given in this world; report 17641, from Ibn Zayd: the best reward is Paradise and the "more" is what He gave them in the world, for which He will not call them to account on the Day of Resurrection, and he recited "and We gave him his reward in this world", saying that what he was given of what he loves in the world was his reward hastened to him there. Ibn Abbas used to say the best reward is for those who bear witness that there is no god but God, which is report 17642. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 71}

Al-Tabari chooses this: God promised the doers of good among His servants, for their good doing, the best reward, that He will repay their obedience with Paradise and whiten their faces, and promised them with it a "more" on top; among the additions to admitting them to Paradise are that He honours them with looking upon Him, gives them chambers of pearls and adds forgiveness and good pleasure, all of which are additions to God's giving beyond the best reward He made for the people of His gardens. By "more" God included all the additions to the best reward without singling out one rather than another, and it is not unlikely from God's bounty that He joins all that for them, so the best-founded view is to keep it general as He kept it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 71}

He then explains "neither dust nor humiliation will cover their faces; those are the companions of Paradise, abiding in it": no gloom or dimness will cover their faces, so that from grief they look as if covered in dust. "Qatar" is dust, plural of "qatara"; he cites a line by al-Farazdaq in praise of Bishr ibn Marwan, picturing the one praised crowned in the cloak of kingship, followed by a surge of men over whom banners and dust are seen (described, not quoted), and the editor gives its places in the diwan, Majaz al-Qur'an and the Lisan, the difference in the diwan's wording and the two lines before it. "Nor humiliation" means no disgrace; "those are the companions of Paradise" means those so described are Paradise's people and dwellers; "abiding in it" means staying for ever, never perishing, so that they should fear losing their delight, and never driven out, so that their pleasure be spoiled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 72}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
