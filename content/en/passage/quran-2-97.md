## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "say: whoever is an enemy to Gabriel, it is he who sent it down upon your heart by God's leave", al-Tabari says the interpreters agree that the verse was revealed in reply to the Jews of the Children of Israel when they claimed Gabriel was their enemy and Michael their friend, and they differ on the occasion. Some say it arose from a debate between them and the Prophet about his prophethood. He gives a report from Ibn Abbas (whose chain the editor judges sound) that a group of Jews asked him four things only a prophet would know, after taking God's covenant from them that they would follow him if he answered: what food Israel forbade himself before the Torah was sent down; how the water of man and woman differ and how a boy or girl comes about; the sleep of this unlettered prophet; and which angel is his ally. He answered that Israel fell ill and vowed that if God healed him he would forbid himself his dearest food and drink, camel meat and milk; that the man's fluid is white and thick and the woman's yellow and thin, and whichever prevails determines the child and resemblance; and that his eyes sleep and his heart does not; and they affirmed all this. When he said his ally was Gabriel, they said had it been another angel they would have followed him, for Gabriel was their enemy; the verse came down through "as if they did not know". A second, mursal report from Shahr ibn Hawshab has the same questions with one on the Spirit, in which they said Gabriel is an angel who comes only with severity and bloodshed, and but for that they would have followed him. From al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza (the editor says the chain is broken) and Mujahid he reports that they said he comes down only with war, hardship and fighting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 378} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 380}
