## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "say: whoever is an enemy to Gabriel, it is he who sent it down upon your heart by God's leave", al-Tabari says the interpreters agree that the verse was revealed in reply to the Jews of the Children of Israel when they claimed Gabriel was their enemy and Michael their friend, and they differ on the occasion. Some say it arose from a debate between them and the Prophet about his prophethood. He gives a report from Ibn Abbas (whose chain the editor judges sound) that a group of Jews asked him four things only a prophet would know, after taking God's covenant from them that they would follow him if he answered: what food Israel forbade himself before the Torah was sent down; how the water of man and woman differ and how a boy or girl comes about; the sleep of this unlettered prophet; and which angel is his ally. He answered that Israel fell ill and vowed that if God healed him he would forbid himself his dearest food and drink, camel meat and milk; that the man's fluid is white and thick and the woman's yellow and thin, and whichever prevails determines the child and resemblance; and that his eyes sleep and his heart does not; and they affirmed all this. When he said his ally was Gabriel, they said had it been another angel they would have followed him, for Gabriel was their enemy; the verse came down through "as if they did not know". A second, mursal report from Shahr ibn Hawshab has the same questions with one on the Spirit, in which they said Gabriel is an angel who comes only with severity and bloodshed, and but for that they would have followed him. From al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza (the editor says the chain is broken) and Mujahid he reports that they said he comes down only with war, hardship and fighting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 378} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 380}

Al-Tabari gives the view of others that the occasion was a debate between 'Umar ibn al-Khattab and the Jews about the Prophet. He reports from al-Sha'bi that 'Umar used to visit the Jews on their study day; when they told him he was the dearest of the Prophet's companions to them, he asked them by God whether they knew that he was God's messenger, and their senior man admitted they did, but said they had an enemy among the angels, Gabriel, angel of harshness, severity and punishment, and a peaceful ally, Michael, angel of mercy and ease. 'Umar said that Gabriel, Michael and what lies between them are an enemy to whoever opposes them and at peace with whoever is at peace with them, and that neither should make peace with the other's enemy; he then met the Prophet, who recited the verses to him, and 'Umar said he had come to tell him, but the Subtle, the All-Aware had forestalled him (the editor says the report is mursal because al-Sha'bi did not meet 'Umar, and refers to Ibn Kathir's version). From Qatada, similar, adding that they said Gabriel discloses their secrets to Muhammad and brings war and famine while Michael brings plenty and peace; from al-Suddi, that 'Umar had land in upper Medina and passed their study house, and adjured them by the All-Merciful whether they found Muhammad in their book; they admitted he is written there but said Gabriel is their enemy, lord of every punishment, fighting and earthquake, and Michael of mercy and rain; asked their stations with God they said right and left, and 'Umar said whoever is an enemy to the one on the right is an enemy to the one on the left, and whoever is an enemy to both is an enemy to God; and from Mujalid through al-Sha'bi, similar, in which they said God sends no messenger without a sponsor among the angels and Muhammad's is Gabriel, their enemy (the editor too calls it mursal). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 380} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 381} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 382} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 383} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 384} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 385}

Al-Tabari reports from Ibn Abi Layla and 'Ata' that the Jews said to the Muslims: had Michael been the one who came down to you we would have followed you, for he comes with mercy and rain while Gabriel comes with punishment and retribution and is our enemy. He then explains the verse: God tells His prophet to say to the Jews who claimed Gabriel is their enemy because he brings force and punishment, not revelation and mercy, that Gabriel is the one who brought the Qur'an down on his heart by God's leave, strengthening his heart as was done for earlier messengers. He reports from Ibn Abbas that when the Jews asked many things and he told them, they said "except Gabriel", since in their view he is lord of punishment and might, and God belied them with the verse; and from Qatada and al-Rabi' that Gabriel brought down the Book on his heart. On why it says "your heart" and not "my heart", he explains that when Arabs command someone to relay what was said to him they may attach the verb to the pronoun of the one speaking of himself or to that of the one addressed, as in Al 'Imran 3:12, "you will be overcome" or "they will be overcome". On "Jibril" he gives the Arab forms: the Hijazis "Jibril and Mikal" without hamza (read so by the readers of Medina and Basra); Tamim, Qays and some of Najd "Jabra'il and Mika'il" (the readers of Kufa), citing a verse of Jarir; the Banu Asad "Jibrin"; some "Jabra'il and Mika'il" with an alif. He rejects the reading "Jabril" of al-Hasan and Ibn Kathir because the pattern "fa'lil" does not exist in Arabic. He holds that "jabr" and "mik" mean servant and little servant and "il" is God, reporting this from Ibn Abbas, 'Ikrima, 'Umayr the freedman of Ibn Abbas, 'Ali ibn al-Husayn and 'Abd Allah ibn al-Harith: Gabriel is servant of God, Michael little servant of God, Israfil servant of the All-Merciful, and "il" is God in Hebrew (the editor calls one narrator of one of these chains weak). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 386} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 387} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 388} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 390}
