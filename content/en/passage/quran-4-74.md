## Summary
Verse 74 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 74 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse (p. 541, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says this is God's urging of the believers to fight his enemy among the unbelievers in all their circumstances, whether winning or losing, and his making light of what the hypocrites say about those who fight the polytheists, and that for them in this fighting is a high station with God. He explains fighting in God's way as fighting in God's religion and the call to it and to entering what he ordered the unbelievers to enter; those who sell their worldly life for the reward of the hereafter and what God promised his obedient there, their selling being their spending their wealth seeking God's pleasure and fighting those they are ordered to fight of the enemies of God and his religion, and their giving their lives for it. Then he tells what is theirs if they do so: whoever fights, seeking to establish God's religion and raise his word, the enemies of God, and is killed by them or overcomes and prevails over them, God will give him in the hereafter a recompense that is beyond measure, and what God called by that name has no measure whose extent servants know (the editor refers to p. 529, note 2, on the word for reward). The editor says the manuscript and print both have "the states of the polytheists" for what the context shows to be "the words of the hypocrites", meaning what the hypocrite says at the Muslims' defeat and at their victory, and that the word for making light is coordinated with the urging; that in the place of the bracketed words both have a word that does not make sense, and he supplied what he judged the sense to be; and that both have "like fighting" where the context requires "for fighting". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 541}

**Reports 9942-9943 (p. 542, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says he showed earlier that the predominant meaning of the verb shara in Arab speech is "he sold", enough to spare repetition. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9942, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the sense is that they sell this life for the next. Yunus (9943, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the verb means both "he sold" and "he took", and that those who sold the hereafter for this world (the word is garbled in the scan and read from the sense). The editor says he added the bracketed words in al-Tabari's text following his practice in hundreds of earlier places, probably dropped by the copyist because the phrase fell at the end of a page and he turned the leaf and wrote the next word; and refers to earlier interpretations of the verb (page numbers scrambled). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 542}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 542}
