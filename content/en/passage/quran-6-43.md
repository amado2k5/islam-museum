## Summary
Verse 43 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 43 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "why did they not humble themselves when our might came on them? But their hearts hardened and Satan made what they were doing seem fair to them" al-Tabari says: this too is speech with something left out, the apparent sense making mention unnecessary: God reported of the peoples who denied their messengers that he seized them with hardship and affliction that they might humble themselves to him, then said "why did they not humble themselves when our might came on them?", without reporting what they did when he seized them with hardship and affliction; the sense is: we sent to communities before you and seized them with hardship and affliction that they might humble themselves, and they did not, "so why did they not humble themselves when our might came on them?" "Lawla" here means "hallan", why not. The Arabs, when they put a raised noun after "lawla", make what follows the predicate and meet it with the command, saying "lawla akhuka la-zurtuka" and "lawla abuka la-darabtuka"; when they put a verb after it, or no noun, they make it a question, saying "lawla ji'tana fa-nukrimaka", "lawla zurta akhaka fa-nazuraka", meaning "why not", as in [[passage/quran-63-10|63:10]], "if only you would defer me to a near term so that I could give alms"; "lawma" acts like "lawla". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 356}

The editor keeps "that they humble themselves to him", where the manuscript has "by him", refers for "lawla" to p. 343 and for the senses to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, and corrects "and meet it with the command" from the manuscript.

So the sense is: why did these peoples who denied their messengers and did not humble themselves when we seized them with hardship and affliction not humble themselves when our might came on them, submitting to their Lord and bowing to obey him, so that their Lord would turn aside his might, his punishment? He has explained the sense of "might" elsewhere; "but their hearts hardened" means they persisted in denying their messengers, insisted on it and were too proud for their Lord's command, thinking little of God's punishment and making light of his torment, from hardness of heart, "and Satan made what they were doing seem fair to them", that is, Satan made fair to them the deeds God dislikes and is angered by in them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 357}

Verse 43 closes here at p. 357, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-44|6:44]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
