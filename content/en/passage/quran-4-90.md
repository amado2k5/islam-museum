## Summary
Verse 90 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 90 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The exception for those who reach a people with a truce (p. 19, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the hypocrites who turn away and refuse to emigrate are to be killed wherever found, except those among them who reach a people with whom you have a truce and treaty, enter among them, become part of them and accept their rule; by entering them such a person has secured his blood, and his women and children are not to be taken captive nor his property seized. Report 10069 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: if they show unbelief, kill them, but if one enters a people with whom you have a treaty, treat him as you treat the protected non-Muslims. Report 10070 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: they have the same security as those people. Report 10071 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ikrima: it came down about Hilal ibn Uwaymir al-Aslami, Suraqa ibn Malik ibn Ju'shum and Khuzayma ibn Amir ibn Abd Manaf. The editor refers back to reports 10052 and 10053. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 19}

**Al-Tabari rejects the reading by descent (p. 20, notes 1-3).** He reports that some grammarians claimed the verb means "those who trace their descent to a people with a treaty", from the usage that a man "connected himself" for "claimed descent", citing a line of al-A'sha about a woman who claimed descent from a people. The editor identifies this grammarian as Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an and explains from the lexicon and a commentator on the poet that the line concerns a woman of that people who had been taken captive and then called on them with the call of pre-Islamic kin loyalty. Al-Tabari answers, in his own reasoning, that this reading has no place here: if claiming descent from a treaty people gave an uncovenanted person their rights, the Prophet would not have fought Quraysh, who were kin of the earliest believers; the believers' right through faith exceeds the covenanters' right through treaty; and his fighting the Quraysh idolaters despite their nearness of descent to the believers is clear evidence that a non-covenanted person's descent to a covenanted one gives him no covenant. If someone thinks that fighting came after this ruling was abrogated, the commentators agreed that the abrogating text is Bara'a (sura 9), revealed after the conquest of Mecca and the entry of Quraysh into Islam. The editor says the manuscript and the printed edition both had a faulty phrase here; he restored "sura Bara'a" on the evidence of al-Nasikh wa'l-Mansukh, Abu Hayyan and al-Qurtubi, who all attribute it to al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 20}

**Those who came with constricted chests (p. 21, note 1).** Al-Tabari: except those among them who came to you with their chests too tight to fight you or their own people, and joined you. The Arabs say of anyone whose heart is too narrow for an action or a speech that he is constricted, and the word for stammering in recitation comes from it. Report 10072 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: they returned and joined you, and their chests were too tight to fight you or their people. The editor refers to the earlier treatment of the word, to Abu Ubayda's Majaz and to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 21}

**Grammar and reading (p. 22, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari holds that the particle "qad" is omitted before the verb, so the sense is "they came to you, their chests having tightened"; the Arabs say "so-and-so came, his mind gone" for "his mind having gone", and with the understood particle a past verb can serve as a circumstantial clause. The editor says this is al-Farra's statement, that the place named in the example lies between Kufa and the lands of Ghatafan, and that the manuscript reading is right in one phrase. Al-Tabari adds that the readers in all the regions read the verb in the familiar form, by the agreement of the authorities, and that a reading with the accusative is reported from al-Hasan al-Basri; it is sound and eloquent Arabic, but he does not allow reciting it, as it is irregular and departs from the readers of Islam (his own view). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 22}

**Had God willed, He would have given them power (pp. 23-24, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says that had God willed, He would have given power over you to those who reached a treaty people and those who came with constricted chests, and they would have fought you alongside your enemies, but God held them back, so obey Him in holding back from them. If they withdraw, do not fight you and make peace: the word for peace here means surrender, as a man says "I handed you my reins" for submitting to another's command. He cites a line of al-Tirimmah, and the editor (pp. 23-24) gives its background: the poet was satirising al-Farazdaq and the houses of Darim and Sa'd; Amr ibn al-Mundhir burnt the Banu Darim on the day of Awara, killing ninety-nine of them; Abu Ubayda said al-Tirimmah did not know that story; the lions of the line are Amr and his men; and a word miswritten in both manuscript and print is corrected. Report 10073 (al-Muthanna, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father) from al-Rabi': the word means peace. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 24}

**The meaning of the denial of a path (p. 24).** Al-Tabari: when these people surrender in peace, God has opened no path for you against their persons, property, children or women to kill, capture or take booty, by permission or licence, so do not touch them except for good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 24}

**Abrogation (pp. 24-26, note).** Al-Tabari then says God abrogated the whole ruling of this verse and the next by the verse on the sacred months having passed ([[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]]). Report 10074 (Ibn Humayd, from Yahya ibn Wadih, from al-Husayn, from Yazid) from Ikrima and al-Hasan: they cite these verses together with [[passage/quran-60-8|60:8]] and [[passage/quran-60-9|60:9]] and say they were abrogated regarding the idolaters by [[passage/quran-9-1|9:1]] and [[passage/quran-9-2|9:2]], which gave them four months to travel and voided what came before, then by the verse after that ([[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]]) with its exception for the repentant and its following verse ([[passage/quran-9-6|9:6]]). Report 10075 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from Qatada: the verse of fighting the idolaters abrogated it. Report 10076 (al-Muthanna, from al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, from Hammam ibn Yahya) from Qatada: it was abrogated afterwards in Bara'a and the Prophet was ordered to fight the idolaters. Report 10077 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: jihad abrogated all of it, and a four-month term was set for them: either they accept Islam or there is jihad. These statements on abrogation are given as transmitted, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 26}
