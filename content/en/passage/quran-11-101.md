**Commentary on verse 101 (pp. 472-474)**

On [[passage/quran-11-101|11:101]] al-Tabari says: We did not punish the people of these towns whose story We have told you, Muhammad, without their deserving Our punishment, so as to put Our punishment where it did not belong; 'but they wronged themselves', that is, by disobeying God and disbelieving in Him they made His punishment and torment due upon themselves; 'their gods whom they called upon besides God availed them nothing' means the gods they invoked as lords did not ward off any of God's punishment from them 'when your Lord's command came', His decree to punish them; 'and they added nothing to them but ruin', that is, loss, destruction and perdition. One says 'tabbabtuhu' of ruining, and from it comes 'woe to you'; he cites a line of Jarir against al-Ra'i al-Numayri, and the editor tells the cause of the lampoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 472}

Report 18553, from Ibn Umar: 'nothing but ruin' means loss, the editor weakening one narrator of the chain and vouching for another; 18554 and 18555, from Mujahid, and 18556 and 18557, from Qatada: loss. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 473}

Al-Tabari adds: this report from God, though about past peoples, is a warning to us, community, that if we follow the way of earlier peoples in opposing Him and His Messenger, He will treat us as they were treated, and a notice that He wrongs none of His creatures, it being the servants who wrong themselves; report 18558, from Ibn Zayd: our Lord made excuse to His creatures, saying 'We did not wrong them' in the punishment of those We punished among the nations, 'but they wronged themselves, their gods availed them nothing…' to 'they added nothing to them but ruin'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 474}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
