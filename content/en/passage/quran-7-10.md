## Summary
Verse 10 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 10 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "We established you on the earth and made for you in it means of livelihood; little do you give thanks", God says: We made the earth smooth for you, people, and made it a place of settled rest for you, a cradle you prepare and a bed you spread, "and made for you in it means of livelihood" by which you live the days of your life, of foods and drinks, a favour from Me to you and kindness; "little do you give thanks" means how little your thanks for these blessings I gave you, since you worship others than Me and take a god besides Me. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "establishing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 316}

"Ma'ayish" is the plural of "ma'isha". The readers differ on it: most readers of the lands read "ma'ayish" without hamza, and Abd al-Rahman al-A'raj read "ma'a'ish" with hamza. Al-Tabari says the right reading to him is without hamza, because it is "mafa'il" from the saying "you lived, you live", the mim being extra and the ya in its ruling vowelled, since its singular "ma'isha" has a vowelled ya whose vowel was moved to the 'ayn in the singular; when pluralised its vowel returns to it because what precedes it is at rest and it is vowelled. The Arabs do the same with ya and waw when what precedes them is at rest and they are vowelled, in the likes of this plural on the pattern "mafa'il". That differs from plurals on the pattern "fa'a'il", in which the ya is extra and not original; for the Arabs put hamza in what comes in that pattern, as in "mada'in" and "sahā'if" and their like, because "mada'in" is the plural of "madina", itself "fa'ila" from "I built the city", and likewise "sahā'if" is the plural of "sahifa", itself "fa'ila" from "I wrote the page"; so the ya in the singular is extra and at rest, and when pluralised it takes hamza because it differs in the plural from the ya it was in the singular, for there it was at rest, and in the plural it is vowelled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 316}

...there it was at rest in its singular and is vowelled in the plural. Had "madina" been made "mif'ala" from "dana yadin" and pluralised on "mafa'il", the eloquent form would be to leave out the hamza and vowel the ya. The Arabs sometimes put hamza in the plural of "mif'ala" in words with ya or waw, though the eloquent of their speech is to leave out the hamza when it comes on "mafa'il", likening its plural to the plural of "fa'ila", as they liken "maf'al" to "fa'il" and say "masil al-ma'" from "sala yasil" and then pluralise it like "fa'il", "amsila", likening it to the plural of "ba'ir", "ab'ira", and likewise the plural of "masir", which is "maf'il", is "musran", likened to "bu'ran", the plural of "ba'ir". On this basis al-A'raj put hamza in "ma'a'ish", which is not the eloquent form in their speech; and the best reading in which God's Book is recited, of the tongues, is the most eloquent and best known, not the most unfamiliar and anomalous. The note refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 317}
