## Summary
Verse 284 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 284 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-284|2:284]]), pp. 101 and 102. Al-Tabari explains the opening of the verse as saying that God owns all that is in the heavens and the earth, small and large; its ordering is His, its turning and changing is in His hand, and nothing of it is hidden from Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} He holds that what is meant here is the witnesses' concealing of testimony: do not conceal it, witnesses, for whoever does so has a corrupt heart and it will not stay hidden, since God knows everything, so fear His punishment; this is a warning and a threat, and this is his own reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} Then God tells what He will do with them in the hereafter, and with those like them who hid a sin in the heart or showed a ruinous act, namely the reckoning for it: if you show, in the testimony you hold against the owner's right, denial and rejection, or hide it, or do other evil deeds, God will reckon it, meaning He will count it against your deeds; He repays whom He wills of the wrongdoers for the evil done and forgives whom He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} In the notes to p. 101 the editor says the manuscript and print give the verse's closing clause neither here nor elsewhere until the commentary on the end of the verse, and that he placed it in its place; in other notes he gives his preferred wording where manuscript and print differ, says that on p. 102 the copyist wrote a singular where the sense needs a plural and moved a phrase out of place in haste, and explains that the expression for 'I counted it against him' means to reckon a sum with him; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over whom the verse means. Some said as he did, that it concerns the witnesses in their concealing of testimony and that anyone like them who hid or showed a sin is included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} In 6449, Ibn Abbas (through Abu Zaida Zakariya ibn Yahya, Ibn Fudayl, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad and Mujahid): it means testimony; in 6450, Ibn Abbas (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad and Miqsam): in testimony. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} Report 6451 opens with a tradition from Dawud when he was asked about the verse, and the page breaks off there; the editor notes on 6449 that Ibn Fudayl is Muhammad ibn Fudayl and that the earlier text had a wrong name; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices are his, and the editor's notes are his, with no museum verdict.

In 6451, Ikrima (through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-Ala and Dawud): it is the testimony when you conceal it; in 6452, Ikrima (through Ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Jafar and Shuba, from Amr): about testimony; in 6453, al-Shabi (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and al-Suddi): about testimony; in 6454, Ibn Abbas (through Yaqub, Hushaym, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad and Miqsam): it came down about concealing testimony and giving it; in 6455, Ikrima (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir): concealing testimony and giving it properly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 103}

Others said it came down rather to inform God's servants that He holds them to account for what their hands earned and what their souls spoke to them, even where they did not act on it. Those who held this then differed, and some said God lifted it by [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 103} In 6456, Abu Hurayra (through Abu Kurayb, Ishaq ibn Sulayman, Musab ibn Thabit, al-Ala ibn Abd al-Rahman and his father): when it came down it weighed heavily on the people, who told the Prophet they would be held to account for what their souls told them and were lost; God then sent down [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] with its supplications, and after each, as Abu Hurayra relays, God answered 'yes'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 104} In 6457, Ibn Abbas (through Abu Kurayb from Waki, and Sufyan ibn Waki from his father, from Sufyan al-Thawri, Adam ibn Sulayman the freedman of Khalid ibn Khalid and Said ibn Jubayr): when it came down something entered their hearts that nothing had entered before, and the Prophet told them to declare hearing, obedience and submission; God put faith in their hearts and sent down [[passage/quran-2-285|2:285]] and [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], and after each supplication God said: I have done so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 105}

The editor's notes on pp. 104-105 are long and are his. On 6456: Ishaq ibn Sulayman al-Razi is reliable, firm and devout, and the six collections took from him; Musab ibn Thabit ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr was spoken against by the imams, weakened by Ahmad, Ibn Main and others, praised by al-Zuhri, and called by Abu Hatim truthful but often mistaken and not strong, while al-Bukhari's al-Kabir records no criticism of him; the editor thinks those who weakened him meant his many errors, and says he did not stand alone in this report. He traces it to Ahmad's Musnad (through Affan and Abd al-Rahman ibn Ibrahim al-Qass from al-Ala, in a longer form), to Muslim's Sahih and Ibn Hibban's Sahih (through Yazid ibn Zurayʻ and Rawh ibn al-Qasim from al-Ala), notes Ibn Kathir's and al-Hafiz's mentions and al-Suyuti's addition of Abu Dawud's work on abrogation, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim, and says part of it returns at 6538 with the same chain. On 6457: Sufyan ibn Waki relates from his father, who relates from Sufyan al-Thawri; the print dropped the words 'my father told us', an error, and the chain comes correctly at 6537; Adam ibn Sulayman al-Qurashi is reliable and the father of Yahya ibn Adam, author of the book on land tax; he traces it to Ahmad's Musnad, to Muslim (through Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba, Abu Kurayb and Ishaq ibn Rahawayh from Waki), and to al-Hakim's Mustadrak, where al-Hakim calls the chain sound though not taken by the two Shaykhs and al-Dhahabi agrees; he disputes the statement in al-Tahdhib that Muslim took it only as corroboration, saying it is a basis since Muslim has it by no other route; and he says its sense comes at 6464 from Said ibn Jubayr as a mursal, so the link is supplied by this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 105}

In 6458, Said ibn Marjana (through Abu al-Raddad al-Misri, Abu Zura Wahb Allah ibn Rashid, Haywa ibn Shurayh, Yazid ibn Abi Habib and Ibn Shihab): I came to Ibn Umar, who recited the verse and said that if God took them to account by it they would perish, and he wept until his tears ran; I went to Ibn Abbas and told him, and he said God forgive Ibn Umar, the Companions of the Prophet were afraid of it as he was, so God sent down [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], which lifted the whispering thoughts and fixed word and deed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 106} In 6459, by another route from Ibn Shihab: Ibn Umar wept until his sobbing was heard, and Ibn Abbas said the Muslims had felt what Ibn Umar felt, so God sent down [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] after it; he said the whispering thoughts were beyond the Muslims' power and the matter ended with the soul having what it earned and bearing what it earned, in word and deed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 107} In 6460, al-Zuhri (through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Mamar): Ibn Umar read it and wept, saying they are held to account for what they tell themselves; a man went to Ibn Abbas, who said God have mercy on Ibn Umar, the Muslims felt something like it until [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 107} In 6461, Mujahid (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq, Jafar ibn Sulayman and Humayd al-Araj): I was with Ibn Umar, who read the verse and wept; I went to Ibn Abbas, who laughed and said God have mercy on Ibn Umar, does he not know why it came down? When it came down it grieved the Companions gravely and they said they were lost; the Prophet told them to declare hearing and obedience, and [[passage/quran-2-285|2:285]] to [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] abrogated it, so they were excused for the soul's talk and held to account for deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 108} In 6462, Salim (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Yazid ibn Harun, Sufyan ibn Husayn and al-Zuhri): his father read it and his eyes filled; his act reached Ibn Abbas, who said he had done as the Companions did when it came down, and the verse after it abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 108} In 6463, Said ibn Jubayr (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Ata ibn al-Sa'ib): this verse was abrogated by [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 109} In 6464, Said ibn Jubayr (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Adam ibn Sulayman): when it came down they asked whether they would be held to account for what their selves told them and their limbs did not do, and [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] came down, with God answering the first supplication 'I have done so'; and this community was given the closing verses of the sura, which earlier nations were not given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 109}

Further notes of the editor, his: on 6458, that Abu al-Raddad al-Misri Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Salam, a teacher of al-Tabari, is reliable (Ibn Abi Hatim: we heard from him in Egypt and he is truthful), that the name of Abu Zura Wahb Allah ibn Rashid is here for the first time printed correctly, and that Said ibn Marjana is Said ibn Abd Allah, freedman of Quraysh, whose mother was Marjana, a reliable tabii whose hearing from Abu Hurayra is established; on 6459, that Ibn Kathir, al-Hafiz in al-Fath (who says al-Tabari narrated it by a sound chain from al-Zuhri) and al-Suyuti (adding Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Dawud's work on abrogation, al-Tabarani and al-Bayhaqi in al-Shuab) mention it; on 6460, that it is mursal, since al-Zuhri heard it from neither Ibn Umar nor Ibn Abbas, and is abridged from the two before it and from 6462, al-Zuhri having heard the story from Said ibn Marjana and Salim ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar; on 6461, that Jafar ibn Sulayman is al-Duba'i, Humayd al-Araj is Humayd ibn Qays al-Makki, reader of Mecca, and Ahmad narrates it from Abd al-Razzaq through Mamar from Humayd, so Abd al-Razzaq heard it from two teachers, and he suggests reading 'he recited' for 'he said' and 'from the soul's talk' for 'of'; on 6462, that he reconsiders his earlier weakening of Sufyan ibn Husayn in his narration from al-Zuhri, thinking Ibn Hibban exaggerated since al-Bukhari lists him without criticism and the imams graded the report sound, that Abu Jafar al-Nahhas in his work on abrogation and al-Hakim (sound chain, al-Dhahabi agreeing) narrate it through Yazid ibn Harun, and that Ibn Kathir calls these sound routes from Ibn Abbas; on 6463, that Abu Ahmad is al-Zubayri, Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr al-Asadi, and that the report is mursal, being Said's account of being told of the abrogation, 6457 perhaps pointing to the same sense; on 6464, that it is also mursal, giving part of the sense of 6457 which Said had from Ibn Abbas in a connected chain, and that part of it returns at 6539 with the same chain and a corrupted name of the narrator from Sufyan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 106} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 108} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 109}

We stop at the end of p. 109, at the end of 6464; the rest is on p. 110. These are reports as transmitted, and the editor's notes and gradings are his, with no museum verdict.
