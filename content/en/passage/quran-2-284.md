## Summary
Verse 284 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 284 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-284|2:284]]), pp. 101 and 102. Al-Tabari explains the opening of the verse as saying that God owns all that is in the heavens and the earth, small and large; its ordering is His, its turning and changing is in His hand, and nothing of it is hidden from Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} He holds that what is meant here is the witnesses' concealing of testimony: do not conceal it, witnesses, for whoever does so has a corrupt heart and it will not stay hidden, since God knows everything, so fear His punishment; this is a warning and a threat, and this is his own reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} Then God tells what He will do with them in the hereafter, and with those like them who hid a sin in the heart or showed a ruinous act, namely the reckoning for it: if you show, in the testimony you hold against the owner's right, denial and rejection, or hide it, or do other evil deeds, God will reckon it, meaning He will count it against your deeds; He repays whom He wills of the wrongdoers for the evil done and forgives whom He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} In the notes to p. 101 the editor says the manuscript and print give the verse's closing clause neither here nor elsewhere until the commentary on the end of the verse, and that he placed it in its place; in other notes he gives his preferred wording where manuscript and print differ, says that on p. 102 the copyist wrote a singular where the sense needs a plural and moved a phrase out of place in haste, and explains that the expression for 'I counted it against him' means to reckon a sum with him; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over whom the verse means. Some said as he did, that it concerns the witnesses in their concealing of testimony and that anyone like them who hid or showed a sin is included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} In 6449, Ibn Abbas (through Abu Zaida Zakariya ibn Yahya, Ibn Fudayl, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad and Mujahid): it means testimony; in 6450, Ibn Abbas (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad and Miqsam): in testimony. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} Report 6451 opens with a tradition from Dawud when he was asked about the verse, and the page breaks off there; the editor notes on 6449 that Ibn Fudayl is Muhammad ibn Fudayl and that the earlier text had a wrong name; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 102} These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices are his, and the editor's notes are his, with no museum verdict.

In 6451, Ikrima (through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-Ala and Dawud): it is the testimony when you conceal it; in 6452, Ikrima (through Ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Jafar and Shuba, from Amr): about testimony; in 6453, al-Shabi (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and al-Suddi): about testimony; in 6454, Ibn Abbas (through Yaqub, Hushaym, Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad and Miqsam): it came down about concealing testimony and giving it; in 6455, Ikrima (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir): concealing testimony and giving it properly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 103}

Others said it came down rather to inform God's servants that He holds them to account for what their hands earned and what their souls spoke to them, even where they did not act on it. Those who held this then differed, and some said God lifted it by [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 103} In 6456, Abu Hurayra (through Abu Kurayb, Ishaq ibn Sulayman, Musab ibn Thabit, al-Ala ibn Abd al-Rahman and his father): when it came down it weighed heavily on the people, who told the Prophet they would be held to account for what their souls told them and were lost; God then sent down [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] with its supplications, and after each, as Abu Hurayra relays, God answered 'yes'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 104} In 6457, Ibn Abbas (through Abu Kurayb from Waki, and Sufyan ibn Waki from his father, from Sufyan al-Thawri, Adam ibn Sulayman the freedman of Khalid ibn Khalid and Said ibn Jubayr): when it came down something entered their hearts that nothing had entered before, and the Prophet told them to declare hearing, obedience and submission; God put faith in their hearts and sent down [[passage/quran-2-285|2:285]] and [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], and after each supplication God said: I have done so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 105}

The editor's notes on pp. 104-105 are long and are his. On 6456: Ishaq ibn Sulayman al-Razi is reliable, firm and devout, and the six collections took from him; Musab ibn Thabit ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr was spoken against by the imams, weakened by Ahmad, Ibn Main and others, praised by al-Zuhri, and called by Abu Hatim truthful but often mistaken and not strong, while al-Bukhari's al-Kabir records no criticism of him; the editor thinks those who weakened him meant his many errors, and says he did not stand alone in this report. He traces it to Ahmad's Musnad (through Affan and Abd al-Rahman ibn Ibrahim al-Qass from al-Ala, in a longer form), to Muslim's Sahih and Ibn Hibban's Sahih (through Yazid ibn Zurayʻ and Rawh ibn al-Qasim from al-Ala), notes Ibn Kathir's and al-Hafiz's mentions and al-Suyuti's addition of Abu Dawud's work on abrogation, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim, and says part of it returns at 6538 with the same chain. On 6457: Sufyan ibn Waki relates from his father, who relates from Sufyan al-Thawri; the print dropped the words 'my father told us', an error, and the chain comes correctly at 6537; Adam ibn Sulayman al-Qurashi is reliable and the father of Yahya ibn Adam, author of the book on land tax; he traces it to Ahmad's Musnad, to Muslim (through Abu Bakr ibn Abi Shayba, Abu Kurayb and Ishaq ibn Rahawayh from Waki), and to al-Hakim's Mustadrak, where al-Hakim calls the chain sound though not taken by the two Shaykhs and al-Dhahabi agrees; he disputes the statement in al-Tahdhib that Muslim took it only as corroboration, saying it is a basis since Muslim has it by no other route; and he says its sense comes at 6464 from Said ibn Jubayr as a mursal, so the link is supplied by this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 105}

In 6458, Said ibn Marjana (through Abu al-Raddad al-Misri, Abu Zura Wahb Allah ibn Rashid, Haywa ibn Shurayh, Yazid ibn Abi Habib and Ibn Shihab): I came to Ibn Umar, who recited the verse and said that if God took them to account by it they would perish, and he wept until his tears ran; I went to Ibn Abbas and told him, and he said God forgive Ibn Umar, the Companions of the Prophet were afraid of it as he was, so God sent down [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], which lifted the whispering thoughts and fixed word and deed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 106} In 6459, by another route from Ibn Shihab: Ibn Umar wept until his sobbing was heard, and Ibn Abbas said the Muslims had felt what Ibn Umar felt, so God sent down [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] after it; he said the whispering thoughts were beyond the Muslims' power and the matter ended with each soul answerable for its word and deed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 107} In 6460, al-Zuhri (through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Mamar): Ibn Umar read it and wept, saying they are held to account for what they tell themselves; a man went to Ibn Abbas, who said God have mercy on Ibn Umar, the Muslims felt something like it until [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 107} In 6461, Mujahid (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Razzaq, Jafar ibn Sulayman and Humayd al-Araj): I was with Ibn Umar, who read the verse and wept; I went to Ibn Abbas, who laughed and said God have mercy on Ibn Umar, does he not know why it came down? When it came down it grieved the Companions gravely and they said they were lost; the Prophet told them to declare hearing and obedience, and [[passage/quran-2-285|2:285]] to [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] abrogated it, so they were excused for the soul's talk and held to account for deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 108} In 6462, Salim (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Yazid ibn Harun, Sufyan ibn Husayn and al-Zuhri): his father read it and his eyes filled; his act reached Ibn Abbas, who said he had done as the Companions did when it came down, and the verse after it abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 108} In 6463, Said ibn Jubayr (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Ata ibn al-Sa'ib): this verse was abrogated by [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 109} In 6464, Said ibn Jubayr (through Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Adam ibn Sulayman): when it came down they asked whether they would be held to account for what their selves told them and their limbs did not do, and [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] came down, with God answering the first supplication 'I have done so'; and this community was given the closing verses of the sura, which earlier nations were not given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 109}

Further notes of the editor, his: on 6458, that Abu al-Raddad al-Misri Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Salam, a teacher of al-Tabari, is reliable (Ibn Abi Hatim: we heard from him in Egypt and he is truthful), that the name of Abu Zura Wahb Allah ibn Rashid is here for the first time printed correctly, and that Said ibn Marjana is Said ibn Abd Allah, freedman of Quraysh, whose mother was Marjana, a reliable tabii whose hearing from Abu Hurayra is established; on 6459, that Ibn Kathir, al-Hafiz in al-Fath (who says al-Tabari narrated it by a sound chain from al-Zuhri) and al-Suyuti (adding Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Dawud's work on abrogation, al-Tabarani and al-Bayhaqi in al-Shuab) mention it; on 6460, that it is mursal, since al-Zuhri heard it from neither Ibn Umar nor Ibn Abbas, and is abridged from the two before it and from 6462, al-Zuhri having heard the story from Said ibn Marjana and Salim ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar; on 6461, that Jafar ibn Sulayman is al-Duba'i, Humayd al-Araj is Humayd ibn Qays al-Makki, reader of Mecca, and Ahmad narrates it from Abd al-Razzaq through Mamar from Humayd, so Abd al-Razzaq heard it from two teachers, and he suggests reading 'he recited' for 'he said' and 'from the soul's talk' for 'of'; on 6462, that he reconsiders his earlier weakening of Sufyan ibn Husayn in his narration from al-Zuhri, thinking Ibn Hibban exaggerated since al-Bukhari lists him without criticism and the imams graded the report sound, that Abu Jafar al-Nahhas in his work on abrogation and al-Hakim (sound chain, al-Dhahabi agreeing) narrate it through Yazid ibn Harun, and that Ibn Kathir calls these sound routes from Ibn Abbas; on 6463, that Abu Ahmad is al-Zubayri, Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr al-Asadi, and that the report is mursal, being Said's account of being told of the abrogation, 6457 perhaps pointing to the same sense; on 6464, that it is also mursal, giving part of the sense of 6457 which Said had from Ibn Abbas in a connected chain, and that part of it returns at 6539 with the same chain and a corrupted name of the narrator from Sufyan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 106} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 108} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 109}

In 6465, Amir al-Shabi (through Abu Kurayb, Jabir ibn Nuh and Ismail): the verse after it, [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], abrogated it. In 6466, al-Shabi (through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mughira): the next verse abrogated it, and a person was reckoned with for what he showed or hid of a secret, then it was abrogated. In 6467, al-Shabi (through Yaqub, Hushaym and Sayyar): there was hardness in it until the next came and abrogated what was before it. In 6468, Ibn Awn (through Yaqub and Ibn Ulayya): the verses were mentioned before al-Shabi up to the end of the passage, and he said it came to this, and he went back to the end of the verse. In 6469, al-Dahhak (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir) from Ibn Masud: the reckoning was before the other verse came down, and when it came it abrogated what preceded; 6470 gives the same from al-Dahhak from Ibn Masud (through al-Husayn, Abu Muadh and Ubayd). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 110}

In 6471, al-Shabi (through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Bayan): it was abrogated; in 6472, Muhammad ibn Kab (through Ibn Waki, his father and Musa ibn Ubayda) and Mujahid (through Sufyan from Jabir, and through Ibrahim ibn Muhajir): [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] abrogated the verse; in 6473, Ikrima and Amir the same; in 6474, al-Hasan (through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hammad and Humayd): it wiped it out; in 6475 and 6476, Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said, and through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Mamar): [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 111} On 6474 the editor explains that Hammad is Hammad ibn Salama and Humayd is Humayd al-Tawil, that the earlier text had 'Hammad ibn Humayd', a name no transmitter bears, and that al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal narrates from Hammad ibn Salama, who narrates from his maternal uncle Humayd al-Tawil, who narrates from al-Hasan; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 111}

In 6477, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): when the verse came down it weighed on the Muslims and was very hard on them, and they asked the Prophet whether, if something arose in their selves that they did not act on, God would hold them to it; he said perhaps they would say as the Children of Israel said, hearing and disobeying; they said no, hearing and obeying; and the Qur'an came down to relieve them with [[passage/quran-2-285|2:285]] and [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], which turned the matter to deeds and left what occurs in hearts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 111} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 112} In 6478, Abu Ubayda ibn Abd Allah ibn Masud (through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hushaym, Sayyar Abu al-Hakam and al-Shabi): the verse after abrogated it. In 6479, al-Suddi (through Musa, Amr and Asbat): at that time they were held to account for what their selves whispered and for what they did; they complained to the Prophet that they could not control the whisperings, and God abrogated it with [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]], so the soul's talk was something beyond their capacity. In 6480, Qatada (through Ammar, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father): Aisha, mother of the believers, said [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 112} The editor notes that the print has a different wording from the manuscript and that he kept the manuscript's, and that the copyist moved a word to the wrong place, which he put back; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 112}

Then al-Tabari gives the view of others, among those who said the sense is God informing His servants that He holds them to account for what their hands earned and their limbs did and what their selves told them without their acting: that the verse is firm and not abrogated, and God reckons His creation for what they did and for what they did not do but resolved in themselves, intended and wanted, forgiving it for the believers and holding to account the unbelievers and hypocrites; the editor refers back to p. 103 and following. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 113} In 6481, Ibn Abbas (through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Muawiya and Ali): it was not abrogated; rather, when God gathers creation on the day of resurrection He says that He will tell them what they hid in themselves that His angels did not see; the believers He tells and forgives what their selves told them, so reckoning here means informing; the people of doubt He tells what they hid of denial, which is the sense of being taken to account for what hearts earned in [[passage/quran-2-225|2:225]], doubt and hypocrisy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 113} The editor says the manuscript and print add after 'denial' a clause saying that this is God's forgiving and punishing whom He wills; he thinks it the copyist's addition and left it out, because al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur gives the report without it in both places; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 113}

In 6482, Ibn Abbas (through Muhammad ibn Sad, his father, his uncle, and his father from his father): this is the secret and open of your deeds that God holds you to account for; no believing servant resolves in himself a good to do it but, if he does it, ten good deeds are written for him, and if he is not enabled to do it one good deed is written because he is a believer; if it is an evil he told himself, God informs him of it on the day secrets are tried, and if he did not act on it God does not hold him until he does, and if he does God overlooks it as in [[passage/quran-46-16|46:16]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 113} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 114} In 6483, al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib): on the day of resurrection God says that His scribes wrote only what appeared of their deeds, and what they kept secret He reckons today, forgiving and punishing whom He wills. In 6484, Qays ibn Abi Hazim (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Ali ibn Asim, Bayan and Bishr): the same, as God's address on that day. In 6485, al-Dahhak (through al-Husayn, Abu Muadh and Ubayd): Ibn Abbas used to say that when people are called to reckoning God tells them what they hid in themselves and did not do, saying nothing escapes Him and He will tell them of the evil they hid that their keepers did not see; this is the reckoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 114} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 115}

In 6486, al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas, the same (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Abu Tumayla and Ubayd ibn Sulayman). In 6487, al-Rabi' (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father): it is firm and nothing abrogated it; God makes the servant know on that day that he hid such and such in his chest, and does not hold him to it. In 6488, al-Hasan (through Amr ibn Ubayd): firm, not abrogated. In 6489 to 6491, Mujahid (through Yaqub, Ibn Ulayya and Ibn Abi Najih; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; and al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl): it means doubt and certainty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 115} The editor says the print put certainty before doubt and he kept the manuscript's order; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 115}

Abu Jafar says: on the report of Ibn Abbas transmitted by Ali ibn Abi Talha (6481), the verse means that whatever deeds you show with your bodies and limbs, or hide in yourselves so that none of His creatures knows it, He will reckon, forgiving all of it for the people of faith and punishing the people of polytheism and hypocrisy in His religion; this is his explanation of what was transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 115} On the report of al-Dahhak from him through Ubayd ibn Sulayman (6486) and the saying of al-Rabi' ibn Anas (6487), it means that if you show what is in your selves by committing sins, or conceal a resolve to do them, God will make you know it on the day of resurrection and forgive whom He wills and punish whom He wills; and Mujahid's saying (6489) is close to what Ali ibn Abi Talha transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 116}

Others, among those who held it firm and unabrogated and agreed that it informs of what God will do with them for what they showed and hid, said it means that God reckons all His creation for all the evil deeds they showed and kept secret and punishes them for it, but His punishment of what they hid without doing is the misfortunes that befall them in this world and that grieve and pain them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 116} In 6492, al-Dahhak (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir): Aisha used to say that whoever intends an evil and does not do it, God sends him worry and grief like what he intended, and that is its expiation. In 6493, al-Dahhak (through al-Husayn, Abu Muadh and Ubayd): Aisha used to say every servant who intends a disobedience or tells himself of it is reckoned with in this world, fearing, grieving and worrying. In 6494, the same from al-Dahhak (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Abu Tumayla): nothing else reaches him, as he intended evil and did none of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 116} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 117}

In 6495, Umayya (through al-Rabi', Asad ibn Musa, Hammad ibn Salama and Ali ibn Zayd) asked Aisha about this verse and about [[passage/quran-4-123|4:123]], and she said no one had asked her about them since she asked the Prophet, who said: Aisha, this is God's following of the servant with the fever, the mishap and the thorn, even a purse he puts in his sleeve and loses and is alarmed, then finds in his pocket, until the believer comes out of his sins as red gold comes out of the furnace. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 117} The editor notes: Ali ibn Zayd is Ibn Judan; Umayya is the daughter of Abd Allah, a tabiiyya who narrated nothing else from Aisha, and Ali ibn Zayd was her husband's stepson; the print reads 'from his mother', an error found also in some copies of al-Tirmidhi, though it would do no harm if sound since a stepson might call her his mother; he traces the report to al-Tayalisi, to Ahmad's Musnad (through Bahz from Hammad ibn Salama) and to al-Tirmidhi (through Rawh ibn Ubada), who called it good and strange from Aisha, known only through Hammad ibn Salama; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 117}

These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices and explanations are his, and the editor's notes and references are his, with no museum verdict.

The rest of the editor's note on report 6495 (p. 118): he says Ibn Abi Hatim narrated it, as Ibn Kathir reports, through Sulayman ibn Harb from Hammad ibn Salama, with 'from his father' for 'from Umayya', a printer's corruption; he gives Ibn Kathir's remark that Ali ibn Zayd ibn Judan is weak and has oddities in his narrations and narrates this report from his father's wife Umm Muhammad Umayya daughter of Abd Allah, who has nothing else from Aisha in the books; he then answers that in his view Ali ibn Zayd is not weak, as he said earlier and favoured in his commentary on the Musnad; he notes al-Suyuti's addition of Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Bayhaqi in al-Shuab; he explains the phrase 'God's following the servant' as what afflicts a person and pains him, which God sends after him to atone for his sins, noting that some sources have a close word and Ibn Kathir a corruption; and he glosses the rare words (the mishap, the small stock of goods, the space between armpit and flank, raw gold, and the smith's bellows). All of this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 118}

Abu Jafar says: the best of the views on the verse is that it is firm and not abrogated; this is his choice. His reason is that abrogation of a ruling occurs only when another ruling negates it in every respect, and [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] has nothing that negates what God told His servants here, since reckoning does not necessarily bring punishment or being held to account for the sins reckoned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 118} He recalls that God told of the criminals that when the books of their deeds are shown on the last day they wonder at a book that leaves out neither small nor great but counts it (it is in [[passage/quran-18-49|18:49]]); so the books count small deeds and great, but their counting does not oblige the people of faith in God and His Messenger and of obedience to be punished for all they count, since God promised them pardon of minor sins if they avoid the major ones ([[passage/quran-4-31|4:31]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 118} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 119} So God's reckoning with His believing servants for what their selves hid does not bring them punishment; rather it is so that He lets them know His favour in pardoning them, as we are told from the Prophet in the report he gives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 119} The editor notes that the print differs from the manuscript here and he kept the manuscript's wording, and that both have a phrase that needs the masculine form as he set it; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 119}

In 6496, Ibn Umar from the Prophet (through Ahmad ibn al-Miqdam, al-Mutamir ibn Sulayman, his father, Qatada and Safwan ibn Muhriz): God draws His believing servant near on the day of resurrection until He places His cover over him and has him acknowledge his sins: do you know it? He says yes; He says: I hid it in the world and forgive it today; then his good deeds are shown to him, whereas the unbeliever is called out before the assembled witnesses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 119} In 6497, Safwan ibn Muhriz (through Ibn Bashshar, Ibn Abi Adi, Said and Hisham, and through Yaqub, Ibn Ulayya and Hisham): while we were circling the House with Ibn Umar a man approached and asked what he had heard from the Prophet about the private converse; he said he heard him say that the believer draws near his Lord until He places His cover over him and has him acknowledge his sins, saying do you know such a thing? and he says 'Lord, forgive' twice, until, when it has gone as far as God wills, He says: I hid them for you in the world and I forgive them for you today; then he is given the record of his good deeds in his right hand, while the unbelievers and hypocrites are called out before the witnesses as those who lied about their Lord ([[passage/quran-11-18|11:18]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 120}

The editor's long notes on 6496-6497 (pp. 119-121) are his: Safwan ibn Muhriz al-Mazini is a reliable and eminent tabii of piety and merit, and 6496 is an abridgment of 6497; Said is Ibn Abi Aruba, the reliable memoriser, and Hisham is al-Dastuwai; the print reads 'Ibn Abi Adi, and Said, and Hisham', a corruption for 'from Said', because Ibn Abi Adi (Muhammad ibn Ibrahim) narrates from Ibn Abi Aruba and Hisham, is not of their generation and did not meet Qatada, and likewise Ibn Bashshar, al-Tabari's teacher, and because the dual in 'both said' refers to Said and Hisham; he notes printing slips in Ibn Kathir's copy of it, and that the report comes again in al-Tabari's commentary in part 14 with the chain correct, though there he separates the chain of Ibn Ulayya from that of Ibn Abi Adi. He traces it to Ahmad's Musnad (through Bahz and Affan from Humam from Qatada, and through Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata from Said), to al-Bukhari in several places (through Hisham, through Said and Hisham, and through Abu Awana from Qatada), to Muslim (through Hisham), and to Abu Jafar al-Nahhas's work on abrogation, who says its chain is one that raises no doubt in the heart and is among the reports of the Sunna people; he adds Ibn Kathir, al-Suyuti (adding Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi in al-Asma wa-l-Sifat) and al-Qastallani (who gives it to al-Nasai in his tafsir and Raqaiq and to Ibn Majah in the Sunna); and he says the manuscript here has 'the unbelievers or the hypocrites' in the accusative, an obvious error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 119} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 120} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 121}

Al-Tabari says that this which God does with His believing servant, letting him know the evil of his deeds until He lets him know His favour by pardoning them, is like His reckoning with him for what he showed of himself and what he hid, then forgiving all of it after letting him know His favour and generosity, and covering it for him; and this is the forgiveness God promised His believing servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 121} The editor sets out the grammar of one sentence and says the print dropped a letter in the verse's wording, which he kept as in the manuscript; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 121}

Then al-Tabari raises an objection and answers it. If someone says that the statement in [[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]] that a soul has what it earned shows that all creatures are held only for the sin their selves earned and rewarded only for the good they earned? It is said: that is so, and a servant is not held for anything except doing what he was forbidden or leaving what he was commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 121} If he says: what then is the sense of God's threat to us over what our selves hid, in His saying that He punishes whom He wills, if a soul has only what it earned, and what our hearts concealed of resolve to sin or wish to disobey was not earned by our limbs? It is said to him: God promised the believers pardon for what is greater than what one of them resolved of sins and did not do, namely pardon of the minor sins when they avoid the major. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 121} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 122} The threat of punishing whom He wills is rather over what is hidden by selves that hid doubt about God and uncertainty about His oneness, or about His prophet's prophethood and what he brought, or about the return and resurrection, among the hypocrites, in the way Ibn Abbas and Mujahid and those who agree with them said that the sense is doubt and certainty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 122} But he says that the one threatened with punishment is the one whose hidden thing is doubt and uncertainty about God and about matters where doubt in God is unbelief; and the one promised forgiveness is the one whose hidden thing is the resolve to go forward to something God forbade, which was originally permissible and which He then made unlawful, or to leave something God commanded, which it was originally permissible to leave and which He then made obligatory; the believer who resolves such a thing and does not make good his resolve nor carry out what his self concealed is not held to it. This is his own explanation and choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 122} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 123} In 6498, from the Prophet (without chain): whoever resolves on a good deed and does not do it, a good deed is written for him; whoever resolves on an evil and does not do it, it is not written against him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 123} The editor says al-Tabari gave no chain, that the reports of God overlooking the soul's talk are in Muslim's Sahih in other wording and in the rest of the Sunna books; and in the notes to p. 122 he sets out the grammar of the long sentence and says the manuscript is undotted in one phrase and that what he set is correct; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 122} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 123}

Al-Tabari concludes that what he has described is what God reckons with His believing servants and then does not punish them for, whereas the one whose hidden thing is doubt about God and distrust of the prophethood of His prophets is the lost one, abiding in the Fire, whom God threatened with a painful punishment. So the sense of the verse is: if you show what is in your selves, people, or hide it so that your selves hold it, God will reckon it, letting your believer know His favour in pardoning and forgiving him, and punishing your hypocrite for the doubt his self holds about his Creator's oneness and the prophethood of his prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 123} The editor says the manuscript has the plural for 'your believer' and 'your hypocrite' and that the print's singular is sounder and better; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 123}

At the close of the verse al-Tabari explains the clause stating God's power over everything: He is able to pardon what this believer's self hid of resolve to err, and to punish this unbeliever for what his self hid of doubt about God's oneness and His prophets' prophethood, and to repay each for what came from him, and over other things. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 123}
