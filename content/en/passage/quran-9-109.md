**Commentary on verse 109 (pp. 490-491)**

Al-Tabari says: the readers differed in reading His words "is he who founded his building". Some of the readers of Medina read it "is he whose building was founded on piety from God and pleasure better, or he whose building was founded", in the passive in both places. Most of the readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read it "is he who founded his building on piety from God and pleasure better, or he who founded his building", describing "he who" as the doer who founded the building. They are two readings of the same sense, and whichever the reader reads, he is right, though to read with the verb directed to "he who", since he is the founder, is dearer to me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 491}

So the sense of the passage is: which of these who built mosques is better, people, in your view: those who began the building of their mosque on fear of God, by obeying Him in its building and performing His obligations and winning God's pleasure for their building of what they built and their doing what they did, or those who began the building of their mosque on the brink of a crumbling bank? By "on the brink of a bank" He means on the edge of a bank, the edge of wells being what has no lining built for it, and the discussion continues on the next page. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the brink and notes that this explanation is Abu Ubayda's in Majaz al-Qur'an, the lining being the side of a well or a grave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 491}

We stop here at the foot of p. 491, in the middle of the commentary on verse 109 (at the explanation of "on the brink of a bank"); the next batch begins at p. 492. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 491}
