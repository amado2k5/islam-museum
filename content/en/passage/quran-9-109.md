**Commentary on verse 109 (pp. 490-494)**

Al-Tabari says: the readers differed in reading His words "is he who founded his building". Some of the readers of Medina read it "is he whose building was founded on piety from God and pleasure better, or he whose building was founded", in the passive in both places. Most of the readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read it "is he who founded his building on piety from God and pleasure better, or he who founded his building", describing "he who" as the doer who founded the building. They are two readings of the same sense, and whichever the reader reads, he is right, though to read with the verb directed to "he who", since he is the founder, is dearer to me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 491}

So the sense of the passage is: which of these who built mosques is better, people, in your view: those who began the building of their mosque on fear of God, by obeying Him in its building and performing His obligations and winning God's pleasure for their building of what they built and their doing what they did, or those who began the building of their mosque on the brink of a crumbling bank? By "on the brink of a bank" He means on the edge of a bank, the edge of wells being what has no lining built for it. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the brink and notes that the explanation of the edge and the lining is Abu Ubayda's in Majaz al-Qur'an, the lining being the side of a well or a grave. The word for "crumbling" means reckless; it is really a form with the letters transposed, so that its last letter was moved, as one says "armed" in two forms, and its root is from a verb meaning to be about to fall, and it was also said to be from a verb meaning to collapse, whoever takes it from this dialect saying "you collapsed, bank" and whoever takes it from the other saying "you were about to fall, bank". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 492}

This is a parable. The Almighty says: which of these two parties is better, and which of these two buildings is firmer, he who began the foundation of his building on obedience to God, knowing that his building is obedience to God and God is pleased with it, or he who began it with hypocrisy and error and with no insight into the rightness of his act as against its error, who does not know when the error of his act and the greatness of his sin will become clear to him so that he demolishes it, like a building raised on the brink of a well-pit, with nothing to hold back from it the water of the floods and of other waters, its earth loose and crumbling, which the floods will soon demolish and scatter? God, glorious is His praise, says "so it collapsed with him into the Fire of Hell", meaning the crumbling bank scattered with its building into the Fire of Hell. Report 17244, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "so it collapsed with him", meaning its foundations, "into the Fire of Hell"; report 17245, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd: "so it collapsed with him", he says so it fell with him. The editor explains the vocabulary of moist earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 492}

Report 17246, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "is he who founded his building on piety from God" to His words "so it collapsed with him into the Fire of Hell", he said, by God it did not stop until it fell into the Fire; it was mentioned to us that a hollow was dug in a spot of it and smoke was seen coming from it. Report 17247, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: Banu Amr ibn Awf asked the Prophet's leave to build it and he gave them leave, and they finished it on Friday and prayed Friday in it, and Saturday and Sunday; he said it collapsed on Monday; he said he had asked them to wait three days, Saturday, Sunday and Monday; "so it collapsed with him into the Fire of Hell", the mosque of the hypocrites collapsed and did not stop short before it fell into the Fire; Ibn Jurayj said it was mentioned to us that men dug into it and saw smoke coming out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 493}

Report 17248, from Jabir, through al-Muthanna, al-Himmani, Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Mukhtar, Abdallah al-Dananj and Talq ibn Habib: "those who took a mosque for harm", he said, I saw the mosque built for harm with smoke coming out of it in the time of the Prophet. The editor notes the report has a sound chain, cited by al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur. Report 17249, from Jabir ibn Abdallah, through Muhammad ibn Marzuq, Abu Salama, Abd al-Aziz ibn al-Mukhtar, al-Dananj and Talq al-Anazi: I saw smoke coming out of the mosque of harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 493}

Report 17250, from Khalaf ibn Yasin al-Kufi, through Sallam ibn Salim al-Khuza'i: I went on pilgrimage with my father in that time, meaning the time of Banu Umayya, and we passed through Medina and I saw the mosque of the two qiblas, meaning the mosque of the Messenger, with the qibla of Jerusalem in it; when the time of Abu Ja'far came they said "the ignorant man enters and does not know the qibla", and this building you see was done at the hand of Abd al-Samad ibn Ali; and I saw the mosque of the hypocrites that God mentioned in the Qur'an, in which is a stone from which smoke comes out, and it is today a rubbish heap. The editor notes that this Khalaf may be Khalaf ibn Yasin ibn Mu'adh al-Zayyat, a liar, and that his father is weak and abandoned in hadith. As for "and God does not guide the wrongdoing people", it means God does not grant success in right guidance in his acts to whoever builds his building in what is not its right or place, and whoever is a hypocrite opposing by his act the command of God and the command of His Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 494}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
