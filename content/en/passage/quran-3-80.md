## Summary
Verse 80 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 80 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 80: two readings of the verb of command.** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed in reading the verb. Most reciters of the Hijaz and Medina read it with the nominative ending, as a fresh statement from God that the Prophet does not command people to make the angels and the prophets lords. They cited a reading they reported from Ibn Masud with a particle of negation that marks a new sentence, and said that putting the particle of our reading in its place requires the nominative ending. The editor notes that al-Farra gave this account in Maani al-Quran. Some Kufans and Basrans read it with the accusative ending, joined to the verb mentioned before it, so that the sense is that it does not befit a human being to do this nor to command you to take the angels and the prophets as gods.

**Al-Tabari's choice and reasoning.** Al-Tabari holds the accusative reading to be the more correct for its connection with what precedes, because the verse came down on account of the people who said to the Prophet, do you want us to worship you; God told them that it is not for His prophet to call people to worship himself or to regard the angels and the prophets as lords, and that what he may do is to call them to be rabbaniyyun. As for what the proponents of the nominative claimed, that the reading of Abd Allah ibn Masud had the emphatic negation, al-Tabari says the report's chain is unsound, relayed by Hajjaj from Harun al-A'war, and adds that even were the chain sound it would carry no proof, since what stands in the copy of the Muslims, which they inherited from their Prophet, may not be left for a reading ascribed to some Companions that was transmitted by people liable to error and lapse. In a note the editor says that Hajjaj is Hajjaj ibn Muhammad al-Misisi al-A'war, who lived in Baghdad then in al-Massisa and died in Baghdad in 206; Ahmad praised his exactness over letters, and he was trustworthy and truthful but changed and became confused late in life, so that Yahya ibn Main told his son to let nobody in to him; the editor cites al-Hafiz, in the entry on Sunayd ibn Dawud, as indicating that Hajjaj narrated while confused and fears that al-Tabari alluded to Sunayd's report from him. Harun al-A'war is Harun ibn Musa Abu Abd Allah al-Ataki, a learned, truthful, reliable scholar with a well-known reading. The editor also sets out the corruptions in the printed text and the manuscript and how he corrected them, attributing some to the copyist's frequent misreadings.

**The sense according to al-Tabari.** Al-Tabari explains that the sense is that it is not for the Prophet to command you, people, to regard the angels and the prophets as lords, meaning gods worshipped apart from God, just as it is not for him to call them to worship himself. Then God denies of His prophet that he would command people to unbelief, meaning the denial of God's oneness after they have become submissive to Him in obedience and humble to Him in servitude; that will never come from him. Al-Qasim relays from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj that the one meant is the Prophet (7322). The editor says he kept the word 'ubuda' as in the manuscript, because the publisher of the printed text had made it 'ubudiyya' wherever it occurred earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 549}
