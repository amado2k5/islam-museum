## Summary
Verse 87 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 87 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 87: "Do not forbid the good things God has made lawful to you", pp. 513-516**

Al-Tabari says: you who believed God and His messenger, do not forbid the good things God has made lawful to you, the pleasant things that souls long for and hearts incline to, by denying them to yourselves as the priests and monks did, forbidding themselves women, good food and pleasant drink, some shutting themselves in hermitages and some roaming the land. Do not do as they did, and do not exceed the limit God set for you in what He permitted and forbade, for God does not love one who goes beyond the limit.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 514}

Report 12336, from Abu Malik, says Uthman ibn Maz'un and some Muslims forbade themselves women and good food and one of them wished to cut off his member, and the verse came down. Reports 12337, 12338 and 12340, from Ikrima, say some Companions had resolved on castration and giving up meat and women. Report 12339, from Ibrahim, says they forbade themselves good things and meat.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 515}

Report 12341, from Abu Qilaba, says some Companions wished to abandon the world, give up women and live as monks; the Prophet stood and spoke sharply, saying that those before you were destroyed by severity, they were severe on themselves and God was severe with them, and those are their remnants in the monasteries and hermitages; worship God, associate nothing with Him, perform the pilgrimage and the lesser pilgrimage, and stay upright and it will be upright for you. Report 12342, from Qatada, says it came down about Companions who wished to take little from the world, leave women and live ascetically, naming among them Ali ibn Abi Talib and Uthman ibn Maz'un. Report 12343, from Abu Abd al-Rahman, has the Prophet say that he does not order them to be priests and monks.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 515}

Report 12344, from Qatada, says some Companions gave up women and meat and wished to take hermitages, and the Prophet said there is no giving up of women and meat, nor taking of hermitages, in his religion. It tells of three men who agreed that one would pray all night without sleeping, one fast all day without breaking, and one avoid women; the Prophet sent for them and said he prays and sleeps, fasts and breaks the fast, and goes to women, and whoever turns from his way is not of him. It adds that he told some Companions that those before them were severe on themselves and God was severe with them, and these are their brothers in the monasteries; worship God, associate nothing with Him, keep up prayer, give alms, fast Ramadan, perform the pilgrimage and stay upright. The editor comments on the chain through Bishr ibn Mu'adh and Jami ibn Hammad.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 516}

**Commentary on verse 87, continued: "Do not transgress; God does not love transgressors", pp. 517-522**

Report 12345, from al-Suddi, says the Prophet sat one day and admonished the people, adding only warnings of God's punishment; ten of his Companions, among them Ali ibn Abi Talib and Uthman ibn Maz'un, said that unless they introduced some practice that showed intense fear they had not reached what their Lord was pleased with, and since the Christians had forbidden things to themselves they would do the same. One forbade himself meat and fat, one forbade sleep and one forbade women. Uthman ibn Maz'un was among those who kept away from his wife; she complained of her state to Aisha and the Prophet's wives, who laughed, and when the Prophet came in and asked why, they told him. He sent for Uthman, who said he had left her for God's sake in order to give himself to worship, and had intended to cut himself off; the Prophet charged him by oath to return to his wife and to break his fast, and he did. Then the Prophet said: what of people who forbid themselves women, food and sleep? I sleep and I pray at night, I break my fast and I fast, and I marry women, so whoever turns from my way is not of me; and the verse came down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 517}

Al-Suddi adds that God told Uthman not to cut himself off, for that is transgression, and told them to expiate their oaths. Report 12346, from Ibn Abbas, says a group of Companions wished to cut their members off, give up the world's pleasures and roam the land as monks do; the Prophet heard, sent for them, and they confirmed it; he said that he fasts and breaks his fast, prays and sleeps and marries, and whoever follows his way is of him. Report 12347 says men including Uthman ibn Maz'un forbade themselves women and meat and took blades to end desire and devote themselves to worship; the Prophet said he had not been told to do that, and had been commanded in his religion to marry, and they replied that they obeyed.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 518}

Report 12348, from Mujahid, says men including Uthman ibn Maz'un and Abdullah ibn Amr wished to give up marriage, castrate themselves and wear coarse hair cloth; and, from Ikrima, that Uthman ibn Maz'un, Ali ibn Abi Talib, Ibn Mas'ud, al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad and Salim, the freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, withdrew, sat in their houses, kept away from women, wore hair cloth, forbade themselves good food and clothing, resolved to pray all night and fast all day, and intended to castrate themselves. The verse came down, and the Prophet sent for them and said your selves have a right and your eyes have a right, so fast and break your fast, pray and sleep, for whoever abandons our way is not of us; they replied that they submitted and would follow what God had sent down. The editor explains the word for hair cloth and discusses the word for castration.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 519}

Report 12349, from Ibn Zayd, tells how Abdullah ibn Rawaha had a guest, came home without having eaten and asked his household, who said there was little food and they had waited for him; he said they had kept his guest waiting for his sake and swore the food was forbidden to him, she said the same, and the guest likewise; seeing this, he told them to eat in God's name, and told the Prophet, who said "you did well", and the verse and the following one on idle oaths came down. Report 12350, from Ibn Abbas, has a man say to the Prophet that when he eats meat he is aroused and desire seizes him, so he forbade himself meat, and the verse came down; the editor traces the report to al-Tirmidhi, who called it good but rare.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 520}

Report 12351, from Ikrima, says some Companions had resolved to leave women and castrate themselves and the verse came down. Al-Tabari notes the interpreters differed on "transgression": al-Suddi, in reports 12352 and 12353, says it is what Uthman ibn Maz'un meant to do in cutting himself off, which he was forbidden; others said it is what the group meant of forbidding women, food, clothing and sleep and taking a way other than their Prophet's, which is Ikrima's view; and others said it is passing from the lawful to the forbidden, and report 12354, from al-Hasan, says do not go beyond what is forbidden to you.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 521}

Al-Tabari prefers the general sense: transgression is a person passing beyond what is his to what is not, in anything, and since God made the prohibition of all aggression general, it must be taken generally unless something specific is brought; no one may go beyond God's limit in anything permitted or forbidden, and whoever does falls among those whom God does not love. It is not impossible that the verse came down about Uthman ibn Maz'un and the group, while its ruling covers anyone like them who forbids himself what God permitted, permits what He forbade, or exceeds a limit He set.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 522}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
