## Summary
Verse 78 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 78 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 78: a group who distort the scripture.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that among the People of the Book, the Jews of the Children of Israel who lived around Medina in the Prophet's time, there is a group, and the pronoun in the word "of them" refers to the People of the Book he named in [[passage/quran-3-75|3:75]]. He explains the verb as distorting, so that you think what they distort in their speech is from God's book and revelation. He then says God denies that what they twisted their tongues with, distorted and invented, is from His book, and that they claim that what they attached to God's book of distortion and falsehood is of what God sent down on His prophets, when it is not what God sent to any of them, but something they invented out of themselves as a fabrication against God. They deliberately say a lie against God, bear false witness about Him and attach to His book what is not of it, seeking leadership and the petty goods of the world. The editor refers to earlier places for the sense of the word for a group, says the printed text read a preposition wrongly through poor reading of the manuscript, and notes that the sense of one sentence is a negation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 535} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 536}

**Reports 7290-7295.** In report 7290 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) they distort it; report 7291 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. In report 7292 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) they are the Jews, who distorted God's book, innovated in it and claimed it was from God; the narrator describes them in a harsh phrase which is the narrator's own wording and not the museum's. Report 7293 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdallah ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same. In report 7294 (Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) they are the Jews, who used to add to God's book what God had not sent down. In report 7295 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) they are a group of the People of the Book, and the twisting is their distorting it from its place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 536}

**The root sense of twisting.** Al-Tabari says the root of this verb is twisting and turning, from the saying that so-and-so twisted so-and-so's hand when he twisted and turned it, and from it comes a verse of Far'an ibn al-A'raf in which he says that God twisted the hand of the one who overcame him. One says he twisted his hand and his tongue; one says no one has thrown so-and-so's back when nobody has defeated him; and one says that a man is stubborn to the last when he is fierce in contention, steadfast in it and not beaten. He cites a verse of Majnun Banu Amir who says that if Layla had any hard edge of contention, he would twist the necks of the quarrelsome. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 536} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 537}

**The editor on the verse of Far'an.** He says Far'an is the son of al-A'raf al-Sadi al-Tamimi, also called the son of Asbagh ibn al-A'raf, and traces the verse to Abu Ubayda's Kitab al-Aqqa (Nawadir al-Makhtutat 7, p. 360), the Hamasa (3: 10), Mujam al-Shuara (317), al-Ayni on the margin of al-Khizana (2: 398) and Lisan al-Arab, and says it will come complete in the commentary (15: 160, Bulaq edition). He says Far'an spoke the lines about his son Munazil, who had been disobedient to his father and struck him because the father married a young woman over his mother; angry for his mother he drove off his father's property and withdrew with her. The lines recall what the father had given for him as a child and reproach him for his disobedience. It is said that Munazil woke to find that God had twisted his hand, and that God afterwards afflicted him with another son who was disobedient to him as he had been to his father and drove off his property. He says Abu Jafar completes the line later, with its first half different, which is one of the variants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 537}

**The editor on the verse of Majnun Banu Amir.** He says the verse is not in his diwan but in al-Aghani (2: 38) with other lines and in Lisan al-Arab, and that before it are lines in which people say the Majnun is seeking consolation and he answers that he is as he was, and that his relatives blamed him for loving Layla. He notes the variant "necks of the mounts" in the Lisan against "necks of the opponents" in al-Aghani as al-Tabari gives it, and says Ibn al-Araabi had "the mounts", meaning that if he learned some of what they say of enmity towards his people and clan he would turn away from her like one jealous for his clan. He explains the first term as the edge of anything and its remainder, and the last as the plural of a verbal noun from the root, so that the meaning is: if they contended with me over Layla with a sharp quarrel I would twist their necks until I took their lives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 537} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 538}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
