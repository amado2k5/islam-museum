**Commentary on verse 52: "You see those with sickness in their hearts hurrying among them, saying: we fear a turn of fortune will strike us", pp. 402-407**

The interpreters differed over whom this verse means. Some said 'Abd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul: report 12166, from 'Atiyya ibn Sa'd, says so, with "hurrying among them" meaning in their alliance; report 12167, from 'Ubada ibn al-Walid ibn 'Ubada ibn al-Samit, says it is Ibn Ubayy for his saying that he feared a reversal striking him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 403}

Others said a group of hypocrites is meant who were loyal to the Jews and deceived the believers, saying: we fear the turn of fortune will favour the Jews against the believers. Reports 12168 and 12169, from Mujahid, say they are the hypocrites in their dealings with the Jews, their confidences and their having their children nursed by them; report 12170, from Qatada, says some hypocrites were friendly to the Jews and loyal to them rather than to the believers; report 12171, from al-Suddi, says the sickness is doubt and the turn of fortune is the idolaters prevailing over them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 403}

Al-Tabari holds that the verse is God's report about some hypocrites who allied with the Jews and Christians and deceived the believers, saying: we fear turns of fortune, whether for the Jews and Christians or for the idol-worshipping polytheists or others against the people of Islam, or that some misfortune will strike these hypocrites so that we need them. It may have been Ibn Ubayy's saying or another's, but undoubtedly the hypocrites'. The sense: you see, Muhammad, those with doubt and sickness in their faith in your prophethood and in believing what you brought, hurrying among them, that is in allying with them and courting them, saying we hurry in allying with them only for fear of a turn that comes upon us. The "turn" is the change of fortune, for which he cites a line of Humayd al-Arqat about averting the turns of time: that time may bring a change and we need their help, so we ally with them. The notes say the printed text altered the manuscript wording and the manuscript is right.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 404}

On "perhaps God will bring the conquest or a command from Him, and they will come to regret what they hid within themselves" he says "perhaps" here means "it may be". The interpreters differed over "the conquest": some said the judgment, as report 12172, from Qatada, says; others said the conquest of Mecca, as report 12173, from al-Suddi, says. He says that "conquest" in Arabic is judgment, as Qatada said, as in the verse in al-A'raf, "our Lord, judge between us and our people in truth"; it may be the judgment God promised His prophet was the conquest of Mecca, since that was among His great judgments and a decisive ruling between faith and unbelief, established among the unbelievers and hypocrites that God raises His word and weakens the plotting of the unbelievers.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 405}

As for "or a command from Him", report 12174, from al-Suddi, says the command is the jizya. Al-Tabari says it may be the jizya or something else, but whichever it is, it brings the believers to prevail over the unbelievers and distresses the hypocrites and does not please them, since God told that when that command comes they will regret what they hid, namely their friendship with the Jews and Christians, their affection for them and their hatred and opposition to the believers. Report 12175, from Qatada, says this is from their affection for the Jews and their deceit toward Islam and its people.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 406}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
