## Summary
Verse 85 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 85 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the first part (pp. 580-581, notes 3-4, 1).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: whoever, Muhammad, becomes an even number to the odd one of your companions and joins them in striving against their enemy and fighting in God's cause, that is the intercession of good, and he has from his intercession a lot in God's reward and abundant honor; and whoever becomes the even to the odd of the disbelievers against the believers and fights them alongside them, that is the intercession of evil, and he has a burden of it. The word for that burden means the lot and portion of wrongdoing and sin, and is derived from the word for the cloth or thing laid on a camel or mount, like a saddle on a beast; one says someone came "so mounted" when he came on a mount prepared for him. The editor refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 581}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 581).** Al-Tabari notes that it was said that people's intercession for one another is meant, and that it is not objectionable that the verse was revealed about what he mentioned and then made general for every intercessor in good or evil. He chooses the first sense because it is in the context of the verse in which God commands His prophet to urge the believers to fight, so it is a promise for whoever answers and a threat for whoever refuses, more fitting than urging people's intercession for one another, which has been mentioned neither before nor after. This is al-Tabari's choice; the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 581}

**Reports 10015-10019 (pp. 581-582, note 1).** Muhammad b. Amr (10015, from Abu Asim, from Isa) reports from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, that it means people's intercession for one another, and al-Muthanna (10016, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) the same. Al-Tabari reports (10017, on the authority of Ibn Mahdi, from Hammad b. Salama, from Humayd) from al-Hasan that whoever intercedes in good has two rewards, arguing from the form of the verb in the verse, which is active. Ibn Waki' (10018, from his father, from Sufyan, from a man) reports from al-Hasan that the reward is written for him as long as its benefit continues. Yunus (10019, from Ibn Wahb) reports that Ibn Zayd was asked and said that righteous intercession lies between you and him and the two are partners in it, and likewise evil intercession, the two being partners as its people were partners. The editor says the note is numbered 10016 though its content belongs to al-Hasan's report 10017; that the print took a text from al-Durr al-Manthur while he kept the manuscript; that the first verb in al-Hasan's words should be read in the passive with doubled middle letter; and he explains al-Hasan to mean that whoever intercedes for his brother and is answered has two rewards, the reward of the good he brought to his brother and a reward like that of the one interceded with for doing the good. These statements are given as transmitted; the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 582}

**Reports 10020-10023 on the burden (pp. 582-583).** Bishr b. Mu'adh (10020, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that the first noun is a lot and the other is sin. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (10021, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that it means a lot. Al-Muthanna (10022, from Ishaq, from Abd Allah b. Abi Ja'far, from his father) reports from al-Rabi' that it is a lot, "a wretched lot". Yunus (10023, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the two words are one, and he recited a passage in the sura of Iron ([[passage/quran-57-28|57:28]]) that uses the dual form of the word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 583}

**Closing clause: disagreement on the meaning of the divine attribute (p. 583).** Al-Tabari records the interpreters' disagreement. Some said it means the Guardian and the Witness. Al-Muthanna (10024, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is the Guardian; al-Muthanna (10025, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid that it is the Witness; Ibn Waki' (10026, from his father, from Sufyan, from "a man named Mujahid", as printed, from Mujahid) the same; al-Qasim (10027, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Mujahid that it is witness, reckoner and guardian; and Ahmad b. Uthman b. Hakim (10028, from Abd al-Rahman b. Sharik, from his father, from Khusayf) from Mujahid Abu al-Hajjaj that it is the Reckoner. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 583}

**Other views (p. 584, note 1).** Others said it means the One who maintains everything by management. Al-Qasim (10029, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports that Abd Allah b. Kathir said it means the one constant in attendance, and the editor explains that one says a man was constant in attendance on his property when he kept to it and managed it well. Others said it means the Able. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (10030, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that it is the Able, and Yunus (10031, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 584}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the linguistic evidence (pp. 584-585, notes 1-5).** Al-Tabari says the correct view is that of those who said it means the Able, because this is, as is mentioned, the sense in the Quraysh dialect, and he gives a line ascribed to al-Zubayr b. Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's uncle, in which a man with a grudge is spared by the speaker, who restrained himself though able to harm him (described, not reproduced). The editor says he did not find it for al-Zubayr but found it for Abu Qays b. Rifa'a in Ibn Sallam's Tabaqat Fuhul al-Shu'ara', and that al-Durr al-Manthur ascribes it to Uhayha b. al-Julah al-Ansari. Al-Tabari adds that it was said to be the origin of the Prophet's saying in hadith 10032 that it is enough sin for a man to neglect those he provides for, in the narration of those who narrate it with this verb, meaning those under his hand and authority, his family and dependants, whose sustenance he measures out; he gives the verb forms (to provide, to nourish, with the noun for sustenance). As for the word in the Jewish poet's line (the editor says he is al-Samaw'al b. Adiya), it means held pending the reckoning and is a different sense; the line has the poet wonder what will become of him when the record of his deeds is brought near and he is called, whether grace or guilt will be his. The editor says this sense is Abu Ubayda's and the best said of that line, and refers to Lisan al-Arab, the divan, al-Asma'iyyat and Majaz al-Qur'an. He traces hadith 10032 to Ahmad's Musnad from Abd Allah b. Amr b. al-As (numbers 6842, 6828, 6819, 6495) and al-Hakim's Mustadrak 1:415, says it is sound, and that its narration has the form meaning "to provide". These statements are given as transmitted; the choices are al-Tabari's, the tracing and grading are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 585}
