## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and do not say of those killed in God's way that they are dead; rather they are alive, but you do not perceive it" al-Tabari says: seek help through patience in obeying Me in striving against your enemy, leaving My disobediences and performing My duties, and do not say of one killed in God's way that he is dead, for the dead is one whose life was taken and senses removed so that he neither enjoys nor knows bliss, while those killed in My way are alive with Me in bliss and provision, rejoicing in what I gave them of My favour. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 214} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 215} He reports from Mujahid that they are alive with their Lord, provided with the fruit of Paradise and finding its scent though not in it; from Qatada that they used to be told that the martyrs' souls are in white birds eating the fruits of Paradise, that their dwellings are the Lote-Tree of the Boundary, and that one who fights in God's way has three good things: if killed he becomes alive and provided for, if he prevails God gives him great reward, and if he dies God gives him good provision; and from al-Rabi' and 'Ikrima that they are in the forms of green birds in Paradise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 215}

He raises a question: what is special about the one killed in God's way, when reports spread from the Prophet describe the state of believers and unbelievers after death in the interval (barzakh)? He answers that what God singled out for the martyrs is the information that they are provided from the foods of Paradise in their barzakh before the resurrection and enjoy what the entrants enjoy after it, which is their excellence, and he cites the verse of Al 'Imran, "do not think those killed in God's way dead". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 216} He reports from Ibn 'Abbas from the Prophet that the martyrs are at Bariq, a river at the gate of Paradise, in a green dome, their provision coming to them morning and evening; the editor judges the chain good; and from a later narrator that their souls are in white domes of Paradise, two wives in each dome, with provision each day of an ox and a fish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 217} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 218}

He answers an objection that the verse gives not the bliss but whether they are dead or alive: the aim is to tell of the bliss they are in, which God had already told of in Al 'Imran, so here He forbids saying they are dead. "But you do not perceive it" means you do not see them to know they are alive, and know it only by His report. "Amwat" is in the nominative with an implied "they". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 218} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 219}
