## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari continues the segment: "And We removed from their hearts hatred, malice, and enmity." God removed hatred, malice, and enmity from the hearts of the pious—from Ibn Uyaynah and Al-Dahhak, the enmity—and they differed about the time of removing it: one narration from Abu Umamah that they enter Paradise with the bitterness of this world in their chests until God removes it, and another from him, a believer does not enter Paradise until God removes the grudge in their hearts and removes the seven fierce swamps from him. It is narrated from Al-Hasan Al-Basri from Ali that the verse was revealed about the people of Badr, and from Ibrahim that Ali said when Ibn Jarmuz, the killer of Al-Zubayr, asked for permission: I hope that I, Talha, and Al-Zubayr are among those whom God said, "We are brothers on opposite beds." In another narration, a man from Hamdan said, "God is more just than that." He shouted at me and said, "If we are not us, then who are they?" And 'brothers on opposite beds': brothers in affection within a family that welcomes one another and does not turn to one another.
