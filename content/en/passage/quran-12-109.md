**Commentary on verse 109 (pp. 293-295)**

On "We sent before you only men whom We inspired, from the people of the towns. Have they not travelled through the land and seen how was the end of those before them? And the home of the Hereafter is better for those who are mindful of God. Will you not reason?", al-Tabari says: We sent before you, Muhammad, only men, not women or angels, to whom We reveal Our signs by calling to obedience to Us and devoting worship to Us alone, "from the people of the towns", that is, of the cities rather than the bedouin. Report 19985, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "We sent before you only men whom We inspired, from the people of the towns", because they were more knowing and more forbearing than the people of the tent-pole. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "qarya" and explains that "'amud" is the post standing in the middle of the tent and "people of the 'amud" are the bedouin, as the context shows and as Ibn Zayd explains, giving a line from Lisan al-'Arab in which a speaker disclaims being one of the tent-pole people. On "have they not travelled through the land" he says: have these polytheists who deny you, Muhammad, reject your prophethood and speak of what you have brought them of God's oneness and sincere obedience and worship of Him not travelled {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 293}

"through the land and seen how was the end of those before them", when they denied Our messengers, did We not bring Our punishment on them and destroy them by it and save from it Our messengers and followers, so that they reflect on that and take a lesson? Report 19986, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "We sent before you only men whom We inspired", he said, they had said "God has not sent down anything on a mortal" [[passage/quran-6-91|6:91]]; and he said, God's words "most people, however eager you are, are not believers", "how many a sign", "do they feel secure that a covering will come upon them" and "have they not travelled through the land and seen" those We destroyed? He said: all of that was said to Quraysh: have they not travelled through the land and looked at their traces, to take a lesson and reflect? On "and the home of the Hereafter is better" he says: this is Our doing in the world to the people who are loyal to Us and obey Us, that when Our punishment comes down on those who disobey Us and associate others with Us, We save them from it, and what they have in the Hereafter is better. What we have mentioned is left unsaid, it being enough that the words "and the home of the Hereafter is better for those who are mindful" point to it, and "dar" is annexed to "al-akhira", which is the same thing, because their wordings differ, as it is said "this is the truth of certainty" [[passage/quran-56-95|56:95]], and as it is said {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 294}

"I came to you last year, the night before last, last night, and Thursday", which is a summary of al-Farra's words, and as the poet said in a verse addressing one who praises Fa'qas and blames Abs, saying that if Abs's lands were to leave him alone he would know humiliation as certain knowledge; the editor says he does not know the poet, that al-Farra' relates it, that "hajin" is the child of an Arab by a non-Arab mother, and that "aqwat al-dar" means the dwelling was emptied. Al-Tabari says: the sense of the speech is therefore: and the home of the Hereafter is better for those who are mindful of God by performing His obligations and avoiding disobedience to Him. On "will you not reason?" he says: will these polytheists not grasp the truth of what We tell them of the evil end of disbelief and the unseen to which its people go, despite what they have seen and heard of what befell the unbelieving nations before them who denied their Lord's messengers? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 295}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
