## Summary
Verse 24 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 24 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 24 (pp. 33-39)**

Al-Tabari said: it is mentioned that when the wife of the Aziz set her heart on Joseph and wished to seduce him, she began to recall to him the good points of his own person and to rouse his longing for her. Report 19013, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: she said to him, Joseph, how fine your hair is! He said: it is the first thing that will fall from my body. She said: how fine your face is! He said: it is for the earth to eat. She did not cease until she made him hopeful; she inclined to him and he inclined to her; they went into the house and she shut the doors, and he went to undo his garment, and behold the form of Jacob stood in the house, biting his finger and saying: Joseph, do not lie with her; for your likeness while you do not is that of a bird in the air of the sky that cannot be reached, and your likeness if you do is that bird when it dies and falls to the ground unable to defend itself; and your likeness while you do not is a strong bull that is not worked, and if you do it is a bull when it dies and ants enter the base of its horns, unable to defend itself. So he tied up his garment and went out running; she caught up with him, took the back of his shirt from behind and tore it until he came out of it and it fell; Joseph threw it off and ran toward the door. The editor notes that al-Tabari gave it in his History, that the word "not" is in the History and missing from the manuscript and the printed text, and that "ishtadda" means to run fast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 34}

Report 19014, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the woman bore down on him, now holding out hope to him, now frightening him, calling him to a pleasure that men need, in her beauty, her loveliness and her rank, while he was a young man in his prime who felt what men feel, until he softened to her at seeing how smitten she was, and he did not guard against her until he inclined to her and she to him, and they were alone in one of his rooms. "Inclining toward something" in Arab speech is a person's talking to himself about doing it without doing it; the editor refers to the earlier explanation of "inclining" and notes that it was not explained adequately there, so he explained it here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 34}

As for Joseph's inclination toward the woman and hers toward him, the people of knowledge said what al-Tabari goes on to relate. Reports 19015 to 19031, from Ibn Abbas through several chains including those of Ibn Abi Mulayka and Abd Allah ibn Abi Yazid, and from Mujahid, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ikrima and al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza: Ibn Abbas was asked how far Joseph's inclination went, and he said that he undid the waist-cord of his garment and sat where one sits for circumcision; in another version she lay back for him and he sat between her legs and undid his garments or hers; from Mujahid: he undid the garment as far as his buttocks and she lay back for him, and in another: he sat where a man sits with his wife; from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza: as for hers, she lay back for him, and as for his, he sat between her legs and took off his clothes; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he loosened the cord of his trousers. The editor's notes explain that the "circumciser" is the one who circumcises, that "himyan" is the cord of the trousers, that the manuscript and the printed text differ in the spelling of "buttocks", with a reference to al-Qurtubi, and that al-Tabari gave report 19015 in his History by the same chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 35}

If someone says: how can Joseph be described so, when he is a prophet of God? It is said: the people of knowledge differed on this. Some said: whichever of the prophets was tried with a fault, God tried him with it only so that he would be fearful of God when he remembered it, and find in his obedience a dread of it and not rely on the breadth of God's forgiveness and mercy. Others said: He tried them with it to make them know the place of His favour to them. Others said: He tried them with it to make them leaders for sinners in hoping for God's mercy and not despairing of His pardon when they repent. As for others who departed from the statements of the early generations and interpreted the Qur'an by their own opinions, they said various things. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 36}

Some of them said: the meaning is that the woman set her heart on Joseph, and Joseph set his heart on her to strike her or do her some harm because of her intent, had he not seen the proof of his Lord, which restrained him from what he had intended of hurting her, and she was not restrained by herself; their witness is "thus We turned away from him evil and indecency", the "evil" being what he had intended of hurting her, which is other than "indecency". Others of them said: the meaning is "she set her heart on him", and the report about her ends; then a new report about Joseph begins, "and he would have set his heart on her had he not seen the proof of his Lord", as though they took the sense to be that Joseph did not incline to her and God only said that but for his seeing the proof he would have, whereas he saw it and did not, as in "had it not been for God's favour on you and His mercy, you would have followed Satan, except a few" [[passage/quran-4-83|4:83]]. The editor notes that the manuscript has the right wording at the start of the passage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 37}

Al-Tabari said: both views are invalidated because the Arabs do not put the answer to "lawla" before it, and do not say "I stood up, were it not for Zayd" meaning "were it not for Zayd, I would have stood up", besides their departing from all the people of knowledge of the Qur'an's interpretation from whom its interpretation is taken. Others of them said: the woman did set her heart on Joseph, and Joseph on the woman, but their inclination was a wavering between doing and leaving, not a resolve or intent; they said there is no blame in the soul's talk or the heart's thought when no resolve or deed goes with them. As for the "proof" Joseph saw, on account of which he left the fault, the people of knowledge differ. Some said he was called to forbear the fault; report 19032, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Uyayna, Uthman ibn Abi Sulayman and Ibn Abi Mulayka: he was called, "Joseph, will you commit adultery and be like the bird whose feathers have fallen, which tries to fly and has none?" Report 19033, from him: he did not yield to the call until he saw the proof. The editor notes that "tamyil" means weighing one of two courses against the other, and that "he did not yield" is the right reading of the manuscript, not "he did not heed". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 39}

Report 19033 continues, from Ibn Abbas: until he saw the proof of his Lord; he said: the likeness of the face of his father, biting his finger, and he said: Joseph, will you commit adultery and be like the bird whose feathers are gone? The editor refers to his earlier note. Report 19034, from Ibn Abbas, through Ziyad ibn Abd Allah al-Hassani, Muhammad ibn Abi Adi, Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Abi Mulayka: he was called, "Son of Jacob, do not be like the bird that has feathers, which lose them when it commits adultery"; he did not yield to the call and went no further; Ibn Jurayj said: more than one person told me that he saw his father biting his finger. Report 19035, from Ibn Abbas: he was called and did not hear, so he was told, "Son of Jacob, do you want to commit adultery and be like the bird plucked of feathers?" Report 19036, from Ibn Abi Mulayka, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Talha and Amr al-Hadrami: it has reached me that when Joseph sat between the woman's legs undoing his waist-cord he was called, "Joseph son of Jacob, do not commit adultery, for when a bird does, its feathers scatter"; he turned away; then he was called and turned away; then Jacob appeared to him biting his finger, and he stood up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 40}

Reports 19037 to 19041, from Ibn Abbas, Qatada and Ibn Abi Mulayka, are similar: "Son of Jacob, do not be like the bird which loses its feathers when it commits adultery and is left without them"; he did not yield to the call and was alarmed; in 19038 until he saw the proof of his Lord, was afraid and fled; in 19040, from Qatada: Joseph was called, "You are written among the prophets, and do the deed of fools?" {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 41}

Others said: the "proof" Joseph saw, because of which he held back from the fault, was the form of Jacob warning him. Report 19042, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Amr ibn Muhammad al-Anqazi, Isra'il, Abu Husayn and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he saw the form, or the likeness of the face of Jacob, biting his finger, and his desire went out of his fingertips. Reports 19043 to 19045, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas, are similar: Jacob appeared to him and struck him on the chest and his desire went out of his fingertips; in 19044: he saw the likeness of his father's face, saying with his palm thus, and he spread his palm. Report 19046, from Ibn Abbas: he saw the form of Jacob with his fingertip on his mouth, warning him, and he fled. Report 19047, from him: when he saw Jacob in the roof of the room, the desire he felt was removed until he went out running to the door of the room, and the woman followed him. The editor notes on 19043 that the printed text and manuscript dropped the father's name from al-Anqazi's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 42}

Report 19048, from al-Hasan: they claimed, and God knows best, that the roof of the room opened and he saw Jacob biting his fingers. Reports 19049 and 19050, from al-Hasan: he saw the likeness of Jacob biting his finger, saying "Joseph, Joseph." Report 19051, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he saw the likeness of Jacob's face and his desire went out of his fingertips. Report 19052, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he saw a form with the face of Jacob biting his fingers; he was struck on the chest and his desire went out of his fingertips; every son of Jacob had twelve sons born to him except Joseph, who lost something through that desire and had only eleven. Reports 19053 and 19054, from Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri from Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman: the proof Joseph saw was Jacob. Report 19055, from Mujahid: Jacob appeared to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 43}

Reports 19056 to 19060, from Mujahid, are similar: Jacob; report 19061, from Mujahid: he sat where a man sits with his wife until he saw the form of Jacob on the walls, and the editor restores "on the walls" from the manuscript. Report 19062, from Mujahid: Jacob appeared to him. Report 19063, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza: he was called, "Son of Jacob, do not be like the bird that has feathers, which when it commits adultery sits without feathers"; he did not turn from the call but sat, then lifted his head and saw the face of Jacob biting his finger, and stood up in fright, out of shame before God; that is God's words "had he not seen the proof of his Lord", the face of Jacob. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 44}

Reports 19064 and 19065, from Ikrima: Jacob appeared to him biting his fingers. Report 19066, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: Jacob appeared, struck him on the chest and his desire went out of his fingertips. Report 19067, from Ali ibn Budhayma: each of them used to have twelve sons born to him except Joseph, who had eleven because of what went out of his desire. Report 19068, from Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far: Joseph's desire reached the point that it went out of his fingertips. Report 19069, from Muhammad ibn Sirin: Jacob appeared biting his fingers, saying, "Joseph son of Jacob son of Isaac son of Abraham, friend of God, your name is among the prophets and you do the deed of fools!" Report 19070, from al-Hasan: he saw Jacob biting his finger saying "Joseph". Report 19071, from Qatada: he saw the form of Jacob, who said, "Joseph, you do the deed of the wicked when you are written among the prophets!" and he was ashamed before him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 45}

Report 19072, from Qatada: he saw a sign among the signs of his Lord by which God held him back from disobeying Him; it is mentioned that Jacob appeared to him until he spoke to him, and God protected him and removed every desire that was in his joints. Report 19073, from al-Hasan: Jacob appeared to him biting one of his fingers. Reports 19074, 19075 and 19076, from Abu Salih and al-Hasan: he saw the form of Jacob in the roof of the room, biting his finger and saying "Joseph". Report 19077, from Shamir ibn Atiyya: he looked at the form of Jacob biting his finger saying "Joseph", and that is when he was held back, stood and moved off. Report 19078, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he saw a form with Jacob's face biting his fingers, he was struck on the chest and his desire went out between his fingertips. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 46}

Reports 19079 to 19082, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Abu Salih and al-Hasan, are similar: he saw the likeness of his father's face and the desire went from his fingertips; the form of Jacob in the roof; he saw Jacob biting his hand. Report 19083, from al-Dahhak: "the proof of his Lord" is a sign from his Lord; they claim Jacob appeared to him and he was ashamed before him. Others said: the proof Joseph saw was what God threatened against adulterers. Report 19084, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through Abu Kurayb, Waki' and Abu Mawdud: he raised his head to the roof of the room and found a writing on the wall of the room with the verse forbidding approach to adultery [[passage/quran-17-32|17:32]]. The editor notes that the manuscript and the printed text add the word "and abhorrence" by a slip of the copyist, mixing the verse with the verse of the women [[passage/quran-4-22|4:22]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 47}

Report 19085, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through Ibn Waki', his father and Abu Mawdud: Joseph raised his head to the roof of the room when he inclined and saw writing on the wall of the room containing the prohibition on approaching adultery. Report 19086, from him: had he not seen in the Qur'an the gravity of adultery. Report 19087, from Abu Sakhr, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb and Nafi' ibn Yazid: I heard al-Qurazi say that the proof Joseph saw was three verses from God's book: "over you are guardians" [[passage/quran-82-10|82:10]], "you are not engaged in any matter" [[passage/quran-10-61|10:61]], and "is He who stands over every soul for what it has earned" [[passage/quran-13-33|13:33]]; Nafi' said: I heard Abu Hilal say the same as al-Qurazi and add a fourth verse, the prohibition of approaching adultery. Report 19088, from al-Qurazi: what God forbade him of adultery. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 48}

Others said: rather he saw the likeness of the angel. Report 19089, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "the proof of his Lord" means the signs of his Lord; he was shown the likeness of the angel. Report 19090, from Ibn Ishaq: some people of knowledge, as it reached him, used to say that the proof Joseph saw, by which evil and indecency were turned away from him, was Jacob biting his finger, and on seeing him he fled; others say it was only the image of Itfir his master when he came near the door, because when he fled from her and she followed him, they found her master at the door. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 49}

Al-Tabari said: the soundest view is that God reported Joseph's and the Aziz's wife's inclination, each toward the other, had Joseph not seen the proof of his Lord, which was a sign from God that restrained him from the indecency he inclined to; it may be that sign was the form of Jacob, it may be the form of the angel, and it may be the warning in the verses God mentioned in the Qur'an against adultery, and there is no decisive proof which of these it was; the right course is to say what God said, believe in it, and leave the rest to Him who knows it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 50}

On "thus, that We might turn evil and indecency away from him" he says: as We showed Joseph Our proof to restrain him from the indecency he inclined to, so We arrange for him, in everything that comes to him of an inclination toward what is not pleasing to Us, what restrains and repels it, that We may turn him away from doing what We forbade and from adultery, to purify him from its stain. The editor refers to the explanations of "turning away" and "indecency". On "he was of Our chosen servants" the readers differed: most of the readers of Medina and Kufa read "mukhlasin" with fatha on the lam, meaning Joseph is one of Our servants whom We made exclusively Our own and chose for Our prophethood and message; some readers of Basra read it with kasra, "mukhlisin", meaning those who made their belief in Our oneness and worship of Us exclusive and did not associate anything with Us. Al-Tabari said: the right view is that these are two well-known readings used by a great many readers, agreeing in meaning, for one whom God made exclusively His own, choosing him, is sincere to God in belief in His oneness and worship, and one who is sincere in belief in God's oneness and worship is among those whom God made His own, so whichever the reader reads he is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 50}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
