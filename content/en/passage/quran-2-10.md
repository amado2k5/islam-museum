## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari says the root of "marad" is illness, said of bodies and of religion alike, and that what is meant by the sickness of the hypocrites' hearts is the sickness of the belief held in them; the mention of the heart stands for the belief, as one says "the city glorified God" meaning its people, and he cites lines by Amr ibn Kulthum and Antara. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 278} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 279}
He takes the sickness to be their doubt about Muhammad and what he brought and their bewilderment over it: they neither hold firm belief nor deny as polytheists do, but waver between the two. He gives Ibn Abbas that it is doubt, and from al-Dahhak through him that it is hypocrisy; al-Suddi, Qatada and al-Rabi' ibn Anas that it is doubt and uncertainty; and Ibn Zayd that it is a sickness in religion and not in bodies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 279} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 280} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 281}
On "so God increased them in sickness" he holds that God laid down new rules and obligations about which they doubted and became suspicious, adding doubt and bewilderment to their earlier doubt, just as the new obligations increased the believers in faith; he cites a verse of al-Tawba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 281}

Al-Tabari says the increase added to the hypocrites' foulness, and to the believers' faith, is the interpretation agreed on, and cites Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi, Qatada and al-Rabi' ibn Anas that the sickness increased in doubt and uncertainty, and Ibn Zayd that it increased them in foulness, evil added to their evil and error to their error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 281} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 282}
On "and theirs is a painful punishment" he explains "alim" as painful, in the sense of "mu'lim", citing lines of Amr ibn Ma'dikarib and Dhu al-Rumma, with al-Rabi' and al-Dahhak that it means causing pain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 283} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 284}
He records the difference over reading "bima kanu yakdhibun": with "yakdhibun" (lie), the reading of most Kufan readers, or "yukadhdhibun" (deny), that of most readers of Medina, the Hijaz and Basra. Al-Tabari chooses "lie", because God opened the account of them by saying they lie in claiming belief, so the threat fits what the account opened with; he argues from Surat al-Munafiqun, where God testifies that they are liars, and from al-Mujadila. He also reports a discussion between Basran and Kufan grammarians over "ma" and "kana" in the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 284} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 285} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 286}
