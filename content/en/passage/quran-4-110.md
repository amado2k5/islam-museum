## Summary
Verse 110 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 110 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 110 and the question of whom it means (pp. 194-195, notes 1-3 on p. 194).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: whoever commits a sin, the word for evil, or wrongs himself by earning what makes him deserve God's punishment, then asks God's forgiveness, that is, turns to God by returning from the evil and the wrong to himself and going back to the works God loves, which wipe out his sin and remove his fault, will find God forgiving, meaning he will find his Lord covering his sin by pardoning him its punishment, and merciful to him. The interpreters differed over whom the verse means. Some said it means those whom God described as treacherous in the verse on not pleading for those who betray themselves; others said it means those who argued for the treacherous, to whom God said that they had argued for them in this life; and he says he has already given the sayers of both views. Al-Tabari's choice, his own, is that it means everyone who did evil or wronged himself, even though it came down about the treacherous and those who argued for them, whose affair God mentioned in the verses before. The editor refers to the language indexes for the word for evil, for seeking forgiveness, forgiving and merciful, and says the printed text has a different form for those who argue for the treacherous, and he kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 195} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 195}

**Reports 10422-10424: the allowance for repentance (pp. 195-196, notes 1-2 on p. 195).** Al-Tabari says a number of interpreters spoke as he did, and gives three reports. Report 10422 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Ibn Abi Adi, from Shu'ba, from Asim, from Abu Wa'il, from Abd Allah, that is Ibn Mas'ud) says that when one of the Children of Israel committed a sin, he would find in the morning the atonement for it written on his door, and if urine touched part of him he would cut it away with scissors. A man said that God had given the Children of Israel good; Abd Allah answered that what God had given the believers was better than what He gave them, since He made water a means of purification for them, and he recalled the verse of 3:135 about those who remember God and ask forgiveness for their sins after a shameful act or wronging themselves, and this verse. Report 10423 (Ya'qub, from Hushaym, from Ibn Awn, from Habib ibn Abi Thabit) tells that a woman came to Abd Allah ibn Mughaffal and asked about a woman who had committed fornication, conceived, and killed her child when she gave birth; Ibn Mughaffal said that she had the Fire, and the woman left weeping; he called her back and said he saw her affair as only one of two, and recited this verse, whereupon she wiped her eye and went on. Report 10424 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) says that God informed His servants of His forbearance, pardon, generosity and the breadth of His mercy and forgiveness: whoever sins, small or great, and then asks God's forgiveness will find God forgiving and merciful, even if his sins were greater than the heavens, the earth and the mountains. The editor's notes: the verb used for cutting off means to clip, and the instrument is scissors; that Habib ibn Abi Thabit al-Asadi came earlier in reports 9035 and 9012; that Abd Allah ibn Mughaffal al-Muzani is among the well-known Companions, one of the weepers at the expedition of Tabuk and one of the ten Umar sent to teach the people of Basra; and that this report is among the finest reports showing the intelligence of the jurist, his insight into his religion and his counsel to people in the affairs of their world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 196}
