## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We appointed with Moses" al-Tabari notes that the reciters differ between "wa'adna", meaning God and Moses made a mutual appointment to meet on the mountain, and "wa'adna" in the form meaning that God alone promised. The first group argue that every appointment between two parties is mutual; the second that mutual appointment belongs to humans, while God alone makes promises and threats. His own choice is that both are sound readings with the same meaning: God promised Moses the mountain and Moses, willingly, promised the meeting, so whichever one reads is right, and God's sole authority over promise and threat does not change the meaning of ordinary speech. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 58} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 59} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 60}
On "Moses" he gives what reached him, that in Coptic it is two words meaning water and tree, "mu" water and "sha" tree, because when his mother cast him into the river in the chest the waves carried him among trees by Pharaoh's house, where Asiya's maidservants found him, so he was named for the place; he reports this from al-Suddi. From Ibn Ishaq he gives the lineage: Moses son of Imran son of Yashar son of Qahith son of Levi son of Jacob son of Isaac son of Abraham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 60} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 61}
On "forty nights" he holds that all forty nights are within the appointment, and rejects the view of some Basran grammarians that it means the end of forty nights, as contrary to what the commentators report and to the apparent wording; the apparent sense is not turned to a hidden one without proof. He relates from Abu al-Aliya that it was Dhu al-Qa'da and ten days of Dhu al-Hijja, that Moses left Aaron in charge and stayed on the mountain, and that the Torah was given him on tablets; from Ibn Ishaq and al-Suddi that the appointment was thirty nights which God completed with ten, with Aaron staying among the Israelites together with al-Samiri. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 61} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 62} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 63}
On "then you took the calf after him while you were wrongdoers" he says the sense is that they took the calf as a god during the days of the appointment after Moses left them, "him" referring to Moses. God, he says, is telling the Jews addressed, who denied Muhammad, what their forebears did and how they denied their messengers despite continual favours, so that they know they follow their forebears' path, and warning them of what befell the earlier ones, such as transformation and cursing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 63}

On the cause of the calf, al-Tabari gives a report from Ibn Abbas through Sufyan ibn Uyayna: al-Samiri recognised Gabriel on the mare at the sea, because Gabriel had fed him as an infant in the cave where his mother hid him for fear of the killing, and he took a handful from under the horse's hoof; he adds that Ibn Mas'ud read the verse as "from the trace of the messenger's horse". It was put into al-Samiri's mind that whatever he cast it on, saying "be such", would be so; so when Moses had gone, he threw it into the jewellery the Israelites had borrowed from Pharaoh's people, and it became a calf, a body that lowed, with wind entering its back and leaving by its mouth, and he said this was their god and Moses's. Aaron stayed among them forbidding it, but they said they would not leave it until Moses returned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 63} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 64}
From al-Suddi: Moses told them to borrow jewellery from the Copts; Aaron told them booty was not lawful for them and to gather it in a pit; al-Samiri threw in the handful and a lowing calf came out; the Israelites miscounted Moses's appointment by taking each night for a day. The report also gives a conversation of Moses with his Lord about al-Samiri. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 64} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 65}
From Ibn Ishaq: Pharaoh used to stir people against the Israelites by saying they had gone off with their wealth. From Ibn Abbas by another chain: al-Samiri came from Bajarma, from a people who worshipped cattle, and had professed Islam among the Israelites; Aaron told them to cast the jewellery into a fire to purify themselves of it; al-Samiri took earth from Gabriel's horse's hoofprint, cast it in and said "be a calf"; his name is given as Musa ibn Zafar, and Aaron stayed with those not led astray, fearing that Moses would say he had divided the Israelites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 65} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 66} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 67}
Ibn Zayd gives a similar report, in which Aaron has the clothes and jewellery burnt as unlawful for them, and al-Samiri notices the mark of Gabriel's mount and takes a handful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 67}
