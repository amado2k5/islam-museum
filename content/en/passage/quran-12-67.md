**Commentary on verse 67 (pp. 164-166)**

On "and he said, O my sons, do not enter by one gate but enter by different gates; I cannot avail you anything against God; judgment belongs to God alone; in Him I have put my trust, and in Him let the trusting put their trust", al-Tabari says: Ya'qub said to his sons, when they wished to leave him for Egypt to buy food: O my sons, do not enter Egypt by one road, but enter by different gates. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 164}

It is reported that he said this to them because they were men of beauty and fine appearance, and he feared the evil eye for them if they entered together by one road, being the sons of one man, so he told them to separate in entering. Report 19487, from al-Dahhak, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Yazid al-Wasiti and Juwaybir: he feared the eye for them. Report 19488, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: God's prophet feared the eye for his sons, who were of fine form and beauty. Report 19489, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they had been given form and beauty, so he feared people's souls for them. Report 19490, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: Ya'qub was anxious for them about the eye. Report 19491, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: Ya'qub feared the eye for his sons. Report 19492, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, through Ibn Waki', Zayd ibn al-Hubab and Abu Ma'shar: he feared the eye for them. The editor notes that the printed text has a different word for "appearance" from the unpointed manuscript, and that he corrects it here without mentioning it again. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 165}

Report 19493, from al-Suddi, through Amr and Asbat: Ya'qub feared the eye for his sons and said, O my sons, do not enter by one gate, lest it be said, these belong to one man, but enter by different gates. Report 19494, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when they, the sons of Ya'qub, were resolved to leave, Ya'qub said, O my sons, do not enter by one gate; he feared people's eyes for them because of their appearance and because they were of one man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 166}

On "and I cannot avail you anything against God", he says: I cannot repel from you anything of God's decree that He has decreed for you, small or great, because His decree is effective among His creation; "judgment belongs only to God" means decree and judgment belong to God alone and not to anything else, for He judges among His creatures as He wills, His judgment takes effect among them and His decree is not turned back; "in Him I have put my trust" means I have trusted in God regarding you and His keeping you for me until He returns you to me safe and sound, not in your entering Egypt by different gates; "and in Him let the trusting put their trust" means let those who entrust their affairs entrust them to God. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "aghna" and "tawakkul". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 166}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
