## Summary
Verse 75 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 75 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "so we showed Abraham the dominion of the heavens and the earth, that he be of those who are certain" al-Tabari says: by "so" he means: as we showed him insight in his religion and the truth against the error they were in, we show him the dominion of the heavens and the earth, meaning their kingship, the ta being added to it as in "jabarut" from "jabr", and as it is said "rahabut is better than rahamut", meaning fear is better than mercy; it is reported from the Arabs by hearsay "his is the malakut of Yemen and Iraq", meaning his is the kingship of that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 470}

The editor says the manuscript has blanks in places and the wording is joined and sound in sense as printed, and refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an.

The interpreters differed on "we show Abraham the dominion of the heavens and earth". Some said the sense is we show him the creation of the heavens and the earth: 13441, from Ibn Abbas: the creation of the heavens and the earth; 13442, from Qatada, the like; 13443, from Ibn Abbas, the like. Others said "malakut" means kingship: 13444 and 13445, from Ikrima: it is "malik", but in the speech of the Nabateans "malakuta". Others said the sense is the signs of the heavens and the earth: 13446 and 13447, from Mujahid: signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 471}

The editor identifies, in 13444 and 13445, Umar ibn Abi Za'ida al-Hamdani al-Wadi'i, brother of Zakariyya ibn Abi Za'ida, who is far more eminent and trusted, though he held the doctrine of qadar and is upright in hadith.

13448, from Mujahid: the seven heavens were opened to Abraham up to the throne and he looked into them, and the seven earths were opened and he looked into them. 13449, from al-Suddi: he was set on a rock and the heavens were opened to him and he looked at God's kingdom in them until he saw his place in Paradise, and the earths were opened to him until he saw the bottom of the earth, which is "we gave him his reward in this world", [[passage/quran-29-27|29:27]], meaning his place in Paradise, and it is also said that his reward was fair praise. 13450, from Mujahid: the heavens were opened to him and he looked at what is in them until his sight reached the throne, and the seven earths were opened and he looked at what is in them. 13451, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the surface of the heavens and the earth was uncovered for him until he looked at them on a rock, the rock on a fish, the fish on the seal of the Lord of might. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 472}

The editor notes on Sa'id's report that al-Tabari has already said what he holds of this kind of report in which there is no proof from the one who tells the truth.

13452, from Salman: when Abraham saw the dominion of the heavens and the earth he saw a servant committing an indecency and prayed against him and he died; then saw another committing an indecency, prayed against him and he died; then another, prayed against him and he died; and God said: bring my servant down lest he destroy my servants. 13453, from Ata: when God raised Abraham in the dominion in the heavens he looked down and saw a servant fornicating, prayed against him and he died; he was raised again, looked down, saw a servant fornicating, prayed against him and he died; he was raised again, looked down, saw a servant fornicating and prayed against him, and was called: gently, Abraham, you are a servant whose prayer is answered, and I deal with my servant in one of three ways: he repents to me and I turn to him, or I bring out of him good offspring, or he persists in what he is in and I am behind him. 13454, from Usama: Abraham, the friend of the Merciful, told himself that he was the most merciful of creatures, and God raised him until he looked over the people of the earth and saw their deeds; when he saw them doing wrong he said: God, destroy them; and his Lord said to him: I am more merciful to my servants than you; go down, perhaps they will repent to me and return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 473}

The editor says the printed text has a different verb at the end and the manuscript's "return" is the good reading.

Others said the sense is what God reported that he showed him of the stars, moon and sun. 13455, from al-Dahhak: the sun, moon and stars; 13456, from Mujahid: the sun and moon; 13457, from Ibn Abbas: the sun, moon and stars. 13458, from Qatada: Abraham was hidden from one of the tyrants and his provision was put in his fingers, so when he sucked a finger he found provision in it; when he came out God showed him the dominion of the heavens and earth, the dominion of the heavens being the sun, moon and stars and that of the earth the mountains, trees and seas. 13459, from Qatada: he was fled with from a pampered tyrant and put in a burrow with his provision in his fingertips, so he never sucked a finger without finding provision in it; when he came out of that burrow God showed him the dominion of the heavens, showing him sun, moon, stars, clouds and a great creation, and the dominion of the earth, showing him mountains, seas, rivers, trees, every kind of beast and a great creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 474}

The editor says that in 13457 "we show him the sun" in the printed text is an addition not in the manuscript, and that a "sarab" is a hole dug in the ground like a cellar.

Abu Jafar said: the best of the views on this for being right is that of those who said God meant by "so we show Abraham the dominion of the heavens and the earth" that he showed him the kingship of the heavens and earth, which is what he created in them of sun, moon, stars, trees, beasts and the rest of his great authority in them, and made clear to him the inner and outer of things, for the sense of "malakut" in Arab speech that we mentioned. As for "that he be of those who are certain" it means he showed him the dominion of the heavens and the earth so that he be of those who affirm God's oneness and know the truth of what he guided him to and made him see of knowing his unity, and of the error his people were in, worshipping idols and taking them as gods apart from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 475}

The editor says the printed text and manuscript have a wording that has no sense, the right form being the one he gave, an error of the copyist's haste.

Ibn Abbas used to say of this (13460) that everything was made plain to him, its secret and open, so that nothing of the deeds of creatures was hidden from him, and when he began to curse the people of sins God said: you cannot bear this, and God returned him as he was before. So the sense on this reading is: we showed him the dominion of the heavens and the earth that he be among those who are certain of the knowledge of everything by sense, not by report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 475}

13461, from Abd al-Rahman ibn A'ish al-Hadrami: God's messenger led us in prayer one morning, and someone said to him: I have not seen your face brighter than this morning. He said: why not, when my Lord appeared to me in the best form and said: what are the exalted assembly disputing about, Muhammad? I said: you know best; and he put his hand between my shoulders and I felt its coolness between my breasts, and knew what is in the heavens and the earth. Then he recited this verse, "so we showed Abraham the dominion of the heavens and the earth, that he be of those who are certain". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 476}

The editor says this is a very difficult report, which in manuscript and print was badly corrupted, its chain worse, and that he corrected it from Abu Jafar's own version in his Dhayl al-Mudhayyal; he identifies al-Abbas ibn al-Walid ibn Mazyad al-Bayruti, Abu Jafar's teacher, trusted, and his father al-Walid ibn Mazyad, trusted, whose books al-Awza'i called sound, and Ibn Jabir, Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid ibn Jabir al-Azdi, trusted; and corrects "I have not seen you happier than today" to "brighter of face", meaning shining more, taking the wording about the Lord's appearing from the Dhayl, the bracketed words from it, and what had dropped out of "he put his hand between my shoulders" from the same version.

We stop here at the foot of p. 476, at the report of Abd al-Rahman ibn A'ish and the editor's notes on it, and al-Tabari continues on p. 477.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
