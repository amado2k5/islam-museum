## Summary
Verse 103 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 103 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Remembering God after the prayer in time of fighting, and report 10380 (p. 164, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari begins the commentary on verse 103 on p. 164. He says the sense is: when you, believers, finish your prayer, facing your enemy in the way he has explained, remember God in all your states, standing, sitting and lying on your sides, by glorifying Him and praying for victory over your enemy so that God may help you, and he sets this beside [[passage/quran-8-45|8:45]], which tells the believers to stand firm when they meet a troop and to remember God often. Report 10380 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) on the command to remember God often: God imposed no obligation on His servants without giving it a known limit and then excusing its people in a case of excuse, except remembrance, for which He set no limit and for whose neglect He excused no one but a person whose mind is overcome; so He commanded remembrance by night and day, on land and sea, on journey and at home, in wealth and poverty, in illness and health, in secret and in the open, in every state. The editor notes that the printed text and manuscript have "known reward" where the context requires "known limit", that the printed text changed the clause quoted and he returned to the manuscript, and that the phrase "which I have explained to you" describes "your prayer". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 164}

**The sense of being secure and of establishing the prayer, reports 10381-10386 (pp. 165-166, notes 1-3 on p. 165).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the command to establish the prayer once secure. Some said the sense is: when you are settled in your homelands and living in your cities, complete the prayer you were allowed to shorten in your fear on your journey. Report 10381 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from a man, from Mujahid): it is leaving the house of travel for the house of residence. Report 10382 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada): when secure in your cities, complete the prayer. Others said the sense is: when you are settled, complete its limits with its bowing and prostration. Report 10383 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): when you are secure after the fear. Report 10384 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): when you are secure, pray the prayer, and do not pray it riding, walking or sitting. Report 10385 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid): complete it; and report 10386 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. The notes refer to his earlier explanations of the word for being secure and of establishing the prayer, and say the words "meaning: complete" are not in the manuscript. Al-Tabari then gives his choice (p. 166) of the more fitting of the two interpretations: that the sense is, when your fear of your enemy has gone, you are secure and your souls are at ease in safety, complete the limits of the prayer that are required of you, shortening none of them. His argument is that God taught His servants through these two verses what is obligatory for them in two states: a state of severe fear, in which He allowed the shortening of the prayer's limits from completeness, and a state of less severe fear, in which He commanded establishing and completing its limits, as He described their taking turns in prayer behind their imam and guarding one another, a state with no shortening since He told His Prophet in it to establish the prayer for them ([[passage/quran-4-102|4:102]]); so the command to establish once secure means: once you are secure from the state in which you were not establishing your prayer, establish it, and that is the state of severe fear. This is al-Tabari's own choice and argument. The editor notes that the printed text changed the manuscript to "complete it with its limits", which he faults. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 166}

**The sense of the prayer being described as timed, and reports 10387-10399 (pp. 167-169, notes 1-4 on p. 167, note 1 on p. 168 and notes 1-2 on p. 169).** On p. 167 begins the commentary on the second clause of verse 103, the clause describing the prayer for the believers as timed, and al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over it. Some said it means an appointed obligation. Report 10387 (Abu al-Sa'ib, from Ibn Fudayl, from Fudayl ibn Marzuq, from Atiyya al-Awfi): appointed; report 10388 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): appointed, "the timed" being "the appointed"; report 10389 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): appointed; report 10390 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Nu'aym, from Sufyan, from Layth, from Mujahid): appointed. Others said it means an obligatory duty. Report 10391 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Abu Raja', from al-Hasan): obligatory; 10392 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid): obligatory; 10393 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) the same; 10394 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Ma'mar ibn Sam, from Abu Ja'far): made incumbent; 10395 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father and grandfather, from Ibn Abbas): the timed is the obligatory; and 10396 (Ahmad ibn Hazim, from Abu Nu'aym, from Ma'mar ibn Yahya, from Abu Ja'far): its obligation. Others said it means paid in instalments, performed in its appointed times. Report 10397 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada): Ibn Mas'ud said the prayer has a time like the time of the pilgrimage; report 10398 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from Zayd ibn Aslam): in instalments, each time one instalment passes another comes, that is, each time one time passes another time comes; report 10399 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from Zayd ibn Aslam) is the same (and is completed on p. 170). The editor's notes: references to his earlier explanations of the word for "writ"; the printed text and manuscript differ in the wording attributed to Atiyya and he kept the manuscript; the chain of 10388 in the printed text is different and wrongly omits "Mu'awiya narrated to me", so he gives the manuscript chain, one that recurs often in this commentary, the nearest being 10380; report 10390 stands before the ones that follow it in the manuscript; Ma'mar ibn Sam is said to be named for his grandfather, as Ma'mar ibn Sam ibn Musa or Ma'mar ibn Yahya ibn Sam, narrated from Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn, from his brother Aban ibn Yahya and from Fatima bint Ali, and was narrated from by Waki', Abu Usama and Abu Nu'aym; Abu Zur'a called him a reliable Kufan, and he is in the Tahdhib, al-Bukhari's History and al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil; the printed text wrongly has "Ma'mar ibn Hisham" and the manuscript "ibn Sham"; Abu Ja'far is al-Baqir, Muhammad ibn Ali ibn al-Husayn ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib, a reliable narrator of much hadith whom al-Nasa'i lists among the jurists of Medina of the followers, and al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar said he was called "the splitter-open of knowledge"; the manuscript wording of 10394 is strange and not allowed by derivation, apparently a scribe's slip, the printed form being right, God willing; Ma'mar ibn Yahya in 10396 is Ma'mar ibn Sam; and "instalment" is the appointed time, as in "I set my money on so-and-so in monthly instalments", the portion paid when its time arrives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 169}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the meaning of "timed" (p. 170, note 1).** Al-Tabari says these views are close in meaning, since what is appointed is obligatory, and what must be performed at one time after another is in instalments. The most fitting sense of the word in his view is that of those who said the prayer was for the believers an obligation paid in instalments, because "timed" is a passive form from the saying "God has timed your obligation for you", the obligation being timed when it has a time at which it must be performed; so the sense is that the prayer was for believers an obligation for which a time of obligatory performance was set and made clear to them. This is al-Tabari's own choice and argument. The editor notes that the printed wording of one clause has no sense, that the manuscript is undotted, and that he read it as given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 170}
