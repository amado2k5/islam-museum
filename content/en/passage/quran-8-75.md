**Commentary on verse 75 (pp. 89-90)**

Al-Tabari says: those who believed in God and His messenger after my explanation of the alliance of the Emigrants and Helpers with one another and the cutting off of alliance with those who believed but did not emigrate until they do, and who emigrated from the land of unbelief to the land of Islam and strove with you, believers, are of you in alliance: what is owed to them of right, of aid in religion and of inheritance, is like what is owed by them to you and among yourselves. Report 16352, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: then He returned the inheritances to blood relatives among those who became Muslim after the alliance of the Emigrants and Helpers, saying in the verse that those bound by kinship are nearer to one another in God's book, that is in inheritance, and that God knows everything. The editor explains that the printed text and manuscript dropped words because the term for kinship recurs, and that he restored them from Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 89}

He continues: relatives by blood are nearer to one another in inheritance, when they are among those for whom God allotted a share, than the confederate and the ally; "in God's book" means in God's ruling written on the Preserved Tablet and in the earlier decree; God knows what is good for His servants in having them inherit from one another by kinship and descent rather than by pact or anything else, and nothing is hidden from Him. The interpreters said the same. Report 16353, from Qatada, through Ahmad ibn al-Miqdam, al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman and his father: the bedouin did not inherit from the emigrant until God sent down the verse on those bound by kinship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 90}

Report 16354, from Isa ibn al-Harith, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh and Ibn Awn: his brother Shurayh ibn al-Harith had a concubine who bore him a girl; the girl grew up, was married and bore a boy, and then the concubine died; Shurayh ibn al-Harith and the boy took their dispute over her inheritance to the judge Shurayh, and Shurayh ibn al-Harith kept saying the boy had no inheritance in God's book; the judge awarded the inheritance to the boy and recited the verse on kinship; Maysara ibn Yazid rode to Ibn al-Zubayr and told him of the judge's ruling and words, and Ibn al-Zubayr wrote; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 90}

We stop here at the foot of p. 90, in the middle of the commentary on verse 75 (within report 16354); the next batch begins at p. 91. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 90}
