**Commentary on verse 12 (pp. 386-397)**

On "it is He who shows you the lightning, in fear and hope, and brings up the heavy clouds; the thunder glorifies His praise, and the angels too for fear of Him; He sends the thunderbolts and strikes with them whom He wills, while they dispute about God, and He is severe in power", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: "it is He who shows you the lightning", meaning that the Lord is the One who shows His servants the lightning, the "huwa" standing for His name, mighty be His praise. We have explained the meaning of "lightning" earlier and mentioned the interpreters' differences over it, which spares repeating it in this place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 386}

"Fear" means fear for the traveller of its harm, for "lightning" in this place is water. Report 20251, from Musa ibn Salim Abu Jahdam, freedman of Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Hajjaj and Hammad: Ibn Abbas wrote to Abu al-Jald asking him about "lightning", and he said, "lightning" is water; the editor notes that his report from Ibn Abbas is mursal, and that Abu al-Jald is Jilan ibn Farwa al-Asadi, a reliable narrator. "And hope" means hope for the resident that it will rain and he will benefit. Report 20252, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "fear" for the traveller on his journeys who fears its harm and hardship, "and hope" for the resident who hopes for its blessing and benefit and for God's provision. Report 20253, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: fear for the traveller and hope for the resident. "And brings up the heavy clouds" means He raises the heavy clouds with rain and begins them; one says "God originated the clouds" when He began them and "the clouds began" when they started. "Clouds" in this place, though in the singular form, is a plural, its singular being "sahaba", which is why He said "heavy" in the plural form; had it been "the heavy cloud" in the singular it would also have been allowed, treating the word "sahab" as singular, as in "who made for you fire from the green tree" [[passage/quran-36-80|36:80]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 387}

The interpreters said the same. Reports 20254 to 20258, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq and Abd Allah; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "brings up the heavy clouds", he said, those with water in them. On "the thunder glorifies His praise", al-Tabari says he has already explained the meaning of "thunder", which spares repeating it here. It is mentioned that the Messenger of God, when he heard the sound of thunder, used to say what is in report 20259, from Ja'far, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Kathir ibn Hisham: it reached us that the Prophet, when he heard loud thunder {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 388}

he said: O God, do not kill us by Your wrath, nor destroy us with Your punishment, and give us well-being before that. The editor notes that Ahmad transmitted it in this wording in the Musnad from the report of Ibn Umar, and al-Bukhari in al-Adab al-Mufrad. Report 20260, from Abu Hurayra, with the chain raised, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Isra'il: when he heard thunder he used to say, glory to Him whom the thunder glorifies with His praise. Report 20261, from Ali, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Mas'ada ibn al-Yasa' al-Bahili and Ja'far ibn Muhammad from his father: when he heard the sound of thunder he used to say, glory to Him for whom you glorify; the editor notes that this Mas'ada is very weak, Ibn Abi Hatim saying his hadith is lost and rejected and Ahmad saying he is nothing. Report 20262, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Ulayya, al-Hakam ibn Aban and Ikrima: when he heard thunder he said, glory to Him for whom you glorify. Report 20263, from al-Aswad ibn Yazid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Ya'la ibn al-Harith and Abu Sakhra: when he heard thunder he said, glory to Him for whom you glorify, or, glory to Him whom the thunder glorifies with His praise and the angels for fear of Him; the editor thinks Abu Sakhra heard this from al-Aswad's brother Abd al-Rahman, not from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 389}

Report 20264, from Tawus, through Abu Ahmad, Ibn Ulayya, Ibn Tawus and Abd al-Karim: when he heard thunder he said, glory to Him for whom you glorify. Report 20265, from al-Awza'i, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Maysara: Ibn Abi Zakariya used to say, whoever says on hearing thunder "glory to God and praise" will not be struck by a thunderbolt; the editor identifies Ibn Abi Zakariya as Abd Allah ibn Abi Zakariya al-Khuza'i al-Shami, one of the jurists of Damascus and a contemporary of Makhul, a reliable Follower with few reports. Al-Tabari says: the meaning of "the thunder glorifies His praise" is that the thunder magnifies God and extols Him, praising Him with His attributes and declaring Him above what the polytheists ascribe to Him, such as taking a consort and a son, exalted and holy is our Lord. "For fear of Him" means the angels glorify from fear of God and dread of Him. As for "He sends the thunderbolts and strikes with them whom He wills", we have explained the meaning of "thunderbolt" earlier, which spares repeating it, with sufficient evidence, and mentioned the reports on it. They differed over whom this verse was revealed about. Some said it was revealed about a disbeliever who mentioned God, exalted and holy, in a way not fitting, so He sent a thunderbolt on him and destroyed him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 390}

Report 20266, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Sahhar al-Abdi, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan, Aban ibn Yazid and Abu Imran al-Jawni: it reached him that the Prophet of God sent to a tyrant to call him, and he said, have you seen your Lord, is He gold, is He silver, is He pearl? While he was arguing with them God sent a cloud, which thundered, and God sent a thunderbolt on him that took off the top of his skull, and God revealed this verse; the editor judges this report mursal with a sound chain. Report 20267, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq ibn Sulayman, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash and Layth: a Jew came to the Prophet and said, tell me of your Lord, of what is He, of pearl or of ruby? A thunderbolt came and took him, and God revealed the verse. Report 20268, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, al-Himmani, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash and Layth: the same. Report 20269, from Ali, through Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Hashim, Sayf, Abu Ruq and Abu Ayyub: a man came to the Prophet and said, Muhammad, tell me about the one you call to, is He ruby, is He gold, or what is He? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 391}

Then a thunderbolt fell on the questioner and burned him, and God revealed "He sends the thunderbolts", the verse. The editor notes that Sayf ibn Umar al-Dabbi, the chronicler, is weak and his hadith discarded, and that this chain is rejected. Report 20270, from Anas ibn Malik, through Muhammad ibn Marzuq, Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Wahhab, Ali ibn Abi Sara al-Shaybani and Thabit al-Bunani: the Prophet once sent a man to one of the tyrants of the Arabs to call him to him, and he said, Messenger of God, he is too defiant for that; he said, go to him and call him. He came and said, the Messenger of God calls you, and he said, who is the Messenger of God and what is God, is He of gold, or silver, or copper? The man came to the Prophet and told him, and he said, go back and call him. He came again and repeated it and was given the same answer as the first time, and a third time the same; and while they were going back and forth in speech God sent a cloud above his head which thundered, and a thunderbolt fell from it and took off the top of his skull, and God revealed the verse. The editor notes that Ali ibn Abi Sara is a weak narrator whom people abandoned, that the chain is very weak, and quotes al-Haythami in Majma' al-Zawa'id that Abu Ya'la and al-Bazzar transmitted it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 392}

Others said it was revealed about a disbeliever who denied the Qur'an and called the Prophet a liar. Report 20271, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that a man denied the Qur'an and called the Prophet a liar, so God sent a thunderbolt on him and destroyed him, and revealed about him "while they dispute about God, and He is severe in power". Others said it was revealed about Arbad, brother of Lubayd ibn Rabi'a, who had intended with Amir ibn al-Tufayl to kill the Messenger of God. Report 20272, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: it was revealed about Arbad, because Arbad and Amir ibn al-Tufayl came to the Prophet, and Amir said, Muhammad, shall I accept Islam and be the successor after you? He said, no. He said, then shall I be over the tent-dwellers and you over the house-dwellers? He said, no. He said, then what? He said, I will give you the reins of the horses to fight with, for you are a horseman. He said, are the reins of the horses not in my hand? By God, I will fill it against you with horses and men of Banu Amir! He said to Arbad, either you spare me him and I strike him with the sword, or I spare you him and you strike him with the sword. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 393}

Arbad said, you spare me him and I strike. Ibn al-Tufayl said, Muhammad, I have a need of you; he said, come near, and he kept coming near and the Prophet kept saying, come near, until he put his hands on his knees and leaned over him, and Arbad drew the sword a little; when the Prophet saw its gleam he took refuge with a verse he used to take refuge with, and Arbad's hand stiffened on the sword, and God sent a thunderbolt on him that burned him; that is the saying of his brother, who says that he feared for Arbad every kind of death and did not dread the rain-stars, and that lightning and thunderbolts bereaved him of the horseman on the day of fierce fighting. Al-Tabari says he has already given the report of Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd with a similar story. On "while they dispute about God" he says: these whom God struck with thunderbolts He struck while they were in dispute about God, mighty and exalted, with His Messenger. On "and He is severe in power" he says: God is severe in His contending in punishing whoever rebelled against Him, defied Him and persisted in disbelief. "Mihal" is a verbal noun from "mahaltu fulanan, fa-ana umahiluhu mimahalatan wa mihalan", and its verb is "mahaltu amhalu mahlan", when one exposes another to what destroys him; from it comes "a pleader whose plea is confirmed", from the report of Jabir that the Qur'an is an intercessor whose intercession is accepted and a pleader whose plea is confirmed; the editor traces it to Ibn Hibban's Sahih. From it too comes the saying of A'sha of Banu Tha'laba, in a verse praising a branch of the nab' tree swaying in the boughs of glory, abundant in giving, severe in mihal, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 394}

as Mu'ammar ibn al-Muthanna used to recite it, according to what I was told from Ali ibn al-Mughira from him; the later transmitters recite it in another form with "great in mihal". Mu'ammar ibn al-Muthanna explained it and claimed that it meant punishment, plotting and deterrent punishment; from it too is the saying of another, in a verse describing the confusion of a matter among peoples, each of whom has prepared for it his tricks and stratagems. The editor notes in the footnotes that the first two verses are by al-A'sha, that the "nab'" is a tree growing on the mountain top from which bows are made, that "the branch" means the chief who rose above his people in nobility, that the other verse is by Dhu al-Rumma from his poem praising Bilal ibn Abi Burda ibn Abi Musa al-Ash'ari, and that "labs" is the confusion of a matter and "shaghazib" is guile and stratagem. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 395}

The interpreters said the same. Report 20273, from Ali, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Hashim, Sayf, Abu Ruq and Abu Ayyub: "and He is severe in power", he said, severe in seizing. Report 20274, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il and Abu Yahya: severe in strength. Report 20275, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: that is, strength and stratagem. Report 20276, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "severe in mihal" means destruction, he said, when it is barren it is severe; and Qatada said severe in stratagem. Report 20277, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and a man: "while they dispute about God", he said, the dispute of Arbad, "and He is severe in power", he said, what struck Arbad of the thunderbolt. Report 20278, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: severe in might. Report 20279, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: severe in strength, "mihal" being strength. Al-Tabari says the statement we cited from Qatada, that "mihal" is stratagem, and the statement Ibn Jurayj gave from Ibn Abbas, indicate that they read "shadid al-mahal" with a fatha on the mim, because the verbal noun of "hila" does not come as "mihal" with a kasra, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 396}

though it can come in the pattern "maf'ala" from it, giving "mahala", as in their saying "a man is helpless, no doubt", where "mahala" is the "maf'ala" from "hila"; with a kasra on the mim it can only be a verbal noun from "mahaltu fulanan umahiluhu mihalan", and "mumahala" is far in meaning from "hila". Al-Tabari says: I know of no one who read it with a fatha on the mim; that being so, the better interpretation is what we said. In a note the editor says that al-A'raj and al-Dahhak read it with a fatha on the mim, and refers to the commentary of Abu Hayyan and Ibn Khalawayh's Shawadhdh al-Qira'at. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 397}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
