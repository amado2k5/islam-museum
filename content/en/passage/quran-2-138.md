## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "the dye of God, and who is better than God in dyeing, and to Him we are worshippers" al-Tabari says: the "sibgha" is the dye of Islam, since when the Christians wish to make their children Christians they put them in water of theirs which they claim is a sanctification for them, like the washing after ritual impurity for the people of Islam, and is a dye for them in Christianity; so God said to His Prophet, say to them, Jews and Christians, rather follow the religion of Abraham, the dye of God which is the best of dyes, the upright, submitting faith, and leave associating partners with God and straying from the highway of His guidance. He gives the possible parsings: "sibgha" in the accusative as an echo of "the religion", in the nominative as a new clause meaning "it is God's dye", or accusative on "we believe with this belief". A group of interpreters said the like of what he said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 117}

From Qatada on "the dye of God": the Jews dye their children Jews and the Christians dye their children Christians, and God's dye is Islam, and there is no dye better or purer than Islam, which is God's religion with which He sent Noah and the prophets after him; from 'Ata': the Jews dyed their children, and so went against the natural disposition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 117} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 118}

The interpreters differ on "the dye of God". Some said it is God's religion, from Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya, al-Rabi', Mujahid, 'Atiyya, al-Suddi, Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd; others said it is God's natural disposition on which He created people, from Mujahid and 'Abd Allah ibn Kathir, so that the meaning is: rather we follow God's natural disposition and creed on which He created His creatures, which is the upright religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 118} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 119} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 120}

On "and to Him we are worshippers" al-Tabari says it is a command from God to His Prophet to say to the Jews and Christians, meaning the creed of those humble before God and submissive to Him in our following the religion of Abraham, not being arrogant in following His command and acknowledging His messengers' mission as the Jews and Christians were arrogant and disbelieved in Muhammad out of pride, injustice and envy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 120}
