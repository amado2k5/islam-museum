## Summary
Verse 1 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 1 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 1 and 2 (pp. 95-104)**

Al-Tabari says the sense of the opening phrase is "this is a disavowal from God and His messenger"; the word for disavowal is in the nominative by an omitted subject, "this", as at the start of Surat al-Nur. He allows that the word could instead be raised by the pronoun referring back to it in "those you made a covenant with", being treated as definite through its qualification "from God and His messenger", giving the sense "the disavowal from God and His messenger to those polytheists you made a covenant with"; he does not reject that course, but prefers the first, since it is the Arabs' way to supply "this" for anything seen, indefinite or definite, saying at the sight of something fine "fine, by God", meaning "this is fine". The editor notes that the printed text and manuscript have the word without the article here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 95}

He then explains that "those you made a covenant with" means those with whom the Messenger made a covenant among the polytheists, since the covenants between Muslims and polytheists were the Messenger's covenants, concluded only by him or by someone on his order; he addressed the believers in this way because they knew its meaning and the Prophet's contracts on behalf of his community were their contracts, for they were content with all his acts among them and accepted his contracts on their behalf, so his contract on them was like their own contracts on themselves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 96}

The interpreters differed over those from whom God and His messenger disavowed the covenant that had been between them and the Messenger, and who were allowed four months to travel the land. Some said they were two kinds of polytheists: one whose term of covenant with the Prophet was shorter than four months and who was given four months' respite, and the other whose covenant had no fixed term and was limited to four months so that he could look to his own interest, after which he was at war with God, His messenger and the believers, to be killed or captured wherever found unless he repented. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 96}

Report 16356, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the Messenger sent Abu Bakr al-Siddiq as leader of the pilgrims in the ninth year to hold the people's pilgrimage, the polytheists still keeping to their places in it; Abu Bakr set out with the Muslims with him, and the sura of disavowal came down annulling the covenant between the Prophet and the polytheists, under which no one coming to the House was to be barred and no one was to be feared in the sacred month, a general covenant between him and the polytheist people. Between him and certain Arab tribes there were also particular covenants for a stated term, so the sura came down about this, and about the hypocrites who stayed behind from Tabuk and what some of them said, God exposing in it the hidden thoughts of people who concealed other than they showed, some named to us and some not. It speaks to the holders of the general covenant among the Arab polytheists, telling them to travel four months, up to the statement that God and His messenger are free of the polytheists, that is after this proof. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 97}

Others said the four months' respite was for those polytheists who had a covenant with the Messenger, while those with none had a term of fifty nights, twenty of Dhu al-Hijja and all of Muharram, since the term of those without a covenant ran to the end of the sacred months, as in [[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]]. They said the proclamation of the disavowal was made on the day of the greater pilgrimage, the day of sacrifice in one view and the day of Arafa in another, which gives fifty days, while the four months were for covenant holders from the day the sura came down, in the first of Shawwal, so their term ended with the end of the sacred months. Some who held this view said the respite began at the same time for both groups, but the covenant holder had four months and the one without a covenant until the sacred months ended, that is the end of Muharram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 97}

Report 16357, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: God set four months for those who had covenanted with His messenger, to travel as they wished, and set the term of those without a covenant at the passing of the sacred months, from the day of sacrifice to the end of Muharram, fifty nights; when the sacred months passed he was told to put the sword to those who had covenanted. Report 16358, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: it is a disavowal of the polytheists who held a covenant on the day it came down; He set the term of those with a covenant before it came down at four months and told them to travel, and the term of those without one at the passing of the sacred months, from the day the disavowal was proclaimed to the end of Muharram, fifty nights, twenty of Dhu al-Hijja and thirty of Muharram; from then no polytheist had covenant or protection, and the term of covenant holders ran four months from the day of proclamation to the tenth of the first Rabi' al-Akhir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 98}

Report 16359, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: before the disavowal came down the Prophet had covenanted with some polytheists of Mecca and elsewhere; it came down to everyone who had covenanted with him, annulling the covenant and giving four months to travel wherever they wished in safety, and to those without a covenant until the sacred months passed, counted from the day it was proclaimed, which was the day of sacrifice, twenty of Dhu al-Hijja and thirty of Muharram, fifty nights; God told His prophet that when Muharram passed he should put the sword to those without a covenant until they entered Islam, and likewise to the covenant holders once four months from the day of sacrifice had passed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 99}

Report 16360, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was told that Ali made the proclamation while Abu Bakr was made leader of the pilgrims, the year in which Muslims and polytheists performed the pilgrimage together, polytheists not doing so afterward; those covenanted were the polytheists of Quraysh at the time of al-Hudaybiyya, who had four months left after the day of sacrifice, and God told His prophet to fulfil their covenant to its term, while those without one had until the end of Muharram; each covenant holder's covenant was thrown back to him and he was ordered to fight them until they testified that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His messenger, nothing else being accepted from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 99}

Others said the start of the four months' postponement and its end were one time for all of them, starting on the day of the greater pilgrimage and ending on the tenth of Rabi' al-Akhir. Report 16361, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: when the verse came down He disavowed the covenant of every polytheist and made none after it except with those who already held one, running each to its term; the four months were for whoever's covenant fell within them, from the tenth of Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram, Safar, Rabi' al-Awwal and ten of Rabi' al-Akhir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 99}

Report 16362, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and others, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Ma'shar: the Messenger sent Abu Bakr as leader of the season in the ninth year and sent Ali ibn Abi Talib with thirty or forty verses of the sura, which he recited to the people, giving the polytheists four months to travel; he recited it on the day of Arafa, setting their term at twenty of Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram, Safar, Rabi' al-Awwal and ten of Rabi' al-Akhir, and recited it to them in their lodgings, adding that no polytheist should perform the pilgrimage after that year nor circle the House naked. Report 16363, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the four months were twenty of Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram, Safar, Rabi' al-Awwal and ten of Rabi' al-Akhir, which was their covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 100}

Report 16364, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the disavowal was to the people of covenant, Khuza'a, Mudlij and anyone with a covenant among them or others; the Messenger came back from Tabuk intending to make the pilgrimage, then said the polytheists attend and circle naked and he did not wish to make it so long as that happened, and sent Abu Bakr and Ali, who went round the people at Dhu al-Majaz, at the places where they traded and at all the seasons, announcing to the covenant holders that they were safe for four months, the consecutive months from twenty at the end of Dhu al-Hijja to ten of Rabi' al-Akhir, after which they had no covenant, and announcing war on all people unless they believed. The editor notes a difference between the manuscript and the printed text in the phrase about those who held a covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 101}

Report 16365, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj, is similar and adds that all the people then believed, so no one travelled, and that after returning from al-Ta'if and reaching Medina the Prophet went on to raid Tabuk. Others among those who held that the term began and ended at one time for all the polytheists said it began the day the sura came down and ended with the sacred months, that is the end of Muharram. Report 16366, from al-Zuhri, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it came down in Shawwal, so the four months were Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja and Muharram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 101}

Others said God's four months' respite for travel was for those whose covenant with the Messenger had a term shorter than four months, while those with a term longer than that were told to have their covenant completed to its term. Report 16367, from al-Kalbi, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the four months were for one whose covenant was shorter, and he completed it for him, while one whose covenant was longer was the one told to have it completed, citing the completing of the covenant to its term in [[passage/quran-9-4|9:4]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 102}

Al-Tabari chooses as the soundest view that the term God set for the covenant polytheists, allowing them to travel in it, was only for those who had backed others against the Messenger and broken their covenant before its term ended; as for those who had neither broken it nor supported anyone against him, God told His prophet to complete the covenant to its term, as in 9:4. If someone thinks that [[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]], on the passing of the sacred months, shows otherwise because it implies a duty to kill every polytheist afterward, he says the matter is not as supposed, for the following verses show the soundness of his view and the error of thinking that the passing of the sacred months made it lawful to kill every polytheist, with or without a covenant, among them [[passage/quran-9-7|9:7]], which tells the Prophet and the believers to be straight with those who covenanted at the Sacred Mosque as long as they are straight, and these are polytheists, by not breaking their truce and not backing their enemy against them. The editor adds notes on the language of the construction with the preposition for "show". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 103}

He adds that in the many reports from the Messenger, when he sent Ali with the sura to the covenant peoples he told him to cry out that whoever held a covenant with the Messenger, his covenant ran to its term, which is the clearest evidence for his view: God never told His prophet to break the covenant of a people who held to it to a term, and gave four months only to those who had broken their covenant before the respite or whose covenant had no fixed term; one whose term was fixed and who had given no ground was told to be given his covenant to its end, and this was the message his crier carried among the Arabs at the season. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 103}

Report 16368, from Abu Hurayra, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Qays, al-Mughira, al-Sha'bi and Muharrir ibn Abi Hurayra: I was with Ali when the Prophet sent him to make the announcement, and when his voice grew hoarse I called out; I asked what they proclaimed and he said four things: that no one circles the Kaaba naked, that whoever has a covenant with the Messenger has it to its term, that none enters Paradise but a believing soul, and that no polytheist performs the pilgrimage after that year. The editor explains the word for hoarse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 103}

The editor notes that al-Tabari gives this with three chains, and describes the narrators: Qays ibn al-Rabi' al-Asadi was judged weak by Ahmad and others, al-Mughira ibn Miqsam is reliable, and Muharrir ibn Abi Hurayra is a reliable Successor with few reports; the chain of this report is weak because of Qays. Report 16369, from Abu Hurayra, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Affan, Qays ibn al-Rabi', al-Shaybani, al-Sha'bi and Muharrir, is similar, with the statement that whoever has a covenant with the Messenger has it to its term; the editor gives its flaw as Qays's weakness and notes that al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak through Shu'ba from Sulayman al-Shaybani and called the chain sound, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. Al-Tabari says Shu'ba also narrated this report but differed from Qays on the term. Report 16370 begins, from Abu Hurayra, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Uthman ibn Umar, Shu'ba, al-Mughira, al-Sha'bi and Muharrir: I was with Ali when the Messenger sent him with the sura to the people of Mecca, and I called out until my voice was hoarse; I asked what he called, and he said they were told to proclaim that none enters Paradise but a believer, and that whoever has between him and the Messenger; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 104}

We stop here at the foot of p. 104, in the middle of the commentary on verses 1 and 2 (within report 16370); the next batch begins at p. 105. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 104}
