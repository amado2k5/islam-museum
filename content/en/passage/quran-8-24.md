## Summary
Verse 24 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 24 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 24 (pp. 463-472)**

Then al-Tabari says on "you who believe, answer God and the Messenger when he calls you to what gives you life" [[passage/quran-8-24|8:24]]: the interpreters differed over the interpretation of "when he calls you to what gives you life". Some said its sense is: answer God and the Messenger when he calls you to belief; report 15867, from al-Suddi: what gives you life is Islam, which gave them life after their death, after their unbelief. Others said it is the truth, reports 15868 to 15871 from Mujahid. Others said the sense is: when he calls you to what is in the Qur'an, report 15872, from Qatada: it is this Qur'an, in which is life, trust, deliverance and protection in this world and the hereafter. Others said the sense is: when he calls you to war and striving against the enemy, report 15873, from Ibn Ishaq: to the war by which God made you mighty after humiliation, strengthened you after weakness and protected you from your enemy after their having overcome you. The editor notes that the printed edition and manuscript drop the word "what" in report 15867 and it is right to keep it, and that the printed edition has "life and chastity" where the manuscript is unpointed and the publisher dropped "and deliverance", which he calls among the worst tampering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 463} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 464} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 465}

Abu Ja'far said: the best of the views is that of those who said its sense is: answer God and the Messenger in obedience when the Messenger calls you to what gives you life of the truth, since that being its sense, included in it is the command to answer them for fighting the enemy and striving and the answer when he calls you to the ruling of the Qur'an, and in answering all that is the life of the one who answers, in this world the remaining of a fair memory, which is a life for him, and in the hereafter eternal life in the gardens abiding in them. As for the saying of those who said its sense is Islam, it is a saying with no sense, since God has described them as believing in His words "you who believe", so there is no reason to say to a believer, answer God and the Messenger when he calls to Islam and belief. Moreover, it came in report 15874, from Abu Hurayra: the Messenger of God came out to Ubayy while he was praying and called him, Ubayy; Ubayy turned to him and did not answer. Then Ubayy shortened the prayer and went to the Prophet and said, peace on you, Messenger of God; he said, and on you; what kept you from answering me when I called you? He said, Messenger of God, I was praying. He said, did you not find in what was revealed to me, answer God and the Messenger when he calls you to what gives you life? He said, yes, Messenger of God, I will not do it again. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 465} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 466}

The editor identifies the men of the chain of 15874: Ahmad ibn al-Miqdam al-Ijli, al-Tabari's teacher, trustworthy; Yazid ibn Zuray' al-Ayshi, trustworthy and a memoriser; Rawh ibn al-Qasim al-Tamimi, trustworthy; al-Ala ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Ya'qub, freedman of al-Huraqa, a trustworthy Follower; and his father Abd al-Rahman ibn Ya'qub, a trustworthy Follower; and says Ahmad gave it at length in the Musnad and al-Tirmidhi in the virtues of the Qur'an calling it good and sound, that Ibn Kathir traced it, and points to al-Bukhari's report of Abu Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla with a similar story. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 466}

Then report 15875, from Abu Hurayra, is another chain for the preceding report: the Messenger of God passed Ubayy as he stood praying and cried out to him, and he did not answer, then he came, and he said, Ubayy, what kept you from answering me when I called you; does God not say [[passage/quran-8-24|8:24]]? Ubayy said: indeed, Messenger of God, you will not call me but I will answer, even if I am praying. The editor notes the words in brackets are not in the manuscript and were added by the printed edition's publisher, who does not know from where, and identifies the men of the chain, Khalid ibn Makhlad al-Qatawani and Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn Abi Kathir. Al-Tabari sees in the two reports a showing that those meant by the verse are those whom the Messenger calls to what gives them life by their answering him to the truth after their Islam, since Ubayy was without doubt a Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 467}

Then al-Tabari says on "know that God comes between a man and his heart, and that to Him you will be gathered" [[passage/quran-8-24|8:24]]: the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said its sense is: He comes between the unbeliever and belief, and between the believer and unbelief. Reports 15876 to 15881, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and some from Ibn Abbas, are in this sense, with "and obedience to God" in 15880; reports 15882 to 15888 are from al-Dahhak: He comes between the unbeliever and obedience to Him and between the believer and disobedience to Him; report 15889, from Ibn Abbas by the way of Ali ibn Abi Talha: between the believer and unbelief and between the unbeliever and belief, and report 15890 from him by the way of Ibn Sa'd: between the unbeliever and obedience to Him and between the believer and disobedience; report 15891 from Mujahid is like this; and reports 15893, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, and 15894, from Abu Salih: between the believer and acts of disobedience. The editor identifies Abd Allah ibn Abd Allah al-Razi in 15876 as the judge of Rayy, trustworthy, and distinguishes him from Abu Ja'far al-Razi al-Tamimi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 467} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 468} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 469} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 470}

Others said its sense is rather that He comes between a man and his reason so that he does not know what he does. Reports 15895 to 15898, from Mujahid: He comes between a man and his reason until He leaves him without reason; report 15899: when He comes between you and your heart, how will you act?; report 15900: He comes between the unbeliever's heart and his doing good. The editor shows that the printed edition altered the manuscript's wording in 15898 because it did not understand it, the point being that the present tense stands for the past, and calls this among the worst tampering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 470} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 471}

Others said its sense is that He comes between a man and his heart so that he is able to believe or disbelieve only by His leave, report 15901, from al-Suddi: He comes between a person and his heart, so he can neither believe nor disbelieve except by His leave. Others said its sense is that He is near to his heart, nothing he shows or hides is hidden from Him, and report 15902, from Qatada: it is like His words [[passage/quran-50-16|50:16]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 471}

Abu Ja'far said: the best of the views, to my mind, is to say that this is a report from God that He has more power over His servants' hearts than they do, and that He comes between them and it when He wills, so that no one with a heart can grasp by it anything of belief or unbelief, or retain or understand anything, except by His leave and will; for coming between one thing and another is a barrier between them, and when He bars a servant's heart from grasping or understanding something, the servant has no way to grasping it. Being so, included in it is the saying of those who said between the believer and unbelief and between the unbeliever and belief, the saying of those who said between him and his reason, and the saying of those who said between him and his heart so that he can neither believe nor disbelieve except by His leave. But it should be said that God made general in His words the report that He comes between a servant and his heart and did not single out any of the senses we mentioned over another; the words can bear all these senses, so the report stands as general until what must be accepted particularises it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 471} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 472}

As for "and that to Him you will be gathered", its sense is: know, believers, along with the knowledge that God comes between a man and his heart, that God who has power over your hearts and has more power over them than you, to Him is your return and your going back on the day of resurrection, and He will pay you the recompense of your deeds, the doer of good by his good and the doer of evil by his evil; so fear Him and be mindful of Him in what He and His Messenger commanded and forbade you not to waste, and not to fail to answer His Messenger when he calls you to what gives you life, which would make His anger due and earn you His painful punishment when you are gathered to Him. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "man" and "gathering". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 472}
