## Summary
Verse 160 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 160 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says the sense is: We forbade the Jews who broke their covenant, disbelieved God's signs, killed their prophets and slandered Mary, good things that had been lawful to them, as a punishment for their wrongdoing. Report 10833, from Qatada: they were punished for a wrong they did and an insolence they committed, so things were forbidden to them for their insolence and wrongdoing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 390}

As for 'hindering many from God's path', the sense is that they hindered God's servants from His religion and the paths He prescribed for His servants, a great hindrance; it lay in saying falsehood about God, claiming it came from Him, altering God's Book and its meanings, and chiefly in denying the prophethood of Muhammad and withholding what they knew of his matter from those ignorant of it. Report 10834, from Mujahid through Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: they hindered themselves and others from the truth; report 10835 says the same through Shibl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 391}

As for 'their taking of usury', it means taking what they added to their capital for the extension of a term after it was due; al-Tabari has explained usury earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 391}

Al-Tabari says 'they were forbidden it' means they were forbidden usury. 'Their eating of people's wealth falsely' means the bribes they took for judgement, the price they took for books they wrote with their own hands and then said: this is from God; and the like of such foul food. God punished them by forbidding them the good things that had been lawful to them. He described them as eating people's wealth falsely because they ate it without right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 392}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
