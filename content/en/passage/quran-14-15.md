**Commentary on verse 15 (pp. 542-545)**

On "they sought victory, and every stubborn tyrant failed", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: the messengers sought victory over their peoples, that is, asked God's help against them, "and every stubborn tyrant failed", he says, every arrogant oppressor perished who swerved from acknowledging God's oneness and devoting worship to Him. "'Anid", "'a'id" and "'anud" have one meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 542}

And from "jabbar" one says "he is a clear tyrant", with the nouns "jabariyya", "jabriyya", "jabarawa", "jibrawa" and "jabarut". The interpreters said the same: report 20612, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa, and al-Harith, al-Hasan and Waraqa, and Ibn Abi Najih: "they sought victory", he said, all the messengers, he says, they asked for help; "stubborn", he said, opposing the truth and shunning it. Report 20613, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: the like. Report 20614, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, and al-Harith, Ishaq and Abd Allah: "they sought victory", he said, all the messengers asked for help; "and every stubborn tyrant failed", he said, one who opposes the truth and shuns it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 543}

Report 20615, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the like, and Ibn Jurayj said, they sought victory over their people. Report 20617, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "they sought victory, and every stubborn tyrant failed", he said, the messengers and the believers were deemed weak by their people, subdued and called liars, and were called to return to their creed; God, mighty and exalted, refused for His messengers and the believers that they return to the creed of disbelief, ordered them to rely on God and to seek victory over the tyrants, and promised to settle them in the land after them; God fulfilled for them what He promised, they sought victory as He ordered them, and every stubborn tyrant failed. Report 20618, from Ibrahim, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, Abu Awana and al-Mughira: "and every stubborn tyrant failed", he said, one who turns away from the truth. Report 20619, from Ibrahim, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Mutarrif ibn Bishr, Hushaym, Mughira and Simak: "and every stubborn tyrant failed", he said, one who turns away from the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 544}

Report 20620, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "they sought victory", he says, the messengers asked for help against their people; "and every stubborn tyrant failed", the "stubborn tyrant" being one who refused to say there is no god but God. Report 20621, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "they sought victory", he said, the messengers asked help against their people; "and every stubborn tyrant failed", he says, stubborn against the truth and turning from it. Report 20622, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: the like, adding turning away, refusing to say there is no god but God. Report 20623, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "and every stubborn tyrant failed", he said, the one stubborn against the truth is the one who deviates from the road; he said, and the Arabs say "the worst of kin is the stubborn one", the one who goes off the road. Report 20624, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "they sought victory, and every stubborn tyrant failed", he said, the "tyrant" is the proud one. Ibn Zayd used to say something contrary to these on the meaning of "they sought victory", saying that it was the nations that sought a decision and were answered. Report 20625, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "they sought victory", he said, their seeking a decision was by affliction; they said, O God, if this {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 545}

We stop here at the foot of p. 545, in the middle of the commentary on verse 15 (inside report 20625 from Ibn Zayd, on the sense of "they sought victory"); the next batch begins at p. 546 (marker 554). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 545}
