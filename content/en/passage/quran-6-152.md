## Summary
Verse 152 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 152 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "do not go near the orphan's property except in the best way, until he reaches his maturity", al-Tabari says: do not go near his property except in what is for its good and increase. He gives (14147) from Mujahid that this is trading with it; (14148) from al-Suddi that he should make the property grow; and (14149) from al-Dahhak that he should seek profit for the orphan in it and take none of its profit, and the editor notes that Fudayl ibn Marzuq appears at 5437 and that he found no biography for Sulayt ibn Bilal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 221}

He gives (14150) from Ibn Zayd that "the best way" is to eat from it in the customary way if poor, and not to eat if rich, citing the wording of [[passage/quran-4-6|4:6]] that the rich should abstain and the poor eat in the customary way; and that he was asked about clothing and said God mentioned eating, not clothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 222}

On "until he reaches his maturity", al-Tabari says that "ashudd" is a plural of "shadd", and "shadd" is strength, the full firmness of his youthful strength and age, as "the shadd of the day" is its rising and stretching; one says "I came to him at the shadd of the day and the madd of the day". He says al-Mufaddal used to recite a line by Antara from his Mu'allaqa, which describes a slain hero whose chest and head are stained with a red dye, and another line by a poet the editor could not identify, about a wife who circles round a man in the height of the day. The editor's note says he found no basis in the Arabic books for the plural examples al-Tabari gives and left them as printed, possibly corrupt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 222}

Some Basran grammarians claimed that "ashudd" is like "anuk", meaning a singular and not a plural (anuk being tin or lead). As for the commentators, they differ over the point at which a person is said to have reached maturity. Some said it is when he reaches puberty: (14151) from Rabi'a that it is puberty, (14152) the same from Zayd ibn Aslam, with Ibn Wahb saying Malik told him the same, and (14153) from al-Sha'bi that "ashudd" is puberty, when good deeds are written for him and bad ones against him. Others said it is said when he reaches thirty years: (14154) from al-Suddi that his maturity is thirty years, then comes after it {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 223}

the words of [[passage/quran-4-6|4:6]], "until they reach marriage". Al-Tabari explains that something is omitted from the speech, left out because what is stated points to it, and that the sense is: do not go near the orphan's property except in the best way until he reaches maturity; then, when he reaches maturity and you perceive sound judgement in him, hand his property over to him; for God did not forbid going near the orphan's property while he is an orphan except in the best way so that after maturity the guardian might go near it in a worse way, but forbade them as protection and safeguard for him, so that they hand it over when he reaches maturity. The notes record where the printed text and the manuscript differ in two places and that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 224}

Then, on "give full measure and weight with justice", he says God means: say, come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you, that you set up no partners with Him, and that you give full measure and weight; that is, do not cheat people of the measure when you measure for them or the weight when you weigh for them, but give them their due, giving them their rights in full; "with justice" means with fairness, as in (14155) from Mujahid. Al-Tabari adds that he explained the meaning of "qist" with evidence earlier and did not want to repeat it, and the editor refers to that place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 224}

Then, on "We charge no soul beyond its capacity", al-Tabari says: we do not charge any soul, in giving full measure and weight, with more than it can bear, what is lawful for it and gives no hardship. God knew that many of His servants find it hard to be generous to another with what is not due; so He told the giver to pay the owner of the right his due and did not charge him with the surplus, because the surplus would constrain his heart, and He told the owner to take his right and did not charge him with accepting less, because the shortfall would constrain his heart. Neither soul is charged with anything that brings hardship or constraint. Al-Tabari says he set this out fully with evidence elsewhere, and the editor refers to the earlier commentary on "charging" and "capacity". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 225}

Then, on "when you speak, be just, even if it concerns a relative", he says it means: when you judge between people and speak, speak the truth among them, be just and fair and do not wrong, even if the person against whom the right and the judgement turn is your relative; and do not let the kinship of a relative or the friendship of a friend between whom and another you judge lead you to say other than the truth in what is brought to you for judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 225}

On "and fulfil God's covenant" he says: fulfil the bequest of God that He enjoined on you, and fulfilling it is to obey Him in what He commanded and forbade and to act by His Book and His Messenger's sunna; that is fulfilling God's covenant. On "that is what He has enjoined on you", God tells His Prophet to say to those of your people who equate idols with God: the matters I mentioned in these two verses are what our Lord covenanted and enjoined on you and commanded you to act on, and not the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and ham, the killing of children, the burying of girls and following Satan's footsteps; and "that you may remember" means He commanded these things so that you remember the outcome of your affair and the error of what you persist in, and desist and return to obedience to your Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 226}

Ibn Abbas used to say that these verses are the muhkam (precise) verses: he gives (14156) from him that they are the muhkam verses, namely the words "say, come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you". The editor's note says that Ali ibn Salih ibn Hayy al-Hamdani is trustworthy, that "Ali ibn Abi Salih" in the copies is a scribe's slip, that al-Hakim gave the report in the Mustadrak and declared it sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing, and that al-Hakim also gave it by another chain through Abu Ishaq and Abdullah ibn Khalifa from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 226}

He gives (14157) that Ka'b al-Ahbar heard a man reciting this verse and said, by Him in whose hand Ka'b's soul is, it is the first thing in the Torah after the basmala, and the editor notes that the chain is sound to Ka'b; (14158) that al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym said to a man, do you want a sheet bearing Muhammad's seal? and then recited these verses; and (14159) that al-Rabi' said, shall I read you a sheet from the Messenger of God? without mentioning the seal, and recited these verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 227}

He gives (14160) that a group came to Alqama and said, you sat with Muhammad's companions, tell us about revelation; he recited these verses of al-An'am to them; they said, we did not ask you about this; he said, we have no revelation other than this; (14161) from al-Suddi that these are the verses He enjoined from the muhkam of the Qur'an; and (14162) from Ibn Zayd on "when you speak, be just" that it means speak the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 228}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
