## Summary
Verse 95 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 95 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari begins with the opening of the verse. God addresses those who believe in Him and His messenger and forbids them to kill game, meaning land game and not sea game, while they are "hurum", that is in the sacred state for hajj or umra. "Hurum" is the plural of "haram", the same word for male and female (a man or a woman may be called haram; the usual forms are "muhrim" and "muhrima"), and "ihram" is entering that state, as one says a people entered ihram when they enter the sacred month or the sanctuary. The clause "whoever kills it among you deliberately" announces the ruling for a pilgrim who kills game on purpose. The people of interpretation then differ about what "deliberate" means, the deliberateness for which God made expiation and compensation due. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 7}

The first view: deliberate means intending to kill the game while forgetting one's ihram; if he kills it remembering his ihram and meaning to kill, no judgment is passed on him and his affair is with God, for that is too grave for a judgment or an expiation. Report 12544, from Mujahid (through Sufyan ibn Waki, Ibn Uyayna and Ibn Abi Najih): he who kills it forgetful of his ihram but intending the killing is the one who is judged; if he kills it remembering his ihram, intending to kill, no judgment is made. Report 12545, Mujahid: one who kills knowing he is in ihram is not judged and has no hajj; the "expiable deliberateness" is hitting it while forgetful of ihram though intending the kill, or hitting it while aiming at something else, and then he is judged once (the editor explains "once" as one time only; if he repeats, God takes retribution). Report 12546 is to the same effect, and 12547 adds only: intending the killing, forgetful of ihram. The editor's notes explain a reading of the word for ihram and correct a printed wording from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 8}

Mujahid's view recurs in reports 12548 to 12552 (among them 12550 and 12551 through Shu'ba and al-Hakam, and 12552 through Ibn Jurayj): the expiable deliberateness is hitting game while aiming at something else, whereas one who hits it neither forgetful nor aiming elsewhere is not judged. Report 12553, from al-Hasan: "deliberately" means killing it forgetful of ihram, and "whoever transgresses after that" means one who kills it deliberately remembering his ihram. Report 12554: al-Hasan gave the opinion that one who kills game deliberately, remembering his ihram, is not judged; Hammad from Ibrahim says the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 9}

Report 12555 is a story told by Hammad ibn Salama: Ja'far ibn Abi Wahshiyya asked him to ask Amr ibn Dinar about the verse; Amr said that Ata used to say it is a choice: he may offer an animal, or feed, or fast. When Hammad told Ja'far, Ja'far laughed and then said that Sa'id ibn Jubayr used to say that an animal from the livestock is judged for him, reaching the Ka'ba, that food and fasting were made expiation only, and that if he cannot find the animal the food is valued and the fast runs from three to ten days. The editor notes that the printed text differs from the manuscript, that he kept some additions to make the sentence stand, and that he found the report nowhere else. Report 12556 repeats Mujahid. Report 12557, from Ibn Zayd: those who are judged are the ones who kill forgetful of ihram or ignorant that it is forbidden; one who kills deliberately after the prohibition, knowing he is in ihram, is left to God's retribution. Report 12558, Mujahid: intending the killing, forgetful of ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 10}

The second view: deliberate means the pilgrim's intending to kill the game while remembering his ihram. Report 12559, from Ata: he is judged in deliberateness, error and forgetfulness. Reports 12560 and 12565, from Tawus: by God, God said only "whoever kills it among you deliberately". Report 12561, from al-Zuhri: the Qur'an came down with the deliberate case and the sunna ran on the case of error, meaning a pilgrim who hits game. Report 12562, from Ibn Abbas: if he kills it deliberately or forgetfully he is judged, and if he returns to it deliberately punishment is hastened unless God pardons. Reports 12563 and 12564, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: expiation was set for the deliberate case, but it was made harsher for error so that they would be on guard. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 11}

Al-Tabari's choice: God forbade killing land game to every pilgrim for as long as he is in ihram, then stated the ruling for one who kills deliberately, without singling out the one who forgot his ihram or the one who erred while remembering it; the revelation covers every pilgrim who kills game deliberately. He holds it impermissible to turn the apparent sense of the revelation into a hidden interpretation with no support in a text of the Book, a report from the messenger, or the consensus of the community. So it is the same whether the killer meant to kill remembering his ihram, meant to kill forgetting it, or aimed at something else and killed it remembering: all owe what God stated, a like of the livestock judged by two just men, or the expiation of feeding the poor, or its equivalent in fasting. This is the view of Ata and al-Zuhri, not that of Mujahid. As for the ruling on error, he says he treated it in his book "Latif al-qawl fi ahkam al-shara'i" and this is not the place, since his aim here is the interpretation of the revelation, which says nothing of error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 12}

Turning to "the like of what he killed of livestock", al-Tabari says it means an equivalent and a counterpart, that is, the compensation for the game killed. He notes that in the reading of Abd Allah (ibn Mas'ud) the word is "its compensation". The readers differ: most Medinan readers and some Basrans join "compensation" to "like" in a genitive construction; most Kufans read "compensation" with tanwin and "like" in the nominative, as "upon him is a compensation, the like of what he killed". Al-Tabari prefers the tanwin and nominative reading, because the compensation is itself the like and there is no ground for joining a thing to itself. He supposes those who read it as a genitive thought that the killer must compensate with a like of the game by a like from the livestock; he says it is not as they take it, for the duty is to compensate the animal killed with its counterpart from the livestock. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 13}

We stop here at the foot of p. 13, in the middle of the commentary on verse 95, at al-Tabari's choice of the reading "fajaza'un mithlu ma qatala".
