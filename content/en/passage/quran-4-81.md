## Summary
Verse 81 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 81 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse: planning words by night (pp. 562-563, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says those meant are the party God reported to have feared people when fighting was prescribed for them; they tell the Prophet, when he commands them something, that his order is obedience and they will obey in what he commands and forbids; when they go out from you, Muhammad, a group of them changes by night what you have said to them. He says any deed done by night is said to be "bayyata", and from it comes "I attacked the enemy by night". The editor refers to earlier interpretations of turning away and of going out (page numbers garbled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 563}

**The citations of poetry (pp. 563-564, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari cites two lines of Ubayda b. Hammam on the sense of night planning, what they firmed up and resolved by night (the lines belong with an account of a marriage proposal made to the poet that he rejected harshly, and one describes what they came with), and a line of al-Namir b. Tawlab al-Ukli that he gave for the verb (a line about a woman's night-time blame). The editor writes at length in his notes. He says Ubayda b. Hammam was of the Banu Malik b. Hanzala of Tamim and that the publisher of Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an took him for the Taghlibi, who is another; that he reasoned out his descent from a genealogy book (the name of the genealogist garbled in the scan); that the account of the two lines shows he belonged to the pre-Islamic period; and that al-Jahiz in al-Hayawan gives the account of the lines among reports on al-Nu'man b. al-Mundhir, when his brother al-Mundhir b. al-Mundhir proposed to Ubayda and was turned down. He lists sources for the lines (Majaz al-Qur'an, al-Hayawan, al-Kamil, al-Marzuqi's al-Azmina wa-l-Amkina, the diwan of al-Aswad b. Ya'fur, the diwan of the Two A'shas, Lisan al-Arab) and variants in the first half-line, explains the word for an unmarried woman as a woman without a husband, virgin or not, says the Mundhir meant is al-Mundhir b. al-Mundhir, al-Nu'man's brother, and that the second half-line is a dig at both, since al-Nu'man made his wife a wet-nurse for a child of Kisra and Kisra called him a slave, and that the lam in the phrase is of lineage; and says (his words) that what he says here is not found in any book, so let it be remembered. He says the manuscript has a corrupt reading in one line, which he corrected. He then says the line of al-Namir is in Majaz al-Qur'an, Khizanat al-Adab and the commentary on the shawahid of al-Mughni (page numbers as printed), that print and manuscript both err at the start of the line, and that he kept Abu Ubayda's version as the best. The note continues on p. 564: the editor says the opening word is the speech of his wife or mother, who blamed him for generosity and kept repeating "listen to me", that the word for vain means falsehood and light-mindedness, that the phrase on blame is not what al-Tabari meant though the commentators explained it so, and that for him it comes from the saying that a man spent the night awake, so the sense is that my blame keeps you awake; so the citation by Abu Ubayda, followed by al-Tabari, does not fit exactly though the matters are close (this is the editor's view). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 564}

**Rest of the commentary (p. 564).** Al-Tabari says God has written down what they change of your speech by night in the books of their deeds that the recording angels write. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 564}

**Reports 9980-9986 (pp. 564-565, note 1).** Bishr b. Mu'adh (9980, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that they change what the Prophet had enjoined. Muhammad b. Abdallah b. Yazi' (9981, from Yusuf b. Khalid, from Nafi' b. Malik, from Ikrima) reports from Ibn Abbas that they changed what the Prophet said. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9982, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports the same from al-Suddi, and (9983, by the same chain) from al-Suddi that they are the hypocrites who, when present with the Prophet, say they are obedient, and when they leave a group of them changes what he says; God has what they say written down. Al-Qasim (9984, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ibn Abbas that they change what the Messenger said. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9985, from his father, his uncle, his father, from his father) reports from Ibn Abbas that they were people who professed belief before the Messenger to be safe in their blood and property, and when they left they went against what they had said, and God faulted them. The editor says the print has "when" with a different particle and he kept the manuscript. Al-Tabari reports (9986, from something told to him from al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) from al-Dahhak that they are the people of hypocrisy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 565}

**The case of the first word and the reading (pp. 565-566, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the first word is in the nominative through an omitted element that the surface of the speech indicates, namely: your command is obedience, or: from us is obedience (the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an). He says the ta in the verb for night planning is vowelled with a fatha by most readers of Medina, Iraq and the rest, since it is the third radical of the doubled verb, and that some Iraqi readers made it silent and assimilated it into the following ta' because the places of articulation are close (the editor refers to al-Farra). Al-Tabari's choice is to leave out the assimilation, since the two letters are different, so leaving it out is the more eloquent of the two Arab usages, the other being allowed and reported. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 566}

**Rest of the verse (p. 566, notes 2-4).** Al-Tabari says God addresses Muhammad: leave, Muhammad, these hypocrites who tell you your command is obeyed and, once they leave you, oppose what you ordered and change it into what you forbade; leave them and the error they are in, and be content with me as avenger against them; entrust your affair to God, trust him in your matters and hand them to him; the one who acts for you in what he commands you is God, its protector, a defender of you and a helper. The editor refers to the interpretation of turning away (page numbers as printed) and of trust and guardian in volume 7. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 566}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 566}
