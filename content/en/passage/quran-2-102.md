## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and they followed what the devils recited over Solomon's kingdom", al-Tabari says those meant are the party of the Jews' scholars who threw God's Book aside, and who preferred the magic the devils recited in Solomon's reign and followed it. On who is meant, the first view is that they are the Jews around the Prophet's place of emigration, because when they contended with him using the Torah they found it agreeing with the Qur'an, so they contended with him using the books that people had written down from soothsayers in Solomon's time. He reports from al-Suddi that the devils used to climb to heaven and eavesdrop on the angels' talk of what would happen on earth and tell the soothsayers, who added seventy words to each word; people wrote this down in books, and it spread among the Children of Israel that the jinn know the unseen. Solomon gathered those books into a chest and buried it under his throne. After he died and a later generation succeeded, a devil in human form led some Israelites to the "treasure" under the throne, and when they brought the books out he said that Solomon had controlled men, devils and birds by this magic; it spread that Solomon was a sorcerer, and when Muhammad came they contended with him using these books, whereupon came "Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved". He reports from al-Rabi' that the Jews kept asking Muhammad about matters of the Torah and God sent down an answer to each, so when they saw this they asked him about magic and disputed with him on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 405} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 406}

Al-Tabari continues al-Rabi's report: the devils took a book and wrote in it magic and soothsaying and buried it under Solomon's seat, Solomon not knowing the unseen; when he left the world they dug it up, deceived people with it and said this was knowledge Solomon kept from them out of envy. From Ibn Zayd, that the People of the Book followed magic when the Messenger came. On who is meant, another view is that they are the Jews who lived in Solomon's time, from Ibn Jurayj. He reports from Ibn Ishaq that the devils wrote kinds of magic, sealed the book with a seal copying Solomon's ring and wrote on its cover "this is what Asaf ibn Barakhya wrote for King Solomon", and buried it under his throne; the remnant of Israel dug it up and said Solomon had done all this by it, so spread magic, and when the Prophet named Solomon among the messengers the Jews of Medina said: do you not marvel at Muhammad, who claims Solomon was a prophet when he was only a sorcerer? and the verse came down. In another report, when Solomon's kingdom was lost a group of jinn and men apostatised; when it returned to him he found their books and buried them under his throne, and after he died jinn and men found them and said this is a book God sent down on Solomon and he hid from us, and made it a religion. Al-Tabari's choice is that the verse is a rebuke of the rabbis who met the Messenger and denied his prophethood while knowing him, and of their following, with their forebears, what the devils recited in Solomon's time, so every Jew who follows it is included since nothing restricts it, as he has already explained how the deeds of forebears are ascribed to later generations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 406} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 409}

On "what the devils recited", "ma" means "that which", and on "tatlu" there are two views: relate, tell and speak, from Mujahid, Qatada, 'Ata' and Ibn Abbas (the devils added to what they heard of the revelation and wrote books of magic and unbelief, buried them under Solomon's throne, then brought them out and read them to people); or follow, act on and relate, from Ibn Abbas and Abu Razin. Al-Tabari holds that "tala" has two meanings in Arabic, following, as in Yunus 10:30, and reading and studying, as in a verse of Hassan, and that God gave no decisive report on which kind of reciting the devils' was; it may have been study, narration and practice, and the Jews followed their way. On "over Solomon's kingdom", "'ala" means "fi", in his kingdom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 409} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 410} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 411}

On "Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved, teaching people magic", al-Tabari asks why disbelief is denied of Solomon when no report had ascribed it to him, and answers that the Jews who followed what the devils recited ascribed it to Solomon, claiming it was his knowledge and that he subdued men and jinn by magic, thereby making their own practice look good to the ignorant, while another group of them disowned him and called him an unbeliever and sorcerer; so God cleared Solomon of magic and unbelief and belied those who claimed he practised it. He then gives reports. From Sa'id ibn Jubayr: Solomon used to collect the magic in the devils' hands and bury it under his seat; the devils pointed men to it, who dug it up and practised it. From Ibn Abbas, a report in which Solomon was tried because of the people of a wife named al-Jarada and his giving her his ring, so that a devil in Solomon's likeness took it and the devils submitted to him; in those days the devils wrote books of magic and unbelief, buried them under Solomon's seat, brought them out and read them to people, saying Solomon had overcome people with these books, and people disowned him and called him an unbeliever until the verse came down. From Abu Mijlaz: Solomon took a pledge from every beast and people saw rhymed spells and magic and said he had worked with these. From 'Imran ibn al-Harith: Ibn Abbas said to a man from Kufa, in a report on the devils' eavesdropping, with seventy lies added to every true word, and Solomon burying it under his seat until a devil pointed to it after his death. Similar reports come from Qatada and Mujahid; and from Shahr ibn Hawshab that the devils wrote the magic while Solomon was absent, heading it "this is what Asaf ibn Barakhya wrote for King Solomon". These are given as narrated, and al-Tabari offers them as the evidence for his reading that Solomon is cleared. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 411} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 412} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 413} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 414} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 415} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 416}
