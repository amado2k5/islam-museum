## Summary
Verse 102 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 102 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and they followed what the devils recited over Solomon's kingdom", al-Tabari says those meant are the party of the Jews' scholars who threw God's Book aside, and who preferred the magic the devils recited in Solomon's reign and followed it. On who is meant, the first view is that they are the Jews around the Prophet's place of emigration, because when they contended with him using the Torah they found it agreeing with the Qur'an, so they contended with him using the books that people had written down from soothsayers in Solomon's time. He reports from al-Suddi that the devils used to climb to heaven and eavesdrop on the angels' talk of what would happen on earth and tell the soothsayers, who added seventy words to each word; people wrote this down in books, and it spread among the Children of Israel that the jinn know the unseen. Solomon gathered those books into a chest and buried it under his throne. After he died and a later generation succeeded, a devil in human form led some Israelites to the "treasure" under the throne, and when they brought the books out he said that Solomon had controlled men, devils and birds by this magic; it spread that Solomon was a sorcerer, and when Muhammad came they contended with him using these books, whereupon came "Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved". He reports from al-Rabi' that the Jews kept asking Muhammad about matters of the Torah and God sent down an answer to each, so when they saw this they asked him about magic and disputed with him on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 405} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 406}

Al-Tabari continues al-Rabi's report: the devils took a book and wrote in it magic and soothsaying and buried it under Solomon's seat, Solomon not knowing the unseen; when he left the world they dug it up, deceived people with it and said this was knowledge Solomon kept from them out of envy. From Ibn Zayd, that the People of the Book followed magic when the Messenger came. On who is meant, another view is that they are the Jews who lived in Solomon's time, from Ibn Jurayj. He reports from Ibn Ishaq that the devils wrote kinds of magic, sealed the book with a seal copying Solomon's ring and wrote on its cover "this is what Asaf ibn Barakhya wrote for King Solomon", and buried it under his throne; the remnant of Israel dug it up and said Solomon had done all this by it, so spread magic, and when the Prophet named Solomon among the messengers the Jews of Medina said: do you not marvel at Muhammad, who claims Solomon was a prophet when he was only a sorcerer? and the verse came down. In another report, when Solomon's kingdom was lost a group of jinn and men apostatised; when it returned to him he found their books and buried them under his throne, and after he died jinn and men found them and said this is a book God sent down on Solomon and he hid from us, and made it a religion. Al-Tabari's choice is that the verse is a rebuke of the rabbis who met the Messenger and denied his prophethood while knowing him, and of their following, with their forebears, what the devils recited in Solomon's time, so every Jew who follows it is included since nothing restricts it, as he has already explained how the deeds of forebears are ascribed to later generations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 406} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 408} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 409}

On "what the devils recited", "ma" means "that which", and on "tatlu" there are two views: relate, tell and speak, from Mujahid, Qatada, 'Ata' and Ibn Abbas (the devils added to what they heard of the revelation and wrote books of magic and unbelief, buried them under Solomon's throne, then brought them out and read them to people); or follow, act on and relate, from Ibn Abbas and Abu Razin. Al-Tabari holds that "tala" has two meanings in Arabic, following, as in Yunus 10:30, and reading and studying, as in a verse of Hassan, and that God gave no decisive report on which kind of reciting the devils' was; it may have been study, narration and practice, and the Jews followed their way. On "over Solomon's kingdom", "'ala" means "fi", in his kingdom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 409} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 410} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 411}

On "Solomon did not disbelieve, but the devils disbelieved, teaching people magic", al-Tabari asks why disbelief is denied of Solomon when no report had ascribed it to him, and answers that the Jews who followed what the devils recited ascribed it to Solomon, claiming it was his knowledge and that he subdued men and jinn by magic, thereby making their own practice look good to the ignorant, while another group of them disowned him and called him an unbeliever and sorcerer; so God cleared Solomon of magic and unbelief and belied those who claimed he practised it. He then gives reports. From Sa'id ibn Jubayr: Solomon used to collect the magic in the devils' hands and bury it under his seat; the devils pointed men to it, who dug it up and practised it. From Ibn Abbas, a report in which Solomon was tried because of the people of a wife named al-Jarada and his giving her his ring, so that a devil in Solomon's likeness took it and the devils submitted to him; in those days the devils wrote books of magic and unbelief, buried them under Solomon's seat, brought them out and read them to people, saying Solomon had overcome people with these books, and people disowned him and called him an unbeliever until the verse came down. From Abu Mijlaz: Solomon took a pledge from every beast and people saw rhymed spells and magic and said he had worked with these. From 'Imran ibn al-Harith: Ibn Abbas said to a man from Kufa, in a report on the devils' eavesdropping, with seventy lies added to every true word, and Solomon burying it under his seat until a devil pointed to it after his death. Similar reports come from Qatada and Mujahid; and from Shahr ibn Hawshab that the devils wrote the magic while Solomon was absent, heading it "this is what Asaf ibn Barakhya wrote for King Solomon". These are given as narrated, and al-Tabari offers them as the evidence for his reading that Solomon is cleared. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 411} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 412} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 413} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 414} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 415} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 416}

Al-Tabari continues Shahr ibn Hawshab's report: after Solomon's death Iblis made a speech saying he was no prophet but a sorcerer, so look for his magic in his belongings and houses, and he showed them the place, while the believers said he was a believing prophet; and from Ibn Ishaq, that when the Prophet named Solomon among the messengers, some Jewish rabbis said: do you not marvel at Muhammad, who claims the son of David was a prophet when he was only a sorcerer? and the verse came down. He then says there is an omission in the speech, the sense being: they followed what the devils recited of magic over Solomon's kingdom, ascribing it to Solomon, and Solomon did not disbelieve by practising magic, but the devils disbelieved, teaching people magic; Qatada also read it so, saying it was not by his counsel or consent but something the devils fabricated without him. Asked whether magic existed only in Solomon's days, he says it existed before, for God tells of Pharaoh's magicians and of Noah's people, but the report here is confined to Solomon's days because the Jews ascribed it to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 416} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 417} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 418} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 419}

On "and what was sent down on the two angels at Babylon, Harut and Marut" al-Tabari sets out the disagreement over "ma". Some said it is a negation meaning "not", from Ibn Abbas and al-Rabi' ibn Anas, so the sense is: they followed what the devils recited, and Solomon did not disbelieve, nor did God send down magic on the two angels, but the devils disbelieved, teaching people magic at Babylon, Harut and Marut, "at Babylon Harut and Marut" being a phrase moved from its place; the two angels would then be Gabriel and Michael, since the Jews' sorcerers claimed God sent magic through them to Solomon, and God belied them. Others said "ma" means "that which", from Qatada and al-Zuhri from 'Abd Allah: Harut and Marut were two angels sent down to judge among people, because the angels had found fault with the judgments of the children of Adam; a woman brought a case before them and they were unjust in her favour, then went to ascend but were prevented and given a choice between punishment in this world or the next, and chose this world's; and they taught people magic but with the condition to say first "we are only a trial, so do not disbelieve". Al-Suddi says the Jews contended with him also over what was sent down on the two angels; Qatada, that magic is of two kinds, one the devils teach and one Harut and Marut teach; Ibn Abbas, that it is the separating of a man from his wife; Ibn Zayd, that the devils and the two angels teach people magic. Al-Tabari says that on this reading the verse means the Jews followed what the devils recited in Solomon's kingdom and what was sent down on the two angels at Babylon, and he will report what is narrated about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 417} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 418} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 419} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 421}

Al-Tabari answers the question whether God may send down magic and have His angels teach it, saying God sent down good and evil and made all of it clear to His servants, and told His messengers to teach His creatures what is lawful and what forbidden, such as adultery, theft and other sins; magic is one of those sins he told them of and forbade them to practise. There is no sin in knowing magic, as there is none in knowing how wine is made or idols carved; the sin is in doing it and in harming one whom it is not lawful to harm. So there is no sin in its being sent down on the two angels or in their teaching those they taught, since it was by God's leave, after telling the learner they were a trial and forbidding magic and unbelief; the sin lies on whoever learns it from them and acts on it. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir thought al-Tabari's course odd and that al-Tabari has clear grounds for it. He then gives another view, that the second "ma" means "that which" and is joined to the first, the first being magic and the last the separating of man and wife, from Mujahid; and a view that it may be "that which" or "not", from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, who when asked said: I do not mind which it is, only that I believe in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 421} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 422} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 423} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 424}

Al-Tabari chooses "ma" as "that which", not a negation, arguing that if it were a negation then "Harut and Marut" would be either an apposition to "the two angels", which would void "they teach none until they say: we are only a trial, and from them people learn what separates man from wife", since they would hold no knowledge to be learned; or an apposition to "people", which would make the devils the teachers of Harut and Marut, and then if they were angels they would be charged with unbelief worse than the sin reported of them, while if they were two men, magic would have ceased with their deaths, yet it exists in every age. So "ma" means "that which" and Harut and Marut are an apposition to the two angels, their endings taking fatha because they are diptotes. To the question how God's angels can teach separating man and wife, he says God told His servants all He commanded and forbade, so it is not strange that He taught it to the two angels and made them a trial to test His servants, the believer purified by refusing to learn and the unbeliever disgraced by learning, the angels being obedient to God in teaching by His leave; a number of God's friends have been worshipped besides Him without harm to them. He reports from al-Hasan that this was laid on them. He then begins the reports on the two angels, the first from Ibn Abbas: when the angels saw the deeds of the children of Adam they asked how they could do such sins; God said had they been in their place they would do the same; they chose Harut and Marut and they were sent down to earth (al-Tabari completes this report over the following pages). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 424} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 425} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 426} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 427}

Al-Tabari gives, on Harut and Marut, a series of reports that he relays from their narrators and does not assert in his own voice. In the rest of the report from Ibn Abbas (the editor says he cannot identify one of its transmitters), the two angels were sent down with everything on earth lawful to them save associating partners with God, theft, adultery, drinking wine and killing, and a woman named "Bidhakht" came before them; they fell into what they were forbidden and killed a beggar who came upon them, and were given the choice of punishment in this world or the next, chose this world's and were set at Babylon. From Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas: when the angels saw the sins of the children of Adam multiply they invoked against them, and God told them that had desire been placed in them they would have done the same; they chose Harut and Marut, and al-Zuhra was sent to them in the form of a Persian woman; they fell into sin, the angels then sought forgiveness for those on earth, and the two chose this world's punishment. From 'Ali: al-Zuhra was a beautiful Persian woman who brought a dispute to them, and they taught her the words by which one ascends to heaven, so she ascended and was changed into a star. From Ka'b al-Ahbar, by two chains: the angels disapproved of human deeds and were told they would do the same in their place, so chose two, who descended and before the day was out did what God had forbidden them. From al-Suddi: the two had found fault with earthly judgments and were given the ten desires, came down at Babylon to judge and ascended at evening; a woman disputing with her husband pleased them (named al-Zuhra in Arabic, Bidakhast in Nabatean, Anahid in Persian), she asked by what words they ascend and descend, they told her and she ascended, God made her forget how to come down and made her a star, and when 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar saw it he would curse it; the two could not ascend that night, knew they were lost, chose this world's punishment and were suspended at Babylon. From al-Rabi' ibn Anas: they were sent down in Idris's time with the desires of the children of Adam placed in them, and a woman came and asked that they worship an idol, kill, or drink wine; they chose wine and fell into sin and killed a man who passed them, and the veil between them and the people of heaven was lifted, so the angels marvelled and knew that one who is unseen fears less, and began to seek forgiveness for those on earth. From Nafi', that Ibn 'Umar spoke so of the red star, saying he had heard it from the Prophet, with a similar account of the choosing of the two angels (the editor judges this chain weak). These reports are relayed as narrated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 427} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 428} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 429} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 430} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 431} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 432} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 433}

From Mujahid, al-Tabari gives another report on Harut and Marut: the angels were amazed at the injustice of the children of Adam, so two were chosen to judge on earth; they judged justly, then were tried by al-Zuhra in the form of a woman bringing a dispute, and they exposed themselves to her; that evening they were not allowed to ascend and sought the help of a man of the children of Adam to pray for them, his prayer was answered, they were offered the punishment of this world or the next, and they were placed at Babylon, said to be hung in iron. The editor notes that the scholars of hadith found these reports on Harut and Marut and al-Zuhra defective, that their sense also comes in a raised report in Ahmad's Musnad through Musa ibn Jubayr from Nafi' from Ibn 'Umar, and he quotes Ibn Kathir that it is most likely Ibn 'Umar's report from Ka'b al-Ahbar and not from the Prophet, belonging to the Israelite reports not to be relied on, and that the plain flow of the Qur'an leaves the story general without elaboration. Al-Tabari then mentions a reading by some readers, "al-malikayn" with kasra, meaning two men of the children of Adam, which he rejects on reasoning and on the agreement of the Companions, the Followers and the readers of the regions. "Babil" is a place name, with two views: Babil Dunbawand, from al-Suddi, or Babylon of Iraq, from 'A'isha in a story of a woman she mentioned. On the meaning of magic, one view is that it is trickery and illusions by which the bewitched sees things otherwise than they are, like a mirage or a passenger on a moving ship; he relays 'A'isha's report that when the Prophet was bewitched it seemed to him that he did things he had not done, a Jew of Banu Zurayq named Labid ibn al-A'sam having bewitched him, and from 'Urwa and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab that the Jews of Banu Zurayq tied knots and put them in a well, God showed him where and he took them out (the editor details the chains and their transmitters, and answers contemporary deniers of the report, relying on al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar and al-Mazari). Those who hold this view say the sorcerer cannot by his magic change a thing from its reality or bring bodies into being, citing the description of Pharaoh's magicians in Ta Ha 20:66, or there would be no distinction between truth and falsehood. Others said the sorcerer may turn a man into a donkey and bring things and bodies into being, and they argue from a report of 'A'isha that follows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 434} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 435} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 436} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 437} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 438} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 439}

From Hisham ibn 'Urwa, from his father, from 'A'isha, al-Tabari relays a report of a woman from Dumat al-Jandal who came to ask the Prophet, after his death, about something of magic she had entered into but not practised; in it she went to Babylon and saw two men hung by their feet, who told her "we are only a trial, so do not disbelieve", she refused, was told to urinate in an oven, and saw an armoured rider come out of her and rise into the sky, and they told her "that was your faith leaving you"; thereafter whatever she wished came to pass, and she repented, saying she had never done anything and never would. (The editor says the chain to 'A'isha is good and quotes Ibn Kathir that it is a strange report and a strange account, that it is not known whether the woman was truthful, and that 'A'isha was truthful in saying that the woman told her.) He then gives the argument of those who hold that the sorcerer can change things: that unless he could do what he claimed he could not separate man and wife, and that God says they learn from the two angels what separates them in reality, not by illusion. Others said magic is a seizing of the eye. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 439} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 440} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 441} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 442}

On "and neither teaches anyone until they say: we are only a trial, so do not disbelieve", he says the two angels teach no one what was sent down on them of separating man and wife until they say: we are only a trial and a test for the children of Adam, so do not disbelieve in your Lord. He reports from al-Suddi that when someone comes wanting magic they warn him, and if he refuses they tell him to go to the ashes and urinate on them; then light comes out of him and rises to heaven, which is faith, and something black like smoke enters his ears, which is God's anger, and when he tells them so they teach him magic. From Qatada, al-Hasan and Ibn Jurayj, that a pledge was taken from them not to teach anyone until they said this, and that only an unbeliever dares magic. "Fitna" here means testing and trial, as in a poet's verse and as one says "I tried the gold in fire"; Qatada says "a trial" means affliction. On "so they learn from them what separates man and wife" he says it is a fresh statement about the learners and not a reply to "neither teach", the sense being that they refuse to accept the warning and so learn; another view takes it as about the Jews, joined to "teaching people magic" and moved from its place. He prefers the first, since joining a clause to what follows is better than to what is separated from it. The pronoun in "minhuma" refers to the two angels, "ma" means "that which", said to be magic or something other than magic, as already set out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 442} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 443} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 444} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 445}

On "between a man and his wife", al-Tabari explains that "mar'" is a man of the children of Adam, its feminine "mar'a"; it is used in the singular and dual but is not pluralised in that form; and the people of the Hijaz call a man's wife "zawj", while Tamim, many of Qays and the people of Najd say "zawja". To those who ask how a sorcerer separates a man and wife, he says magic is making a thing appear to a person otherwise than it is, so its separating them is making each see the other as unlike the beauty they are, until one finds the other ugly, turns away, and the husband brings about a separation; the sorcerer is thus the separator by producing the cause, since the Arabs ascribe a thing to its cause. He reports from Qatada that their separating is taking each away from the other and making each hated by the other. Those who refused that the two angels teach separation took "so they learn from them" to mean they learn in place of what the angels taught them something to separate with, citing verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 445} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 446} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 447} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 448}

On "but they harm none by it except by God's leave" he says those who learn what separates do not harm anyone by what they learned except one on whom God has decreed that it will harm him; one whom God has shielded from its harm and protected from the evil of magic, spells and charms is not harmed. "Idhn" in Arabic has several senses: command without compulsion, which cannot be meant here because God forbade separating a man and his wife; letting a person have free rein; and knowledge, as in a verse of al-Hutay'a and in "be on notice of war from God" (2:279), and this is the meaning of the verse: "except by God's knowledge", that is, of what was foreknown that it would harm him; from Sufyan, "by God's decree". On "they learn what harms them and does not benefit them" he says they learn the magic that harms them in their religion and does not benefit them in their return, though in the present world they earn a living by it. On "they knew that whoever buys it has no share in the Hereafter" the party meant are those who threw God's Book aside and followed what the devils recited; they knew that one who bought magic with God's Book, preferring it, has no share in the Hereafter. Qatada reports that the People of the Book knew by God's covenant with them that the sorcerer has no portion on the Day of Resurrection; al-Suddi, that those meant are the Jews; Mujahid, that it is whoever bought what separates man and wife; and Ibn Zayd, that the Jews knew the Torah says whoever bought magic and left God's religion has no share in the Hereafter and the Fire is his abode. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 446} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 447} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 449} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 450} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 451}

On "whoever buys it" al-Tabari notes the lam is the oath-lam entering the conditional and the rules that follow for the verb. On "he has no share in the Hereafter" they differ on the meaning of "khalaq": some said a share, from Mujahid, al-Suddi and Sufyan; others a proof, from Qatada; others religion, from al-Hasan; and others standing, from Ibn Abbas. Al-Tabari prefers "share", for that is its sense in Arabic, citing the Prophet's saying "God will support this religion with people who have no khalaq", that is no share in Islam (the editor gives its routes in Ahmad from Abu Bakra and elsewhere from Anas), and a verse of Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt; so the verse means he has no portion of Paradise in the Hereafter, having neither belief nor righteous deed to be repaid, which denies him a share of the good and not of the evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 451} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 452} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 453} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 454}

On "evil is that for which they sold themselves, if only they knew" he says "sharaw" means "sold", so evil is what one who learns magic sells himself for, if he knew its bad end, reported from al-Suddi. Asked how it says "if only they knew" after "they knew", he says the sense is by the moved phrase, and that the censure in "evil is what they sold" is of those who learned from the two angels what separates man and wife, content to take magic in place of their religion in ignorance of the bad end of their act; the speech then returns to the party who threw God's Book aside, reporting that they knew that whoever buys magic has no share in the Hereafter, yet commit sins knowingly, out of obstinacy and transgression. He rejects those who said "they knew" means the devils and "if only they knew" the people, because the interpreters agree the first is about the Jews and the verses throughout censure the Jews. He gives another view, that knowledge is denied of them in "if only they knew" because they did not act on what they knew, and one whose act contradicts his knowledge is like the ignorant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 452} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 455} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 456}
