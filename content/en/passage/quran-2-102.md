

Al-Tabari adds that one may say to a doer "had you known, you would have stopped" though he knows what he does, citing the verse of Ka'b ibn Zuhayr al-Muzani describing a wolf and a raven that followed him for his provisions; he told them "if you knew it", denying their knowledge, then asked "did you not know". This reading, he says, has a way out, but it is against the plain sense of the address, and the Qur'an is to be read by its plain sense until evidence requires otherwise (the editor notes that al-Sharif al-Murtada transmits his words in his Amali). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 456} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 457}
