**Commentary on verse 108: hiding from people and not from God (pp. 191-193, notes 1-3 on p. 191, notes 1-3 on p. 192 and note 1 on p. 193).** Al-Tabari says those who betray themselves hide what they did of betrayal, shame and disobedience from people, who can do nothing to them except to mention the ugliness of what they did when they learn of it, out of shyness before them and fear of an ugly reputation; and they do not hide from God, who is aware of them and from whom nothing of their deeds is hidden, in whose hand are punishment, exemplary penalty and quick torment, and who is more worthy that one should be shy of Him and more deserving of reverence, in that He should not see them where they would not wish any of His creatures to see them. He is with them means God is witness over them, when they arrange by night what is not pleasing of speech, changing it from its true form and lying in it. He says he has already explained the sense of the verb for this night-planning elsewhere, that it is any speech or matter set in order by night. It is also reported of some of Tayyi' that in their speech the word means to change, and he gives a line by al-Aswad ibn Amir ibn Juwayn al-Ta'i, in which the poet reproaches a man called Abd al-Malik for altering his words and calls him an ungrateful servant. Another view, reported from Abu Razin, takes the word to mean that they compose, and the reports 10419 (Muhammad ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-A'mash, from Abu Razin), 10420 (Ahmad ibn Sinan al-Wasiti, from Abu Yahya al-Himmani, from Sufyan, from al-A'mash, from Abu Razin, to the same effect) and 10421 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from al-A'mash, from Abu Razin, the same) give that view. Al-Tabari says this is close in meaning to what he said, since composing is arranging and changing a thing from what it is and turning it from its meaning to another. It has also been said that the hiding from people and not from God refers to the group who went to the Prophet to ask for the defence of Ibn Ubayriq and to argue for him, as reported earlier from Ibn Abbas and others. On the closing clause, he says God encompasses what these people do who hide from people in the crime they committed out of shame before them, in their arranging what is not pleasing of speech and in their other deeds: He counts it, nothing of it hidden, keeping it against them until He gives them their repayment. The editor's notes: the printed text has a different passive in both places for what they did, and the manuscript is right; a reference to the earlier discussion of the night-planning in volume 8, pp. 562-563; the name of the poet, which the printed text and manuscript give as son of Jarir where the right form is son of Juwayn, and the editor's account of al-Aswad and of his father Abu Amir ibn Juwayn, with whom Imru' al-Qays stayed (al-Aghani 8:90, 95), whom Ibn Durayd calls both chiefs (al-Ishtiqaq, p. 233), and whom Ibn Hazm (Jamhara, p. 379) calls a poet whose son Qabisa ibn al-Aswad came to the Prophet; that he could not find the line anywhere though he knew it and hopes to add its source in another edition; that Abu Razin is Abu Razin al-Asadi, Mas'ud ibn Malik, who came earlier in reports 4291-4294 and 4791-4793; that the printed text and manuscript have "in the Abiraq" where the context needs "in Ibn Ubayriq"; and, on p. 193, references to the language indexes for the words for encompassing and for the one who encompasses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 193} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 193}
