## Summary
Verse 160 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 160 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 160: help and forsaking (pp. 347-348).** Abu Ja'far: if God helps you, believers, against those who opposed and were hostile to you among His enemies, no one among people will overcome you while He helps you, even if His creatures from all its corners gathered against you; so do not fear God's enemies because your number is small and theirs large, so long as you keep to His command and are upright in obeying Him and His messenger, for victory and triumph are yours and not theirs. If your Lord forsakes you because you opposed His command and left obedience to Him and His messenger, leaving you to yourselves, who will help you after that? Despair of people's help, for you will find no helper after God's forsaking, if He forsakes you; so do not leave His command and obedience to Him and His messenger lest you perish through His forsaking. He then tells them to rely on their Lord rather than all His creatures, be content with Him in place of any other, submit to His decree and fight His enemies in His cause, and He will suffice them by His aid and supply them with His help. Ibn Ishaq (8135): if God helps you none of the people will overcome you and the forsaking of those who forsook you will not harm you; if He forsakes you the people will not help you; do not leave My command for the people, put aside the people's command for Mine, and let the believers rely on God and not on the people (from Ibn Hisham 3: 124, the continuation of reports ending at 8132; the editor notes that Ibn Hisham abridged the report and began from the words 'do not leave', and that the copyist, he judges, dropped what the editor restored in brackets from the Sira). In footnotes on p. 347 the editor says that 'ayistu' is a dialect form of 'ya'istu', and that he put 'nasiran' (a helper) in brackets in place of 'amran' in the printed text and the manuscript, being dissatisfied with its sense and inferring from the meaning of the verse, while fearing that the copyist omitted something or wrote something corrupted he could not trace. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 347} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 348}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 348}
