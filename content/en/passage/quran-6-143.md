## Summary
Verse 143 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 143 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 143, "eight pairs: of sheep two and of goats two. Say: is it the two males He forbade, or the two females, or what the wombs of the two females contain? Tell me with knowledge, if you are truthful", al-Tabari says this is a rebuke from God to those who set up idols as equals to Him among the worshippers of statues, who slit the bahira, freed the sa'iba and tied the wasila, and a teaching from Him to His Prophet and the believers of the argument against them over their forbidding what they forbade. He said to the believers: He it is who raised trellised and untrellised gardens, and of the cattle He raised load-bearers and bedding, and then explained the load-bearers and bedding saying "eight pairs". "Eight" is in the accusative because it is an explanation of the load-bearers and bedding, a substitute for them, as if the sense were: of the cattle He raised eight pairs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 183}

"Of sheep two and of goats two" make four, because each of the two is a pair: the female is the mate of the male and the male the mate of the female, and so with goats and all other animals, which is why He said "eight pairs", as He said "of everything We created two mates" (51:49), and "He made from it its mate to dwell with her" (7:189) and "keep your wife" (33:37). He gives (14067) al-Dahhak: of sheep two, male and female, of cattle two, male and female, and of camels two, male and female. One says of two that they are a pair, as Labid said describing the howdahs of travelling women, "of each curtained howdah whose boughs a pair shades, over it a thin curtain and its figured cover"; the note explains the words of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 183}

Then He told them: eat of what God provided you of these fruits and meats and ride these load-bearers, believers, and do not follow the footsteps of Satan in forbidding what these ignorant people forbade without My command. And say, Muhammad, to those who forbade what they forbade of crops and cattle, in following Satan, of the idol worshippers: has your Lord forbidden the two males of sheep and goats, you liars against God? If they claim that and affirm it, they call themselves liars and show their ignorance, because if they say He forbade the two males, they make forbidden every pair of males born of sheep and goats while they enjoy the flesh and backs of the males of them. "Or the two females": if they say our Lord forbade the two females, they make forbidden to themselves the flesh and backs of every female of sheep and goats, which also falsifies them and refutes their claim since they enjoyed the flesh and backs of some. "Or what the wombs of the two females contain" says: or did He forbid what the wombs of the ewe and the she-goat contain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 184}

In this too, if they affirmed it and said He forbade us what the wombs of the two females contain, their saying is shown void and their lie shown, since by that admission they would be admitting that God forbade them the males and females of sheep and goats, to eat their flesh or ride their backs, whereas they enjoyed some of both. "Ma" in "or what the wombs contain" is in the accusative, joined to "the two females". "Tell me with knowledge" means: say to them, inform me with knowledge of this, rightly, which of these your Lord forbade you and how He forbade, "if you are truthful" in what you ascribe to your Lord of your claim and attribute to Him of your prohibition; the editor refers to al-Farra'. This is only God's informing His Prophet that everything these idolaters said in this matter and attributed to God is a lie against Him, that He forbade none of it, and that they only followed Satan's footsteps and opposed His command. He gives (14068) Qatada: of all this I forbade little or much, male or female; and (14069) Qatada: ask them whether it is the two males He forbade. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 185}

He completes the report of Qatada (14069): that is, "I forbade none of this", and he mentioned the camels and cattle in the same way. Then he gives (14070) from Mujahid that "eight pairs" came in the matter of what God forbade of the bahira; and (14071) from him through Ibn Jurayj that it concerns the bahira and sa'iba. Ibn Jurayj said the sense is: from where did you forbid this, from the side of the two males, or the two females, or what the wombs hold? They hold only a male or a female, so where did the prohibition come from? They answered: we found our fathers doing so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 186}

He gives (14072) from al-Suddi that God sent down for them eight pairs of what He had counted, male and female: two of sheep, two of goats, two of cattle and two of camels. Did He forbid them the two males, or the two females, or what the wombs contain, when the womb holds only a male or a female? He forbade neither male nor female of the eight; this was mentioned only because of what they had forbidden of livestock. And (14073) from al-Hasan: "what the wombs contain" is what the womb carried. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 186}

He gives (14074) from Ibn Zayd that "is it the two males He forbade" answers their saying that what is in the bellies of these livestock is reserved to their males and forbidden to their wives; that "an'am" means camels, cattle, sheep and goats, the ones God named eight pairs; that their phrase "livestock and tillage, forbidden" means they fenced them off from whom they wished; and that "livestock whose backs are forbidden" means no one may ride them. So He asked which of the two He had forbidden to these, that is, making them lawful to some and forbidden to others. Then (14075) from Ibn Abbas: does the womb hold anything but a male or a female, so do they forbid some and permit others? And (14076) from him through the Sa'd family chain: these are four pairs of sheep and goats, then camels and cattle; "I forbade none of that", and "tell me with knowledge" means that all of it is lawful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 187}

He then turns to language. "Al-da'n" (sheep) is a plural with no singular from its own wording, and it is also pluralised as "al-da'in" with the first letter voweled with a fatha or a kasra, like "al-sha'ir" (barley) and "al-shi'ir", as "al-'abd" is pluralised "'abid" and "'ibid". The single male is "da'in", the female "da'ina", and its plural "dawa'in". The editor's note says the fatha and then the kasra are both attested for "al-da'in" and "al-sha'ir", that he did not manage to find the same for "al-'abid" though it exists as he recalls, and that the kasra on the letter da is a Tamimi dialect form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 187}

Likewise "al-ma'z" (goats) is a plural without a singular, and so is "al-ma'za"; as for "al-ma'iz" (a single goat), its plural is "mawa'iz". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 188}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
