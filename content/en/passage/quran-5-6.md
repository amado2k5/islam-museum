## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 6 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 6: who is addressed and when ablution is due (pp. 7-13)**

Al-Tabari says the sense is: believers, when you rise to the prayer while not in a state of purity, wash your faces with water and your hands to the elbows. Interpreters then differed over the words 'when you rise to the prayer': does it cover every occasion of rising to it, or only some? Some said as al-Tabari does, that only some occasions are meant, namely rising to it while not in purity. Report 11300, from Ikrima, who was asked whether one makes ablution every hour: Ibn Abbas said there is no ablution except after a hadath, a breaking of purity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 7}

Report 11301, from Ikrima, says Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas prayed several prayers with one ablution, and report 11302 has him saying: pray with your purity as long as you have not broken it. The editor notes that al-Bukhari says Mas'ud ibn Ali al-Shaybani heard Ikrima, that the transmission is mursal, and that Shu'ba and Yahya al-Qattan reported from him. Report 11303 has a questioner ask Ubayda al-Salmani what makes ablution obligatory; he said: the hadath. Reports 11304 and 11305 tell of men with Abu Musa on the bank of the Tigris who made ablution and prayed the noon prayer; when the call to the afternoon prayer was made some rose to make ablution from the Tigris, and Abu Musa said there is no ablution except for the one who has broken it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 8}

Reports 11306 and 11307 give the same account from Qatada, from Waqi' ibn Sahban, from Tarif ibn Yazid (or Yazid ibn Tarif), from Abu Musa. The editor notes that reports 11304 to 11307 are four chains for one report, that chain 11305 is certainly inverted, that the variant forms of the name are not found in the biographical works and are known only from al-Tabari, and that Ibn Hajar's judgment of 'unknown' applies to the man who narrates from Tarif and not to Tarif's teacher. Report 11308: Abu Khalid made ablution beside Abu al-Aliya for the noon or afternoon prayer and asked whether he might pray with it, since he would not return to his family until the night prayer; Abu al-Aliya said there is no harm, and taught that once a person has made ablution he remains in it until he breaks it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 9}

Reports 11309 and 11310, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib: ablution without a hadath is transgression. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir cites them, calls the statement strange coming from Sa'id, and reads it as meaning that whoever believes ablution obligatory is a transgressor, while ablution as a recommended act is established by the Sunna. Reports 11311 and 11312, from al-A'mash: he saw Ibrahim pray the noon, afternoon and sunset prayers with one ablution, and he himself prayed the prayers with one ablution, and Ibrahim likewise. Report 11313: al-Hasan was asked about a man who prays all the prayers with one ablution and said there is no harm as long as he does not break it; report 11314 is the same from al-Dahhak; report 11315 says al-Aswad ibn Yazid prayed the prayers with one ablution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 10}

Report 11316, from al-Suddi, gives the sense: when you rise while not in purity. Report 11317 says al-Aswad had a small wooden bowl enough for one man to drink, made ablution from it and prayed all the prayers with that ablution. Report 11318, from Jabir ibn Abdallah: he prayed the prayers with one ablution, and when he urinated or broke it he made ablution and wiped his leather socks with what was left of his water; asked whether he did this by his own opinion, he said he had seen the Messenger of God do it. The editor notes that al-Fadl ibn al-Mubashshir was judged weak by Ibn Ma'in and that Ibn Majah has the report as no. 511. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 11}

Others said the sense is: when you rise from your sleep to the prayer. Report 11319, from Zayd ibn Aslam, gives the sense as rising from sleep; report 11320 is the same through Malik ibn Anas; report 11321, from al-Suddi, says the same. The editor refers to the Muwatta, p. 21. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 11}

Others said that every occasion of a person's rising to prayer is meant, and that he must renew his purity for it. In report 11322 Mas'ud ibn Ali asks Ikrima whether, having made ablution for the morning prayer and gone to the market, he may pray the noon prayer; Ikrima answers that Ali ibn Abi Talib used to say, and then recites the verse. The editor notes that answering by reciting the verse is permissible in this context, as if he had said that Ali did so and recited the verse. Report 11323, from Ikrima: Ali made ablution at every prayer and recited this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 12}

Report 11324, from Ibn Sirin: the caliphs made ablution for every prayer. Report 11325, from Anas: Umar ibn al-Khattab made a light ablution and said, this is the ablution of one who has not broken purity; the editor notes that Ibn Kathir cites it and calls its chain sound. Report 11326, from al-Nazzal: he saw Ali pray the noon prayer and sit for the people in the courtyard, then water was brought and he washed his face and hands and wiped his head and feet, saying: this is the ablution of one who has not broken purity. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir calls these good chains from Ali that strengthen one another, and that there is a difference over al-Nazzal's being a Companion. Report 11327, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim from Hushaym from al-Mughira, begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 13}

The rest of report 11327, from Ibrahim: Ali took from a large jar just the measure he needed and made a light ablution, saying: this is the ablution of one who has not broken purity. The editor explains that 'iktala' means he took a measured amount of water, and that 'hubb' is a large jar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 14}

Others said this was a command from God to His prophet and the believers to make ablution for every prayer, which was then abrogated by relief. Report 11328, from Ibn Ishaq: Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban asked Ubayd Allah ibn Abdallah ibn Umar whence came Abdallah ibn Umar's ablution for every prayer, whether pure or not. He answered that Asma, daughter of Zayd ibn al-Khattab, told him that Abdallah ibn Hanzala ibn Abi Amir, the son of the man washed by the angels, told her that the Prophet had been commanded to make ablution at every prayer, which was hard on him, so he was commanded the tooth-stick instead and ablution was lifted from him except after a hadath; Abdallah thought he had the strength for it and so made ablution. The editor's notes give the narrators, say Abu Dawud has the report as no. 48, and quote Ibn Kathir that the chain is sound and that Ibn Ishaq states he heard it directly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 14}

Report 11329 gives the same account through Ibn Ishaq from Muhammad ibn Talha ibn Yazid ibn Ruqana from Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Habban; it repeats the one before. The editor notes that he corrected 'from Abu Ishaq' to 'from Ibn Ishaq' and discusses the difference between Ubayd Allah and Abdallah, the two sons of Abdallah ibn Umar, in the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 15}

Report 11330, from Buraida: the Prophet used to make ablution for every prayer, but in the year of the conquest he prayed the prayers with one ablution and wiped his leather socks; Umar said, you have done something you did not use to do, and he answered: I did it deliberately. The editor's notes give the narrators and say Ahmad has it in his Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 16}

Report 11331 gives the same from Buraida, saying it was on the day of the conquest of Mecca. The editor notes that Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and al-Bayhaqi have it, Ibn Majah as no. 510, and that al-Tirmidhi questioned Sufyan's route through Muharib ibn Dithar, which is reported both connected and mursal, judging the mursal form sounder than Waki's; the editor does not agree, since the report is well known from Sulayman from his father and al-Thawri may have narrated it sometimes connected and sometimes mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 17}

Report 11332, from Sulayman ibn Buraida, says the Prophet used to make ablution for every prayer; this is the mursal form. Report 11333, from Buraida: the Prophet prayed all the prayers with one ablution, and Umar said, you did something you did not do; he said, I did it deliberately, Umar. Report 11334 says that when he conquered Mecca he prayed the noon, afternoon, sunset and night prayers with one ablution. Report 11335, from Ibn Umar, says the same of these four prayers; the editor notes its chain is very weak, and that one narrator, al-Hakam ibn Zuhayr, was accused of fabricating. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 18}

Abu Ja'far (al-Tabari) says: the soundest view in our opinion is that of those who said God meant by 'when you rise to the prayer' every occasion of rising to it, but it is a command of obligation to wash what God ordered washed for one rising to his prayer after a hadath that broke his purity and before renewing ablution, and a command of recommendation for one who is already in purity with no hadath since. This is why the Prophet made ablution for every prayer before the conquest of Mecca and then, on that day, prayed all the prayers with one ablution: to teach his community that his renewing purity for each prayer was taking the more excellent course, preferring what God loves most and hastening to what his Lord recommended, and not an obligation upon him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 19}

Al-Tabari answers anyone who supposes that the report of Abdallah ibn Hanzala, that the Prophet was commanded to make ablution at each prayer, shows obligation: to say that God commanded His prophet to do something can mean obligation, guidance, recommendation, permission or release, and when a statement allows these, the most fitting sense is the one the proof agrees upon; and the proof agrees that God did not make ablution for every prayer obligatory on His prophet or His servants and then abrogate it. The editor notes the construction of the sentence and that al-Tabari uses 'haqiqa' in the sense of 'haqq'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 19}

He holds that this consensus is clear evidence for what he says: that what the Prophet did was to prefer what God recommended to him and to His believing servants, and that his leaving it on that occasion was a concession to his community and a notice that ablution is neither obligatory nor binding except after a hadath that breaks purity. He then gives reports in the same sense, among them report 11336, from Anas: the Prophet was brought a small bowl and made ablution; asked whether he made ablution at every prayer, Anas said yes, and asked about themselves, he said: we prayed the prayers with one ablution. The editor notes that al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi have it, and that al-Tirmidhi calls it good and sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 20}

Report 11337, from Abu Ghutayf: he prayed the noon prayer with Ibn Umar, who then sat in a room of his house; when the afternoon call came he asked for water and made ablution, went out to pray and returned; at the sunset call he again asked for water and made ablution. Abu Ghutayf asked whether this was a sunna, and Ibn Umar said no, for his ablution for the morning prayer would suffice for all the prayers as long as he did not break it, but he had heard the Messenger of God say: whoever makes ablution while in purity has ten good deeds written for him, and he desired that. The editor notes that al-Tirmidhi judged Abu Ghutayf weak, that Abu Dawud has the report as no. 62 and Ibn Majah as no. 512, and that al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah have the preceding report of Anas (11336). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 21}

Report 11338, from Ibn Umar, gives the saying about the ten good deeds in brief. Some said that this verse was revealed to tell the Prophet that ablution is required of him only when he rises to prayer and not for other acts: when he had broken purity he used to refrain from every act until he made ablution, and God by this verse permitted him to do whatever he wished after a hadath except pray, whether he made ablution or not. Report 11339, from Alqama ibn al-Fughwa: when the Messenger of God had passed water we would speak to him and he would not answer, and greet him and he would not return the greeting until he reached his house and made ablution as for prayer; when asked, he said this went on until the verse of concession was revealed. The editor notes that the report is problematic and its chain weak because of Jabir al-Ju'fi, who was accused of lying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 22}

In his notes on pp. 23-24 the editor sets out the disagreements in the chain of 11339: 'Sufyan' or 'Shayban' as the one Mu'awiya ibn Hisham narrates from; 'Abdallah ibn Alqama ibn Waqqas' or 'Abdallah ibn Alqama ibn al-Fughwa' as the narrator from his father; and who stands between Jabir al-Ju'fi and Abdallah. He lists where the report appears in al-Tahawi, Ibn Kathir, al-Jassas, Ibn Hajar, Ibn al-Athir, al-Haythami, al-Nahhas and al-Suyuti, and explains that he changed the printed text to 'Shayban', following the manuscript, and to 'ibn al-Fughwa', following the other sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 23}

**Commentary on 'wash your faces': the limit of the face (pp. 23-27)**

Al-Tabari says interpreters differed over the limit of the face that God ordered the one rising to prayer to wash. Some said it is what shows of a person's skin from where the hair of the head grows, sloping down to the end of the chin in length, and between the two ears in width. They said the ear and what is inward in the mouth, nose and eye are not part of the face and washing any of it is not required in ablution. As for what hair covers, such as the chin covered by the beard and the temples covered by the side-hair, passing water over the hair suffices in place of washing the skin beneath, because the face, for them, is what appears to the eye of the one looking at it and nothing else. The editor's notes discuss the manuscript and printed readings here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 25}

Reports 11340 to 11343, from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i: what runs over the beard of the water suffices, and what runs from his face onto his beard is enough for him. Report 11344, from al-Mughira on combing water through the beard: what passes over your beard suffices. Report 11345, from Mansur: he saw Ibrahim make ablution without combing through his beard. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 26}

Report 11346, from Ibrahim: what runs over it suffices without combing through. Report 11347, from Yunus: when al-Hasan made ablution he wiped his beard with his face; reports 11348 to 11350 say he did not comb through his beard, and report 11352 that he did not make the water reach the roots of his beard. Report 11351, from Ibn Sirin: washing the beard is not part of the sunna. In report 11353 Sa'id al-Zubaydi asks Ibrahim whether he should comb water through his beard at ablution, and he says no, what your hand passes over suffices. In report 11354 Ibn Ulayya asks Shu'ba about combing through the beard, and he cites al-Mughira, from Ibrahim: what runs of water from his face onto his beard is enough. The editor notes that Abu Dawud and Ibn Hibban trusted Sa'id al-Zubaydi and that al-Bukhari said his reports are not corroborated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 27}

Report 11355 says Ibn Shihab and Rabi'a made ablution, passed water over their beards, and neither combed through his beard. In report 11356 al-Walid ibn Muslim asks Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz about rubbing the cheek-hair in ablution; he says it is not obligatory and that he saw Makhul make ablution without doing it. Report 11357, from al-Hasan: rubbing the cheek-hair in ablution is not obligatory. Report 11358, from Ibrahim: what passes over his beard of the water is enough for him. In report 11359 Sulayman ibn Abi Zaynab asks al-Qasim ibn Muhammad what to do with his beard at ablution and is told: I am not one of those who wash their beards. The editor's notes give the narrators of these chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 28}

Report 11360, from Abu Amr: rubbing the cheek-hair and interlacing the beard are not obligatory in ablution. The editor notes that 'arak' is rubbing, that 'tashbik' is running the fingers into the beard from beneath, and that a report from Ibn Umar says the Prophet rubbed his cheek-hair and interlaced his beard with his fingers when he made ablution. Al-Tabari then cites those who held this view on washing the inward parts of the mouth and nose. Report 11361, from Ibn Abbas: were it not for the smacking of the lips in prayer I would not rinse the mouth. Report 11362: Ata was asked about a man who prayed without rinsing and said that what the Book does not name suffices. Report 11363, from Ibrahim: rinsing the mouth and sniffing water are not obligatory parts of ablution. Report 11364: al-Dahhak forbade them these in ablution in Ramadan. Report 11365, from al-Hasan, about one who forgot them: if he remembers after entering the prayer let him continue, and if not let him rinse and sniff. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 29}

Report 11366: Shu'ba asked al-Hakam and Qatada about a man who remembers in prayer that he did not rinse or sniff, and they said he continues his prayer. Al-Tabari then cites those who held that the two ears are not part of the face. Reports 11367, 11368 and 11369 give Ibn Umar's saying that the ears belong to the head; the last adds: when you wipe the head, wipe them too. The editor's notes give the narrators, including Ghaylan ibn Abdallah, a client of Quraysh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 30}

Report 11370, from Ghaylan: Ibn Umar was asked by a man who forgot to wipe his ears and said the ears belong to the head, seeing no harm in it. Reports 11371 and 11372 give the same saying of Ibn Umar, report 11373 gives it from Ibn Abbas, and reports 11374 and 11375 from al-Hasan and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib. The editor's note identifies Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an and Yusuf ibn Mihran. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 31}

Reports 11376 and 11377 give the same saying of Ibn Umar, and report 11378 gives it from al-Hasan. Report 11379 gives it as a saying of the Prophet, 'the ears belong to the head', from Abu Umama or Abu Hurayra, with Ibn Bazi' in doubt. In report 11380 Hammad says he does not know whether it is from Abu Umama or from the Prophet, and report 11381 has it as the Prophet's. The editor's notes identify Mu'alla ibn Mansur, Sinan ibn Rabi'a and Shahr ibn Hawshab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 32}

Report 11382 gives it from Sulayman ibn Musa as a mursal report, report 11383 from Abu Hurayra, and report 11384 from al-Hasan. The editor notes that Ahmad, Abu Dawud (no. 134), Ibn Majah (no. 444) and al-Bayhaqi have the report, that scholars have long debated whether the sentence is an interpolation into the hadith or a saying of the Prophet, that he judges the hadith sound since it is reported by many chains, some good, which strengthen one another, and that Ibn Bazi's doubt is an error and Abu Umama is correct. Others said that the face is everything below the growth of the hair of the head to the end of the chin in length, and from ear to ear in width, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 33}

what shows of it to the eye and what is hidden, including the roots of the beard growing on the chin and the cheek-hair, the inside of the mouth and nose, and the part of the ears that faces the face. All of it, in their view, is the face God ordered washed, and if the one making ablution leaves out any of it his prayer with that ablution is not valid. Reports 11385 and 11386, from Nafi': Ibn Umar wetted the roots of his beard and worked his hands in to the roots until the water ran from it. Report 11387: when he made ablution he combed through his beard until he reached the roots of the hair. Report 11388, from al-Azraq ibn Qays: he saw Ibn Umar make ablution and comb through his beard. The editor notes that he follows the manuscript reading for the word meaning 'flowing'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 34}

Report 11389, from Nafi': Ibn Umar combed water through his beard until it reached the roots of the hair. Report 11390: Ubayd ibn Umayr, when he made ablution, worked his fingers into the roots of the facial hair, rubbing the skin. Report 11391, from Ibn Umar: he rubbed his cheek-hair somewhat and sometimes interlaced his beard with his fingers and sometimes left it; report 11392 says the same of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari. In report 11393 Muslim saw Ibn Abi Layla make ablution and wash his beard, saying: whoever of you can make the water reach the roots of the hair, let him. Report 11394, from Ata: it is incumbent on him to wet the roots of the hair. Report 11395: Mujahid used to comb through his beard. The editor expresses doubt about one phrase in 11390, noting the manuscript has it so, and that al-Bayhaqi reports 11391 from Nafi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 35}

Reports 11396 to 11398, from al-Hakam: Mujahid combed through his beard when he made ablution. Report 11399, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: what is it with the beard, that it is washed before it grows and, once grown, is not washed? Report 11400: Ibn Umar combed through his beard at ablution; report 11401 says the same of Tawus, reports 11402 and 11403 of Ibn Sirin, and report 11404 has Shu'ba say, from al-Hakam, that Mujahid combed through his beard. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 36}

Reports 11405 and 11406: Ibn Sirin made ablution and combed his beard; report 11407, from al-Dahhak, says the same. Report 11408, from Anas ibn Malik: I saw the Prophet make ablution and comb his beard, and I said: why do you do this, prophet of God? He said: my Lord commanded me to. The editor notes that the report turns on Yazid al-Raqashi, who is weak, that he does not know a Zayd al-Hudari, and that Ibn Majah has it as no. 431 and al-Hakim in the Mustadrak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 37}

Report 11409, from Anas: I gave the Prophet water for ablution and he put his fingers under his chin and combed through his beard, saying: with this my Lord, mighty and majestic, commanded me. Report 11410 is similar, through Salam ibn Salm al-Madini. Report 11411, from Anas, has the Prophet say: thus my Lord commanded me, and put his fingers into his beard and combed it. The editor notes that of Salam ibn Salm Ahmad said he related disavowed reports, al-Bukhari said they abandoned him and al-Nasa'i said he is not trustworthy; the last report is weak because of Yazid al-Raqashi; and 'hanak' means what is beneath the chin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 38}

Report 11412, from Umm Salama: the Messenger of God made ablution and combed his beard. The editor notes that its chain includes Khalid ibn Ilyas, of whom Ahmad said his reports are abandoned. Report 11413, from Abu Ayyub: we saw the Prophet make ablution and comb his beard. The editor notes it is weak because of Wasil ibn al-Sa'ib and Abu Sawra, and that Ibn Majah has it as no. 433. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 39}

Report 11414, from Abu Umama: the Prophet combed his beard. The editor discusses the doubt whether a narrator is Umar ibn Sulaym or Sulayman al-Bahili, and notes that al-Tabarani and Ibn Abi Shayba have it. In report 11415 Hassan ibn Bilal al-Muzani saw Ammar ibn Yasir make ablution and comb his beard, and asked about it Ammar said: I saw the Messenger of God do it. The editor notes that Abd al-Karim Abu Umayya is weak, that al-Bukhari and Ibn Uyayna denied that he heard it from Hassan, and that Ibn Majah has it as no. 429, with al-Hakim and al-Tirmidhi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 40}

Report 11416, from Yazid al-Raqashi and Qatada: when the Messenger of God made ablution he rubbed his cheek-hair and interlaced his beard with his fingers. Report 11417, from Jubayr ibn Nufayr, says the same of the Prophet; the editor notes it is mursal and that Ahmad judged one narrator, Abu Mahdi Sa'id ibn Sinan, weak. Report 11418 begins with the chain from Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Ahmasi through Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Tanafisi from Wasil al-Raqashi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 41}

The rest of report 11418, from Abu Ayyub: when the Messenger of God made ablution he rinsed his mouth and wiped his beard from beneath with water. The editor notes that the name Abu Sawra appears in al-Ahmasi's version as 'Abu Sawda', an error, that Ahmad has the report in his Musnad, and that its chain is weak. Al-Tabari then cites those who held that the inward parts of nose and mouth are washed. Report 11419, from Mujahid: sniffing water is half of ablution. Report 11420, from Hammad, on a man who remembers in prayer that he did not rinse or sniff: he leaves, then rinses and sniffs. In report 11421 Abu Sinan asks Hammad in Kufa about one who left rinsing and sniffing and prayed, and he says he thinks he must repeat the prayer. Report 11422, from Qatada: if a man left rinsing, sniffing, his ear or part of his foot until he entered his prayer, he turns away, makes ablution and repeats the prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 42}

Al-Tabari then cites those who said that the part of the ears facing forward belongs to the face and the part facing back to the head. Reports 11423 to 11429, from al-Sha'bi, give this in close wordings: the forward part of the ears is of the face, the back part of the head; or the inner part is of the face and the outer of the head. Report 11430, from Ibn Abbas: Ali said, shall I make ablution for you as the Messenger of God did? When he washed his face he pressed his thumbs on the forward part of his ears, and when he wiped his head he wiped his ears from their backs. The editor notes that Abu Dawud has it as no. 117, that Ahmad has it, and that al-Bukhari judged it weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 43}

Abu Ja'far says: the soundest view in our opinion is that of those who said that the face God ordered washed by the one rising to prayer is everything that slopes from where the hair of the head grows to the end of the chin in length, and between the ears in width, of what is apparent to the eye, excluding what is inward in the mouth, nose and eye, what is covered by the hair of the beard, cheek and moustache, and the ears. His reason is that all agree the eyes are part of the face, and yet all agree that washing the outside of the lids without the water reaching beneath them suffices; if that is a consensus based on the Messenger's instruction to his community, then the same holds for whatever part of the ablution areas is covered by something that water reaches only with trouble and effort. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 44}

Al-Tabari adds that the inward parts of nose and mouth and the hair of the beard, temples and moustache are like the eyes in the trouble of getting water to them. It is therefore clear that when Companions and Successors washed beneath the beard, the cheek-hair and the moustache and inside the nose and mouth, they were choosing the harder of two courses, as Ibn Umar chose to pour water beneath his eyelids, and not treating it as an obligation. Whoever supposes they did it as an obligation went against their method and ignored analogy; and there is no report that any Companion required one who did not reach the roots of his beard and cheek-hair, or left rinsing and sniffing, to repeat his prayer. As for report 11431, 'when one of you makes ablution let him blow out his nose', which the book gives without a chain and which al-Bukhari has in similar words, it is no proof that blowing out is obligatory, since the consensus is that it is not an obligation requiring repetition of the prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 45}

As for the ears, the consensus that leaving them unwashed, or washing the forward part with the face, does not invalidate the prayer, together with the consensus that omitting any part of the face that must be washed does invalidate it, shows that the correct view is the one held by those Companions that the ears are not part of the face, not al-Sha'bi's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 46}

**Commentary on 'and your hands to the elbows' (pp. 46-48)**

Al-Tabari says interpreters differed over whether the elbows are part of the hand that must be washed, after agreeing that washing the hand up to them is obligatory. Malik ibn Anas was asked whether he thought one should wash behind the elbows in ablution. He said: what he was ordered is to reach the elbows; God said 'wash your faces', so is this man to go and wash behind them? Told that he washes to the elbows and the ankles and goes no further, he said: I do not know what 'goes no further' means, what he was ordered is to reach the elbows and ankles. The editor corrects wording in the source and explains Malik's remark as astonishment at someone told to wash his face who goes off washing what is behind it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 46}

Al-Shafi'i said he knew of no one who disagreed that the elbows are among what is washed, as if the sense were: wash your faces and your hands until the elbows are washed. Others said God made washing the hands up to the elbows obligatory, so the elbows are the end of what He made obligatory, and the end is not included in the limit, as night is not included in the fast since it is the end of fasting; this is the view of Zufar ibn al-Hudhayl. Abu Ja'far says: the correct view is that washing the hands up to the elbows is an obligation, and leaving any of it makes the prayer invalid, while the elbows and beyond are recommended, as the Prophet recommended by the saying of report 11432: my community will come bright-faced and bright-limbed from the traces of ablution, so whoever can lengthen his brightness, let him. The editor notes that al-Bukhari has it in similar words and explains the terms 'ghurra' and 'tahjil'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 47}

So the prayer of one who leaves them unwashed, or what lies beyond them, is not spoiled, for al-Tabari showed earlier that any limit set by 'ila' (to) may in Arabic include or exclude the limit; and when the language allows both, no one may judge that it is included unless his judgment cannot be opposed, and in his view no such authority has ruled that the elbows are included in what must be washed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 48}

**Commentary on 'and wipe your heads' (p. 48)**

Al-Tabari says interpreters differed over the manner of the wiping God commanded. Some said: wipe with water whatever part of your heads you please when you rise to the prayer. In report 11433 the wiping of the head was mentioned before al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, who asked Nafi' how Ibn Umar wiped; he said a single wipe, and described him wiping the front of his head; al-Qasim said: Ibn Umar was the most learned and understanding of us. Report 11434, from Nafi': when Ibn Umar made ablution he put his palms back in the water and wiped the front of his head with his hands. Report 11435, from Nafi', begins: Ibn Umar would place the inside of his right palm on the water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 48}

The rest of report 11435, from Nafi': Ibn Umar placed the inside of his right palm on the water without shaking it, then wiped with it between his two 'horns' (the sides of the head) to the forehead once, adding nothing to it, each time a single wipe coming from the forehead to the side. The editor corrects a dual pronoun to the singular and explains that the 'qarn' is the edge and side of the head. Report 11436: when Ibn Umar made ablution he wiped the front of his head. Report 11437, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: it suffices you to wipe the front of your head if you wear a turban, and so for a woman. Report 11438, from Nafi': he saw Ibn Umar wipe his crown once, and Sufyan said that if he wipes a single hair it suffices him. Report 11439, from Ibrahim: whichever sides of your head you touch with water suffices you; reports 11440 and 11441 give the same from al-Sha'bi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 49}

Report 11442, from Nafi': Ibn Umar wiped his head like this, and Ayyub put his palm in the middle of his head and passed it over the front. Report 11443, from Sufyan: if he wipes his head with a single finger it suffices him. In report 11444 al-Walid ibn Muslim asks Abu Amr what suffices of wiping the head and is told that wiping from the front of the head to the nape is dearer to him; report 11445 is similar. Others said the sense is: wipe all your heads, and if a man does not wipe his whole head with water his prayer with that ablution is not valid. Report 11446, from Malik: whoever wiped part of his head without covering it repeats the prayer, like one who washed part of his face or forearm; asked how to wipe the head, he said one begins at the front of the face, takes the hands round to the nape, then brings them back to where he began. Others said wiping less than three fingers does not suffice, which is the view of Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 50}

Abu Ja'far says: the correct view in our opinion is that God the Exalted commanded the one rising to prayer to wipe his head, along with the rest of what he commanded him to wash or wipe, and did not set a limit that may not be fallen short of or exceeded. So whatever the one making ablution wipes of his head, so that it can be said he wiped his head, he has fulfilled what God made obligatory, since he comes under the name of one who wipes his head. If someone says that God in the case of tayammum speaks of wiping the faces and hands, and asks whether wiping part of the face and hands suffices, the answer is: whatever is wiped with earth in the points scholars disputed, some saying it suffices and some not, suffices because it falls under the name of those who wipe with it; and what is agreed not to suffice is to be accepted as the proof came from the Prophet; no one has a proof against us here, since our principle is that what comes general in the verses of the Book is general until something is specified that must be accepted. He has set out the reason for this elsewhere. The editor's notes correct the wording and refer to the commentary on the tayammum verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 51}

The head that God commanded to be wiped is the area where the hair of the head grows, not what goes beyond it toward the nape behind, nor what slopes down from it in front toward the forehead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 52}

**Commentary on 'and your feet to the ankles' (pp. 52-55)**

Al-Tabari says the readers differed in how to read this. A group of readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read 'arjulakum' in the accusative, meaning: when you rise to the prayer, wash your faces, your hands to the elbows, and your feet to the ankles, and wipe your heads. It is then a case of something delayed with the sense of being advanced, the feet being accusative by joining to the hands, and they understood that God commanded the feet to be washed and not wiped. He then gives those who said washing is meant. Report 11447, from Abu Qilaba: a man prayed with a spot the size of a nail dry on the top of his foot, and when he finished Umar told him to repeat his ablution and his prayer. Report 11448, from Ibn Mas'ud: comb water through your toes, lest the fire comb them. The editor's notes introduce Abdallah ibn Hasan and Huzayl ibn Shurahbil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 52}

Report 11449, from al-Mughira ibn Hunayn: the Prophet saw a man making ablution and washing his feet and said: this is what I was commanded. The editor notes it is mursal and weak because of Murajja ibn Raja. Report 11450, from Mus'ab ibn Sa'd: Umar ibn al-Khattab saw people making ablution and said: comb between the toes. Report 11451, from al-Qasim: Ibn Umar took off his leather socks, then made ablution, washed his feet and combed his toes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 53}

In report 11452 Ibrahim asks al-Aswad whether he saw Umar wash his feet thoroughly, and he says yes. Report 11453: Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz told Ibn Abi Suwayd that it had reached them of three men who saw the Prophet wash his feet thoroughly, the least of them your cousin al-Mughira. Report 11454, from Ali: wash the feet to the ankles. Report 11455, from Abu Qilaba: Umar saw a man who had left a spot like a nail on the top of his foot and ordered him to repeat his ablution and prayer. In report 11456 Shayba ibn Nisah accompanied al-Qasim ibn Muhammad to Mecca, saw him pour water between his toes at ablution and asked why; he said he had seen Ibn Umar do it. Report 11457, from Ibrahim on the verse: the command went back to washing. Report 11458 begins, through al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Suda'i from his father from Hafs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 54}

The rest of report 11458, from Abu Abd al-Rahman: al-Hasan and al-Husayn recited to him 'arjulikum' in the genitive; Ali heard it, as he was judging between people, and said 'arjulakum' in the accusative: this is of the advanced and delayed in speech. The editor notes that Hafs al-Ghadiri's reports are abandoned and that Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami was the reciter of Kufa. Report 11459, from Ibn Abbas: he read it in the accusative and said the command went back to washing; report 11460, from Urwa, reads it in the accusative and says the same; report 11461 says Abdallah ibn Mas'ud read it in the accusative. Report 11462 begins with the chain of Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 55}

The rest of report 11462, from al-Suddi: the sense of 'and your feet to the ankles' is: wash your faces, wash your feet and wipe your heads, which is a matter of advancing and delaying. Report 11463, from Shayban: it was confirmed to him that Ali read 'arjulakum' in the accusative. Report 11464, from Urwa: the command returned to washing; report 11465 says the same from Ikrima. Report 11466, from al-A'mash: the companions of Abdallah read it in the accusative and so washed. Report 11467, from Ali: wash the two feet to the ankles. In report 11468 Abd Khayr's son relates that his father saw Ali make ablution and wash the top of his feet, saying: had I not seen the Messenger of God do this, I would have thought the sole of the foot fitter than its top. The editor notes the chain is sound and that Ahmad reports it in his Musnad by several routes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 56}

Report 11469, from Ata: I have not seen anyone wipe the feet. Report 11470: Mujahid read 'arjulakum' in the accusative and said it returned to washing; report 11471 says al-A'mash read it in the accusative. In report 11472 Malik is asked whether the reading is 'arjulakum' or 'arjulikum' and says it is washing and not wiping, the feet are not wiped, they are washed; asked about one who wipes, whether it suffices him, he says no. Report 11473, from al-Dahhak: wash them thoroughly. Other readers from the Hijaz and Iraq read 'arjulikum' in the genitive; they understood that God commanded His servants to wipe the feet in ablution and not to wash them, and made the feet joined to the head, hence the genitive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 57}

Al-Tabari then gives the interpreters who held this. Report 11474, from Ibn Abbas: ablution is two washings and two wipings. Report 11475: Musa ibn Anas told Anas that al-Hajjaj preached to them at al-Ahwaz, mentioned purification, and said: wash your faces and hands, wipe your heads and feet, and that nothing of the son of Adam is nearer to his filth than his feet, so wash their undersides, tops and heels. Anas said: God spoke the truth and al-Hajjaj lied; God said 'and wipe your heads and your feet'. When Anas wiped his feet he wetted them. The editor notes that Musa ibn Anas was judge of Basra and that 'khabath' here means urine and excrement. Report 11476, from Anas: the Qur'an came down with wiping, and the sunna is washing. Report 11477, again from Musa ibn Anas, begins with the same account. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 58}

The rest of report 11477 gives al-Hajjaj's sermon: wash your faces, hands and feet, their tops, undersides and heels, for that is nearer to what removes your two filths; and Anas said: God spoke the truth and al-Hajjaj lied. Report 11478, from Ikrima: there is no washing on the feet, wiping alone was revealed for them. Report 11479, from Abu Ja'far: wipe your head and feet. Report 11480, from al-Sha'bi: Gabriel came down with wiping; do you not see that tayammum wipes what was washed and drops what was wiped? Reports 11481, 11482 and 11483, from al-Sha'bi (Amir), give a close sense: in tayammum he is commanded to wipe what he is commanded to wash in ablution, and what he is commanded to wipe is dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 59}

The rest of report 11483 says what is dropped in tayammum is what is commanded to be wiped in ablution: the head and the two feet. Report 11484, from al-Sha'bi, is similar. In report 11485 Isma'il tells Amir that some say Gabriel came down with the washing of the feet, and he answers that Gabriel came down with wiping. Report 11486, from one who accompanied Ikrima to Wasit: he never saw him wash his feet, he only wiped them. Report 11487, from Qatada: God made two washings and two wipings obligatory. Reports 11488 and 11489 say Alqama read 'arjulikum' with the lam in the genitive. The editor's note introduces Yahya ibn Waththab, the reciter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 60}

Report 11490: Mujahid read 'arjulikum' in the genitive; report 11491 says the same of al-Sha'bi, report 11492 of Abu Ja'far (Yazid ibn al-Qa'qa'), and report 11493 of al-Dahhak, with a kasra. Abu Ja'far (al-Tabari) says: the correct view in our opinion is that God commanded that the feet be wiped with water as a whole in ablution, as He commanded the wiping of the whole face with earth in tayammum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 61}

When the one making ablution does this to them he deserves the name of 'wiper and washer', since washing them is passing water over them or the water reaching them, and wiping them is passing the hand or what takes its place over them; whoever does this is both washer and wiper. And since 'wiping' can bear the two senses, general and particular, that is wiping part or wiping the whole, the readers differed over 'arjulakum': some put it in the accusative, taking the obligation on the feet to be washing and rejecting wiping them despite the many reports from the Prophet of a general wiping with water, and some put it in the genitive, taking the obligation to be wiping. Because he holds that general wiping of the feet with water is meant, some disliked that the one making ablution should be satisfied with putting his feet in water without wiping them with his hand or what replaces it. Report 11494: Tawus was asked about a man who makes ablution and puts his feet in the water and said: I do not count that as of any use. The editor notes there is an error in this chain that he could not resolve. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 62}

Those who allowed it did so taking washing to be meant. Report 11495, from al-Hasan, on a man making ablution in a boat: no harm in dipping his feet in. Report 11496, from al-Hasan, on one who makes ablution at the edge of a boat: he stirs his feet in the water. Al-Tabari then argues: since wiping has two senses, covering the feet with water and covering only part of them, and since, by the evidences he will give, God's intent in wiping them is covering, and covering them so has the sense of both washing and wiping, the correctness of both readings is clear, the accusative and the genitive. For covering the feet by wiping them with water is washing them, and passing the hand or what replaces it over them is wiping them. The accusative is sound because of its sense of covering by passing water over them, and the genitive because of its sense of passing the hand or its substitute over them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 63}

However, though both readings are good and sound, the one al-Tabari likes best is the genitive, for the reason given, that it joins both senses of wiping, and because it follows 'wipe your heads', so joining it to the heads, close by, is better than joining it to the hands, from which it is separated by 'wipe your heads'. If someone asks what proves that wiping the feet means covering and not partial, as with the head, the answer is the many reports that the Prophet said: woe to the heels and the soles of the feet from the fire. If wiping part of the foot sufficed there would be no woe on the part left unwiped, for one who has fulfilled what God made obligatory of washing has not earned woe; and the woe due to the heel of one who leaves it unwashed in ablution is the clearest proof that covering the whole foot with water is obligatory. He then gives the reports. Report 11497, from Muhammad ibn Ziyad: Abu Hurayra used to pass by as they made ablution from a water vessel and say: complete the ablution; Abu al-Qasim said: woe to the heel-tendons from the fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 64}

Reports 11498 to 11502 give the same report from Muhammad ibn Ziyad from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet: woe to the heels from the fire; in 11499 Abu Hurayra passes by people making ablution badly and tells them to complete it; reports 11501 and 11502 come through Hammad ibn Salama. Report 11503, from Suhayl from his father from Abu Hurayra, adds: on the Day of Resurrection. The editor notes that reports 11497 to 11502 are six routes for Muhammad ibn Ziyad's report, and that the hadith is sound, reported by al-Bukhari, Muslim and Ahmad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 65}

Report 11504, from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet: woe to the heels from the fire, with Isma'il's version 'woe to the heel-tendons'. In report 11505 Salim al-Dawsi went in with Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr to Aisha; he asked for water for ablution, and Aisha said: Abd al-Rahman, complete the ablution, for I heard the Messenger of God say: woe to the heels from the fire. Report 11506, from Abu Salim: he went out with Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr in the funeral of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, and they passed Aisha's room, and Abd al-Rahman asked for water for ablution, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 66}

and he heard Aisha call to him: Abd al-Rahman, complete the ablution, for I heard the Messenger of God say: woe to the heels from the fire. Report 11507, from Salim, client of Daws, has him hear Aisha say the same to her brother Abd al-Rahman. The editor sets out the several names under which Salim appears in the reports and notes that al-Bukhari said the form 'Abu Salim al-Mahri' is not sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 67}

Reports 11508 and 11509, from Abu Salama: Aisha saw Abd al-Rahman making ablution and said: complete the ablution, for I heard the Messenger of God say: woe to the heels; the second has 'woe to the heel-tendons from the fire'. Report 11510, from Abu Abdallah, client of Shaddad ibn al-Had: he went in to Aisha while Abd al-Rahman was with her; Abd al-Rahman made ablution, rose and turned away, and Aisha called him, and when he came to her she said: I heard the Messenger of God say: woe to the heels from the fire. The editor notes that Muslim, al-Tahawi and al-Bukhari in his history have it, and that he corrected the printed text, which had turned one man into two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 68}

Report 11511, from Jabir ibn Abdallah: the Messenger of God said: woe to the heel-tendons from the fire, by the route of Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i from Sa'id or Shu'ayb ibn Abi Karb. Report 11512 begins with the chain of Khallad ibn Aslam from al-Nadr from Shu'ba. The editor notes that al-Tabari gives this report by three routes from Abu Ishaq through report 11516 and that Ahmad has it in his Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 69}

We stop here at the foot of p. 69, at the start of report 11512, in the commentary on 'and your feet to the ankles' in verse 6; the rest follows in the next batch.
