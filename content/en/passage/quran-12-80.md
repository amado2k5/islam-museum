**Commentary on verse 80 (pp. 203-209)**

On "so when they despaired of him they withdrew to confer privately; the eldest of them said, do you not know that your father took a pledge from you in God's name, and before this you were remiss concerning Joseph? I will not leave the land until my father permits me or God decides for me, and He is the best of judges", al-Tabari says: "when they despaired of him" means when they despaired of Joseph releasing Benjamin and taking one of them in his place, and of his granting what they asked. "Istay'asu" is of the form "istaf'alu" from "ya'isa the man of such a thing". Report 19617, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: they despaired of him and saw how firm he was in his course. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 203}

On "they withdrew to confer privately", he says: they conferred with one another, no one else mingling with them. "Najiyy" is a group of men conferring; it is used for one and for a group, as one says "a just man" and "just men", "a people of visitors" and "of breakers of fast"; it is a verbal noun from "I conferred with so-and-so", turned into an attribute. Evidence is God's word "and We brought him near in private converse" in Sura Maryam, which describes one person with it, while here "they withdrew to confer" uses it of a group. "Najiyy" has the plural "anjiya", as Labid said in a verse in which he recalls attending the councils at al-Afaqa; the editor explains that the verse is from lines he addressed to his daughter Busra recalling his long life, that al-Afaqa is a place where the well-known day between Labid and al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad al-Absi took place, and that "ardaf al-muluk" refers to the "rdif" who accompanies a king and stands in for him when he rises from his seat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 204}

A group of men may also be called "najwa", as God said "when they are in private conference" and "there is no secret conference of three", meaning the people who confer; and "najwa" can also be a verbal noun, as God said "secret conferences are only from Satan", from which one says "najawtu anjuw najwa", being in this place the conferring itself. Al-Tabari cites a poet's verse in which a father tells his son to keep his secret when men confer in private, where "najwa" and "najiyy" have one meaning, conferring, and the two forms are put together. The editor says the poet is al-Saltan al-Abdi, from his well-known counsel to his son, and explains the two forms of "khibb". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 205}

The interpreters said the same: report 19618, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: "they withdrew to confer privately"; Shim'un, whom he had held as a pledge, is mentioned in this connection in the report; they were alone together, conferring among themselves. Report 19619, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they withdrew alone to confer. Report 19620, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: some of them were alone with others, then they said: what do you think? On "the eldest of them said", the people of knowledge differed over who was meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 205}

Some said: the eldest of them in intellect and knowledge, not in age, is meant, and he is Shim'un; they said Rubil was older than he by birth. Reports 19621 to 19624, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Ibn Jurayj: he is Shim'un, who stayed behind, and older than he, or than them, by birth was Rubil. Others said: rather the eldest in age is meant, who is Rubil: report 19625, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "the eldest of them" is Rubil, Joseph's brother, the son of his maternal aunt, who forbade them to kill him. Report 19626, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Rubil, who advised them not to kill him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 206}

Report 19627, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: "the eldest of them said", in age (the printed text and the manuscript read "in knowledge"; the editor says he found nothing to confirm the sense and judges "in age" to be meant), "your father took a pledge from you in God's name, and before this you were remiss concerning Joseph, so I will not leave the land"; so Rubil stayed in Egypt and the nine came back to Ya'qub and told him, and he wept and said: my sons, you never go but one of you is lost! You went once and lost Joseph, you went a second time and lost Shim'un, and now you have lost Rubil! Report 19628, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "when they despaired of him they withdrew to confer", saying, what do you think? And Rubil, as he was told, being the eldest of the men, said: do you not know that your father took a pledge from you in God's name that you would bring him back unless you were encompassed, and before this you were remiss concerning Joseph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 207}

Al-Tabari says: the soundest of the views is that of those who said "the eldest of them" means Rubil, since all of them agree that he was the oldest in age. The Arabs understand, when told "so-and-so is the eldest of the people" without qualification, only one of two meanings: either leadership and eminence over them, or age; for intellect they qualify it and say "he is their eldest in intellect", and when it is unqualified nothing but the first two is understood. The interpreters also said that Shim'un, though he held the place of knowledge and intellect that God gave him, had no leadership or eminence over his brothers, so it is known that he was not meant by "the eldest of them"; that being so, only the other sense remains, age. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 207}

Those al-Tabari has mentioned all said Rubil was the oldest of the men in age, which confirms the view he chose. On "do you not know that your father took a pledge from you in God's name", he says: do you not know, O people, that your father Ya'qub took from you God's covenants and pledges that you would bring him back to him together unless you were encompassed; "and before this you were remiss concerning Joseph", that is, before this deed of yours, your remissness with Joseph; or, did you not know before this your remissness with Joseph? Taken this way, "ma" is in the accusative. It is also possible that "and before this you were remiss concerning Joseph" is a fresh statement and what precedes a complete statement, so that "ma" is in the nominative, as though it said: and before this was your remissness with Joseph. It is also possible that "ma" is a redundant particle, so the sense is: and before this you were remiss with Joseph. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "mawthiq", says he added the text of the verse in one place, explains "sila", and corrects the printed wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 208}

On "I will not leave the land", the land where I am, Egypt, so as to depart from it, "until my father permits me" to leave it. Report 19629, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "I will not leave the land" where I am today until my father permits me to leave it. Report 19630, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: Shim'un said "I will not leave the land until my father permits me or God decides for me, and He is the best of judges". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 208}

On "or God decides for me", he says: or my Lord decrees for me to leave it and leave my brother Benjamin; otherwise I will not leave; "and He is the best of judges" means God is the best of those who judge and the most just of those who decide between people. Abu Salih used to say of it: report 19631, from Abu Salih, through al-Husayn ibn Yazid al-Subay'i, Abd al-Salam ibn Harb and Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: "until my father permits me or God decides for me", he said: by the sword. It is as though Abu Salih took "or God decides for me" to mean: or God decrees for me war against whoever prevented me from returning with my brother Benjamin to his father Ya'qub, so that I fight for him. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "hukm". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 209}
