**Commentary on verse 80 (pp. 203-206)**

On "so when they despaired of him they withdrew to confer privately; the eldest of them said, do you not know that your father took a pledge from you in God's name, and before this you were remiss concerning Joseph? I will not leave the land until my father permits me or God decides for me, and He is the best of judges", al-Tabari says: "when they despaired of him" means when they despaired of Joseph releasing Benjamin and taking one of them in his place, and of his granting what they asked. "Istay'asu" is of the form "istaf'alu" from "ya'isa the man of such a thing". Report 19617, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: they despaired of him and saw how firm he was in his course. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 203}

On "they withdrew to confer privately", he says: they conferred with one another, no one else mingling with them. "Najiyy" is a group of men conferring; it is used for one and for a group, as one says "a just man" and "just men", "a people of visitors" and "of breakers of fast"; it is a verbal noun from "I conferred with so-and-so", turned into an attribute. Evidence is God's word "and We brought him near in private converse" in Sura Maryam, which describes one person with it, while here "they withdrew to confer" uses it of a group. "Najiyy" has the plural "anjiya", as Labid said in a verse in which he recalls attending the councils at al-Afaqa; the editor explains that the verse is from lines he addressed to his daughter Busra recalling his long life, that al-Afaqa is a place where the well-known day between Labid and al-Rabi' ibn Ziyad al-Absi took place, and that "ardaf al-muluk" refers to the "rdif" who accompanies a king and stands in for him when he rises from his seat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 204}

A group of men may also be called "najwa", as God said "when they are in private conference" and "there is no secret conference of three", meaning the people who confer; and "najwa" can also be a verbal noun, as God said "secret conferences are only from Satan", from which one says "najawtu anjuw najwa", being in this place the conferring itself. Al-Tabari cites a poet's verse in which a father tells his son to keep his secret when men confer in private, where "najwa" and "najiyy" have one meaning, conferring, and the two forms are put together. The editor says the poet is al-Saltan al-Abdi, from his well-known counsel to his son, and explains the two forms of "khibb". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 205}

The interpreters said the same: report 19618, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: "they withdrew to confer privately"; Shim'un, whom he had held as a pledge, is mentioned in this connection in the report; they were alone together, conferring among themselves. Report 19619, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they withdrew alone to confer. Report 19620, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: some of them were alone with others, then they said: what do you think? On "the eldest of them said", the people of knowledge differed over who was meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 205}

Some said: the eldest of them in intellect and knowledge, not in age, is meant, and he is Shim'un; they said Rubil was older than he by birth. Reports 19621 to 19624, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Ibn Jurayj: he is Shim'un, who stayed behind, and older than he, or than them, by birth was Rubil. Others said: rather the eldest in age is meant, who is Rubil: report 19625, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "the eldest of them" is Rubil, Joseph's brother, the son of his maternal aunt, who forbade them to kill him. Report 19626, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Rubil, who advised them not to kill him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 206}

We stop here at the foot of p. 206, in the middle of the commentary on verse 80 (after report 19626 on who is meant by "the eldest of them"); the next batch begins at p. 207. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 206}
