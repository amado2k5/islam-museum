**Commentary on verse 39: "whoever repents after his wrongdoing and mends, God turns to him", pp. 298-300**

Al-Tabari says "whoever repents" among these thieves means returns from what God dislikes of disobeying Him to what He is pleased with of obeying Him, "after his wrongdoing", which is his transgression and doing what God forbade, stealing people's property, "and mends", meaning mends himself by making it do what it dislikes in obedience to God and repenting to Him of what he was in, of disobedience.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 298}

Mujahid is reported to have said his repentance here is the penalty applied to him, and report 11916, from Ibn 'Abbas, says "God turned to him" means the penalty, which the editor explains as God's turning to him after the penalty applied for his repentance. Report 11917, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr, says a woman stole jewellery and those she stole from came to the Prophet saying she had stolen from them; he said cut her right hand, and the woman asked if there was repentance, and he said that today she was free of her sin as on the day her mother bore her, and God revealed "whoever repents after his wrongdoing and mends, God turns to him". The editor says Ahmad's chain for it is sound and she was the Makhzumi woman whose story is firm in the two Sahihs.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 299}

"God turns to him" means God brings him back to what He loves and is pleased with from what He dislikes and is angered by of disobeying Him, and "God is forgiving and merciful" means He covers the sins of one who repented and turned from his sins to obedience by pardoning the punishment for them on the Day of Resurrection and not exposing him by them before witnesses, merciful to him and to His repenting servants.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 300}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
