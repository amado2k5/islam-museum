## Summary
Verse 37 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/nasi|intercalation (nasi')]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 37 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 37 (pp. 243-247)**

Al-Tabari says: intercalation is only an increase in unbelief. The word is a verbal noun from the saying "I prolonged your days" and "may God prolong your term", that is add to the days of your life; anything added to a thing is called by this word, which is why milk diluted with water is so called, and a pregnant woman, from the increase of the child in her, and why one says of driving a she-camel on that her pace may increase. It might also be a form of the pattern "fa'il" turned from "maf'ul", like "accursed" and "slain", meaning only the postponed month is an increase in unbelief. The first sense, he says, seems closer to the meaning: the postponement the polytheists make of the four sacred months, making their forbidden lawful and their lawful forbidden, is an increase in their unbelief and their denial of God's rulings and signs. Some reciters read the word without the hamza and without the lengthening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 243}

The reciters differed over "by which the unbelievers are led astray": most Kufans read it in the passive, meaning that God leads astray by the intercalation they innovated those who disbelieved; most of the reciters of Medina and Basra and some Kufans read it as "stray", meaning that those who disbelieved depart from the path God made for His servants to follow to His good pleasure; and it is reported of al-Hasan al-Basri that he read it as "lead astray", meaning that those who disbelieved lead people astray by the intercalation they instituted. Al-Tabari says the right view is that these are two well-known readings, each read by people of knowledge of the Qur'an, and close in sense, since one whom God leads astray is astray and one who strays does so by God's leading him astray and forsaking him, so whichever the reader reads he is right. The right reading of the word for intercalation is with the hamza, on the pattern "fa'il", as the widespread reading among the reciters of the cities. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 244}

As for "they make it lawful one year", it means those who disbelieve make the postponement lawful, the pronoun referring to it; the sense is that they make lawful for a year what they postponed forbidding of the four sacred months and forbid it another year "to match the number God forbade", that is to agree by their making lawful and forbidding with the number of months God forbade, "and so make lawful what God forbade; the evil of their deeds was made fair to them", that is the evil and ugly of their deeds, and what goes against God's command and obedience to Him, was made to seem good and dear to them; "and God does not guide the unbelieving people" means He does not grant success in fair and beautiful deeds with which God is pleased to those who deny His oneness and the prophethood of Muhammad, but forsakes them from guidance as He forsook these people over the sacred months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 245}

Report 16706, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the intercalation is that Junada ibn Awf ibn Umayya al-Kinani used to attend the season every year, his by-name being Abu Thumama, and would cry out that Abu Thumama is not reproached or faulted, and that the Safar of the first year was lawful this year, and the people made it lawful, so he forbade Safar one year and Muharram another; that is God's words that intercalation is only an increase in unbelief; they leave Muharram one year and forbid it the next. The editor refers to the accounts of the intercalators and of Junada in Ibn Hisham's Sira and the Muhabbar, says he was the intercalator against whom Islam came, and explains the spelling of the word for "faulted". Al-Tabari says this explanation of Ibn Abbas shows the soundness of the reading without the hamza and the lengthening, taking the word as a verb from "I forgot the thing", and from God's words at [[passage/quran-9-67|9:67]] "they forgot God so He forgot them", meaning they left God so He left them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 246}

Report 16707, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his grandfather: it is Muharram, which they forbade one year and Safar another, and another Safar was added among the sacred months; they forbade Safar once and permitted it once, and God reproached that; Hawazin, Ghatafan and Banu Sulaym did it. Report 16708, from Abu Wa'il, through Ibn Waki, Jarir and Mansur: the intercalator was a man of Banu Kinana, a man of counsel among them, who made Muharram into Safar for a year, so they raided in it and took booty, and forbade it for a year; report 16709 is similar, adding that this verse then came down. Report 16710, from Mujahid, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Idris and Layth: a man of Banu Kinana used to come every year at the season on a donkey of his and say, people, I am not reproached or faulted and what I say is not turned back; we have forbidden Muharram and put off Safar; and the next year he would say the same, saying we have forbidden Safar and put off Muharram; that is "to match the number God forbade", meaning the four, "and so make lawful what God forbade" by the postponing of that sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 247}

Report 16711, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: the intercalation was Muharram, which he forbade one year and Safar another, so Safar was the addition, and they put off the months until they made Safar into Muharram and made lawful what God forbade; Hawazin, Ghatafan and Banu Sulaym revered it and were those who did that in the pre-Islamic time. Report 16712, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: some people of error added Safar to the sacred months, and their spokesman would stand at the season and say that their gods had forbidden Muharram that year, so they forbade it that year, and the next year that their gods had forbidden Safar, so they forbade it, the two being called the two Safars; the first to intercalate were Banu Malik ibn Kinana, who were three, Abu Thumama Safwan ibn Umayya of Banu Fuqaym ibn al-Harith of Kinana; the report continues on the next page. The editor notes that the manuscript says three but names one, that Abu Thumama is called Junada ibn Awf in report 16706, and that other sources name al-Qalammas and Ibn Ishaq names the first to postpone the months among the Arabs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 247}
