## Summary
Verse 89 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 89 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-89|7:89]], al-Tabari says: Shuayb said to his people, when they called him to return to their creed and threatened to expel him and his followers from their town: "we should be forging a lie against God", that is, inventing a lie and conjecturing a falsehood against Him, "if we returned to your creed after God has saved us from it" by showing us its error and the rightness of the guidance we follow; "it is not for us to return to it unless God our Lord wills" means unless it has already been in God's knowledge that we return to it, so that His decree goes through us and His will is carried out; "our Lord embraces everything in knowledge" means His knowledge encompasses all, so nothing that was or is to be is hidden from Him, and if it was in His knowledge that we would return it must be, and otherwise we are not returning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 562}

(14858) from al-Suddi: it is not fitting for us to return to your associating partners after God has saved us from it unless God our Lord wills; God does not will polytheism, but we say, unless God has known something, for He embraces everything in knowledge. "In God we trust" means on Him we rely in our affairs and lean on Him against what you threaten us with of your polytheism, for He suffices whoever trusts in Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 563}

Then Shuayb turned to his Lord in prayer against his people when he despaired of their success and his hope of their submitting to God in obedience and acknowledging the message ended, fearing for himself and the believers who followed him that their transgressors would bring them ruin, by hastening the punishment. He said "Our Lord, open between us and our people with truth", that is, judge between us and them by Your just judgment in which there is no injustice or partiality, "and You are the best of openers", the best of judges. Al-Farra mentions that the people of Oman call a judge a "fatih" and "fattah"; Abu Ubayda says it is the dialect of Murad, and he cites a line in which the poet tells the Banu Isam he has no need of their judging, which the editor ascribes to al-Asar al-Jufi or Muhammad ibn Hamran. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 563}

(14859) from Ibn Abbas: I did not know what "Our Lord, open between us and our people with truth" meant until I heard the daughter of Dhu Yazan say, come and I will "open" you, meaning, I will go to law with you; (14860) from him that it means judge between us and our people; (14861) like the first; (14862) and (14863) from Qatada: judge between us and our people with the truth; (14864) from al-Suddi: judge between us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 564}

(14865) from al-Hasan al-Basri that "iftah" is "judge", and he sets it beside [[passage/quran-48-1|48:1]], meaning We have given judgment for you, a clear judgment; (14866) from Ibn Abbas that "iftah" is "judge"; and (14867) from him again that he did not know the phrase until he heard the daughter of Dhu Yazan say to her husband, come, I will "open" you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 565}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
