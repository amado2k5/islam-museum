## Summary
Verse 9 of [[passage/quran-14|Surat Ibrahim]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 9 of 52 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 9 (pp. 529-536)**

On "has not the report reached you of those before you, the people of Noah, Ad and Thamud, and those after them, none knows them but God? Their messengers came to them with clear proofs, but they put their hands to their mouths and said, we disbelieve in what you were sent with, and we are in doubt, disquieting, about what you call us to", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise, reporting Moses's words to his people: my people, "has not the report reached you of those before you", the report of those before you of the nations that passed before you, "the people of Noah, Ad and Thamud", the people of Noah being an explanatory substitute for "those" and "Ad" being conjoined to "the people of Noah", "and those after them", meaning after the people of Noah, Ad and Thamud, "none knows them but God", he says, none counts their number or knows their sum but God. Report 20590, from Amr ibn Maymun, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and Abu Ishaq: "and Ad and Thamud and those after them, none knows them but God", he said, the genealogists lie. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 529}

Report 20591, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Isra'il, Abu Ishaq and Amr ibn Maymun: the like. Report 20592, from Ibn Mas'ud, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Isra'il, Abu Ishaq and Amr ibn Maymun: he used to read it "wa-'adan wa-thamuda wa-alladhina min ba'dihim la ya'lamuhum illa Allah" and then say, the genealogists lie. Report 20593, from Abd Allah, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Amr ibn Maymun: the like; in a note the editor corrects the name of one of the narrators in the chain. On "their messengers came to them with clear proofs" he says: their messengers, whom God sent to them, came to these nations calling them to devote worship to Him, "with clear proofs", meaning with arguments and indications of the truth of what they called them to, miracles. On "but they put their hands to their mouths" the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said its meaning is: they bit their fingers in rage at them for their calling them to what they called them to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 530}

Report 20594, from Abd Allah, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: "they put their hands to their mouths", he said, they bit them in rage. Report 20595, from Abd Allah, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: "they put their hands to their mouths", he said, in rage, like this, and he bit his hand. Report 20596, from Abd Allah, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: he said, they bit them. Report 20597, from Abd Allah, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Raja' al-Basri, Isra'il, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: they bit their fingers; the editor notes that al-Hakim related it in the Mustadrak, calling it sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs, and al-Dhahabi agreed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 531}

Report 20598, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through al-Muthanna, al-Himmani, Sharik, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: "they put their hands to their mouths", he said, they bit the tips of their fingers. Report 20599, from Abd Allah, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba, Abu Ishaq and Hubayra: he said, that he puts his finger in his mouth. Report 20600, from Abd Allah, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abu Qatan, Shu'ba, Abu Ishaq and Hubayra: and Shu'ba put the tips of his left fingers on his mouth. Report 20601, from Abd Allah, through al-Hasan, Yahya ibn Abbad, Shu'ba, Abu Ishaq and Hubayra: he said, like this, and put his fingers into his mouth. Report 20602, from Abd Allah, through al-Hasan, Affan, Shu'ba, Abu Ishaq and Hubayra: and Affan showed us, putting the tips of the fingers of his spread palm in his mouth, and mentioned that Shu'ba showed it so; in the footnotes the editor identifies the narrators of these chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 532}

Report 20603, from Abd Allah, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan, Isra'il, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: "they put their hands to their mouths", he said, they bit their fingertips, and Sufyan said, they bit in rage. Report 20604, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: on "they put their hands to their mouths", he recited "they bite their fingertips at you in rage" [[passage/quran-3-119|3:119]], saying this is "they put their hands to their mouths", they put their fingers in their mouths; he said, when a person is enraged he bites his hand. Others said the meaning is that when they heard God's Book they marvelled at it and put their hands to their mouths. Report 20605, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "they put their hands to their mouths", he said, when they heard God's Book they marvelled and returned their hands to their mouths. Others said the meaning is that they called them liars with their mouths. Report 20606, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 533}

and Isa and Ibn Abi Najih, and al-Harith, al-Hasan and Waraqa: "they put their hands to their mouths", he said, they rejected their word to them and called them liars. Reports 20607 and 20608, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa, and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the like. Report 20609, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "their messengers came to them with clear proofs but they put their hands to their mouths", he says, their people called their messengers liars and rejected what they brought of the clear proofs, answering them with their mouths, and said "we are in doubt about what you call us to, disquieting". Report 20610, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: he said, they rejected to the messengers what they brought. Abu Ja'far says: it is as though Mujahid took "they put their hands to their mouths" to mean they rejected God's favours, which, had they accepted them, would have been favours and blessings with them, but they did not accept them, and took "in their mouths" to mean "with their mouths", that is, with their tongues in their mouths. It has been reported heard from some Arabs "God admit you 'bi-' Paradise", meaning "into" Paradise, and this verse is recited, the editor not knowing its author and its reciter being al-Farra', in which the author says he turns away "bi-" his daughter from Laqit and his clan but does not turn her away from his own tribe, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 534}

he meaning "I desire her", that is a daughter of his, "away from Laqit", and does not desire her away from my tribe. Others said the meaning is rather that they used to put their hands on the messengers' mouths, answering their speech and calling them liars. Others said this is a parable, and what is meant is that they held back from accepting the truth they were commanded to accept, and neither believed nor submitted. He said, one says to a man who holds back from answering and does not answer, "he returned his hand to his mouth", and some mentioned that the Arabs say "I spoke to so-and-so about a need and he returned his hand to his mouth" when he fell silent and did not answer; the editor notes that this is the saying of Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an. Abu Ja'far says: this too is a view with no ground, because God, mighty be His mention, told that they said "we disbelieve in what you were sent with", so they did answer, with denial. Abu Ja'far says: the most like correct of these views in my opinion on the interpretation of this verse is what we reported from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, that they returned their hands to their mouths {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 535}

and bit them in rage at the messengers, as God, mighty and exalted, described their brothers the hypocrites, saying "when they are alone they bite their fingertips at you in rage" [[passage/quran-3-119|3:119]]; this is the known speech and the understood meaning of "returning the hand to the mouth". On "and they said: we disbelieve in what you were sent with", He says, mighty and exalted: they said to their messengers, we disbelieve in what the one who sent you sent you with, of calling to leave off worshipping idols and images, "and we are in doubt" about the truth of what you call us to of God's oneness, "disquieting", he says, that doubt makes us uneasy, that is, it brings us suspicion and misgiving about it; one says "araba al-rajul" when he brings suspicion, "yuribu iraba". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 536}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
