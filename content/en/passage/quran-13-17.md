**Commentary on verse 17 (pp. 408-415)**

On "He sends down water from the sky, and valleys flow according to their measure, and the torrent carries a swelling foam; and from what they kindle in the fire, seeking ornament or utensils, comes a foam like it. Thus God strikes truth and falsehood; as for the foam, it goes as scum, and what benefits the people remains in the earth. Thus God strikes parables", al-Tabari says: this is a parable God struck for truth and falsehood, for faith in Him and disbelief. He says, mighty be His praise: the likeness of truth in its fixity and of falsehood in its vanishing is like water {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 408}

which God sent down from the sky to the earth; "and valleys flow according to their measure" means the valleys carried it according to their fullness, the large by its size and the small by its smallness; "and the torrent carries a swelling foam" means the torrent produced by that water God sent down from the sky carried a foam rising on top of the torrent. This is one of the two parables of truth and falsehood: the truth is the remaining water God sent down from the sky, and the foam that gives no benefit is falsehood. The other parable is "and from what they kindle in the fire, seeking ornament": He, mighty be His praise, says, and another parable for truth and falsehood is silver or gold which people kindle in the fire seeking ornament they will make or utensils, those being of copper, lead and iron kindled so that a utensil may be made from them to be used; "a foam like it" means that from what they kindle of these things comes a foam like the torrent's foam, giving no benefit and going to nothing, as the torrent's foam gives no benefit and goes to nothing. "Foam" is in the nominative by "from what they kindle in the fire", and the sense is: from what they kindle in the fire comes foam like the torrent's foam in the vanity of its foam, while pure gold and silver remain. God, exalted, says "thus God strikes truth and falsehood", meaning: as He likened faith and disbelief, in the vanity of disbelief and its owner's loss at God's reckoning, to the remaining, useful part of the torrent's water and the pure gold and silver, so He likens truth and falsehood; "as for the foam, it goes as scum" means the foam that rose on the torrent and on the gold, silver, copper and lead when they are kindled goes away, driven off by the winds, thrown by the water and clinging to the trees and the sides of the valley; "and what benefits the people" of the water, gold, silver and lead {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 409}

and copper; the water stays in the earth and is drunk by it, while gold and silver stay for the people. "Thus God strikes parables" he says: as He struck this parable for faith and disbelief, so He strikes parables. The interpreters said the same. Report 20311, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "He sends down water from the sky, and valleys flow according to their measure", this is a parable God struck: hearts carried of it according to their certainty and their doubt; with doubt no action is of benefit, while with certainty God benefits its people; that is His saying "as for the foam, it goes as scum", which is doubt, "and what benefits people remains in the earth", which is certainty, as jewelry is put in the fire and its pure part is taken and its dross is left in the fire; so God accepts certainty and leaves doubt. Report 20312, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "the torrent carries a swelling foam", he says, the torrent carried what was in the valley of wood and refuse; "and from what they kindle in the fire" is the gold, silver, jewelry, utensils, copper and iron, copper and iron having dross, and God made the likeness of their dross the foam of the water; what benefits the people is the gold and silver, and what benefits the earth is the water it drank and then put forth plants. He made that a parable of the righteous deed, which stays for its people, and the evil deed, which vanishes from its people as this foam goes; so too the guidance and the truth came from God, and whoever acts by the truth has it and it remains as what benefits people remains in the earth. Likewise iron cannot be made into a knife or a sword until it enters the fire and the fire eats its dross, so that its good part comes out and is of use, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 410}

and so falsehood vanishes on the Day of Resurrection when people are made to stand and deeds are shown: falsehood swerves and perishes, and the people of truth benefit by the truth. Report 20313, from al-Hasan, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Abu Raja': "seeking ornament", gold and silver, "or utensils", copper and iron, as when fire is kindled on gold, silver, copper and iron and the pure part is left; "thus God strikes truth and falsehood", so the truth remains for its people and they benefit from it. Report 20314, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Za'farani, Hajjaj ibn Muhammad, Ibn Jurayj and Abd Allah ibn Kathir: "valleys flow according to their measure", he said, what they could hold of their fullness; "the torrent carries a swelling foam", he said, the speech ended there; then it begins anew, "and from what they kindle in the fire seeking ornament or utensils, a foam like it", he said, the utensils are iron, copper, lead and the like, "a foam like it", the dross of those is like the foam of the torrent; "and what benefits the people remains in the earth" and "as for the foam, it goes as scum", he said, that is the parable of truth and falsehood. Report 20315, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Abd Allah ibn Kathir: similarly, adding that "goes as scum" means congealing in the earth, and "what benefits the people remains in the earth" means the water, the two being parables, the parable of truth and falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 411}

Report 20316, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "a swelling foam" is the torrent's, like the dross of iron and jewelry, "goes as scum" is congealing in the earth, and they are two parables of truth and falsehood. Reports 20317 and 20318, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, and Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa: "according to their measure", by their fullness; "a swelling foam", the foam is the torrent's; "seeking ornament or utensils, a foam like it", the dross of iron and jewelry; "goes as scum", congealing in the earth; "what benefits the people remains in the earth", the water; two parables of truth and falsehood. Report 20319, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "He sends down water from the sky, and valleys flow according to their measure", the small by its smallness and the large by its largeness, "a swelling foam", meaning rising; "jufa'" is what clings to the trees; these are three parables God struck in one: as this foam vanishes and becomes scum from which no benefit or blessing is hoped, so falsehood vanishes from its people; as this water stays in the earth and the earth becomes green and brings forth its plants, so the truth remains for its people; and in "from what they kindle in the fire", as pure gold and silver remain when entered into the fire and the dross goes, so the truth remains {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 412}

for its people; and "or utensils, a foam like it" he says is this iron and brass, which are of use and have benefits: as the pure part of this iron and this brass remains when entered into the fire and the dross goes, so the truth remains for its people as their pure part remains. Report 20320, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: similarly; "as for the foam, it goes as scum" clinging to the trees and becoming nothing, this is the parable of falsehood, "and what benefits the people remains in the earth", and this brings forth plants, which is the parable of truth. Report 20321, from Awf, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Hawdha ibn Khalifa: it reached me that this is a parable God struck for truth and falsehood, the small according to its measure, the large according to its measure and what is between according to its measure; "a swelling foam" he says means a great one; and where the water settles the foam goes as scum, the wind carries it off and it is nothing, and the pure water that benefits people remains, from which are their drink, their plants and their benefit; and the likeness of the foam is everything kindled in the fire, gold, silver, copper and iron, whose dross goes while what benefits remains in their hands; the dross and foam are the parable of falsehood, and what benefits people of what is gathered in their hands is the money in their hands. Report 20322, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: this is a parable God struck for truth and falsehood; this foam gives no benefit, and "or utensils, a foam like it" gives no benefit {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 413}

either; the water stayed in the earth and benefited the people, and the sound jewelry made from this stayed and the people benefited from it. Report 20323, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "valleys according to their measure", the small by its smallness and the large by its largeness. Report 20324, from Ata', through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Talha ibn Amr: God struck a parable for truth and falsehood, making the parable of the truth like the torrent that stays in the earth and the parable of falsehood like the foam that does not benefit people. By "rabiyan" al-Tabari means rising and swollen, from "raba al-shay' yarbu rubuwwan, fa-huwa rabin"; from it a rise in the ground like a hillock is called "rabiya", and from it God's words "it stirs and swells" [[passage/quran-41-39|41:39]]. Copper, lead and iron are called "mata'" in this place because they are enjoyed, and everything people enjoy is "mata'", as al-Mush'ath al-'Amiri said in a verse urging enjoyment of what one gets ahead of death; the editor notes that the rest of the lines describe what befalls him after his death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 414}

As for "jufa'": report 20325, I was told from Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna, who said that Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' said, one says "the pot has been emptied of its scum" when it boils and its foam spills out or when it settles and none is left. One of the Basran Arabic scholars claimed that "goes as scum" means the earth soaks it up, saying that "jafa al-wadi" and "ajfa" mean it dried up, and "injafa al-wadi" when it brought that flotsam; and from the Arabs it is related that they say "jafa'tu al-qidr ajfa'uha" when you remove its scum, which is the foam on its top, and "ajfa'tuha ijfa'an" is a dialect, and they said "jafa'tu al-rajul jafa'an", I threw him down. It is said that "goes as scum" is in the sense of "jafa", since it is a verbal noun from the saying "jafa'a al-wadi ghutha'ahu", and it came out in the form of a noun while being a verbal noun; so the Arabs do with the verbal noun of every action in which something has been gathered together, such as "qumash", "duqaq", "hutam" and "ghutha'", bringing it out in the noun form, as they do in saying "a'taytuhu 'ata'an" meaning giving; had the true verbal noun of "qumash" been meant it would be said "qamashahu qamshan". The editor notes that some of these statements have no parallel in the dictionaries. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 415}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
