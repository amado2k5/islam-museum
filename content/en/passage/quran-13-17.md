**Commentary on verse 17 (pp. 408-409)**

On "He sends down water from the sky, and valleys flow according to their measure, and the torrent carries a swelling foam; and from what they kindle in the fire, seeking ornament or utensils, comes a foam like it. Thus God strikes truth and falsehood; as for the foam, it goes as scum, and what benefits the people remains in the earth. Thus God strikes parables", al-Tabari says: this is a parable God struck for truth and falsehood, for faith in Him and disbelief. He says, mighty be His praise: the likeness of truth in its fixity and of falsehood in its vanishing is like water {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 408}

which God sent down from the sky to the earth; "and valleys flow according to their measure" means the valleys carried it according to their fullness, the large by its size and the small by its smallness; "and the torrent carries a swelling foam" means the torrent produced by that water God sent down from the sky carried a foam rising on top of the torrent. This is one of the two parables of truth and falsehood: the truth is the remaining water God sent down from the sky, and the foam that gives no benefit is falsehood. The other parable is "and from what they kindle in the fire, seeking ornament": He, mighty be His praise, says, and another parable for truth and falsehood is silver or gold which people kindle in the fire seeking ornament they will make or utensils, those being of copper, lead and iron kindled so that a utensil may be made from them to be used; "a foam like it" means that from what they kindle of these things comes a foam like the torrent's foam, giving no benefit and going to nothing, as the torrent's foam gives no benefit and goes to nothing. "Foam" is in the nominative by "from what they kindle in the fire", and the sense is: from what they kindle in the fire comes foam like the torrent's foam in the vanity of its foam, while pure gold and silver remain. God, exalted, says "thus God strikes truth and falsehood", meaning: as He likened faith and disbelief, in the vanity of disbelief and its owner's loss at God's reckoning, to the remaining, useful part of the torrent's water and the pure gold and silver, so He likens truth and falsehood; "as for the foam, it goes as scum" means the foam that rose on the torrent and on the gold, silver, copper and lead when they are kindled goes away, driven off by the winds, thrown by the water and clinging to the trees and the sides of the valley; "and what benefits the people" of the water, gold, silver and lead {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 409}

We stop here at the foot of p. 409, in the middle of the commentary on verse 17 (at "and what benefits the people"); the next batch begins at p. 410 (marker 418). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 409}
