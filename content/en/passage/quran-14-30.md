## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains 'al-Andal' as 'partners,' which is the plural of 'Nad', and points out that he clarified its meaning with previous evidences, and he quotes Qatadah that 'al-Andalu' refers to the partners. He presents the difference among the reciters in the recitation of 'Li Dhulahu': The majority of the Kufan reciters read it with the affirmative 'li dhulu' (to mislead), meaning 'to mislead' meaning to mislead people from the path of Allah, meaning to mislead people from the path of Allah. The general recitation of 'Li Dhula'u' is 'to mislead' with the nominative meaning 'so that those who make the equals for Allah' may be misguided. Then he says in 'Say Tamattu': It is a command, a threat, a rebuke, and a rebuke, with no permission for them to enjoy worldly pleasure or worship. The evidence is his attachment to 'for your destiny is to the Fire': the world is fleeting for them, and they will go to the Fire, and they will know there the absence of the sins they enjoyed from Allah.
