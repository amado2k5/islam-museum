## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "We gave Moses the Book and followed him with messengers", the Book is the Torah, "qaffayna" means We sent one after another, from "qafa" the nape, and "the messengers" are the prophets, meaning that all who were sent after Moses until the time of Jesus were sent to uphold the Torah on his path. On "and We gave Jesus son of Mary the clear proofs", they are what God showed by his hands of proofs, such as raising the dead and healing the born blind; he reports from Ibn Abbas raising the dead, fashioning from clay the likeness of a bird that became a bird by God's leave, healing illnesses and telling of what people stored in their homes. "We strengthened him" means We made him strong and helped him, reported from al-Dahhak, with a verse of al-'Ajjaj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 317} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 319}

On "the Holy Spirit" al-Tabari lists three views. Some said it is Gabriel, reported from Qatada, al-Suddi, al-Dahhak and al-Rabi'; he also gives a report from Shahr ibn Hawshab that some Jews asked the Prophet about the Spirit and he adjured them by God whether they knew it to be Gabriel, who comes to him, and they agreed (the editor notes the report is mursal). Ibn Zayd said it is the Gospel, as the Qur'an too is called a spirit. From Ibn Abbas comes the view that it is the name by which Jesus revived the dead. Al-Tabari prefers Gabriel, because the verse in al-Ma'ida (5:110) joins "I strengthened you with the Holy Spirit" to "I taught you the Book, wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel"; were the Spirit the Gospel, the two phrases would repeat each other to no purpose, though all God's books are a spirit from Him by which hearts are given life. Gabriel is called a spirit and linked to "quds", which is purity, because he was brought into being as a spirit from God without a father, as Jesus is also called a spirit of God. On the meaning of "quds", he reports al-Suddi (blessing), and Ibn Zayd and Ka'b (God Himself), with the name "al-Quddus" cited. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 321} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 322} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 323}
