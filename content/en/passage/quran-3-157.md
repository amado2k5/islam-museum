## Summary
Verse 157 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 157 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 157: death and killing in God's way, and what is gathered of this world (p. 337).** Abu Ja'far: God addresses His believing servants, forbidding them to doubt that all affairs are in His hand and that giving life and death belongs to Him, as the hypocrites doubted, and commands them to fight and to battle His enemies with certainty that no one is killed in war or dies on a journey except one whose term has come. He then promised them forgiveness and mercy for their fighting in His way and told them that death or killing in His way is better for them than what they gather in this world of its fleeting goods and comfortable living, for the sake of which they are slow to fight and hold back from meeting the enemy. Ibn Ishaq (8117): death will come and cannot be avoided, so death in God's way or being killed is better, if they knew and were certain, than what they gather in this world, for the sake of which they hold back from fighting out of fear of death and killing. In the footnotes the editor corrects the printed text by the manuscript and by Ibn Hisham (3: 123, the continuation of 8116), explains 'zahrat al-dunya' as its beauty, brilliance, freshness, abundance of good and comfortable living, and notes that the Sira has 'zahada fi al-akhira' without the 'wa'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 337}

**The omitted answer to the condition (pp. 337-338).** Abu Ja'far: the sentence begins with the conditional particle and omits its answer, because the clause on forgiveness and mercy that follows carries the sense of the answer, being a promise in the form of a statement. His reading is: if you are killed in God's way or die, God will forgive you and have mercy on you; He pointed to this by what He said, and joined to the pointer the report of its superiority over what they prefer of this world and gather in it. He relates that a certain Basran grammarian was asked how forgiveness and mercy could be an answer, and said the way is to take it as: if you die or are killed, that is mercy and forgiveness for you, since it is in My way, and that is better than what you gather; the lam entered that clause because it entered the conditional, as in the verse of Surat al-Hashr [[passage/quran-59-12|59:12]] (the scan gives the surah name garbled as al-Mashr). The footnotes (pp. 337-338) record the editor's corrections of the printed text and the manuscript: 'fa-yukhatibu' to 'yukhatibu', 'wa-qatlan' to 'qatlan', 'jaza'' to 'jawab' for the answer of the conditional, 'jawaz' to 'jawab'. In the footnote on p. 338 he says the sentence of the Basran grammarian, as it stands in the printed text and manuscript, has hardly any sense; he replaces 'al-qawl' with 'al-wajh' with certainty, suspects the copyist misread the old manuscript before 'rahma', and reads the last word by a light stroke over its alif, after which the sentence is coherent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 337} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 338}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 338}
