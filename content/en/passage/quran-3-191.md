## Summary
Verse 191 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 191 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 191: those who remember (pp. 474-475, reports 8354-8355).** Al-Tabari says this describes the people of understanding mentioned before it, and stands in the genitive as a substitute for them; the meaning is that in the creation and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of understanding who remember God standing in their prayer, sitting in their tashahhud and outside their prayer, and lying on their sides. Ibn Jurayj (8354), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: it is remembering God in prayer and outside prayer, and reciting the Qur'an. Qatada (8355), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: these are all your states, son of Adam, so remember Him while on your side, as an ease and a lightening from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 475}

**A question of grammar (p. 475).** Al-Tabari raises a question: how is a prepositional phrase, which is an attribute, joined to standing and sitting, which are nouns? He answers that the phrase is in the sense of a noun, meaning sleeping or lying down, so joining it to them is good, and he cites another verse, [[passage/quran-10-12|10:12]], in which the sitting and the standing are joined to what means lying. The editor's note says that for al-Tabari the "attribute" is the preposition, as in many earlier places (1: 299 note 1), and refers to al-Farra' (1: 250). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 475}

**Reflecting on creation (p. 475).** Al-Tabari says they take a lesson from the work of its maker and know that none makes it but one with no likeness, the owner of everything, its provider, creator, director and the one able over it, in whose hand are enriching and impoverishing, honouring and humbling, giving life and death, misery and happiness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 475}

**What they say (p. 476).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that they reflect, saying; the word "saying" is omitted because the speech shows it. By their denial of vanity he understands that creation was not made in vain or in play but for a great matter of reward, punishment, reckoning and recompense. He holds that the singular pointing word means the creation in the heavens and earth, and what shows this is their appeal to their Lord to guard them from the punishment of Hell; had the meaning been the heavens and earth themselves, what follows would have no intelligible sense, since they are evidence of their Maker, not of reward and punishment, and the evidence of reward and punishment is command and prohibition. So the person of understanding, on seeing those under command and prohibition, says they were not created in vain, which is declaring God above doing anything in vain, and that they were created for a great matter, Paradise or the Fire; then they turn to Him asking that He protect them from the punishment and not make them among those who disobeyed Him and went against His command, so as to be among the people of Hell. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 476}
