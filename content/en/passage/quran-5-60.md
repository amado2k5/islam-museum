**Commentary on verse 60: "Say: shall I tell you of something worse than that as a reward with God", pp. 435-439**

Al-Tabari says: God tells His prophet to say to those of the People of the Book and the unbelievers who took your religion as mockery and play: shall I tell you, People of the Book, of a worse reward than what you resent in us, our belief in God, in what was sent down to us and what was sent down before us? The word for reward is a passive form whose middle letter dropped and passed its vowel to the first, coming out like other words of that shape, for which he cites a line of poetry. The editor deals in a long note with a gap in the manuscript at this point and explains the line and its words.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 435}

Report 12220, from al-Suddi, says the reward means a recompense with God, and report 12221, from Ibn Zayd, that recompense can be in good and in evil, citing a verse of the surah of the cave on recompense. The editor notes that number 12222 was skipped in the numbering by oversight.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 436}

Al-Tabari says "whom God cursed" is in the genitive, referring back to "worse than that", so the sense is: shall I tell you of something worse than that as a reward with God, of one whom God cursed. It would also be correct to take it as nominative, beginning a new clause, and not wrong to take it as accusative governed by "tell you". "Whom God cursed" means whom God drove far from His mercy; "was angry with and made of them apes and swine" means He made of them transformed beings in anger and displeasure, hastening disgrace and exemplary punishment for them in this world. As for the reason some were made apes, he has given part of it earlier and will give the rest elsewhere.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 437}

On the reason some were made swine, report 12223, from Umar ibn Kathir ibn Aflah, says he was told that a woman of the Children of Israel in a town where their king lived, when the people had all drifted toward ruin, still held to a remnant of true religion and called people to God; whenever a group gathered to her she told them they must strive for God's religion and go out, the king went out against them and killed them all while she escaped, and this happened three times until she despaired and said that if this religion had a protector and helper he would have made it prevail. She went to bed grieving, and in the morning the townspeople were running about as swine, transformed by God in that night, and she said: today I know that God has given His religion strength. The editor gives the narrator's biography and corrects his name.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 438}

Reports 12224 and 12225, from Mujahid, say the apes and swine were transformed from the Jews. Al-Tabari notes that another reason for the transformation has been given, which he will set out in its place.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 439}

**Commentary on verse 60, continued: "and worshipped the idol", and its readings, pp. 439-441**

Al-Tabari notes that the readers differed over "and worshipped the idol". The readers of the Hijaz, Syria, Basra and some of Kufa read it as a past verb with the idol in the accusative, in the sense "and made of them those who worshipped the idol". A group of Kufans read it as a noun with the idol in the genitive by annexation, meaning "and the servants of the idol"; reports 12226 and 12227, from Yahya ibn Waththab and al-A'mash, say they read it so.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 439}

Al-Farra said that if there is a dialectal form like those of two other adjectives it is a possible way, but otherwise the use in a poet's line against the Banu Lubna is poetic licence, allowed in verse for the rhyme but not in recitation. Others read it with two u-vowels as a plural of a plural of "slave", as with a word for fruits; this is reported of al-A'mash. It is reported of Abu Ja'far the reader that he read it as a passive with the idol in the nominative, as one says "Abdullah was struck", in report 12228.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 440}

Al-Tabari holds this last reading meaningless, because God began the report by blaming a people, and their worship of the idol was part of the blame, whereas a report that the idol was worshipped is not of the kind with which the verse begins or ends. He notes that Buraida al-Aslami read "and the worshipper of the idol", in report 12229. Had it been read as a verb form with the idol in the genitive it would have a sound place in Arabic, meaning "the worshippers of the idol" with the feminine ending dropped for annexation, and he cites a line of rajaz for such dropping, though he does not permit reciting it so, since the reading of the authoritative readers is otherwise. He then says the readers' reading is one of the two ways he mentioned first.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 441}

We stop here at the foot of p. 441, where al-Tabari says the readers' reading is one of the first two ways; the rest of verse 60 follows in the next batch. The pages of commentary end at p. 585, before the indexes beginning at p. 587.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
