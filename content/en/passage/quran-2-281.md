## Summary
Verse 281 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 281 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-281|2:281]]), pp. 39 to 40. Al-Tabari says it was said that this verse too is the last revealed in the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 39} 6311, Ibn Abbas (through Ikrima): the last verse revealed to the Prophet is the one that commands guarding against a day of return to God; 6312, Ibn Abbas (through his family chain): it is the last verse of the Book revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 40} The editor gives biographies of the transmitters of the first chain (Abu Tumayla Yahya ibn Wadih, al-Husayn ibn Waqid, and Yazid al-Nahwi, whom he says Abu Muslim killed in 131 for commanding the good), grades the chain sound, notes that the apparent sense conflicts with report 6310, and quotes Ibn Hajar's harmonization in Fath al-Bari that this verse closes the verses revealed on usury because it is joined to them, as al-Bukhari's chapter heading suggests; this is all the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 40} These are reports as transmitted, with no museum verdict.

6313, Atiya: the last verse revealed was the one about returning to God; the editor says Sahl ibn Amir in its chain is very weak and that copyists confused his name in manuscript and print. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 41} 6314, al-Suddi: the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 41} 6315, Ibn Abbas by two routes (al-Dahhak; Ibn Jurayj): it was the last verse revealed in the Qur'an; Ibn Jurayj added that people say the Prophet lived nine nights after it, fell ill on a Saturday and died on the Monday; the editor reads the word for "fell ill" in that sense, disagrees with another explainer of it, and cites Aisha's words about the day the Prophet fell ill, which is the editor's note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 41} 6316, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib: it reached him that the latest part of the Qur'an in relation to the Throne is the verse of debt ([[passage/quran-2-282|2:282]]); the editor judges the chain to Ibn al-Musayyib sound but the report weak for lack of a link above him, and notes Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 41}

Abu Ja'far explains the verse as a warning: beware, people, a day when you are returned to God and meet Him with misdeeds that destroy you, shame you, expose you or ruin you, bringing a punishment you cannot bear. It is a day of recompense for deeds, not a day of seeking favour, retraction or repentance, a day when each soul is paid in full for what it put forward of bad and good; no small or great deed is left out, each is brought and repaid with justice, and none is wronged. How could one be wronged who is repaid evil with its like and good tenfold? Rather it is justice for the wrongdoer and generosity for the doer of good; so let a person fear his Lord and take heed lest the day come upon him with his back heavy with burdens and his good works light, for God warned and left no excuse, advised and conveyed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 42} The editor's notes list differences between manuscript and print in this passage and fault the print for changing what the manuscript had. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 42}
