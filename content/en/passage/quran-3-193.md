## Summary
Verse 193 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 193 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 193: who is the caller? (pp. 480-481, reports 8361-8365).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the caller mentioned. Some said it is the Qur'an. Muhammad ibn Ka'b (8361), through al-Muthanna from Qabisa ibn 'Uqba from Sufyan from Musa ibn 'Ubayda: it is the Book, and not all of them met the Prophet (the last word is garbled in the scan and the sense follows from the context). Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi (8362), through al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Mansur ibn Hakim from Kharija from Musa ibn 'Ubayda: not all people heard the Prophet, but the caller is the Qur'an. The editor's note says Qabisa ibn 'Uqba ibn Muhammad al-Suwa'i came earlier at reports 2792 and 489, is reliable and is used by the Six, though some criticised his narration from Sufyan al-Thawri, whom he heard as a boy, saying he erred in some of it; that Musa ibn 'Ubayda ibn Nashit al-Rabadhi is very weak and came at reports 1875, 1876 and 3291; and that he did not know Mansur ibn Hakim or Kharija, found no entry for them and fears distortion in the two names. Others said it is Muhammad. Ibn Jurayj (8363), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: it is Muhammad. Ibn Zayd (8364), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: that is the Messenger of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 481}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 481-482).** Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two views is Muhammad ibn Ka'b's, that the caller is the Qur'an, because many of those God described in these verses never saw the Prophet or met him to hear his call; they heard the Qur'an, like what God reports of the jinn when they heard His speech recited to them ([[passage/quran-72-1|72:1]] - [[passage/quran-72-2|72:2]]). Qatada (8365), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: they heard a call from God, answered it well and were patient with it; God tells you of the believer among humans and the believer among the jinn, how each spoke, and he then gave what each said. Al-Tabari adds that it was said the lam in "to belief" has the sense of "to", as in another verse ([[passage/quran-7-43|7:43]]) meaning "guided us to this", and as a rajaz poet (al-'Ajjaj, says the editor) used a verb meaning "inspired to her" for "inspired her", compare [[passage/quran-99-5|99:5]] (the editor refers to earlier places, 1: 169 and 6: 405). It was also said the sense may be: we heard a caller for belief calling that they believe in their Lord (the note refers to al-Farra' 1: 250 and Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an 1: 111). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 482}

**The meaning of the call (p. 482).** Al-Tabari says the sense of the verse is: Lord, we heard a summoner calling to belief, that is to trust in You, acknowledge Your oneness, follow Your messenger and obey him in what he brought from You of command and prohibition, and we trusted in that; so cover our faults and do not expose us with them on the Day of Gathering before the witnesses by punishing us for them, but expiate them for us and wipe out our evil deeds by Your favour and mercy; and take us to You among the abrar and gather us in their gathering and with them. The abrar are the plural of barr, those who were dutiful to God by obeying and serving Him until they pleased Him and He was pleased with them (the editor refers to earlier explanations of the word; the references are garbled in the scan). These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 482}
