## Summary
Verse 104 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 104 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says this is a command from God to His prophet Muhammad to say to those whom He had alerted by the preceding verses, from "God splits the grain and the date-stone" to "and He is the Subtle, the All-Aware", those who set up idols and rivals to Him and deny God and His messenger: "enlightenment has come to you from your Lord", that is, what you see by, to tell guidance from error and belief from unbelief. The footnotes correct wordings in the print and manuscript and explain the grammar of the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 23}

"Basa'ir" is the plural of "basira", meaning the clear, evident proof (the editor refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, and cites a line of al-As'ar al-Ju'fi from a poem in which he reproaches his half-brothers, who took the blood-money for their father's killing and consumed it, until he himself took the vengeance; he adds that the lexicographers explain "basira" there as blood). Report 13703, from Ibn Zayd: the "basa'ir" are guidance, insights in their hearts for their religion, not insights of the head, and he recited 22:46 (it is not the eyes that go blind but the hearts in the breasts); report 13704, from Qatada: that is, a clear proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 24}

On "whoever sees, it is for his own soul", he says: whoever takes in the proofs of God, knows them, acknowledges them and believes in what they show of God's oneness and the truth of His messenger, has gained his own share, worked for himself and sought good for himself; "and whoever is blind, it is against it": whoever draws no inference from them and does not believe what they show of belief in God, His messenger and His revelation, but turns blind to what they point to, harms his own soul and wrongs it, not another. On "and I am not a guardian over you" he says: I am no watcher who counts your deeds against you; I am a messenger who conveys what I was sent with, and God is the guardian over you, from whom none of your deeds is hidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 25}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
