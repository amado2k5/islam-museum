**Commentary on verse 167 (pp. 204-207)**

Al-Tabari says "and when your Lord proclaimed" means: remember, Muhammad, when your Lord announced and made known; the verb is a reflexive form of the verb for announcing. He cites a line by al-A'sha, Maymun ibn Qays, in which he says that his neighbours announced that day their departure; the editor gives the wording of the line in the divan and the sense of the word for departing, which also occurs in the Prophet's sermon in his illness in the sense of a departure drawing near. He refers to his earlier explanation, with evidence, of the verb for giving permission or notice. Reports 15297 and 15298, from Mujahid: "your Lord commanded". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 204}

On "that He would send against them", he explains: your Lord announced that He would send against the Jews those who would inflict on them an evil punishment. It is said that these are the Arabs, whom God sent against the Jews, to fight those of them who did not become Muslim and did not pay the jizya, and for those who paid the jizya that was an abasement and humiliation. The people of interpretation say the same. Report 15299, from Ibn Abbas: it is the jizya, and those who inflict it are Muhammad and his community until the Day of Resurrection. Report 15300: it is poverty and the taking of the jizya from them. Report 15301: they are the Jews and the humiliation and poverty set upon them. Report 15302, from Qatada: God sent against them this tribe of the Arabs, and they are in torment from them until the Day of Resurrection; so too report 15303, in which Ma'mar passes on a saying of Abd al-Karim al-Jazari that it is recommended that the Nabateans be sent for the jizya, which report 15308 gives from Ibn al-Musayyib. The editor suspects that the sense of the verb for sending is better taken as "dispatch". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 204} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 205} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 206}

Reports 15304 and 15305, from Sa'id: they are the Arabs, and the "evil punishment" is the land tax (kharaj); the first to impose the kharaj was Moses, who collected it for seven years. Report 15306, from Sa'id: they are the People of the Book; God sent the Arabs against them to collect the kharaj until the Day of Resurrection, and that is the evil punishment; no prophet ever collected the kharaj except Moses, for thirteen years and then he stopped, and the Prophet. Report 15307, from Qatada, as before. Report 15309, from al-Suddi: your Lord sends the Arabs against the Children of Israel, who inflict an evil punishment, taking the jizya from them and killing them. Report 15310, from Ibn Zayd: "He will send against the Jews". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 206} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 207}

Of "your Lord is swift in punishment, and He is forgiving, merciful", al-Tabari says: your Lord, Muhammad, is swift to punish whoever deserves punishment from Him for disbelief in Him and disobedience; and He is one who forgives the sins of whoever repents of them, turns back and returns to obedience, covering them by pardoning them, merciful to him in not punishing him for his offence after his repentance, because He accepts repentance and overlooks the slip. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "swift in punishment", "forgiving" and "merciful". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 207}

We stop here at the foot of p. 207, at the close of the commentary on verse 167; the next batch begins at p. 208. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 207}
