## Verse 17 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 264-267)

Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-17|3:17]]) says of these descriptions that the patient are those who endured in hardship and affliction and at the time of battle; the truthful are those who were true to God in their words by making real their acknowledgement of Him, His messenger and what he brought, by acting on what He commanded and refraining from what He forbade; and the obedient are those who obey Him. He had explained all these words earlier with evidence and reports, so he need not repeat them. In 6752, Qatada (Bishr, Yazid, Said): the truthful are people whose mouths were true and whose hearts and tongues were upright, true in secret and in public; the patient are people who bore with obedience to God and bore from His forbidden things; the obedient are those who obey God; and the spenders are those who pay the zakat of their wealth and put it where God commanded, spending their wealth in the ways He allowed. Al-Tabari says that all these descriptions are in the genitive, following the relative clause in the preceding verse, and that the genitive shows that clause itself follows the phrase for the God-fearing with their Lord. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of these words and to al-Farra (1: 199). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 264-265}

Al-Tabari then records the interpreters' difference over who are described as seeking forgiveness at the pre-dawn hours, in three views.

The first is that they are those who pray at those hours. In 6753, Qatada (Bishr, Yazid, Said): the people of prayer. In 6754, Qatada (al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father): they pray at the pre-dawn.

The second is that they are those who ask forgiveness. In 6755, Ibrahim ibn Hatib from his father (Ibn Waki, his father, Hurayth ibn Abi Matar): I heard a man at the pre-dawn in a corner of the mosque say: My Lord, You commanded me and I obeyed You, and this is the pre-dawn, so forgive me; I looked, and it was Ibn Mas'ud. In 6756, Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid ibn Jabir (al-Muthanna, Ishaq, al-Walid ibn Muslim), from Sulayman ibn Musa, from Nafi: Ibn Umar used to pass the night in prayer and ask, Nafi, has the pre-dawn come? If Nafi said no he went back to prayer; if yes he sat asking forgiveness and supplicating until morning. In 6757, Anas ibn Malik (Ibn Waki, his father, some Basrans): we were commanded to ask forgiveness seventy times at the pre-dawn. In 6758, Ja'far ibn Muhammad (al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Zayd ibn al-Hubab, Abu Ya'qub al-Dabbi): whoever prays in the night and then asks forgiveness seventy times at its end is written among those who seek forgiveness at the pre-dawn.

The third is that they are those who attend the dawn prayer in congregation. In 6759, Zayd ibn Aslam (al-Muthanna, Isma'il ibn Maslama, brother of al-Qa'nabi, Ya'qub ibn Abd al-Rahman). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 265-267}

In his notes on report 6755 the editor writes that Hurayth ibn Abi Matar, Amr al-Fazari, Abu Amr al-Hannat, narrated from al-Sha'bi and al-Hakam ibn Utayba and Sharik, Ibn Numayr and Waki narrated from him; that Ibn Ma'in said of him 'nothing', Abu Hatim 'weak in hadith', and al-Bukhari 'there is something in him, not strong in their view'; that al-Bukhari gave a hanging report of his in the chapter on sacrifices; and that he is in al-Tahdhib. He found no biography of Ibrahim ibn Hatib or his father and fears a corruption or omission in the name, and that this Hatib may be Hatib ibn Abi Balta'a the Companion; the same report is in Ibn Kathir (2: 113), who gave no comment. In his note on 6759 he says the brother of Isma'il is Abdullah ibn Maslama ibn Qa'nab al-Harithi al-Qa'nabi, teacher of al-Bukhari, Muslim and Abu Dawud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 266-267}

Al-Tabari's own choice is that the best of these views is that they are the ones who ask their Lord to cover their disgrace at the pre-dawn hours, a word that is the plural of 'sahar', and that the most apparent sense is that their asking is by supplication, though it may mean that they expose themselves to His forgiveness by deeds and prayer, the supplication being the most apparent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 267}
