## Summary
Verse 48 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 48 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 48 (pp. 7-12)**

Al-Tabari says that by "when Satan made their deeds seem fair to them" God means when Satan made their deeds look good to them, in the manner reported. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "made fair" and notes that the publisher dropped the words "and Satan" from the first report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 7}

Report 16183, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: Iblis came on the day of Badr with a force of devils carrying his banner, in the form of a man of the Banu Mudlij, while the devil took the form of Suraqa ibn Malik ibn Ju'shum, and told the idolaters nobody could defeat them that day and he was their protector. When the ranks formed, the Messenger of God took a handful of dust and threw it in the faces of the idolaters and they turned back. Gabriel advanced on Iblis; seeing him, with his hand in the hand of one of the idolaters, Iblis pulled his hand away and fled with his party. The man said, Suraqa, do you claim to be our protector? and he answered that he saw what they did not see, which was when he saw the angels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 7}

Report 16184, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: Iblis came to the idolaters on a horse in the form of Suraqa ibn Malik ibn Ju'shum of the Banu Mudlij, and said nobody could defeat them that day; asked who he was, he said he was their neighbour Suraqa and that the Kinana had come to them. Report 16185, from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq and Yazid ibn Ruman: when Quraysh resolved to march they remembered the war between them and the Banu Bakr, and this nearly turned them back; Iblis appeared to them as Suraqa, one of the nobles of Kinana, and said he was their protector against Kinana coming at them with anything they disliked, so they set out quickly. The editor notes the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and that the publisher altered what the manuscript has. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 8}

Report 16186, from Ibn Ishaq on the verse: it recounts Iblis leading them on and taking the shape of Suraqa when they recalled their war with the Banu Bakr; when the two hosts came into sight and the enemy of God saw God's forces of angels, with which He had strengthened His messenger and the believers, he turned on his heels. He spoke truly that he saw what they did not, and said he feared God; he led them in and then abandoned them. Ibn Ishaq adds that they were told the idolaters used to see him at every halt in the form of Suraqa without denying it, until on the day of Badr the man who saw him turn back was al-Harith ibn Hisham or Umayr ibn Wahb al-Jumahi, who said, "Where to, Suraq?", and the enemy of God stood up and went. The editor traces the report in the Sira and lists what the publisher changed in its wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 9}

Report 16187, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: we were told he saw Gabriel come down with the angels, and claimed he had no power against them; Qatada then judges Iblis's words "I fear God" to be a lie, since he knew he had no strength or protection, and this is his habit with those who obey him: when truth and falsehood meet he hands them over and disowns them. Report 16188, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: on the day of Badr Iblis marched with his banner and forces among the idolaters and put it in their hearts that nobody would defeat them; when he saw the reinforcement of angels he turned and went back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 9}

Report 16189, from Talha ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Kurayz, through Ahmad ibn al-Faraj, Abd al-Malik ibn al-Majishun, Malik and Ibrahim ibn Abi Abla: the Messenger of God said Iblis was never seen on any day smaller, lower, more driven off or angrier than on the day of Arafa, because of the descent of mercy and pardon of sins he sees, except for what he saw on the day of Badr; asked what he saw, he said he saw Gabriel marshalling the angels. The editor notes that Malik gives it in the Muwatta, that the report is a mursal one, gives biographies of the men in the chain and explains the verb "marshal" as arranging and lining them up for battle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 10}

Report 16190, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sulayman ibn al-Mughira and Humayd ibn Hilal: he saw Gabriel with a cloak wound round his head, walking before the Prophet with a bridle in his hand. Report 16191, also from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki and Hashim ibn al-Qasim: Iblis marched with the idolaters at Badr with his banner and forces and put it in their hearts that nobody would defeat them while they fought for the religion of their fathers; when they met he turned back and said he saw the angels. Report 16192, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Ma'shar: Quraysh said they feared only the Banu Bakr, and Iblis, as Suraqa, told them he was their protector against them and nobody could defeat them that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 11}

Al-Tabari then returns to the meaning: the sense is "God is hearing and knowing" in all these circumstances, and when Satan made their march against the believers look good to them and told them nobody of the sons of Adam could defeat them, so they should be calm, and that he was their protector against Kinana coming from behind, so they should turn their force on Muhammad and his companions; "when the two hosts came in sight", God's forces of believers and Satan's forces of idolaters advanced and saw each other; "he turned on his heels" means he went backwards, fleeing. The verb is conjugated nakasa, yankusu and yanksu, and al-Tabari cites a line of Zuhayr in praise of Hirm ibn Sinan, describing men who do not turn back from battle once caught in it; the editor explains its words. "I am free of you, I see what you do not see" means he sees the angels God sent as reinforcement and the idolaters do not, and "I fear God" is a lie of the enemy of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 11}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
