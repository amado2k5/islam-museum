## Summary
Verse 38 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 38 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "We said, go down from it all" al-Tabari refers back to his comments on verse 36 and reports from Abu Salih that those meant are Adam, Hawwa, the serpent and Iblis. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 548}
On "and if there comes to you guidance from Me" he says the sense is "if it comes to you", the "ma" after "in" being emphatic, and the heavy "nun" distinguishing it from a "ma" meaning "that which"; he sets out the Basran grammarians' view on the nun and the objections to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 548} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 549}
He explains "guidance" here as clear instruction and right direction, and reports from Abu al-Aliya that it means the prophets, messengers and explanation. On Abu al-Aliya's reading the address includes Adam, his wife and their descendants, which he compares with verses in surah Fussilat and surah al-Baqara and with Ibn Mas'ud's reading, since Adam himself was a prophet and messenger to his children. Al-Tabari's own choice is that the address is to those sent down from heaven, Adam, his wife and Iblis, and the sense is: if instruction and direction to My path comes to you, those who follow it have no fear nor shall they grieve, even if disobedience came before, to teach that He is the Accepting and Merciful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 549} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 550}
He says this address is God's way with all His creatures, and a teaching for those named at the start of the surah that, if they repent and follow the instruction brought by Muhammad, they are among the secure in the Hereafter, and if they perish in their disbelief before repenting they are among the people of the Fire, abiding there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 550} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 551}
On "whoever follows My guidance" he reports from Abu al-Aliya that it means My explanation, and on "no fear shall be upon them" that they are secure from God's punishment in the terrors of the Resurrection and do not grieve over what they left behind. From Ibn Zayd he reports that it means no fear for you ahead, nothing weighing more on a dying person than what comes after death, so He reassured them of that and consoled them about the world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 551}
