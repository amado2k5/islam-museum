## Summary
Verse 8 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/assassination-attempt-of-amir-and-arbad|Assassination Attempt of Amir and Arbad]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 8 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 8 (pp. 358-365)**

On "God knows what every female bears, and what the wombs lose, and what they gain; and everything with Him is in measure", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: "if you wonder, their saying is wondrous: when we are dust, shall we really be in a new creation?", denying God's power to restore them as a new creation after their perishing and decay, yet not denying His power to originate them and shape them in the wombs and to direct and turn them there from state to state; so He began the report on that anew, the meaning being as described, and said, mighty be His praise, "God knows what every female bears, and what the wombs lose, and what they gain", that is, what the wombs diminish from their carrying in the nine months by sending out menstrual blood, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 358}

"and what they gain" in their carrying beyond the nine months, completing what was lost in the nine months by the sending out of menstrual blood, "and everything with Him is in measure": nothing exceeds its fixed extent, and no matter He willed and ordered falls short of His ordering, just as the carrying of a female neither exceeds what was decreed for it nor falls short of the measure set for it. "Miqdar" is the form "mif'al" from "qadr". The interpreters said the same. Report 20163, from Ibn Abbas, through Ya'qub ibn Mahan, al-Qasim ibn Malik, Dawud ibn Abi Hind and Ikrima: every day of blood a woman sees during her pregnancy adds a day to the pregnancy. Report 20164, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "what the wombs lose" means the miscarriage; "and what they gain" he says is what the womb adds to the carrying over what it lost until it bears the child at full term; for some women carry ten months, some nine, some more and some less, and that is the loss and the gain God mentioned, all of it by His knowledge. Report 20165, from Mujahid or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Sa'id ibn Yahya al-Umawi, Abd al-Salam and Khusayf: its loss is less than nine and the gain more than nine. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 359}

Report 20166, from Mujahid, through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Abu Bishr: the "loss" is the blood the pregnant woman sees during her pregnancy, a decrease in the child, and the "gain" is what exceeds nine months, completing the decrease. Reports 20167 to 20169, from Mujahid, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-Samad and Shu'ba; Ibn Bashshar and Muhammad ibn Ja'far; and al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal and Hushaym: the same. Report 20170, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Abd al-Salam and Khusayf: if she sees blood for less than nine months, the term goes beyond nine by the like of the days of menstruation. Report 20171, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Ibn Abi Najih: "what the wombs lose" is the flow of blood, "and what they gain" its retention. Report 20172, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: the woman's shedding of blood until the child is stunted, and "what they gain" is that if she does not shed it the child is completed and grows large. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 360}

Report 20173, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Shu'ba and Ja'far: a woman sees blood and carries more than nine months. Report 20174, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Hasan, Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah, Hushaym and Abu Bishr: it is the woman who sees blood in her pregnancy. Report 20175, from Mujahid, through Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: the shedding of blood until the child is stunted, and the gain is that if the woman does not shed it the child is completed and grows large. Report 20176, from Uthman ibn al-Aswad, through al-Hakam ibn Musa and Miqal: I said to Mujahid, my wife saw blood and I hope she is pregnant. Al-Tabari says: that is how it is in the book. Mujahid said: that is the loss of the wombs, and the child keeps falling into decrease while she sees blood; when the blood stops it falls into increase and continues until complete. The editor notes that the point al-Tabari doubted is that seeing blood means menstruation, and a woman who sees it is not pregnant, yet he then says he hopes she is pregnant; the editor differs from him, taking "I hope" in the sense of certainty, not hope, as if he said my wife saw blood while pregnant, and some women see blood while pregnant at the time of their menses, as women told him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 361}

Report 20177, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah, Hushaym and Abu Bishr: the "loss" is the pregnant woman seeing blood in her pregnancy, a decrease in the child, and what exceeds the nine months is the "gain", the completion of the birth. Reports 20178 and 20179, from Ikrima, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-Wahhab and Dawud, and Abd al-A'la: whenever she loses with blood, that adds to the pregnancy. Report 20180, from Ikrima, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Abbad ibn al-Awwam and Asim: the womb's loss is blood during pregnancy; each day the womb loses in blood adds a day to the pregnancy until she completes it clean. Report 20181, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Abbad, Sa'id and Ya'la ibn Muslim: the same. Report 20182, from Ikrima, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, al-Walid ibn Salih, Abu Yazid and Asim: it is menstruation during pregnancy, so for every day she menstruates in pregnancy a day is added in her purity until she completes nine months clean. Report 20183, from Ikrima, through Yazid ibn Harun and Imran ibn Hudayr: whatever blood she sees in her pregnancy is added to her pregnancy. Report 20184, from al-Dahhak, through Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan, Ishaq and Juwaybir: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 362}

"the wombs lose" is less than nine and "what they gain" more than nine. Report 20185, from al-Dahhak, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Ibn al-Mubarak and al-Hasan ibn Yahya: a child may be born at two years, and al-Dahhak was born at two years; the "loss" is below nine and the gain beyond nine months. Report 20186, from al-Dahhak, through Sufyan and Juwaybir: below nine and above nine. Report 20187, from him: I was born at two years. Report 20188, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd ibn Nasr, Ibn al-Mubarak and al-Hasan ibn Yahya: his mother carried him two years; "what the wombs lose" is what falls short of nine, "what they gain" what is above nine. Report 20189, from al-Dahhak, through Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Juwaybir: "every female", every female of God's creation. Report 20190, from al-Dahhak and al-Hasan, through Hushaym, Juwaybir and Mansur: the "loss" is less than nine months. In a note the editor says that al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, the commentator, said of himself that he was born after two years, and refers to report 20199, where he says he was born with his incisors already grown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 363}

Report 20191, from Aisha, through Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Dawud ibn Abd al-Rahman, Ibn Jurayj and Jamila bint Sa'd: a pregnancy lasts no more than two years, the time it takes the shadow of a spindle to move. Report 20192, from Atiyya al-Awfi, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Fudayl ibn Marzuq: "what the wombs lose" is a pregnancy of nine months and under, "what they gain" is beyond nine. Report 20193, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Amr ibn Thabit and his father: a woman's menses during the pregnancy. Report 20194, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the "loss" is the miscarriage, "what they gain" is beyond the nine months. Report 20195, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through the same chain: if a woman sees blood in pregnancy, that is the "loss" for the child, a decrease in the child's nourishment and an increase in the pregnancy. Report 20196, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: al-Hasan used to say that "ghaydawa" is a woman giving birth at six or seven months or below the limit, and Qatada said the gain is whatever exceeds nine months; the editor notes that "ghaydawa" is a verbal noun the dictionaries do not record and deserves to be recorded, since it is from the speech of al-Hasan al-Basri. Report 20197, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays and Salim al-Afţas: the womb's loss is that she sees blood during {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 364}

her pregnancy, and for every span in which she sees blood she gains a like span in her pregnancy. Report 20198, from Mujahid, through Abd al-Aziz, Hammad ibn Salama and Qays ibn Sa'd: when the pregnant woman sees blood it was greater for the child; the original is garbled at this point and unclear. Report 20199, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: the "loss" is a decrease from the term and the "gain" is what exceeds the term, because women do not give birth after one fixed term; a child is born at six months and lives, born at two years and lives, and in between; and he said he was born at two years with his incisors already grown. Report 20200, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the wombs' loss is the shedding that comes upon women during pregnancy; when that shedding comes it is not counted toward the pregnancy and it shortens her carrying until it lifts, and when it lifts she begins a fresh term of nine months; while she sees blood the wombs lose and diminish and the child is thin, and when the blood lifts the child grows and she counts the nine months of pregnancy from the time it lifts, whatever came before not counting, being a shedding that lapses entirely. On "and everything with Him is in measure": report 20201, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: yes, by God, He has preserved for them their provision and their terms and set a known term for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 365}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
