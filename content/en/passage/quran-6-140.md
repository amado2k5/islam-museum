## Summary
Verse 140 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/wad|burying daughters alive (wa'd)]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 140 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 140, "lost are those who killed their children out of folly, without knowledge, and forbade what God provided them, forging a lie against God; they have gone astray and were not guided", al-Tabari says: ruined are these forgers of lies against their Lord, who set idols and statues equal to Him, for whom their partners made attractive the killing of their children and the forbidding of what He had graced them with of their property, so they killed their children in obedience to those partners and forbade what God made lawful to them and made provision from their cattle. "Out of folly" means they did it from ignorance of what is for and against them, from deficient minds, weak wits and little grasp of the immediate harm and the later grief of God's great punishment for it; "forging a lie against God" means falsely ascribing and guessing falsely against Him; "they have gone astray" means they left the high road of truth in their doing and departed from the even path; "and were not guided" means those who did that were not on guidance and rectitude in their acts nor granted success in them. The editor notes that all copies read "and forbidding what I forbade them of their property", which he finds contradicts the explanation and corrects to "what I graced them with". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 153}

This verse came down about those whose case God mentioned in these verses beginning "they assigned God a share of what He created of crops and cattle", those who slit the ears of the bahira, let the sa'iba go free and buried girls alive. He relates (13950) from Ikrima that it came down about those of Rabi'a and Mudar who buried girls alive: a man would make it a condition on his wife that she keep one girl alive and bury another; when the girl was born whom she was bound to bury, the man would go off in the morning or evening from his wife and say to her, you are to me as my mother's back if I come back to you and you have not buried her; so she would dig a trench in the ground and send for her women, who gathered around her, and when she saw him returning she thrust the child into her pit and levelled the earth over her. The note explains the word "khadd", to dig a trench. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 154}

He gives (13951) al-Suddi: then He mentioned what they did with their children and their property; (13952) Qatada: this was the doing of the people of the pre-Islamic era; one would kill his daughter for fear of being taken captive and of poverty, and feed his dog; and "forbade what God provided them" refers to people of that time who made bahira, sa'iba, wasila and hami as an arbitrary decree of the devils over their property; and (13953) Abd al-Aziz: if you wish to know the ignorance of the Arabs, read what follows the hundredth verse of surat al-An'am. Abu Razin took "they have gone astray" to mean that they had gone astray before these acts, killing children and forbidding provision, by other matters (13954). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 155}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
