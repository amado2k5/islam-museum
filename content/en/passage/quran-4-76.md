## Summary
Verse 76 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 76 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse (pp. 546-547, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: those who believed God and his Messenger and are certain of his promise to the people of faith fight in obedience to God and the course of his religion and law, while those who denied God's oneness and called his Messenger and what he brought from his Lord a lie fight in obedience to Satan, his path and the course he laid down for his allies among the unbelievers. It is God's strengthening of the resolve of the believers among the Messenger's companions and his urging them against his enemies and the enemies of his religion; he orders them to fight those who take Satan as protector and obey him against obedience to God, in denying him, and who help him. He explains Satan's scheming as what he plotted against the believers by rallying his allies among the unbelievers against the Messenger and the people of faith, and says: do not fear Satan's allies, they are his party and helpers, and his party are people of frailty and weakness. The editor says the print differs from the manuscript in a phrase and the manuscript is right, and refers to the lexicon indexes for the interpretation of the words for path and protector, to the earlier interpretation of the word for the transgressing power (page numbers garbled in the scan), and to the earlier interpretation of scheming in volume 7 (as printed). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 547}

**Why they are called weak (p. 547).** Al-Tabari says God described them as weak because they do not fight hoping for reward nor leave off fighting fearing punishment; they fight out of zeal or envy of the believers for what God gave them of his bounty. The believer fights hoping for God's great reward and abstains, if he abstains, from fear of God's threat for abstaining, so he fights with insight into what awaits him with God if killed and what he has of booty and victory if he survives. The unbeliever fights warily of being killed and despairing of any return, and so has weakness and fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 547}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 547}
