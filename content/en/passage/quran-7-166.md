**Commentary on verse 166 (p. 203)**

Al-Tabari says: when they rebelled against what they had been forbidden, their transgression in the Sabbath and their treating as lawful the hunting and eating of fish that God had forbidden, and persisted in it, God said to them "be despised apes", that is, far from all good. The people of interpretation say the same. Report 15294, from Qatada: when the people rebelled against disobedience, "We said to them be despised apes", and they became apes with tails, howling, after having been men and women. Report 15295, from Ibn Abbas: God made apes and pigs of them, and he claimed that the youths of the people became apes and the elders became pigs. Report 15296, from al-Suddi from Abu Malik or Sa'id ibn Jubayr: Moses saw a man carrying reeds on the Sabbath and struck off his neck. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of the verbs for rebelling and for being driven away. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 203}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
