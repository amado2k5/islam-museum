## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 34 to 35 in a single passage. He explains the verse "Does He promise you that when you die and you will be dust and bones that you will be brought out":... The verse, Allah Almighty says: They said to them: Does Salih promise you that when you die and you will be dust in your graves and bones that have gone away and their bones remain, that you will be brought out of your graves alive as you were before your death? "Annakum" is repeated twice, meaning: "He promises you that when you die and you are dust and bones, you will be taken out once," because there is no distinction between the first "ankum" and its predicate with "idha." The Arabs do the same with every name they assumed and its sisters, then objected to the penalty without its predicate, repeating the name once and deleting it again, saying: I think that if you sit with us, you are a benefactor; if you omit the first "annak" of the second, it is good; if you affirm both, it is good; and if you do not object between them with anything, it is not permissible. It is wrong to say: I think you are sitting.
