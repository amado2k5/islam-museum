## Summary
Verse 266 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 266 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

In ([[passage/quran-2-266|2:266]]) from p. 541, al-Tabari says the sense of the verse is linked to what comes before it, the prohibition on voiding charity by reproach and harm like one who spends his wealth to be seen by people, whose likeness is that of the stone; then God asks whether one of you would like to have a garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 541} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} The editor notes that this verse returns to the preceding one and that al-Tabari wrote what shows its link with ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} Would like means loves; a garden means an orchard of palms and vines with rivers beneath it, that is, beneath the garden; with all kinds of fruit in it, the pronoun for him returning to the one, and in it to the garden; and old age struck him, meaning one of you, with weak offspring. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} The editor refers to the earlier explanation of love and of garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542}

Al-Tabari says God made the orchard of palms and vines a likeness of the spending of the hypocrite who spends it to be seen by people and not seeking God's pleasure, for by what appears to them of his charity and visible deed, people praise him and commend him for it in his lifetime, in its beauty like the orchard's beauty, with all kinds of fruit in it, because his outward deed in the world has every immediate good, by which he protects himself, his blood, his property and his offspring, wins people's praise and fair mention, takes his share of the spoils, with many other things hard to count, so that he has in it every good in the world; this is al-Tabari's exposition and construction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} In notes the editor numbers the places of the tangled sentence and gives its sequence, and corrects a word in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542}

He then says that old age struck the owner of the garden, whose offspring were weak, small children, so a whirlwind with fire in it struck the garden and it burned, meaning that the wind with fire burned his garden when he needed it and needed its fruit in his old age, weak to tend it, with his children small and unable to revive it or look after it, so he was left with nothing when he needed his garden and its fruit most, by the blight that struck it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} So too, al-Tabari says, for the one who spends his wealth to be seen by people: God extinguished his light, took away the beauty of his deed and voided his reward until he met Him and came back to it when he needed his deed most, when there is no chance to ask forgiveness or be excused for his sins and no repentance, and his deed vanished as the garden burned when its owner was old and his children small, when he needed it most, and its benefits were lost to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} This likeness is the counterpart of the other likeness of the stone; this is al-Tabari's interpretation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543}

Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed on this verse, but the meanings of their statements, though their turns differ, return to the sense he gave, and the best at making its meaning clear and the nearest to the truth is al-Suddi; report 6091 from him begins at the foot of p. 543 and we stop before it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} The editor refers in notes to earlier places in the explanation of offspring and to al-Tabari's abridgement, and explains the phrase no chance as no seeking forgiveness or reconciliation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's interpretation and preference for al-Suddi are attributed to him, and the notes are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.
