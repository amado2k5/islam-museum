## Summary
Verse 266 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 266 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

In ([[passage/quran-2-266|2:266]]) from p. 541, al-Tabari says the sense of the verse is linked to what comes before it, the prohibition on voiding charity by reproach and harm like one who spends his wealth to be seen by people, whose likeness is that of the stone; then God asks whether one of you would like to have a garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 541} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} The editor notes that this verse returns to the preceding one and that al-Tabari wrote what shows its link with ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} Would like means loves; a garden means an orchard of palms and vines with rivers beneath it, that is, beneath the garden; with all kinds of fruit in it, the pronoun for him returning to the one, and in it to the garden; and old age struck him, meaning one of you, with weak offspring. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} The editor refers to the earlier explanation of love and of garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542}

Al-Tabari says God made the orchard of palms and vines a likeness of the spending of the hypocrite who spends it to be seen by people and not seeking God's pleasure, for by what appears to them of his charity and visible deed, people praise him and commend him for it in his lifetime, in its beauty like the orchard's beauty, with all kinds of fruit in it, because his outward deed in the world has every immediate good, by which he protects himself, his blood, his property and his offspring, wins people's praise and fair mention, takes his share of the spoils, with many other things hard to count, so that he has in it every good in the world; this is al-Tabari's exposition and construction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542} In notes the editor numbers the places of the tangled sentence and gives its sequence, and corrects a word in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 542}

He then says that old age struck the owner of the garden, whose offspring were weak, small children, so a whirlwind with fire in it struck the garden and it burned, meaning that the wind with fire burned his garden when he needed it and needed its fruit in his old age, weak to tend it, with his children small and unable to revive it or look after it, so he was left with nothing when he needed his garden and its fruit most, by the blight that struck it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} So too, al-Tabari says, for the one who spends his wealth to be seen by people: God extinguished his light, took away the beauty of his deed and voided his reward until he met Him and came back to it when he needed his deed most, when there is no chance to ask forgiveness or be excused for his sins and no repentance, and his deed vanished as the garden burned when its owner was old and his children small, when he needed it most, and its benefits were lost to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} This likeness is the counterpart of the other likeness of the stone; this is al-Tabari's interpretation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543}

Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed on this verse, but the meanings of their statements, though their turns differ, return to the sense he gave, and the best at making its meaning clear and the nearest to the truth is al-Suddi; report 6091 from him begins at the foot of p. 543 and we stop before it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} The editor refers in notes to earlier places in the explanation of offspring and to al-Tabari's abridgement, and explains the phrase no chance as no seeking forgiveness or reconciliation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's interpretation and preference for al-Suddi are attributed to him, and the notes are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-266|2:266]]). Report 6091 from al-Suddi: this is another likeness of showing-off spending; he spends his wealth to be seen by people and his wealth goes from him while he shows off, so God does not reward him for it; on the day of resurrection he needs his spending and finds that showing off has burned it away, as this man spent on his garden until, when it matured and his dependants multiplied and he needed it, a scorching wind came and burned it and he found nothing of it; so too the one who spends to show off. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 543} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 544} The editor notes that the manuscript's word for the wind is unintelligible and that the printed text's is that of al-Durr al-Manthur and the other reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 544}

Mujahid (6092, 6093): the likeness is of one who neglects obedience to God until he dies; he says, would one of you like to have a worldly life in which he does not act in obedience to God, like this man who has gardens; so his likeness after death is that of this man when his garden burned while he was old and could do nothing for it, and his children small and could do nothing for it; so too the one who neglected, after death, every thing is a regret to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 544}

Ata (6094): Umar asked people about this verse and found none who satisfied him, until Ibn Abbas, who was behind him, said, Commander of the Faithful, I find something about it in myself; he turned to him and said move over here, do not belittle yourself; he said this is a likeness God drew, asking whether one of you would like to do the deeds of the people of good and happiness all his life, then when he needs most to close it with good as his life is spent and his term near, close it with the deeds of the people of misery, spoiling it all, and so burn it when he needed it most. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 544} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 545} Ibn Abi Mulayka (6095): Umar recited the verse and said it is a likeness drawn for a person who does a good deed until, near the end of his life when he needs it most, he does evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 545}

Ubayd ibn Umayr (6096): Umar asked the Companions of the Messenger of God what they thought the verse was revealed about, and they said God knows best; Umar became angry and said say we know or we do not know; Ibn Abbas said I find something about it in myself, Commander of the Faithful, and Umar said speak, nephew, and do not belittle yourself; he said it was drawn as a likeness for a deed; Umar said what deed; he said for a deed; Umar said a rich man doing good deeds, then God sent Satan to him and he did sins until he drowned all his deeds; and he heard Abd Allah ibn Abi Mulayka relate the like from Ibn Abbas, who heard it from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 545} Ibn Jurayj (6097), by a chain through Ibn Abi Mulayka from Ubayd ibn Umayr and from Ibn Abbas: Umar asked the Companions and he mentioned the like, except that Umar said a man doing good deeds, then Satan was sent to him and he did sins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 546}

In notes the editor says al-Hafiz Ibn Hajar refers to report 6094 in Fath al-Bari; that Muhammad ibn Sulaym al-Makki, transmitter of 6095, relates from Ibn Abi Mulayka and Ibn Hajar did not see a report from him from anyone else; that al-Bukhari relates the report through Hisham ibn Yusuf from Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Hajar refers to al-Tabari's route through Ibn al-Mubarak; and that al-Hakim records report 6097 in al-Mustadrak and says it is sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs, which al-Dhahabi accepts; these sources and gradings are the editor's, not al-Tabari's, and he corrects words in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 545} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 546} Report 6098 begins at the foot of p. 546 and we stop before it. These reports are given as transmitted, with no verdict from the museum.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-266|2:266]]) from p. 546. Report 6098, from Ibn Jurayj via Ata and Mujahid, that it is a likeness drawn for deeds; and from Ibn Abbas, that it is a likeness for a person who begins with a good deed, which is the likeness of the garden, then does wrong near the end of his life and persists until he dies in it, so the whirlwind with fire is the likeness of the wrongdoing he died in; Ibn Abbas said the garden is his livelihood and his children's, which burned, and he could not defend his garden because of his age nor his offspring because of their youth; such is his case, meeting his Lord when he is poorest in need of Him and finding nothing with Him, unable to defend himself from any of God's punishment, and so there is no repentance once deeds cease at death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 546} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 547} From Mujahid via Ibn Abbas: it is the likeness of one who neglects obedience to God until he dies, and Mujahid said it is like one with a worldly life in which he does not obey God, and after death he is like this man when his garden burned while he was old and could do nothing for it and his children small and could do nothing, and so for the one who neglected, after death everything is a regret to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 547}

Qatada (6099): a wind with fierce scorching heat struck it, and God makes the signs clear that they might reflect; this is a likeness, so take in from God His likenesses, as He says in ([[passage/quran-29-43|29:43]]) that He draws likenesses for people and none grasp them but those with knowledge; this is a man whose age had grown, whose bones had thinned and whose dependants were many, and his garden burned along with all that, when he needed it most; it says, would one of you like his deed to be lost to him on the day of resurrection when he needs it most. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 547} The editor corrects in notes the pointing of a word in the manuscript, citing a saying of Uthman and a line about a she-camel on weakness of bones. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 547} Qatada (6100): his garden went when he needed it most, when he was old and too weak to earn, and his offspring did not help him; and al-Hasan used to say it burned and went when he needed it most, and asked would one of you like his deed to go when he needs it most. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 547}

Ibn Abbas (6101): God drew a fine likeness, and all His likenesses are fine; he made it in his youth, then old age struck him with weak offspring at the end of his life, and a whirlwind with fire came and burned his orchard, and he had not the strength to plant its like nor did his progeny have anything good to return to him; so too the unbeliever on the day of resurrection, when he is returned to God, has no good with which to seek pardon, as this man has no strength to plant an orchard like his, and finds no good he put forward for himself, and is deprived of his reward when he is poorest in need of it; it is a likeness God drew for the believer and the unbeliever in what they are given in this world: how the believer is saved in the hereafter and honour and delight stored up for him while wealth was withheld from him in this world, and for the unbeliever wealth was spread out in this world that comes to an end and evil stored for him that never leaves him, and he abides in it humiliated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 548} In notes the editor says the manuscript has a blank and disorder in this report, that he kept the printed text, which is the report's text as al-Suyuti gives it in al-Durr al-Manthur, attributing it to Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim, and as Ibn Kathir gives it, that he left a phrase supplied by the printer in one place he could not find in those sources, and that he would himself have conjectured it as that he disbelieved in meeting his Lord, and God knows best; these are the editor's remarks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 548}

Al-Rabi' (6102): this is a likeness God drew, and the man had grown old and weak with young children and God tried them in their garden; so God sent on it a whirlwind with fire and it burned, and the man could not defend his garden because of old age nor his sons because of their youth, so his garden went when he needed it most; would one of you like to live in error and sins until death comes, and come on the day of resurrection with his deed lost to him when he needs it most, and He says: son of Adam, you came to Me when you needed good most, so where is what you put forward for yourself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 549} The editor says what he put in brackets is what the printer of the printed text supplied, he thinks, where the manuscript had a blank, and that the manuscript is corrupt in places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 549} Report 6103 from Ibn Zayd begins on p. 549 and runs on, and we stop before it. These reports are given as transmitted, the corrections are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
