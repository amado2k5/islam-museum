## Summary
Verse 128 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 128 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 128, "on the day He gathers them all: O company of jinn, you have taken much from mankind": by "the day He gathers them all" He means the day He gathers those who set idols and statues equal to God, and other idolaters, together with their allies among the devils who inspired them with adorned speech to deceive, so that they might dispute with the believers; He gathers them at the standing place of resurrection and says to the jinn, "O company of jinn, you have taken much from mankind", the words "He says to the jinn" being dropped because the speech itself shows it. He meant by "you have taken much" that they had done much misleading and seducing. He gives (13885) Ibn Abbas: you led many of them astray; (13886) Qatada the same; and (13887) Mujahid: many are those you seduced. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 115}

Report 13888 gives the same from Mujahid, and 13889 from al-Hasan: you led many of mankind astray. Then "and their allies among mankind said: our Lord, some of us profited from others". Al-Tabari says the human allies of the jinn will answer, "our Lord, some of us profited from others in the world". As for mankind's profit from the jinn, he gives (13890) Ibn Jurayj: in pre-Islamic times a man would stop at a place and say, I seek refuge with the master of this valley; that was their profit, and they offered it as an excuse on the Day of Resurrection. As for the jinn's profit from mankind, it was the reverence people showed them when seeking refuge with them, so that they said, we have become masters of the jinn and the "hinn". The editor notes that the printed text changed "jinn" to "mankind" because the unpointed manuscript was not well read, explains "hinn" with kasra as a tribe of the jinn, and refers to al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 116}

On "and we reached our term that You appointed for us", al-Tabari says they said, we have reached the time You set for our death, meaning that they profited from one another through the days of their life until death; he gives (13891) al-Suddi: the term is death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 117}

Then "He will say: the Fire is your abode, you remain in it, except as God wills; your Lord is wise and knowing". Al-Tabari says this is a report from God of what He will say to those He gathers on the Day of Resurrection, those who set idols as equals to Him and their companions among the jinn; the report of what will be is given in the form of the report of what has been, because the speech before it has that sense. God says to the jinn's allies among mankind, the Fire is your abode, meaning the fire of Hell is the place where you stay. "Mathwa" is the pattern "maf'al" from "thawa" in a place, when one stays in it. The note refers to the earlier explanation of "mathwa". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 117}

"Remaining in it" means staying in it, "except as God wills" means except for what God wills of the length of time between their raising from the graves and their going to Hell, which is the period God excepted from their remaining in the Fire; "your Lord is wise" in His management of His creation and His turning them as He wills from state to state, and "knowing" of the outcomes of His management of them and where their affairs end, in good and evil. It is related that Ibn Abbas understood this exception to mean that God left the extent of their punishment to His will; report 13892 from Ibn Abbas says this verse is one in which no one should pass judgement on God about His creation, not sending them down to paradise or to the fire. The editor notes that the printed text added "an" and spoiled the sense, and that the saying is a prohibition on people saying that so-and-so is in paradise and so-and-so in the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 118}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
