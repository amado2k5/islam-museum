## Summary
Verse 249 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 249 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-249|2:249]]) begins on p. 338. Al-Tabari says the report leaves out something understood, namely that the Ark came to them, so they believed their prophet, acknowledged that God had sent Talut as king and submitted to him; this is shown by Talut's setting out with them, which he would not do except after their consent, since he was not one who could force them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 338} He explains the setting out as moving off with the troops, the root meaning being cutting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 338} It was said that Talut left Jerusalem with eighty thousand fighters, none staying behind but one with an ailment, an old man or one excused, from Wahb (5707) and al-Suddi (5708). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 339} The editor explains the words for rallying and for the sick. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 339} Al-Tabari then explains that God was testing them by a river to know how they obeyed Him, and Qatada (5709): God tests His creation by what He wills to know who obeys Him and who disobeys. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 339}

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-249|2:249]]) from p. 340: it was said that Talut told them of the river's test because they complained to him of the scarcity of water between them and their enemy and asked him to pray that God run a river between them, from Wahb (5710). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 340} It was said the river is between the Jordan and Palestine, from al-Rabi' and Qatada (5711 to 5713), and from Ibn 'Abbas, a river between Palestine and the Jordan, sweet and pleasant in water (5714). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 340} Others said it is the river of Palestine, from Ibn 'Abbas and al-Suddi (5715, 5716). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 341}

Al-Tabari explains that Talut told his troops, when they complained of thirst, that God would test them by a river, then told them the test was that whoever drank from its water was not of those loyal to him and obedient, nor of those who believe in God and meeting Him; this is shown by God's bringing out of the believers those who did not cross the river, and then singling out the believers as they drew near Goliath. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 341} He says the pronouns of drinking and tasting return to the river, the sense being its water; the sense of not tasting it is not sampling it, meaning being of his party; then he excepts those who scoop with their hands, so whoever did not taste the river's water but a handful he scooped is of his party. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 341} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 342} The editor notes in the margin the exegetes' views on the exception, whether from the first or second clause, says al-Tabari seems to mean two groups, one who drank and a believing one who tasted only a handful, and that the manuscript's wording is disordered; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 342}

Al-Tabari then gives the readers' differences on the word: most readers of Medina and Basra read it with the short vowel after the first letter for a single scooping, others with the rounded vowel for the water that lies in the scooper's palm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 342} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 343} He chooses, as his choice and argument, the rounded vowel, a palmful of water, since the verbal noun of scooping is a different form and the open-vowel form is the verbal noun of a different verb, so the rounded form is closer to a noun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 343} The editor says this is a fine point seldom found in dictionaries. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 343}

It was reported that most of them drank, and whoever drank grew thirsty while whoever scooped a palmful was satisfied: Qatada (5717, 5718), that they drank according to their certainty, the unbelievers drinking and not being satisfied while the believers scooped a palmful that sufficed and quenched; and al-Rabi' (5719) similarly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 343} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 344} Al-Suddi (5720): when the Ark was found in Talut's house they believed and set out, eighty thousand; Goliath was among the mightiest of men; they drank out of fear of him, and four thousand crossed with Talut while seventy-six thousand turned back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 344} Ibn Zayd (5721): God put on Talut's tongue that none should accompany him but one with the intention to fight, so no believer held back and no hypocrite followed, and some who went out turned back as unbelievers while the rest took the palmful and it sufficed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 344} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 345} The editor judges that a line has dropped out in the copying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 344} Ibn 'Abbas (5722): each drank according to what was in his heart; whoever scooped a palmful and obeyed was satisfied by his obedience and whoever drank much disobeyed and was not satisfied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 345} Wahb (5723) similarly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 345}

On the next clause, al-Tabari says the pronoun returns to the river, and that Talut and those who believed with him crossed it and said they had no strength that day against Goliath and his troops. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 345} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 346} They differed over the number who crossed with him: some said it was the number of the men of Badr, three hundred and some ten, from al-Bara' b. 'Azib (5724, 5725). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 346} The editor notes in the margin that al-Bukhari and Ahmad relayed al-Bara''s report and that al-Tabari gave it by six chains; these are the scholars' judgements and references. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 346} These are reports from Companions, Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.
