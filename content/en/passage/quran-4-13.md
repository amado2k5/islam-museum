## Summary
Verse 13 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 13 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of the boundaries: the views (pp. 68-69, reports 8790-8791).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed on the boundaries ascribed to God at the opening of [[passage/quran-4-13|verse 13]]. Some said it means God's conditions, and Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8790, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that they are God's conditions. Others said the sense is obedience to God, and al-Muthanna (8791, from Abu Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is obedience to God, meaning the inheritances God named. Others said the sense is God's way and command, and others that it is God's obligations. At this place the editor remarks that the treatment of the term here has a detail not seen before in the earlier volumes, of the highest quality and precision, and refers to the earlier places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 68} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 69}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the meaning of the boundaries (pp. 69-70).** Abu Jafar says the soundest view to him is that the boundary of a thing is what separates it from another, which is why the limits of a house and of lands are called boundaries, for they separate what is bounded by them from what is not. So the sense of the verse is: this division which your Lord divided for you, and the obligations He decreed for the living among you from your dead as He decreed and explained them in the two verses, are separations between obedience to God and disobedience in the division of your dead's inheritances, as Ibn Abbas said. The word obedience was left out and the sense is the boundaries of obedience to God, in view of the hearers' knowing the sense. The proof of the soundness of this, to him, is what follows about the reward of whoever obeys and the verse after it about the one who disobeys ([[passage/quran-4-14|4:14]]). The sense is then: this division by which your Lord divided among you the inheritances of your dead consists of separations He drew between obedience and disobedience and limits you are to stop at and not exceed, so that the people of His obedience may be known from the people of His disobedience in what He commanded you of dividing your inheritances and in what He forbade you of it. He then told what He has prepared for each party, saying to those who obey in acting as He commanded and stopping at what He limited in the division of inheritances and elsewhere, and avoiding what He forbade, that He will admit them to orchards under whose plants and trees rivers run, remaining in them for ever, neither dying there, perishing nor being driven out. Abu Jafar then begins to explain the success with which God closed the promise, saying that God's admitting them into the gardens He described in the manner He described, and the sentence is cut off in the middle at the end of the page. The editor's notes: both texts have a wording in which the plural word for separations is treated as a verbal noun of the verb to separate, but the lexicographers did not make it a verbal noun in this sense, the verbal noun being the singular form, the plural form being the noun of "so-and-so went out from me"; he thinks the copyist dropped something and that the original wording was: for this reason the limits of a house and of lands are called boundaries, which are their separations, because of their separating, the plural being of a word meaning the barrier between two things like the boundary; that both texts have the word obedience linked to God where what was left out is only the word obedience, so that he would have preferred the sentence otherwise; that the printing and the manuscript both dropped the conjunction before the verse after it, a corruption, and he calls this an evident, clear proof in the interpretation of the term and prays for mercy and good reward for Abu Jafar on account of his book; and that the printing has a wording differing from the manuscript because its copyist could not read what the manuscript had and changed it, the manuscript having a word like the heads of a letter, the right reading of which is "so that it may be known". He refers for the gardens and abiding to the earlier language indexes. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
