## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "Hypocrites, for Allah, not associating partners with Him, and whoever associates partners with Allah - it is as if he has fallen from the sky, and the birds snatch him away or the wind blows with him to a distant place": Avoid, O people, idol worship and the word of polytheism, remaining steadfast for Allah, sincerely dedicating oneness to Him, and dedicating obedience and worship to Him alone, excluding idols and idols, and not associating anything with Him, for whoever associates anything with Allah besides Him, is like him in his distance from guidance, the truth being hit, his destruction, and his departure from his Lord, like one who falls from the sky and is snatched away by birds. So he perished, or the wind fell with him in a remote place, meaning from afar, as in their saying: 'God pushed him away and crushed him.' There are two languages: 'the wind crushed him' and 'he crushed it.' From this, the long palm tree was called 'the palm tree of Sahuq,' and from it the poet's saying: 'We had a neighbor, and a filthy one disturbed her, crushing the seeds in the foot.' It is narrated as 'tashaq.' In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid.
