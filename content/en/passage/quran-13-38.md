**Commentary on verse 38 (pp. 475-477)**

On "We sent messengers before you and gave them wives and offspring, and it was not for any messenger to bring a sign except by God's leave; for every term is a book", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: We sent, Muhammad, messengers before you to nations that had passed away before your nation, and made them human beings like you, with wives whom they married, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 475}

and offspring whom they begot, and We did not make them angels who neither eat nor drink nor marry, so as to make the messenger to your people an angel like them, but We sent to them human beings like them, as We sent to those before them of the other nations human beings like them; "and it was not for any messenger to bring a sign except by God's leave", He says, mighty be His praise: no messenger whom God sent to His creation is able to bring his community a sign and token, such as moving the mountains, transferring a city from one place to another, reviving the dead and the like of such signs, "except by God's leave", that is, except by God's command to the mountains to move, to the earth to shift, and to the dead to live; "for every term is a book", that is, for every term God has decreed there is a book He has written, which is with Him. It has also been said its meaning is: for every Book God sent down from heaven there is a term. Report 20460, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq ibn Yusuf and Juwaybir: "for every term is a book", he says, for every book sent down from heaven there is a term, and God erases of it what He wills and confirms, and with Him is the Mother of the Book. Al-Tabari says: on this view it is parallel to God's saying "the stupor of death comes with truth" [[passage/quran-50-19|50:19]], and Abu Bakr used to read it {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 476}

"the stupor of truth comes with death", because the stupor of death comes with the truth and the truth comes with it; so too the term has a book and the book has a term. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 477}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
