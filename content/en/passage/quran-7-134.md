**Commentary on verse 134 (pp. 70-73)**

Al-Tabari says: "when the plague fell on them" means when God's punishment came on them and His wrath descended. The interpreters then differed over what that plague was. Some said it was the pestilence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 70}

Report 15033, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub al-Qummi and Ja'far ibn al-Mughira: Moses commanded his people of the Children of Israel, after Pharaoh's people had come with the five signs, the flood and what God mentioned in this verse, and had not believed or sent the Children of Israel, saying: let each man of you slaughter a ram, dip his palm in its blood and strike it on his door. The Copts asked the Israelites, why do you work this blood on your doors? They said, God will send a punishment on you, and we shall be safe while you perish. The Copts said, God knows you only by these marks? They said, so our prophet commanded us. In the morning seventy thousand of Pharaoh's people had been struck by plague, and by evening they could not bury one another. Pharaoh then said, ask your Lord by the covenant He made with you, if you lift the plague from us, which is pestilence, we shall believe and send the Children of Israel with you; he prayed and it was lifted, and Pharaoh was the most faithful of them to his word, saying to Moses, go with the Children of Israel wherever you will. The editor notes that the printed text has a different word and the manuscript's bad writing supports the reading he kept. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 71}

Report 15034, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Habawayh al-Razi, Abu Dawud al-Hafari, Ya'qub, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "if you lift the plague from us" means the pestilence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 71}

Others said it is punishment. Report 15035, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the plague is punishment; report 15036, from Mujahid, is the same; report 15037, from Qatada: punishment; report 15038, from Qatada through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: punishment; report 15039, from Ibn Zayd through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the plague is the punishment God set upon them in locusts, lice and the rest, and each time they made a covenant with Him and broke it. Al-Tabari has already explained the meaning of "plague". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 72}

Al-Tabari holds the better of the two views to be that God reported of Pharaoh and his people that when the plague fell on them, which is God's punishment and wrath, they took refuge with Moses, asking him to ask his Lord to lift it. It may be that the plague was the flood, locusts, lice, frogs and blood, since each was a punishment for them; it may be that it was a pestilence, but God did not tell us that, and no report from the Messenger of God has come sound as to which it was that we would have to accept. So the right course is to say of it what God said, "when the plague fell on them", and not go beyond it except by an explanation about which the interpreters do not disagree, namely: when God's punishment and wrath came on them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 72}

On "they said, Moses, call on your Lord by the covenant He has made with you" he says: by what He charged and commanded you; "if you lift the plague from us" means if you remove the punishment we are in; "we shall believe you" means we shall hold true what you brought and called to, and acknowledge it to you; "and send the Children of Israel with you" means we shall let them go with you and not stop them from going where they wish. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "plague", "covenant" and "removal". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 73}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
