## Summary
Verse 1 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 1 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 1 (pp. 361-385)**

The commentary on Surat al-Anfal begins on p. 361, after two title pages (359-360), with the basmala; then al-Tabari says, on "they ask you about the spoils; say, the spoils belong to God and the Messenger": the interpreters differed over the meaning of the "anfal" that God mentions in this place. Some said they are the booty, and said the sense is: your companions ask you, Muhammad, about the booty that you and your companions took on the day of Badr, whose it is; say, it belongs to God and the Messenger. Reports 15628, from Ikrima, 15629 and 15630, from Mujahid, 15631 and 15632, from al-Dahhak, 15633 and 15634, from Ibn Abbas, 15635, from Qatada, 15636, from Ibn Zayd, and 15637, from Ata: the anfal are the booty. Others said they are the spoils of the raiding parties, and report 15638, from Ali ibn Salih ibn Hayy: the raiding parties. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 361} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 362}

Others said the anfal are what strays from the polytheists to the Muslims, of a slave or a beast and the like. Report 15639, from Ata: it is what strays from the polytheists to the Muslims without fighting, a beast, slave or goods, which belongs to the Prophet to do with as he wishes; report 15640, from Ata: what strays from the polytheists to the Muslims without fighting, of a slave, a female slave, goods or baggage, belongs to the Prophet to do with as he wishes, the editor explaining "thiql" as the traveller's baggage and retinue; report 15641, from al-Zuhri that Ibn Abbas was asked about the anfal and said the stripped gear and the horse; report 15642, from Ibn Abbas: the anfal is what is taken of goods that fall after the booty is divided, which is an extra for God and the Messenger; report 15643, from Muhammad ibn Shihab that a man said to Ibn Abbas, what are the anfal? He said the horse, the coat of mail and the spear, the editor identifying Uthman ibn Abi Sulayman al-Nawfali as trustworthy and a judge of Mecca and noting that Abu Ubayd gave it in Kitab al-Amwal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 363}

Report 15644, from Ata: the anfal are the stray horse, the coat of mail and the garment; report 15645, from Ibn Abbas: a man would give a man the gear and the horse of another as an extra, the editor noting a mark in the manuscript for reversing the order of "horse" and "gear"; report 15646, from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: I heard a man ask Ibn Abbas about the anfal and Ibn Abbas said the horse is of the extra and the gear is of the extra; he returned to his question and Ibn Abbas said the same again, then the man said the anfal that God mentions in His book, what are they? He kept on asking him until he nearly drove him out, and Ibn Abbas said, do you know what this is like? It is like Sabigh whom Umar ibn al-Khattab beat, the editor noting that Malik gave it in the Muwatta; report 15647, from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: Ibn Abbas said that when Umar was asked about something he would say I neither command you nor forbid you; then Ibn Abbas said by God, God sent His prophet only as a restrainer and commander, making lawful and forbidding; al-Qasim said a man was set on Ibn Abbas asking about the anfal and Ibn Abbas said a man would give a man the horse and weapons of another as an extra; the man returned to him and he said the same, then returned again until he angered him, and Ibn Abbas said, do you know what this is like? Like Sabigh whom Umar beat until the blood ran down his heels, or his feet, and the man said as for you, God has taken vengeance on you for Umar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 363} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 364}

Report 15647 closes with the further report from Ata: they ask you about what strays from the polytheists to the Muslims without fighting, of a beast or slave, which is a bonus for the Prophet. In a note on the Sabigh story the editor says he came to Umar in Medina and began asking about the ambiguous verses of the Qur'an, and Umar beat him until his head bled and he said enough, Commander of the Believers, what I used to feel in my head has gone; Umar banished him to Basra and wrote to them not to sit with him, and he stayed lowly among his people after having been their chief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 365}
Others said the "nafl" is the fifth God assigned to the people of the fifth. Report 15648, from Mujahid: it is the fifth; the Emigrants said, why is this fifth not lifted from us, why is it taken out of ours? and God said it belongs to God and the Messenger; report 15649, from Mujahid: they asked the Prophet about the fifth after the four fifths and the verse was revealed. Al-Tabari says: the best of these views on the meaning of the anfal is that of those who said they are additions the imam adds for some of the army or all of it, either from his own, over and above their rights in the division, or from what reaches him by way of bonus or one of its causes, to encourage him and to urge those with him in his army to what is good for them and for the Muslims or for one of the two parties. Into this may come what Ibn Abbas said, that it is the horse and the coat of mail and the like, and what Ata said, that it is what returns from the polytheists to the Muslims of a slave or horse, since that is for the imam to decide when what they got was not by victory and force, doing what is good for the people of Islam; and into it may come what the army overcame by force. The editor gives the manuscript's reading of the phrase "from his own" and says the printed edition's wording has no sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 365} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 366}
He says it is the best of the views because "nafl" in Arab speech is an addition to a thing, one saying "I gave you a nafl of such" and "I added to you" when you added to him, and the "anfal" are the plural of "nafl"; he cites a line of Labid ibn Rabi'a at the opening of one of his poems, saying that fear of our Lord is the best addition. Since that is the sense, everyone of the fighters of the army who is given more than his share of the booty, whether for hardship he endured or service he rendered to the Muslims, by the ruler's giving him a bonus, then the ruling on it for him is like the gear a killer strips, and he is one given a bonus on what is added, because the addition is a "nafl" even though he may in some cases be entitled to it by right, and it is not of the booty on which division falls; and so too whatever is granted by small gift to one who has no share in the booty is a "nafl", for though it was taken by force it is not of what division fell upon. The editor explains "radakha" as giving a modest small gift, and notes differences of wording in the sentence between manuscript and printed edition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 366}
The difference between booty and bonus, then, is that booty is what God gave back to the Muslims of the property of the polytheists by victory and force, whether someone gave a bonus from it or not, and the bonus is what a man is given for service and hardship from the army without a division. Since that is the meaning of "nafl", the sense of the speech is: your companions ask you, Muhammad, about the surplus of the property on which division falls from the booty of the unbelievers of Quraysh who were killed at Badr, whose it is; say to them, it belongs to God and His Messenger, not to you, who places it where he wills. The cause for which this verse was revealed was disputed. Some said it was revealed about the booty of Badr, because the Prophet had promised bonuses to some for hardship; some endured it and others stayed with the Messenger, and they disputed over it after the war ended, so God sent down this verse teaching them that what the Messenger of God did with it is effective and permitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 367}
Report 15650, from Ibn Abbas: the Prophet said, whoever comes to such and such a place shall have such and such, or does such and such shall have such and such; the young men hurried to it and the elders stayed by the standards, and when God gave them victory they came to ask for what the Prophet had set for them, and the elders said do not go off with it without us; so God sent down the verse [[passage/quran-8-1|8:1]], with "so fear God and set right what is between you". Report 15651, from Ibn Abbas: on the day of Badr the Messenger of God said whoever does such and such shall have such and such; the young men hurried to it and the elders stayed under the standards, and when the booty came they came to ask for what was set for them, and the elders said do not take it all to yourselves, we were a support for you and under the standards and had you been routed you would have turned to us; they quarrelled, and God sent down the verse [[passage/quran-8-1|8:1]]; report 15652, from Ibn Abbas, in the same sense, with the young men refusing and saying the Messenger of God set it for us, and God sent down the verse, saying that was better for them, and so too obey me, for I know; report 15653, from Ikrima in the same sense, with the elders saying at the division, we are the people of the standards and were a support for you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 367} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 368} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 369}
The editor says al-Tabari gives Ibn Abbas's report by four chains, that it is sound in chain, given by al-Hakim in the Mustadrak who calls it sound though not included by the two compilers, with al-Dhahabi agreeing, by al-Bayhaqi in the Great Sunan, traced by Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, and by Abu Dawud in his Sunan (no. 2737), ending there with "so too obey me, for I know better than you the outcome of this"; and that the "rid'" is the helper who supports a man and strengthens his back, and "the people were routed" means they fled. He identifies Ishaq ibn Shahin al-Wasiti, al-Tabari's teacher, Khalid ibn Abd Allah al-Tahhan and Abd al-A'la ibn Abd al-A'la al-Qurashi al-Sami, trustworthy. Report 15654, from Ubada ibn al-Samit: God sent down the verse when the people differed over the booty on the day of Badr, and the Messenger of God divided it among them equally; report 15655, from Abu Umama al-Bahili: I asked Ubada ibn al-Samit about the anfal and he said it was revealed about us, the people of Badr, when we differed over the bonus and our characters turned bad over it, so God took it from our hands and gave it to the Messenger of God, who divided it among the Muslims equally, and in that was fear of God, obedience to His Messenger and setting right what is between. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 369} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 370} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 371}
In the notes the editor identifies the men of the chain of 15654: Ishaq ibn al-Hajjaj al-Tahuni; Ya'qub ibn Muhammad ibn Isa al-Zuhri, disputed and trustworthy God willing; al-Mughira ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith al-Makhzumi, disputed, held trustworthy by Ibn Ma'in; his father Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith ibn Ayyash, trustworthy; Sulayman ibn Musa al-Umawi al-Ashdaq, trustworthy; Makhul the Syrian jurist and Follower, taken captive from Kabul, with a hesitancy in his speech; Abu Sallam al-Aswad the Abyssinian, Mamtur, of the first generation of the Followers of Syria; and Abu Umama al-Bahili, Sudayy ibn Ajlan, a Companion of the Messenger of God. He says this report, from Ibn Ishaq, is in Ibn Hisham's Sira, that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad by two chains, and also al-Bayhaqi and al-Hakim in the Mustadrak, who calls it sound on Muslim's condition with al-Dhahabi agreeing; al-Haythami mentions it and says the men of both chains are trustworthy; and Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti trace it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 370} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 371}
Others said the verse was rather revealed because one of the companions of the Messenger of God asked him for something of the spoils before the division and he did not give it to him, it being shared among the army, so God made all of it the Messenger's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 371}
Report 15656, from Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas: I came to the Prophet on the day of Badr with a sword and said, Messenger of God, this sword is one with which God has healed my breast of the polytheists; I asked him for it and he said, this is neither mine nor yours. When I turned away I said, I fear he will give it to one who did not endure what I endured; and the Messenger of God was behind me, and I said, I fear something has come down about me. He said, the sword has become mine; and he gave it to me, and the verse [[passage/quran-8-1|8:1]] came down. Report 15657, from Sa'd in the same sense: this is neither mine nor yours; I went back and said, perhaps this will be given to one who did not endure what I endured; the messenger came to me and I said, something has happened about me; when I reached him he said, Sa'd, you asked me for the sword and it was not mine, and it has become mine, so it is yours; and the verse came down. The editor says al-Tabari gives Sa'd's report by seven chains with different wordings, except 15659, which has a break in its chain, that the first chain is sound, that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad, Abu Dawud as no. 2740, and al-Hakim, who calls the chain sound with al-Dhahabi agreeing, and al-Bayhaqi, and that Ibn Kathir mentions it, saying Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i gave it and al-Tirmidhi called it good and sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 371} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 372}
Report 15658, from Sa'd: I got a sword on the day of Badr and it pleased me and I said, Messenger of God, give it to me, so God sent down the verse. Report 15659, from Muhammad ibn Ubayd Allah from Sa'd: on the day of Badr my brother Umayr was killed and I killed Sa'id ibn al-As and took his sword, which was called Dhu al-Katifa, and brought it to the Prophet who said, go and throw it in the store; I threw it and went back, with something in me that only God knows from the killing of my brother and the taking of my spoil, and I had gone only a short way when the Surah of the Spoils came down on him, and he said, go and take your sword. The editor explains "katifa" as a broad long iron blade and "qabad" as the place where the booty is gathered, and says Muhammad ibn Ubayd Allah, Abu Awn al-Thaqafi, is a trustworthy Follower who did not reach Sa'd, so his narration is mursal and the report weak by the break; that Abu Ubayd gave it in Kitab al-Amwal with "al-As ibn Sa'id", the right form being as Ibn Hajar says in al-Isaba, the disagreement being over who killed him, Ali ibn Abi Talib or Sa'd, and he refers to al-Rawd al-Unuf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 372} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 373} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 374}
Report 15660, from Abu Usayd Malik ibn Rabi'a: I got the sword of the Banu A'idh on the day of Badr, the sword being called al-Marzuban; when the Messenger of God commanded them to return what they had of the bonus goods, I came with it and threw it among the bonus goods, and the Messenger of God never refused anything he was asked for; al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam al-Makhzumi saw it and asked the Messenger of God, who gave it to him. Report 15661, from Uthman ibn al-Arqam: the Messenger of God said on the day of Badr, return what was of the bonus goods; Abu Usayd al-Sa'idi put down the sword of Ibn A'idh, al-Marzuban, and al-Arqam knew it and said give it to me, Messenger of God, and he gave it to him. The editor sets out in a long note the names he corrected in the printed edition and manuscript, and notes that Ibn Ishaq gave it in his Sira, that 15661 is an abridgement of the one before, and that of Yahya ibn Imran ibn Uthman ibn al-Arqam Ibn Abi Hatim says an unknown elder of Medina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 374} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 375}
Report 15662, from Sa'd: I got a sword and brought it to the Messenger of God and said, give it to me as a bonus; he said, put it down; then I stood and said, give it to me as a bonus; he said, put it down; then I stood and said, give it to me as a bonus, am I to be made like one who did nothing? and the Prophet said, put it where you took it from; and this verse came down. Report 15663, from Sa'd: I took a sword from the booty and said, Messenger of God, give me this, and the verse came down. Report 15664, from Mujahid: Sa'd said, I had taken the sword of Sa'id ibn al-As ibn Umayya and came to the Messenger of God and said give me this sword, Messenger of God, and he was silent, and the verse came down up to "if you are believers", and the Messenger of God gave it to me. The editor says 15662 is sound in chain and is given by Ahmad, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi, Muslim in his Sahih, al-Bayhaqi, Ibn Kathir and Abu Ja'far al-Nahhas in al-Nasikh wa al-Mansukh, and that 15664 is weak in chain because Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban al-Umawi is a liar who forged hadiths, but he adds that the mention of Sa'id ibn al-As in this report is consistent, since his sword was famous among his son al-As ibn Sa'id, who was killed at Badr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 376} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 377}
Others said the verse was revealed because the companions of the Messenger of God asked that the booty be divided among them on the day of Badr, and God let them know that it belongs to God and His Messenger, not to them, and they have no part in it. They said "about" in this place means "from", the sense being "they ask you from the spoils", and said Ibn Mas'ud used to read it so, without "about", on this interpretation. Report 15665, from al-A'mash: the companions of Abd Allah used to read it without "about"; report 15666, from al-Dahhak: it is so in Ibn Mas'ud's reading. Report 15667, from Ibn Abbas: the anfal are the booty, which was the Messenger of God's alone, none having any part in it, whatever the Muslim raiding parties took they brought to him, and whoever kept back a needle or a thread of it is guilty of misappropriation; they asked the Messenger of God to give them some of it, and God said: they ask you about the spoils; say, the spoils are Mine, I have given them to My messenger, you have no part in them, so fear God and set right what is between you; then God sent down [[passage/quran-8-41|8:41]], and he divided that fifth for the Messenger of God and those named in the verse. The editor explains "ghulul" as treachery in the booty, and says Ali ibn Abi Talha's chain is broken since he did not hear the commentary from Ibn Abbas, and that al-Bayhaqi gave it by this route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 378}
Report 15668, from Ibn Jurayj: it was revealed about the Emigrants and the Helpers who were at Badr; they disagreed and were in three parties, and the verse came down… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 378}
…and God made the Messenger its owner to divide as God showed him. Report 15669, from Amr ibn Shu'ayb from his father from his grandfather: the people asked the Prophet for the booty on the day of Badr and the verse came down; report 15670, from al-Dahhak: they ask you to give them a bonus; report 15671, from Ikrima: they ask you for the spoils. The editor says al-Hajjaj ibn Artat is trustworthy but practised concealment in chains in narrating from Amr ibn Shu'ayb, that Muhammad ibn Nasr said the greater part of his hadith is mursal, concealed and altered in wording, that this one does not state hearing, so the report is weak by that flaw; and that Amr ibn Shu'ayb was faulted for narrating much from his father from his grandfather, Abu Zur'a saying he heard only a few hadiths and took a sheet he had and narrated it, though he is trustworthy in himself. Al-Tabari chooses: the best view is that God reported in this verse of people who asked the Messenger of God for the spoils to be given them, and God told them they are God's and that He has given them to His Messenger; and if that is the sense, it may be that it was revealed because of the companions of the Messenger of God differing over them, or because of Sa'd's asking for the sword, or because of the request of those who asked that it be divided among the army. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 378} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 380}
They differed over it, whether it is abrogated or not. Some said it is abrogated, and said [[passage/quran-8-41|8:41]] abrogated it. Report 15672, from Mujahid and Ikrima: the spoils were God's and the Messenger's and the verse of the fifth abrogated them; report 15673, from al-Suddi: Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas got a sword at Badr and he and others disputed over it, so they asked the Prophet and he took it from them, and God said "they ask you about the spoils", the booty that day being the Prophet's alone, and God abrogated it with the fifth; report 15674, from Mujahid: the verse abrogated it; report 15675, from Mujahid and Ikrima, or Ikrima and Amir: the spoils were abrogated by the verse of the fifth. The editor notes that Salim, freedman of Umm Muhammad, appears in the chain and is Salim Abu Ubayd Allah, freedman of Umm Ali, a leading companion of Mujahid, whom Ibn Hibban listed among the trustworthy. Others said it is firm and not abrogated, the sense being "say, the spoils are God's", and they are without doubt God's together with the world and what is in it and the hereafter, "and the Messenger's", who places them where God commanded him to place them. Report 15676, from Ibn Zayd: "they ask you about the spoils" up to "if you are believers"; so submit to God and His Messenger, who judge about them as they will and put them where they wish, and they said yes; then after the forty came the verse of the fifth, [[passage/quran-8-41|8:41]], and you have four fifths; and the Prophet said on the day of Khaybar this fifth is returned to your poor; God and His Messenger do with that fifth what they like and put it where they like; then God told us what He likes of that, and he recited [[passage/quran-59-7|59:7]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 380} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 381}
Al-Tabari chooses: the right thing to say is that God made the spoils for His prophet to give a bonus to whom he wills; he gave the killer the stripped gear, gave the army a quarter at the outward leg and a third at the return after the fifth, and gave some people after their shares a camel each in some expeditions; so God made the ruling on the spoils the Prophet's, giving bonuses as he sees fit for the good of the Muslims, and those after him of the imams are to follow his practice in that. There is nothing in the verse to show that its ruling is abrogated, since it admits the sense described; it is not permitted to judge that a ruling the Qur'an brought down is abrogated except by a proof one must submit to, and he has shown in several places in his books that nothing is abrogated except what a ruling that comes after it nullifies in all its senses, or a report comes that makes it binding that one abrogates the other. The editor explains "bad'a" as the start of a raid and "raj'a" as the return from it, and that when a detachment set out from the body of the army advancing on the enemy and struck at the enemy's strength, it had a quarter and the rest of the army shared in three quarters of what they took, while if it did so at the army's return it had a third, since the second round is harder on them and the danger greater. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 381} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 382}
It is reported of Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab that he denied that anyone may be given a bonus after the Messenger of God, interpreting God's words "say: the spoils are God's and the Messenger's"; report 15677, from Muhammad ibn Amr: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab sent his servant to some people who asked him about something and said, you sent to me asking about the spoils, and there is no bonus after the Messenger of God. Al-Tabari says he has shown that the imams may follow the Messenger of God in their expeditions by his practice and give bonuses on the lines of what he gave, when giving a bonus is for the good of the Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 382}
On "so fear God and set right what is between you and obey God and His Messenger, if you are believers", al-Tabari says: fear God, people, and guard against Him by obeying Him and avoiding disobedience, and set right the state among you. The interpreters differed over what is meant by "set right what is between you". Some said it is a command from God to those who took the booty on the day of Badr and were present at the battle with the Messenger of God, when they differed over the booty, to give back what they got of it to one another. Report 15678, from Qatada: the Prophet of God would give a believer the stripped gear of an unbeliever he killed as a bonus, and then God sent down the verse and commanded them to give back to one another; report 15679, from Ibn Jurayj: it reached me that the Prophet used to give a man a bonus according to his effort and service as he saw fit, until on the day of Badr, when people filled their hands with booty, the weak among the people said the strong have gone off with the booty; they mentioned it to the Prophet and the verse came down, so that the strong would give back to the weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 383}
Others said this is a charge from God on the people and a prohibition against dispute over the booty and other matters. Report 15680, from Mujahid: He charged them; report 15681, from Ibn Abbas: this is a charge from God on the believers to fear Him and set right what is between them, Sufyan saying this was when they differed over the booty on the day of Badr; report 15682, from al-Suddi: that is, do not abuse one another. The people of Arabic differed over the reason for the feminine in "bayn": some Basran grammarians said he added "dhat" to "bayn" and made it a "dhat" because some things take a feminine name and others a masculine, like "dar" and "ha'it", which are feminine and masculine; others said he meant by "dhat baynikum" the state belonging to the between, as in "dhat al-isha'", the hour in which the evening meal is, and they do not use a masculine for a feminine or a feminine for a masculine but for a reason. Al-Tabari says this second view is the better of the two, for the reason he gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 384}
As for "obey God and His Messenger", the sense is: come to God's command and His Messenger's command, you who seek the spoils, in what God gave back to you, since he has made clear to you its ways and uses, "if you are believers" meaning if you trust the Messenger of God in what he brought you from your Lord. Report 15683, from Ibn Zayd: submit to God and His Messenger, who judge about them as they will and put them where they wish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 385}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
