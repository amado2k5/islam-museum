## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and among them are unlettered ones", al-Tabari says those meant are among the Jews, reported from Abu al-'Aliya, al-Rabi' and Mujahid; "ummiyyun" are those who neither write nor read. He cites the saying "We are an unlettered community, neither writing nor reckoning", which the editor calls a sound report. He reports the same from Ibrahim and Ibn Zayd, and from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak a differing view, that they are people who believed no messenger and no scripture God sent and wrote a book with their own hands. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 257} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 258}

Al-Tabari rejects the view of Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak that the "ummiyyun" are people who believed no messenger and no book, saying it runs against common Arabic usage, in which an "ummi" is one who does not write; the editor notes that Ibn Kathir doubts this report from Ibn Abbas on that chain. Al-Tabari thinks the "ummi" is so called as if tied to his mother, since writing was among men not women, citing "who sent among the unlettered a messenger from among them"; and he prefers al-Nakha'i's view that it means one who cannot write. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 258} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 259}
"They do not know the Book" means the Torah, that they do not know its rulings and obligations, being like cattle, reported from Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd and al-Dahhak. On "except wishful thinking" they differ: Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak and Mujahid, lies spoken with their mouths; Qatada and Abu al-'Aliya, that they wish on God what is not theirs; Ibn Abbas through 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, tales; Ibn Zayd, that they wished and said "we are of the People of the Book" when they were not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 259} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 260} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 261}
In his own choice al-Tabari prefers Ibn Abbas's report through al-Dahhak and Mujahid's view, that the "amani" here is invention and conjecture of falsehood, citing 'Uthman's saying "I never sang nor invented". His evidence is "they only conjecture", since one who recites the book is not said to conjecture, nor does one who wishes conjecture what he wishes, so "amani" is not of the Book's kind, as in "they have no knowledge of it but the following of conjecture". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 263}
