## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains, "And the jinn We created before from the fire of poison": by jinn here he meant Iblis, the father of the jinn, who was created before Adam — from Qatadah, Iblis was created before Adam, but Adam was created the last creation, so Iblis envied him for the honor God gave him, saying, 'I am my fire and this is clay.' In "Fire of Poisons," there are sayings: from Ibn Abbas the hot poisons that kill; in another narration from him, it was struck by a tornado with fire and burned; and from Al-Dahhak who blazed from the fire of poisons; and it is narrated from Ibn Abbas that Iblis was from a living angel called the jinn, created from the fire of poisons, and that the jinn mentioned in the Qur'an were created from a fire of fire; and from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, these poisons are part of seventy parts from which the jinn emerged. It is mentioned that some Arabs said the poisons are at night and day, while others say the heat is the day and the poisons are at night. It is narrated from Wahb ibn Munabbih that the jinn are of various kinds: pure jinn are wind that neither eat nor drink, nor die or reproduce. Among them are the species that eat, drink, marry, and die, including the saliva, the ghoul, and the like.
