## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "yes, whoever submits his face to God while doing good" he says it is not as the claimants said, but whoever submits his face to God while doing good is the one who enters and enjoys Paradise, from al-Suddi. "Islam of the face" is humbling oneself to obey Him and submitting to His command; the root of "islam" is "istislam", and the Muslim is called so for the yielding of his limbs to the obedience of his Lord, from al-Rabi' (he was sincere to God), and he cites a verse of Zayd ibn Amr ibn Nufayl. The face is singled out because it is the noblest of a person's members and the most sacred to him, so if he yields it, the rest are more fit to yield; and the Arabs ascribe a thing to "its face" meaning the thing itself, as in a verse of al-A'sha and one of Dhu al-Rumma. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 510} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 511}

Al-Tabari completes his account of submitting the face: the sense is, yes, whoever submits his body to God, his person yielding to Him in obedience, while doing good in that, his reward is with his Lord; "face" is mentioned in place of "person" because the speech shows the meaning. "While doing good" means while he is in a state of doing good, so it means whoever is sincere in his obedience and worship of God, doing well in doing so. On "his reward is with his Lord, and no fear shall be upon them, nor shall they grieve" he says: for the one who submits his face to God, doing good, is his repayment and reward with God in the Hereafter; "no fear upon them" of His punishment and the torment of Hell and of what they bring of their deeds; "nor shall they grieve" over what they left behind in the world or that they be denied the bliss they came to. He explains the singular in "his reward" and the plural in "upon them" by "man" being singular in form and plural in sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 511} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 512} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 513}
