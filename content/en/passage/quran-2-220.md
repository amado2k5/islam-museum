## In al-Tabari's commentary

The interpreters differed over what this verse, "they ask you about orphans", was revealed about. Some said it was revealed about those who set apart the wealth of the orphans in their care and disliked mixing with them in food or otherwise, when the prohibition came down on approaching an orphan's property except in the best way (6:152) and the warning on consuming orphans' property unjustly (4:10). {c(349)} From Ibn 'Abbas: when the prohibition came down they set apart the orphans' property and told the Prophet, and the permission to mix with them came down, so they mixed with them (4182); al-Hakim relates it, grades its chain sound and al-Dhahabi agrees. From Ibn 'Abbas: whoever had an orphan went and set his food apart from the orphan's food and his drink from his drink, and whatever was left of the orphan's food was kept for him until he ate it or it spoiled; this weighed on them, they told the Prophet, the verse came down, and they mixed their food with theirs and their drink with theirs (4183); from Sa'id, meaning Ibn 'Abbas as the report's context shows: we would make food for the orphan and some was left over, and they left it until it spoiled, so the verse came down (4184). {c(349,350)} From 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: when the prohibition came down, mixing with them was avoided and they were wary of everything, even water; when the permission came down they mixed with them (4185). From Qatada: God had sent down earlier, in surat al-Isra', the prohibition (17:34), and it weighed heavily on them, so they would not mix with them in food or anything else; this became hard for them, and God sent down the permission (4186, 4187). {c(350,351)} From al-Rabi': we were told that the prohibition was sent down in al-Isra' up to their reaching maturity, it weighed on them, and the permission came down, including mixing with them in riding the animal, drinking the milk and being served by the servant; the guardian who manages their affairs is not at fault if he rides the animal, drinks the milk or is served by the servant (4188). {c(351)}

To complete the report from Ibn 'Abbas (4189): the man set apart the orphan's food, drink and vessels, which weighed on the Muslims, so the permission to mix with them came down; al-Nasa'i relates it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 351} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 352} From al-Sha'bi: when the warning on consuming orphans' property came down, people avoided orphans, and a man would set apart his food, property and drink; this became hard, so the verse came down; al-Sha'bi said, whoever mixes with an orphan should be generous to him, and whoever mixes in order to eat from his property should not do so (4190). From Ibn 'Abbas by 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: the Muslims disliked taking orphans in and felt qualms about mixing with them in anything, so they asked the Prophet and the verse came down (4191). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 352} From 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah: when surat al-Nisa' came down people set apart their food, then came to the Prophet and said, it is hard for us to set apart the orphans' food when they eat with us, and the verse came down. From Mujahid: they separated their food, milk and relishes from theirs, which was hard, and mixing is in pasture and relishes. From Ibn 'Abbas: milk, the servant's service and riding the animal; and Ibn Juraij added, and in dwellings, which were then scarce (4192). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 352} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 353} From Ibn 'Abbas: people avoided the orphan's property and food until it spoiled, meat or otherwise; they complained to the Prophet and the verse came down (4193), also in al-Nasa'i. From Mujahid (Abu 'Asim doubting the chain): mixing with the orphan in pasture and relishes (4194). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 353}

Others said, rather, avoiding an orphan's property was a trait of the Arabs, so they sought a ruling because it was hard on them, and were answered as God set out in His book. From al-Suddi: the Arabs were strict about the orphan, so as not to eat with him from one bowl, ride a camel for him or use a servant for him; they asked the Prophet, who said that putting his property and affairs right is better, and if one mixes with him, eats with him, feeds him, rides his mount and carries him on it, uses his servant and serves him, that is better still (4195). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 353} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 354} From Ibn 'Abbas: when someone had an orphan in his care he put the orphan's food aside and his milk aside for fear of sin, and hardship befell the believers so they had nothing to provide servants for the orphans, and the verse came down (4196). From al-Dahhak: in the Jahiliyya they held the orphan's matter in great regard and would not touch anything of his property, ride a beast for him or feed him; in Islam severe hardship befell them until they needed the orphans' property, so they asked the Prophet about the orphans and mixing with them, and mixing here means riding the animal, the servant's service and drinking the milk (4197). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 354}

Al-Tabari says the meaning of the verse is: your companions ask you, Muhammad, about the orphans' property and their mixing their own with it in spending, food, drink, lodging and service; say to them that your showing them kindness by putting their property right, without diminishing any of it and without taking a fee from it for doing so, is better for you with God and greater in reward, and better for them in this world through their property being preserved; and if you mix, sharing your wealth with theirs in spending, food, drink and lodging, and add something from their property in return for managing their affairs, they are your brothers and brothers help one another, so the wealthy help the needy and the strong help the weak; if you gain some benefit from their property while looking to their interest as an affectionate brother to his brother, that is lawful for you, since you are brothers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 354} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 355} From Ibn Zayd: a man may mix with his brother (4198); from Ibrahim: I dislike that an orphan's property should be like filth (`urra) (4199); from 'A'isha: I dislike that an orphan's property with me should be filth, so I mix his food with mine and his drink with mine (4200); the editor notes the report is in Ibn Kathir's tafsir and al-Durr al-manthur and that he found it nowhere else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 355}

Al-Tabari raises a grammatical question: why is "ikhwanukum" in the nominative, while "rijalan aw rukbanan" in another place of the surah (2:239) is in the accusative? He answers that the meanings differ: believers' orphans are the believers' brothers whether they mix with them or not, so the sense is, if you mix with them, they are your brothers, the nominative resting on an understood "they"; had the sense been that they become brothers by the mixing, the reading would be accusative, meaning "mix with your brothers". "On foot or riding" is accusative because they are transient states with which "he" does not fit. He illustrates with "if you wear clothes, then white", accusative as a command and nominative as a statement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 356} Asked whether the accusative is allowed for "ikhwanukum", he says it is permissible in Arabic, but forbidden in reading because the readers agree on the nominative; in Arabic it is permissible because repeating the verb fits: "your brothers you mix with". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 357} (The editor notes that the fourth part of the old manuscript he relied on begins at the start of the commentary on this verse.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 357}

On "God knows the corrupter from the one who sets right", al-Tabari says: your Lord has permitted you to mix with the orphans, so fear God in yourselves lest you mix with them while wanting to consume their property wrongfully, making the mixing a means to ruin it, for He knows what each who mixes with his orphan intends, to ruin his property and eat it wrongfully or to set it right and make it grow, and nothing is hidden from Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 357} From Ibn Zayd: God knows, when you mix your property with his, whether you want to set his right or ruin it and eat it without right (4201); from al-Sha'bi: whoever mixes with an orphan should be generous to him, and whoever mixes in order to eat his property should not (4202); the editor notes that the chain is incomplete in print and restores it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 358}

On "and had God willed He would have burdened you (la-a`natakum)": it means, had God willed He would have forbidden what He allowed you of mixing your property with your orphans', which would have strained you and been hard, so that you could not fulfil what God's right required of you in it; but He gave you leave out of mercy. The interpreters differed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 358} From Mujahid (Abu 'Asim doubting the chain): He would have forbidden you the pasture and relishes (4203), which al-Tabari explains as the orphan's guardian grazing his own beasts with the orphan's and eating from his relish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 359} From Ibn 'Abbas: had God willed He would have constrained you and made it narrow, but He widened and eased (and he cites the verse of al-Nisa' on the poor guardian eating with propriety, 4:6) (4204); from Qatada: strained you so that you could not fulfil a right or discharge an obligation (4205); from al-Rabi' the same (4206); from al-Suddi: made it hard for you (4207); from Ibn Zayd: made the matter burdensome, which is `anat (4208); from Ibn 'Abbas: had God willed He would have made what you take of the orphans' property a destroyer (muwbiq) (4209). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 359} Al-Tabari says these views are close in meaning: whoever is forbidden something has been constrained in it, whoever is constrained is put in difficulty, whoever is put in difficulty is strained, and all return to hardship; so one says "`anita" of a man when a matter is hard on him, as in "it grieves him what hardship you suffer" (9:128) and "that is for whoever of you fears hardship" (4:25), and "a`natahu" when one lays on him what strains him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 359} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 360} Others said it means "He would have destroyed and ruined you"; from Ibn 'Abbas, He would have made what you took of the orphans' property a destroyer (4210, 4211). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 360} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 361}

On "God is mighty, wise": it means He is mighty in His authority, nothing stopping Him from the punishment He would bring on you had He burdened you with obligations you could not fulfil and then fell short; but out of His bounty He spared you that burden; and He is wise in that and in all His rulings and governance, no flaw or deficiency entering His acts, since they are the acts of the Wise who does not ignore the outcomes of matters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 361}
