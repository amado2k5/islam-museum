**Commentary on verse 35 (pp. 86-89)**

On [[passage/quran-10-35|10:35]] he says: say to these polytheists, is there among your partners whom you call upon besides God, their gods and idols, any who guides to the truth, that is, guides one who has strayed out of his error to the right way and sets straight one who has swerved from guidance onto the clear straight road? They cannot claim that their gods guide a stray or set right a deviant, for if they claimed it, what can be seen would belie them and trial by sight would show their helplessness. When they say no and admit it, say to them: God guides the one who strayed from guidance to the truth; is then He who guides the stray to the truth more worthy to be followed in what He calls to, or one who cannot be guided unless he is guided? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 86}

The readers differed on the verb. Most of the readers of Medina read it with the ha silent and the dal doubled, joining two silent letters. What led them to this, it seems, is that they took the root of the word to be "is not guided", found the mushaf spelling different from what they read, and so dropped the ta when it merged into the dal, kept the ha silent as it was, and doubled the dal to absorb the ta; they did likewise in their readings at [[passage/quran-4-154|4:154]] and [[passage/quran-36-49|36:49]]. Some readers of Mecca, Syria and Basra read it with the ha voweled with fatha and the dal doubled, moving the vowel of the ta onto the silent ha and merging the ta into the dal. The editor refers to earlier and later places on this reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 87}

Some readers of Kufa read it with kasra on the ha and the dal doubled, along the lines the Medinans intended but with the ha given kasra for the dal's kasra, to avoid a fatha followed by a kasra in one letter. Most of the readers of Kufa read it with a silent ha and a single dal, saying the Arabs use the plain form in the sense of "was guided", so that the sense is: or one who is not guided unless he is guided. The editor notes that the printed text spoiled the wording here and that he keeps the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 88}

Al-Tabari says the most correct reading is that with fatha on the ha and the dal doubled, for the reason given for those who read so, and because no one learned in the speech of the Arabs rejects its soundness, though some object; and the best speech to read is that in the most eloquent of the dialects in which God's speech came down. So the sense is: is he who guides to the truth more worthy to be followed, or he who is not guided to anything unless he is guided? Some interpreters claimed it means one who cannot move from his place unless he is moved. Report 17660, from Mujahid: it means the idols, and God guides from them and from others whom He wills, for what He wills; report 17661, from him: the idol. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 88}

As for "what is the matter with you, how do you judge?": do you not know that one who guides to the truth is more worthy of being followed than one who is not guided to anything unless another guide guides him? Then leave following and worshipping one who is guided to nothing, follow the one who guides you in the darknesses of land and sea, and give worship to Him sincerely and alone, apart from the gods and idols you join with Him in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 89}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
