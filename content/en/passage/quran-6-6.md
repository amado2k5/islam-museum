## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 6 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: have these who deny my signs and reject your prophethood not seen how many generations I destroyed before them, the peoples for whom I prepared the lands and earth as I never prepared them for these, and gave them in it what I never gave them? Report 13072, from Qatada: "we established them in the land as we never established you": we gave them what we never gave you. The rain fell and the trees yielded fruit to them, the earth gave them the yield of its plants, they cut the rocks of its mountains, the sky poured out its rain on them and springs gushed beneath them by my leave; then they scorned their Lord's favour, disobeyed their Creator's messenger, defied the command of their Maker and rebelled until my word was due against them, and I seized them for the sins they committed and punished them for what their hands earned, destroying some by the quake and some by the cry and others by other kinds of punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 263}

"We sent the sky upon them in abundance" means the rain, and "abundance" means copious and constant; "and we raised up other generations after them" means we brought into being other generations after those we destroyed, starting others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 263}

The editor keeps "a preparation I had not prepared" from the manuscript.

If someone asks what is the sense of "as we never established you" and who is addressed, when the report began at the start of the verse about an absent people, the answer is that the one addressed by "you" is the one reported on by "have they not seen", but the report carries the sense of "say": say, Muhammad, to these people who denied the truth when it came to them, have they not seen how many generations before them we destroyed, whom we established on earth as we never established you? The Arabs, when they report of someone absent and put "say" into it, turn the report now to the absent and now to the address, saying "I said to Abdallah: how generous he is" and "...how generous you are"; this is very common in their speech and verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 264}

A Basran grammarian used to say that it is as if he told the Prophet and then addressed him with them, and cited [[passage/quran-10-22|10:22]], where the wording is of the absent though the address is to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 264}

The editor refers to earlier pages of volumes 1, 2, 3 and 6.

Verse 6 closes here at p. 264, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-7|6:7]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
