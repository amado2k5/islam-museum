## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 48 to 49 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Say, indeed my Lord throws the truth, the Signifier of the unseen": He says to His Prophet Muhammad: "Say," O Muhammad, to the polytheists of your people, "Indeed, my Lord throws the truth," which is revelation. He means: He brings it down from the sky, then casts it to His Prophet Muhammad, the "Knower of the unseen," meaning: a Knower of what is hidden from sight, nor manifest, and what is not from what is in existence. This is an attribute of the Lord, except that He is raised to indicate His coming after the predicate. The Arabs do the same when the adjective appears after the predicate, by following the adjective as the parsing of what is in The predicate, they said: Your father is the noble, so the noble one is nominative as you described, and the accusative is permissible because it is an adjective for the father. He follows its parsing with 'Say, the truth has come,' meaning: Tell them, O Muhammad: The Qur'an has come, and God revealed, 'And what does falsehood begin,' meaning: 'And what creates falsehood in creation,' and falsehood is, as interpreters, Iblis, 'and what he repeats,' meaning he does not know. In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah and Ibn Zayd.
