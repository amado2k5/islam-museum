## Summary
Verse 117 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/repentance-of-the-three-accepted|Repentance of the Three Accepted]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 117 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 117 (pp. 539-542)**

Al-Tabari says: God granted the turning to His command and obedience to His Prophet Muhammad, to the emigrants who left their homes and kin for the abode of Islam, and to the helpers of His Messenger for God's sake, who followed the Messenger in the hour of hardship, in expenses, mounts, provisions and water, after the hearts of some of them had almost swerved from the truth and doubted their religion because of the difficulty and hardship of the journey and campaign; then He gave them the turning back and steadfastness in their religion and sight of the truth that had almost been confused for them. "He is kind and merciful to them" means kind to those whose hearts were touched by that because of the hardship of their journey, and merciful in not destroying them by taking faith from them after what they had endured for God and borne of hardship and adversity. The editor refers to earlier explanations of emigrant, hardship, swerving, party, kind and merciful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 539}

Report 17423 is from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the hour of hardship was the campaign of Tabuk. Report 17424 is from Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Aqil, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they set out on a campaign, two or three men to a camel, in fierce heat, and suffered such thirst that they slaughtered their camels, squeezed out the stomachs and drank the water; it was a hardship of water, of mounts and of expenses. The editor says Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Aqil is weak in transmission and not relied on for his memory, and that the campaign is undoubtedly Tabuk. Report 17425 is from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the hour of hardship was Tabuk; they suffered so greatly that two men would split one date between them, and sucked a single date and drank water after it. Report 17426 is from Mujahid: the Tabuk campaign; report 17427 is from Jabir, through Zakariya ibn Adi, Ibn al-Mubarak, Ma'mar and Abdullah ibn Muhammad ibn Aqil: hardship of mounts, of provisions and of water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 540}

Report 17428 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they are those who followed the Messenger of God on the Tabuk campaign toward Syria in the fiercest heat, with the hardship God knows; it was mentioned to us that two men would split a date between them, and a group would pass a date around, one sucking it and drinking after it, then the next, and so God turned to them and brought them home from their campaign. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 541}

Reports 17429 and 17430 are from Ibn Abbas, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Amr ibn al-Harith, Sa'id ibn Abi Hilal, Utba ibn Abi Utba and Nafi ibn Jubayr ibn Mut'im, and through Ishaq ibn Ziyada al-Attar, Ya'qub ibn Muhammad and Ibn Wahb: Umar ibn al-Khattab was asked about the hardship and said we set out with the Messenger of God for Tabuk in great heat and halted at a place where thirst struck us until we thought our necks would be cut off; a man would go looking for water and not return until he thought his neck would be cut off, and a man would slaughter his camel, squeeze out its stomach contents and drink them, putting what was left on his liver. Abu Bakr said, "Messenger of God, God has accustomed you to good in prayer, so pray for us." He asked whether he would like that and Abu Bakr said yes; he raised his hands and had not lowered them when the sky brought clouds that shaded and then poured, and they filled what they had; then we went to look and did not find it, for it had not passed beyond the camp. The editor explains *farth* as the contents of the stomach, discusses the wording in the manuscript, printed text and hadith books for two phrases, says all men of the chain are trustworthy, that al-Haythami cited it in the Majma al-Zawa'id from al-Bazzar and al-Tabarani, and that al-Suyuti attributed it to Ibn Jarir, Ibn Khuzayma, Ibn Hibban, al-Hakim (who judged it sound) and others. For Ishaq ibn Ziyada al-Attar, al-Tabari's teacher, he found no notice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 542}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
