## Summary
Verse 23 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 23 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 23: "two men of those who fear God, whom God had favoured", pp. 176-179**

Al-Tabari says this is God's report on the two righteous men of Moses' people, Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh, who kept faith with what Moses entrusted to them, not telling their people of the giants' might and size, and whom God describes as fearing Him and being mindful of Him in His commands and prohibitions.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 176}

Reports 11664 and 11665, from Mujahid, name them Caleb and Joshua, the second adding that they were among the chiefs. Report 11666, from Mujahid, says all the chiefs returned forbidding their tribes to fight except Joshua and Caleb, who ordered the tribes to fight the giants; the tribes disobeyed them and obeyed the others, and these are the two God favoured. The editor records the variant spellings of Caleb's name in the printed text and the manuscript.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 176}

Report 11667, from Mujahid, is the same, adding that they were among the twelve chiefs. Report 11668, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the chiefs returned to Moses and told him what they saw; he told them to hide it from the camp, for if told, the people would lose heart and not enter the city; each went and told a relative, except Joshua and Caleb, who kept silent. Report 11669, from al-Suddi, says the two who kept silent were Joshua son of Nun, Moses' attendant, and Caleb son of Jephunneh, Moses' in-law. Report 11670, from 'Atiyya, names Caleb and Joshua, Moses' attendant.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 177}

Report 11671, from Ibn 'Abbas, and report 11672, from Qatada, name Joshua and Caleb. Report 11673, from al-Rabi', says Moses told the chiefs on their return not to tell anyone what they saw, for God would open the land for them; but the people spread the talk among the Children of Israel, and two men stood up, one reportedly Joshua and the other Caleb, and said enter upon them by the gate, up to "if you are believers".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 178}

Al-Tabari then notes that the readers differed on how to read "fear": those of the Hijaz, Iraq and Syria read it with the active form, meaning that the two were of Moses' people and feared God, and God favoured them with success. He says Qatada reported that in some readings it is "who fear God, whom God had favoured", and report 11674 gives this; al-Tabari holds it supports this interpretation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 179}

He adds that Sa'id ibn Jubayr read it with the passive form, as report 11675 relays, with a caution about whether one narrator heard from his teacher; al-Tabari takes Sa'id to mean the two were of the giants' clan, had become Muslims and followed Moses, so were descendants of the giants whom the Children of Israel feared, though differing from them in religion, and notes that a similar interpretation is reported from Ibn 'Abbas.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 179}

Report 11676, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the Holy Land is the city of the giants; when Moses camped near it he sent twelve men, the chiefs, to bring news. One of the giants met them, put them in his cloak, carried them to the city and called his people, who asked who they were; they said they were Moses' people sent to bring news of them. The giants gave them a single grape, a man's load, and said to tell Moses and his people to judge the size of their fruit. When they returned, the people told Moses to go with his Lord and fight. On this report the two men were of the city, had become Muslims and followed Moses and Aaron, and said: enter upon them by the gate.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 180}

Al-Tabari comments that on this reading and interpretation none of the twelve chiefs kept hidden what Moses had told them to hide; they all spread it, and the two who said "enter upon them by the gate" were sons of the giants, who had become Muslims and followed the prophet. He then chooses the reading with the active form as the sounder, because the readers of the regions agree on it: what the readings have widely transmitted is a proof not to be contradicted, while a lone reading may contain error or slip. The authority also agrees that the two were men of Moses' companions from the Children of Israel, Joshua and Caleb, which he holds the correct interpretation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 180}

On "whom God had favoured", al-Tabari says it means favoured with obedience to God in obeying His prophet Moses, following his order and refraining from spreading what they saw of the giants, which the other chiefs did. He mentions a view that the favour was fear, given in report 11677 from Sahl ibn 'Ali. The editor gives notes on some narrators in its chain and says he could not find Ishaq ibn al-Qasim.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 181}

Report 11678, from al-Dahhak, says the favour was guidance, so that they were on Moses' religion while in the giants' city.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 182}

**Commentary on verse 23, continued: "enter upon them by the gate", pp. 182-183**

Al-Tabari says this is the two God-fearing men's word to the Children of Israel, who had lost heart on hearing the report of the chiefs who spread what they saw: enter upon them by the gate of their city, for God is with you and will help you, and once you enter the gate you will overcome them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 182}

Report 11679, from Ibn Ishaq, from some who knew the earlier scripture, says that when the Children of Israel meant to go back to Egypt on hearing the chiefs, Moses and Aaron fell on their faces in prostration before the assembly, and Joshua and Caleb tore their clothes and told the assembly that the land they had crossed was good, that their Lord was pleased with it and had given it to them, and that it flowed with milk and honey; they asked them not to disobey God nor fear the people of the land, who were their bread and whose pride had left them, with God on their side. The assembly wanted to stone them. The editor compares this with the Torah text in the Book of Numbers and urges comparing Ibn Ishaq's reports with that old translation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 183}

Report 11680, from Qatada, says twelve men were sent as scouts; ten made their people timid and made entering hateful to them, while the two told their people to enter and follow God's command and that they would prevail. Report 11681, from Mujahid, says "the gate" is the giants' village.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 183}

We stop here at the foot of p. 183, after report 11681 from Mujahid on the giants' gate; the rest of verse 23 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
