## Summary
Verse 61 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 61 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "he is the subduer over his servants, and sends guardians over you, until when death comes to one of you our messengers take him, and they do not neglect" al-Tabari says: God is the one who overcomes his creation and is above them by his power, not the overcome one among their idols and images, abased by his lowliness; "and sends guardians over you" are his angels who follow one another over you night and day, guarding your deeds and counting them, neglecting nothing of guarding and counting them and losing nothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 409}

Report 13323, from al-Suddi: they are the angels who follow in turn, guarding him and guarding his deed. 13324, from Qatada: guardians, son of Adam, who guard your deed, your provision and your term, and when that is completed you are seized to your Lord. Al-Tabari explains: your Lord guards you by messengers who succeed one another, sending them to guard you and your deeds until death attends you and God's command descends, and when that comes to one of you our angels charged with seizing souls take him, "and they do not neglect" in it so as to lose it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 409}

The editor says the printed text has "the overcome one in his lowliness", an error and bad handling, the manuscript being right, and refers for "the subduer", "guarding" and "taking" to earlier passages.

Al-Tabari raises a question: is it not the Angel of Death who seizes souls, so how is it said "our messengers took him", messengers being plural and he one, and did God not say, [[passage/quran-32-11|32:11]], that the Angel of Death charged with them takes them? He answers: it may be that God helped the Angel of Death with helpers from himself who carry this out at his command, so the taking is ascribed to the Angel of Death though done by his helpers since they did it by his order, as the killing done by a ruler's agents and the flogging they carry out at his command is ascribed to the ruler though he did not do it himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 410}

A group of interpreters understood it so: 13325 and 13326, from Ibn Abbas: the Angel of Death has helpers among the angels; 13327, from Ibrahim: the helpers of the Angel of Death; 13328, from Ibrahim: the messengers take the souls and the Angel of Death goes off with them; 13329, 13330 and 13331, from Ibn Abbas and Ibrahim: the helpers of the Angel of Death among the angels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 410}

The editor says that in 13329 the printed text's explanation of the verse differs entirely from the manuscript, so he kept the manuscript's; and that 13330 is not in the manuscript, so he put it in brackets, supposing it a copyist's repetition because its chain is that of the one before except that it lacks Ibrahim between al-Hasan ibn Ubaydallah and Ibn Abbas.

13332, from Qatada: the Angel of Death has messengers whom he sends and the matter is raised to him; al-Kalbi said that the Angel of Death himself takes charge, handing the soul, if a believer, to the angels of mercy and, if an unbeliever, to the angels of punishment. 13333, from Qatada: the messengers take charge of seizing it and then hand it to the Angel of Death. 13334, from Ibrahim: the messengers take him, then the Angel of Death takes the souls from them; then from Mujahid: the earth was made for the Angel of Death like a basin, from which he takes wherever he wills, and he was given helpers who take the souls and then he takes them from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 411}

13335 and 13336, from Ibn Abbas and Ibrahim: the helpers of the Angel of Death among the angels; 13337, from Ibrahim: they take him and then hand him to the Angel of Death. 13338, from Abu Jafar al-Razi: I asked al-Rabi ibn Anas about the Angel of Death, is he alone the one who seizes souls; he said he is the one in charge of the souls and has helpers in it, and he cites on this [[passage/quran-7-37|7:37]], and says the Angel of Death is the one for whom each step goes from east to west, and that the believers' souls are at the lote-tree in Paradise. 13339, from Mujahid: there is no house of hair or of clay but the Angel of Death goes round it twice every day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 412}

Al-Tabari says the sense of "tafrit" is neglect, as shown before, and so the interpreters took it here: 13340, from Ibn Abbas: they do not neglect; 13341, from al-Suddi: they do not neglect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 413}

Verse 61 closes here at p. 413, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-62|6:62]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
