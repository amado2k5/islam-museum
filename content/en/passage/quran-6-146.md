## Summary
Verse 146 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 146 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 146, "and to those who were Jews We forbade every one with a claw": al-Tabari says God forbade the Jews every clawed creature, meaning among beasts and birds those without split digits, such as camels, ostriches, geese and ducks. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on the word "hadu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 198}

He gives (14092) and (14093) from Ibn Abbas that "every one with a claw" is the camel, the ostrich and the like among animals; (14094) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that it is whatever does not have spread digits; and (14095) from him that it is everything with separate digits, the rooster among them. The editor's note says this is the manuscript's wording; the earlier publisher of the commentary first thought the right reading was "not separate digits" to agree with what comes before and after, but he found Ibn Kathir reporting it from Sa'id in the words "everything with separate digits, the rooster among them", so he preferred the reading in the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 198}

He gives (14096) and (14097) from Mujahid that "every one with a claw" is the ostrich and the camel; (14098) from Qatada that it used to be said to be the camel, the ostrich and their like among birds and fish, and in another report from him that it is camels and ostriches, the claw of the camel's hand and foot, the ostrich likewise, and that of birds the duck and its like were forbidden them, and everything without split digits; (14099) from al-Suddi that it is camels and ostriches; and (14100) from Mujahid: the ostrich and the camel, "shaqqan shaqqan", which he explained as meaning that whatever has legs that are not spread, the Jews did not eat, while chickens and sparrows they ate because their legs are spread. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 199}

He gives (14101) from Mujahid in the same sense, and says Ibn Jurayj asked al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza the meaning of "shaqqan shaqqan"; he said: whatever of the animals' legs is not spread, while what is spread the Jews ate. The legs of chickens and sparrows are spread, so they ate them; the camel's foot is not spread, nor the ostrich's, nor the goose's, so the Jews eat no camels, ostriches, geese or anything whose leg is not spread, nor the wild donkey. Ibn Zayd (14102) used to say it means camels alone. The editor's note says the printed text reads "faqat" and the manuscript "qatt", which is right, and that "al-wazzina" is the goose, plural "al-wazzin". Al-Tabari then prefers the view of Ibn Abbas and those who spoke as he did, because God reported that He forbade the Jews every one with a claw, so nothing may be taken out of the generality of that report except what the scholars agreed is out; the ostrich and every beast or bird with a claw and unsplit digits falls in the plain wording of the revelation, so it must be judged included, since no report came from God or His messenger that some of it is outside, and most of the community agree it is included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 200}

He takes up "and of cattle and sheep We forbade them their fats, except what their backs carry", and says the commentators differed over which fats God forbade the Jews. Some said the fat of the thurub in particular; the note says "thurub" is the plural of "tharb", the thin fat covering the stomach and intestines. He gives (14103) from Qatada that it is the thurub, and that it was recalled that the Prophet used to say: God fight the Jews, God forbade them the thurub and then they ate their price. The note says Qatada reported it as a mursal report, while al-Bukhari reported it with a chain to the Prophet and the group of compilers transmitted it. Others said it was any fat not mixed with bone or on bone, and he gives (14104) from Ibn Jurayj that only the tharb was forbidden, and any such fat not in a bone. Others said the fat of the tharb and the kidneys, and he gives (14105) from al-Suddi: the tharb and the fat of the two kidneys, and the Jews used to say that Israel forbade it so they forbid it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 201}

He gives (14106) from Ibn Zayd that He forbade them the thurub and the kidneys, and al-Tabari says this is how it stands in his copy from Yunus, though he thinks it was "the kidneys" in its other form. He then gives his choice: God reported that He forbade the Jews the fats of cattle and sheep except what He excepted, what their backs carry, or the entrails, or what is mixed with bone; so every fat other than what God excepted in His book was forbidden them. Reports from the Prophet agree in this sense, as in his saying: God fight the Jews, the fats were forbidden them so they melted them, then sold them and ate the price. The note says the compilers all transmitted it, and that "to melt the fat" means to render it and draw out its oil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 202}

As for "except what their backs carry", it means the fats of the flank and what clings to the back, which were not forbidden them. He gives (14107) from Ibn Abbas that it is the fat that clings to the back; (14108) from al-Suddi that it is the fat tails; and (14108m) from Abu Salih that the fat tail is among what their backs carry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 202}

He then takes up "or the entrails" ("al-hawaya"), a plural whose singular is "hawiya'", "hawiya" or "hawiyya", meaning what is coiled in the belly, gathered and rounded; they are the "daughters of milk", the "maba'ir" (the intestinal tract), also called the "marabid", and the intestines are in them. The note explains the forms "rabd", "marbid" and "rabid", and says "daughters of milk" are the smaller intestines. The sense for him is: of cattle and sheep We forbade them their fats, except what their backs carry or what the entrails carry; "al-hawaya" is nominative, joined to "backs", and the "ma" after "illa" is accusative as an exception from "the fats", and the editor refers to al-Farra'. He gives (14109) from Ibn Abbas that "al-hawaya" is the mab'ar, and (14110) from Mujahid that it is the mab'ar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 203}

He gives (14111) from Mujahid that "al-hawaya" is the mab'ar and the marbid, and (14112) from him the mab'ar; (14113) and (14114) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr the maba'ir; (14115) and (14116) from Qatada the mab'ar; (14117) from al-Dahhak the mab'ar, and (14118) from him that it means the bellies other than the thurub; (14119) from Ibn Abbas that it is the mab'ar; and (14120) from al-Suddi the maba'ir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 204}

He gives (14121) from Ibn Zayd that "al-hawaya" are the marabid in which the intestines lie, in their middle, which are the "daughters of milk" and are called "marabid" in the speech of the Arabs. He then takes up "or what is mixed with bone": God forbade the Jews the fats of cattle and sheep except what their backs or their entrails carry, which He made lawful to them, and what is mixed with bone is also lawful to them. "Or what is mixed with bone" is joined to "except what their backs carry", so its "ma" is accusative, joined to the first "ma", and it means the fat of the tail, the flank and the like. He gives (14122) from Ibn Jurayj that the fat of the tail with the coccyx is lawful, and everything on the legs, flank, head and eye that is mixed with bone is lawful; the note says the coccyx is the bone at the root of the tail; and (14123) from al-Suddi that it is any fat on a bone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 205}

He takes up "that We recompensed them for their transgression, and We are truthful": what God forbade the Jews of clawed animals and birds with unsplit digits and of the fats of cattle and sheep, He forbade as a punishment from Him for their evil deeds and their transgression against their Lord, and the editor refers to the earlier commentary on recompense and transgression. He gives (14124) from Qatada that it was forbidden them as punishment for their transgression, and (14125) from Ibn Zayd: We did that to them for their transgression. "We are truthful" means truthful in this report about these Jews concerning the fats and the meats of livestock and birds forbidden them, and in the rest of what He reports; they are the liars in claiming that Israel forbade it for himself and that they forbade it because of his prohibition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 206}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
