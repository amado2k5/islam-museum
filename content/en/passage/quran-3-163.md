## Summary
Verse 163 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 163 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 163: the difference in stations (8172-8176, pp. 367-368).** Abu Ja'far: the sense is that those who followed God's pleasure and those who returned with His anger hold different ranks with God. For the one who followed His pleasure there is honour and abundant reward; for the one who incurred His anger, humiliation and painful punishment. Ibn Ishaq (8172), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: each has ranks according to what he did, in Paradise and in the Fire, and God does not lose sight of the people of obedience as against the people of disobedience; the editor notes that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 124) as a continuation of reports ending with 8171. Ibn 'Abbas (8173), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather: their ranks are by their deeds. Others said the meaning is that those who followed God's pleasure have honourable stations with Him: Mujahid (8174), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr, from Abu 'Asim, from 'Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, likened it to another verse with the same sense, and al-Suddi (8175), through Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat, explained it as: they have ranks with God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 367}

**The comparison with tiers, and the verse of poetry (p. 368).** It was also said that the expression is like saying "they are tiers". Al-Tabari cites a line by Ibn Harma in which the poet laments his people and the speed with which destruction reached them, picturing fate passing over them like a flood in the bends of a valley. In his note the editor says the line is in Sibawayh's Kitab (1: 206-207), Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 107), the Khizana and the Lisan (root d-r-j), in a wording differing from that of al-Tabari and Abu 'Ubayda; that Sibawayh used it for the adverbial accusative of the flood's course and for the predicate of "they"; that he restored to the manuscript's sound reading what the printed text had corrupted; and he explains "rajm" as throwing and what is thrown, and the flood's course as its channel and path. (The scanned text of this page is badly garbled around the line and the note, so this describes the sense of the note and does not quote its words.) In the first footnote he refers to the earlier explanation of "degree" (the cross-reference numbers are garbled in the scanned text). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 368}

**End of the verse (p. 368).** Al-Tabari then explains that God knows what the people of obedience and of disobedience do, nothing of their deeds is hidden from Him, and He counts them for both parties until every soul is paid what it earned of good and evil. Ibn Ishaq (8176), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: God does not lose sight of the people of obedience as against the people of disobedience; the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 124), a continuation of reports including 8172, of which the editor says this is a part. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 368}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 368}
