## Summary
Verse 75 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 75 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse: the rescue (p. 543, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: what is the matter with you, believers, that you leave fighting in God's way and in defence of the oppressed among you? As for the men, they had accepted Islam in Mecca and their clans overpowered them by force, hurt them and put them to torment and hardship in their bodies to turn them from their religion, and God urged the believers to rescue them from the hands of the unbelievers who had overpowered them, saying: what is the matter with you that you do not stand up for the oppressed of your religion and community, whom the unbelievers abase to tempt them and turn them from their religion? The oppression covered boys and women also, boys being the plural of walad. He says these oppressed people pray to their Lord to save them from the trial of those polytheists who oppress them and to bring them out of the town whose people do wrong. He says the Arabs call any city a qarya, and here, according to the interpreters, it is Mecca. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 543}

**The grammar of the description of the town (p. 543, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the descriptive word is in the genitive because it describes the "people" and its pronoun returns to the town; and that is what the Arabs do when an adjective precedes a noun that carries a pronoun referring to an earlier noun: they make it follow the case of the earlier noun, as in "I passed the generous man whose father..." The editor says the manuscript has a slip of the copyist here and the print is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 543}

**The second request in the prayer (p. 543).** Al-Tabari says the oppressed also ask in their prayer that their Lord appoint for them, from himself, one who takes charge of their affair and suffices them in their present trial from the people of unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 543}

**Rest of the second request (p. 544, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the oppressed ask that God appoint for them, from himself, one who will help them against those of that town who wrong them by barring them from his path, until he gives them victory and raises his religion. The editor refers to the lexicon indexes for the words for protector and helper and says the print has a variant in al-Tabari's phrase where the manuscript is right. (Note 3 is marked in the text, but no text for it is printed at the foot of the page.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 544}

**Reports 9944-9947 (pp. 544-545).** Muhammad b. Amr (9944, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that God ordered the believers to fight in defence of the oppressed believers in Mecca. Al-Muthanna (9945, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that the boys are the children, the town is Mecca, and the believers were ordered to fight for believers who were there. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9946, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the sense is: why do you hold back from fighting, out of obedience to God and on behalf of the weak; the town is Mecca. Al-Muthanna (9947, from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Uthman b. Ata, from a narrator whose name is garbled in the scan) reports from Ibn Abbas that he took the opening conjunction as meaning "and on behalf of", that is, on behalf of the oppressed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 545}

**Reports 9948-9950 (pp. 545-546, notes 1-4).** Al-Qasim (9948, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Kathir, who heard Muhammad b. Muslim b. Shihab) reports that he said the sense is: in God's way and the way of the oppressed. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9949, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) reports from al-Hasan and Qatada that a man left the unjust town for the righteous town and death overtook him on the road; he leaned his chest toward the righteous town, the angels of mercy and the angels of punishment disputed over him, and they were told to measure which town was nearer; they found him nearer to the righteous one by a span. Some said God brought the righteous town closer to him, and the angels of mercy took him. The editor explains that "he moved his chest away" means he strained, dying, to turn his chest toward the righteous town, turning aside from the unjust one; he says the phrase "nothing rescued him but that" was unpointed in the manuscript and the print's publisher dropped it because he could not read it well; and that the verb for the angels' dispute, in this sense, is not found in the dictionaries but he judges it sound, one of the correct additions to what they record. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9950, from his father, his uncle, his father, from his father) reports from Ibn Abbas that they were Muslims in Mecca who could not leave to emigrate, and God excused them; the town is Mecca. The editor says the print differs from the manuscript in a phrase and the manuscript is right. A second report follows under the same number (9950 m, continuing onto p. 546): Yunus (from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the sense is: why do you not fight for these weak, poor people who ask God to bring them out of that town, having no strength, until God saves them and their religion? The town is Mecca. The editor says the print differs from the manuscript in a word and the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 546}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 546}
