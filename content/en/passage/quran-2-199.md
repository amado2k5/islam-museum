## Summary
Verse 199 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/hums|the Hums]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 199 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "then pour forth from where the people pour forth": al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on this, on who is addressed and who "the people" are. Some said those addressed are Quraysh and their offspring, called the Hums in the Jahiliyya, who were commanded in Islam to pour forth from 'Arafat, from which the rest of the people other than the Hums poured, because Quraysh used to say we do not leave the Haram, and so they did not attend the standing of the people at 'Arafa, and God commanded them to stand with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 184} From 'A'isha: Quraysh and those on their religion, the Hums, used to stand at al-Muzdalifa saying we are the dwellers of God, while everyone else stood at 'Arafa, and God sent down the verse; the editor explains "qatin" as the settled residents of a house. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 184} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 185} From 'Urwa, who wrote to 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan: the Hums were the religion of Quraysh, who were then polytheists, and those whom Quraysh bore among Khuza'a and Banu Kinana, who did not depart from 'Arafa but from al-Muzdalifa, the Sacred Landmark; Banu 'Amir were Hums because Quraysh had borne them, and it was to them that "then pour forth" was said, all the Arabs having poured forth from 'Arafa except the Hums. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 185} The editor makes clear that by "I heard it narrated from him" 'Urwa means his maternal aunt 'A'isha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 185} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 186} From Ibn 'Abbas: the Arabs stood at 'Arafa while Quraysh stood short of it at al-Muzdalifa, and the verse came down, and the Prophet moved the standing place to the Arabs' standing place at 'Arafa; the editor says Husayn ibn 'Ubayd Allah is weak and the report is found only in al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 186} From 'Ata': from where the mass of the people pour forth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 186}

In the report from Mujahid (3835): on the day of 'Arafa God descends to the lowest heaven among the angels and says come to My servants who believed My promise and affirmed My messengers; it is asked, what is their reward? and the answer is that You forgive them; that is the meaning of "then pour forth from where the people pour forth and ask God's forgiveness; God is forgiving, merciful". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 186} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 187} From Mujahid: it is 'Arafa, and Quraysh used to say we are the Hums, people of the Haram, we do not leave the Haram behind and we pour forth from al-Muzdalifa, so they were commanded to reach 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 187} From Qatada: Quraysh and every ally and sister's son of theirs did not pour forth from 'Arafat but from al-Mughammas, saying we are the people of God and do not leave His sanctuary; God commanded them to pour forth from where the people poured forth and told them that the practice of Ibrahim and Isma'il was pouring forth from 'Arafat; al-Suddi and al-Rabi' the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 187} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 188}

From 'Abd Allah ibn Abi Najih, in a long report: Quraysh, I do not know whether before the Elephant or after, devised the matter of the Hums as an opinion among themselves, saying we are the sons of Ibrahim, the people of the sanctuary, the custodians of the House and dwellers of Mecca, and no Arab has a right or standing like ours, so do not hold anything outside the sanctuary sacred as you hold the Haram. They gave up standing at 'Arafa and pouring forth from it, though they knew and admitted that it is among the sites of the hajj and the religion of Ibrahim, and then gave those Arabs they bore who dwelt outside the Haram the same standing; Kinana and Khuza'a joined them. Then they added rules, saying that the Hums should not make dried curds or clarify butter while in ihram, nor enter a hair tent, nor take shade except in leather tents; then they went further, saying that people from outside should not eat food they brought with them from outside the Haram when they came for hajj or 'umra, and should not circle the House at first except in the clothes of the Hums, and if they found none, they circled naked. They imposed this on the Arabs, who accepted it, until God sent Muhammad and sent down the verse, meaning Quraysh by "you" and the Arabs by "the people", raising them in the practice of hajj to 'Arafat, to stand there and pour forth from it; thus God did away with the Hums and what Quraysh had devised, through Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 188} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 189} The editor notes that this report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira with additions and differences which he records in the notes, and explains aqit, sal' and hurum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 188} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 189} From 'A'isha: Quraysh used to stand at Quzah and the people at 'Arafa, and God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 189}

Others said that those addressed by "then pour forth" are all Muslims, and "from where the people poured forth" means from Jam', "the people" being Ibrahim, the Friend of the Merciful; from al-Dahhak: it is Ibrahim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 189} The editor notes that Ibn Kathir remarked that Ibn Jarir recorded this from al-Dahhak alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 190}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: what he considers right is that the verse is about Quraysh and those who adopted the Hums with them among the rest of the Arabs, because the authorities among the interpreters agree that this is its meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 190} The verse then means, in his reading: whoever makes the hajj binding in them, let there be no rafath, fusuq or disputing in the hajj, then pour forth from where the people pour forth and ask God's forgiveness, and it is an instance of what is put first in sense but later in order. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 190} He adds that were it not for that agreement he would say the better of the two readings is al-Dahhak's, because pouring forth from 'Arafat comes before pouring forth from Jam' and before the remembrance at the Sacred Landmark became obligatory, so there is no sense in commanding a pouring forth from a place whose time of pouring forth had passed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 190} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 191} To the objection that "the people" are many while Ibrahim is one, he replies that the Arabs often use the plural to indicate one, as in the verse in Al 'Imran (3:173), where the speaker was one man, Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud al-Ashja'i according to the reports of the biographers, and in the verse in al-Mu'minun (23:51), said to refer to the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 191} The editor notes that al-Tabari did not take this course in his commentary on the Al 'Imran verse, and that he will return to complete this part of the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 191}

On "and ask God's forgiveness; God is forgiving, merciful": al-Tabari says that when you pour forth from 'Arafat returning to Mina, remember God at the Sacred Landmark and call on Him and worship Him there, as He remembered you with His guidance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 192} There are two readings of "then": one is al-Dahhak's, that the meaning is: then pour forth and return to Mina from where Ibrahim poured forth from the Sacred Landmark, and ask Me forgiveness for your sins, for I am forgiving of them and merciful to you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 192} For this al-Tabari gives the report of al-'Abbas ibn Mirdas al-Sulami from the Prophet: I called on God on the day of 'Arafa to forgive my community its sins and He answered that He had forgiven, except for the sins between them and His creatures; I repeated the supplication and received no answer; at the morning at al-Muzdalifa I said, Lord, You are able to compensate this wronged one and forgive this wrongdoer, and He answered that He had forgiven. He laughed, and they said we saw you laugh on a day you did not laugh; he said, I laughed at Iblis when he heard what happened, calling out for woe and destruction and putting dust on his head. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 192} The editor notes that its chain includes 'Abd Allah ibn Kinana, of unknown standing, whose hadith al-Bukhari said was not sound; that al-Bayhaqi said the hadith has many supporting reports, and if sound through them it is a proof, and if not, God has said He forgives lesser sins to whom He wills; and that Ibn Majah, al-Bayhaqi and 'Abd Allah ibn Ahmad reported it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 193}

From Ibn 'Umar: the Messenger of God addressed us on the evening of 'Arafa, saying, people, God has been gracious to you in this station of yours, accepting from your doers of good and giving your doers of good what they asked, and granting your wrongdoers to your doers of good, except the claims between you; pour forth in God's name. At the morning at Jam' he said, God has been gracious to you in this station, accepting from your doers of good and granting your wrongdoers to your doers of good, and the claims between you He has compensated from Himself; pour forth in God's name. His companions said, you took us away yesterday sad and grieved and today glad and happy; he said, yesterday I asked my Lord for something He did not grant me, the claims, and He refused; today Jibril came and said your Lord greets you and says, I have undertaken to compensate for the claims from Myself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 193} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 194} The editor notes that Abu Nu'aym reported it in al-Hilya and called it gharib, 'Abd al-'Aziz ibn Abi Rawwad being alone in reporting it from Nafi', and that al-Mundhiri cited a similar report from 'Ubada ibn al-Samit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 194} Al-Tabari says these two reports show that God's forgiveness of the claims between His creatures takes place on the morning at Jam', at the time of which God says "then pour forth ... and ask God's forgiveness", for then He is forgiving of them out of favour and merciful to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 194} The other reading is: then pour forth from 'Arafa to the Sacred Landmark, and when you have poured forth to it, remember God there as He guided you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 195}
