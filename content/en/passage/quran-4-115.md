## Summary
Verse 115 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 115 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 115 and reports 10427-10428 (pp. 204-205, note 1 on p. 204 and notes 1-5 on p. 205).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: whoever breaks away from the Messenger Muhammad as his enemy, parting from him in hostility, after the guidance has become clear to him, meaning after it has become clear to him that he is God's Messenger and that what he brought from God guides to the truth and a straight way, and follows a way other than that of those who affirm the truth and walks a course other than theirs, which is disbelief in God, since disbelief in God and His Messenger is other than the believers' way and course, God will let him turn to what he turned to: He will make his helper what he sought help from, of idols and images that neither avail him nor ward off any of God's punishment. Report 10427 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says that what he turned to means false gods, and report 10428 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. The clause about roasting means that God will make him a roaster in the fire of Jahannam, that is burn him in it, a sense he says he already explained; and the clause on the evil destination means that Jahannam is evil as a place to which one goes. He adds that the verse came down about the traitors mentioned in the verse on not pleading for them, when the one of them who refused repentance, Tu'ma ibn Ubayriq, joined the idol-worshipping polytheists in Mecca as an apostate who had parted from the Messenger and his religion. The editor's notes: references to earlier explanations of the word for breaking away (volume 8, p. 319 and 336, and volume 3, pp. 115-116), of the word for helper (p. 17, note 3), of roasting (volume 8, pp. 27-29, 231 and 484), of "evil" (p. 101, note 2) and of the destination (p. 101, note 3); and that the printed text had the word for roasting with a doubled letter where the manuscript is right, the word meaning roast meat, called so because it is roasted by fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 205} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 205}
