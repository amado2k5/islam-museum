## Summary
Verse 122 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 122 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The two parties (p. 165).** Abu Ja'far says the sense is that God is hearing and knowing when two parties of the believers resolved to lose heart, and that the two parties are said to be Banu Salima and Banu Haritha, as was related to him. The editor refers back to his note on the vowelling of Salima. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 165}

**Reports on the two parties (pp. 166-168).**
- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7720): Banu Haritha were toward Uhud and Banu Salima toward Sal', and this was the day of the Trench. Abu Ja'far remarks that he has already shown it was Uhud, which suffices without repetition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 166}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7721): it was the day of Uhud, and the two parties were two clans of the Ansar who resolved on something and God preserved them; Qatada adds that it was told to them that when the verse came down they said they would not wish not to have resolved as they did, since God had made plain He was their ally. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 166}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7722): like Qatada's words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 166}
- From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7723): the Prophet went out to Uhud with a thousand men, having promised them victory if they were steadfast. Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul returned with three hundred, and Abu Jabir al-Sulami followed them calling them back; they said they knew of no fighting and that if he obeyed them he would return with them. Banu Salima and Banu Haritha resolved to return when he did, but God preserved them, and the Prophet remained with seven hundred. The editor says it is in al-Tabari's History (3: 12), the completion of report 7717, and that the bracketed addition is from the History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 166} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Ikrima (7724): it came down about Banu Salima of the Khazraj and Banu Haritha of the Aws, whose head was Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul; this is Ikrima's attribution as transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, Ibn Abbas (7725): they are Banu Haritha and Banu Salima. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7726): Banu Salima of Jusham ibn al-Khazraj and Banu Haritha of the Nabit of the Aws, the two wings; the editor places it in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 112), continuing 7719. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7727): two parties of the Ansar who resolved to lose heart, and God preserved them and routed their enemy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Uyayna, Amr ibn Dinar, Jabir ibn Abd Allah (7728): they are Banu Salima and Banu Haritha, and we would not like not to have resolved so, because God made Himself their ally. In his note the editor says al-Bukhari reports it in the Sahih (Fath 8: 169 and 7: 275) through Ali ibn Abd Allah from Sufyan ibn Uyayna in different wording, and that the printed text had an error, the right reading being the manuscript's, which the publisher had not read well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From Ahmad ibn Hazim, Abu Nu'aym, Ibn Uyayna, Amr, Jabir (7729): the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 167}
- From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Ibn Zayd (7730): this was the day of Uhud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 168}

**The meaning of losing heart (p. 168).** He says their resolve meant to grow weak and be cowardly about meeting their enemy, as in the usage that a man lost heart in meeting his enemy. From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Abbas (7731): this word means cowardice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 168}

**Al-Tabari's statement (p. 168).** Abu Ja'far says their resolve was to withdraw from the Prophet and the believers when Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul withdrew with his men, out of cowardice and not doubt about Islam or hypocrisy; God preserved them, they went on with the Prophet and left Ibn Ubayy and the hypocrites, and God praised both parties for their firmness in the truth and told them that He was their ally and helper against their enemies among the unbelievers. The editor refers to the explanation of the word for alliance in Volume 6 (p. 497, note 1); his note continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 168}

- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7732): God is the one who defends them from what they resolved in their weakness, which came from frailty and feebleness that touched them without any doubt in their religion; so He took charge of repelling it with His mercy until they were safe and joined their Prophet. Then comes the comment on the closing clause, beginning that whoever among the believers has weakness, which continues on p. 169. The editor notes that the printed text has a different word, and that he preferred the manuscript and Ibn Hisham, because that fits the context and he does not think the slip came from al-Tabari's original. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 168}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 168 in the commentary on verse 122 and continue from p. 169. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 168}
