## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "Praise be to Allah, Creator of the heavens and the earth, who made the angels messengers, first wings two, three, and four. He increases in creation as He wills. Indeed, Allah is capable of all things." This means complete gratitude to the deity to whom worship is not worthy except Him, and no one else should have the Creator of the seven heavens and the earth, "who made the angels messengers" to whomever He wills among His servants. In whatever He wills of His commands and prohibitions, "He has wings two, three, and four," He says: Companions of wings: meaning angels; some have two, some have three, and some have four. As Qatadah said: "Those with wings are two, three, and four." Some said: He has two, three, and four wings. The Arabs differed regarding the reason for omitting the measure of two, three, and four, which is a translation of 'janha' and 'jannah' as indefinite. Some grammarians from Basra said: In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah.
