## Summary
Verse 120 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 120 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of distress and joy (p. 155).** Al-Tabari says that good reaching the believers means their gaining joy through prevailing over their enemy, people entering their religion in succession, believing their Prophet and helping them against their enemies, and that this distresses these people; harm reaching them means distress through a detachment of theirs failing, an enemy striking them, or a dispute arising within their community, and that these people rejoice at it. The editor refers to an earlier explanation in the book of the verb used. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 155}

- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7705): when they see among the people of Islam harmony, unity and prevailing over their enemy it enrages and distresses them; when they see division and disagreement, or a flank of the Muslims struck, it pleases them and they delight in it. Whenever a generation of them appears, God belies its talk, voids its argument and exposes its weakness, and this is his decree on those of them who passed and those who remain until the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 155} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 156}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7706): they are the hypocrites; seeing the Muslims united and prevailing enrages them greatly, and division and a flank of the Muslims being struck pleases them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 156}
- From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7707; the number is not legible in the scanned text and is inferred from the sequence): when they see unity and harmony among the believers it distresses them, and when they see division and disagreement they rejoice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 156}

**Patience, godwariness and their scheming (p. 156).** Al-Tabari says that if the believers hold fast to obedience to God and following his command, avoid what he forbade, including taking intimates from these Jews instead of the believers, and fear their Lord so as not to go ahead of him in what he made binding of his right and his Messenger's right, their scheming harms them in nothing. By scheming he means the ruses they seek against the Muslims and their plotting to turn them from guidance and the path of truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 156}

**The readings of the verb of harm and their explanation (pp. 157-158).** Al-Tabari records that the reciters differed. A group of the people of the Hijaz and some Basrans read it in the light form with a kasra on the first radical letter, from a verb meaning to harm, and a saying is recorded from the Arabs in this form; but al-Tabari says he knows of no one who read it in the form that this wording would require, and the editor refers to al-Farra' in Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 232). A group of the people of Medina and most Kufan reciters read it with a damma and a doubled final letter, from another verb of the same meaning. He gives two explanations of the damma. First, that the final letter follows the vowel of the letter before it, because the base is jussive but the doubling prevents the jussive, so it takes the nearest vowel of the preceding letters, as people say a command form of the verb for drawing out. Second, that it is truly indicative, with the particle of negation meaning "is not" and the particle that normally introduces the apodosis dropped because the hearer knows where it belongs, so the sense is that their scheming does not harm them at all. He cites a line by Sawwar ibn al-Mudarrib al-Sa'di al-Tamimi in support, and the editor lists its sources (Nawadir of Abu Zayd 45, al-Kamil 1: 300, Hamasat of Ibn al-Shajari 54-55, Ma'ani of al-Farra' 1: 232). Al-Tabari adds that giving the final letter a fatha or kasra would also be permissible, as in the two forms of that command, and the editor says the earlier statement he refers to is al-Farra's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 157} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 158}

**The editor's note on Sawwar's lines (p. 158).** The editor relates that Sawwar spoke the lines when he struck al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf al-Thaqafi in the face, after al-Hajjaj wrote to the Banu Tamim to send a force against the Kharijites, and Sawwar fled; that Darabjird is a town in Fars where al-Muhallab was then fighting the Kharijites led by Qatari ibn al-Fuja'a; that the sense is that if al-Hajjaj will only be satisfied by sending him back to fight Qatari, he will not reach his satisfaction; that the "road of the permit-holders" refers to those posted at the gates of towns and frontiers who stop travellers lacking a pass from their commander; and that al-Tabari's point of evidence is the use of the negation particle in the sense of "is not". The lines are not reproduced. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 158}

**Encompassing (p. 158).** Al-Tabari says that God encompasses all that these unbelievers do among his servants and in his lands, of corruption, barring from his path, hostility toward the people of his religion and other disobedience; he keeps it and nothing escapes him until he gives them their due recompense and lets them taste his punishment for it. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of encompassing in volumes 5 and 2. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 158}
