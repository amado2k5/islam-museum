## Place in the museum

**The angels' question and the plea of weakness (pp. 100-101, notes 1-3 on p. 100 and 1-4 on p. 101).** This commentary covers [[passage/quran-4-97|4:97]], [[passage/quran-4-98|4:98]] and [[passage/quran-4-99|4:99]] together. Abu Ja'far says that those whose souls the angels take as who injured themselves are those who earned God's anger and wrath for themselves; the meaning of wrongdoing was explained earlier. The angels ask them about their condition in their religion, and they answer that they were powerless where they lived, that the polytheists weakened them by their numbers and strength and kept them from faith and from following the messenger; this, in his judgement, is a feeble excuse and a flimsy argument. The angels' reply asks about the breadth of God's earth, meaning they could have left their land and homes, parted from those who stopped them from faith, and gone to a land whose people would protect them from the authority of the polytheists, where they could affirm God's oneness, worship Him and follow His prophet. God then says that Hell is their lodging, that is their destination in the hereafter and their dwelling, and that Hell is a bad end for its people. Then He excepted the helpless whom the polytheists weakened, those unable to migrate from the land of polytheism to the land of Islam through hardship, lack of means and ignorance of the road, from those for whom He said Hell is the lodging: for them it is not their lodging, because of their excuse. In note 4 the editor remarks that this sentence stacks many prepositional links to what precedes, and he feared the reader would tire. The accusative of the helpless is for Abu Ja'far (his grammatical choice) an exception from the pronoun in the clause on their lodging; in note 5 the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and says Abu Ja'far departed here from his usual method, postponing the discussion of the first verse to the end of the commentary on the verse (the page reference is blank in print). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 101}

**Hope of pardon and the occasion (pp. 101-102, note 6 on p. 101 and notes 1-3 on p. 102).** Abu Ja'far says that God's word about these helpless ones, the hope He expresses of pardoning them, means that because of their excuse, being believers, He will favour them with pardon for leaving migration, since they did not leave it by choice or by preferring the land of unbelief to the land of Islam but through inability to move from it. The closing words describe God as one who forbears by His favour toward the sins of His servants by leaving punishment, and who covers their sins by His pardon. The editor says the printed text has an extended form of the verb of favour and he kept the manuscript's, and that the manuscript has a different particle in the phrase on inability, the printed one being better. Abu Ja'far then says it was reported that these two verses and the one after them were sent down about people of Mecca who had become Muslim and held back from migrating with the Prophet when he migrated; some were put to the trial and gave way, and took part with the polytheists in the fighting against the Muslims, and God refused to accept the excuse they offered (note 3 explains the trial as the severe torture with which believers were tested). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 102}

**Reports 10259-10260 (p. 102).** Report 10259 (Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, from Ibn Fudayl, from Ash'ath, from Ikrima): there were people in Mecca who had become Muslim, and whoever of them died there was lost, and he mentions what was sent down about them; Ibn Abbas said: I am one of them, and my mother; Ikrima said: al-Abbas was one of them. Report 10260 (Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Ramadi, from Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, from Muhammad ibn Sharik, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas): a group in Mecca had become Muslim and concealed their Islam, and then they were brought out... The report is cut at the foot of the page and continues on p. 103. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 102}
