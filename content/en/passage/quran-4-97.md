## Summary
Verse 97 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 97 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The angels' question and the plea of weakness (pp. 100-101, notes 1-3 on p. 100 and 1-4 on p. 101).** This commentary covers [[passage/quran-4-97|4:97]], [[passage/quran-4-98|4:98]] and [[passage/quran-4-99|4:99]] together. Abu Ja'far says that those whose souls the angels take as who injured themselves are those who earned God's anger and wrath for themselves; the meaning of wrongdoing was explained earlier. The angels ask them about their condition in their religion, and they answer that they were powerless where they lived, that the polytheists weakened them by their numbers and strength and kept them from faith and from following the messenger; this, in his judgement, is a feeble excuse and a flimsy argument. The angels' reply asks about the breadth of God's earth, meaning they could have left their land and homes, parted from those who stopped them from faith, and gone to a land whose people would protect them from the authority of the polytheists, where they could affirm God's oneness, worship Him and follow His prophet. God then says that Hell is their lodging, that is their destination in the hereafter and their dwelling, and that Hell is a bad end for its people. Then He excepted the helpless whom the polytheists weakened, those unable to migrate from the land of polytheism to the land of Islam through hardship, lack of means and ignorance of the road, from those for whom He said Hell is the lodging: for them it is not their lodging, because of their excuse. In note 4 the editor remarks that this sentence stacks many prepositional links to what precedes, and he feared the reader would tire. The accusative of the helpless is for Abu Ja'far (his grammatical choice) an exception from the pronoun in the clause on their lodging; in note 5 the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and says Abu Ja'far departed here from his usual method, postponing the discussion of the first verse to the end of the commentary on the verse (the page reference is blank in print). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 101}

**Hope of pardon and the occasion (pp. 101-102, note 6 on p. 101 and notes 1-3 on p. 102).** Abu Ja'far says that God's word about these helpless ones, the hope He expresses of pardoning them, means that because of their excuse, being believers, He will favour them with pardon for leaving migration, since they did not leave it by choice or by preferring the land of unbelief to the land of Islam but through inability to move from it. The closing words describe God as one who forbears by His favour toward the sins of His servants by leaving punishment, and who covers their sins by His pardon. The editor says the printed text has an extended form of the verb of favour and he kept the manuscript's, and that the manuscript has a different particle in the phrase on inability, the printed one being better. Abu Ja'far then says it was reported that these two verses and the one after them were sent down about people of Mecca who had become Muslim and held back from migrating with the Prophet when he migrated; some were put to the trial and gave way, and took part with the polytheists in the fighting against the Muslims, and God refused to accept the excuse they offered (note 3 explains the trial as the severe torture with which believers were tested). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 102}

**Reports 10259-10260 (p. 102).** Report 10259 (Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, from Ibn Fudayl, from Ash'ath, from Ikrima): there were people in Mecca who had become Muslim, and whoever of them died there was lost, and he mentions what was sent down about them; Ibn Abbas said: I am one of them, and my mother; Ikrima said: al-Abbas was one of them. Report 10260 (Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Ramadi, from Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, from Muhammad ibn Sharik, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas): a group in Mecca had become Muslim and concealed their Islam, and then they were brought out... The report is cut at the foot of the page and continues on p. 103. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 102}

**The rest of report 10260 and reports 10261-10262 (pp. 103-104, notes 1-2 on p. 103 and note 1 on p. 104).** The rest of report 10260 (from Ibn Abbas): the polytheists took them out with them on the day of Badr and some were struck down; the Muslims said: these companions of ours were Muslims and were compelled! They asked forgiveness for them, and the verse was sent down, and a letter went to the Muslims remaining in Mecca that there was no excuse for them. They left, the polytheists caught up with them and they gave in to the trial; a verse of sura al-Ankabut was sent down about them ([[passage/quran-29-10|29:10]]) and the Muslims wrote it to them, so they grieved and despaired of all good; then a verse of sura an-Nahl ([[passage/quran-16-110|16:110]]) was sent down about them, and the Muslims wrote that God had made an exit for them. They left, the polytheists overtook them, and they fought until those who were to escape escaped and those who were to be killed were killed. The editor says the printed text added a word in the phrase on there being no excuse, and he kept the manuscript's, which agrees with Ibn Kathir; Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Ramadi, a teacher of al-Tabari, is trustworthy, Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri was dealt with earlier, and Muhammad ibn Sharik al-Makki, Abu Umara, was called trustworthy by Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in; Ibn Kathir traced the report to the Tafsir of Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Suyuti adds Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi, and it is in the Sunan al-Kubra in other wording through Ikrima; the phrase "they gave the trial" is found so in all the sources except Ibn Kathir, who has a word for precaution, which the editor calls an error, the meaning being that they disbelieved after having been Muslim (see the note on 10266). Report 10261 (Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Wahb, from Hayat or Ibn Lahi'a, the doubt being Yunus's, from Abu al-Aswad, who heard a freed slave of Ibn Abbas say from Ibn Abbas): Muslims were with the polytheists swelling their numbers against the Prophet; an arrow shot would strike one of them and kill him, or he would be struck and killed, and God sent down the verse about them as far as the question on migration. Report 10262 (Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, from Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Muqri, from Hayat, from Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Nawfal al-Asadi): a force for Yemen was imposed on the people of Medina and I was enrolled in it; I met Ikrima, freed slave of Ibn Abbas, and he forbade me most strongly, saying Ibn Abbas had told him that Muslims were with the polytheists, and then the like of Yunus's report from Ibn Wahb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 104}

**The editor's note on 10261-10262 (p. 104, note 1).** The editor says al-Bukhari gave the report by the second chain from Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-Muqri from Hayat and another, and al-Bayhaqi from Muhammad ibn Maslama al-Wasiti from al-Muqri; the unnamed man in their chains seems to be Ibn Lahi'a, as in the first chain. He quotes Ibn Hajar's Fath that al-Tabarani said only al-Layth and Ibn Lahi'a narrated it from Abu al-Aswad, and that Ibn Hajar answered him with al-Bukhari's route through Hayat and with Ibn Abi Hatim's having given Ibn Lahi'a's. Abu Abd al-Rahman al-Muqri is Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-Adawi, and Abu al-Aswad is Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Nawfal al-Asadi, the orphan of Urwa; both were dealt with earlier. He quotes Ibn Hajar that the word for the force means an army and that the people of Medina were made to send one to fight the Syrians in the caliphate of Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr in Mecca. The editor says the printed text dropped "to Yemen", which is certainly in the manuscript though absent from the other transmissions, and which shows that Ibn Hajar erred in his reasoning; he seems to have inferred it to clear Ikrima of the Kharijite view attributed to him, saying that the story shows Ikrima's innocence since he forbade fighting Muslims so strongly. The editor holds that this point needs more investigation (the note is signed Mahmud Muhammad Shakir). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 104}

**Reports 10263-10264 (pp. 104-106, notes 1-3 on p. 105 and 1-3 on p. 106).** Report 10263 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas): they are people who stayed behind after the Prophet and left off going out with him; whoever of them died before joining the Prophet, the angels struck his face and back. Report 10264 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Juraij, from Ikrima): it was sent down about Qays ibn al-Fakih ibn al-Mughira, al-Harith ibn Zam'a ibn al-Aswad, Qays ibn al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Abu al-As ibn Munabbih ibn al-Hajjaj and Ali ibn Umayya ibn Khalaf. The editor says the names are so in the manuscript, the printed text and al-Durr al-Manthur, and that Ibn Hisham's Sira and Imta' al-Asma give Abu Qays ibn al-Fakih, Abu Qays ibn al-Walid and al-As ibn Munabbih; he thinks it a copyists' slip, not a fault of the report. The report continues: when the polytheists of Quraysh and their followers went out to protect Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and the Quraysh caravan from the Prophet and his companions, and to seek what had been taken from them at Nakhla, youths went out with them unwilling (the editor prefers the manuscript's form to that of the printed text and al-Durr), who had become Muslim and gathered at Badr without appointment, and were killed at Badr as unbelievers, having turned back from Islam; these are the men named. The editor explains Nakhla as the raid of Abd Allah ibn Jahsh al-Asadi to Batn Nakhla between Mecca and Ta'if, where a Quraysh caravan with Amr ibn al-Hadrami passed; they killed Amr, took two prisoners and brought the caravan and the two captives to Medina, and the Prophet told them he had not ordered them to fight in the sacred month (he refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira). Ibn Juraij adds that Mujahid said it was sent down about the weak among the unbelievers of Quraysh killed at Badr, and that Ikrima said the exception means the very old man, the old woman, the little girls and the boys. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 106}

**Reports 10265-10266 (pp. 106-107, notes 1-3 on p. 106 and 1-3 on p. 107).** Report 10265 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): when al-Abbas, Aqil and Nawfal were taken prisoner (al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's uncle, and the sons of his brothers, Aqil ibn Abi Talib and Nawfal ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Muttalib), the Prophet told al-Abbas to ransom himself and his two nephews; he said: Messenger of God, did we not pray toward your direction of prayer and testify your testimony? He said: Abbas, you disputed and were bested! (that is, overcome in dispute); then he recited the verse containing the question on the breadth of the earth. So from the day it was sent down, whoever had become Muslim and not migrated was an unbeliever until he migrated, except the helpless; al-Suddi glosses the two terms of the clause on inability as a means in property and a road. Ibn Abbas said: I was one of them, of the children. The editor notes that both the printed text and the manuscript have "nephew" in the singular, that the dual seems right as he printed it, and that the singular is also correct. Report 10266 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Amr ibn Dinar, who heard Ikrima): there were people in Mecca who had testified to God's oneness; when the polytheists went to Badr they took them along and they were killed, and the verse was sent down; the Muslims in Medina wrote it to those in Mecca; some left and the polytheists overtook them on the way, and some gave in to the trial, so the verse of al-Ankabut ([[passage/quran-29-10|29:10]]) was sent down and they wrote it, and about those who gave in to the trial God sent down the verse of an-Nahl ([[passage/quran-16-110|16:110]]). Ibn Uyayna added: Muhammad ibn Ishaq told me they were five youths of Quraysh: Ali ibn Umayya, Abu Qays ibn al-Fakih, Zam'a ibn al-Aswad, Abu al-As ibn Munabbih, and I forgot the fifth. The editor glosses "gave the trial" as disbelieved after being Muslim (referring to his note on 10260); "Abu Qays ibn al-Fakih" is right here, but "Abu al-As ibn Munabbih" should be "al-As ibn Munabbih", and the fifth in Ibn Ishaq's report is Abu Qays ibn al-Walid as before; Ibn Ishaq's report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 107}

**Reports 10267-10269 (pp. 107-109, note 1 on p. 108).** Report 10267 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): we were told the verse was sent down about people of Mecca who spoke the words of Islam, then went out with Abu Jahl (the narrator gives him a hostile epithet that is not repeated here) and were killed at Badr; they offered a plea that was no excuse, and God refused to accept it from them; the exception concerns people of Mecca whom God excused. Ibn Abbas used to say: I and my mother were among those. Report 10268 (I was told, from al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman, from al-Dahhak): they were hypocrites who stayed back from the Prophet, did not go out with him to Medina, and went out with the polytheists of Quraysh to Badr, where they were struck down among those struck. (The chain begins with an unnamed "I was told".) Report 10269 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, who asked Ibn Zayd): when the Prophet was sent and became prominent, faith welled up and hypocrisy welled up with it (the editor glosses the verb as water gushing from the ground); men came to the Prophet and said: were it not that we fear these people would torture us, we would have become Muslims, but we testify that there is no god but God and that you are His messenger. When Badr came the polytheists said: none is to stay behind, or we raze his house and take his property! Those men went out with them; one party was killed and one taken. The killed are those meant by the whole verse including the question on migration and the abode of the Fire; then God excused the truthful with the exception, those who would have perished had they left, and the hoped-for pardon concerns their staying among the polytheists. The captives said: Prophet, you know we used to come to you and testify, and we went out with these people from fear! God then sent down two verses of sura al-Anfal ([[passage/quran-8-70|8:70]] and [[passage/quran-8-71|8:71]]) addressed to the Prophet about the prisoners of Badr (the wording is not repeated), and Ibn Zayd takes the forgiveness in them to be for what they did in going out with the polytheists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 109}

**Reports 10270-10276 (pp. 109-110, note 1 on p. 109 and notes 1-2 on p. 110).** Report 10270 (Muhammad ibn Khalid ibn Khidash, from his father, from Hammad ibn Zayd, from Ayyub, from Abd Allah ibn Abi Mulayka, from Ibn Abbas): I and my mother were among those God excused. The editor says Muhammad ibn Khalid ibn Khidash ibn Ajlan al-Muhallabi narrated from his father and was said sometimes to bring odd reports from him, that Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy, that his father Khalid is truthful, and that al-Bukhari gave the report through Sulayman ibn Harb and through Abu al-Nu'man from Hammad ibn Zayd, and al-Bayhaqi also. Report 10271 (Abu Kurayb, from Yahya ibn Adam, from Sharik, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas): I am one of the helpless. Reports 10272-10273 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, and al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, similarly): the weak among the unbelievers of Quraysh killed at Badr are meant. Report 10274 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Yazid, who heard Ibn Abbas): I and my mother were among the helpless of women and children. The editor says the manuscript and printed text have Abd Allah, certainly an error for Ubayd Allah ibn Abi Yazid al-Makki, and that al-Bukhari gave the report through Abd Allah ibn Muhammad from Ibn Uyayna, and al-Bayhaqi also. Report 10275 (al-Muthanna, from Hajjaj, from Hammad, from Ali ibn Zayd, from Abd Allah or Ibrahim ibn Abd Allah al-Qurashi, from Abu Hurayra): the Prophet used to pray after the noon prayer for the release of al-Walid, Salama ibn Hisham, Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi'a and the weak Muslims from the hands of the polytheists. The editor says Ali ibn Zayd ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Mulayka al-Taymi narrated from Anas and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, had much hadith but some weakness and is not used as proof, and Ahmad said he is nothing; Abd Allah is Abd Allah ibn Ibrahim ibn Qarit al-Kinani, ally of Banu Zuhra, also called Ibrahim ibn Abd Allah ibn Qarit, who narrates from Abu Hurayra, and "Ubayd Allah" in the manuscript and printed text is an error; the report is weak, but Ibn Kathir said it has a witness in the Sahih by another route, meaning what al-Bukhari gave. Report 10276 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid on the clause on inability): weak believers in Mecca, about whom the companions of Muhammad said... The report is cut at the foot of the page and continues on p. 111. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 110}

**The rest of report 10276, reports 10277-10281 and the grammar of the word for "take" (pp. 111-112, notes 1-3 on p. 111 and note 1 on p. 112).** The rest of report 10276 (Mujahid): they were weak believers in Mecca, about whom the companions of Muhammad said that they stood in the position of those killed at Badr as weak men alongside the unbelievers of Quraysh, and God sent down about them the verse on those with no means. Report 10277 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) gives the same. Al-Tabari then explains the clause on being unable to find a means through reports: 10278 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Amr, from Ikrima): the means is the ability to rise and go to Medina, and the way is the road to it; 10279 and 10280 (Mujahid, by two chains): the way is a road to Medina; 10281 (al-Suddi): the means is money and the way is the road to Medina. In the notes the editor Shakir refers to the earlier explanation of "way" in volume 1, remarks that al-Tabari, contrary to his habit, has placed this part of his discussion out of its place, and explains that by "nasb" al-Tabari means the fatha. Al-Tabari then gives two views on the grammar of the word for "takes": the first, that it is in the position of fatha as a past verb, since the past form is fixed on fatha in every case; the second (p. 112), that it stands in the position of the present with the sense of the future, as "tatawaffahum" with one of the two ta' letters dropped, saying that the Arabs drop one of two ta' letters when they meet at the start of a word and sometimes keep both. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an for all of this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 112}
