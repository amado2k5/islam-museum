## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 25-26 as a single section. He interprets the words, “And Noah said, ‘My Lord, do not leave upon the earth any of the disbelievers.’” He explains that by “inhabitant” he means one who roams the earth, going and coming within it. The word “inhabitant” (dayyār) is derived from the root “dawāran” (to roam), and the letters yā’ and wāw have come together. The yā’ precedes the wāw, which is silent, and the wāw is assimilated into it, resulting in a doubled yā’. This is similar to the saying, “The living are standing” (hayy al-qiyam) from “qumtu” (I stood), when it is actually “qaywām” (standing). The Arabs say, “There is no inhabitant there, no Arab, no sound, no whistling, no blowing of a fire,” meaning by all of these, “There is no one there.” In this section, he quotes sayings attributed to Ibn Zayd and Sufyan.
