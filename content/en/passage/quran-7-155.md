**Commentary on verse 155 (pp. 140-144)**

Al-Tabari says: Moses chose seventy men of his people for the time and term God had set for him to meet Him with them, in repentance for what their foolish ones did with the calf. Report 15152, from al-Suddi, through Musa ibn Harun, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat: God told Moses to come to Him with some of the Children of Israel who would apologise to Him for worshipping the calf, and set them an appointment; Moses chose seventy men under his eye and went with them to apologise; when they came to the place they asked to see God openly, as at [[passage/quran-2-55|2:55]], since he had spoken with Him, and the thunderbolt took them and they died; Moses stood weeping and called on God as related at [[passage/quran-7-155|7:155]], asking what he would say to the Children of Israel when he came to them having destroyed their best. The editor notes that this report appeared at length as no. 958. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 140}

Report 15153, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Moses chose seventy men of the Children of Israel, the best of the best, and said, go to God and repent to Him for what you did, and ask Him for repentance for those you left behind of your people; fast, purify yourselves and your clothes. He went out with them to Mount Sinai for an appointment his Lord had set, and Moses would not come to it except with permission and knowledge. The seventy said to Moses, ask that we may hear our Lord's speech. When Moses neared the mountain the pillar of cloud settled on it until it covered the whole mountain; Moses drew near and went into it and told the people to come close. When God spoke to him a dazzling light fell on his brow that no child of Adam could look at, so a veil was struck between. The people came near until, entering the cloud, they fell prostrate and heard Him speaking to Moses, commanding and forbidding him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 140}

The report continues: when God had finished His command, the cloud lifted from Moses and he came to them, and they told Moses they would not believe him until they saw God openly, so the tremor took them, which is the thunderbolt, and their spirits were snatched and they all died. Moses stood entreating, calling on and turning to his Lord, saying that had He willed He would have destroyed them before, and him with them; they had acted foolishly, would He destroy those behind him of the Children of Israel? The editor notes that the printed text and manuscript have a meaningless word where he put "snatched", meaning died suddenly, and that this report appeared as no. 957. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 141}

Report 15154, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: God had told him to choose seventy of his people, so he chose seventy and went out with them to call on their Lord; among what they asked was, O God, give us what You gave no one after us; God disliked this in their prayer, and the tremor took them. Report 15155, from Maymun: "for Our appointment", for the time they were promised. Report 15156, from Mujahid: he chose them to complete the promise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 141}

Others said the tremor took them only because of what they claimed against Moses, that he killed Aaron. Report 15157, from Ali, through Ibn Bashshar, Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Yaman, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Umara ibn Abd al-Saluli: Moses, Aaron, Shubar and Shubayr went to the foot of a mountain; Aaron slept on a bed and God took him. When Moses returned to the Children of Israel they said, where is Aaron? He said, God took him. They said, you killed him, you envied us his character and gentleness. He said, choose whom you will, and they chose seventy men. When they reached him they said, Aaron, who killed you? He said, no one killed me, God took me. They said, Moses, you will never be disobeyed after today. The tremor took them, and Moses turned right and left calling on God as at [[passage/quran-7-155|7:155]], and God revived them and made them all prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 142}

In a long note the editor says Umara ibn Abd al-Saluli is the brother of Salim and Zayd, whom al-Ijli declared reliable; he related from Ali and Hudhayfa and only Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani related from him; Ahmad said his hadith is sound, while Abu Hatim called him an unknown elder whose hadith is not used as proof. Ibn Kathir quoted the report and said it is a very strange report and that he does not know Umara; al-Suyuti traced it in al-Durr al-Manthur and attributed it to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn Abi al-Dunya, Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu al-Shaykh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 142}

Report 15158, from Ali, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba, Abu Ishaq and a man of Banu Salul: Aaron was of good character and loved among the Children of Israel; when he died, Moses buried him; when Moses came to the Children of Israel they said, where is Aaron? He said, he died. They said, you killed him. So he chose seventy of them, and when they came to the grave Moses said, were you killed or did you die? He said, I died. They were struck senseless, and Moses said, Lord, what shall I tell the Children of Israel, if I return they will say you killed them? They were revived and made prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 143}

Report 15159, from Abu Sa'id al-Raqashi, through Abd Allah ibn al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal, his father and al-Rabi' ibn Habib: they were men a little over twenty and not past forty, because one of twenty has lost his ignorance and youthfulness, and one who has not passed forty has lost none of his mind. The editor gives the transmitters' biographies, says he found none for Abd Allah ibn al-Hajjaj, that al-Rabi' ibn Habib al-Hanafi is reliable, and that there are two or more men called Abu Sa'id al-Raqashi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 143}

Others said the tremor took the people only because they did not separate from the calf-worshippers, not because they were among its worshippers. Report 15160, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: it is told us that Ibn Abbas used to say the tremor reached them only because they did not leave the people when they set up the calf, though they disliked joining them in it. Report 15161, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: they were among those who did not say that, but did not join them in it; the tremor took them because they had not parted from their people when they took the calf; when they went out and prayed, God made them die and then revived them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 144}

Report 15162, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Sa'd: "miqat" is the appointment; when the tremor took them after Moses took out the seventy of his people to call on God and ask Him to lift the affliction, and they were not answered, Moses knew they had committed the same disobedience his people had. Abu Sa'd said: Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi told me they were not answered because they had not forbidden them wrong or enjoined right; the tremor took them, they died, and God revived them. Reports 15163 and 15164, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Abu Usama, Awn and Sa'id ibn Hayyan, and through Ibn Bashshar and Muhammad ibn Ja'far: the tremor took them because they neither approved nor forbade the calf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 144}

The scholars of Arabic differed over why "his people, seventy men" is in the accusative. A grammarian of Basra said it means "Moses chose from his people seventy men", and when "from" is dropped the verb acts directly, as al-Farazdaq said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 144}

We stop here at the foot of p. 144, in the middle of the Basra grammarian's discussion of the accusative of "his people, seventy men" in verse 155; the next batch begins at p. 145. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 144}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
