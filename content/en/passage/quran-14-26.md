## Summary
Verse 26 of [[passage/quran-14|Surat Ibrahim]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 26 of 52 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 26 (pp. 583-587)**

On "and the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree, uprooted from the surface of the earth, with no stability", al-Tabari says, mighty be His mention: the likeness of associating partners with God, which is "the bad word", is "like a bad tree". The interpreters differed over which tree it is. Most of them said: the colocynth. Report 20737, from Anas ibn Malik, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Mu'awiya ibn Qurra: on this phrase "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree", he said, the shiryan; I said, what is the shiryan? A man with him said, the colocynth, and Mu'awiya agreed. Report 20738, from Anas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Shu'ba and Mu'awiya ibn Qurra: he said, the colocynth. Report 20739, from Anas, through al-Hasan, Amr ibn al-Haytham, Shu'ba and Mu'awiya ibn Qurra: he said, the shiryan, meaning the colocynth. Report 20740, from Anas ibn Malik, through Ahmad ibn Mansur, Nu'aym ibn Hammad, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ibn Jurayj, al-A'mash and Hayyan ibn Shu'ba, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 583}

on "like a bad tree", he said, the shiryan; I said to Anas, what is the shiryan? He said, the colocynth; the editor notes that the narrator's name in this chain is unsettled. Report 20741, from Anas, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Shu'ayb: I went out with Abu al-Aliyah wanting Anas ibn Malik, and we came to him, and he said, "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree", that is the colocynth. Report 20742, from Anas, through al-Hasan, Isma'il ibn Ibrahim and Shu'ayb ibn al-Hubhab: the like. Report 20743, from Anas ibn Malik, through al-Muthanna, Adam al-Asqalani, Shu'ba and Abu Iyas: "the bad tree" is the shiryan; I said, what is the shiryan? He said, the colocynth. Report 20744, from Anas, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Hammad and Shu'ayb: that is the colocynth. Report 20745, from Shu'ayb, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj and Mahdi ibn Maymun: Anas said, "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree", the verse; he said, that is the colocynth; have you not seen how the winds toss it to the right and the left? Report 20746, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 584}

"like a bad tree", the colocynth. Others said: this tree was not created on the earth. Report 20747, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad al-Za'farani, Affan, Abu Kudayna and Qabus from his father: "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree, uprooted from the surface of the earth, with no stability", he said, this is a parable God struck, and this tree was not created on the face of the earth. A report has been related from the Messenger of God authenticating the view of whoever said it is the colocynth; if it is sound, no other view may be stated, and if not, it is a tree with the attribute with which God described it. Report 20748, from Anas ibn Malik, through Sawwar ibn Abd Allah, his father, Hammad ibn Salama and Shu'ayb ibn al-Hubhab: the Messenger of God said of "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree, uprooted from the surface of the earth, with no stability", it is the colocynth; Shu'ayb said, I told Abu al-Aliyah and he said, so they used to say; the editor recalls al-Tirmidhi's saying that only Hammad ibn Salama raised this report to the Prophet and that the unraised form is sounder. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 585}

On "uprooted from the surface of the earth" he says, torn out by the root; one says "ijtathathtu al-shay' ajtathuhu ijtithathan" when you uproot it. The interpreters said the same: report 20749, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "uprooted from the surface of the earth", he said, torn out by the root from the surface of the earth. "With no stability", he says, this tree has no stability or root in the earth on which to stand firm. This tree, described by God with this description, was made a parable for the disbelief and associating partners of the disbeliever; he says, the disbeliever's disbelief and his deed, which is disobedience to God, has no firmness in the earth and no ascent in the sky, because nothing of it rises to God. Report 20750, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree, uprooted from the surface of the earth, with no stability", God struck the parable of the bad tree as the likeness of the disbeliever, he says, the bad tree is uprooted from the surface of the earth with no stability; the disbeliever's deed is not accepted and does not rise to God, so he has no firm root in the earth and no branch in the sky; he says, he has no righteous deed in this world or in the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 586}

Report 20751, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree, uprooted from the surface of the earth, with no stability", Qatada said, a man met a man of knowledge and said, what do you say of "the bad word"? He said, I know no resting place for it on earth nor ascent in the sky, except that it clings to the neck of its owner until he meets the Resurrection with it. Report 20752, from Abu al-Aliyah, through Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id and Qatada: the wind tugged a man's cloak and he cursed it, and the Messenger of God said, do not curse it, for it is commanded, and whoever curses something that does not deserve it, the curse returns on him; the editor notes that al-Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud related the report and that al-Tirmidhi called it good and strange. Report 20753, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Abu Ja'far: "the likeness of a bad word is like a bad tree", he said, this is the disbeliever, who has no deed on earth and no mention in the sky; "uprooted from the surface of the earth, with no stability", he said, his deed does not ascend to the sky and does not stand on the earth. It was asked, then where are their deeds? He said, they carry their burdens on their backs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 587}

We stop here at the foot of p. 587, after report 20753 in the commentary on verse 26; the next batch begins at p. 588 (marker 596). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 587}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
