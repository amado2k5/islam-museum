## Summary
Verse 124 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 124 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 173).** Abu Ja'far says the meaning is: God helped you at Badr when you were humbled, when you said to the believers among your Companions that it should suffice them for their Lord to aid them with a force of angels, three thousand sent down; that was the day of Badr. The exegetes then differ on which day they were promised the angels' presence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 173}

**First view: the promise was at Badr and its condition was not met (pp. 173-174).** Some said God promised the believers at Badr to aid them with His angels if the enemy came upon them at once; they did not, so the aid was not sent. In his note the editor says the manuscript has a plainly wrong form here, is full of slips at this place, and that he corrected what was evident without marking many similar cases.
- From Humayd ibn Mas'ada, Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, Dawud, Amir (7743): the Muslims were told that Kurz ibn Jabir al-Muharibi was to reinforce the polytheists, and that distressed them, so the verses were said to them; the defeat then reached Kurz and he turned back and did not reinforce them with the five thousand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 173}
- From Ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-A'la, Dawud, Amir (7744): the same, adding that those who would come upon them at once were Kurz and his men, to whom the defeat reached, so they did not reinforce them and the five thousand did not come down; afterwards the Muslims were reinforced with a thousand, making four thousand angels with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 174}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7745): this is the day of Badr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 174}
- From Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Dawud, al-Sha'bi (7746): the Muslims were told that Kurz ibn Jabir al-Muharibi meant to reinforce the polytheists at Badr and it distressed them, so God sent down the verses; the polytheists' defeat reached him and he did not reinforce his men, and the Muslims were not reinforced with the five thousand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 174}

**Second view: they were steadfast and God reinforced them (pp. 174-176).** Others said the promise was made at Badr, the believers were steadfast and godwary, and God reinforced them with His angels as He had promised:
- From Abu Kurayb, Yunus ibn Bukayr, Ibn Ishaq, Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr, from some of Banu Sa'ida (7747): I heard Abu Usayd Malik ibn Rabi'a, after he lost his sight, say: if I were with you at Badr now with my sight, I would tell you of the pass the angels came out of, without doubt or dispute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 174} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 175}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7748): the same from Abu Usayd, who was at Badr. The editor says it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 286), that it comes later with another chain in different wording and attributed to Uhud and not Badr, and that he will comment on it there (the number of that later report is not clear in the scanned print). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 175}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr, who was told it from Ibn Abbas (7749): a man of Ghifar said: my cousin and I went up a mountain overlooking Badr, both of us polytheists, waiting to see who would be defeated so we could plunder with the plunderers. While we were on the mountain a cloud came near and we heard the neighing of horses, and I heard a voice say "advance, Hayzum". My cousin's heart's covering was torn off and he died on the spot; I nearly perished but held on. The notes explain the word for defeat and how it is used in asking who gets the victory; that the call is one used to urge horses forward; that Hayzum is the name of a horse among the angels' horses that day, said to be Jibril's; that the manuscript has a corrupted reading here; and that the heart's covering is likened to a woman's veil. The report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 285). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 175}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, al-Hasan ibn Umara, al-Hakam ibn Utayba, Miqsam, freedman of Abdullah ibn al-Harith, Ibn Abbas (7750): the angels fought on no day except Badr; on the other days they were numbers and reinforcement and did not strike. In Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 286). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 175}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, his father Ishaq ibn Yasar, men of Banu Mazin ibn al-Najjar, Abu Dawud al-Mazini, who was at Badr (7751): I was following one of the polytheists at Badr to strike him when his head fell before my sword reached him, and I knew that someone else had killed him. In Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 286). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 176}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, Husayn ibn Abd Allah ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Abbas, Ikrima, freedman of Ibn Abbas, Abu Rafi', freedman of the Prophet (7752): I was a slave of al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib, and Islam had entered our household; al-Abbas, Umm al-Fadl and I became Muslim, but al-Abbas feared his people, kept his Islam hidden, and had much money spread among them. Abu Lahab stayed behind from Badr and sent al-As ibn Hisham ibn al-Mughira in his place, as all did, no man staying behind without sending another. When the news came of the Quraysh's losses we felt strength and honour in ourselves. I was a weak man making arrows, carving them in the room of Zamzam; as I sat there with Umm al-Fadl, pleased with the news, Abu Lahab came dragging his feet and sat at the tent-rope of the room, his back to mine. People said Abu Sufyan ibn al-Harith ibn Abd al-Muttalib had arrived. Abu Lahab said: come to me, nephew, you have the news. He sat with him while people stood about, and Abu Lahab asked how it had gone with the people. He replied: nothing, by God, except that we met them and gave them our shoulders, and they killed and captured us as they liked, and by God... The report continues on p. 177. The editor says the printed wording for the phrase on strength and honour is of no use, that the manuscript's is a corrupt form, and that he adopted Ibn Hisham's, and explains the tent-rope as the lowered side of the room, from the rope that fastens a tent to the ground. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 176}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 176 in the commentary on verses 124 and 125 and continue from p. 177. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 176}
