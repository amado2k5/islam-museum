## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 21 to 25 as a single unit. He interprets the phrase “abiding therein for ages” as meaning that these tyrants in this world will abide in Hell, remaining therein for ages. The reciters differed in their reading of the word “abiding.” The majority of reciters from Medina and Basra, and some reciters from Kufa, read it as “abiding” with an alif. The general readers of Kufa read it as “labithin” without an alif, and the more eloquent and correct of the two readings in terms of pronunciation in Arabic is the reading of those who read it with an alif. This is because the Arabs hardly ever apply the adjective when it comes in the form of fa’il, so that it acts on something and makes it accusative. They hardly ever say: This is a man who is stingy with his money, and he is not difficult for us, and he is not an adversary to us, because fa’il does not come as an adjective except in praise or blame, so praise and blame do not act on anything else. And if they want to act on a noun or something else, they make it a subject, so they say: He is stingy with his money, and he is greedy for what we have. Therefore I said: “labith” and he quotes in the passage sayings on the authority of Qatada, Al-Hasan and Sufyan.
