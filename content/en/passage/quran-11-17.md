## Summary
Verse 17 of [[passage/quran-11|Surat Hud]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 17 of 123 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 17 (pp. 269-281)**

On [[passage/quran-11-17|11:17]] al-Tabari says: 'is he who is on a clear proof from his Lord', whose religion has been made clear to him so that he sees it, 'and a witness from Him follows it'. The interpreters differed. Some said by 'he who is on a clear proof from his Lord' Muhammad is meant. Report 18030, from Muhammad b. al-Hanafiyya: I said to my father, father, are you the follower in 'and a witness from Him follows it'? He said no, by God, my son, I wish I were he, but it is his tongue. Reports 18031 to 18034, from al-Hasan: his tongue; report 18035, from Qatada: it is Muhammad, who was on a clear proof from his Lord; reports 18036, 18037 and 18038, from al-Hasan and Qatada: his tongue is the witness. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'clear proof', 'follow' and 'witness'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 270}

Report 18039, from al-Hasan, likewise. Others said that by 'and a witness from Him follows it' Muhammad is meant. Reports 18040, 18041 and 18042, from al-Hasan b. Ali: the witness is Muhammad; the editor says Sulayman al-Allaf related this from al-Hasan b. Ali and al-Bukhari called it mursal, and that the printed text and manuscript have the name 'al-Husayn', an error. Report 18043, from Ibn Zayd: the Messenger of God was on a clear proof from his Lord and the Qur'an follows, a witness too from God that he is God's messenger. Reports 18044 to 18047, from Mujahid, Ikrima, Ibrahim and Sufyan: the Prophet, Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 272}

Others said it is Ali b. Abi Talib. Report 18048, from Abd Allah b. Nujayy: Ali said, there is no man of Quraysh in whom a verse or two did not come down, and a man said to him, and in you, what came down? Ali said: do you not read the verse that came down in Hud, 'and a witness from Him follows it'? The editor says its chain contains Jabir al-Ju'fi, who is weak to a severe degree, and that Abd Allah b. Nujayy is not strong, and quotes al-Dhahabi that the objectionable element is from Jabir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 272}

Others said it is Gabriel. Report 18049, from Ibn Abbas, that he used to say Gabriel; report 18050, from Ibrahim: Gabriel; report 18051, from him: they say Ali, but it is Gabriel; report 18052, from Mujahid: it is Gabriel, who recited the Torah, the Gospel and the Qur'an and is the witness from God; reports 18053 to 18059, from Ibrahim, Mujahid, al-Suddi from Abu Salih, and al-Dahhak: Gabriel. Report 18060, from al-Dahhak: it means Muhammad is on a clear proof from God, 'and a witness from Him follows it' is Gabriel, a witness from God, reciting to Muhammad what he was sent with. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 273}

Reports 18061, 18062 and 18063, from Abu al-Aliya, Ikrima and Ibrahim: Gabriel; report 18064, from Ibn Abbas: it means Muhammad is on a clear proof from his Lord, 'a witness from Him follows it' is Gabriel, a witness from God by what he recites of God's Book sent down on Muhammad, and it is also said that the angel with him guards him; report 18065, from Mujahid: Gabriel. Others said it is an angel who guards him. Reports 18066, 18067, 18068, 18069 and 18071, from Mujahid: with him is a guardian from God, an angel; report 18070, from him: the angel guards him, and 'they follow it as it should be followed' in [[passage/quran-2-121|2:121]] means they follow it as it should be followed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 275}

Al-Tabari says the soundest of these sayings on 'and a witness from Him follows it' is that of those who said it is Gabriel, because His words 'and before it the book of Moses as a guide and mercy' show it to be right: the Prophet did not recite the book of Moses before the Qur'an, so that would be a proof for those who said it means Muhammad's tongue, or Muhammad himself, or Ali, and it is not known that anyone recited that before the Qur'an or brought it, of those the interpreters named, but Gabriel. If someone says then 'book of Moses' should be read in the accusative, he replies that the readers of the cities agree on the nominative, so no one may differ, and had the reading come in the accusative it would be sound reading and meaning. The reason for their nominative is that they began the report of the coming of the book of Moses before our book sent down on Muhammad, and the sense of Gabriel's reciting it before the Qur'an is meant, even though the report is a fresh one, as the speech itself shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 276}

As for 'a guide', it is in the accusative as a circumstantial of 'the book of Moses', and 'and mercy' is joined to 'guide', as if it said: before it the book of Moses as a guide for the Children of Israel whom they follow, and a mercy from God, which he recited to Moses. Report 18072, from Ibrahim: before it he came with the book to Moses. A phrase is left out in the speech, omitted because what is said shows it: is he who is on a clear proof from his Lord, and a witness from Him follows it, and before it the book of Moses as a guide and mercy, like one who wanders in error, not guided to right, not knowing truth from falsehood and seeking by his deeds only the life of this world and its adornment? This is like [[passage/quran-39-9|39:9]]. The evidence is that it follows 'whoever wants the life of this world'; then it says, is this better than one who is on a clear proof from his Lord? The Arabs often do this when what is said shows what they mean by what they omit, and he cites a line of Imru' al-Qays that swears and omits the answer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 277}

The editor notes in tracing the line of Imru' al-Qays that it is missing from most transmissions and that al-Baghdadi quoted verses from the poem. His words 'those believe in it' mean those I mentioned affirm and acknowledge it, even if these polytheists who say Muhammad invented it disbelieve in it. His words 'whoever disbelieves in it among the factions, the Fire is his promised place' mean whoever disbelieves in this Qur'an and denies it is from God, among the factions, those who band together on their creeds, 'the Fire is his promised place', that he will end in it in the next life through his denial. 'So be not in doubt about it' means be not in doubt that the promised place of whoever disbelieves in the Qur'an among the factions is the Fire and that this Qur'an we sent down to you is from God; then He begins a report about the Qur'an: this Qur'an we sent down to you, Muhammad, is the truth from your Lord, no doubt in it, but most people do not affirm that it is so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 278}

If someone asks whether the Prophet was in doubt that the Qur'an is from God and true, that he was told 'so be not in doubt about it', the reply is that this is like [[passage/quran-10-94|10:94]], which al-Tabari explained there. Report 18073, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: no hadith of the Messenger of God reached me in its right form but I found its confirmation in God's Book, until he said 'none of this community hears of me, Jew or Christian, and does not believe in what I was sent with, but enters the Fire'; Sa'id said I asked, where is this in God's Book? until I came to this verse, 'and before it the book of Moses as a guide and mercy; those believe in it, and whoever disbelieves in it among the factions, the Fire is his promised place', and he said: from the people of all the creeds. Reports 18074, 18075 and 18076, from him, to the same effect: the factions are all the creeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 279}

Report 18077, from Qatada: the disbelievers are all factions on disbelief; report 18078, from him on [[passage/quran-13-36|13:36]]: that is, they disbelieve part of it, and they are the Jews and Christians; he said it has reached us that the Prophet used to say none of this community hears of me, nor Jew nor Christian, and dies before believing in me, but enters the Fire; report 18079, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, from the Prophet: whoever of my community, or a Jew or a Christian, hears of me and does not believe in me will not enter Paradise. The editor says al-Hakim relates it as a connected report from Ibn Abbas, calls it sound and al-Dhahabi agrees, that Muslim relates it from Abu Hurayra, and that Ahmad relates it in his Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 281}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
