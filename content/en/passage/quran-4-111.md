## Summary
Verse 111 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 111 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 111 (p. 196, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that whoever commits a sin knowingly and deliberately only draws its burden, harm, disgrace and shame upon himself and not on any other of God's creation. So he addresses those who argue for these traitors: do not argue, for even if you are their clan, kin and neighbours, you are free of the sin they committed and of the liability for which they are answerable, and if you defend them or contend on their account you will be like them, so do not defend or contend. On the closing clause, God was ever knowing of what you do, you who argue for those who betray themselves, in your arguing for them and your other acts and the acts of others; He counts them against you and against them until He repays all of you for them; and wise in His management of you and of all His creation. He adds that it is said the verse came down about Banu Ubayriq, as he gave from those who said so earlier. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the words for earning and for sin (volume 8, p. 267 and volume 4, p. 328, with notes), says that the printed text and manuscript read "whenever you defended" where the context requires "and you, whenever you defended", that the language indexes give the words for knowing and wise, and that the earlier reports he means are 10409 to 10418. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 196}
