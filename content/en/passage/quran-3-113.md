## Summary
Verse 113 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 113 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The two groups are not alike (p. 118).** Al-Tabari says the denial of equality means the two groups of the People of the Book, the believers among them and the disbelievers, are not equal but differ in goodness and corruption, good and evil. It is said because the passage has mentioned the two groups God mentioned in [[passage/quran-3-110|3:110]], and God then reports the state of the two with Him, the believing and the disbelieving, and then begins to describe the believing group among the People of the Book; this continues on the next page. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word for "alike". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 118}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 118}

**Praise of the believing group (pp. 118-119).** Al-Tabari says that after describing the defiant group among the People of the Book as fainthearted and weak of heart, living with humiliation and abasement, bound to need and destitution and bearing disgrace in this world and shame in the next, God began the report on the believing group, praising and commending it, in these three verses down to the one ending with the God-fearing; the word for the upright community is nominative through the preceding phrase. The editor glosses a word for faintheartedness as cowardice and weakness of heart and corrects a letter at the start of the sentence from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 118} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 119}

**A mistake of some grammarians (pp. 119-120).** Al-Tabari says a group of the early Kufan and Basran grammarians supposed that the upright community is an explanatory substitute for the word of equality, so that the sense would be: an upright community reciting God's book and another disbelieving are not equal, and that mention of the other group was left out as one suffices. They illustrated it with a line of Abu Dhu'ayb about a heart that he disobeyed in seeking its way, asking whether its pursuit was right, without saying "or not right", and with another line, by a poet the editor could not identify, about care and treachery. The editor notes this is close to al-Farra's text and some of his witnesses (Ma'ani al-Qur'an 1:230-231), explains the technical word for substitute, refers to Abu Dhu'ayb's line in volume 1, corrects a printed reading from the manuscript and al-Farra's book, and says he cannot tell whether the poet addresses a woman or means treachery and will not decide until he finds companions of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 119} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 120}

**Al-Tabari's reply (p. 120).** He says that by their own rules it is wrong to say "equal whether you stood" without going on to "or you sat"; they permit dropping the second only where one is enough, such as "I do not mind" and "I do not know", and not in what falls short of that like the word of equality, which is not satisfied with one. So they neglected, in applying the verse as they reported, their own rules of Arabic, permitting a deletion they do not permit with that word, and they erred in the verse's meaning; the word here has the sense of completion and sufficiency, not what those quoted took it to be. The editor explains the grammar of one word in the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 120}

**The occasion (7644, 7645, pp. 120-121).** It is mentioned that the three verses were revealed about a group of Jews who became Muslims and whose Islam was good. From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Muhammad ibn Ishaq, Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas (7644): when Abdullah ibn Salam, Tha'laba ibn Sa'ya, Usayd ibn Sa'ya, Asad ibn Ubayd and those of the Jews who became Muslims with them believed, affirmed and grew attached to Islam, the rabbis of the Jews and their disbelievers said that none believed in Muhammad or followed him except their wicked ones, and had they been among their best they would not have left their fathers' religion for another; so God sent down these verses regarding what they said, down to the mention of the righteous. From Abu Kurayb, Yunus ibn Bukayr, Ibn Ishaq, a client of Zayd ibn Thabit, Sa'id ibn Jubayr or Ikrima, Ibn Abbas (7645): similar. The editor says both reports are in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2:206), where the wording "our wicked ones" is the better, and that he corrected the name of Yunus ibn Bukayr, a chain that recurs often. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 120} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 121}

**Qatada and Ibn Jurayj (7646, 7647, p. 121).** From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7646): not all the people perished, for God had a remnant among them; the editor says he kept the printed reading as it agrees with al-Durr al-Manthur (2:64-65). From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7647): the upright community are Abdullah ibn Salam, his brother Tha'laba, Sa'ya, Mubashshir, and Usayd and Asad, sons of Ka'b; the editor corrects "Sa'ya" from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 121}

**Second view: the People of the Book and Muhammad's community are not equal (7648, 7649, pp. 121-122).** Others said the sense is that the People of the Book and the community of Muhammad that stands by God's right are not equal with God. From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, al-Hasan ibn Yazid al-Ijli, Abdullah ibn Mas'ud (7648): the People of the Book and Muhammad's community are not equal; from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7649): these Jews are not like this upright community. The editor says Abu Asim is al-Dahhak ibn Makhlad al-Nabil and Isa is Ibn Maymun al-Jurashi al-Makki, that al-Hasan ibn Yazid al-Ijli is a reliable Successor listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy and noted by al-Bukhari in his Kabir and Ibn Abi Hatim without criticism, that Ibn Kathir has the report (2:224), and that it has a continuation by the same chain at 7660, which al-Suyuti joined as one report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 121} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 122}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 122).** He says he has shown that the sounder of the two views is that of those who held that the account is complete at the denial of equality, as God's report on the matter of the believers of the People of the Book and the disbelievers among them, and that what follows is a new statement praising their believers and describing them, as Ibn Abbas, Qatada and Ibn Jurayj said. The upright community means a group firm on the truth; he explained the meaning of "community" earlier, and the editor refers to p. 106. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 122}

**The meaning of "upright" (7650-7654, pp. 122-123).** The interpreters differed. Some said just: from Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7650). Others said standing by God's book and what it commands: from Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7651): standing by God's book, duties and limits; from Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7652) similarly; from Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his father, Ibn Abbas (7653): a guided community standing by God's command, not drawn away from it and leaving it as others left and neglected it. Others said obedient: from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7654): these Jews are not like this community which is devout to God, the devout being the obedient. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 122} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 123}

**Al-Tabari's weighing (7655, pp. 123-124).** Al-Tabari says the soundest of these for the verse is what Ibn Abbas and Qatada and those who agreed with them said, though the other views are close in meaning, because upright means straight on guidance, God's book, duties and the laws of religion, and justice, obedience and other kinds of good are among the traits of those upright on God's book and His messenger's sunna. A parallel is the report of al-Nu'man ibn Bashir from the Prophet, the likeness of one who stands by God's limits and one who falls into them is that of people who boarded a ship (7655); the one who stands by the limits is the one who holds firm to what God commanded and avoids what He forbade. For him the sense is a group of the People of the Book holding fast to God's book, firm in acting by it and by what His messenger established for them. The editor says the report is sound and that al-Tabari alludes to it without its chain; Ahmad has it in the Musnad (4:268 ff.) from Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash, al-Sha'bi, al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, as the likeness of one who stands by God's limits and the one who persists in breaching them, like people who cast lots over a ship at sea, some getting the lower part and some the upper; those below would go up to draw water and pour on those above, who said they would not let them harm them, and those below said they would then pierce it from below to draw water; if the others seize their hands, all are saved, and if they leave them, all drown. Al-Bukhari has it (5:94 and 5:216-217 of the Fath) by several routes from al-Sha'bi. The editor corrects a wording in the note, found in both manuscript and printed text, that spoilt the sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 123} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 124}

**Reading God's book in the night hours (pp. 125-126).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that they read God's book in the hours of the night, pondering and reflecting on it, the signs meaning the lessons and admonitions He sent down in His book. The hours of night are the plural of the word "ina", for which he cites a line of al-Mutanakhkhil al-Hudhali describing his son (the editor says it will later appear in al-Tabari ascribed to al-Munakhkhal al-Yashkuri, an error; its places are the Diwan of the Hudhalis 2:35, Majaz al-Qur'an 1:102, Ibn Hisham's Sira 2:206 and Lisan al-Arab; it is from his elegy for his son Uthayla; the version Ibn al-Anbari gave, as the Lisan has it, is a confusion with another line; he explains the sweetness and bitterness and the arrow-shaft in the line, and says a word in the manuscript was unpointed so he replaced it with the Lisan's). It is also said that the singular of the plural is a shortened form, as the singular of "intestines" is a shortened form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 125} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 126}

**What the interpreters said about the night hours (7656-7661, pp. 126-127).** Some said the hours of night: from Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7656); from Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7657); from al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Abdullah ibn Kathir (7658), who said they heard the Arabs say that. Others said the middle of the night: from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7659). Others said it means a people who prayed the late evening prayer: from Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, al-Hasan ibn Yazid al-Ijli, Ibn Mas'ud (7660): it is the late-evening prayer, which they pray and others of the People of the Book do not. From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Yahya ibn Ayyub, Ubaydallah ibn Zahr, Sulayman, Zirr ibn Hubaysh, Ibn Mas'ud (7661): God's messenger was kept from us one night, being with some of his family, and did not come for the night prayer until part of the night had gone; he came, and some of us were praying and some lying down, and he gave us good news and said none of the People of the Book prays this prayer; so God sent down the verse. The editor says 7660 completes the earlier report 7648, which al-Suyuti joined (2:65) and ascribed to al-Firyabi, al-Bukhari in his Tarikh, Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn Jarir, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim; that al-Bukhari gave only the last part, briefly, in the entry on al-Hasan ibn Yazid, and that Umar ibn Dharr related it from him as a mursal report; that Ubaydallah ibn Zahr al-Damri al-Ifriqi was judged trustworthy by al-Bukhari as al-Tirmidhi quotes and by Ahmad ibn Salih, weakened by Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in and Ibn al-Madini, and called soft in hadith by Abu Hatim and "no harm, truthful" by Abu Zur'a; the note continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 127}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 127 in the commentary on verse 113 and continue from p. 128. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 127}
