**Commentary on verse 28: "if you stretch out your hand to kill me", pp. 213-214**

Al-Tabari says this reports what the slain son of Adam said to his brother after he said "I will kill you": if you stretch your hand to me to kill me, I will not stretch mine to you to kill you. The cause of his saying this without resisting is disputed. Some said he said it to make known that he did not hold killing him or raising his hand lawful where God had not permitted it. Report 11727, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr, says the slain one was the stronger of the two but scrupulousness stopped him from raising his hand to his brother, and report 11728, from Ibn 'Abbas, says he meant that he would not defend himself and would hold back his hand.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 213}

Others said nothing stopped him from what he wanted but that God had made it binding on them that one whom another meant to kill should not resist; report 11729, from Mujahid, says it was written for them that if a man wished to kill another, he left him and did not resist.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 214}

Al-Tabari's choice is that God had already forbidden killing a person without right, and the slain son spoke so because killing his brother was as forbidden to him as killing him was to his brother. As for not resisting, nothing shows he knew of the killer's resolve and left off defending himself; a group of scholars report the killer struck him by surprise while asleep and crushed his head with a rock, and where the verse does not show he was commanded not to resist, claiming what is not in it requires proof. He explains "I fear God, Lord of the worlds" as: I fear that the Lord of all creation will punish me for stretching my hand to you.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 214}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
