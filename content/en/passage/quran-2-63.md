## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We took your covenant" al-Tabari says "mithaq" is a form from "wathiqa", a binding, whether by oath or pledge, and means the covenant that God reports He took in the verses "worship none but God, and be good to parents". He gives Ibn Zayd on its cause: when Moses returned with the tablets they said they would not take them until they saw God openly; a thunderbolt struck them and God revived them, yet they refused the book, so He sent angels who uprooted the mountain over them, and they were told to take the book or it would be thrown on them, and they took it with the covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 156} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 157}
On "and We raised the Mount above you" he says "Tur" is the Arabic for a mountain, though some say it is the name of a particular mountain, the one on which God spoke to Moses. He reports from Mujahid that it was the mountain on which their Lord had shown Himself, that God drew its base out of the earth and raised it over them like a canopy, that Tur in Syriac means mountain, and that they were told to believe or it would fall on them; and from Qatada and Abu al-Aliya that God uprooted it and raised it above their heads to frighten them, and they acknowledged. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 157} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 158}

Al-Tabari completes the reports on "Tur": Ikrima says it is the mountain; al-Suddi that when they refused to prostrate God told the mountain to fall on them, and they fell prostrate on one side while watching with the other, and He had mercy on them and lifted it; Ibn Abbas that it is the mountain on which the Torah came down with the Israelites below it; and from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Abbas, that a "tur" is a mountain that grows vegetation, and one that does not is not a tur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 158} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 159}
On "take what We gave you with strength" he gives the grammarians' differences about an omission: some Basrans say "and We said to you" is omitted, some Kufans say taking a covenant is itself a saying so no omission need be assumed; al-Tabari holds that any speech whose meaning is understood is enough in itself. "What We gave you" is what they were commanded in the Torah, and "with strength" means with earnestness, which he reports from Mujahid, Abu al-Aliya, Qatada, al-Suddi and Ibn Zayd as acting on it with diligence, obedience and sincerity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 159} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 160} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 161}
On "and remember what is in it, that you may fear God" he says the meaning is: remember what is in the book We gave you of promise and warning, encouragement and caution, recite it and take heed of it, so you may fear My punishment; Ibn Abbas says "you will leave what you are doing", and Abu al-Aliya and Ibn Zayd say it means what is in the Torah, acting on it and not forgetting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 161} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 162}
