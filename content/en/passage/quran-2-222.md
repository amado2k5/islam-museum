## Summary
Verse 222 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 222 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

He means that the Prophet's Companions ask him about menstruation (see [[passage/quran-2-222|2:222]]). Al-Tabari notes that the word for it is formed as a verbal noun and noun from the verb whose past tense has a fatha on its middle letter and whose present has a kasra, since the Arabs build the verbal noun of such verbs on the pattern maf'al and the noun on maf'il, and this is heard in defective roots with parallels such as ma'ish and ma'ash, ma'ib and ma'ab; he cites a rajaz line of Ru'ba in praise of al-Harith ibn Sulaym al-Hujaymi, and the editor sets out its reading from the diwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 372} The people asked about menstruation, as we are told, because before God's clarification they would not share a house with a menstruating woman, eat with her from one vessel or drink with her; God taught them through the verse that what is required of them during their wives' menses is only to avoid intercourse, not sleeping beside them, eating with them or drinking with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 372} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 373} From Qatada: the people of the Jahiliyya did not share a house with a menstruating woman or eat with her from one vessel; God forbade her vulva while she menstruates and permitted all else, that she dye your head, eat of your food and lie in your bed if she wears an izar tied about her apart from you (4231); from al-Rabi' the same (4232). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 373}

It is also said that they asked because during menses they avoided the place of the blood and approached women from behind, so God forbade them to come near until the women were clean, then allowed them, once clean, to come to them from where He had commanded, and forbade coming to them from behind in every case. From Mujahid: they avoided women during menstruation and came to them from behind, so they asked the Prophet and the verse came down, to the part about coming from where God commanded: in the vulva, not going beyond it (4233). The editor corrects the wording from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 373} It is said that the questioner was Thabit ibn al-Dahdah al-Ansari, from al-Suddi (4234). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 374}

On the answer that it is "harm": say to whoever asks about menstruation, it is harm; harm is what one is hurt by of what is disliked, and here it is called harm for its foul smell, filth and impurity, and it combines several meanings. The interpreters differed in explaining it, though some of their sayings are close in meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 374} Some said it is filth, from al-Suddi (4235) and Qatada (4236). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 374} Others said it is blood, from Mujahid: the harm is the blood (4237). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 375}

On the command to withdraw from women in menstruation: it means avoiding intercourse with women during their menses; from Ibn 'Abbas: avoid having intercourse with them (4238). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 375} The scholars differed on what a man must avoid of a menstruating woman. Some said he must avoid her whole body, touching it with any part of his own; from Muhammad, that is Ibn Sirin, who said: I asked 'Ubayda, what is lawful to me of my wife when she is menstruating? He said, the bed is one and the covers are separate (4239). The editor notes that the printed text and the manuscript both have the two terms reversed and that the right wording comes by another route at no. 4241. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 375}

From Nudba, the freedwoman of the family of 'Abbas: Maymuna bint al-Harith, or Hafsa bint 'Umar, sent me to the wife of 'Abdallah ibn 'Abbas, and I found her bed set apart from his, and thought it was estrangement; I asked her and she said, I am menstruating and when I do he withdraws from my bed; I returned and told Maymuna, who sent me to Ibn 'Abbas: your mother says, have you turned from the sunna of the Messenger of God? By God, the Prophet used to sleep with one of his wives while she menstruated, with only a cloth between them not passing the knees (4240). The editor notes that Nudba is trustworthy and is mentioned by Ibn Hibban; that Ahmad's Musnad relates it from Maymuna definitely, without the doubt between her and Hafsa; that "from al-Zuhri from 'Urwa" is an error and the right chain is "from al-Zuhri from Habib, mawla of 'Urwa, from Nudba", Habib being al-A'war, a trustworthy tabi'i whom Muslim cites; and that al-Bayhaqi, al-Nasa'i and Abu Dawud relate it by this route in shorter form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 376} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 377} From Muhammad ibn Sirin: I asked 'Ubayda, what does a man have of his wife when she is menstruating? He said, the bed is one and the covers are separate, and if he finds nothing but to throw some of his garment over her, he throws it over her (4241). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 376}

Those who held this view argued that God commanded withdrawing from women in menstruation without singling out anything, so the command covers their whole bodies and it is obligatory to withdraw from every part of them while they menstruate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 377}

Others said, rather, what God commanded withdrawal from is the place of harm, the outlet of the blood. From Masruq ibn al-Ajda': I said to 'A'isha, what is lawful to a man of his wife when she is menstruating? She said, everything but intercourse (4242). The note says Marwan al-Asfar is a trustworthy tabi'i; that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti took it from al-Tabari; and that the editor counts it as raised (marfu') in meaning though worded as 'A'isha's own words, since a Companion stating what is lawful and forbidden is trusted to state it only from the one from whom such things are taken, unless indications show he says it by his own judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 378} From Qatada: it was related to us that 'A'isha said, where then were those who kept two beds and two covers? (4243); from Masruq: what is forbidden to a man of his wife when she is menstruating? She said, her vulva (4244); and Masruq rode to 'A'isha and greeted her, and she said, Abu 'A'isha, welcome; he came in and said, I want to ask you something and I am shy; she said, I am your mother and you are my son; he said, what does a man have of his wife while she menstruates? She said, everything but her vulva (4245); from Maymun ibn Mihran, from 'A'isha: he has what is above the izar (4246). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 378} From Nafi', that 'A'isha said about lying with a menstruating woman: no harm in it if she wears an izar (4247); from Abu Ma'shar, from 'A'isha: everything but the vulva (4248), where the note says the chain is defective because Abu Ma'shar Ziyad ibn Kulayb did not meet 'A'isha, and that Ibn Hazm's al-Muhalla gives the right chain through Ibrahim al-Nakha'i from Masruq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 379} From Ibn 'Abbas: if the menstruating woman puts a cloth or something that holds back the harm on her vulva, there is no harm in her husband touching her skin (4249), the note saying the chain is broken because Muhammad ibn Ibrahim's report from Ibn 'Abbas is mursal; from him also: what is above the izar (4250); and from him: guard against blood the size of a sandal's place (4251). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 379} From Umm Salama: no harm in lying with a menstruating woman if there is a rag over her vulva (4252); the note says the chain is sound and that its meaning is also firm from her as a report of the Prophet, in al-Bayhaqi and Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 380} From al-Hasan: a man has of his wife everything but the vulva (4253); from him also: they sleep in one cover if there is a cloth over the vulva (4254); from Mujahid, on a man's play with his wife while she menstruates: what lies between the thighs, buttocks and navel is allowed, so long as it is not the rear or the place of menses (4255); from 'Amir al-Sha'bi, on touching her: if she has held back the harm (4256); from 'Ikrima: everything of the menstruating woman is lawful to you except the passage of blood (4257). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 380} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381}

Al-Tabari gives the ground of those who held this view: proof stands from the many reports of the Prophet that he touched his wives while they menstruated, and had it been obligatory to withdraw from all of them he would not have done so; once that is sound, it is known that what God meant was withdrawing from part of her body and not all, and that this must be intercourse, which is agreed forbidden to the husband in her front passage, as against the other parts of her body about which there is disagreement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381}

Others said, rather, what God commanded withdrawal from is what lies between the navel and the knee, and he may have what is above that and below it. From Shurayh, on the menstruating woman: he has what is above the navel (4258); from Ibn 'Abbas, asked what a menstruating woman's husband has of her: what is above the izar (4259); from Shurayh: he has what is above her navel (4260). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381}

From Waqid ibn Muhammad ibn Zayd ibn 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab was asked, what does a man have of a menstruating woman? He said, what is above the izar (4261). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 382} The ground of those who held this view is the sound report of the Prophet: from Maymuna, when the Prophet wished to touch one of his wives while she was menstruating he told her to put on an izar and she did (4262); from her also, that the Prophet used to touch her while she menstruated above the izar (4263); from 'A'isha, when one of us menstruated he told her, she wrapped an izar, and then he touched her (4264, 4265). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 382} The notes say these reports are in the two Sahihs and the Sunan, and that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti give their wording and refer to al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 382} They said: what the Prophet did is permitted, and everything else of the menstruating woman's body must be avoided because the verse is general (see [[passage/quran-2-222|2:222]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 383} Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that of those who said a man has of his menstruating wife what is above the wrapped part and what is below it, for the ground mentioned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 383}

On "do not approach them until they are clean": the readers differed in reading it; some read it light, with a short unemphasized form and damma, others with the doubled form and fatha. Those who read it light take the sense as, do not approach the women in their menses until the blood of menses stops and they become clean, and a group of interpreters said this; from Mujahid, the stopping of the blood (4266); from Sufyan, or 'Uthman ibn al-Aswad, until the blood stops from them (4267); from 'Ikrima, until the blood stops (4268). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 383} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 384} Those who read it doubled meant until they wash with water, since to them the word means "until they cleanse themselves", the ta' being assimilated into the following letter because their points of articulation are close. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 384}

Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two readings is the doubled one with fatha, meaning until they wash, because all agree that it is forbidden for a man to approach his wife after her menstrual blood stops until she is clean; the difference is over the cleansing that makes intercourse lawful: some said it is washing with water, so her husband may not approach her until she has washed her whole body; some said it is ablution for prayer; others said it is washing the private part, and when she has washed it that is her cleansing by which her husband may have intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 384} Since all agree she is not lawful to her husband merely by the stopping of the blood until she is clean, the better reading is the one least likely to mislead its hearer, and the light reading cannot be secured against a hearer thinking that a menstruating woman's husband may have intercourse after the blood stops and before she has washed and cleansed herself. So the verse means: they ask you about menstruation; say, it is harm; so avoid intercourse with your wives in the time of their menses and do not approach them until they wash and cleanse themselves of their menses after it stops. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 385} (The editor notes that 'Ubaydallah al-'Ataki of report 4268 saw Anas and narrated from 'Ikrima and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, and that from here he follows the manuscript's wording "the readers" without comment.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 383} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 384}

On "when they have cleansed themselves, come to them from where God commanded you": it means when they have washed and cleansed themselves with water, have intercourse with them. If one asks, is intercourse then obligatory? The answer is no; this permits what had been prevented before and releases what was barred during menstruation, like the command to hunt after leaving ihram (5:2) and to disperse in the land when the prayer is finished (62:10), and the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 385} The interpreters differed. Some said it means when they have washed; from Ibn 'Abbas, when she is cleansed of the blood and has cleansed herself with water (4269); from Mujahid, when they have washed (4270); from 'Ikrima, they have washed (4271); from Sufyan or 'Uthman ibn al-Aswad, when they have washed (4272); from al-Hasan, on a menstruating woman who sees she is clean: her husband does not come to her until she has washed and prayer is lawful for her (4273); from Ibrahim, that he disliked having intercourse with her until she had washed (4275). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 386} (The editor notes that number 4274 fell out of the printed numbering.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 386}

Others said it means "when they have cleansed themselves for prayer"; from Tawus and Mujahid, when the woman is clean of the blood and her husband wishes to tell her to perform ablution before she washes, if desire overtakes him, let him proceed (4276). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 386} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 387}

Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two interpretations is that of those who said it means when they have washed, since all agree that she does not become clean by ablution with the cleanness that makes prayer lawful for her; and the saying can only be one of two things: either it means when they have cleansed themselves of impurity, which would make intercourse lawful whenever the blood stops provided no impurity shows, and he knows of no permissible usage for that except a forced reading; or it means when they have cleansed themselves for prayer, and the agreement of the whole community that prayer is not lawful for her except by washing is the clearest evidence that intercourse is forbidden until she has washed, and that the sense is, when they have washed and become clean with the cleanness that makes prayer valid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 387}

On the command to come to them from where God commanded (see [[passage/quran-2-222|2:222]]), the interpreters differed. Some said it means come to your wives, when they are clean, from the aspect from which I forbade you to approach them in menstruation, which is the passage God ordered left in menstruation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 388} From Ibn 'Abbas: from where He commanded you to withdraw (4277); from him: in the vulva, not going beyond it to anything else, for whoever does any of that has transgressed (4278); from 'Ikrima: from where He commanded you to withdraw (4279). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 388} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr: while Mujahid and I were sitting with Ibn 'Abbas a man came and stood at his head and said, Abu al-'Abbas, will you not cure me on the verse of menstruation? He said, yes, and recited it to its end, and Ibn 'Abbas said, from where the blood came, from there you were commanded to come (4280). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 388} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 389} From Mujahid: the other passage of a woman is the same as a man's, then he recited the verses and said, from where He commanded you to withdraw (4281); from him: they were commanded to come to them from where they had been forbidden (4282); from him: in the vulva and not beyond it (4283); from him: when they are clean, come to them from where it was forbidden in menstruation (4284); from Sufyan, or 'Uthman ibn al-Aswad: by withdrawing from it (4285). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 389} From Qatada: from the aspect from which menstruation comes, when she is clean and not menstruating, and do not go beyond that to anything else (4286); in another report from him: clean women, without intercourse and without menstruation, from the aspect from which menstruation comes, and Sa'id said, I know it only from Ibn 'Abbas (4287); from al-Rabi' and Mujahid: from where you were forbidden in menstruation, and guard against the other passages (4288); from Ibrahim: in the vulva (4289). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 390} The notes give lexical explanations of the euphemism for intercourse and of the plural of "tahir" (clean) as "tawahir", referring to later numbers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 390}

Others said it means come to them from the aspect from which God commanded you to come to them, and that aspect is the time of cleanness rather than menstruation; so the sense of the one who says this is, come to them at the time of their cleanness, not at the time of their menses. From Ibn 'Abbas: he means that he comes to her when she is clean and not menstruating (4290); from Abu Razin: from the time of cleanness (4291, 4292); in one report, come to them at the time of cleanness (4293); in another, from the time of cleanness, and do not come to them at the time of the menstrual period (4294). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 390} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 391} From 'Ikrima: when they have washed, come to them from where God commanded you, meaning clean, not menstruating (4295); from Qatada: clean, not menstruating (4296). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 391} The note explains the phrase "at the time of cleanness" as in the state of cleanness and at its beginning, when she can enter on her waiting period. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 390}
