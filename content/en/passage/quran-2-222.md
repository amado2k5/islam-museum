## Summary
Verse 222 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 222 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

He means that the Prophet's Companions ask him about menstruation (see [[passage/quran-2-222|2:222]]). Al-Tabari notes that the word for it is formed as a verbal noun and noun from the verb whose past tense has a fatha on its middle letter and whose present has a kasra, since the Arabs build the verbal noun of such verbs on the pattern maf'al and the noun on maf'il, and this is heard in defective roots with parallels such as ma'ish and ma'ash, ma'ib and ma'ab; he cites a rajaz line of Ru'ba in praise of al-Harith ibn Sulaym al-Hujaymi, and the editor sets out its reading from the diwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 372} The people asked about menstruation, as we are told, because before God's clarification they would not share a house with a menstruating woman, eat with her from one vessel or drink with her; God taught them through the verse that what is required of them during their wives' menses is only to avoid intercourse, not sleeping beside them, eating with them or drinking with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 372} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 373} From Qatada: the people of the Jahiliyya did not share a house with a menstruating woman or eat with her from one vessel; God forbade her vulva while she menstruates and permitted all else, that she dye your head, eat of your food and lie in your bed if she wears an izar tied about her apart from you (4231); from al-Rabi' the same (4232). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 373}

It is also said that they asked because during menses they avoided the place of the blood and approached women from behind, so God forbade them to come near until the women were clean, then allowed them, once clean, to come to them from where He had commanded, and forbade coming to them from behind in every case. From Mujahid: they avoided women during menstruation and came to them from behind, so they asked the Prophet and the verse came down, to the part about coming from where God commanded: in the vulva, not going beyond it (4233). The editor corrects the wording from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 373} It is said that the questioner was Thabit ibn al-Dahdah al-Ansari, from al-Suddi (4234). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 374}

On the answer that it is "harm": say to whoever asks about menstruation, it is harm; harm is what one is hurt by of what is disliked, and here it is called harm for its foul smell, filth and impurity, and it combines several meanings. The interpreters differed in explaining it, though some of their sayings are close in meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 374} Some said it is filth, from al-Suddi (4235) and Qatada (4236). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 374} Others said it is blood, from Mujahid: the harm is the blood (4237). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 375}

On the command to withdraw from women in menstruation: it means avoiding intercourse with women during their menses; from Ibn 'Abbas: avoid having intercourse with them (4238). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 375} The scholars differed on what a man must avoid of a menstruating woman. Some said he must avoid her whole body, touching it with any part of his own; from Muhammad, that is Ibn Sirin, who said: I asked 'Ubayda, what is lawful to me of my wife when she is menstruating? He said, the bed is one and the covers are separate (4239). The editor notes that the printed text and the manuscript both have the two terms reversed and that the right wording comes by another route at no. 4241. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 375}

From Nudba, the freedwoman of the family of 'Abbas: Maymuna bint al-Harith, or Hafsa bint 'Umar, sent me to the wife of 'Abdallah ibn 'Abbas, and I found her bed set apart from his, and thought it was estrangement; I asked her and she said, I am menstruating and when I do he withdraws from my bed; I returned and told Maymuna, who sent me to Ibn 'Abbas: your mother says, have you turned from the sunna of the Messenger of God? By God, the Prophet used to sleep with one of his wives while she menstruated, with only a cloth between them not passing the knees (4240). The editor notes that Nudba is trustworthy and is mentioned by Ibn Hibban; that Ahmad's Musnad relates it from Maymuna definitely, without the doubt between her and Hafsa; that "from al-Zuhri from 'Urwa" is an error and the right chain is "from al-Zuhri from Habib, mawla of 'Urwa, from Nudba", Habib being al-A'war, a trustworthy tabi'i whom Muslim cites; and that al-Bayhaqi, al-Nasa'i and Abu Dawud relate it by this route in shorter form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 376} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 377} From Muhammad ibn Sirin: I asked 'Ubayda, what does a man have of his wife when she is menstruating? He said, the bed is one and the covers are separate, and if he finds nothing but to throw some of his garment over her, he throws it over her (4241). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 376}

Those who held this view argued that God commanded withdrawing from women in menstruation without singling out anything, so the command covers their whole bodies and it is obligatory to withdraw from every part of them while they menstruate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 377}

Others said, rather, what God commanded withdrawal from is the place of harm, the outlet of the blood. From Masruq ibn al-Ajda': I said to 'A'isha, what is lawful to a man of his wife when she is menstruating? She said, everything but intercourse (4242). The note says Marwan al-Asfar is a trustworthy tabi'i; that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti took it from al-Tabari; and that the editor counts it as raised (marfu') in meaning though worded as 'A'isha's own words, since a Companion stating what is lawful and forbidden is trusted to state it only from the one from whom such things are taken, unless indications show he says it by his own judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 378} From Qatada: it was related to us that 'A'isha said, where then were those who kept two beds and two covers? (4243); from Masruq: what is forbidden to a man of his wife when she is menstruating? She said, her vulva (4244); and Masruq rode to 'A'isha and greeted her, and she said, Abu 'A'isha, welcome; he came in and said, I want to ask you something and I am shy; she said, I am your mother and you are my son; he said, what does a man have of his wife while she menstruates? She said, everything but her vulva (4245); from Maymun ibn Mihran, from 'A'isha: he has what is above the izar (4246). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 378} From Nafi', that 'A'isha said about lying with a menstruating woman: no harm in it if she wears an izar (4247); from Abu Ma'shar, from 'A'isha: everything but the vulva (4248), where the note says the chain is defective because Abu Ma'shar Ziyad ibn Kulayb did not meet 'A'isha, and that Ibn Hazm's al-Muhalla gives the right chain through Ibrahim al-Nakha'i from Masruq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 379} From Ibn 'Abbas: if the menstruating woman puts a cloth or something that holds back the harm on her vulva, there is no harm in her husband touching her skin (4249), the note saying the chain is broken because Muhammad ibn Ibrahim's report from Ibn 'Abbas is mursal; from him also: what is above the izar (4250); and from him: guard against blood the size of a sandal's place (4251). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 379} From Umm Salama: no harm in lying with a menstruating woman if there is a rag over her vulva (4252); the note says the chain is sound and that its meaning is also firm from her as a report of the Prophet, in al-Bayhaqi and Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 380} From al-Hasan: a man has of his wife everything but the vulva (4253); from him also: they sleep in one cover if there is a cloth over the vulva (4254); from Mujahid, on a man's play with his wife while she menstruates: what lies between the thighs, buttocks and navel is allowed, so long as it is not the rear or the place of menses (4255); from 'Amir al-Sha'bi, on touching her: if she has held back the harm (4256); from 'Ikrima: everything of the menstruating woman is lawful to you except the passage of blood (4257). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 380} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381}

Al-Tabari gives the ground of those who held this view: proof stands from the many reports of the Prophet that he touched his wives while they menstruated, and had it been obligatory to withdraw from all of them he would not have done so; once that is sound, it is known that what God meant was withdrawing from part of her body and not all, and that this must be intercourse, which is agreed forbidden to the husband in her front passage, as against the other parts of her body about which there is disagreement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381}

Others said, rather, what God commanded withdrawal from is what lies between the navel and the knee, and he may have what is above that and below it. From Shurayh, on the menstruating woman: he has what is above the navel (4258); from Ibn 'Abbas, asked what a menstruating woman's husband has of her: what is above the izar (4259); from Shurayh: he has what is above her navel (4260). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 381}
