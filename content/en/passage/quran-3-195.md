## Summary
Verse 195 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 195 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 195: the answer (pp. 486-488, reports 8367-8369).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: God answered those who prayed, telling them He does not let the work of any worker among them who did good go to waste, man or woman (the editor's note says he chose a form of the sentence different from the printed text, and that the copyist made many slips). It is mentioned that the Prophet was asked why men are mentioned in the emigration and not women, and this verse came down. Mujahid (8367), through Muhammad ibn Bashshar from Mu'ammal from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih: Umm Salama said "O Messenger of God, men are mentioned in the emigration and we are not?" and the verse came down. The editor's note says the chain is sound; Mu'ammal is Ibn Isma'il, reliable (report 2057); Sufyan here is al-Thawri, though Mu'ammal also narrates from Ibn 'Uyayna; al-Tabari narrates the same report from Ibn Humayd from Mu'ammal in the commentary on a verse of al-Ahzab (22: 8, Bulaq; the verse number prints as 25 in the scan) as its occasion; al-Hakim gives it in the Mustadrak (2: 416) through al-Husayn ibn Hafs from Sufyan al-Thawri from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid from Umm Salama, with the verse of al-Ahzab on Muslim men and women ([[passage/quran-33-35|33:35]]) sent down and then this verse, calling it sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs though they did not report it, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Husayn ibn Hafs al-Hamdhani al-Isbahani is reliable (report 2435); Ibn Kathir mentions the other report of al-Tabari in al-Ahzab (6: 533); Ahmad gives it in the Musnad (6: 301, 305) as the occasion of the al-Ahzab verse by two routes from Umm Salama, through 'Abd Allah ibn Rafi', her client, and through 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Shayba al-Makki al-Hajabi; al-Nasa'i reported it in Tafsir (it is in the Sunan al-Kubra) as Ibn Hajar says in Tahdhib al-Tahdhib; al-Tabari gives it at the al-Ahzab place through Yahya ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Hatib from Umm Salama, a reliable and eminent tabi'i; and it is in al-Durr al-Manthur (5: 200). 'Amr ibn Dinar (8368), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn 'Uyayna: I heard a man of Umm Salama's descendants say: Umm Salama said "O Messenger of God, I do not hear God mention women in the emigration at all?" and the verse came down. 'Amr ibn Dinar (8369), through al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman from Asad ibn Musa from Sufyan from a man of Umm Salama's descendants from Umm Salama, likewise. The editor's note says the man is unnamed here but known from another chain; al-Tirmidhi gives him unnamed (4: 88), as does Sa'id ibn Mansur according to Ibn Kathir (2: 326); al-Hakim names him in the Mustadrak (2: 300) through Ya'qub ibn Humayd from Ibn 'Uyayna from 'Amr ibn Dinar from Salama ibn Abi Salama, of Umm Salama's descendants, calling it sound on al-Bukhari's condition, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; Ya'qub ibn Humayd ibn Ka'sib was declared reliable at reports 4779 and 4880 and al-Dhahabi's objection to al-Hakim over his hadith was noted there, so the editor finds his agreement here strange; Salama ibn Abi Salama is Salama ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar ibn Abi Salama, named after his remoter grandfather, and Umm Salama was the mother of his grandfather 'Umar ibn Abi Salama; he has an entry in Tahdhib al-Tahdhib and in al-Bukhari's Kabir (2/2/81) and Ibn Abi Hatim (2/1/166); and al-Suyuti gives the report (2: 112) without naming the tabi'i, adding 'Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Tabarani as sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 488}

**The sense of answering (p. 488).** Al-Tabari says it was said that the verb for "answered" has the sense of "responded", as the poet said (Ka'b ibn Sa'd al-Ghanawi, according to the note, which refers to two earlier places in volume 1) in a line about a caller who called who would respond to generosity, and at that none responded; the line is described here and not reproduced.

**The particle "min" in the verse (p. 489).** Al-Tabari says the particle "min" entered the phrase that mentions male and female as a "substitute" (tarjama) and an explanation of the word "minkum", the sense being: I do not waste the work of a worker among you, whether male or female; and this "min" is not one that may be dropped from the sentence in a negative context, because it came in with a sense without which the sentence does not stand. He mentions that some grammarians of Basra claimed it entered here as it enters in the saying "there has come to you some news", and said it is better here because the negation had entered the verb. Some grammarians of Kufa rejected this, saying that "min" enters and leaves only where there is negation and that negation did not reach this place; they gave as a parallel a sentence in which "nor" cannot be inserted because negation did not follow it, and said that "min" here is explanatory. In the editor's notes, "tarjama" is "substitute", as earlier in other places in the preceding volumes, and he thinks al-Tabari meant by "tafsir" an explanation and not a distinction, referring to the glossary of terms; "explanatory" for al-Tabari means "clarifying"; and for the addition of "min" in negation he refers to earlier places. He notes that the printed edition has the verb of "entering" with a ya', which is an error, and that the manuscript is undotted, so the right reading is the one he has set.

**The believers belong to one another (p. 489).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: you believers, who remember God in all your states, are joined to one another in support, community and religion, and the judgement on all of you in what God does to you is like the judgement on one of you, that He does not waste the work of a male or female among you. The note says the printed edition has the word "question" in place of "community" and that the right word is from the manuscript.

**Those who emigrated and were driven from their homes (pp. 490-491).** Al-Tabari says the emigrants are those who left their people and kin among the disbelievers, for the sake of God, to join their brothers who believed in God and trusted His Messenger; those driven from their homes are the emigrants whom the polytheists of Quraysh expelled from their homes in Mecca; being harmed on the road to God means being harmed for obeying their Lord and worshipping Him with sincere religion, which is the path of God in which the polytheists of Mecca harmed those who believed in the Messenger; and fighting and being killed are in that same path. In the editor's note the printed edition and the manuscript both reverse the order in this place; he set what he judged right, though one of the readings allows what the manuscript has, and he refers to the readings of the verse. Al-Tabari says that the effacing of their evil deeds means wiping them away, showing them grace by His pardon and mercy and forgiving them (the editor says he explained this a little earlier, before p. 482); that the gardens are orchards; that the reward is a recompense for what they did and the trials they endured in God and in His way, and that it comes from God. He then says that God holds every kind of recompense for their deeds, which no description reaches, since it is what no eye has seen, no ear heard and no human heart conceived, and he relates:

**Report 8370 (p. 491).** 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, from his uncle 'Abd Allah ibn Wahb, from 'Amr ibn al-Harith, that Abu 'Uthana al-Ma'afiri told him he heard 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As say: I heard the Messenger of God say that the first company to enter paradise will be the poor among the emigrants, who are the ones hardships are warded off by, who hear and obey when they are ordered, and whose need of the ruler, if one has it, is not met until he dies with it still in his chest; that God will call paradise at the Rising and it will come with its adornment and finery, and He will say: where are my servants who fought in my way and were killed, and were harmed, and strove? Enter paradise; and they enter it without punishment or reckoning; the angels come and prostrate and say: our Lord, we glorify and sanctify You, who are these You have preferred over us? and the Lord says: these are my servants who fought in my way and were harmed; then the angels enter upon them from every gate, and in the report they recite to them the verse of al-Ra'd, [[passage/quran-13-24|13:24]], on peace upon them and the good end of the abode. In the editor's note, Abu 'Uthana al-Ma'afiri is Hayy ibn Yu'min ibn 'Ujayl al-Misri, a reliable Successor whom Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in and others declared reliable, with entries in al-Tahdhib, al-Bukhari's al-Kabir, Ibn Sa'd and Ibn Abi Hatim; the report was transmitted by al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak (2: 71-72) through Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Abd al-Hakam from Ibn Wahb with this chain, who said its chain is sound though the two Shaykhs did not issue it, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Tabarani transmitted it through Ahmad ibn Salih from Ibn Wahb, as Ibn Kathir (4: 519) reports; Ahmad gave it in the Musnad in similar words as no. 6570 through Ma'ruf ibn Suwayd al-Judhami from Abu 'Uthana, and again as no. 6571 through Ibn Lahi'a from Abu 'Uthana; Abu Nu'aym gave it abridged in al-Hilya through Ma'ruf ibn Suwayd (1: 347); and al-Haythami mentions it in Majma' al-Zawa'id (10: 259) from the two Musnad versions, saying of the first that al-Bazzar and al-Tabarani also transmitted it and that their men are reliable, and of the second that the men of al-Tabarani are the men of the Sahih except Abu 'Uthana, who is reliable. The note continues on p. 492: al-Suyuti mentions it (2: 112) and attributes it to Ibn Jarir, Abu al-Shaykh, al-Tabarani, al-Hakim, who declared it sound, and al-Bayhaqi in al-Shu'ab, and again (4: 57-58) to Ahmad, al-Bazzar, Ibn Jarir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Hibban, Abu al-Shaykh, al-Hakim, who declared it sound, Ibn Mardawayh, Abu Nu'aym in al-Hilya and al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman; and Ibn Kathir did not mention it at this place but under the sura of al-Ra'd, as the editor indicated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 491} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 492}

**The readers' difference on fighting and being killed (p. 492).** Al-Tabari says the readers differ in the two verbs. Some read both lightly, meaning that they killed those they killed of the polytheists and were killed. Others read the second with doubling, meaning that they fought the polytheists and the polytheists killed them one after another, killing after killing. Most readers of Medina and some of Kufa read it lightly with the sense that they fought the polytheists and killed (the sentence is incomplete in the scan). Most readers of Kufa read it with killing first and fighting after, meaning that some of them were killed and those who remained fought. Al-Tabari then says that the reading he does not allow anyone to go beyond is one of two: fighting then being killed, both light, or being killed then fighting, both light, because these are the readings transmitted by inheritance and any other is irregular in his view; and whichever of the two a reader recites, he is right, because each is widespread among the readers of Islam and their meanings agree. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 492}
