## Summary
Verse 195 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 195 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 195: the answer (pp. 486-488, reports 8367-8369).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: God answered those who prayed, telling them He does not let the work of any worker among them who did good go to waste, man or woman (the editor's note says he chose a form of the sentence different from the printed text, and that the copyist made many slips). It is mentioned that the Prophet was asked why men are mentioned in the emigration and not women, and this verse came down. Mujahid (8367), through Muhammad ibn Bashshar from Mu'ammal from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih: Umm Salama said "O Messenger of God, men are mentioned in the emigration and we are not?" and the verse came down. The editor's note says the chain is sound; Mu'ammal is Ibn Isma'il, reliable (report 2057); Sufyan here is al-Thawri, though Mu'ammal also narrates from Ibn 'Uyayna; al-Tabari narrates the same report from Ibn Humayd from Mu'ammal in the commentary on a verse of al-Ahzab (22: 8, Bulaq; the verse number prints as 25 in the scan) as its occasion; al-Hakim gives it in the Mustadrak (2: 416) through al-Husayn ibn Hafs from Sufyan al-Thawri from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid from Umm Salama, with the verse of al-Ahzab on Muslim men and women ([[passage/quran-33-35|33:35]]) sent down and then this verse, calling it sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs though they did not report it, and al-Dhahabi agreed; al-Husayn ibn Hafs al-Hamdhani al-Isbahani is reliable (report 2435); Ibn Kathir mentions the other report of al-Tabari in al-Ahzab (6: 533); Ahmad gives it in the Musnad (6: 301, 305) as the occasion of the al-Ahzab verse by two routes from Umm Salama, through 'Abd Allah ibn Rafi', her client, and through 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Shayba al-Makki al-Hajabi; al-Nasa'i reported it in Tafsir (it is in the Sunan al-Kubra) as Ibn Hajar says in Tahdhib al-Tahdhib; al-Tabari gives it at the al-Ahzab place through Yahya ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Hatib from Umm Salama, a reliable and eminent tabi'i; and it is in al-Durr al-Manthur (5: 200). 'Amr ibn Dinar (8368), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn 'Uyayna: I heard a man of Umm Salama's descendants say: Umm Salama said "O Messenger of God, I do not hear God mention women in the emigration at all?" and the verse came down. 'Amr ibn Dinar (8369), through al-Rabi' ibn Sulayman from Asad ibn Musa from Sufyan from a man of Umm Salama's descendants from Umm Salama, likewise. The editor's note says the man is unnamed here but known from another chain; al-Tirmidhi gives him unnamed (4: 88), as does Sa'id ibn Mansur according to Ibn Kathir (2: 326); al-Hakim names him in the Mustadrak (2: 300) through Ya'qub ibn Humayd from Ibn 'Uyayna from 'Amr ibn Dinar from Salama ibn Abi Salama, of Umm Salama's descendants, calling it sound on al-Bukhari's condition, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; Ya'qub ibn Humayd ibn Ka'sib was declared reliable at reports 4779 and 4880 and al-Dhahabi's objection to al-Hakim over his hadith was noted there, so the editor finds his agreement here strange; Salama ibn Abi Salama is Salama ibn 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar ibn Abi Salama, named after his remoter grandfather, and Umm Salama was the mother of his grandfather 'Umar ibn Abi Salama; he has an entry in Tahdhib al-Tahdhib and in al-Bukhari's Kabir (2/2/81) and Ibn Abi Hatim (2/1/166); and al-Suyuti gives the report (2: 112) without naming the tabi'i, adding 'Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Tabarani as sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 488}

**The sense of answering (p. 488).** Al-Tabari says it was said that the verb for "answered" has the sense of "responded", as the poet said (Ka'b ibn Sa'd al-Ghanawi, according to the note, which refers to two earlier places in volume 1) in a line about a caller who called who would respond to generosity, and at that none responded; the line is described here and not reproduced. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
