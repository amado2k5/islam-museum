## Summary
Verse 12 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 12 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "He said: what prevented you from prostrating when I commanded you? He said: I am better than him; You created me from fire and him from clay", this is a report from God of what He said to Iblis when he disobeyed and did not prostrate to Adam when commanded to; God said to Iblis, what prevented you from leaving prostration to Adam when I commanded you to prostrate? Iblis said, I am better than Adam, You created me from fire and him from clay. If someone asks, tell us about Iblis, was the blame laid on him for the prostration or for leaving it? If for leaving it, how was he told "what prevented you from not prostrating when I commanded you"? And if the denunciation were of prostrating, that would contradict what the revelation says elsewhere in the Qur'an and what Muslims know, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 323}

the answer is that the blame fell on Iblis only for disobeying his Lord by leaving prostration to Adam when commanded to prostrate to him. Yet there is disagreement among the knowers of Arab speech over the sense of "what prevented you from not prostrating"; al-Tabari begins with what they said and then gives the soundest. Some Basran grammarians said it means "what prevented you from prostrating", with "la" superfluous here, as the poet used it in a line saying his generosity refused "la" avarice, making "la" a filler with which they joined the speech. Yunus claimed that Abu Amr used to put "avarice" in the genitive and make "la" annexed to it, meaning his generosity refused the word "no" that belongs to avarice. The note records that the line is transmitted differently in the sources. Some Kufan grammarians said the like, but claimed that the reason "la" enters is that there is a negation at the start of the speech, meaning "he was not among those who prostrated", for the Arabs sometimes repeat the negation in speech that contains a negation, as confirmation and emphasis, as in a line they cite, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 324}

in which they joined "ma" and "in", both negations, for emphasis; the note says it is in al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and that "fawalij" is the plural of "falij", the two-humped camel brought from Sind, and "fuyul" the plural of "fil", elephant. Another of them said "la" is neither filler nor addition, but "prevention" here has the sense of "saying", the meaning being: who said to you, do not prostrate, when I commanded you to prostrate; "an" enters because prevention is in sense a saying though not in wording, as is done in other speech close to saying but different in wording, like "I called out that you not stand" and "I swore that you not sit"; and he said that whoever puts "avarice" in the genitive means the word of avarice, because "la" is the word of avarice. And some said the sense of "prevention" is putting a barrier between a person and what he wants, the prevented being forced by it to the opposite of what he was prevented from; so when Iblis was addressed with prevention, the meaning was: what forced you not to prostrate? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 325}

Al-Tabari says the right view to him is that there is an omission in the speech, which the evident wording makes sufficient, the meaning being: what prevented you from prostrating so that it compelled you not to prostrate; "compelled you" is left out as the hearers know from His words "except Iblis, who was not among the prostrating" that this is the sense, and "what prevented you" then works on "an" as "compelled you" would have if it had appeared. He says this is soundest because it was shown earlier that nothing in God's Book may be without meaning and every word has a sound sense, which shows the falsity of the claim that "la" is a meaningless filler. As for the claim that "prevention" here means "saying", so that "la" enters with "an": although prevention can be speech and action, people do not commonly use "prevention" for a command to leave something, because one commanded to leave an act who is able both to do and leave it, and does it, is not said to have done it while prevented from it except by straining the speech; prevention from an act is a barrier between him and it, and he cannot be a doer of it while barred, else he would be both barred and not barred, prevented and not prevented, a contradiction as the note shows. Moreover, Iblis did not obey God's command to prostrate to Adam because of arrogance, so how would he obey another in leaving God's command and obedience by not prostrating, so that it could be said to him, what did someone say to you, do not prostrate to Adam, when I commanded you to prostrate to him? Rather the meaning is as he said: what prevented you from prostrating to him so that it compelled you, or drove you, or forced you not to prostrate to him. As for "I am better than him", that is God's report of Iblis's answer when asked what prevented him from prostrating to Adam, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 326}

...so that it compelled him not to prostrate to him and forced him to oppose His command and leave obedience: the preventer from prostration, and what called him to go against his Lord's command in that, was that he is stronger and mightier and more excellent by the excellence of the genus from which he was created, fire, over that from which Adam was created, clay. The enemy of God was ignorant of the way of the truth and erred in the path of right, since it is known that the essence of fire is lightness, rashness, agitation and rising on high, and what is in its essence of this is what moved the wretch, after the misery decreed for him by God in the Previous Book, to be arrogant about prostrating to Adam and to belittle his Lord's command, so it brought him ruin and perdition; and it was known that the essence of clay is gravity, forbearance, mildness, modesty and steadiness, and that in its essence was what called Adam, after the felicity decreed for him by his Lord in the Previous Book, to repent of his error and ask his Lord for pardon and forgiveness. That is why al-Hasan and Ibn Sirin used to say, the first to reason by analogy was Iblis, meaning wrong analogy, which is this error of his statement and his distance from hitting the truth about the excellence with which God distinguished Adam over the rest of His creation: creating him with His hand, breathing into him of His spirit, making the angels prostrate to him, teaching him the names of everything, besides the other honours He gave him. The ignorant one turned away from all that and set out to argue that he was created from fire and Adam from clay, and in that too he is no match even had Adam no honour from God besides; how much more when the honours with which he was distinguished are too many to count and tiresome to enumerate. The notes record differences between the printed text and the manuscript in places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 327}

He gives (14355) from Ibn Sirin that the first to reason by analogy was Iblis and the sun and moon were worshipped only through analogies, and the note says Amr ibn Malik al-Rasibi in the chain is of unaccepted hadith and steals hadith; and (14356) from al-Hasan on "You created me from fire and him from clay" that Iblis made an analogy and was the first to do so. The commentators said the like: he gives (14357) from Ibn Abbas that when God created Adam He said to the angels who were with Iblis in particular, not the angels in the heavens, prostrate to Adam, and they all prostrated except Iblis, who was arrogant, as he had told himself in his pride and delusion, I will not prostrate to him, I am better than him, older and stronger in creation, You created me from fire and him from clay, saying that fire is stronger than clay; and (14358) from Mujahid, on "You created me from fire", that He then made his offspring from water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 328}

Al-Tabari says that what the enemy of God said is not an answer to what he was asked, because God said to him, what prevented you from prostrating? and he did not answer that what prevented him from prostrating was that he was created from fire and Adam from clay, but began a statement about himself that points to where the answer lies, saying, I am better than him, You created me from fire and him from clay. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 329}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
