## Summary
Verse 268 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 268 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-268|2:268]]), pp. 571 to 572. Al-Tabari explains that the devil, on account of charity and paying the obligatory zakat from their wealth, promises people poverty and commands them to disobey God and leave obedience, while God promises to cover for them what they did by overlooking punishment for it and forgive them through charity, and promises to replace what they gave in charity, favouring them with His gifts and widening their provision; this is al-Tabari's explanation, and the editor in a note explains the interposed clause in the syntax and refers to earlier commentary on the words for indecency, forgiveness and bounty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 571} In 6168, Ibn Abbas: two from God and two from the devil; the devil promises poverty, meaning do not spend your money, hold it back, for you will need it, and commands indecency; God promises forgiveness for these sins and bounty in provision. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 571} 6169, Qatada: forgiveness for your indecencies and bounty for your poverty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 571}

In 6170, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, from the Prophet: the devil has a touch on the son of Adam and the angel has a touch; the devil's touch is a threat of evil and denial of the truth, the angel's touch is a promise of good and affirming the truth; whoever finds the latter should know it is from God and praise God, and whoever finds the other should seek refuge in God from the devil, and then he recited the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 571} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 572} The editor dwells on its chain: Abu al-Ahwas is Sallam ibn Sulaym; Ata ibn al-Sa'ib changed at the end of his life, and one who heard from him early has a sound report, and it appears Abu al-Ahwas heard him before the change; Murra is al-Tayyib ibn Sharahil al-Hamdani; he says al-Tirmidhi relates it from Hannad by this chain and calls it good and strange, saying he knows it as raised only from Abu al-Ahwas's report, that Ibn Kathir, Ibn Hibban, al-Nasa'i and al-Suyuti cite it, and that later stopped reports (6171 to 6176) give the like from Ibn Mas'ud; he holds that being also reported as stopped is no real flaw, since raising is an addition from a reliable narrator and such a thing cannot be known by opinion, so it is raised in effect. These gradings and sources are the editor's alone, and al-Tabari here reaches no preference between the reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 572} We stop at 6170. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the sources and gradings are the editor's, with no museum verdict.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-268|2:268]]), pp. 573 to 575, containing the reports stopped at Ibn Mas'ud on the two touches. In 6171, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud: a person has a touch from the angel and a touch from the devil; the angel's is a promise of good and affirming the truth, the devil's a threat of evil and denial of the truth; and he recited the verse; Amr ibn Qays al-Mula'i adds that they heard in this report that it used to be said that if one feels the angel's touch let him praise God and ask for His bounty, and if one feels the devil's let him seek forgiveness and refuge in God from the devil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 573} The editor gives the biographies of al-Hakam ibn Bashir and Amr ibn Qays, corrects the grandfather's name in the print, holds the report to be stopped in wording but raised in effect, and calls Amr's closing remark an interrupted report in this chain though sound by other chains; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 573}

In 6172, from Ibn Mas'ud by Ata ibn al-Sa'ib from Abu al-Ahwas or from Murra, to the like effect, adding that if you find some of the first, praise God for it, and if some of the other, seek refuge with God from the devil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 573} The editor says this Abu al-Ahwas is Awf ibn Malik ibn Nadla, a reliable Follower whom Ibn Ma'in and others declared reliable; that Ata's hesitation between Abu al-Ahwas and Murra does not matter since it passes from one reliable man to another; that it may be one of Ata's slips since Ibn Ulayya, a Basran, heard from him after his change; and that Ibn Kathir gives Mis'ar's report from Ata from Abu al-Ahwas as stopped; these assessments are the editor's alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 573} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 574}

6173, al-Zuhri from Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah ibn Utba from Ibn Mas'ud, to the same effect, which the editor takes as another sound chain supporting Ata's report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 574} 6174, from Murra al-Hamdani that Ibn Mas'ud said the like and recited the verse; the editor says its chain is sound because Hammad ibn Salama heard from Ata before his change, and corrects the form of a word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 574} 6175, from Amir ibn Abda from Ibn Mas'ud, similarly; the editor gives the biographies of al-Musayyab ibn Rafi, Amir ibn Abda and Fitr and counts it a third sound chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 574} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 575} 6176, from Murra ibn Sharahil from Ibn Mas'ud, as before, reciting the verse; the editor calls the chain good because Jarir heard from Ata after his change, but raised to soundness by the earlier supporting chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 575} All these rulings and sources are the editor's, not al-Tabari's or the museum's.

Al-Tabari then explains the close of the verse: God is vast in the bounty He has promised to give them and in His treasuries, and knowing of their spending and charity, counting it for them until He repays them on arriving before Him in the next life; this is his explanation, and the editor refers to earlier commentary on the phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 575} Here ([[passage/quran-2-268|2:268]]) ends in what was read. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the sources and gradings are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
