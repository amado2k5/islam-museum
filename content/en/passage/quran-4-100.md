## Summary
Verse 100 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 100 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 100: the meaning of emigrating, of "muragham" and of room (pp. 112-113, notes 1-4 on p. 112 and 1-5 on p. 113).** The commentary on verse 100 of sura 4 begins on p. 112 (the page headers run the verses 97-100 together; verses 97-99 are treated in the file for [[passage/quran-4-97|4:97]], with [[passage/quran-4-98|4:98]] and [[passage/quran-4-99|4:99]]). Al-Tabari says that to emigrate in God's way means to leave the land of polytheism and its people, fleeing with one's religion to the land of Islam and its believing people; that "God's way" is the course of His religion and the path He laid down for His creatures, which is the upright religion; and that the muragham is the place of movement in the lands and the place to go, from the verb "raghama" used of a man and his people. He cites a line of al-Nabigha al-Ja'di describing a secure refuge like a great mountain whose flanks are sought for shelter; the editor traces it to the poet's diwan, Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an and the Lisan, says he could not find its place in the poem, and does not know what the comparing particle in it attaches to (the line is described, not reproduced). On "room", al-Tabari says it can mean ease in the matter of their religion in Mecca, that is, the removal of the polytheists' earlier prevention of them from showing their religion and worshipping their Lord openly. The editor says (p. 113, note 1) that the sentence as printed and in the manuscript is not well formed, that he thinks the copyist dropped some of al-Tabari's words, and he proposes a reconstruction adding the possibility of room in livelihood to room in religion; in note 2 he says the first publisher deleted the word "kana" in the sentence thinking it superfluous, but it is sound Arabic marking the past. Al-Tabari then states the ruling of the one who sets out as an emigrant and death overtakes him before he reaches the land of emigration: his reward is incumbent on God, meaning the reward of his deed and the recompense of his emigration and of leaving his homeland and clan, and he glosses the Forgiving as the one who covers the sins of His believing servants by pardoning them their punishment and the Merciful as gentle toward them. The notes explain "ikhtaramathu al-maniyya" (death took him from among his companions) and refer to earlier volumes for the explanation of reward and of kana, ghafur and rahim.
**Occasion of revelation: reports 10282-10284 (pp. 113-114, notes 1-2 on p. 114).** Al-Tabari says it was reported that this verse came down because of a Muslim living in Mecca who set out when he heard that God had sent down the two preceding verses, and died on the road before reaching Medina. Report 10282 (Yaqub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): a man of Khuza'a called Damra ibn al-Ays (or al-Ays ibn Damra ibn al-Zinba'), who was ill when the order to emigrate came, told his family to lay out his bed and carry him to the Prophet; they did so and death reached him at al-Tan'im, and the verse came down. Report 10283 (Muhammad ibn Bashshar, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says the same of Damra ibn al-Ays ibn al-Zinba' (or someone son of Damra), who died on reaching al-Tan'im. Report 10284 (al-Muthanna, from Amr ibn Awn, from Hushaym, from al-Awwam al-Taymi) is like Yaqub's report, adding that he was of Khuza'a. The editor says al-Bayhaqi gave the first in his Sunan; that this is the story of one man whose name and father's name are disputed in more than ten forms, as Ibn Hajar says in al-Isaba; that al-Tabari gives most of these forms in reports 10282-10295, so the editor does not point out the disagreement in each place; that al-Tan'im is a place outside the sacred territory between Mecca and Sarif from which people of Mecca enter the state of sanctity for the lesser pilgrimage; and that he found no entry for "al-Awwam al-Taymi" in the biographical works and fears the right reading is "al-Awwam, from al-Taymi", meaning al-Awwam ibn Hawshab from Ibrahim al-Taymi.
**Reports 10285-10287 (p. 115, notes 1-3).** Report 10285 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): when these verses came down a believer in Mecca called Damra, who was ill, said that he had enough money to take him to Medina and beyond and that he knew the way, and asked to be taken out; when he crossed the sacred territory God took him, and the verse came down. Report 10286 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada): an ill Muslim said he had no excuse, being a guide on the road and well off, and asked to be carried; he was carried, death overtook him on the road, and the verse came down about him. Report 10287 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Uyayna, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Ikrima): a man of Banu Damra, ill, asked to be taken out toward "al-rawh"; they took him out, and when he reached al-Hashas (the report finishes on p. 116) he died and the verse came down. The editor says "a guide on the road" means one who knows it; that he keeps the manuscript reading "came down" where the first printing has "came down about him" in 10286; that "al-rawh" means ease, relief and the coolness of the breeze; and that al-Hashas is a place in the Hijaz which Yaqut calls a mountain overlooking Dhu Tuwa near Mecca, with two lines by a Hijazi poet longing for the gazelles of that place and for a wounded liver (described, not reproduced).
**Reports 10288-10290 (pp. 116-117, notes 1-5 on p. 116 and note 1 on p. 117).** Report 10288 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from al-Mundhir ibn Tha'laba, from Alba' ibn Ahmar al-Yashkuri): it came down about a man of Khuza'a (the editor says al-Mundhir ibn Tha'laba is printed as "Tha'laba ibn al-Mundhir ibn Harb al-Ta'i" and refers to number 7190 for Alba'). Report 10289 (Muhammad ibn Bashshar, from Abu Amir, from Qurra, from al-Dahhak): when a man of Mecca heard that the angels had struck the faces and backs of Banu Kinana, he told his family to take him out, being close to death; he was carried to a mountain track that al-Dahhak named, died there, and the verse came down. The editor says this report is missing from the manuscript, and explains "adnafa" (the sick man grew heavy and near death) and "aqaba" (a rough track in the mountain). Report 10290 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): when Damra ibn Jundub al-Damri heard what had come down he told his family, being in pain, to saddle his mount because the two Akhshabs (the two mountains of Mecca) were suffocating him, hoping to go out and find relief; he mounted toward Medina and died on the way, and the verse came down. As he turned toward Medina he said that he was an emigrant to God and His Messenger. The editor says the first publisher altered the manuscript's wording, which does no harm though the manuscript's is also correct.
**Reports 10291-10292 (pp. 117-118, notes 1-3 on p. 117).** Report 10291 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ikrima): when the verse on those the angels take came down, Jundub ibn Damra al-Jundu'i said that God had left no excuse or plea for him; he set out as a very old man and died part of the way, and the companions of the Prophet said he had died before emigrating and they did not know whether he was in a state of allegiance or not, and the verse came down. Report 10292 (al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman, from al-Dahhak): when the verse came down about those killed beside the polytheists of Quraysh at Badr, a man of Banu Layth, on the religion of the Prophet and living in Mecca, one of those God excused, an old man and chronically ill, heard of it and told his family he would not spend that night in Mecca; he was taken out and when he reached al-Tan'im on the Medina road death came, and the verse came down about him. The editor says the first printing has a word with no acceptable sense where the manuscript has "wasiyyan", a corruption of what he prints, "wasib" being a man whom illness has long seized; that the first printing adds "ill" and the manuscript has the passive verb without it, which the Durr al-Manthur supports.
**Reports 10293-10294 and the start of 10295 (p. 118, notes 1-2).** Report 10293 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): a man of Banu Kinana emigrated to reach the Prophet and died on the road; his people mocked him, saying he had neither reached what he wanted nor stayed among his family to be tended and buried, and the Qur'an came down about him. Report 10294 (Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Ramadi, from Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, from Sharik, from Amr ibn Dinar, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas): there was in Mecca a sick man called Damra of Banu Bakr who told his family to take him out of Mecca because of the heat, and when they asked where, he pointed toward Medina, and the verse came down. Report 10295 (al-Harith ibn Abi Usama, from Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban, from Qays, from Salim al-Aftas, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): when the verse on the unequal standing of those who sit and those who strive came down, some Muslims in Mecca who were harmed took it as a concession, until the verse on the superiority of the strivers over the sitters came; they said God had made the superiority clear and conceded the harmed; then the verse on those the angels take came, and they said this was binding; then the exception of the weak came, and Damra ibn al-Ays said... (the report is cut at the foot of the page and continues on p. 119). The notes say the first printing has a different word order and the phrase "of those who were in Mecca" where the editor keeps the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 113}

**The rest of report 10295 and its notes (p. 119, notes 1-2).** The rest of report 10295 (Sa'id ibn Jubayr): Damra ibn al-Ays al-Zuraqi, of Banu Layth, who had lost his sight, said that he had means, money and slaves, and asked to be carried. He set out ill, death reached him at al-Tan'im and he was buried by the mosque of al-Tan'im, and the verse came down about him. The editor says al-Harith ibn Abi Usama is named after his grandfather (al-Harith ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Usama al-Tamimi), born in Shawwal 186 and died on the day of Arafa in 282 at ninety-six, reliable, with an entry in the Tarikh Baghdad, and al-Tabari narrates from him in the tafsir and the history; that Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban al-Umawi, of the line of Sa'id ibn al-As, is reported to have been called by Ibn Ma'in a vile liar who fabricates hadith; and that Qays is Qays ibn al-Rabi'. He adds that readers have seen how the reports differ over the name of the man who set out, that he stopped pointing it out because verifying his name and his father's name is almost impossible, and, in note 2, that the manuscript has a different wording from the printed text, where the printed one is right and the manuscript's is a copyist's slip.
**The disagreement over "muragham": reports 10296-10304 (pp. 119-121, notes 1-2 on p. 121).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the muragham. Some said it is moving from land to land: report 10296 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas), 10297 (al-Dahhak: a place of moving to), 10298 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi': a place of moving to), and 10299 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abu Sufyan, from Ma'mar, from al-Hasan or Qatada: a place of moving to). Mujahid, in reports 10300, 10301 and 10302, is reported to have said it is latitude, distance and removal from what the emigrant dislikes. Others said it is a means of livelihood: report 10303 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi). Others said the muragham is the muhajar, the place of emigration: report 10304 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd). Al-Tabari says (p. 121) that he has already shown the soundest of these views earlier; the editor points to pp. 112-113.
**The disagreement over "room": reports 10305-10308 and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 121-122, note 2 on p. 121).** Al-Tabari says they also differed over the room mentioned in the verse. Some said it is room in provision: report 10305 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas), 10306 (al-Rabi') and 10307 (al-Dahhak). Others said otherwise: report 10308 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says it is from misguidance to guidance and from poverty ("ayla) to wealth. Al-Tabari then gives his choice (p. 122): God has reported that whoever emigrates in His way will find in the earth a place of movement and room, and room includes ease in provision and wealth after poverty, and ease from the distress of worry and affliction in which the believers lived among the polytheists of Mecca, and the other senses of relief from what God disliked for the believers in staying under polytheist rule. He says God gave no evidence that He meant only some of these senses, so all are included, among them the narrowing of the chest at being unable to show faith and keep pure the affirmation of God's oneness and to part from rivals and gods.
**The reading of the verse for the fighter: report 10309 (p. 122, note 1).** Al-Tabari says some scholars read the verse as applying to the fighter who sets out to fight and is overtaken by death after leaving his home, so that he has his share of the spoils although he did not attend the battle. Report 10309 (al-Muthanna, from Yusuf ibn Adi, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Ibn Lahi'a, from Yazid ibn Abi Habib): the people of Medina say that whoever sets out marching has his share due, and they read the verse so. The editor says Yusuf ibn Adi ibn Zuraiq al-Taymi was a Kufan who settled in Egypt, died there in 232 and is reliable, and that Yazid ibn Abi Habib al-Misri has come earlier. This view is given as transmitted and the museum gives no verdict on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 119}
