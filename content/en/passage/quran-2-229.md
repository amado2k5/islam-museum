## Summary
Verse 229 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 229 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The interpreters differed over the interpretation of ([[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]). Some said it points to the number of divorces within which a man may take back his wife, and the number by which his wife becomes irrevocably separate from him. They said the verse was revealed because, in the Jahiliyya and in Islam before its revelation, there was no limit to divorce at which the wife became separate from her husband so long as he took her back during her waiting period; so God set a limit, at the reaching of which the divorced wife becomes forbidden to the man unless after another husband, and she becomes more in control of herself than he. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 538} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 539} At this point the editor records that the old division in the copy from which his old manuscript was taken ends here, followed by a certificate of audition dated Sha'ban 408 AH, given before al-Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Khasibi, from Abu Muhammad al-Farghani, from Abu Ja'far al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 539}

From 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, a man used to divorce as often as he liked, and if he took his wife back before her waiting period ended she remained his wife; a man of the Ansar became angry with his wife and said, I will not approach you and you will not be free of me; she asked how, and he said, I will divorce you and when your term nears I will take you back, then divorce you again; she complained to the Prophet and the verse was revealed (4779). In another narration from Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father, a man in the time of the Prophet told his wife he would neither shelter her nor leave her free; she went to the Prophet and the verse was revealed, and people began afresh, both those who had divorced and those who had not (4780). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 539} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 540} The editor notes that both are mursal, since 'Urwa was a Follower, and traces them to the Muwatta', the Musnad of al-Shafi'i, al-Bayhaqi's Sunan and Ibn Abi Hatim's commentary; he adds that al-Tirmidhi, al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi narrated it connected through 'A'isha, via Ya'la ibn Shubayb from Hisham from his father. He holds Ya'la reliable and the raising of the chain an acceptable addition from a reliable narrator; al-Hakim called the chain sound and al-Dhahabi objected by weakening Ya'qub ibn Humayd, which the editor finds surprising because al-Tirmidhi has Qutayba's narration from Ya'la. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 540} From Qatada, in the Jahiliyya a man would divorce three, ten or more times and take her back while she was in her waiting period, and God made the limit of divorce three (4781, 4782). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 541} From Ibn Zayd, divorce had no limit before then; a man could divorce his wife a hundred times and take her back before she became free, starting a new divorce so as to harm her, until God, knowing this of him, made divorce three, two times and then keeping in what is right or release with kindness (4783). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 541}
