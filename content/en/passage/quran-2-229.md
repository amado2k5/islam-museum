## Summary
Verse 229 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 229 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The interpreters differed over the interpretation of ([[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]). Some said it points to the number of divorces within which a man may take back his wife, and the number by which his wife becomes irrevocably separate from him. They said the verse was revealed because, in the Jahiliyya and in Islam before its revelation, there was no limit to divorce at which the wife became separate from her husband so long as he took her back during her waiting period; so God set a limit, at the reaching of which the divorced wife becomes forbidden to the man unless after another husband, and she becomes more in control of herself than he. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 538} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 539} At this point the editor records that the old division in the copy from which his old manuscript was taken ends here, followed by a certificate of audition dated Sha'ban 408 AH, given before al-Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Khasibi, from Abu Muhammad al-Farghani, from Abu Ja'far al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 539}

From 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, a man used to divorce as often as he liked, and if he took his wife back before her waiting period ended she remained his wife; a man of the Ansar became angry with his wife and said, I will not approach you and you will not be free of me; she asked how, and he said, I will divorce you and when your term nears I will take you back, then divorce you again; she complained to the Prophet and the verse was revealed (4779). In another narration from Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father, a man in the time of the Prophet told his wife he would neither shelter her nor leave her free; she went to the Prophet and the verse was revealed, and people began afresh, both those who had divorced and those who had not (4780). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 539} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 540} The editor notes that both are mursal, since 'Urwa was a Follower, and traces them to the Muwatta', the Musnad of al-Shafi'i, al-Bayhaqi's Sunan and Ibn Abi Hatim's commentary; he adds that al-Tirmidhi, al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi narrated it connected through 'A'isha, via Ya'la ibn Shubayb from Hisham from his father. He holds Ya'la reliable and the raising of the chain an acceptable addition from a reliable narrator; al-Hakim called the chain sound and al-Dhahabi objected by weakening Ya'qub ibn Humayd, which the editor finds surprising because al-Tirmidhi has Qutayba's narration from Ya'la. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 540} From Qatada, in the Jahiliyya a man would divorce three, ten or more times and take her back while she was in her waiting period, and God made the limit of divorce three (4781, 4782). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 541} From Ibn Zayd, divorce had no limit before then; a man could divorce his wife a hundred times and take her back before she became free, starting a new divorce so as to harm her, until God, knowing this of him, made divorce three, with two divorces and then, as the verse goes on, keeping her or releasing her (4783). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 541}

From al-Suddi, the "twice" means the term in which the husband may take her back (4784); from 'Ikrima, when he has divorced her twice, if he wants to take her back he has the right, and if he wishes he divorces her again, and she is then not lawful to him until she marries another husband (4785). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 541} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 542} Al-Tabari says the interpretation of the verse on this report is that the number of divorces within which husbands may take their wives back, when consummated, is two, and what is then required of the one who takes her back after the two is to keep her in what is right or release her with kindness, since he has no right of return after the third. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 542}

Others said the verse was revealed to teach God's servants the sunna of divorcing their wives when they want to, and not as an indication of the number by which a woman becomes separate. From Ibn Mas'ud: he divorces her after she is pure and before intercourse, then leaves her until she is pure again, then divorces her if he wishes; if he wants to take her back he does, then if he wishes he divorces her, or otherwise leaves her until three menses are complete and she is separate (4786). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 542} From Ibn 'Abbas: when a man divorces his wife twice he should fear God over the third, either keeping her with good companionship or releasing her with kindness without wronging any of her right (4787). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 542} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 543} From Mujahid: he divorces her while pure without intercourse; when she menstruates and is pure the qur' is complete; then he divorces the second time as the first, so these are two divorces and two quru'; then in the third he either keeps or releases (4788, 4789). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 543} The editor gives the sources of Ibn Mas'ud's report in al-Nasa'i, al-Bayhaqi and Ibn Majah and explains an idiom about dressing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 542} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 543} The interpretation on these views, for al-Tabari, is that the sunna of divorce is two divorces, one in each period of purity, and then what is required is keeping or releasing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 543}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice: closest to the apparent wording is what 'Urwa and Qatada said, and those who said the like, that the verse indicates the number of divorces by which a woman becomes forbidden and the right of return ends, and the number within which the right exists. His reason is that God says in the next verse ([[passage/quran-2-230|2:230]]) what tells His servants the degree by which a woman becomes forbidden to her husband except after another husband, and it does not say when divorce is permitted and when not, which would direct the interpretation to what is reported from Ibn Mas'ud and Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 544}

They also differed over what keeping and releasing mean. Some said it points to what is required of husbands after taking back wives from the second divorce: living with them in what is right or parting by divorce. From 'Ata', at the third, either he keeps in what is right or releases with kindness; from Mujahid, a man has more right to his wife within two divorces than anyone else, but once he pronounces the third she has nothing to do with him and waits for another (4790). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 544} From Abu Razin, a man came to the Prophet and asked about the third divorce after the mention of two; the Prophet said the keeping or releasing is the third (4791, 4792, 4793). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 545} The editor says the three narrations are one hadith with three chains, mursal and weak. He accepts Isma'il ibn Sumay' and Abu Razin al-Asadi as reliable, points out Ibn Kathir's slip in attributing it to the Musnad, and then objects to al-Tabari's reliance on a mursal report; he finds the meaning of the report false, since the third divorce is mentioned in the next verse and otherwise would be a fourth, which is his own view, set out in his book on the system of divorce in Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 545} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 546} From Mujahid, the release is in the third (4794); from Qatada, divorce had no time limit until the verse was revealed, and in the third it is keeping or release (4795). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 546}

Others said that what is meant is what is required of them after the second divorce: taking back in what is right, or release by leaving off taking them back until the waiting period ends and the women have more control of themselves; they denied the first group's view that it points to the third divorce. From al-Suddi (4796); from al-Dahhak, release is to leave her until her waiting period passes (4797), and that two divorces with a taking back between are meant, so he is told to keep or release, and if he divorces a third time she is not lawful to him until she marries another husband (4798). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 546} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 547}

Al-Tabari says those who held this seem to mean that divorce is twice, with keeping or releasing in each. This is a position the apparent wording allows, but for the report from the Prophet narrated by Isma'il ibn Sumay' from Abu Razin, since following the report from the Messenger of God is better for us than anything else. His interpretation of the verse is therefore that the divorce in which husbands may take back their wives is two, and then, once they have taken them back after the second, they either keep them in what is right or release them with kindness by the third divorce, so that she becomes separate from them and their right of return is cancelled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 547}

Al-Tabari then asks what that keeping in what is right is. He cites al-Dahhak, what is right is that he treats her with good companionship (4799), and Ibn 'Abbas, that he fear God over the third and either keep her with good companionship (4800). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 547} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 548} And what is release with kindness? Ibn 'Abbas: he releases her without wronging her right at all (4801), and also that it is the solemn covenant (4802); al-Suddi: kindness is that he give her due, neither hurting nor insulting her (4803); al-Dahhak: that he leaves her until her waiting period passes and gives her the dower, if any is owed, with the consolation gift according to his means (4804); 'Ata' al-Khurasani from Ibn 'Abbas, the solemn covenant is the keeping or the releasing (4805). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 548} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 549} Al-Tabari says the subject of keeping and releasing is omitted, the sense of the wording being enough to indicate it, so that the obligatory course then is keeping or releasing; he explained the like in his commentary on the verse on retaliation in this surah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 549}

On the next part of the verse ([[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]), God forbids men to take from their wives, when they intend to divorce them, anything of the dower they gave them; the duty is release with kindness, that is paying them their dues of dower, consolation gift and the like, unless the two fear they cannot keep God's limits. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 549} Al-Tabari then gives the variant readings. Most people of the Hijaz and Basra read the verb of fearing in the active, so that the man and the woman fear. It is reported that in Ubayy ibn Ka'b's reading the verb for thinking stands in place of fearing; Maymun ibn Mihran reported that in Ubayy's old copy the ransom was a single divorce, and they found an old copy of Ubayy's that had that wording (4806). The Arabs sometimes put thinking in the place of fearing and the reverse, because the meanings are close, and he cites a line by Abu al-Ghul al-Tuhawi, on whom the editor adds that he could not trace the persons named in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 550} Others, of Medina and Kufa, read it in the passive; it is said that Hamza al-Zayyat read it so on account of the reading of Ibn Mas'ud. Al-Tabari holds this a mistake if it rests on Ibn Mas'ud's reading, because Ibn Mas'ud applied the verb of fearing to the particle alone, which is sound, as in the line of Abu Mihjan al-Thaqafi asking to be buried beside a vine. Whoever reads the passive in that sense has the verb act both on its subject and on the particle, which the Arabs do not say. The editor reports al-Farra' on this, and that Hamza may not have read it so with certainty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 551} Al-Tabari holds the passive reading valid in another sense, namely unless it is feared for them that they will not keep God's limits; this is the correct reading in his view, because the later words about fearing show that the first has that sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 551} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 552}

He asks what state is meant when it is feared that they will not keep God's limits so that the man may take what he gave her. He says it is the state of her rebelliousness and her showing him dislike, so that it is feared she will leave obedience to God in what she owes her husband, and, through her falling short of his rights, that he will fail to pay what he owes her; this is the state in which the Prophet allowed Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas to take what he had given his wife. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 552} From 'Ikrima from Ibn 'Abbas, the first khul' in Islam was that of the sister of 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy, who came to the Prophet and said that nothing would ever unite her head and his, and told of her dislike of his looks; her husband said, I gave her the best of my property, a garden, and she should give me back my garden; he asked her, what do you say, and she said yes, and if he wishes I will add to it; so he separated them (4807). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 552} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 553} The editor says its chain is sound and it is in al-Bukhari and Ibn Majah, that the woman is Jamila bint 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and the husband Thabit ibn Qays, and gives the story of their son Muhammad ibn Thabit from al-Mustadrak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 553}

From 'A'isha, Habiba bint Sahl was married to Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, who struck her and broke her shoulder bone; she went to the Prophet after dawn and complained of him; he called Thabit and said, take part of what she has and part from her; Thabit asked whether that was right, and he said yes; Thabit said, I gave her two gardens as dower and they are in her hand; he said, take them both and part from her, and he did so (4808). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 554} The editor explains the word for the thin bone at the edge of the shoulder, gives the sources in Abu Dawud and al-Bayhaqi, and shows that Abu 'Amr al-Sadusi is Sa'id ibn Salama, one narrator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 554} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 555} From 'Amra, from Habiba bint Sahl al-Ansariyya, who was married to Thabit ibn Qays; the Prophet saw her at his door in the dark before dawn and asked who she was, and she said, I am Habiba bint Sahl, it is not me and not Thabit; when Thabit came the Prophet said, this is Habiba, she says what God willed her to say; she said, everything he gave me is with me; he said, take it from her, and he took it and she stayed in her house (4809). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 555} The editor traces it to the Muwatta', al-Umm, Ahmad's Musnad, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Hibban and notes the narrations of Ibn Sa'd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 555} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 556} From Jamila bint Ubayy ibn Salul, she was with Thabit ibn Qays and rebelled against him; the Prophet sent for her and asked what she disliked in Thabit; she said, by God, I dislike nothing in his religion or character, only that I dislike his ugliness; he asked, will you return the garden; she said yes, and she returned it and they were separated (4810). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 556} Al-Tabari says it is reported that the verse was revealed about Thabit ibn Qays and his wife; from Ibn Jurayj, about Thabit and Habiba, who complained of him to the Prophet; he asked, will you return his garden, she said yes, he called Thabit and told him, Thabit asked whether that sat well with him, the Prophet said yes, and Thabit said, I have done it (4811). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 556} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 557} The editor, agreeing with Ibn Hajar in the Fath, holds these two accounts to be about two women, Jamila and Habiba, who both obtained khul' from Thabit ibn Qays, because both reports are well known, both chains are sound and the wording differs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 556}

Al-Tabari then gives the interpreters' differences over the fear that they will not keep God's limits. Some said it is when the woman shows bad character and treatment toward her husband; then he may take what she gives as ransom for separation. From Ibn 'Abbas, unless the rebellion and bad character come from her and she calls on you to accept a ransom, in which case there is no fault on you for what she pays (4812). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 557} From 'Urwa, ransom is not lawful until the wrongdoing comes from her, and he did not say that it was unlawful for him until she says that she will not fulfil an oath for him nor wash from major impurity for him (4813, 4815, 4816); from Jabir ibn Zayd, when the harm comes from her, ransom is lawful (4814). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 557} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 558} From 'Amir al-Sha'bi, about a woman who said to her husband that she would not fulfil an oath for him nor obey a command, he said, when a woman dislikes her husband let him take from her and leave her (4817). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 558} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr, on the woman who seeks khul': he admonishes her, then if she does not stop he forsakes her, then if she does not stop he strikes her, then if she does not stop he takes the matter to the ruler, who sends an arbiter from his family and one from hers; whichever of the two spouses is the more wrong the ruler returns and takes his hand firmly, and if she is the rebellious one he tells him to accept khul' (4818). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 558} From al-Rabi', when the woman is content, grateful and obedient, he may not harm her until she buys her freedom, and what he takes from her then is forbidden; when the rebellion, dislike and wrongdoing come from her, he may take what she gave (4819). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 558} From al-Zuhri, it is not lawful for a man to give his wife khul' unless it comes from her side; to harm her until she seeks khul' is not right (4820). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 559} From al-Dahhak, God's limits are that the woman is rebellious, and God commands the husband to admonish her with the Book of God, then if she accepts it well, otherwise to forsake her, then to speak harshly to her to bring her back to obedience, then to strike her without injury, and if she persists in refusing the ransom is lawful to him (4821). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 559}

Others said the fear is that she will not fulfil an oath for him nor obey a command and will say she will not wash from major impurity for him; then in their view it is lawful for him to take what he gave her for separating from her. From al-Hasan, when she says that, khul' is lawful (4822). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 559} The editor corrects wordings of the printed text in several places on these pages. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 558} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 559}

To complete the reports of those who said the fear is the woman's rebellion and what she says: from al-Hasan, if she says she will not fulfil an oath for him, nor obey a command, nor wash from major impurity, nor keep any of God's limits, then her property becomes lawful to him (4823); from al-Sha'bi, when she shows hatred of him and says she will not fulfil an oath for him or obey his command (4824), and he used to wonder at those who say the ransom is not lawful until she says she will not wash from major impurity (4825). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 559} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 560} From Ibrahim on the rebellious wife, a woman may disobey her husband and then obey him, but when she disobeys him and does not fulfil his oath, at that point the ransom is lawful (4826); from al-Suddi, the same, with her saying she will not honour him (4827). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 560} From Miqsam, on Ibn Mas'ud's reading of the verse in al-Nisa' ([[passage/quran-4-19|4:19]]), when she disobeys and hurts you, what you took from her is lawful to you (4828). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 560} From Mujahid, khul', and it is not lawful for him unless the woman says she will not fulfil his oath or obey his command, and he accepts it for fear of wronging her if he keeps her and exceeding the right (4829). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 561}

Others said the fear is that she starts by telling him with her own tongue that she dislikes him. From 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, khul' is lawful when the woman says to her husband, I dislike you and do not love you, I am afraid to sleep by your side and not give you your right, and she is content with khul' (4830). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 561} Others said what makes it lawful for him to take the ransom is a fear on the part of both that they will not keep God's limits, because each dislikes the other's company. From 'Amir, her property is lawful to him because of his rebellion and hers (4831); from Tawus, ransom is lawful by what God said, and he did not say the words of the foolish, "I will not fulfil an oath for you", but it is lawful where God said unless they fear they will not keep His limits, in what He made binding on each of them toward the other in dealing and companionship (4832); from al-Qasim ibn Muhammad the same (4833); and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, khul' is not lawful until they fear that they will not keep God's limits in the dealing between them (4834). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 561} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 562}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice: the soundest of these views is that of those who said it is not lawful for the man to take ransom from his wife for separating from her until each of them fears disobedience to God for himself or herself, in falling short of what is owed to the other, as reported from Tawus and al-Hasan and those who said the like; for God made it lawful for the husband to take the ransom only when Muslims fear for the two that they will not keep God's limits. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 562} To the objection that, if this is so, it should be forbidden for the man to accept ransom when the rebellion is hers alone and not equally his dislike, he replies that her rebellion calls him to fall short of what is owed her and to repay her bad conduct, and that is what makes Muslims fear for the two; when the falling short has already occurred on both sides and bad dealing has appeared, there is no place for fear, since the feared thing is already there: one fears a thing before it happens. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 562} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 563} The editor praises al-Tabari's understanding and insight into human nature, which is the editor's opinion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 563}

He then turns to the next clause of ([[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]), which speaks of fearing they will not keep God's limits, and explains the interpreters' differences over those limits. Some said it is the woman's slighting of her husband's right, poor obedience to him and hurting him with words. Ibn 'Abbas: it is her leaving the keeping of God's limits by slighting her husband's right and bad character, saying to him she will not fulfil an oath, share his bed or obey a command, and then the ransom is lawful to him (4835); al-Hasan, when she says she will not wash from major impurity for him (4836); al-Zuhri, khul' is lawful when they fear they will not keep God's limits and the rendering of those limits in their dealings (4837). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 563} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 564} Others said it means that they will not obey God: 'Amir (4838), and Ibn 'Abbas, the limits are obedience (4839). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 564} The editor gives the biography of Yazid ibn Ibrahim al-Tustari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 564}

Al-Tabari chooses this meaning: if you fear they will not keep what God made binding on them of the duties which He imposed on each toward the other, of dealing in what is right and companionship in what is good, then there is no blame on them in what she pays as ransom. This includes what was reported from Ibn 'Abbas, al-Sha'bi, al-Hasan and al-Zuhri, since the husband's due from the wife includes her obedience in what God made obedience due, that she not hurt him with words and not refuse him when he calls her for his need; when she goes against this she has lost the limits God commanded her to keep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 564} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 565} Keeping God's limits means acting by them, guarding them and not neglecting them, as he explained earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 565}
