**Commentary on verse 30 (pp. 80-83)**

On [[passage/quran-10-30|10:30]], al-Tabari records the readers' difference on the first verb. Some read it with ba, in the sense that at that moment every soul is tried on what it advanced of good or evil, and Mujahid was among those who read and took it so. Reports 17654, 17655 and 17656, from Mujahid: it is tried. In a note the editor says the printed text has a different preposition and that he could not make out the manuscript reading well, and refers to the gloss on "trial". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 80}

A group of the people of Kufa and some of the people of the Hijaz read it with ta, and they differed on its meaning. Some said it means every soul follows what it advanced in the world for that day. A report to that effect is related from the Prophet through a line and chain that al-Tabari does not accept: for every people what they worshipped besides God is made to appear on the Day of Resurrection, and they follow it until it leads them into the Fire, and he recited this verse. The editor says he found the text of this report nowhere else, with or without a chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 81}

Others said it means that each reads the book of his good and bad deeds, as in [[passage/quran-17-13|17:13]]. Others said it means sees with their own eyes; report 17657, from Ibn Zayd: what it did, it sees. The editor notes that the printed text has the ba reading throughout and the manuscript has no dots, and that the ta reading is right, as the commentary on this reading makes clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 82}

Al-Tabari says the correct view is that these are two well-known readings, each read by leading readers, and their meanings are close: whoever follows in the next life what he sent ahead is brought onto his destination and learns what he sent of good or bad, and whoever is tried on what he sent ahead is tried only once he has arrived where his deeds have placed him; so in both cases he follows what he sent ahead and is tested by it, and whichever reading a reader chooses he is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 82}

As for "they are returned to God, their true Master", it means that on that day these polytheists return to God, who is their Lord and Owner, the Truth about whom there is no doubt, and not to the gods and rivals they claimed as lords; "what they used to invent is lost to them" means that the lie and fabrication they conjectured against God, claiming their idols were God's partners and would bring them near to Him, comes to nothing. Report 17658, from Ibn Zayd: what they called upon with Him of rivals and gods, since they made them rivals and gods beside God out of invention and falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 82}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
