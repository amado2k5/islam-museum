**Commentary on verse 131: God's dominion and the charge to earlier and later people (pp. 295-296)**

Al-Tabari says that to God belongs all that is in the seven heavens and the seven earths. The verse follows the talk of separation to direct people to where they should turn at parting from a wife, that they may cry to Him in need, poverty and loneliness, and to remind them that He who owns all things cannot be prevented from enriching whom He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 295}

He then says that God charged those given the Book before them, the People of the Torah and the Gospel, and them, to fear God; that denying His charge harms only oneself; and that those who disobeyed before were punished as the Jews and Christians were, when God changed their ease of living and their security and made some of them apes and swine. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 295}

Report 10674, from Ali through Abu Rawq and Sayf, explains 'rich' as rich beyond His creatures and 'praised' as one whom they must praise. The editor notes that the printed 'security of the drink' is an error for 'the self' (al-sirb), meaning self, property, family and children. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 296}

Al-Tabari adds that to God belongs all that the heavens and earth contain, nothing can be refused Him, and all creatures depend on Him for their keeping and survival; He is the Rich, who has no need, and the Praised, who is owed praise for His good deeds, so people should keep that good by fearing Him and hastening to obey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 296}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
