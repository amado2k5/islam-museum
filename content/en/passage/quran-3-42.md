## Summary
Verse 42 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 42 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The senses of choosing and purifying.** Al-Tabari turns to the next verse, saying the sense is: God is hearing and knowing, when the wife of Imran said what she said and when the angels said to Mary that God has chosen her; choosing means selecting and preferring her for obedience to Him and for the honour He gave her; purifying means cleansing her religion of doubt and the impurities found in the religions of the women of Adam's descendants; and the preference over the women of the worlds means choosing her over them in her time for her obedience to Him, so He raised her above them. The editor refers to earlier treatments of choosing, purifying and 'the worlds'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 393}

**The hadith of the best women.** Al-Tabari cites what is reported from the Messenger of God that he said: the best of its women is Mary daughter of Imran, and the best of its women is Khadija daughter of Khuwaylid, meaning by this the best of the women of Paradise. Report 7026 (al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Suda'i, from Muhadir ibn al-Muwarri, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father, from Abdullah ibn Jafar) says he heard Ali in Iraq say he heard the Messenger of God say the best of its women is Mary daughter of Imran and the best of its women is Khadija. Report 7027 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from al-Mundhir ibn Abdullah al-Hizami, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father, from Abdullah ibn Jafar ibn Abi Talib) says the Messenger of God said the best women of Paradise are Mary daughter of Imran and Khadija daughter of Khuwaylid. In tracing the first, the editor says Muhadir ibn al-Muwarri al-Hamdani al-Kufi is trustworthy, declared so by Ahmad, Abu Hatim and Ibn Sa'd, gives the vocalization of both names, and says the hadith is in Ahmad's Musnad from Abdullah ibn Numayr, Waki and Muhammad ibn Bishr, and from his son Abdullah via Abu Khaythama, Waki and Abu Muawiya, all from Hisham ibn Urwa; al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Tirmidhi report it by Hisham ibn Urwa, al-Hakim in the Mustadrak, Ibn Kathir cites it in his tafsir and history from the report of the two Sahihs, and al-Suyuti attributes it also to Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn Mardawayh. In tracing the second he says al-Mundhir ibn Abdullah ibn al-Mundhir al-Hizami is trustworthy, one of the noble men of Quraysh known for generosity and virtue, whom al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim list without criticism; that it is the same hadith as before, but here from Abdullah ibn Jafar from the Prophet and there from his account through Ali, so it is either a Companion's mursal or the narrator from Hisham abbreviated by omitting Ali; and he thinks it likeliest that Abdullah ibn Jafar heard it from the Messenger of God and also through Ali and so narrated it both ways, and that it is sound in any case. These are reports given as transmitted; the tracing and grading belong to the editor and those he cites, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 393} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 394}

**Qatada's report and the editor's tracing.** Report 7028 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says it was told to Qatada that the Prophet of God used to say that enough for a person among the women of the world are Mary daughter of Imran, the wife of Pharaoh, Khadija daughter of Khuwaylid and Fatima daughter of Muhammad. Qatada adds a second report that the best of the women who ride camels are the righteous women of Quraysh, kindest to a child in its infancy and most careful of a husband in his property, and a third that, had he known Mary rode a camel, he would not have preferred anyone over her. The editor says the report is mursal and is really three hadiths, each opened by Qatada with 'it was told us'. The first is established as connected: Ahmad has it in the Musnad (12418) from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada, from Anas ibn Malik, raised to the Prophet; al-Hakim has it in the Mustadrak from al-Qati'i, from Abdullah ibn Ahmad, from his father (though he says al-Qati'i gave it from Ahmad's book on the merits of the household, not the Musnad); al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Hibban in his Sahih give it through Abd al-Razzaq. The editor notes that al-Tirmidhi calls it sound, that al-Hakim calls it sound on the conditions of the two shaykhs, who did not record it in this wording, that al-Hakim's remark may wrongly suggest that the two shaykhs narrated something from Anas, which they did not, that Ibn Kathir cites it in his History and commentary, and that Ibn Hajar, citing al-Tirmidhi in the Fath, calls its chain sound. He says the second wording comes in the next report, and the third is a munkar wording not known to be established by any connected chain; he holds the correct view to be that it is Abu Hurayra's own remark. In a note he adds that Arabic allows the singular pronoun to follow a superlative while referring to a plural, as in poetry and in reports, and points to earlier passages. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 395} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 396}

**The report of Abu Hurayra.** Report 7029 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says Abu Hurayra used to narrate that the Prophet said the best of camel-riding women are the righteous women of Quraysh, kindest to a child and most careful of a husband in his property, and Abu Hurayra added that Mary never rode a camel. The editor holds the chain broken, since Qatada was born in 61, after Abu Hurayra's death, and his phrase 'used to narrate' resembles a bare relay; the text itself is sound. Ahmad gives it (7637, 7995, 7638), as do al-Bukhari and Muslim by routes from Abu Hurayra; Ibn Kathir cites the Musnad version and says Muslim alone recorded it by this route. The editor states that the sound versions have Abu Hurayra saying the last sentence from himself, and that raising it to the Prophet in the wording of the previous hadith is, as he said, a munkar wording. He also comments on the word for 'righteous' in the manuscript, a plural of a form not recorded in the dictionaries. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 396} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 397}

**The four women.** Report 7030 (al-Tabari, 'it was told me' from Ammar, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father) says Thabit al-Bunani used to narrate from Anas ibn Malik that the Messenger of God said the best women of the world are four: Mary daughter of Imran, Asiya daughter of Muzahim the wife of Pharaoh, Khadija daughter of Khuwaylid and Fatima daughter of Muhammad. The editor calls the chain weak because al-Tabari's informant is unnamed, notes that Ibn Abi Ja'far is Abdullah al-Razi, reliable by the judgement of Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim, and that his father is probably Isa ibn Mahan, reliable by the judgement of Ibn al-Madini and Ibn Sa'd, though he found nothing showing the father met Thabit. He adds that Ibn Kathir says Ibn Mardawayh narrated it through Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far and Ibn Asakir through Tamim ibn Ziyad, but without showing their chains, so neither can be assessed, and that the sound route of the first report makes this one unnecessary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 397}

**The report of Abu Musa.** Report 7031 (al-Muthanna, from Adam al-Asqalani, from Shu'ba, from Amr ibn Murra, from Murra al-Hamdani, from Abu Musa al-Ash'ari) says the Messenger of God said many men have reached perfection but of women only Mary, the wife of Pharaoh, Khadija and Fatima, as the printed text has it. The editor notes that Adam is Adam ibn Abi Iyas, al-Bukhari's teacher, that Amr ibn Murra is al-Jamali al-Muradi, and that Murra al-Hamdani is Murra ibn Sharahil, a reliable senior follower; al-Bukhari gave it from Adam by this chain at length and by Waki from Shu'ba; Ibn Kathir cites it and says the group except Abu Dawud recorded it from Shu'ba; al-Suyuti adds Ibn Abi Shayba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 397} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 398}

**The report of Fatima daughter of al-Husayn.** Report 7032 (al-Muthanna, from Abu al-Aswad al-Misri, from Ibn Lahi'a, from Umara ibn Ghaziyya, from Muhammad ibn Abdullah ibn Amr ibn Uthman) says Fatima daughter of al-Husayn ibn Ali told him that Fatima daughter of the Messenger of God said: the Prophet came in while she was with Aisha and whispered to her, so she wept, then whispered again and she laughed; Aisha asked, and she refused to disclose his secret. After his death she told Aisha that he had said Gabriel used to review the Qur'an with him once a year and had done so twice this year; that no prophet lives other than half the age of the one before; that Jesus lived 120 years while he was sixty and thought he would die that year; that no woman had been afflicted as she would be, so she should not be less patient than any woman; she wept, and then he said she was the lady of the women of Paradise except Mary the virgin. He died that year. The editor weakens the chain for its break: Fatima daughter of al-Husayn's narration from her grandmother al-Zahra is plainly mursal, since al-Zahra died six months after her father when al-Hasan and al-Husayn were small. He found it in no collection except here; Ibn Hajar refers to it twice in the Fath, attributing it only to al-Tabari, and in one place wrongly calls it a second route from Aisha. He says the core of the story is established from Aisha in the two Sahihs but without mention of Jesus or his age or the sentence about affliction, and that the age given for Jesus is very munkar, nobody known having said it, a sign of the report's weakness. On the chain he notes that Abu al-Aswad is al-Nadr ibn Abd al-Jabbar, reliable; Umara is reliable and Muslim recorded from him; Muhammad is reliable and known as al-Dibaj for his beauty; the manuscript and printed text wrongly give his father as Abd al-Rahman where Abdullah is certain; and Fatima daughter of al-Husayn was a reliable follower who lived nearly ninety years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 398} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 399}

**The merit of Khadija.** Report 7033 (al-Muthanna, from Abu al-Aswad, from Ibn Lahi'a, from Amr ibn al-Harith) says Abu Ziyad al-Himyari heard Ammar ibn Sa'd say that the Messenger of God said Khadija was preferred over the women of his community as Mary was preferred over the women of her own world. The editor calls the chain weak in any case: Abu Ziyad al-Himyari is unidentified, and Ammar ibn Sa'd the muezzin is a follower whose narration from the Prophet al-Tahdhib calls mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 399} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 400}

**The sense of purifying, and the preference of her time.** Al-Tabari says he has stated the sense of purifying as Mujahid did, namely cleansing the religion of filth and doubt; the editor notes that the manuscript and print read 'Mujahid said' and that he kept the reading the context requires. Report 7034 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) has Mujahid saying He made her good in faith, and 7035 gives the same through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. Report 7036 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says the preference was over the people of her day. The editor refers to p. 393 note 4 for the references on the word for the worlds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 400}

**The report of Ibn Ishaq.** Al-Tabari says the angels, as Ibn Ishaq related, addressed this to Mary face to face. Report 7037 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq) says Mary was confined in the church and with her was a boy named Joseph whose parents had dedicated him as a confined devotee; when their water ran out each took a jar to the wilderness where fresh water was, filled it and returned, and the angels meanwhile kept addressing Mary, so that Zakariyya, hearing it, said the daughter of Imran had a great matter. The editor glosses a verb in the report as drawing sweet water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 401}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
