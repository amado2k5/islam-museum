## Summary
Verse 42 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 42 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The senses of choosing and purifying.** Al-Tabari turns to the next verse, saying the sense is: God is hearing and knowing, when the wife of Imran said what she said and when the angels said to Mary that God has chosen her; choosing means selecting and preferring her for obedience to Him and for the honour He gave her; purifying means cleansing her religion of doubt and the impurities found in the religions of the women of Adam's descendants; and the preference over the women of the worlds means choosing her over them in her time for her obedience to Him, so He raised her above them. The editor refers to earlier treatments of choosing, purifying and 'the worlds'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 393}

**The hadith of the best women.** Al-Tabari cites what is reported from the Messenger of God that he said: the best of its women is Mary daughter of Imran, and the best of its women is Khadija daughter of Khuwaylid, meaning by this the best of the women of Paradise. Report 7026 (al-Husayn ibn Ali al-Suda'i, from Muhadir ibn al-Muwarri, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father, from Abdullah ibn Jafar) says he heard Ali in Iraq say he heard the Messenger of God say the best of its women is Mary daughter of Imran and the best of its women is Khadija. Report 7027 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from al-Mundhir ibn Abdullah al-Hizami, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father, from Abdullah ibn Jafar ibn Abi Talib) says the Messenger of God said the best women of Paradise are Mary daughter of Imran and Khadija daughter of Khuwaylid. In tracing the first, the editor says Muhadir ibn al-Muwarri al-Hamdani al-Kufi is trustworthy, declared so by Ahmad, Abu Hatim and Ibn Sa'd, gives the vocalization of both names, and says the hadith is in Ahmad's Musnad from Abdullah ibn Numayr, Waki and Muhammad ibn Bishr, and from his son Abdullah via Abu Khaythama, Waki and Abu Muawiya, all from Hisham ibn Urwa; al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Tirmidhi report it by Hisham ibn Urwa, al-Hakim in the Mustadrak, Ibn Kathir cites it in his tafsir and history from the report of the two Sahihs, and al-Suyuti attributes it also to Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn Mardawayh. In tracing the second he says al-Mundhir ibn Abdullah ibn al-Mundhir al-Hizami is trustworthy, one of the noble men of Quraysh known for generosity and virtue, whom al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim list without criticism; that it is the same hadith as before, but here from Abdullah ibn Jafar from the Prophet and there from his account through Ali, so it is either a Companion's mursal or the narrator from Hisham abbreviated by omitting Ali; and he thinks it likeliest that Abdullah ibn Jafar heard it from the Messenger of God and also through Ali and so narrated it both ways, and that it is sound in any case. These are reports given as transmitted; the tracing and grading belong to the editor and those he cites, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 393} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 394}
