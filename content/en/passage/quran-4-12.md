## Summary
Verse 12 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 12 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The husbands' portion of their wives' estates (p. 51).** Abu Jafar says the clause means: you, people, take a half of your wives' property and estate after their death if they leave no child, neither male nor female, on the day death befalls them; if they leave a child, male or female, you take a quarter of it, as an inheritance from them, and that is out of what remains of their estates and property after paying the debts they die owing and carrying out their lawful bequests if they made any. The editor notes that the printing has a preposition that does not stand in two places and that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 51}

**The wives' portion (p. 52).** On the next clause Abu Jafar says that your wives, people, take a quarter of your property and estate after your death if you die leaving no child, male or female; if you die leaving a child, male or female, one or several, your wives take an eighth of the estate you leave, after paying your debts and carrying out your lawful bequests. As to why the bequest is named before the debt here, he says the sense is that what He fixed for those He assigned in these verses is theirs only after taking out whichever of the two is on the deceased's estate, bequest or debt, so it makes no difference whether the bequest is named before the debt or the debt before the bequest, since the sense is not that both are taken from his property, in which case naming the debt first would be better. The editor records that both texts have a feminine plural where the right reading is the masculine, and that the printing added a word "one of" that has no sense there and spoils what Abu Jafar meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 52}

**Start of the discussion of the kalala (pp. 53-54, reports 8745-8748).** Abu Jafar says the sense is: if a man or a woman is inherited from as a kalala. The reciters differed: most reciters of the Islamic lands read the passive, meaning the man is inherited from as one whose lineage is wrapped around, so the kalala on this reading is a verbal noun from the saying that lineage "wrapped around" him, in the sense that kinship bent round upon him; some read the causative active, meaning a man who leaves an inheritance to one who wraps around him, that is who attaches to him by kinship as a brother or sister. The interpreters differed on the kalala. Some said it is whatever excludes parent and child. For this al-Walid ibn Shuja' al-Sakuni (8745, from Ali ibn Mushir from Asim from al-Sha'bi) reports that Abu Bakr said: I have formed an opinion on the kalala; if it is right it is from God alone, who has no partner, and if it is wrong it is from me and from Satan, and God is free of it: that the kalala is what excludes child and parent; and when Umar became caliph he said: I am ashamed before God to differ from Abu Bakr over an opinion he held. Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim (8746, from Hushaym from Asim al-Ahwal from al-Sha'bi) reports that Abu Bakr said of the kalala: I say about it by my opinion, and if it is right it is from God, that it is what is less than child and parent, and that Umar said after him: I am ashamed before God to differ from Abu Bakr. [Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la] (8747, from Sufyan from Asim al-Ahwal from al-Sha'bi) reports that Abu Bakr and Umar ibn al-Khattab both said the kalala is one with neither child nor parent. Ibn Waki' (8748, from his father from Imran ibn Hudayr from al-Sumayt) reports that Umar was a man who was left-handed, and went out one day saying with his hand, and the report is cut off at the end of the page. In his notes the editor says: the printing has a jussive in "if it be a mistake" and he kept the manuscript; the printing added a formula of blessing after the names of Abu Bakr and Umar and he kept the manuscript without noting it again; both texts dropped the preposition "from" before Satan, which he completed from the commentary of Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi and al-Durr al-Manthur. He traces 8745 in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi and al-Durr al-Manthur, which also attributes it to Abd al-Razzaq, Sa'id ibn Mansur, Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn al-Mundhir, and records that the wording is "when Umar was stabbed" in the Durr and al-Bayhaqi, "when Umar took charge" in Ibn Kathir, and that one of al-Bayhaqi's two versions and al-Baghawi's read "when he succeeded" as in al-Tabari. He identifies Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la al-Sadafi al-Misri as al-Tabari's teacher, from whom he relates much; the printing has "Abu Bishr ibn Abd al-A'la" and the manuscript "Abu Bishr Abd al-A'la", neither a known narrator and certainly not among Abu Jafar's teachers, so he judged it a scribal corruption and put the right name in brackets. In his note on 8748 he says the descriptions of Umar in the reports are of a man who worked with both hands, called "ambidextrous" in the lexicon, and that a man who works with the left alone is left-handed; the word used here seems to mean working with the left hand, which is strange to the lexicographers; Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam said this is how it is reported in the hadith but in Arab usage the right form is the one for ambidextrous, and Ibn al-Sikkit said not to say the form used here; the note breaks off at this point and continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 53} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 54}

**End of report 8748 and its notes (p. 55).** The report is completed: Umar went out one day gesturing with his hand, turning it, and said that a time had come upon him when he did not know what the kalala was, and that the kalala is what excludes child and parent. The editor explains that the narrator's words "saying with his hand thus" mean he moved it, pointed or gestured, since the verb "to say" in Arab usage covers pointing, moving and gesturing. He traces the report in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan al-Kubra through Muhammad ibn Nasr from Abd al-A'la from Hammad from Imran ibn Hudayr from al-Sumayt ibn Umayr, in different and shorter wording, and in al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur, abridged and attributed only to Ibn Abi Shayba; Imran ibn Hudayr al-Sadusi was introduced at no. 2634, and al-Sumayt is Sumayt ibn Umayr al-Sadusi (also given as Sumayr or Amr), listed in the Tahdhib, al-Bukhari's Kabir and Ibn Abi Hatim. In another note he says the report came this way in the text before him and he cannot tell whether the fault is the copyist's or the report was so, and that he found the full report nowhere else. The rest of his note on the word for left-handedness, cut off at the foot of p. 54, does not appear in the scanned text of this page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 55}

**Reports that the kalala is one without child or parent (pp. 55-57, reports 8749-8764).** Abu Jafar gives many reports of this sense. Ibn Waki' (8749, from his father from Sufyan from Jabir from Amir) reports from Abu Bakr that the kalala is what excludes child and parent. Yunus (8750, from Sufyan from Amr ibn Dinar from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is one with neither child nor parent, and again Yunus (8751, from Ibn Wahb from Ibn Jurayj from Amr ibn Dinar from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad) the same; Muhammad ibn Bashshar (8752, from Mu'ammal from Sufyan from Amr ibn Dinar from al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya) from Ibn Abbas that it is what excludes child and parent. Then Ibn Bashshar and Ibn Waki' (8753; the opening of the chain is garbled in the page text, the rest being Ibn Waki' from his father from Isra'il from Abu Ishaq from Salim ibn Abd) from Ibn Abbas, likewise; Ibn Waki' (8754, from his father from Isra'il from Abu Ishaq from Salim ibn Abd al-Saluli) with the same sense; and al-Muthanna (8755, number not legible in the text and inferred here from the sequence, from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas on the clause about a man or woman whose estate goes by way of kalala: the kalala is one who left no child and no parent. Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Muharibi (8756, from Abu al-Ahwas from Abu Ishaq) reports Salim ibn Abd as saying that he saw them all agree that whoever dies leaving neither child nor parent is a kalala; Tamim ibn al-Muntasir (8757, from Ishaq ibn Yusuf from Sharik from Abu Ishaq) the same, with the word "consensus"; Ibn Bashshar (8758, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Abu Ishaq) that it is what excludes child and parent; and Ibn Waki' (8759, from Ibn Fudayl from Ash'ath from Abu Ishaq) that he met them saying that when a man has left no child and no parent his estate goes by way of kalala. In his note the editor says that 8753, then 8759, 8758, 8757, 8756 and 8754, are different routes of the report of Salim ibn Abd al-Saluli from Ibn Abbas, which al-Tabari will give again at 8768; that al-Bayhaqi has it in the Sunan al-Kubra through Yahya ibn Yahya from Hushaym from Zakariyya ibn Abi Za'ida from Abu Ishaq, and points to the Isra'il route of 8754 and 8753. He lists Salim ibn Abd al-Saluli (also given as Salim ibn Abd Allah), a Kufan, in al-Bukhari's Kabir, Ibn Abi Hatim and Ta'jil al-Manfa'a, quoting al-Bukhari and Abu Hatim that he narrated from Hudhayfa and Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i narrated from him, with the Hafiz adding "only"; Ibn Hibban held him reliable and said he took part in the Tabaristan campaign; another authority, whose name is garbled in the page text, called him a reliable Kufan and said there were three reliable brothers of the Saluli clan in Kufa, Salim, Umara and Zayd, sons of Abd. He adds that the chains of al-Tabari and al-Bayhaqi show that Salim narrated also from Companions other than Hudhayfa, namely Ibn Abbas. Bishr ibn Mu'adh (8760, from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that the kalala is one with neither child nor parent, neither father nor grandfather, neither son nor daughter, and that the siblings meant here are the maternal ones. Ibn al-Muthanna (8761, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba) reports from al-Hakam that it is whatever is below child and parent. Yunus (8762, from Ibn Wahb) reports Ibn Zayd: the kalala is anyone from whom neither parent nor child inherits, and anyone with no child and no parent has his estate pass by way of kalala, whether man or woman. al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8763, from Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar) reports from Qatada, al-Zuhri and Abu Ishaq that it is one with neither child nor parent, and Ibn Waki' (8764, from Muhammad ibn Muhammad from Ma'mar) the same from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 55} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 56} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 57}

**Two further views: what is below the child, and what excludes the father (pp. 57-58, report 8765).** Abu Jafar says others held that the kalala is whatever is below the child. This is also said to be an opinion of Ibn Abbas, namely the report he gave earlier from Tawus that Ibn Abbas gave the maternal siblings a sixth alongside the parents (the editor identifies it as report 8734). Others said the kalala is what excludes only the father; Ibn al-Muthanna (8765, from Sahl ibn Yusuf from Shu'ba) reports that he asked al-Hakam about the kalala and was told it is what is below the father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 57} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 58}

**The grammar of the word and al-Tabari's choice (p. 58).** Abu Jafar records that the grammarians differed on what puts the word kalala in the accusative. Some Basrans said that, if one wishes, it is the predicate of the verb "to be" and the passive verb is a description of the man; or "to be" may stand without a predicate, like "occurred", with the word in the accusative as a circumstantial of state, as in "he is struck standing". Others said the word is the predicate of "to be", so that the one inherited from is not the kalala but the heir is. Abu Jafar's own view is that it is accusative by way of departure from the passive verb, which is the predicate of "to be", and that though so accusative it is not a state-clause but a verbal noun from the sense: the sense being a man inherited from by one whose lineage is wrapped around his, the one thus connected being left unstated since the verb indicates him. The editor notes that the printing has the usual form of the verb while the manuscript seems to have another form of the same root, which he judged better and kept. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 58}

**What is called a kalala? First view: the deceased himself (pp. 58-59, reports 8766-8768).** Abu Jafar says the scholars differed on what is named kalala. Some said it is the one inherited from, the dead man himself, so called when someone other than his father and child inherits from him. Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8766, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi on the kalala: one who left no parent and no child. Ibn Waki' (8767; the number is garbled in the page text and taken from the editor's note; from Ibn Uyayna from Sulayman al-Ahwal from Tawus) reports from Ibn Abbas: "I was the last of the people to be with Umar and I heard him say: the saying is what I said; I asked: and what did you say? He said: the kalala is one who has no child." Ibn Waki' (8768, from his father and Yahya ibn Adam from Isra'il from Abu Ishaq from Salim ibn Abd) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is one with neither child nor parent. The editor's notes: in 8766 the printing has "their saying on the kalala" and he kept the manuscript, which is right; in 8767 the printing added a formula of blessing after Umar's name and he kept the manuscript, the printing also dropped the word "the saying" in Umar's reply, while the manuscript doubles a word through the copyist's haste, so he took the right wording from al-Bayhaqi's Sunan al-Kubra. He identifies Sulayman al-Ahwal as Sulayman ibn Abi Muslim al-Makki, maternal uncle of Ibn Abi Najih, reliable, narrated from by the compilers of the six books. He traces 8767 in al-Bayhaqi through Sa'dan ibn Nasr from Sufyan ibn Uyayna and quotes al-Bayhaqi's comment that what they narrated from Umar and Ibn Abbas on the interpretation of the kalala is closer to the indications of the Book and the Sunna than this version and more likely to be sound, given that this version stands alone while the reports against it are many; Ibn Kathir refers to it and says something contrary to the well-known view is reported from Ibn Abbas, that the sound report from him is the first, and that perhaps the narrator did not grasp what he meant. The editor adds that Abu Jafar did not overlook this: he followed with the famous report of Ibn Abbas through Salim ibn Abd al-Saluli (8753-8759) by another route and was content with that as his comment on the view that Tawus alone reported from Ibn Abbas. On 8768 he notes that there are two chains, Ibn Waki' from his father (given at 8754) and Ibn Waki' from Yahya ibn Adam, a chain al-Tabari did not list earlier among those of this report, and that the printing and manuscript both have "Sulayman ibn Abd", an error for Salim ibn Abd al-Saluli as in the earlier chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 58} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 59}

**Second view: the heirs; third view: the dead and the living together; al-Tabari's choice (pp. 60-61, reports 8769-8771).** Abu Jafar says others held that the kalala are the heirs who inherit from the dead man, brothers, sisters or others, when they are neither child nor parent, according to the differences already recorded; and others that it is the dead and the living together. Yunus (8769, from Ibn Wahb) reports Ibn Zayd: the kalala is the dead man who has neither child nor parent, or the living; all of them are kalala, this one inheriting by way of kalala and that one being inherited from by way of it. The editor notes that the manuscript has the verb of inheriting in the active form in both places, a copyist's slip, the right reading being the printing's. Abu Jafar then says the correct view, to him, is what these others said: the kalala are those who inherit from the dead man apart from his child and parent, because of the soundness of the report he gave from Jabir ibn Abd Allah, who said: "Messenger of God, only a kalala will inherit from me, so how is it with inheritance?" (the editor identifies it as report 8730), and because of what Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim (8770, from Ibn Ulayya from Ibn Awn from Amr ibn Sa'id) reports: we were with Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman in the slave market; he stood up, came back and said: this is the last of three men of the Banu Sa'd who told me this hadith, saying that Sa'd fell gravely ill at Mecca and the Messenger of God came to visit him, and Sa'd said: "I have much wealth and no heir but a kalala; shall I bequeath all my wealth?" He said: no. Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim (8771, from Ibn Ulayya from Ishaq ibn Suwayd from al-Ala ibn Ziyad) also reports that an old man came to Umar and said: "I am old and have no heir but a kalala of bedouin whose kinship is distant; may I bequeath a third of my wealth?" He said: no. Abu Jafar closes that these reports show the soundness of what he said about the meaning of kalala: the heirs of the deceased, not the deceased, from among those other than his father and child. The editor's notes: Amr ibn Sa'id al-Qurashi narrated from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Abu al-Aliya, al-Sha'bi and Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Himyari, and Ayyub, Yunus, Ibn Awn and others narrated from him; he is reliable. Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Himyari was narrated from by the six books and himself narrated from Abu Bakra, Ibn Umar, Abu Hurayra, Ibn Abbas and three of the sons of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (the three named in this report); Ibn Sa'd called him reliable, with hadiths; both are in the Tahdhib. The report of Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas about the bequest and his saying that he is inherited from by way of a kalala is given by Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat and by Ahmad in his Musnad, both from Affan ibn Muslim from Wuhayb from Abd Allah ibn Uthman ibn Khuthaym from Amr ibn al-Qari from his father from his grandfather, and by Ibn Abd al-Barr in al-Isti'ab and Ibn al-Athir in Usd al-Ghaba, who says "the three" have it, meaning Ibn Manda, Abu Nu'aym and Ibn Abd al-Barr. On the old man's phrase "of distant kinship" he says it means far in lineage, from the saying that a person "grew distant from me", and that the lexicographers gave no attestation for it, and this is its attestation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 60} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 61}

**Start of the next clause: maternal siblings (pp. 61-62, reports 8772-8778).** Abu Jafar moves to the next clause of [[passage/quran-4-12|verse 12]], saying that God means by it that the man whose estate passes by way of kalala has a sibling on his mother's side, male or female. Ibn Bashshar (8772, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Ya'la ibn Ata' from al-Qasim) reports that Sa'd used to read the verse with an addition specifying the mother, and Sa'd said that it is for his mother. Ibn al-Muthanna (8773, from Abd al-Rahman from Shu'ba from Ya'la ibn Ata') reports that al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a said he read to Sa'd, who said it is the mother's; Ibn al-Muthanna (8774, from Wahb ibn Jarir from Shu'ba from Ya'la ibn Ata' from al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a ibn Qanif) the same. Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim (8775, number not legible and inferred from the sequence, from Hushaym from Ya'la ibn Ata') reports that al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a heard Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas read the verse with that addition showing that the sibling is from the mother. Bishr ibn Mu'adh (8776, number likewise not legible, from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that these are the maternal siblings: if one, he has a sixth, and if more than that, they divide the third among themselves, males and females equal in it. The editor notes that the numbering of the printing skips number 8777. Then the report of Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8778, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat) from al-Suddi on the verse begins and is cut off at the end of the page. The editor's notes: the printing has "al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a from Fatik", a plain error; the manuscript has what he gave, though the copyist wrote and pointed it badly and the publishers changed it. Al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a is al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a ibn Qanif al-Thaqafi, named after his grandfather, so al-Qasim ibn Abd Allah ibn Rabi'a ibn Qanif al-Thaqafi, reliable, with no narrator from him but Ya'la ibn Ata' al-Amiri, whose biography and chain were given earlier at nos. 1755-1757. The report of Sa'd is in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan al-Kubra and al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur, who adds Sa'id ibn Mansur, Abd ibn Humayd, al-Darimi, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 61} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 62} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 62 in the commentary on verse 12 of sura 4, in report 8778 from al-Suddi on the maternal siblings, which is cut off at the end of the page, and continue from p. 63. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 62}
