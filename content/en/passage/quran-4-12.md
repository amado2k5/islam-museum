## Summary
Verse 12 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 12 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The husbands' portion of their wives' estates (p. 51).** Abu Jafar says the clause means: you, people, take a half of your wives' property and estate after their death if they leave no child, neither male nor female, on the day death befalls them; if they leave a child, male or female, you take a quarter of it, as an inheritance from them, and that is out of what remains of their estates and property after paying the debts they die owing and carrying out their lawful bequests if they made any. The editor notes that the printing has a preposition that does not stand in two places and that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 51}

**The wives' portion (p. 52).** On the next clause Abu Jafar says that your wives, people, take a quarter of your property and estate after your death if you die leaving no child, male or female; if you die leaving a child, male or female, one or several, your wives take an eighth of the estate you leave, after paying your debts and carrying out your lawful bequests. As to why the bequest is named before the debt here, he says the sense is that what He fixed for those He assigned in these verses is theirs only after taking out whichever of the two is on the deceased's estate, bequest or debt, so it makes no difference whether the bequest is named before the debt or the debt before the bequest, since the sense is not that both are taken from his property, in which case naming the debt first would be better. The editor records that both texts have a feminine plural where the right reading is the masculine, and that the printing added a word "one of" that has no sense there and spoils what Abu Jafar meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 52}

**Start of the discussion of the kalala (pp. 53-54, reports 8745-8748).** Abu Jafar says the sense is: if a man or a woman is inherited from as a kalala. The reciters differed: most reciters of the Islamic lands read the passive, meaning the man is inherited from as one whose lineage is wrapped around, so the kalala on this reading is a verbal noun from the saying that lineage "wrapped around" him, in the sense that kinship bent round upon him; some read the causative active, meaning a man who leaves an inheritance to one who wraps around him, that is who attaches to him by kinship as a brother or sister. The interpreters differed on the kalala. Some said it is whatever excludes parent and child. For this al-Walid ibn Shuja' al-Sakuni (8745, from Ali ibn Mushir from Asim from al-Sha'bi) reports that Abu Bakr said: I have formed an opinion on the kalala; if it is right it is from God alone, who has no partner, and if it is wrong it is from me and from Satan, and God is free of it: that the kalala is what excludes child and parent; and when Umar became caliph he said: I am ashamed before God to differ from Abu Bakr over an opinion he held. Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim (8746, from Hushaym from Asim al-Ahwal from al-Sha'bi) reports that Abu Bakr said of the kalala: I say about it by my opinion, and if it is right it is from God, that it is what is less than child and parent, and that Umar said after him: I am ashamed before God to differ from Abu Bakr. [Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la] (8747, from Sufyan from Asim al-Ahwal from al-Sha'bi) reports that Abu Bakr and Umar ibn al-Khattab both said the kalala is one with neither child nor parent. Ibn Waki' (8748, from his father from Imran ibn Hudayr from al-Sumayt) reports that Umar was a man who was left-handed, and went out one day saying with his hand, and the report is cut off at the end of the page. In his notes the editor says: the printing has a jussive in "if it be a mistake" and he kept the manuscript; the printing added a formula of blessing after the names of Abu Bakr and Umar and he kept the manuscript without noting it again; both texts dropped the preposition "from" before Satan, which he completed from the commentary of Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi and al-Durr al-Manthur. He traces 8745 in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi and al-Durr al-Manthur, which also attributes it to Abd al-Razzaq, Sa'id ibn Mansur, Ibn Abi Shayba and Ibn al-Mundhir, and records that the wording is "when Umar was stabbed" in the Durr and al-Bayhaqi, "when Umar took charge" in Ibn Kathir, and that one of al-Bayhaqi's two versions and al-Baghawi's read "when he succeeded" as in al-Tabari. He identifies Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la al-Sadafi al-Misri as al-Tabari's teacher, from whom he relates much; the printing has "Abu Bishr ibn Abd al-A'la" and the manuscript "Abu Bishr Abd al-A'la", neither a known narrator and certainly not among Abu Jafar's teachers, so he judged it a scribal corruption and put the right name in brackets. In his note on 8748 he says the descriptions of Umar in the reports are of a man who worked with both hands, called "ambidextrous" in the lexicon, and that a man who works with the left alone is left-handed; the word used here seems to mean working with the left hand, which is strange to the lexicographers; Abu Ubayd al-Qasim ibn Sallam said this is how it is reported in the hadith but in Arab usage the right form is the one for ambidextrous, and Ibn al-Sikkit said not to say the form used here; the note breaks off at this point and continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 53} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 54} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 54 in the commentary on verse 12 of sura 4, in report 8748 about Umar at the start of the discussion of the kalala, which is cut off at the end of the page together with its editor's note, and continue from p. 55. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 54}
