## Summary
Verse 55 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 55 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

By "so we explain the signs, and so that the way of the sinners be plain" al-Tabari means: as we explained to you, Muhammad, in this surah from its beginning and opening to this place, our proof against the idol-worshipping polytheists and our arguments, distinguished and made plain to you, so we explain to you our marks and arguments in every truth that the people of falsehood of the other communities deny, making them plain to you until its truth is plain from its falsehood and its sound from its unsound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 394}

The readers differed in "wa-li-tastabina sabil al-mujrimin". Most readers of Medina read "li-tastabina" with ta and "sabila" in the accusative, as "tastabina" addresses the Prophet, as though meaning, as they take it, "that you, Muhammad, may see plain the way of the sinners". Ibn Zayd took it so: that you, Muhammad, may see plain the way of the sinners who asked you to drive away the group of your companions they asked you to drive away; 13299, from him: those who tell you to drive these away. Some Meccans and some Basrans read "li-tastabina" with ta and "sabilu" raised, since the point is the way, which they treat as feminine, as though the sense were "and so we explain the signs, that the way of the sinners be clear to you and the believers". Most readers of Kufa read "li-yastabina" with ya and "sabilu" raised, since the verb is of the way, which they treat as masculine; their meaning and that of those who read with ta and raised "sabil" is one, the difference being only in making "sabil" masculine or feminine. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 395}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

Al-Tabari holds the better of the two readings of "sabil" to be the raised, because God explained his signs in his book and revelation so that the truth be made plain from the false by them to all who are addressed by them, not some and not others, and whoever read "sabila" in the accusative made the making plain limited to the Prophet. As for reading "li-tastabina", whether with ta or ya makes no difference, for some Arabs make "sabil" masculine, Tamim and the people of Najd, and some make it feminine, the people of the Hijaz; they are two widespread readings among the readers of the cities and two well-known dialects of the Arabs, reading by one does not differ from reading by the other, and there is no ground for choosing one over the other once "sabil" is raised, for the reason we gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 396}

The interpreters said as we said on "we explain the signs": 13300, from Qatada: we make the signs plain, and 13301, from Ibn Zayd: we make plain.

The editor refers for "way" and "becoming plain" to the language indexes.

Verse 55 closes here at p. 396, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-56|6:56]].
