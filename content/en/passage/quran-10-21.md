**Commentary on verse 21 (pp. 49-50)**

He then explains "When We let people taste mercy after a hardship that touched them, they plot against Our signs. Say: God is swifter in plotting; Our messengers write down what you plot". When We give the polytheists relief after distress and ease after a hardship that struck them (it is also said that rain after drought is meant); "hardship" is difficulty and "mercy" relief; "they plot against Our signs" means mockery and denial. The editor refers to the glosses on tasting, hardship, touching and plotting. Reports 17592, 17593 and 17594, from Mujahid: mockery and denial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 49}

On "God is swifter in plotting": say to these polytheists who mock Our proofs, Muhammad, God is swifter in plotting, that is swifter in subtle stratagem against you, in drawing you on and in punishing you, than your plotting against God's signs; the editor explains the word for stratagem as cunning and plotting. The Arabs are content with "when" in place of "I did" and "they did", which is why the verb is dropped after it, and the editor refers to al-Farra; the sense is: when We let people taste mercy after a hardship that touched them, they plotted against Our signs, "when they plot" standing for "they plotted". "Our messengers write down what you plot" means our guardians whom We send to you, people, write down against you what you plot against Our signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 50}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
