## Summary
Verse 41 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 41 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The request for a sign.** Al-Tabari says Zechariah told his Lord that if this call and the voice he heard were the voice of His angels and a good news from Him, He should give him a sign that it was so, to remove what Satan had whispered and cast into his heart, that the voice was other than the angels' and the news from other than God. Report 7004 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says he said that if the voice was from Him, He should give him a sign. Al-Tabari says he has already explained the meaning of the word for sign, that it is a mark; the editor refers to the earlier passage and to the language index. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 384}

**The omission of the hamza in the word for 'sign'.** Al-Tabari says the Arabic scholars differed on why the Arabs leave the hamza out of this word, when they normally put it on every ya that follows a silent alif. Some said it was 'ayya', and the doubling was heavy for them so they changed it to alif because the letter before it has fatha, as they say 'ayma fulan'. Others said it is a shortened 'fa'ila' form; the questioners asked why the Arabs diminish it as 'uyayya' and not 'uwayya', and they replied as with Fatima, 'Futayma'; they were answered that the Arabs diminish the 'fa'ila' form to 'fu'ayla' only when it is a name in the sense of 'so-and-so', not otherwise. Others said it is a 'fa'la' form whose first ya became alif as in 'haja' and 'qama'; they were answered that the Arabs do that only in triliteral nouns, and one who rejected the view said that if it were so, the word for 'date-stone' would be 'naya' and for 'life' 'haya'. The editor says the first speaker is al-Kisa'i and his companions, the questioners are al-Farra and his companions, refers to Lisan al-Arab, thinks all these replies are al-Farra's as the text of the Lisan shows, finds some disorder in al-Tabari's text, suspecting the copyist dropped or moved something, explains 'sons of three' as the triliteral noun, and says both witnesses wrote a word in a corrupt form and the right one has the ya doubled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 384} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 385}

**The start of al-Tabari's comment on the sign.** Al-Tabari says God punished Zechariah, as is reported, for asking the sign after the angels had addressed him with the glad tidings face to face, so He made his sign, to confirm that the news he heard was from God, and the comment continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 385}

**A sign from himself.** Al-Tabari says God made Zechariah's sign a sign from Himself, combining in it the mark he had asked for, to show him that the news was truly from God, and as a sifting of him from his slip and the unsoundness of his words and request. The editor says the printed text has a corrupt word without sense, that in the manuscript the copyist first wrote one word, then smudged its letters and wrote others over them, and the first publisher could read it only as the copyist had fled from; the sense of the sentence is that the sign was that his tongue was held so he spoke to the people only by gesture, as he was told. A group of commentators said as al-Tabari does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 385} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 386}

**The reports of punishment.** Report 7005 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says he was punished because the angels addressed him directly with the news of John, and he asked a sign after their speech, so his tongue was seized and he could speak only by sign and gesture. Report 7006 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) says it was a punishment for asking a sign after the angels' direct address. Report 7007 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says 'it was told us, and God knows' that he was punished for this, so his tongue was seized, and 7008 (Ammar ibn al-Hasan, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) is similar. Report 7009 (Abu Ubayd al-Wassabi, from Muhammad ibn Humayr, from Safwan ibn Amr, from Jubayr ibn Nufayr) says his tongue swelled in his mouth until it filled it, and God released it after three days. The editor says Abu Ubayd is Muhammad ibn Hafs and both printed and manuscript forms are wrong; that Safwan ibn Amr al-Saksaki al-Himsi was trustworthy and reliable and narrated from Abdullah ibn Busr al-Mazini and Jubayr ibn Nufayr; and that Jubayr ibn Nufayr lived in the time of the Prophet when he was still in the pre-Islamic state, became Muslim in the time of Abu Bakr, narrated from the Messenger of God and Abu Bakr in the mursal manner and from Abu Dharr, Abu al-Darda and others, and Abu Hatim called him trustworthy and among the elder successors of Syria; the printed text wrote his name wrongly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 386} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 387}

**The accusative of the particle.** Al-Tabari says the readers chose the accusative for the particle before the verb because the sense is: your sign is that you will not speak to people in the future for three days, and the particle is the one that goes with the future, not the one that goes with nouns and puts them in the accusative; had the sense been that you are in this state for three days, the nominative would be the proper form, as the particle would then be the lightened form of the heavy one, but that is not allowed for the reason he described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 387} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 388}

**The sense of the word for a gesture, and the commentators' difference.** Al-Tabari says the usual sense of this word among the Arabs is a gesture with the lips, sometimes used for a gesture with the brows and eyes, though not often, and it is also said of hidden speech like a whisper with a lowered voice; he cites a line of Ju'ayya ibn Ayidh on heroes who speak in murmurs and rumbling like roaring. The verb is used in several forms, and 'he struck him a blow and he convulsed' means he writhed unto death, for which he cites a line of a rajaz poet. The editor says the printed text wrote the poet's name in a senseless form, that Ibn al-Sikkit in Tahdhib al-Alfaz named him Ju'ayya ibn Ayidh al-Nasri, that al-Amidi in al-Mu'talif wa-l-Mukhtalif gave his genealogy and al-Khizana mentions him, expresses surprise at a commentator on al-Tabari who asserted confidently that he is Ju'ayya ibn Ayidh the Kufan grammarian, says he did not find the line in the books before him but remembers it, and does not know the rajaz poet (referring to Lisan al-Arab). Al-Tabari then says the commentators differed on the sense meant: some said it meant that he would not speak to people for three days except by moving the lips, without uttering speech with the tongue. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 388}

**Mujahid's view and the others.** Report 7010 (Abu Kurayb, from Jabir ibn Nuh, from al-Nadr ibn Arabi, from Mujahid) says it is moving the lips, as does 7011 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) a gesture with his lips, and 7012 (through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl). Others said it means a gesture and sign: report 7013 (Ibn Waki, from his father, from Salama ibn Nubayt, from al-Dahhak) says a sign, 7014 (al-Dahhak) that he points with hand or head and does not speak, 7015 (Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) that his tongue was seized so he addressed people by hand, 7016 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq) a sign, 7017 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) that his sign was not to speak to people for three days except by gesture, though he could remember God, and that the word means sign, 7018 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) a gesture, 7019 (Ammar, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) the same, 7020 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) a sign, 7021 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah ibn Kathir) a sign, and 7022 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) that he held his tongue and gestured with his hand to his people to glorify God morning and evening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 390}

**Remembering the Lord, and glorifying.** Al-Tabari says God told Zechariah that his sign was not to speak to people for three days except by gesture, without dumbness, defect or illness, and to remember his Lord much, for he would not be prevented from remembering Him or glorifying Him or any other remembrance. Report 7023 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abu Ma'shar, from Muhammad ibn Kab) says that if God had permitted anyone to leave off remembrance, He would have permitted Zechariah. The editor refers to the earlier treatment of glorifying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 390} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 391}

**Evening and early morning.** Al-Tabari says the evening is from the sun's decline until it sets, citing a line of Humayd ibn Thawr al-Hilali, since the shade begins its return at the decline and ends at sunset. The editor says the line is from a poem he composed when Umar ibn al-Khattab warned the poets that none should write amorous verse on a woman or be flogged; he escaped the penalty by writing of a tree he named Malik's tree, its people complained to Umar, the editor gives the lines and remarks that the man was light-hearted. Al-Tabari then says the early morning is the verbal noun of 'so-and-so set out early on an errand', going out between dawn and forenoon, and also 'he rose early, rising early'; he cites a half-line of Umar ibn Abi Rabia and a line of Jarir answering Hakim ibn Muayya al-Rabi'i. The date palm is also said to ripen early with a related verb, and the early fruit is the first to ripen. The editor says the ode is on Num, a Quraysh woman of Banu Jumah, that Abu Ubayda glosses 'splitting the staff' as separation, and that this is a text the dictionaries lack and Abu Ja'far preserved, and it is correct. Report 7024 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the early morning is the first of dawn and the evening from the sun's inclination until it sets; the editor notes the manuscript wrote the phrase corruptly. Report 7025 is the same through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 391} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 392} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 393}
