## Summary
Verse 26 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 26 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of verse 26: explaining the lawful and the forbidden, the paths of those before us, and repentance (p. 209).** Abu Jafar says that God's will to explain means He explains to them His lawful and forbidden; that His guiding them to the paths of those before them means He sets them right on the ways of earlier believers in God and His prophets and their methods in what was forbidden to them of marrying mothers, daughters and sisters and the rest of what is forbidden in the two verses where He explained which women are forbidden; and that His turning to them means He wishes to bring them back to obedience from the disobedience they were in by doing that before Islam and before what was revealed to His prophet on it, and to pass over, by their repentance, the ugliness of what went before their return. He then explains the two attributes: God has knowledge of what sets His servants right in their religion, their world and their other affairs, and of what they do and leave of what He made lawful or forbidden, keeping all of it for them, and He is wise in His management of them and in the way He directs them. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of guidance and of sunna, and to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. Abu Jafar then says the grammarians differed over the sense of the clause about God's will to explain. Some said the meaning is that God wills this for the sake of explaining to you, as was said in [[passage/quran-42-15|42:15]] with a kasra on the particle, because its meaning is that he was commanded this for that reason.

**The second view on the particle of the will-to-explain clause: the particles take each other's place (pp. 210-211).** Al-Tabari reports that others said the meaning is that God wills to explain to them and to guide them to the ways of those before them. They say it is the habit of the Arabs to alternate among the particle meaning 'in order that', the lam with that meaning, and the particle 'an' after verbs of willing and commanding, putting each in the place of the others, so that one says 'I commanded you that you go' or 'I commanded you so that you go', and likewise with 'I wanted'. They cite two verses of sura 6 on being commanded to submit to God ([[passage/quran-6-71|6:71]] and [[passage/quran-6-14|6:14]]) and two on the wish to put out God's light, one with the lam and one with 'an' ([[passage/quran-61-8|61:8]] and [[passage/quran-9-32|9:32]]); the editor says the printed number of the second sura 9 reference is smudged and that he read it from the content of the verse. They explain the use of 'an' in the sense of 'in order that' after 'I wanted' and 'I commanded' by saying that both verbs ask for the future and do not take a past tense, so one does not say 'I commanded you that you stood'; since 'an' can accompany a past tense with other verbs, they say, the speakers reinforced its future sense with the other particles. They add that the Arabs sometimes joined these particles in one phrase because their meanings agree and their forms differ. Al-Tabari gives a line by an unknown poet that joins three of them, and a second line, attributed to al-Ajjaj and to Ru'ba, that joins two words of negation for emphasis. The editor's notes: the first poet is unknown, the line is traced to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, al-Insaf and al-Khizana, and he explains words in it (a worn waterskin, a deadly desert, barren ground) and says the sense is the speaker's ruin and loss; the second line is in al-Ajjaj's diwan, and he judges it al-Ajjaj's rather than Ru'ba's because it comes from a poem in which the father reproaches his son Ru'ba in old age, and he quotes lines from its end, which are not repeated here, and explains several rare words. He also records that the manuscript and the printed text carry a faulty form in one recitation phrase and that he restored the recitation wording, that the printed text altered two words of al-Tabari's sentence and he followed the manuscript and al-Farra's wording, and that he supplied a bracketed addition from the context and al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 211}

**The conditions on exchanging the particles, and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 211-212).** Al-Tabari continues the argument of this group: 'an' and the 'in order that' particles may replace each other only where the verb that brings them is not accompanied by a past or a non-future sense; where it is, the exchange is not allowed, so one does not say 'I thought that he might stand' with the lam in place of 'an', because the 'an' that follows 'think' occurs with past, future and nouns. The editor notes that this summarises al-Farra's statement in Ma'ani al-Qur'an and gives his examples. Abu Jafar then says that the sounder of the two views in his opinion is that of those who said the lam here has the sense of 'an', that is, God wills to explain to them, for the reason given by those who said it. This is al-Tabari's choice.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 212}
