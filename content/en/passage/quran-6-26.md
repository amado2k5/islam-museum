## Summary
Verse 26 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 26 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The interpreters differed on "they forbid it and keep away from it". Some said it means: these polytheists who deny God's signs forbid people to follow Muhammad and accept him, "and keep away from it" means keep far from him. Report 13159, from Ibn al-Hanafiyya: they hold back from the Prophet and do not answer him, and forbid people to follow him. 13160, from Ibn Abbas: they forbid people to believe in Muhammad, "and keep away from him", staying far from him. 13161, from al-Suddi: they forbid that Muhammad be followed while they themselves keep away from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 311}

At 13159 the editor identifies Hani ibn Sa'id al-Nakha'i, sound in hadith, Hajjaj ibn Arta'a, Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd and Ibn al-Hanafiyya, Muhammad ibn Ali ibn Abi Talib.

Report 13162, from Ibn Abbas: "they forbid it and keep away from it" means they do not meet him and let no one come to him. 13163, from Abu Mu'adh: "they forbid it" means about Muhammad. 13164, from Qatada: they combined forbidding and keeping away, and "na'y" is distancing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 312}

The editor says the manuscript has "and 'nahy' is distancing", which is without doubt an error, the printed text being right.

Others said it means: they forbid it, the Qur'an, from being listened to and acted on. Report 13165, from Qatada: they forbid the Qur'an and the Prophet, "and keep away from it", distancing themselves. 13166 and 13167, from Mujahid: Quraysh, from the remembrance, "and keep away from it", distancing. 13168, from Qatada: they forbid the Qur'an and the Prophet and keep away from him. 13169, from Ibn Zayd: "wa-yan'awna 'anhu" means they put him far. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 313}

At 13169 the editor says the printed text has "yab'alun" and the manuscript "yab'alunahu", and he preferred to read it as he gave it.

Others said it means: they forbid harm to Muhammad and keep away from his religion and from following him. Reports 13170, 13171 and 13172, from Ibn Abbas through an unnamed transmitter on the authority of Habib ibn Abi Thabit: it was revealed about Abu Talib, who forbade that Muhammad be harmed and kept away from what he brought, that he believe in it. 13173 and 13174, from al-Qasim ibn Mukhaymira: Abu Talib forbade harm to the Prophet and did not accept him as true. 13175, 13176 and 13177 give the like from Ibn Abbas, al-Qasim and Habib. 13178, from Ata ibn Dinar: it was revealed about Abu Talib, who forbade people to harm the Messenger of God and kept away from the guidance he brought. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 314}

In his notes the editor identifies al-Qasim ibn Mukhaymira al-Hamdani, Abu Urwa, trusted, who related from Abdullah ibn Amr, Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Abu Umama; Abu Muhammad al-Asadi, whom he did not know, fearing he is Abd al-Aziz ibn Siyah al-Asadi, truthful and one of the great Shi'a; Ubaydullah ibn Musa al-Absi; Sa'id ibn Abi Ayyub al-Khuza'i al-Misri, trusted and exact; and Ata ibn Dinar al-Misri, one of Egypt's trusted men.

Al-Tabari then chooses: the best of these views on the verse is that of those who said: "they forbid it" means they forbid others than themselves from following Muhammad and keep away from following him. For the verses before it speak of the whole company of the polytheists who hold others equal to him, report their denial of the Messenger of God and their turning from the revelation God sent him, so "they forbid it" must be a report about them, since nothing has come to turn the report from them to others; rather what is before and after this verse shows the soundness of his view that this is a report of the company of the polytheists of the Messenger of God's people and not of a particular one among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 315}

The sense of the verse is: if these polytheists see, Muhammad, every sign they do not believe in it, until when they come to you disputing they say "what you brought us is only the tales of the ancients and their reports"; and they forbid listening to the revelation and keep away from you, far from you and from following you; "and they destroy only themselves" means that by turning others from God's way, turning from his revelation and disbelieving in their Lord, they destroy only themselves and none other, for by it they earn God's wrath and his painful punishment, which they cannot withstand; "and they do not perceive" means they do not know what they earn of ruin and destruction by their act. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 315}

The Arabs say of anyone far from something "na'a anhu, so he is na'in" and it is heard from them "na'aytuka" for "I went far from you", while when they mean "I drove you from me" they say "an'aytuka"; and an example of "na'aytuka" meaning "I went far from you" is a line of al-Hutay'a, from his poem praising the commander of the faithful Umar ibn al-Khattab and excusing himself for his satire on al-Zibriqan ibn Badr, addressing a beloved who withdrew from him leaving only a phantom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 316}

The editor refers for "destruction" to p. 263 and for "perceive" to volumes 6 and 1, corrects "it is heard from them 'na'aytuka'" from the manuscript, and says the line is in al-Hutay'a's Diwan, p. 31, from his poem in praise of Umar ibn al-Khattab.

Verse 26 closes here at p. 316, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-27|6:27]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
