## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "when Moses said to his people: you have wronged yourselves by taking the calf" al-Tabari holds that their wronging themselves was doing what made them liable to punishment, namely turning back by taking the calf as a lord after Moses left; Moses then told them to repent and submit to God's command, and that their repentance was to kill themselves, repentance being a return from what God dislikes to what pleases Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 72} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 73}
On the meaning of "slay yourselves" reports are given from the Successors and others. Abu Abd al-Rahman says they aimed at the throats and stabbed one another; Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Mujahid say they rose against one another with knives, none pitying near or far kin, until Moses signalled with his garment and they dropped their weapons, revealing seventy thousand dead, and God told Moses that was enough. Ibn Abbas, through Ikrima, says those who had worshipped the calf sat huddled and those who had not rose to kill them, a heavy darkness fell on them, and when it lifted the dead numbered seventy thousand, and for every one killed and every one who remained there was repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 73} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 74}
In the long report from al-Suddi, Moses on returning threw down the tablets and seized his brother's head, then questioned al-Samiri, burned the calf and scattered it in the sea and made them drink of it; then it was said God would accept their repentance only through killing themselves, so they formed two lines and fought with swords until seventy thousand were dead, and Moses and Aaron prayed that God spare a remnant; they were told to lay down arms, those slain being martyrs and those left expiated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 74} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 75}
From Mujahid: a man killed his father and his son, though in another version a man did not kill his father or brother. From Abu al-Aliya: they were later told repentance had been accepted for killer and killed. From Ibn Shihab: Moses stood with arms raised in prayer while men held up his arms, then God stayed their hands and told Moses the slain were alive with Him, provided for, and the survivors' repentance was accepted. Qatada says it was martyrdom for the slain and repentance for the living; Ibn Abbas gives the dead as seventy thousand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 75} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 76}
The reports agree on the figure of seventy thousand as transmitted, and some carry the label of martyrdom; they are given here with their narrators, and al-Tabari passes no verdict on them.
