**Commentary on verse 146 (pp. 112-116)**

**On "I will turn away from My signs those who are arrogant in the land without right"**

Al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its meaning. Some said: I will remove from them the understanding of the Book. Report 15122, from Ibn Uyayna, through Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Marwazi and Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Bakr: I remove from them the understanding of the Qur'an and turn them from My signs. The editor gives biographies of Ahmad ibn Mansur al-Ramadi and Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Khuza'i al-San'ani, whom he calls truthful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 112}

Al-Tabari remarks that Ibn Uyayna's interpretation shows he took this as a warning from God to the unbelievers among those to whom our Prophet was sent, rather than to Moses' people, since the Qur'an was sent down on our Prophet Muhammad and not on Moses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 113}

Others said: I will turn them from taking lessons from the proofs. Report 15123, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: from the creation of the heavens and the earth and the signs in them, I will turn them from reflecting on them and learning from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 113}

Al-Tabari prefers that God has said He turns away from His signs, which are His proofs and marks of the truth of what He commanded His servants and made obligatory on them of obedience in His oneness and justice and other duties; the heavens and the earth and everything He created are among His signs, and the Qur'an too. The report is general that He turns away from His signs those arrogant in the land without right, those against whom God's word has been fulfilled that they will not believe, so they are turned from understanding all His signs and learning from them, for had they been given success to understand some and been guided to take a lesson, they would have been admonished and returned to the truth, which they will not do. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "sign". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 113}

**On "if they see every sign they do not believe in it"**

Al-Tabari says: if those who are arrogant in the land without right, their arrogance being their tyranny in it and their disdain for belief in God and His messenger and for submission to His command and prohibition, though they are His servants whom He feeds with His favour and brings His provision each morning and evening, see every sign, that is, every proof of God's oneness and lordship and every indication that worship is fitting only for Him alone, they do not believe in it, meaning they do not accept that it points to what it proves, but say, it is magic and a lie. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 114}

"And if they see the way of right guidance they do not take it as a way": if they see the road of guidance and soundness, by which they would be saved from ruin and reach everlasting bliss, they do not take it or adopt it, out of ignorance and bewilderment; "and if they see the way of error", the road of destruction on which they would go astray and perish. The editor refers to the explanations of "arrogance", "way", "right guidance" and "error" earlier, notes that the printed text reads a different letter in "feeds them" and that the manuscript form is right, and explains the phrase "brought back his right to him" with a verse by a poet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 114}

He says "take it as a way" means to follow it and make it a path for themselves, because God turned them from His signs and sealed their hearts, so they do not prosper; "that is because they denied Our signs and were heedless of them" means: We turned them from Our signs so that they would not reason about or understand them and so take a lesson, be reminded and return, a punishment from Us for their denial of Our signs; and they were heedless of Our proofs, which bear witness to the truth of what We commanded and forbade them, not reflecting on them, diverted from them, taking no lesson, so our Lord's word was fulfilled against them and they perished. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 115}

**The readings of "rushd"**

The readers differed over the reading of the word. Most readers of Medina, some of Mecca and some of Basra read it with damma on the ra and sukun on the shin; most readers of Kufa and some of Mecca read it with fatha on both. The scholars of Arabic then differed over the meaning of each. It is related of Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' that with damma and sukun it means righteousness, as in the verse of sura al-Nisa' where the word occurs, and so he read it, and that with fatha on both it means right guidance in religion, as in the sura al-Kahf words of Moses to his companion, meaning uprightness and correctness in religion. The editor notes that our reading and the reading of the seven in al-Kahf has damma and sukun, and that the reading of Abu Amr is as al-Tabari relates. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 115}

Al-Kisa'i used to say they are two dialects with one meaning, like the pairs for "illness" and "grief". Al-Tabari chooses that they are two well-attested readings among the readers of the cities, agreeing in meaning, so whichever the reader reads is correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 116}
