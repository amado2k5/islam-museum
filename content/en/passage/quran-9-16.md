**Commentary on verse 16 (pp. 163-165)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses the believers whom He told to fight these polytheists who broke their covenant, urging them to struggle against them: do you suppose, believers, that God will leave you without a trial to test you and without an examination to distinguish the one sincere in his religion from the false, "while God has not yet known those of you who strove", that is, do you suppose you will be left without a test by which those who stand with Him and strive in His way are known from those who neglect and fall short in His command, and who "took no confidant besides God, His messenger and the believers". A "confidant" is something that enters into something else, from "so-and-so entered into such a thing", and here it means the intimate circle of the polytheists; God forbade the believers to take their polytheist enemies as allies to whom they disclose their secrets; "God is aware of what you do" means He knows of your taking, besides God, His messenger and the believers, allies and confidants after He forbade it, and that and the rest of your deeds are not hidden from Him, and He will repay you, good for good and evil for evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 164}

Report 16548, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: a confidant is one with whom one enters into alliance with the polytheists; report 16549, from al-Rabi': a hidden dealing; report 16550, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: God refused to leave them without testing, and he cited verses at [[passage/quran-3-142|3:142]], [[passage/quran-2-214|2:214]] and [[passage/quran-29-2|29:2]], showing that He does not leave them until He tests and examines them. Report 16551, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the confidant is unbelief and hypocrisy, or one of the two. The editor notes that the opening of one of the verses cited had dropped from the manuscript and printed text. Al-Tabari adds that the opening is "or do you suppose" and not "do you suppose" because it is a question interjected in the middle of speech, so "or" is added to distinguish it from a question that begins the speech, and refers to the earlier parallels and to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 165}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
