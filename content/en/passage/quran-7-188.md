**Commentary on verse 188 (pp. 301-303)**

Al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to those who ask you about the Hour, "when will it come to anchor?", I have no power over any benefit or harm to myself, meaning I cannot draw a benefit to myself or ward off a harm that befalls me, except what God wills me to have power over by strengthening me and helping me; "and if I knew the unseen", if I knew what will be of what has not yet been, "I would have increased the good", that is, laid up much good. The interpreters differed over the meaning of "good". Some said I would have done more righteous work; report 15494, from Ibn Jurayj: "I have no power over benefit or harm to myself" means guidance and misguidance, and "if I knew the unseen", when I shall die, I would have done more righteous work; report 15495, from Mujahid, the same; report 15496, from Ibn Zayd: I would have avoided what comes of evil and guarded against it. Others said: if I knew the unseen I would have made ready for the barren year out of the fertile one, known dearness from cheapness and prepared for it in the time of cheapness. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "power", "the unseen", "increase" and "good". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 301} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 302}

"And no harm would touch me" means no hurt would touch me; "I am only a warner and a bearer of good news" means I am only a messenger of God sent to you, warning of His punishment those of you who disobey Him and go against His command, and giving good news of His reward and honour to those of you who believe in Him and obey; and "to people who believe" means who trust that I am God's messenger and acknowledge the truth of what I brought them from Him. The editor says the printed edition altered the word for "truth" in many places and the manuscript is right, "haqiqa" being a verbal noun meaning truthfulness and the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 303}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
