## Summary
Verse 159 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 159 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 159: the extra 'ma' (pp. 340-341).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning of the opening is 'by a mercy from God', the 'ma' being a 'silah', that is, an extra particle, whose entry into the sentence he explained at the verse of the gnat parable [[passage/quran-2-26|2:26]]. The Arabs use it as a silah with a definite and with an indefinite noun: for the definite, the verse on the breaking of the covenant [[passage/quran-5-13|5:13]] (the scan also points to Surat al-Nisa' [[passage/quran-4-155|4:155]]); for the indefinite, the verse of Surat al-Mu'minun [[passage/quran-23-40|23:40]]. Sometimes it is made a noun while keeping the sense of a silah, and then what follows is raised as a silah or lowered by following what precedes. He cites a line, which the editor attributes to Hassan ibn Thabit, Ka'b ibn Malik or another, on the love of the Prophet Muhammad as a distinction over others, in which 'ghayr' is raised with an implied 'huwa' if treated as a silah and lowered if it follows 'man'. When what follows is definite, following is the eloquent form and raising is allowed in Arabic. The editor refers to 1: 404, 190 and 405, 4: 406 and 548, and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 244-245). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 340} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 341}

**The meaning of gentleness (8119-8122, pp. 341-342).** Qatada (8119): by a mercy from God you were gentle to them. Abu Ja'far explains 'fazz' as the rough and 'ghaliz al-qalb' as the hard of heart, without mercy or compassion, and says this was not the Prophet's description, as God described him in the verse of Surat al-Tawba [[passage/quran-9-128|9:128]]. His reading is that through God's mercy and compassion for the Prophet and for the believers among his companions, he was gentle to them and his disposition was easy for them, so that he bore the harm of those who harmed him, pardoned the offence of the offender and overlooked much in those who would have left him and not followed him, nor what he was sent with of mercy, had he been harsh; but God had mercy on them and on him with them (the scan of this passage on p. 341 is badly garbled and the sense is taken from context; the point is noted here). Qatada (8120): God made him free of harshness and roughness and made him near and merciful to the believers, compassionate; we were told that his description in the Torah is that he is not harsh nor rough nor loud in the markets, does not repay an evil with the like of it but pardons and forgives. Al-Rabi' (8121) similarly, through 'Ammar. Ibn Ishaq (8122): it mentions his gentleness to them and patience with them, because of their weakness and little endurance of harshness had it come from him in all that they differed in of what was made obligatory on them in obeying their prophet (the editor notes that this report comes in the Sira after 8124). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 341} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 342}

**The meaning of 'infaddu' (8123-8124, p. 342).** Abu Ja'far: it means they would have scattered from around you. Ibn 'Abbas through Ibn Jurayj (8123): they would have turned away from you. Ibn Ishaq (8124): they would have left you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 342}

**Pardon and asking forgiveness (8125, p. 343).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is to pass over the harm that his followers and believing companions caused him, and to pray to his Lord for their forgiveness for the offence they committed and for which they deserved punishment. Ibn Ishaq (8125): pass over them and ask forgiveness for the sins of those of the people of faith who fell into them. In a footnote the editor remarks that Ibn Hisham made the word 'their sins' part of the verse and placed it in brackets with the verse's wording, which he finds strange, and indicates the place of the report in the Sira (3: 123). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 343}

**The disagreement over why he was told to consult (8126-8131, pp. 343-345).** The scholars of interpretation differed on what the Prophet was told to consult them about and why. First view: he was told to consult his companions on war stratagems and when meeting the enemy, to please their hearts, win them over to their religion and let them see that he listens to them and takes their help, although God had made him independent of them by His direction. Qatada (8126): God told him this while revelation came to him because it is more pleasing to the hearts of the people, and when people consult one another seeking God's face, He settles for them the soundest course. Al-Rabi' (8127) similarly through 'Ammar. Ibn Ishaq (8128): so that you show them you listen to them and take their help though you do not need them, winning them thereby to their religion. Second view: he was told so that the best opinion and soundest course in management would be made clear to him, because of the merit God knew in consultation. Al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim (8129), through Ibn Waki': God told him to consult only for the merit He knew in it. Al-Hasan (8130), through al-Qasim: no people ever consulted without being guided to the soundest of their affairs. In a footnote the editor gives the life of Iyas ibn Dughfal al-Harithi, Abu Dughfal, who reported from al-Hasan, Abu Nadra and 'Ata', from whom Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi and Abu 'Amir al-'Aqadi reported, and says he is reliable (thiqa) and listed in al-Tahdhib. Third view: God told him to consult his companions on what He told him to consult them on, though he was independent of their opinions by His direction, so that the believers after him would follow him in what concerned their religion, take his practice as a way and follow the example they saw him give in his life, consulting among themselves and then acting on what their assembly agreed; for when believers consult on their religion following the truth, God does not leave them without His grace and success in the right course. Those who said this compared it with the praise of the people of faith in the verse of consultation [[passage/quran-42-38|42:38]]. Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna (8131), through Sawwar ibn 'Abdallah al-'Anbari: it is for the believers, that they consult on what no report from the Prophet has reached them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 343} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 344} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 345}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 345-346).** Abu Ja'far holds that the most correct view is that God told His Prophet to consult his companions on what troubled him of his enemy's affairs and war stratagems, to win over those whose insight into Islam was not such that he was safe from Satan's temptation, and to teach his community the way matters that trouble them after him are approached and sought, so that they follow him in crises and consult among themselves as they saw him do. As for the Prophet, God taught him the aim of what troubled him by His revelation or by inspiring him with the right course; as for his community, if they consult following his practice, in mutual sincerity, seeking the truth and all wanting the right, without inclining to desire or turning from guidance, God directs and grants them success. In footnotes on p. 345 the editor comments on the grammar of 'his companions and followers' as object of 'consulting', corrects the printed text by the manuscript, explains 'tawakhkhi al-amr' as seeking and aiming at it, notes that al-Shafi'i uses it often, and refers to his brother Ahmad's note on al-Shafi'i's Risala (p. 504, note 2; the note is cut at the page end and finished at the top of p. 346). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 345} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 346}

**Resolve and reliance (8132-8134, p. 346).** Abu Ja'far: when your resolve is firm through our steadying and directing you in what troubles you of religion and world, go ahead with what we commanded, whether it agrees with your companions' views or opposes them, and rely on your Lord in all you do and leave, and accept His decree rather than the views and help of others of His creatures; those who rely are those content with His decree and submitted to His judgement, whether it agrees with their desire or opposes it. Ibn Ishaq (8132): when you resolve on a matter that came from Me, or a matter of your religion in fighting your enemy that only this can set right for you and them, go ahead as commanded, against those who differ from you and with those who agree, and rely, that is, be content with Him over the servants (from Ibn Hisham 3: 123-124, the completion of the reports ending at 8128). Qatada (8133): God told him that when he resolves on a matter he should go ahead with it, keep to God's command and rely on Him. Al-Rabi' (8134) similarly through 'Ammar. The editor notes that the 'wa' of 'wa-tawakkal' in the manuscript, printed text and Sira is allowable as it belongs to the commentary, whereas the verse has 'fa', so he placed the 'wa' outside the brackets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 346}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 346}
