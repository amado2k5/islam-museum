## Summary
Verse 264 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 264 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

In ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]) from p. 521, al-Tabari says the address is to those who believe, that is, who trust God and His Messenger, and that the prohibition is on voiding the reward of their charity with reproach and harm, as the unbelief of the one who spends his wealth to be seen by people voids it; showing off is his displaying his deed to them, spending his wealth where people see outwardly that he wants God and praise him for it, though he does not want God by it nor seek reward from Him, and he spends it outwardly only for people to praise him and say that he is generous and a good man and so speak well of him, not knowing the intention he conceals, nor that he is in denial of God and the Last Day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 521} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} The editor explains in notes that the manuscript is repaired by adding a word the sense requires, and thinks the printed text altered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 521}

Al-Tabari then explains the description of this spender as one who does not believe in God and the Last Day: he does not trust in God's oneness and lordship, nor that he will be raised after his death and requited for his deed, so as to make his deed for God's face and the seeking of His reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} He says this is the description of the hypocrite, and his reason is that one who displays his unbelief and announces his associating others with God is known not to be showing off in any of his deeds, for the one who shows off displays to people a deed that is outwardly for God while the intention of its doer within is people's praise, whereas the unbeliever's case is not hidden from anyone, that all his acts are for Satan when he proclaims his unbelief, not for God, and one so placed is not showing off by his deeds; this argument and construction are al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} The editor notes a word dropped in the manuscript that the sense requires, which he supplied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} Interpreters spoke along the lines al-Tabari gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} Amr ibn Huraith (6039): a man goes to fight, does not steal or fornicate or take plunder, and still does not come back even; asked why, he said that a man goes out and when God's trial that was decreed for him strikes, he abuses and curses his commander and the hour he went to fight and says he will never go on a raid with him again; this is against him and not for him, like spending in God's way followed by reproach and harm, and God has drawn its likeness in the Qur'an in the prohibition on voiding charity with reproach and harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523} The editor gives a biography of Abu Hani al-Khawlani and of Amr ibn Huraith, who relates from him, and corrects a reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523}

Al-Tabari then explains the likeness of this spender in ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]) from p. 523: the pronoun refers to the one who spends his wealth to be seen by people and does not believe in God and the Last Day, and his likeness is that of a smooth stone with earth on it; the word for stone is both singular and plural: for one who takes it as a plural, the singular is a different form, like date and dates and palm and palms, and for one who takes it as a singular, its plural is a different form, and he cites a line of al-Akhyal al-Ta'i about birds alighting on smooth stones. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523} The editor notes that the plural with a kasra on the first letter and no vowel on the second is al-Kisa'i's view, which was criticised and judged mistaken, and refers to al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and an earlier explanation of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523} We stop here. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's explanation and argument are attributed to him, and the corrections and references are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.
