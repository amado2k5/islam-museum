## Summary
Verse 264 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 264 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

In ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]) from p. 521, al-Tabari says the address is to those who believe, that is, who trust God and His Messenger, and that the prohibition is on voiding the reward of their charity with reproach and harm, as the unbelief of the one who spends his wealth to be seen by people voids it; showing off is his displaying his deed to them, spending his wealth where people see outwardly that he wants God and praise him for it, though he does not want God by it nor seek reward from Him, and he spends it outwardly only for people to praise him and say that he is generous and a good man and so speak well of him, not knowing the intention he conceals, nor that he is in denial of God and the Last Day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 521} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} The editor explains in notes that the manuscript is repaired by adding a word the sense requires, and thinks the printed text altered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 521}

Al-Tabari then explains the description of this spender as one who does not believe in God and the Last Day: he does not trust in God's oneness and lordship, nor that he will be raised after his death and requited for his deed, so as to make his deed for God's face and the seeking of His reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} He says this is the description of the hypocrite, and his reason is that one who displays his unbelief and announces his associating others with God is known not to be showing off in any of his deeds, for the one who shows off displays to people a deed that is outwardly for God while the intention of its doer within is people's praise, whereas the unbeliever's case is not hidden from anyone, that all his acts are for Satan when he proclaims his unbelief, not for God, and one so placed is not showing off by his deeds; this argument and construction are al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} The editor notes a word dropped in the manuscript that the sense requires, which he supplied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} Interpreters spoke along the lines al-Tabari gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} Amr ibn Huraith (6039): a man goes to fight, does not steal or fornicate or take plunder, and still does not come back even; asked why, he said that a man goes out and when God's trial that was decreed for him strikes, he abuses and curses his commander and the hour he went to fight and says he will never go on a raid with him again; this is against him and not for him, like spending in God's way followed by reproach and harm, and God has drawn its likeness in the Qur'an in the prohibition on voiding charity with reproach and harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 522} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523} The editor gives a biography of Abu Hani al-Khawlani and of Amr ibn Huraith, who relates from him, and corrects a reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523}

Al-Tabari then explains the likeness of this spender in ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]) from p. 523: the pronoun refers to the one who spends his wealth to be seen by people and does not believe in God and the Last Day, and his likeness is that of a smooth stone with earth on it; the word for stone is both singular and plural: for one who takes it as a plural, the singular is a different form, like date and dates and palm and palms, and for one who takes it as a singular, its plural is a different form, and he cites a line of al-Akhyal al-Ta'i about birds alighting on smooth stones. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523} The editor notes that the plural with a kasra on the first letter and no vowel on the second is al-Kisa'i's view, which was criticised and judged mistaken, and refers to al-Qurtubi, Abu Hayyan and an earlier explanation of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 523} We stop here. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's explanation and argument are attributed to him, and the corrections and references are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]) from p. 523. Al-Tabari says the word for the stone is the smooth rock, meaning smooth stones; that the earth on it is on the stone; and that the downpour is heavy, great rain, citing a line of Imru' al-Qays about streaming rain; one says the sky poured and it pours. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 524} The hard bare stone is a stone with nothing on it, plant or otherwise, and land where nothing grows, and likewise of heads; he cites a line of Ru'ba about a forehead whose hair has gone so it gleams like a smooth rock, the saying a cooking pot that is slow to boil, and a line of Ta'abbata Sharran denying that he is a cloud with no good in it or a bare smooth stone with no good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 524} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 525} In notes the editor explains Imru' al-Qays's line and Ru'ba's lines with their words, says that the sense of the bare stone is not found in many dictionaries, gives the variant wordings of Ta'abbata Sharran's line and explains cloud, cold and the isolated one, and corrects a reading of the manuscript; these remarks are the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 524} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 525}

Al-Tabari says he returns to the hypocrites for whose deeds the likeness was drawn: so their deeds are like the stone with earth on it, which the downpour struck, taking away the earth on it and leaving it clean with no earth; Muslims outwardly see that they have deeds, as earth is seen on this stone, through what they show them; but on the day of resurrection, when they come to God, all of it vanishes because it was not for God, as the downpour took off what was on the stone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 525} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 526} The denial of their power means that on the day of resurrection they have no power over the reward of anything they earned in the world, because they did not act for their return nor to seek what is with God in the hereafter, but to be seen by people and for their praise, and their share of their deeds is what they intended by them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 526} He adds that God does not guide the unbelieving people to hit the truth in their spending and otherwise by giving them success, since they prefer falsehood, but leaves them in their error to wander. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 526} So He addressed the believers not to be like the hypocrites whose deeds this likeness describes, voiding the reward of their charity by reproach and harm as the reward of the hypocrite's spending is void with God; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 526} The editor keeps the manuscript's reading and corrects a dropped word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 526}

Interpreters spoke along the lines of al-Tabari. Qatada (6040): this is a likeness God drew for the deeds of the unbelievers on the day of resurrection, having no power over anything they earned, as the rain left the rock with nothing on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 526} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} Al-Rabi' (6041): the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} Al-Suddi (6042): showing off took away his spending as the rain took the earth of the rock, leaving it clean; and He told the believers not to void their charity by reproach and harm so that it be voided as the showing-off charity was. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} Al-Dahhak (6043): that a man not spend his wealth is better than that he spend it and follow it with reproach and harm, and God drew the likeness of both. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} Ibn Abbas (6044): so too the hypocrite on the day of resurrection has no power over anything he earned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} Ibn Jurayj (6045): he reproaches with his charity and harms the recipient until he voids it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 528} Ibn Zayd (6046): do you think the downpour leaves any earth on the rock? So your reproach and harm leave nothing of what you spent; and he read on to ([[passage/quran-2-272|2:272]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 528} The editor's notes correct phrases of the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 527} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 528}

Al-Tabari then says he has explained the meaning of the stone sufficiently but wanted to cite those among the interpreters who said as he did: Ibn Abbas (6047), like the rock; al-Dahhak (6048), the stone is the rock; al-Rabi' (6049), the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 528} These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's explanation is attributed to him, and the corrections and notes on the lines are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]) from p. 528. Al-Tabari cites those who spoke as he did on the three words. The stone: al-Suddi (6050), Qatada (6051) and Ibn Abbas (6052), that it is the rock called by that name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 529} The downpour: al-Suddi (6053), al-Dahhak (6054), Qatada (6055) and al-Rabi' (6056), heavy rain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 529} The bare stone: al-Suddi (6057), clean; Ibn Abbas (6058, 6059, 6062), he left it clean with nothing on it; al-Dahhak (6060), he left it bare; Qatada (6061), nothing on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 529} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 530} This completes al-Tabari's comment on the likeness of the one who spends to be seen in ([[passage/quran-2-264|2:264]]), by the explanation and choice given before.
