## Summary
Surat al-Fath: surah 48 in the order of the Mushaf, Medinan, 29 verses, and revelation order 111 among the surahs. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Content
Its verses are recorded in the museum one by one under the records passage/quran-48-‹verse number›.

## In Tantawi's al-Tafsir al-Wasit
In the "introduction and preface" to the sura in [[work/al-tafsir-al-wasit|al-Tafsir al-Wasit]], [[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Tantawi]] says that it is Medinan, has 29 verses and came down after [[event/treaty-of-hudaybiyya|the truce of al-Hudaybiyya]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5763} He quotes [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] that it came down when the Prophet returned from al-Hudaybiyya in Dhu al-Qa'da of year 6 AH, after the idolaters had barred him from the Sacred Mosque when he meant to perform the lesser pilgrimage and then inclined to a settlement under which he would go back that year and come the next; he agreed to it "despite the reluctance of a group of the Companions". {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5763}

He observes that many suras and verses came down after campaigns, for the Muslims to learn from them: [[passage/quran-8|al-Anfal]] after [[event/battle-of-badr|Badr]], dozens of verses of [[passage/quran-3|Al Imran]] after [[event/battle-of-uhud|Uhud]], verses of al-Hashr after [[event/expedition-of-banu-al-nadir|the Banu al-Nadir]], verses of al-Ahzab after [[event/battle-of-the-trench|the Confederates]], and this sura after al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5763} He then summarises the story of the truce: the Prophet's dream that he had entered the Sacred Mosque; his setting out with some fourteen hundred Companions carrying no weapons but sheathed swords, and driving the sacrificial animals with them; Bishr ibn Sufyan al-Ka'bi's report at Usfan that Quraysh were preparing to fight; their taking a rough route shown them by a man of Aslam until they camped at al-Hudaybiyya; the kneeling of the she-camel al-Qaswa'; the Prophet's saying that whatever plan Quraysh proposed asking him to honour ties of kinship he would grant; and then Quraysh's envoys, the first of them Budayl ibn Warqa' al-Khuza'i. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5763} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.5764}
