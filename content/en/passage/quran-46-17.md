## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase: "And he who said to his parents, 'Woe to you, do you promise me that I will go out, while centuries before me have passed,' while they were seeking help from Allah. Woe to you, I believe. Indeed, the promise of Allah is true." He says, 'These are nothing but legends of the first people.' This is a description from Allah, the Exalted, a misguided disbeliever, and his parents disobedient, and they are diligent in advising him and praying to Allah. Their prayers and advice to him only increase him to the truth, and their advice to him is only out of rebellion and rebellion against Allah, and persistence in His ignorance. Allah, the Exalted, says: 'And He who said to his parents' His call to faith in God, to acknowledge that God sent His creation from their graves, and to reward them for their deeds—'Woe to you' says: 'Filthy to you while we are rotten.' 'Do you promise me to come out?' He says, 'Do you promise me to come out alive after my destruction and affliction in it?' Also: And from Qatadah, 'Do you promise me to be released?' to be resurrected after death. From Muammar, from Qatadah, regarding the phrase: 'Do you promise me to be released?' he said: It means resurrection after death. In the Maqta', he quotes statements from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, and Al-Hasan.
