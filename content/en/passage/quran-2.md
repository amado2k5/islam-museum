## Summary
Surat al-Baqara: surah 2 in the order of the Mushaf, Medinan, 286 verses, and revelation order 87 among the surahs. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Content
Its verses are recorded in the museum one by one under the records passage/quran-2-‹verse number›.

## In Tantawi's al-Tafsir al-Wasit
[[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Muhammad Sayyid Tantawi]] opens his commentary on the sura in [[work/al-tafsir-al-wasit|al-Tafsir al-Wasit]] by saying that it is the longest sura of the Qur'an, filling about two and a half of its thirty parts, and that it has 286 verses, or by another view 287. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.27} It is named al-Baqara (the Cow) because it alone tells the story of the cow that Moses' people were ordered to slaughter after a man was killed among them and his killer was unknown. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.27} He holds that it is Medinan by consensus: its revelation began after [[event/hijra|the Hijra]], most of it came down in the first years after it, and its revelation continued until shortly before the Prophet's death; it contains the last verse of the Qur'an to be revealed, [[passage/quran-2-281|al-Baqara 281]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.27}

He sees a clear correspondence between it and [[passage/quran-1|al-Fatiha]]: al-Fatiha sums up the themes of divinity, worship and the request for guidance, and al-Baqara sets them out in detail. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.27} He cites hadith on its merit, among them Abu Hurayra's report in Ahmad, Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i that Satan does not enter a house in which Surat al-Baqara is recited, and quotes [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]] that it is called "the tent of the Qur'an" for its greatness and its many rulings and lessons. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.27} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.28} He then summarises its themes quarter by quarter: the division of people before the Qur'an's guidance into believers, unbelievers and hypocrites; the call to all people to worship God alone and the challenge to produce a sura like it; the story of [[person/adam|Adam]]; and then a long treatment of the Children of Israel in more than a hundred verses, since it was revealed in Medina, where many Jews lived. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.28} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.29} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.30}
