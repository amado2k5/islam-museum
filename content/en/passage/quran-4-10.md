## Summary
Verse 10 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 10 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 10 (pp. 26-30, reports 8722-8724).** Abu Jafar says those who consume orphans' property unjustly means without right, and that what they swallow into their bellies, on the Day of Resurrection, is fire, the fire of Hell, because of their eating the orphans' wealth wrongfully in this world, and they will burn in a blazing fire for it. Al-Suddi (8722, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat): when a man gets up eating an orphan's wealth wrongfully, he will be raised on the Day of Resurrection with the flame of fire coming out of his mouth, his ears, his nose and his eyes, and whoever sees him knows him as one who ate an orphan's wealth (the editor corrects forms in the printing and manuscript in three places here). Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (8723, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq from Mamar from Abu Harun al-Abdi): the Prophet told us about the night he was taken up, saying: I looked and there was a people with lips like camels' lips, and someone appointed over them took hold of their lips and put pieces of fire into their mouths which came out of their lower parts; I said, Jibril, who are these? He said: those who consume orphans' property unjustly. In the editor's note, Abu Harun al-Abdi is Umara ibn Juwayn, who narrated from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and Ibn Umar, and is weak and was called a liar; al-Daraqutni said he changes colours, a Kharijite and a Shi'i, and Ibn Hibban said he narrated from Abu Sa'id what was not his hadith and that his hadith may not be written down except for wonder (al-Tahdhib); and the report is recorded by Ibn Kathir in his commentary (2: 360) and by al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 124), who attributes it to Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim. Ibn Zayd (8724, through Yunus from Ibn Wahb) said that his father said: this is about the polytheists, when they did not give inheritance to them and ate their wealth. Al-Tabari then explains that the word translated "will burn" comes from a root for warming oneself at a fire, and cites a line by al-Farazdaq describing the dog of the clan fighting for its family's fire to lie by it; the word is then used of anyone who personally takes part in war, fighting or a quarrel, and he cites a line of al-Harith ibn Ubbad al-Bakri from the War of al-Basus and another of al-Ajjaj (poetry described, not reproduced). In the editor's long notes (pp. 27-29), the line is attributed in Lisan al-Arab to Imru al-Qays, an error he corrects; he traces it to al-Farazdaq's diwan, to al-Naqa'id and to Lisan; says it comes from lines describing days of cold and drought and praising his tribe, and explains their words; traces al-Ajjaj's line in his diwan to his famous long poem and explains the word for hearth stones and the root; and says al-Harith ibn Ubbad's line is among his famous lines from the War of al-Basus, in which he stood aside until his son Bujayr was sent to Muhalhil, who killed him (with references to al-Fakhir of al-Mufaddal ibn Salama and al-Khizana); the printing has an incorrect form in places and the manuscript and the diwan are right. Al-Tabari says the poet makes what he went through of the hardship of war like the touch of fire and its heat. The reciters differed: most reciters of Medina and Iraq read the verb with a fatha on the first letter, in the sense given, while some Meccans and some Kufans read it with a damma, meaning "be burned", from the phrase for a roasted sheep. Al-Tabari says the fatha is preferable, because all reciters agree on a fatha in [[passage/quran-92-15|92:15]] and because [[passage/quran-37-163|37:163]] indicates that the fatha is the better. As for the blaze, it is the intensity of Hell's heat; one says the war "blazed" when it grew fierce; it was originally a passive form and was turned to the form of an adjective, as in "a dyed hand" and "an oiled beard", which really have passive forms; so the sense is: they will burn in a kindled fire, lit and flaming with fierce heat, because God described it as kindled in [[passage/quran-81-12|81:12]], and then told that the eaters of orphans' wealth burn in it in that state; so the word here is a description of Hell. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 27} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 28} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 29} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 30} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 30}
