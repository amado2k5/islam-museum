## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And do not kill the soul which Allah has forbidden except by right. And whoever is killed unjustly - We have made for his heir authority, so let him not exceed limits in killing; indeed, he is ever victorious": He also decreed that "do not kill," O people, "the soul that Allah has forbidden" to kill "except by right," and it has the right not to be killed except for disbelief after Islam, or adultery after chastity, or killing a soul. Even if it is a disbeliever whose disbelief has not preceded Islam, then it is a covenant and safety for her not to be killed beforehand, as follows: And from Qatadah, regarding His saying, "And do not kill the soul which Allah has forbidden except by right," and by Allah, we do not know of the permissibility of the blood of a Muslim except in one of three cases, except for a man who was deliberately killed and is liable to sacrifice, or adultery after chastity is subject to stoning, or a disbeliever after Islam is punishable by death. From al-Zuhri, from Urwah or others, he said: It was said to Abu Bakr: Do you kill anyone who thinks he should not pay zakat? He said: If they withheld any of what they acknowledged for the Messenger of Allah, I would have killed them. In the Maqta', he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, and al-Dhahhak.
