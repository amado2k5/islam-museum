## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "And We set up the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be wronged in the least, even if it is the weight of a mustard seed. We bring it forth. And sufficient is Us as accountants": "And We will set the scales" — justice, which means "justice." He made justice, which is a unified adjective of scales, and it is a plural because it is in the doctrine of justice, contentment, and consideration. And His saying: "for the Day of Resurrection" means the people of the Day of Resurrection, and whoever is brought to God on that day from among His creation. Some Arabs interpreted this as "fi" as if its meaning is: "We will set the scales of justice on the Day of Resurrection." And His saying: "So no soul will be wronged in the least," it means: God will not wrong any soul among those who have been wronged by something by punishing it for a sin it did not commit or by depriving it of the reward of its deeds and obedience to it, but the doer of good is rewarded with goodness, and no wrongdoer is punished except for his wrongdoing. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Ibn Zayd.
