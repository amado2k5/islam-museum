## Summary
Verse 253 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 253 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens ([[passage/quran-2-253|2:253]]) on p. 378: the pointing word refers to the messengers whose stories God recounted in this chapter, such as Moses son of Amram, Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, Samuel, David and the rest whose accounts are given in it; he says God favoured some over others, speaking to some, and the one He spoke to among them was Moses, and raised some by degrees in honour and standing; this is his interpretation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 378} From Mujahid (5755, the number being unclear in the scan, followed by 5756 to like effect), explaining the favouring: God spoke to Moses and sent Muhammad to all people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 378} We stop at the last page read.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-253|2:253]]) from p. 379: as support for what he said about the favouring of the messengers, al-Tabari cites (5757), without a chain, a saying of the Prophet that he was given five things given to none before him: he was sent to the red and the black, he was helped by fear cast in the enemy at a month's distance, the earth was made a place of prayer and a means of purification for him, spoils were made lawful to him, and he was told to ask and it would be granted, and he kept his request in reserve as intercession for his community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 379} In the note the editor says it is from Ibn Abbas, found in the Musnad and elsewhere, with a different wording in al-Bukhari and Muslim, and judges it sound; this is his grading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 379}

Al-Tabari then explains what Jesus son of Mary was given: proofs and evidences of his prophethood, such as healing the born-blind and the leper and giving life to the dead, with the Gospel sent down to him; he glosses the word for strengthening as support and help, says that the Holy Spirit is Gabriel, and refers to his earlier discussion of the differences over the meaning of the Holy Spirit and his preferred view there, which he does not repeat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 380} This is his interpretation.

Al-Tabari then explains the conditional about the fighting of those after the messengers: had God willed, those who came after the messengers He had favoured, and after Jesus, would not have fought, though signs had reached them that deter whoever God guides; the clear proofs are what made the truth plain to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 380} It has been said that the pronoun refers to Moses and Jesus, and so it is reported from Qatada (5758) and from al-Rabi' (5759): after Moses and Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 380} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 381}

On the next part, al-Tabari says: those who came after the messengers differed, since God did not will that they not fight, and fought after the clear proofs had come from their Lord forbidding fighting and discord and after the proof of God's oneness and His messengers' mission was established against them; some disbelieved and some believed, and they committed disbelief and sins knowingly, deliberately. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 381} He says that had God willed to protect them and grant them success and so restrain them from disobedience, they would neither have fought nor differed, but the outcome rests on His decision, granting success in obedience to one so that he believes and obeys, and abandoning another so that he disbelieves and disobeys; this is his interpretation and argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 381} In the notes the editor corrects wording and a scribal slip; these are his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 379} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 381}
