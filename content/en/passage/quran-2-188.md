## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "do not consume one another's wealth among yourselves by falsehood, nor offer it to the rulers so that you may consume part of other people's wealth sinfully while you know", al-Tabari says the meaning is that some of you should not consume the property of others by falsehood: God made one who consumes his brother's property by falsehood like one who consumes his own. He compares "do not defame yourselves" and "do not kill yourselves", meaning do not defame or kill one another, since God made the believers brothers, and says the Arabs likewise refer to themselves by their brothers, citing the verse "my brother and yours in the hollow of al-Nusayr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 548} The editor discusses the verse and its attribution and notes disagreement over its author and wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 548} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 549} Al-Tabari says consuming it by falsehood means taking it by other than the way God made lawful for those who take it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 549} "Nor offer it to the rulers" means to take it in dispute to the rulers, to consume a portion of other people's wealth sinfully while you know; "sinfully" means by the forbidden that God has forbidden, and "while you know" means you do it deliberately, knowing it is disobedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 549} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 550}

From Ibn 'Abbas, this concerns a man who owes money with no proof against him, and denies it and takes the dispute to the rulers while knowing the right is against him, knowing he is a sinner consuming the forbidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 550} From Mujahid, do not dispute while you are in the wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 550} From Qatada, it used to be said that whoever goes with his opponent while he is in the wrong is a sinner until he returns to the right; the judge's ruling does not make lawful for you what is forbidden or make true what is false, for a judge rules by what he sees and what the witnesses testify, and he is a man who errs and is right; and one for whom a ruling is given falsely, his dispute is not ended until God brings them together on the Day of Resurrection and rules for the one in the right against the one in the wrong in a better way than he was ruled for in this world. The editor corrects the printed wording from Ibn Kathir's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 550}
