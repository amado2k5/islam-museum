**Commentary on verse 94: "God will test you with some game within reach of your hands and spears", pp. 582-585**

Al-Tabari says: you who believed God and His messenger, God will test you with something of game, that is, some of the game; He said "something" because He did not test them with sea game but with land game, so the test is with some and not all. "Within reach of your hands and spears" means either by hand, like eggs and chicks, or by the hit of arrow and spear, like wild asses, cattle and gazelles, testing you with it while you are in the sacred state for lesser or greater pilgrimage.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 583}

Reports 12537 to 12539, from Mujahid, say the hands reach small game, taking chicks and eggs, and the spears reach big game; 12540 and 12541, from him, say it is game that cannot flee; report 12542, from Ibn Abbas, says it is the weak and small game with which God tests His servants in their sacred state, so that if they wished they could take it by hand, and He forbade them to go near it; and 12543, from Mujahid, says chicks and eggs and what cannot flee.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 584}

On "that God may know who fears Him unseen", al-Tabari says the sense is that God tests you, believers, with some game in your sacred state so that He may know those who obey Him and believe in Him and keep to His limits, command and prohibition, and who fears Him and so guards against what He forbade and avoids it for fear of His punishment "unseen", meaning in this world where he does not see Him. "Unseen" is the verbal noun of a matter being absent from me, and the Arabs call what is not seen "unseen". So the sense is that God's friends know who fears God and guards against what He forbade of game and other things, without seeing or witnessing Him. "Whoever transgresses after that" means whoever exceeds God's limit after being tried by the prohibition of game while in the sacred state, and treats as lawful what God forbade by taking and killing it; "has a painful punishment" means a hurting and distressing one.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 585}
