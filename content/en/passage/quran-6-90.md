## Summary
Verse 90 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 90 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "those are they whom God guided, so follow their guidance" al-Tabari says: those, these people to whom we entrusted our signs and who do not disbelieve in them, are those whom God guided to his true religion, to keeping what they were charged with keeping of the verses of his book, to upholding its limits, following its lawful and forbidden, acting on what God's command is in it and refraining from what he forbids in it, and God, glorified be his praise, gave them success in that; "so follow their guidance" says: by the work they did and the course they followed, by the guidance we guided them to and the success we gave them, "follow", Muhammad, that is, act, take to it and walk in it, for it is work for God in which there is pleasure and a course by which whoever follows it is guided. This interpretation is on the view of those who took "we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it" to be the prophets named in the earlier verses, which is the view we chose. As for the view of those who took the people entrusted with it to be the people of Medina or the angels, they made "if these disbelieve in it, we have entrusted it to a people who do not disbelieve in it" an aside between the two statements, and then joined "those are they whom God guided, so follow their guidance" to "those are they to whom we gave the book, the judgement and prophethood". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 519}

13531, from Ibn Jurayj: "we gave him Isaac and Jacob" to "those are they whom God guided, so follow their guidance", Muhammad; 13532, from Ibn Zayd: "those are they whom God guided", Muhammad, "so follow their guidance", and do not follow these; 13533, from al-Suddi: then he returned to the Prophet and said "those are they whom God guided, so follow their guidance"; 13534, from Ibn Abbas: then he said of the prophets he named in this verse "so follow their guidance". The sense of following a man in Arab speech is following his track and taking his guidance; one says "so-and-so follows so-and-so" when he heads his way and follows his trace, "qidat", "qudwa", "qidwa" and "qidya". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 520}

The editor says the printed text wrote "qudwa" in place of "qidya", a plain error against the manuscript, which is right.

On "say: I ask you no reward for it; it is only a reminder to the worlds" he says: say to these whom I told you to remind by my signs lest a soul be given up for what it earned, of the polytheists of your people, Muhammad: "I ask you no reward" for my reminding you and the guidance I call you to and the Qur'an I brought you, no compensation that I take from you and no wage that I receive from you; that is from me only a reminder to you and to everyone like you who persists in falsehood, of God's might coming on you and his anger descending on you for your associating others with him and your unbelief, and a warning to you all before a hard punishment, that you may remember and be held back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 520}

The editor refers for "reward" and "reminder" to earlier passages.

Verse 90 closes here on p. 520, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-91|6:91]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
