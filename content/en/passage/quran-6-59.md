## Summary
Verse 59 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 59 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "with him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them but he; and he knows what is in land and sea" al-Tabari says: with God are the keys of the unseen. "Mafatih" is the plural of "miftah" or "miftah", and whoever says "miftah" pluralises it "mafatih" while whoever says "miftah" pluralises "mafatih". By "with him are the keys of the unseen" he means the storehouses of the unseen. Report 13305, from al-Suddi: the storehouses of the unseen. 13306, from Ibn Mas'ud: your prophet was given everything but the keys of the unseen. 13307, from Ibn Abbas: they are five: "with God is the knowledge of the Hour and he sends down the rain" to "God is knowing, aware", [[passage/quran-31-34|31:34]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 402}

The editor says the printed text has "he says: with him are the keys of the unseen" and the manuscript is right; identifies Abdullah ibn Salama al-Muradi, a trusted Follower, a jurist of Kufa after the Companions; and says the chain of 13307 is sound and Ahmad related it in his Musnad.

So the sense is: God knows best the wrongdoers among his creatures, what they deserve and what he will do with them, for with him is knowledge of what is hidden altogether from his creatures, which they have not been informed of and have not grasped and will never know; "and he knows what is in land and sea" says: and with him is also knowledge of what is not hidden from you, since what is on land and sea that is plain to the eye the servants know; so the sense is: with God is knowledge of what is hidden from you, people, which you do not know and will never know, which he kept for himself, and with that he also knows all that you all know, nothing hidden from him, for there is nothing but what is hidden from people or not hidden from them. So he reported that with him is knowledge of everything that was and will be and what will be that has not yet been, and that is the unseen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 402}

The editor keeps "and they will never know it nor grasp it" as the context requires, in place of the wording in print and manuscript, and refers for "the unseen" to p. 371.

On "no leaf falls but he knows it, nor a grain in the darknesses of the earth, nor anything wet or dry, but it is in a clear book" al-Tabari says: no leaf falls in the deserts and wilds or in the cities and villages but God knows it, "nor a grain in the darknesses of the earth, nor anything wet or dry, but it is in a clear book" says: nor anything else of what exists or will exist and does not yet, but it is recorded in the preserved tablet, written in it, its number and total drawn up, with the time at which it comes to be and the state in which it passes away. By "clear" he means that it makes plain the truth of what is in it, by the existence of what was drawn up in it as it was drawn up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 403}

Al-Tabari raises a question: what is the point of recording in the preserved tablet and clear book what is not hidden from him, who knows it all and whose forgetting is not to be feared? He answers that God does what he wills; it may be a trial for those who keep it and a test for those charged with writing down the servants' deeds, who are told to write them and then to compare them with what God recorded in the preserved tablet, and it was said that this is the sense of [[passage/quran-45-29|45:29]] on having what they did copied down; or it may be for another reason that he knows best, either an argument to bring against some of his angels or against the children of Adam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 403}

Report 13308, from Abdullah ibn al-Harith: there is no tree on earth, nor the place of a needle's prick, but an angel is set over it who brings God knowledge of it: its dryness when it dries and its moisture when it is moist. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 404}

The editor says the printed text has a different wording and the manuscript is right, the printer's change being meddling; he identifies Ziyad ibn Yahya al-Hassani Abu al-Khattab, trusted, whom the authors of the six books cite, and corrects the manuscript's and Ibn Kathir's attribution to Abdullah; Malik ibn Sa'ir al-Tamimi, whom critics split between trusting and weakening; and Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad, client of Abdullah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal al-Hashimi, who is trusted. He notes that Ibn Kathir gave the report through Ibn Abi Hatim, and that al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur added Ibn Abi Shayba and Abu al-Shaykh.

Verse 59 closes here at p. 404, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-60|6:60]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
