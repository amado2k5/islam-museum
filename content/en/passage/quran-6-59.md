## Summary
Verse 59 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 59 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "with him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them but he; and he knows what is in land and sea" al-Tabari says: with God are the keys of the unseen. "Mafatih" is the plural of "miftah" or "miftah", and whoever says "miftah" pluralises it "mafatih" while whoever says "miftah" pluralises "mafatih". By "with him are the keys of the unseen" he means the storehouses of the unseen. Report 13305, from al-Suddi: the storehouses of the unseen. 13306, from Ibn Mas'ud: your prophet was given everything but the keys of the unseen. 13307, from Ibn Abbas: they are five: "with God is the knowledge of the Hour and he sends down the rain" to "God is knowing, aware", [[passage/quran-31-34|31:34]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 402}

The editor says the printed text has "he says: with him are the keys of the unseen" and the manuscript is right; identifies Abdullah ibn Salama al-Muradi, a trusted Follower, a jurist of Kufa after the Companions; and says the chain of 13307 is sound and Ahmad related it in his Musnad.

So the sense is: God knows best the wrongdoers among his creatures, what they deserve and what he will do with them, for with him is knowledge of what is hidden altogether from his creatures, which they have not been informed of and have not grasped and will never know; "and he knows what is in land and sea" says: and with him is also knowledge of what is not hidden from you, since what is on land and sea that is plain to the eye the servants know; so the sense is: with God is knowledge of what is hidden from you, people, which you do not know and will never know, which he kept for himself, and with that he also knows all that you all know, nothing hidden from him, for there is nothing but what is hidden from people or not hidden from them. So he reported that with him is knowledge of everything that was and will be and what will be that has not yet been, and that is the unseen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 402}

The editor keeps "and they will never know it nor grasp it" as the context requires, in place of the wording in print and manuscript, and refers for "the unseen" to p. 371.

We stop here at the foot of p. 402, after the commentary on "and he knows what is in land and sea", and al-Tabari goes on to the rest of verse 59 on p. 403.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
