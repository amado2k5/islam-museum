## Summary
Verse 66 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 66 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse (p. 525, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that had God made it obligatory for those who claim to believe in what was sent down to Muhammad, those who take their cases to the taghut, to kill themselves, and had he commanded it, or to leave their homes and emigrate to another abode, only a small number of them would have done it; that is, they would not have killed themselves with their own hands, nor emigrated to God and his Messenger in obedience. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of the verb for decreeing, at p. 170 of this volume, note 1. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 525}

**Reports 9918-9921 (pp. 525-526, note 1).** Muhammad b. Amr (9918, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that the Jews are meant, "or a word resembling it", and the Arabs, as the companions of Moses were commanded. The editor says the print and al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur dropped the words "or a word resembling it" and added a pronoun at the start, and that the words mean a word close in sense to the verb "he means", such as "he intends". Al-Muthanna (9919, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid likewise, that the companions of Moses were ordered to kill one another (the print says by the throats). Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9920, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that Thabit b. Qays b. Shammas and a Jewish man boasted; the Jew said God decreed that we kill ourselves and we did, and Thabit said had it been decreed on us we would have done it; the next clause of the same verse came down about this. Al-Muthanna (9921, from Ishaq, from Abu Zuhayr, from Isma'il) reports from Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i that when the verse came down a man said, had we been ordered we would have done it, and praise be to God who spared us; this reached the Prophet, who said that among my community are men in whose hearts faith is firmer than the rooted mountains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 526}

**The grammarians on the nominative of the exception (pp. 526-527, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the people of Arabic differed over why the word after the exception is in the nominative. Some Basran grammarians claimed it is a substitute for the names implied in the verb; some Kufan grammarians said it is nominative on the intent of repetition, and gave as an example a well-known line of poetry about a brother parted from his brother except the two stars called the Farqadan. The editor says this line is rightly ascribed to Hadrami b. Amir al-Asadi and is wrongly ascribed to Sawwar b. al-Mudarrib, lists the works that cite it (Sibawayh, Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, al-Bayan wa'l-Tabyin, al-Kamil and others), and says he did not find the lines of Amr b. Ma'dikarib that al-Tabari mentions. He gives lines that come before the line cited and glosses rare words in them: the one whose parting brings grief, turning the soul away, grieved me, the great calamity, the companion soul that stays with its owner until death, and a horse of easy turn and frequent running. (Note: the line order on the scan of p. 527 is scrambled, with the lines of poetry and their notes mixed into al-Tabari's text.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 527}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 527).** Al-Tabari says the most correct of the views is that the word is in the nominative by the sense the clause indicates, since the meaning is that had it been decreed on them, only a small number of them would have done it, and the verb is put in the plural for the people mentioned earlier because the act is theirs; he then cites a line of Amr b. Ma'dikarib and calls it good poetry; his argument continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 527}

**The rest of al-Tabari's choice (p. 528, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari completes his argument: the speech returns to those already mentioned as claiming belief, and then excepts the small number from them, so the word is in the nominative by the sense he described, since the act is negated of it. He adds that in the Syrian codices the word reads in the accusative, and that if it is read so its reader is not at fault in his grammar, because that is the well-known usage of the Arabs when the verb is occupied with something containing a pronoun for those already mentioned and the small number is then excepted from them. The editor glosses the word for fault as misfortune, toil and harm, says the print and the manuscript both have an unintelligible phrase here, explains that the "kinaya" is the pronoun, and refers to Abu Ubayda's remark in Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 528}

**Commentary on the second part of the verse (pp. 528-529, notes 3-5, report 9922).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that had these hypocrites, who claim belief while taking their cases to the taghut and turning away from you in aversion, done what they are reminded of, obedience to God and stopping at his command, there would be good for them in their near world and in the final return, and they would be firmer and steadier in their affairs (the editor says that had he said "stronger for them in it" he would have preferred it, though both are right). For the hypocrite acts on doubt, so his work goes for nothing, his toil melts into dust, and he acts in slackness and weakness; had he acted with insight he would have earned a reward from his work, a store with God, and been stronger in his work and steadier in himself through faith in God's promise for his obedience. For this reason some said the sense of the word for steadying is confirmation of belief; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9922, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that it is confirmation, for when he believes he is steadier in himself and his resolve is sounder. Al-Tabari says this parallels [[passage/quran-2-265|2:265]] and that he explained it in its place sufficiently not to repeat it (the editor refers to that verse's commentary in volume 5). The editor also refers, in notes 3 and 4, to the earlier interpretation of the admonition word (p. 299, note 4) and of the steadying word (page numbers scrambled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 529}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 529}
