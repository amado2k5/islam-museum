## Summary
Verse 24 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 24 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "He said: go down, some of you enemies to others; on earth you will have a dwelling place and enjoyment for a time", he says: this is a report from God of what He did with Iblis and his offspring, Adam and his children and the serpent; God says to Adam, Eve, Iblis and the serpent, go down from heaven to earth, some of you enemies of others. He gives (14413) from al-Suddi that He cursed the serpent, cut off its legs, left it to go on its belly and made its provision of dust, and Adam, Eve, Iblis and the serpent were sent down to earth; and (14414) from Abu Salih that they were Adam, Eve and the serpent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 358}

"On earth you will have a dwelling place" means, Adam, Eve, Iblis and serpent, you have on earth a place of settled rest that you settle in and a bed you spread; he gives (14415) from Abu al-Aliya that this is His words in [[passage/quran-2-22|2:22]], "who made the earth a bed for you"; and it is reported from Ibn Abbas, (14416), that "dwelling place" means the graves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 358}

Al-Tabari says the right view is that God told Adam, Eve, Iblis and the serpent, when they were sent down to earth, that they are enemies to one another and that they have on earth a dwelling in which to settle, and He did not limit it to a dwelling while they live and not after their death, but made the report general, that they have a dwelling in it, so it stands in its generality: it is their settled place in their lives on its back and after their death within it, as He said in [[passage/quran-77-25|77:25]], "have We not made the earth a receptacle for the living and the dead?" As for "and enjoyment for a time", He says you have enjoyment in it that you enjoy until the end of the world, which is the "time" He mentioned; he gives (14417) from Ibn Abbas that it means until the Day of Resurrection and the end of the world. "Hin" is itself a time, but of unknown measure, as shown by Jarir's line whose sense is "a time, not a time"; the note says Sibawayh cited it for dropping "la" and annexing the first "hin" to the second, and that what al-Tabari says is Abu Ubayda's view in Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 359}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
