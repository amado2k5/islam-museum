**Commentary on verse 17: the refutation of the Messiah's divinity and God's dominion (pp. 146-150)**

On 'those who say that God is the Messiah, son of Mary, have disbelieved', al-Tabari says this is God's blame of the Christians and of Christianity, who turned aside from the paths of peace, and an argument for His Prophet Muhammad against their lie in claiming a son for God. The meaning is: I swear that those who said God is the Messiah son of Mary have disbelieved, their disbelief being their covering of the truth by not denying a child to God and claiming the Messiah is God, a lie and slander against Him. He has explained the meaning of 'the Messiah' earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 146}

Then 'say: who has any power against God if He wished to destroy the Messiah, son of Mary, and his mother and everyone on earth?': God tells His Prophet to say to the Christians who lied and strayed from the right way by saying God is the Messiah son of Mary: who has any power over God, that is, who could hold back anything of God's command and turn it away once He decreed it. The wording comes from the saying 'I have power over so-and-so's affair' when someone cannot carry out an order except through another, and the editor remarks that this explanation is rarely found in the dictionaries. 'If He wished to destroy the Messiah, his mother and everyone on earth' means who can turn back any of God's command should He will to destroy them all by removing them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 147}

God tells His Prophet to say to these ignorant Christians: if the Messiah were as you claim, that he is God, which he is not, he could have turned away God's command when it came to destroy him and his mother; yet He destroyed his mother and he could not stop His command concerning her. In this is a lesson for you if you take it and a proof against you if you reason, that the Messiah is a human being like the rest of Adam's children, and that God is the One who is not overcome or forced and whose command is not turned back, the Living, the Everlasting, who gives life and death, brings into being and brings to an end, and does not die. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 148}

Then 'to God belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what is between them; He creates what He wills': God directs all that is in the heavens and the earth and between them, destroying whom He wills and keeping what He wills, bringing into being what He wishes and removing what He pleases, with no hindrance to hold Him and no one to turn Him back, carrying out His judgement and decree on them, unlike the Messiah who, if his Lord willed to destroy him and his mother, would have no power to repel it. How could one be a god to be worshipped who is unable to repel the harm another intends for him? The god worshipped is the One who owns everything and directs all in heaven, earth and between them. He said 'between them' although he named the heavens in the plural, because the sense is what lies between these two kinds of things, and al-Tabari cites a poet's line, by al-Ra'i, where a dual is used and the sense then returns to the plural. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 148}

In the note the editor explains that the lines come from a poem in which al-Ra'i addresses his daughter Khulayda about the cares he spent the night with, glosses its words for cares, young pregnant she-camels and those that did not conceive, and explains the point of the proof; the museum describes it and does not reproduce it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 149}

On 'He creates what He wills', al-Tabari says He brings into being what He wills and brings it from nothing into existence, which no one but God, the One, the Subduer, can do. He means that He alone arranges and directs the heavens and the earth and between them, ends and removes, and brings into being what is not yet there; so how, you liars, do you claim the Messiah is a god when he can do none of that, cannot even keep harm from himself or his mother or draw benefit to her except by His leave? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 149}

On 'and God has power over all things' he says: God, the one worshipped, is able over everything and owns everything, nothing He wills defeats Him and nothing He seeks overcomes Him, and He is able to destroy the Messiah, his mother and everyone on earth, not the helpless one who cannot keep from himself a harm sent by God nor keep his mother from perishing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 150}

We stop here at the end of the commentary on verse 17 on p. 150; verse 18 begins on the same page.
