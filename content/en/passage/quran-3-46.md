## Summary
Verse 46 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 46 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The circumstantial accusative and the sense.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God gives Mary tidings of the Word whose name has been given, eminent with God and addressing people while an infant in his resting place; the verb, though it has the nominative form because it is in the shape of the imperfect free of governing particles, stands in the place of the accusative. He cites a line of an unnamed rajaz poet, whom the editor could not identify, about a man who feeds his camels at night with a sharp sword. The editor refers to al-Farra (1:213), Ibn al-Shajari's dictations (2:167) and the Khizana (2:345), and explains the verses as describing a generous man who slaughters his camels when guests arrive, with the cutting sword and the one that cleaves the stroke. Al-Tabari says the word for the cradle means the infant's resting place while suckling, and report 7071 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abbas) says so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 416} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 417}

**The mature age.** Al-Tabari says the word means one past youth and before old age, said of a man and of a woman, and cites a line of Udhafir al-Fuqaymi, as the editor identifies the poet. In a note the editor tells the story: Udhafir used to hire out his camels to Mecca, and a man of Hanifa from Basra hired a camel for himself and his wife, named Sha'far, so he composed a rajaz about them; the editor explains some words of the lines. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 417} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 418}

**What the speech shows.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that Jesus addresses people as a child in his resting place, as evidence of his mother's innocence of what the slanderers charged her with and as proof of his prophethood, and as a full-grown adult after maturity, through God's revelation, His command and prohibition and the book sent down to him. The editor remarks on two readings: the printed text has a reading that cannot be right where the manuscript's word is badly written but clear, and the printed 'accused' differs from the manuscript as noted earlier. Al-Tabari says God told His servants this although people usually speak as adults and old men, as an argument against the Christians who say falsehood of him: from when He created him he was a child, then an adult, subject to events and changing with the passing of times and days from small to large and state to state, which would not be possible for him had he been what the heretics say. So the claim of the delegation of Najran who argued with the Messenger of God about him is belied, and the Messenger argued against them with it and told them that he was like other children of Adam except for the honour with which God distinguished him. The editor says he put the phrase 'since He created him' in brackets because after long effort he could find no sense in the words of the manuscript and print and suspected a miscopying, corruption or omission. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 418} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 419}

**Reports.** Report 7072 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says God tells them of the states through which he turned in his life as the sons of Adam turn through their ages, small and large, except that God distinguished him with speech in the cradle as a sign of his prophethood and a showing to servants of the places of His power. Report 7073 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says he addresses them young and grown, as does 7074 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi). Report 7075 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the mature age means the forbearing; 7076 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says he addressed them young, grown and mature, and Mujahid said the mature one is the forbearing. Report 7077 (printed 7017 in the OCR, numbered here from context) (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says he addressed them as a boy in the cradle and as a grown man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 419}

**The other view and the righteous.** Al-Tabari says others held that the word means he will address them when he appears; report 7078 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says Jesus has already spoken to them in the cradle and will speak to them after he kills the Dajjal, being then mature. The accusative on the word is by coordination with the place of the verb. 'Of the righteous' means among their number and friends, since people of righteousness are of one another in religion and merit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 420} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
