## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And if We willed, We could take what We revealed to you, then you will find for you a guardian over us": He mentions it: And if We will, We can take what We have given you of the knowledge We have revealed to you from this Qur'an to follow it, but you do not know it, and then you will not find for yourself a guardian in what We do to you, meaning: a guardian who stands for you, preventing us from doing that to you, nor a supporter to support you and stand between us and what we want through you. He said: Abdullah ibn Mas'ud interpreted the meaning of Allah's departure as lifting it from the hearts of its readers. The narration was mentioned accordingly: from Abdul Aziz ibn Rafi', from Bandar, from Ma'qil, who said: I said to Abdullah, and he mentioned that it is easy on the Quran: How can it be when we have established it in our hearts and our Qur'ans? He said: It is easy upon him at night, so that no part of it remains in a Mushaf or in a man's chest. Then Abdullah recited: 'And if We will, We can take away what We have revealed to you.'
