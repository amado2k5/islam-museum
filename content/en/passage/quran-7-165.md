**Commentary on verse 165 (pp. 199-202)**

Al-Tabari says: when the party that transgressed in the Sabbath left what God had commanded them, the abandoning of transgression, and neglected what the preaching party had warned them of, God's punishment for their disobedience, and presumed to treat as lawful what God forbade, God saved those among them who forbade "evil", meaning disobedience to God and treating His sacred things as lawful, "and We seized the wrongdoers", that is, those who transgressed in the Sabbath and treated as lawful the hunting and eating of fish that God had forbidden. He brought down His might on them and destroyed them with a severe, wretched punishment, because they opposed His command and left obedience for disobedience, which is "wickedness" (fisq). The people of interpretation say the same. Report 15287, from Ibn Jurayj: "when they forgot" means when they forgot the admonition of the believers who said "why do you preach to a people". Report 15288, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas on "those who forbid evil": I wish I knew what was the evil that they forbade. The editor lists corrections he made to al-Tabari's wording between the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 199} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 200}

He then turns to the readers' differences over "a wretched punishment" (bi'isin). Most readers of Medina read it with a kasrah on the first letter, a light second letter and no hamza, on the pattern "fi'l". Most readers of Kufa and Basra read it on the pattern "fa'il", from "wretchedness", with a fathah on the first letter, a kasrah on the hamza and a lengthened vowel. Some Meccans read it the same way but with a kasrah on the first letter, and some Kufans read "bay'isin", with a fathah on the first letter, a silent second letter and a kasrah on the hamza, on the pattern "fay'il". Al-Tabari says that last form is irregular for the scholars of Arabic, because in a "fay'il" word not derived from roots with a weak middle letter the fathah on the middle letter is the standard usage, while the kasrah is used with those roots; he adds that whoever read it so may have followed this pattern. Another of the Kufans is reported to have read "bay'asin" with a fathah on the hamza, on the pattern "fay'al" like the word "sayqal" (polisher). It is related from some Basrans that they read "ba'isin" on the pattern "fa'il", citing a line by Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat in which he wishes to meet the one he loves alone and without any harm; the editor lists the differing versions of that line in the Khizana, al-Ayni and the divan, and holds that al-Tabari's reading is right. Yet another of them is reported to have read "bi'sa", with a kasrah on the first letter and a fathah on the last, in the sense "wretched is the punishment". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 200} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 201}

Al-Tabari's choice is that the soundest reading is the one that reads "ba'isin" with a fathah on the first letter and a lengthened kasrah on the hamza, on the pattern "fa'il", and he cites a line by Dhu al-Isba' al-Adwani in which he describes his anger at a cousin who was his enemy, who schemed against him and stirred others up; the editor gives its source in the Aghani and Majaz al-Qur'an and explains its words. Al-Tabari adds that the interpreters agree the word means "severe", which shows his choice sound. Reports 15289 to 15293: from Ibn Abbas "painful, agonizing"; from Mujahid "severe" and "painful, severe"; from Qatada "agonizing"; from Ibn Zayd "a severe punishment". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 201} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 202}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
