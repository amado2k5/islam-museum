## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari's treatment of the sealing of hearts and hearing is set out under [[concept/khatm-ala-al-qulub|the sealing of hearts]]: the sense of khatm, Mujahid's reports on ran and stamping, the Abu Hurayra report, and his reply to those who read the sealing as a description of arrogance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 258} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 261}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "and over their sight is a covering"
Al-Tabari takes "and over their sight is a covering" as a fresh predicate after the report on the sealing of hearts and hearing is complete, so "ghishawa" is in the nominative. This is the sound reading in his view for two reasons: the authoritative readers and scholars agree on it while the dissenter stands alone, and sealing is never ascribed to the eyes in God's book, in a report from the Prophet, or in Arab usage; he cites the verse of al-Jathiya, which names sealing of hearing and heart and a covering over the sight. He says reading it in the accusative is not permitted to him or anyone, though it has a recognised grammatical route, either an implied "He placed" or following the case of the preceding word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 264}
He gives a matching report from Ibn Abbas through the al-Awfi chain, which the edition's editor notes is weak, and Ibn Jurayj's saying that the seal is on the heart and hearing and the covering on the sight, with a verse of al-Shura as support. He explains ghishawa as a cover in Arabic usage, citing a line by al-Harith ibn Khalid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 265}
