## Summary
Verse 29 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 29 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The prohibition on consuming one another's property without right (pp. 216-217, reports 9140-9142).** Abu Jafar says that calling the believers means that they have believed God and His messenger, and that what is forbidden is that one of them take another's property by what is forbidden him, such as usury, gambling and other things God forbade, except where there is trade. The editor notes that the manuscript and the printed text have a different wording at the start of his sentence and that the context requires the one he adopted, and refers to earlier explanations of wrongful eating of property in other volumes; the page numbers in that note are garbled in the scan. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9140, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that wrongful eating means by usury, gambling, giving short measure and wrongdoing, and that trade is that a man may make a thousand on a dirham if he can. The editor notes that the printed text has other wording than the manuscript, that he does not know why it was changed, and that his reading matches al-Durr al-Manthur. Ibn al-Muthanna (9141, from Muhammad b. al-Fadl Abu al-Nu'man, from Khalid al-Tahhan, from Dawud b. Abi Hind) reports from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas that it is a man who buys goods, returns them and returns a dirham with them; Ibn al-Muthanna (9142, from Abd al-Wahhab from Dawud) reports from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, of a man who buys a garment and says that if he likes it he will take it and otherwise return it with a dirham, that this is what God meant. In a note on 9141 the editor says Abu al-Nu'man is 'Arim, whose biography came earlier under number 3387, that the manuscript has Muhammad b. al-Mufaddal, and that the publisher of the printed text changed the name to Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal and dropped Abu al-Nu'man, which he calls the worst breach of trust; he identifies Khalid al-Tahhan as Khalid b. Abd Allah b. Abd al-Rahman al-Wasiti, with a biography earlier under 5434 (the second number printed is garbled). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 217}

**Another view: no eating of another's food except by purchase, and its abrogation (p. 217).** Others said the verse came down forbidding people to eat one another's food except through a purchase, that eating without buying was prohibited by it until this was abrogated by a verse of sura 24 lifting blame from those with excuses for eating in homes and the like ([[passage/quran-24-61|24:61]]), part of which Abu Jafar quotes.

**Report 9143: reluctance to eat at others' houses, and its abrogation (p. 218).** Muhammad b. Humayd (9143, from Yahya b. Wadih from al-Hasan b. Waqid from Yazid al-Nahwi) reports from Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri that after the verse came down a man became reluctant to eat at anyone's house, and this was abrogated by the verse in sura 24 ([[passage/quran-24-61|24:61]]), of which the narrator gives the opening up to where it speaks of people gathering to eat. A rich man would invite a relative to food, and he would say 'I am hesitant' (the narrator glosses the word as scruple) and 'the poor have more right to it than I', so eating was made lawful of what God's name was said over, and the food of the People of the Book was made lawful. The editor has four notes: he is astonished that the manuscript and the printed text agree in giving the sura 24 verse wrongly although it was quoted correctly a few lines earlier; the word for hesitancy in this sense is old Arabic but missing from the dictionaries, so he recorded it; the printed text reversed a word order and he kept the manuscript; and the report seems incomplete, and al-Suyuti abridged it heavily in al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 218}

**Al-Tabari's choice: no abrogation here (pp. 218-219).** Abu Jafar says the sounder of the two views is al-Suddi's, because God forbade the eating of our property among us wrongfully, the Muslims do not differ that this is forbidden, and God never made it lawful; so the view that the verse forbade a man to eat his brother's food as hospitality and was then abrogated has no sense, for the scholars of the community all transmitted widely that entertaining a guest and feeding people were among the praiseworthy acts of the polytheists and of Islam, which God praised in their doers and encouraged, and that He never forbade it in any age. Hospitality therefore lies outside wrongful eating and is neither abrogating nor abrogated, because abrogation applies only to what was shown to be forbidden; thus the view he stated is sound, that the wrongful means what God forbade His servants in His revelation or on His messenger's tongue, and whatever disagrees with it is isolated. The editor says a bracketed phrase in al-Tabari's sentence is certainly corrupt, that he found no acceptable reading and bracketed it, and that the sentence would run well without it; and that the clause about what disagrees is joined to the clause on the soundness of the view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 219}

**The two readings of the word for trade (pp. 219-220).** The reciters differed. Some read it in the nominative, treating the verb as complete and needing no predicate, meaning unless a trade occurs or takes place with the agreement of both sides, so that eating is then lawful; this is the reading of most people of the Hijaz and of Basra. Others, the general body of the Kufan reciters, read it in the accusative, with the properties that you eat understood, so that the trade is the predicate. The editor notes that the printed text altered a phrase and he restored the manuscript's, and refers to the fuller discussion of the two readings at the debt verse in sura 2, in volume 6, while noting that the sense differs between the two verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 220}

**Al-Tabari's choice of reading, his remark on the Sufis, and report 9144 (pp. 220-221).** Abu Jafar says both readings are sound and permissible because both are widespread among the reciters of the cities and their meanings are close, but that he prefers the accusative, for two reasons: the verb carries a reference to the properties, and even without it, with one indefinite noun, the accusative is fluent Arabic as is the nominative, and he cites half a line of verse for this; the editor says the whole line was given earlier in volume 6. Abu Jafar then says that the verse refutes the claim of the ignorant among the Sufis who reject seeking food through trade and crafts; this is his statement. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9144, from Yazid from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that trade is a provision from God and one of His lawful things for whoever seeks it with truthfulness and piety, and that they used to be told that the honest, truthful trader will be with the seven in the shade of the Throne on the Day of Resurrection. In a note the editor gives the sound report of the seven whom God shades: the just ruler, the youth raised in worship, the man whose heart is tied to the mosque, the two who love each other for God, the one who remembers God in solitude, the one who refuses a woman of rank and beauty out of fear of God, and the one who gives charity in secret; he traces it to al-Tirmidhi from Abu Hurayra and to his grading of it as sound. Another note says that in the sentence on earning by trades and crafts the copyist wrote a word meaning for them where by them is right, that is, by the trades and crafts, and that the printed text changed what is in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 221}

**The meaning of mutual consent: reports 9145-9148 (pp. 221-222).** Muhammad b. Amr (9145, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) and al-Muthanna (9146, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih) report from Mujahid that the consent is in a trade of sale or a gift one gives to another. Ibn Waki (9147, from his father from al-Qasim from Sulayman al-Ju'fi from his father, the form of his name being garbled in the scan) reports from Maymun b. Mihran that the Messenger of God said a sale is by mutual consent, the option comes after the handclasp, and it is not lawful for a Muslim to cheat a Muslim. The editor says it is a mursal report, traced by Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, and that al-Suyuti ascribed it to Ibn Jarir alone. Al-Qasim (9148, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj) reports that he asked Ata whether the hand-passing is a sale, and Ata said no, until he gives him the choice, the choice being after the sale becomes binding, to take or to leave. The editor explains that two men 'pass hands' when they trade and strike hands and one wipes over the other's hand, a form of their sales in pre-Islamic times. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 222}

**First view on the meaning of consent: the option until the two parties separate (pp. 222-224, reports 9149-9159).** The people of knowledge differed over the meaning of consent in trade. Some said it is that each of the two traders is given the choice, after concluding the sale, to carry it through or cancel it, or they separate bodily from the place where they concluded it, consenting to the contract before any rescission. Ibn Bashshar (9149, from Mu'adh b. Hisham from his father from Qatada from Muhammad b. Sirin) reports from Shurayh that two men brought a dispute, one having sold the other a hooded cloak; the seller said he had sought the buyer's approval and not obtained it; Shurayh said satisfy him as he satisfied you; he said he had given him dirhams and still no approval; Shurayh said satisfy him; he said he had and the man was not satisfied; and Shurayh said the two parties to a sale have the option so long as they have not separated. The editor explains that the word for the party means the seller or the buyer. Ibn Bashshar (9150, from Mu'ammal from Sufyan from Abd Allah b. Abi al-Safar from al-Sha'bi) reports the same from Shurayh, and the editor says Abd Allah b. Abi al-Safar al-Hamdani al-Thawri is reliable though not a large transmitter and is in al-Tahdhib; Ibn al-Muthanna (9151, from Muhammad b. Ja'far from Shu'ba from al-Hakam) reports the same from Shurayh; Ibn al-Muthanna (9152, from Shu'ba from Jabir from Abu al-Duha) reports it from Shurayh, adding that Abu al-Duha said Shurayh used to relate something similar from the Messenger of God, and the editor says the report that the two parties have the option is sound, given by al-Bukhari, Muslim and others. Al-Husayn b. Yazid al-Tahhan (9153, from Ishaq b. Mansur from Abd al-Salam from a man from Abu Hawshab) reports from Maymun that he bought a fine cloth from Ibn Sirin, who named a price, and Maymun asked for a better one; Ibn Sirin said take it or leave it; he took it, and when he weighed out the price Ibn Sirin put down the dirhams and said choose the dirhams or the goods, and Maymun chose the goods and took them. The editor notes that the first name is given wrongly as al-Hasan in both the manuscript and the printed text, that he found no transmitter with the name Abu Hawshab, and that the chain is certainly corrupt. Abu Kurayb (9154, from Hushaym from Isma'il b. Salim) reports that al-Sha'bi said the two parties have the option until they separate, and once they have gone their ways the sale is binding. Muhammad b. Isma'il al-Ahmasi (the report number is smudged in the printed text, and by the sequence it is 9155; from Muhammad b. Ubayd from Sufyan b. Dinar) reports from Zabya that he was in the market with Ali when a girl came to a fruit seller, bought fruit for a dirham, then said she did not want it and asked for her dirham back; the seller refused, and Ali took it from him and gave it to her. The editor says he read the name from the manuscript by effort and does not know who he is, that the printed text misread it badly, and that he found the report nowhere else. Ibn Humayd (9156, from Jarir from Mughira) reports that al-Sha'bi gave a ruling for a man who had bought a horse, the sale having become binding for him, and the buyer then returned it before they separated; al-Sha'bi ruled it binding on him, but then Abu al-Duha testified that Shurayh had ruled in a like case that he return it to its owner, and al-Sha'bi went back to Shurayh's ruling. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9157, from Hushaym from Hisham from Ibn Sirin) reports from Shurayh that where the buyer claims the sale was made binding and the seller denies it, the proof is two just witnesses that the two separated by consent after a sale or an option, and otherwise the seller's oath that they separated after a sale without an option (the editor supplied a bracketed word from the context); Ya'qub (9158, from Ibn Ulayya from Ayyub) reports the same from Muhammad; Humayd b. Mas'ada (9159, from Bishr b. al-Mufaddal from Ibn Awn from Ibn Sirin) reports from Shurayh the proof of two just witnesses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 224}

**The ground of this view: reports 9160-9162 (pp. 224-225).** Abu Jafar says the reason of those who hold this view is the report of Ibn al-Muthanna (9160, from Yahya b. Sa'id from Ubayd Allah from Nafi) from Ibn Umar from the Prophet that every two parties to a sale have no sale between them until they separate, unless it is a sale with an option. The editor says Yahya is al-Qattan; that the manuscript and printed text have Abd Allah, a brother of Ubayd Allah, but that he judges Ubayd Allah correct, since the report is known from his transmission and since al-Mizzi in Tahdhib al-Kamal does not mention a narration of al-Qattan from Abd Allah; and he traces it to Ahmad's Musnad and to al-Bukhari and Muslim through Abd Allah b. Dinar from Ibn Umar. Abu Kurayb (9161, from Marwan b. Mu'awiya from Yahya b. Ayyub) reports that when Abu Zur'a made a sale with a man he would say to him give me the option, then would relate from Abu Hurayra that the Messenger of God said two do not separate except by consent. The editor says Yahya b. Ayyub al-Bajali is reliable, that Ibn Ma'in said there is no harm in him although some report a weakening from Ibn Ma'in, that he narrates from his grandfather Abu Zur'a, a reliable Tabi'i, that the report is given by Abu Dawud and by al-Bayhaqi in al-Sunan al-Kubra and is mentioned by al-Suyuti, who ascribed it to al-Tabari alone (the printed number for this report in the note is 9191, which by the sequence should be 9161). Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9162, from Ibn Ulayya from Ayyub) reports from Abu Qilaba that the Messenger of God called out O people of al-Baqi', they heard his voice, he called again and they craned to look until they knew it was his voice, and then he said let no two parties to a sale separate except by consent. The editor says the chain is mursal because Abu Qilaba was a Tabi'i, that he does not know whether it is so in al-Tabari or whether the Companion's name dropped out through the copyists, that al-Bayhaqi gives a similar report through Khalid al-Hadhdha from Abu Qilaba from Anas with a good chain, and that al-Suyuti mentions al-Tabari's report as mursal.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 225 in the commentary on verse 29 of sura 4, in the discussion of the meaning of mutual consent in trade, after the first view (the option until the two parties separate) and its reports through 9162, and continue from p. 226. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 225}
