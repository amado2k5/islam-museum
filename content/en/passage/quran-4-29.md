## Summary
Verse 29 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 29 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The prohibition on consuming one another's property without right (pp. 216-217, reports 9140-9142).** Abu Jafar says that calling the believers means that they have believed God and His messenger, and that what is forbidden is that one of them take another's property by what is forbidden him, such as usury, gambling and other things God forbade, except where there is trade. The editor notes that the manuscript and the printed text have a different wording at the start of his sentence and that the context requires the one he adopted, and refers to earlier explanations of wrongful eating of property in other volumes; the page numbers in that note are garbled in the scan. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9140, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that wrongful eating means by usury, gambling, giving short measure and wrongdoing, and that trade is that a man may make a thousand on a dirham if he can. The editor notes that the printed text has other wording than the manuscript, that he does not know why it was changed, and that his reading matches al-Durr al-Manthur. Ibn al-Muthanna (9141, from Muhammad b. al-Fadl Abu al-Nu'man, from Khalid al-Tahhan, from Dawud b. Abi Hind) reports from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas that it is a man who buys goods, returns them and returns a dirham with them; Ibn al-Muthanna (9142, from Abd al-Wahhab from Dawud) reports from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, of a man who buys a garment and says that if he likes it he will take it and otherwise return it with a dirham, that this is what God meant. In a note on 9141 the editor says Abu al-Nu'man is 'Arim, whose biography came earlier under number 3387, that the manuscript has Muhammad b. al-Mufaddal, and that the publisher of the printed text changed the name to Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal and dropped Abu al-Nu'man, which he calls the worst breach of trust; he identifies Khalid al-Tahhan as Khalid b. Abd Allah b. Abd al-Rahman al-Wasiti, with a biography earlier under 5434 (the second number printed is garbled). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 217}

**Another view: no eating of another's food except by purchase, and its abrogation (p. 217).** Others said the verse came down forbidding people to eat one another's food except through a purchase, that eating without buying was prohibited by it until this was abrogated by a verse of sura 24 lifting blame from those with excuses for eating in homes and the like ([[passage/quran-24-61|24:61]]), part of which Abu Jafar quotes.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 217 in the commentary on verse 29 of sura 4, at the opening of the view that the prohibition on eating one another's property wrongfully concerned eating one another's food except by purchase and was lifted by a verse of sura 24, and continue from p. 218. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 217}
