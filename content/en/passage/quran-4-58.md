## Summary
Verse 58 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 58 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The view that it concerns the rulers (pp. 490-491, reports 9839-9844, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom the verse means. Some said the rulers of the Muslims. Musa b. Abd al-Rahman al-Masruqi (9839, from Abu Usama, from Abu Mukayn) reports from Zayd b. Aslam that it was sent down about the holders of command; Abu Kurayb (9840, from Ibn Idris, from Layth) from Shahr that it came down about the commanders especially; Abu Kurayb (9841, from Ibn Idris, from Isma'il) from Mus'ab b. Sa'd that Ali spoke words in which he was right: it is the leader's duty to rule by what God sent down and to discharge the trust, and when he does, it is the people's duty to hear, obey and answer when called; Jabir b. Nuh (9842, from Isma'il, from Mus'ab) from Ali similarly; Muhammad b. Ubayd al-Muharibi (9843, from Musa b. Umayr) from Makhul that the holders of command are the people of the verse before it ([[passage/quran-4-59|4:59]]); and Yunus (9844, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) from his father that they are the rulers, commanded to return trusts. The editor notes that Abu Usama is Hammad b. Usama b. Zayd al-Qurashi and Abu Mukayn is Nuh b. Rabi'a; and that Mus'ab b. Sa'd b. Abi Waqqas al-Zuhri narrated from his father, Ali, Talha and Ikrima b. Abi Jahl, a trustworthy Follower, of whom Ibn Sa'd said he was trustworthy and narrated much. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 491}

**The view that the sultans are to admonish women (p. 491, report 9845, notes 1-2).** Others said the ruler was commanded to admonish women. Al-Muthanna (9845, from Abdullah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that it means the sultan, who admonish women. The editor says the print has "the people" for "the women" and the manuscript is unpointed; the intent is that rulers should admonish women in disobedience and the like until they return them to their husbands, which is the view ascribed to Ibn Abbas in the tafsir books. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 491}

**The view that the address is to the Prophet about the Ka'ba key (pp. 491-492, reports 9846-9847, notes).** Others said the address was to the Prophet over the keys of the Ka'ba, being told to return them to Uthman b. Talha. Al-Qasim (9846, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports that it came down about Uthman b. Talha b. Abi Talha, from whom the Prophet took the Ka'ba key and entered the House on the day of the conquest, then came out reciting this verse, called Uthman and gave him the key; and Umar b. al-Khattab said, when he came out reciting it, that he had not heard him recite it before. Al-Qasim (9847, from al-Husayn, from al-Zanji b. Khalid) reports from al-Zuhri that he handed it to him and said: help him. The editor records the differences of print, manuscript and Ibn Kathir over the key being singular or plural and over a phrase of devotion, and says he corrected the manuscript text in line with Ibn Kathir; and that al-Zanji b. Khalid is Muslim b. Khalid b. Qarwa Abu Khalid al-Zanji, the Meccan jurist, whose nickname is explained in several ways, among them his extreme dark colour and a story of his eating dates; that al-Shafi'i learned jurisprudence from him before meeting Malik; that al-Bukhari said he reports what is disowned, his reports being written down but not used as proof; and that the editor takes him as truthful though weak in his reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 492}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 492-493, report 9848, notes).** Abu Ja'far says the best of these views in his opinion is that this is God's address to the rulers of the Muslims to give the trust back to those whose affairs they govern, of their fay', their rights and what they were trusted with, with justice among them in judgment and equal division. He argues from what God admonished the subjects with in the next verse, the command to obey God, the Messenger and the holders of command: He commanded obedience to them, charged the shepherd with the flock and the flock with obedience. Yunus (9848, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) reports from his father that the holders of command are the sultans, and Ibn Zayd recited the verse of Al Imran on giving and removing dominion ([[passage/quran-3-26|3:26]]), then said that the scholars are those who attend the sultan, that the trusts are the fay' they were trusted to collect and divide and the charities they were trusted to collect and distribute, and that the whole verse commands the rulers, after which He turned to us and commanded obedience. The editor notes that this wording is corrupt in print and manuscript (the note is blurred in the scan); that the print's publisher dropped a sentence he did not understand and altered the context, which the editor calls a grave corruption and tampering with the interpreters' words; that the speaker is Ibn Zayd after quoting his father's interpretation; that those who attend the sultan are those near him in his councils whom he consults; and that the scholars advise the rulers on dividing fay' and charity and so are those trusted with the religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 493}

**Every trustee included (pp. 493-494, reports 9849-9850, note 1).** Al-Tabari says it may well have come down about Uthman b. Talha, as Ibn Jurayj said, and meant every trustee, so that it takes in the rulers and everyone trusted with something in religion or the world; hence those who said the meaning is paying debts and returning people's rights. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9849, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) reports from Ibn Abbas that God allowed neither the well-off nor the poor to keep it; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9850, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada, from al-Hasan, that the Prophet used to say: return the trust to whoever trusted you and do not betray whoever betrayed you. The note adds that Ibn Kathir gives a like report from al-Hasan from Samura, given by Ahmad and the Sunan compilers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 494}

**The sense of the verse (p. 494).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: God commands you, company of the Muslims' rulers, to return what your subjects entrusted to you of their fay', rights, wealth and charities to whom it belongs, not wronging its owners, not taking any of it for yourselves, not putting any of it where it does not belong, and not taking it except from those God allowed you to take it from before it reached your hands; and He commands you, when you judge among your subjects, to rule with equity and fairness, which is God's ruling sent down in His book and made plain on His messenger's tongue, so that you do not go beyond it and wrong them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 494}

**The admonition and the close of the verse (pp. 494-495, notes).** Al-Tabari says God is the best of things to admonish with, and the admonition is excellent in His command to return trusts and judge justly; God has always heard what you say when you judge among people, and sees what you do with what you were trusted with of your subjects' rights and wealth and what you decide among them, in justice or in injustice; nothing is hidden from Him, and He keeps it all until He repays the doer of good with good and the evildoer with evil, or pardons by His favour. The editor says the print altered a phrase in the manuscript it did not understand and so its sentence has no meaning, and changed another wording without reason; he refers to earlier explanations of the word for "excellent". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 495}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 495}
