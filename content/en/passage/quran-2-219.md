## Summary
Verse 219 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/maysar|gambling (maysir)]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 219 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "they ask you about wine and gambling; say, in both is great sin and benefits for people, but their sin is greater than their benefit": it means, your companions ask you, Muhammad, about wine and drinking it. "Khamr" is every drink that "khammara" the mind, covering and veiling it, from "I covered (khamartu) the vessel" when covered it, and "a woman's khimar" because she covers her head with it; one says "he walks to you in khamar", meaning in concealment, as al-'Ajjaj says, and whatever mixes with and overwhelms the mind, of sickness or intoxication, is "khamr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 321} "Maysir" is of the form "maf'il" from "this matter was 'yasara' for me" when it became due to me, "yasir" being the one who is owed by arrows, a share or other, and then the gambler was called "yasir" or "yasar", as in two cited lines; the editor remarks on the sense in the dictionaries and on the poets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 321} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 322} Mujahid said much the same: maysir is gambling, so called from their saying "aysiru wa-jzuru", "put down such and such" (4106); and from him: all gambling is of maysir, even children playing with walnuts (4107); from 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud: beware of these marked dice that you throw with a throw, for they are of maysir (4108-4110). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 322}

From Muhammad ibn Sirin: gambling is maysir (4111); everything with a stake is of maysir (4112); all gambling is maysir, even playing backgammon on standing, shouting or the feather a man puts on his head (4113); every game with gambling in it, of drinking, shouting or standing, is of maysir (4114). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 323} From al-Hasan: maysir is gambling (4115); from Tawus and 'Ata': all gambling is of maysir, even children playing with dice and walnuts (4116); from Sa'id (4117); from 'Ubaydallah, that is Ibn Mas'ud: beware of these two dice thrown with a throw, for they are of maysir (4118); from Qatada: maysir is all gambling (4119). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 323} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 324} From 'Ubaydallah ibn 'Umar, that he heard 'Umar ibn 'Ubaydallah say to al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: backgammon is maysir, what of chess, is it maysir? and al-Qasim said, whatever distracts from remembrance of God and from prayer is maysir (4120). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 324} From Ibn 'Abbas: maysir is gambling; in the Jahiliyya a man would wager his family and property, and whichever beat his companion went off with the other's family and property (4121); from al-Suddi (4122) and Qatada (4123); from Mujahid and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: all gambling, even the walnuts children play with (4124); from al-Dahhak (4125), Qatada (4126) and Ibn 'Umar: gambling is of maysir (4127); from Mujahid: maysir is the arrows of the Arabs and the dice of the Persians, and 'Ata' ibn Maysara claimed it is all gambling (4128); from Makhul (4129) and Ibn 'Umar (4130). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 324} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 325}

On "say, in both is great sin and benefits for people": it means, say to them, Muhammad, in both, wine and gambling, is great sin. From al-Suddi: the sin of wine is that a man drinks and becomes drunk and harms people, and the sin of gambling is that a man gambles, withholds what is due and wrongs (4131); from Mujahid: this is the first fault found with wine (4132); from Ibn 'Abbas: it means what is lost of the religion in one who drinks it (4133). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 325} Al-Tabari says the better view for wine is what al-Suddi said, the drinker's loss of reason when drunk until knowledge of his Lord leaves him, which is the gravest of sins, and this is, God willing, the meaning of Ibn 'Abbas's words; and for gambling it is being occupied by it from remembrance of God and from prayer and the arising of enmity and hatred between the gamblers, as God described it in "Satan only wants to cause enmity and hatred between you through wine and gambling and to turn you from the remembrance of God and from prayer" (5:91). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 326}

On "and benefits for people": the benefits of wine were its price before it was forbidden and the pleasure they reached by drinking it, and he cites two lines of al-A'sha describing it and a line of Hassan, with the editor's explanation and corrections to the printed wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 326} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 327} The benefits of gambling are the shares of the slaughtered camel they get; they would cast arrows over a camel, and when one beat his companion he slaughtered it and they divided it into tenths by the number of arrows; he cites a line of al-A'sha, and the editor notes that gambling was counted among them as a mark of generosity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 327} The interpreters said likewise: from Mujahid, the benefits here are what they get of the camel (4134); from al-Suddi, the benefit of wine is in its pleasure and price and that of gambling in what is won (4135); from Mujahid, their benefits before they were forbidden (4136); from Ibn 'Abbas, the pleasure and delight they got when they drank it (4137). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 328}

The readers differed on "in both is great (kabir) sin": most of the people of Medina and some of Kufa and Basra read "kabir" with ba', meaning that in drinking this and gambling this is a great measure of sin; others from the two cities read "kathir" with tha', meaning a multitude of sins, as though they saw "ithm" as meaning "sins" though singular in form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 328}

Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two readings is that of those who read "kabir" with ba', because all agree on reading "and their sin is greater (akbar) than their benefit" with ba'; this is clear evidence that the description of the first sin is greatness and magnitude, not number, for had number been meant it would have said "more (akthar) than their benefit". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 329}

On "and their sin is greater than their benefit", he takes it to mean that the sin of drinking wine and of this gambling is a greater harm to them than the benefit they gain by them, because when drunk they leapt on one another and fought, and when they gambled evil arose between them because of it, which led them to the things for which they sin. The verse was revealed about wine before its prohibition was made explicit, so God ascribed the sin to the two, though the sin lies in their causes, since it arises from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 329} A number of interpreters said instead that it means their sin after prohibition is greater than their benefit before it: from Ibn 'Abbas, their benefits were before prohibition and their sin after they were forbidden (4138); from al-Rabi', the same (4139); from al-Dahhak, their sin after prohibition is greater than their benefit before it (4140); from Ibn 'Abbas by 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, what goes from religion, and the sin in it, is greater than what they get of delight when they drink it (4141). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 329} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 330}

Al-Tabari says he chose his reading because of the abundant, mutually supporting reports that this verse was revealed before the prohibition of wine and gambling, so the sin God ascribed to them is the sin arising from their causes, not the sin after prohibition. He then gives the reports for revelation before the prohibition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 330} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when the verse on wine and gambling came down, some disliked it because of "in both is great sin" and some drank because of "and benefits for people", until the verse in al-Nisa' (4:43) came down; they then left it at prayer times and drank it at other times, until the verse in al-Ma'ida (5:90) came down (4142). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 330}

From 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar: God sent down three things on wine; first the verse of al-Baqara, and they said, we benefit from it and drink it as God said; then the verse of al-Nisa' came down, and they said, we will not drink it near prayer; then the verse of al-Ma'ida came down, and the Prophet said, wine is forbidden (4143). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 331} The editor's note on this chain: Abu 'Amir is al-'Aqadi, trustworthy; Muhammad ibn Abi Humayd al-Ansari is weak, munkar al-hadith, with agreement on his weakness; "Abu Tawba al-Misri" is no known narrator and comes from Muhammad ibn Abi Humayd's confusion, the right name being Abu Tu'ma al-Umawi, mawla of 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, a trustworthy tabi'i and reciter in Egypt; al-Tayalisi relates it in his Musnad with an added story of the wineskins being slit; Ibn Kathir corrected the name in his tafsir by writing "that is Abu Tu'ma"; and al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-manthur, attributing it to al-Tayalisi, al-Tabari, Ibn Abi Hatim, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 331}

From 'Ikrima and al-Hasan: God said not to approach prayer when drunk (4:43) and said they ask about wine and gambling (2:219), and the verse in al-Ma'ida abrogated them (4144). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 332} From Abu al-Qumus Zayd ibn 'Ali: God sent down three times on wine; first the verse of al-Baqara, and those Muslims whom God willed drank on that, until two men drank, entered prayer and began to talk incoherently, and the verse of al-Nisa' came down, so they avoided it at prayer, until a man drank it and began to lament those killed at Badr in verse; word reached the Prophet, who came in alarm dragging his cloak, and when the man saw him he said, I seek refuge in God from the anger of God and His messenger, by God I shall never taste it again; then God sent down its prohibition in the two verses of al-Ma'ida, and 'Umar ibn al-Khattab said, we desist, we desist (4145). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 332} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 333} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 334} The editor notes that the transmitters of this report attribute the verses to Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, that 'A'isha denied it, and that the lines are from Abu Bakr ibn Shu'ub mixed with those of Buhayr ibn 'Abdallah al-Qushayri; that Abu al-Qumus's report is mursal and no proof; and that al-Hafiz (Ibn Hajar) weakened it because it conflicts with al-Fakihi's sound report from 'A'isha that Abu Bakr never said a line of poetry in the Jahiliyya or Islam and that he and 'Uthman had left wine in the Jahiliyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 332} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 333} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 334}

From al-Sha'bi: four verses came down on wine: the verse of al-Baqara, which they left; then the verse of al-Nahl (16:67), and they drank; then the two verses of al-Ma'ida (4146). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 334}
