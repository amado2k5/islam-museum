## Summary
Verse 50 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 50 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 50 (pp. 133-137)**

On "and the king said, bring him to me", al-Tabari says: when the messenger who had been sent to Joseph returned and told them Joseph's interpretation of the king's dream, the king knew its truth and soundness and said: bring me the one who interpreted my dream. Report 19392, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the cupbearer left Joseph with the interpretation he had given and came to the king and told him; when he told him what was in his mind as clear as day, and he knew that what Joseph said would come to pass, he said: bring him to me. Report 19393, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: when the king's messenger came to him he said: bring him to me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 133}

On "when the messenger came to him he said, return to your lord", al-Tabari says Joseph told the messenger to go back to his master (the editor refers to the earlier discussion of the word "rabb") and "ask him what is the matter with the women who cut their hands". He refused to go out with the messenger and answer the king until the truth of his case was known among them, regarding what he had been accused of in the matter of the women, so he told the messenger to ask the king about the women who cut their hands and the woman because of whom he had been imprisoned. The editor corrects "qarafuhu", meaning "accused him", from the manuscript against the printed "qadhafuhu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 133}

Report 19394, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: ask about the woman because of whose affair he was imprisoned, about what had happened. Report 19395, from al-Suddi: when the king's messenger came and told him, he said bring him to me; when the messenger came and called him to the king, Joseph refused to go out with him. Al-Suddi said that Ibn Abbas said: had Joseph gone out that day before the king learned his affair, there would still have been a grievance against him in the heart of the Aziz, meaning the husband of the woman who had sought him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 134}

Report 19396, from Abu Hurayra, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq and a man from Abu al-Zinad, from the Prophet: God have mercy on Joseph, how patient he was; were I the one imprisoned and then sent for, I would have gone out quickly. The editor calls the chain weak because the man who narrated from Abu al-Zinad is unnamed. Report 19397, from Abu Hurayra, through Ibn Waki', Muhammad ibn Bishr, Muhammad ibn Amr and Abu Salama, from the Prophet: had I stayed in prison as long as Joseph and then the caller come to me, I would have answered. The editor explains that al-Tabari reports the line of Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Salama by three paths, that Muhammad ibn Amr ibn Alqama ibn Waqqas al-Laythi is reliable and was used by the compilers of the six books, that Ahmad reports it in the Musnad and Ibn Kathir quotes it, and that al-Haythami in Majma' al-Zawa'id calls him sound in hadith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 134}

Report 19398, through Yunus ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Wahb, Sulayman ibn Bilal, Muhammad ibn Amr and Abu Salama, from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet, the same. Report 19399, through Zakariyya ibn Aban al-Misri, Sa'id ibn Tulayd, Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Qasim, Bakr ibn Mudar, Amr ibn al-Harith, Yunus ibn Yazid and Ibn Shihab, from Abu Salama and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, from Abu Hurayra: had I stayed in prison as long as Joseph I would have answered the caller. The editor says its chain is sound, that al-Bukhari reports it in his Sahih from Sa'id ibn Tulayd, and that the name of al-Tabari's teacher Zakariyya ibn Aban was misread in the printed edition and is corrected from the manuscript. Report 19400, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb and Ibn Shihab, from Abu Salama and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet, the same: another path for the same report. Report 19401 begins, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Affan ibn Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 135}

Report 19401 continues: Hammad, from Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Salama, from Abu Hurayra, that the Prophet recited this verse and said: had it been I, I would have hastened to answer and not sought an excuse. The editor says Ahmad reports it in his Musnad by this path. Report 19402, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal and Hammad, from Thabit, from the Prophet, and Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Salama from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet: had one been sent to me I would have hastened to answer and not sought an excuse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 136}

Report 19403, from Ikrima, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Uyayna and Amr ibn Dinar, that the Prophet said: I marvel at Joseph, his patience and generosity, God forgive him, when he was asked about the lean and fat cows; in his place I would have told them nothing until I had stipulated that they release me; and I marvel at him when the messenger came, for in his place I would have rushed to the door, but he wished to have an excuse. The editor calls this a mursal report. Report 19404, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: God's prophet wished not to go out until he had an excuse. Report 19405 begins at the end of the page, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 136}

Report 19405 concludes, from Ibn Jurayj: Joseph wanted the excuse before he left the prison. On "my Lord is well aware of their plot", al-Tabari says God knows what the women did to him and will do to others, none of it hidden from Him, and He stands behind their recompense for it. It is also said to mean: my master Itfir the Aziz, the husband of the woman who sought me, knows my innocence of the evil I was accused of. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "kayd" and to the language indexes for "alim", and corrects the wording from the manuscript against the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 137}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
