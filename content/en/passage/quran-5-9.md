**Commentary on verse 9: God's promise to the believers (pp. 98-99)**

Al-Tabari says: God has promised, people, those who believed God and his Messenger, acknowledged what came to them from their Lord, acted on what God bound them to and kept the covenants they made by saying they would hear and obey, so they heard God's command and prohibition and obeyed. By 'forgiveness for them' he means the covering of their past sins and veiling them by his pardon, not punishing them or exposing them for them; 'a great reward' means that besides pardoning their past sins they have a recompense for their deeds and for keeping their covenants, whose greatness is unlimited and whose end only God knows. If someone asks that God said he made a promise but did not say what, where is the report of what was promised? The answer is yes, he did say: what was promised is 'for them is forgiveness and a great reward'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 98}

If the objector says this is a new nominal sentence, and had it been the thing promised it would be 'forgiveness and a great reward' without 'for them', so that 'for them' shows the sentence has begun and the report of the promise has ended, the answer is that though that is the apparent form, the apparent form is enough to indicate the hidden sense: God promised those who believed and did righteous deeds to forgive them and reward them greatly. It is the Arabs' way to attach 'that' to a promise, and it was dropped because a promise is a saying, after which a new sentence follows, as if it said: God said, to those who believe and do good, forgiveness and a great reward. Some Basran grammarians said it was said within the promise made to them, so the sense is that they have forgiveness and a great reward in what he promised them. The editor notes that al-Qurtubi gives this sense from al-Hasan and explains the use of 'bala' to answer a question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 99}
