## Summary
Verse 257 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 257 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The first page of the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-257|2:257]]) that we can read is p. 427, because printed pp. 423-426 are missing from the scan; we therefore begin in the middle of an argument of al-Tabari whose start and preceding reports are not visible, and only pages actually read are cited. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 427} Al-Tabari says here that what precedes shows the verse to be particular in sense, and that it came down about those among the Christians who disbelieved in Muhammad and those among the idol-worshippers who believed in him and had not acknowledged the prophethood of Jesus, and the other communities whose people denied Jesus; this is his statement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 427} Asked whether the Christians were upon the truth before Muhammad was sent, so that they then denied him, he answers that whoever of them was on the religion of Jesus was on the truth, and that these are whom God addressed in ([[passage/quran-4-136|4:136]]) where the believers are told to believe in God and His messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 427}

Al-Tabari then asks whether those described as taken out could be others than those Mujahid and Abda mean, Abda being Abda ibn Abi Luba'ba according to the editor, namely believers in Jesus, or others than those who apostatised. He answers yes: it can mean that the taghuts as their allies stand between them and faith and lead them astray so that they disbelieve, so that misleading them is, in effect, taking them out of faith, that is turning them from it and depriving them of its good though they were never in it, as a man says, my father took me out of his inheritance, when someone else came to own it in his father's lifetime and so deprived him of his share, though he had never owned it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 427} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 428} He says this is possible even though what Mujahid and Abda said is closer to the meaning of the verse; this is his statement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 428} In the notes the editor identifies Abda, corrects readings between the printed text and manuscript, and explains the word for share. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 427} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 428}

Al-Tabari then answers a question about the grammar, how the report about the taghut is put in the plural though taghut is singular: taghut is a name for both the plural and singular, and may also be pluralised as tawaghit, like the phrases for a just man and just people, and he cites a verse by al-Abbas ibn Mirdas about his people of Sulaym; in the note the editor gives the context of the poem as the day of Hunayn and notes another account of the plural of the word for brother, and these are his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 428}

On the verse's close, al-Tabari says that those who disbelieved are the people of the Fire, who remain in it for ever, meaning in the fire of Hell, as distinct from the believers, with no end or limit; this is his interpretation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 429} It is reported as his statement and the museum does not rule on it.
