## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And We saved him and Lot to the land which We have blessed for the worlds": We saved Abraham and Lot from their enemies, Nimrod and his people, from the land of Iraq, "to the land which We had blessed for the worlds," which is the land of the Levant. May God's blessings be upon him, he left his people and their religion and migrated to the Levant. This story, which God told of the story of Abraham and his people, serves as a reminder to the people of Muhammad from Quraysh that they had adopted idols in their worship, and Muhammad harmed them despite forbidding them from worshiping them, and their prayers to worship God, sincerely devoking Him in religion. He was the path of their father Abraham's enemies and their opposition to his religion. In his disassociation from her worship and sincerity in worship of God, and in their prayers for disassociation from idols and patience in what was met from them, he followed the path of his father Abraham, and that he was his guide from among the outer branches of them, just as Abraham was expelled from among his people when they went too far in their misguidance to migrate from the land of the Levant. In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah, Hasan, and Al-Rabi'.
