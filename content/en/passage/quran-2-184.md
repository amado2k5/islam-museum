## Summary
Verse 184 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "a limited number of days", al-Tabari says the sense is that fasting has been prescribed for you for a limited number of days, and that "days" is in the accusative through an implied verb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 413}

Al-Tabari says "days" is in the accusative by an implied verb, as if it were said "it has been prescribed for you to fast a limited number of days". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 414} He then reports the differing views on the "limited days". Some said they are the fast of three days of each month, which was prescribed before Ramadan, from 'Ata', Ibn 'Abbas (who adds that it was then abrogated by the fast of Ramadan) and Qatada; in the report from Mu'adh ibn Jabal the Prophet, on coming to Medina, fasted 'Ashura and three days of each month until Ramadan was made obligatory, and the editor traces the report and comments on some of its narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 414} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 415} Others said those three days were voluntary and that the verse means the days of Ramadan, not those days; in the report of Shu'ba from 'Amr ibn Murra, "our companions told us" that the Prophet on arriving told them to fast three days of each month voluntarily, not as an obligation, and then the fast of Ramadan was revealed; the editor discusses the chain at length and the phrase "our companions told us". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 415} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 416}

Al-Tabari chooses that the "limited days" are the days of the month of Ramadan, because no report has come, strong enough to be proof, that any fast other than Ramadan was made obligatory on the Muslims and then abrogated by it, and because God made clear in the course of the verse that the fast is that of "the month of Ramadan"; anyone claiming another obligatory fast is asked for proof from a report of that standing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 417} He allows also that "fasting has been prescribed for you" means the month of Ramadan has been prescribed, and that "limited" means days whose totals and hours are counted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 417}

On "whoever of you is ill or on a journey, then a number of other days", he says: whoever is ill among those charged with the fast, or is healthy but travelling, owes the number of days he broke, from days other than those of his illness or journey; the nominative of "a number" is like the nominative in "a following with kindness". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 418} On "and for those who are able to do it, a ransom of feeding a poor person", the reading of the Muslims at large is "yutiqunahu", the reading of the copies of the Qur'an, which al-Tabari holds none may depart from since all transmitted it generation after generation; it is reported that Ibn 'Abbas read "yutawwaqunahu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 418} The commentators then differ on its sense. Some said that this was at the first prescription of the fast: whoever of the residents could fast might fast if he wished, or break the fast and ransom by feeding a poor person for each day, until it was abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 418} In the report from Mu'adh ibn Jabal, whoever wished fasted and whoever wished broke the fast and fed a poor person; then God made the fast obligatory on the healthy resident and left feeding in place for the old man unable to fast; in Shu'ba's report the people were not used to fasting and it was hard on them, and then the verse came and the concession was for the sick and the traveller. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 419}

On the view that the clause was abrogated, al-Tabari reports from 'Alqama, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, 'Ikrima, al-Hasan al-Basri, al-Sha'bi, Ibn 'Umar, 'Ubayda, Salama ibn al-Akwa', Ibn Shihab and Ibn 'Abbas, and 'Ata' that "and for those who are able to do it, a ransom" was a concession by which whoever wished fasted, or broke the fast and fed a poor person (half a sa' for each day in 'Alqama's report), and that "whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast it" then abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 421} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 422} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 423} Reports from Ibrahim, 'Ata' and Ibn Shihab add that the verse remained for the old man unable to fast, who gives half a sa' in alms for each day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 422} Al-Sha'bi said the concession came down only for the sick and the traveller; Ibn 'Abbas said the ransom was abrogated because the later prescription does not mention it; and in the report of Salama ibn al-Akwa', in the time of the Prophet whoever wished fasted and whoever wished broke the fast and ransomed, until "whoever of you witnesses the month" was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 421} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 422} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 423} From al-Dahhak, the first fast ran from the late evening prayer to the same time next day, with food and intercourse forbidden after that prayer; the later prescription then made food and intercourse lawful by night and abrogated the ransom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 424} The editor notes in footnotes weaknesses in some narrators and corrections to narrators' names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 419} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 421} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 422}

Others said that the clause was a particular ruling for the old man and old woman who are able to fast: they were allowed to ransom their fast by feeding a poor person and to break it, and "whoever of you witnesses the month" then abrogated that, so that the fast bound them like the young, unless they cannot manage it, in which case the earlier ruling stands for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 424} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 425} From Ibn 'Abbas, through Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the ruling stands for the old man and old woman if they cannot fast, and for the pregnant and nursing woman if they fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 425}

Further reports on the view that the ruling was particular: from 'Ikrima, Qatada and al-Rabi', the old man and old woman able to fast were allowed to break the fast and feed, then the concession was abrogated for them if they were able, and remained for the pregnant and nursing woman, who may break the fast and feed; Qatada said that the people of knowledge held, and hoped, that the concession stands for the old man and woman when they cannot fast, and for the pregnant woman if she fears for what is in her womb and the nursing woman if she fears for her child. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 426}

Others, among those who read "yutiqunahu", said this was not abrogated, nor any part of it, but a standing ruling from the time of revelation until the Hour; its sense is "for those who were able to fast in their youth and health, when they fall ill or grow old and cannot manage the fast, a ransom of feeding a poor person", not that people able to fast were permitted to break it if they ransomed. In al-Suddi's report: the man who was able and had fasted before, then pain, thirst or a long illness befalls him, or the nursing woman who cannot fast; for these there is the feeding of a poor person in place of each day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 427} From Ibn 'Abbas, the pregnant woman who fears for herself and the nursing woman who fears for her child in Ramadan break the fast and feed a poor person for each day and do not make up the fast; he said to a concubine of his who was pregnant or nursing, "you are in the position of those unable to fast, you must feed a poor person for each day and there is no making up"; and from Ibn 'Umar the same as Ibn 'Abbas for the pregnant and nursing. The editor notes in footnotes that these are fatwas of Ibn 'Abbas and discusses their chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 427} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 428} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 429} In another report from Ibn 'Abbas, it is the old man who could fast Ramadan in his youth and cannot now, who gives alms to one poor person for each day; and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, it is the old man unable to fast and the pregnant woman excused from the fast, each owing a mudd of wheat for every day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 429}

Others read "yutawwaqunahu" and said it is the old man and old woman who have grown too old to fast, who are charged with the fast and cannot bear it, so they may break the fast and feed a poor person for each day; they said the verse's ruling has stood since it was revealed and was not abrogated, and rejected those who called it abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 429} Al-Tabari reports this reading from Ibn 'Abbas, 'Ata', 'Ikrima, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Mujahid and 'A'isha, with Ibn 'Abbas saying "it stands for people today"; and from 'Ikrima, "those who are able fast it, but those who are made to bear it cannot manage it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 429} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 430}

Al-Tabari continues the reports from Ibn 'Abbas on "yutawwaqunahu" as meaning "they take on the burden of it, or force themselves to it", and that it applies to the old man unable to fast, who breaks the fast and feeds a poor person each day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 431} In one report from Mujahid and 'Ata' on Ibn 'Abbas, this is an abrogated verse with a concession only for the old man unable to fast or a sick man who knows he will not recover; in another report from Mujahid on Ibn 'Abbas, he used to say it is not abrogated, so the reports from him on this conflict, as al-Tabari gives them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 431} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 432} From 'Ali ibn Abi Talha on Ibn 'Abbas: whoever can fast only with hardship may break the fast and feed a poor person each day, as may the pregnant, the nursing, the old man and the permanently ill; and from Mujahid, who ordered a questioner's wife, whose ninth month fell in Ramadan and a fierce heat, to break the fast and feed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 432} From 'Ali ibn Abi Talib: it is the old man who cannot fast; he breaks the fast and feeds a poor person in place of each day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 433} From 'Ata': the old man who cannot fast ransoms each day with a poor person, but whoever can fast with hardship should fast and has no excuse; from Tawus the same, saying he did not know the amount except that it is a day's food; and from al-Dahhak the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 433} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 434} The editor notes in footnotes corrections to narrators' names and his view that "yutawwaqunahu" is the likely reading in some places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 431} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 432} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 433}

Al-Tabari chooses that "and for those who are able to do it, a ransom of feeding a poor person" is abrogated by "whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast it", because the pronoun in "able to do it" refers to the fast, so it means "for those able to fast, a ransom of feeding a poor person", and the Muslims are agreed that a healthy resident man able to fast Ramadan may not break it and ransom with feeding, so the verse is known to be abrogated. He adds that this is supported by the reports of Mu'adh ibn Jabal, Ibn 'Umar and Salama ibn al-Akwa' that, after the verse came down, they had the choice between fasting and breaking the fast with a ransom until "whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast it" was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 434} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 435}

Al-Tabari then raises an imagined objection: how can he claim agreement when some say the pregnant and nursing woman may break the fast if they fear for their children even if physically able, and there is the report from the Prophet through Anas, "God has lifted the fast and half the prayer from the traveller, the pregnant and the nursing woman"? He answers that he claimed no agreement about the pregnant and nursing woman, only about the men who... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 435} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 436} (the answer continues on the next page). The editor's long footnote identifies the Companion Anas al-Ka'bi of this report and distinguishes him from Anas ibn Malik al-Ansari, and traces the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 435} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 436}

Al-Tabari completes his answer to the objection: he holds that the pregnant and nursing woman are not meant by "those who are able to do it", for had they been meant, without the men, Arabic usage would have said "for the women who are able", and since agreement is established that the healthy resident man who is able is not permitted to break the fast and ransom, the men fall outside the verse and it is known that the women were not meant by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 436} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 437} As for the report from the Prophet, if sound, its sense for him is that God lifted the fast from the pregnant and nursing woman while they are unable to do it until they can and make it up, as it was lifted from the traveller during his journey until he settles and makes it up, not that they were told to ransom and break the fast without making it up; had the report meant that the ransom was lifted from them, the traveller would owe no making up, which he holds contrary to the plain book of God and to what all the Muslims agree on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 437} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 438} This is al-Tabari's own choice; the reports on pp. 427-429 record Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn 'Umar saying that the pregnant and nursing woman who fear break the fast and feed, with no making up in Ibn 'Abbas's report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 427} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 428} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 429} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 437}

He notes that some Basran grammarians claimed "able to do it" means "able to feed", and holds that this is contrary to the commentators' interpretation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 438} He regards the reading "yutawwaqunahu" as contrary to the copies of the Qur'an held by the Muslims, and holds that no one may object by opinion to what the Muslims transmitted openly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 438} "Fidya" is recompense, from "I ransomed this with that", meaning I gave it in exchange; the sense is that for those able to fast there is a recompense of feeding a poor person for each day they break. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 438}

He then sets out the readings of "fidya of feeding a poor person": most of the reciters of Medina read it with "fidya" in construct with "food", and most of the reciters of Iraq read "fidya" with nunation and "food" in apposition explaining it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 438} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 439} Al-Tabari prefers the construct reading, since "fidya" is the name of the act and distinct from the food with which the fast is redeemed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 439} For "poor person" some read the singular, one poor person for each day, from Abu 'Amr and the greater part of the reciters of Iraq, and others read the plural, "poor persons", for the whole month, from al-Hasan; al-Tabari chooses the singular, since stating the ruling for one day leads to the ruling for the whole month, and not the reverse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 439} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 440}

The scholars differed over the amount of food: some said half a sa' of wheat; some a mudd of wheat and of their other staple foods; some half a sa' of wheat or a sa' of dates or raisins; some whatever the one who broke the fast ate that day; and some a pre-dawn meal and an evening meal for the poor person; al-Tabari had mentioned some of these views earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 440} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 441}

On "whoever volunteers good, it is better for him", the commentators differ. Some said: whoever adds to the feeding of one poor person by feeding another, from Mujahid and 'Ata' on Ibn 'Abbas, and from Tawus, 'Ata' and al-Suddi (feeding two poor people). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 441} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 442} Others said: whoever volunteers good by fasting along with the ransom, from Ibn Shihab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 442} Others said: whoever gives the poor person more than the measure of his food; the names of those who said this continue on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 442}
