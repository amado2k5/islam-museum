## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "a limited number of days", al-Tabari says the sense is that fasting has been prescribed for you for a limited number of days, and that "days" is in the accusative through an implied verb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 413}

Al-Tabari says "days" is in the accusative by an implied verb, as if it were said "it has been prescribed for you to fast a limited number of days". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 414} He then reports the differing views on the "limited days". Some said they are the fast of three days of each month, which was prescribed before Ramadan, from 'Ata', Ibn 'Abbas (who adds that it was then abrogated by the fast of Ramadan) and Qatada; in the report from Mu'adh ibn Jabal the Prophet, on coming to Medina, fasted 'Ashura and three days of each month until Ramadan was made obligatory, and the editor traces the report and comments on some of its narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 414} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 415} Others said those three days were voluntary and that the verse means the days of Ramadan, not those days; in the report of Shu'ba from 'Amr ibn Murra, "our companions told us" that the Prophet on arriving told them to fast three days of each month voluntarily, not as an obligation, and then the fast of Ramadan was revealed; the editor discusses the chain at length and the phrase "our companions told us". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 415} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 416}

Al-Tabari chooses that the "limited days" are the days of the month of Ramadan, because no report has come, strong enough to be proof, that any fast other than Ramadan was made obligatory on the Muslims and then abrogated by it, and because God made clear in the course of the verse that the fast is that of "the month of Ramadan"; anyone claiming another obligatory fast is asked for proof from a report of that standing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 417} He allows also that "fasting has been prescribed for you" means the month of Ramadan has been prescribed, and that "limited" means days whose totals and hours are counted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 417}

On "whoever of you is ill or on a journey, then a number of other days", he says: whoever is ill among those charged with the fast, or is healthy but travelling, owes the number of days he broke, from days other than those of his illness or journey; the nominative of "a number" is like the nominative in "a following with kindness". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 418} On "and for those who are able to do it, a ransom of feeding a poor person", the reading of the Muslims at large is "yutiqunahu", the reading of the copies of the Qur'an, which al-Tabari holds none may depart from since all transmitted it generation after generation; it is reported that Ibn 'Abbas read "yutawwaqunahu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 418} The commentators then differ on its sense. Some said that this was at the first prescription of the fast: whoever of the residents could fast might fast if he wished, or break the fast and ransom by feeding a poor person for each day, until it was abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 418} In the report from Mu'adh ibn Jabal, whoever wished fasted and whoever wished broke the fast and fed a poor person; then God made the fast obligatory on the healthy resident and left feeding in place for the old man unable to fast; in Shu'ba's report the people were not used to fasting and it was hard on them, and then the verse came and the concession was for the sick and the traveller. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 419}
