## Summary
Verse 121 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 121 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Link to the preceding verses (p. 159).** Al-Tabari says that if the believers are patient and godwary the scheming of these Jews will not harm them at all, but God will help them against them as he helped them at Badr when they were weak; and if they oppose his command, do not hold to the duties he laid on them and do not guard against what he forbade, what befell them at Uhud will befall them. So he reminds them of that day when their Prophet went out early to place the believers in their positions for battle. He holds that the report of what happens if they are not patient is left unstated because the flow of the speech indicates it: it mentions what God does for them in turning aside their enemies' scheming if they are patient, then follows with a reminder of the trial at Uhud, when some of them opposed the command of the Messenger of God and disputed among themselves. The address is outwardly to the Prophet, but those meant are the ones forbidden to take the Jews as intimates, and the particle at the start has its construction from the sense of the speech as he has explained. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 159}

**The differing view on which day is meant (pp. 159-160).** Some said it is the day of Uhud; others said it is the day of the Confederates. Those who said Uhud: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 159}

- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7708): the Prophet walked on his feet that day, placing the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 160}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7709): it is the day of Uhud; the Prophet of God went out from his family to Uhud to place the believers in their battle positions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 160}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7710): the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 160}
- From Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, Ibn Abbas (7711): it is the day of Uhud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 160}
- From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7712): this is the day of Uhud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 160}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7713): this is among what was revealed on the day of Uhud; the editor says the report is abridged from the Sira of Ibn Hisham (3: 112). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 160}

Those who said the Confederates are introduced at the foot of p. 160 with the report of Muhammad ibn Sinan al-Qazzaz from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, which continues on p. 161.

**The Confederates view (p. 161).** The transmitter begun at the foot of the previous page continues:
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan al-Qazzaz, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7714; the number is unreadable in the scanned print and is inferred from the sequence): the one meant is Muhammad, who went out to set up the believers' battle stations on the day of the Confederates. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 161}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 161).** Abu Ja'far says the sounder of the two views, in his judgement, is that the day is Uhud, because the following verse speaks of the two parties who resolved to lose heart, and there is no disagreement among the exegetes that these were Banu Salima and Banu Haritha, nor among the biographers that what is said of them took place at Uhud and not on the day of the Confederates. In his note the editor gives the vowelling of Salima (a fatha on the first letter and a kasra on the lam), says no other Arab group has the name with that kasra, and traces the tribe's line to Jusham ibn al-Khazraj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 161}

**An objection and its answer (pp. 161-162).** Al-Tabari imagines a questioner asking how this can be Uhud when the Prophet set out for Uhud only after leading the Friday prayer in Medina. He cites Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, who took it from al-Zuhri, Muhammad ibn Yahya ibn Hibban, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, al-Husayn ibn Abd al-Rahman and other scholars of the campaigns (7715): after the Friday prayer the Prophet set out for Uhud and put on his armour; a man of the Ansar had died that day and he prayed over him, then he came out and said it is not fitting for a prophet who has put on his armour to take it off until he has fought. The editor says the chain is in the Sira of Ibn Hisham (3: 64), that Abu Ja'far abridged Ibn Ishaq's account there (3: 67-68), that the armour word means the strong coat of mail and equipment of war in general, and that he restored Ibn Hisham's wording because the printed text and the manuscript gave a wording he thought a copyist's haste. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 161} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 162}

**Al-Tabari's reply (p. 162).** He answers that the departure may have been in the afternoon, but the positioning of the believers came before it: the polytheists had camped near Uhud on Wednesday and stayed through Thursday and Friday, then the Prophet went to them after Friday prayer and by Saturday, mid-Shawwal, was at the pass of Uhud (7716, Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from those named); the editor says Abu Ja'far gathered this from separate places in Ibn Ishaq's account of Uhud. To a second questioner, who asks how the positioning could have been in the morning before the departure when it means taking up a place, he replies that it was a day or two before meeting the enemy, when he consulted his Companions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 162}

**The Prophet's consultation (pp. 162-164).**
- From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7717): when he heard of the polytheists' arrival he asked his Companions what to do. Some said to go out against those dogs, while the Ansar said no enemy had beaten them when it came to their homes, still less with the Prophet among them. He called Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul, whom he had never called before, who advised going out; the Prophet had liked the idea of fighting them in the lanes of Medina. Al-Nu'man ibn Malik al-Ansari came and asked not to be denied Paradise since he bore witness to the oneness of God and the Prophet's mission and would not flee the advance; the Prophet said he spoke truly, and he was killed that day. Then the Prophet called for his armour and put it on; they regretted advising him while revelation came to him, apologised and told him to do as he saw fit, and he said it is not fitting for a prophet to put on his armour and then lay it down until he has fought. The editor notes that it is in al-Tabari's History (3: 11-12). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 162} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 163}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, from al-Zuhri, Ibn Yahya ibn Hibban, Asim, al-Husayn and others (7718): the Prophet said he had seen cattle in a dream and read it as good, a notch in the edge of his sword, and his hand in a strong coat of mail, which he read as Medina; he suggested staying in Medina, where, if the polytheists stayed, they would stay in the worst place, and if they entered, the Muslims would fight them there. Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul agreed. Men who had missed Badr urged going out so that the enemy would not think them cowardly, and Abdullah repeated the advice to stay, saying women and boys would throw stones from above; the men kept on until the Prophet went in and put on his armour. The editor says it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 66-67) as the continuation of report 7715, and explains the sword's edge as its striking tip and the notch as a break in its blade. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 163} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 164}

Al-Tabari concludes that this consultation was the Prophet's placing of the believers, as the transmitters describe. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 164}

**Language (p. 164).** He says the verb is used as in "I lodged the people in a house", and a reading attributed to Abdullah ibn Mas'ud has a passive form with a preposition before the believers; he allows it, as one says one thing both ways for other verbs. He cites a line on seeking forgiveness as an example of dropping a preposition, which the editor traces to Volume 1 and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 233), noting that the paragraph is from al-Farra; the line is not reproduced here. He reports Arab usage for the related verb, used of bringing camels back to their resting place, and says the plural in the verse is that of a seat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 164}

**The sense (p. 165).** Abu Ja'far says the meaning is: remember, Muhammad, when you set out from your family to take up a camp and a place for fighting their enemy. On the closing attributes he says God hears what the believers say in the consultation about where to meet the enemy, both those who said to go out and those who said to stay in Medina, and what the Prophet advised, and knows the best of these opinions and what the hearts of both groups of advisers conceal. The editor notes he chose a reading different from the print and manuscript because the context requires it. Report 7719 (Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq): hearing what they say, knowing what they conceal; the editor places it in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 112), continuing 7713. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 165}
