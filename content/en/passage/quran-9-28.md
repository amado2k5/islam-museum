**Commentary on verse 28 (pp. 190-192)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses the believers in Him and His messenger who acknowledged His oneness: the polytheists are only impure. The interpreters differed over the sense of "impure" and the reason He called them so. Some said He called them that because they fall into a state of major impurity and do not wash, so they are impure and must not approach the Sacred Mosque, since one in that state should not enter a mosque. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 190}

Report 16591, from Ma'mar, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Muhammad ibn Thawr: I do not know that Qatada said other than that "impure" is the state of major impurity; report 16592, from Ma'mar: it reached me that the Prophet met Hudhayfa and took his hand, and Hudhayfa said "Messenger of God, I am in a state of major impurity", and he said the believer is not made impure. Report 16593, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: that is, people in that state. Others said the sense is that the polytheists are only the filth of a pig or a dog; al-Tabari notes that this is related from Ibn Abbas by a route other than Humayd's and says he disliked to mention it. The words "so let them not approach the Sacred Mosque after this year" tell the believers not to let them approach it by entering the sanctuary; what is meant is barring them from entering the sanctuary, since entering the sanctuary is approaching the Sacred Mosque. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 191}

The interpreters differed over this too. Some said much as he says. Report 16594, from Ata, through Bishr, Ibn al-Muthanna, Abu Asim and Ibn Jurayj: the whole sanctuary is a direction of prayer and a mosque, and "do not approach the Sacred Mosque" does not mean the mosque alone but Mecca and the sanctuary; he said so more than once. Report 16595 is related of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, through Abd al-Karim ibn Abi Umayr, al-Walid ibn Muslim and Abu Amr: he wrote to bar Jews and Christians from entering the mosques of the Muslims, following his prohibition with God's words that the polytheists are impure. Report 16596, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Fudayl and Ash'ath: do not shake their hands, and whoever shakes them should make ablution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 192}

As for "after this year of theirs", it means after the year in which Ali made the proclamation of the sura, the year Abu Bakr led the people's pilgrimage, the ninth year after the emigration. Report 16597, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it is the year in which Abu Bakr made the pilgrimage and Ali made the proclamation, nine years after the Messenger's emigration, and the next year the Prophet made the farewell pilgrimage, having made none before or after. "If you fear poverty" tells the believers that if they fear want and need by barring the polytheists from approaching the Sacred Mosque, "God will enrich you from His bounty if He wills"; one says "he was needy" with the verbal noun of need, and a line by Uhayha ibn al-Jallah says that the poor man does not know when his riches will come, nor the rich when he will be in need. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the line and to Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 192}

We stop here at the foot of p. 192, in the middle of the commentary on verse 28 (after the explanation of "poverty"); the next batch begins at p. 193. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 192}
