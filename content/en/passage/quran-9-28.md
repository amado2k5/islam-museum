## Summary
Verse 28 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 28 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 28 (pp. 190-198)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses the believers in Him and His messenger who acknowledged His oneness: the polytheists are only impure. The interpreters differed over the sense of "impure" and the reason He called them so. Some said He called them that because they fall into a state of major impurity and do not wash, so they are impure and must not approach the Sacred Mosque, since one in that state should not enter a mosque. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 190}

Report 16591, from Ma'mar, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Muhammad ibn Thawr: I do not know that Qatada said other than that "impure" is the state of major impurity; report 16592, from Ma'mar: it reached me that the Prophet met Hudhayfa and took his hand, and Hudhayfa said "Messenger of God, I am in a state of major impurity", and he said the believer is not made impure. Report 16593, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: that is, people in that state. Others said the sense is that the polytheists are only the filth of a pig or a dog; al-Tabari notes that this is related from Ibn Abbas by a route other than Humayd's and says he disliked to mention it. The words "so let them not approach the Sacred Mosque after this year" tell the believers not to let them approach it by entering the sanctuary; what is meant is barring them from entering the sanctuary, since entering the sanctuary is approaching the Sacred Mosque. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 191}

The interpreters differed over this too. Some said much as he says. Report 16594, from Ata, through Bishr, Ibn al-Muthanna, Abu Asim and Ibn Jurayj: the whole sanctuary is a direction of prayer and a mosque, and "do not approach the Sacred Mosque" does not mean the mosque alone but Mecca and the sanctuary; he said so more than once. Report 16595 is related of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz, through Abd al-Karim ibn Abi Umayr, al-Walid ibn Muslim and Abu Amr: he wrote to bar Jews and Christians from entering the mosques of the Muslims, following his prohibition with God's words that the polytheists are impure. Report 16596, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Fudayl and Ash'ath: do not shake their hands, and whoever shakes them should make ablution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 192}

As for "after this year of theirs", it means after the year in which Ali made the proclamation of the sura, the year Abu Bakr led the people's pilgrimage, the ninth year after the emigration. Report 16597, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it is the year in which Abu Bakr made the pilgrimage and Ali made the proclamation, nine years after the Messenger's emigration, and the next year the Prophet made the farewell pilgrimage, having made none before or after. "If you fear poverty" tells the believers that if they fear want and need by barring the polytheists from approaching the Sacred Mosque, "God will enrich you from His bounty if He wills"; one says "he was needy" with the verbal noun of need, and a line by Uhayha ibn al-Jallah says that the poor man does not know when his riches will come, nor the rich when he will be in need. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the line and to Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 192}

Al-Tabari relates of some that there are Arabs who say the verb for need with a waw. It is reported of Amr ibn Fa'id that he took the words "if you fear poverty" in the sense of "since you feared", saying the people had indeed been afraid, like a man's saying to his father "if you are my father, honour me", meaning since you are; the editor describes Amr ibn Fa'id al-Aswari as one who reported readings of the Qur'an, rejected and abandoned in hadith. It was said to them, al-Tabari says, because the believers feared that with the polytheists cut off from the sanctuary their trade would be cut and harm would reach them; God made them safe from want and compensated them for what they disliked losing with something better, the poll tax, saying to them to fight those who do not believe in God and the last day up to "humbled", in [[passage/quran-9-29|9:29]]. Some said the compensation was the sending of rain upon them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 193}

Report 16598, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah, Mu'awiya and Ali: when God banished the polytheists from the Sacred Mosque, Satan cast grief into the believers' hearts, saying where will you eat now the polytheists are banished and the caravans cut off; God said if you fear poverty He would enrich them from His bounty if He willed, and told them to fight the People of the Book, enriching them from His bounty. Report 16599, from Ikrima, through Hannad ibn al-Sari, Abu al-Ahwas and Simak: the polytheists used to come to the House bringing food and trading there; when they were forbidden to come, the Muslims asked where their food would come from, and God sent down the verse and sent rain on them and their good increased until the polytheists were gone; report 16600 is similar. Report 16601, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal, Sufyan and Waqid: when the verse came down it was hard on the Messenger's Companions, who asked who would bring their food and goods, and the verse on poverty came down; report 16602: the polytheists used to come to them with trade, and "poverty" means want. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 194}

Report 16603, from Atiyya al-Awfi, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Idris and his father: the Muslims said they used to gain from their trade and sales, and the verse came down. Report 16604, from Atiyya, through Abu Kurayb: when it was said that no polytheist should perform the pilgrimage after the year they said they used to profit from sales at the season, and the verse came down, meaning in place of the sales they lost. Report 16605, from al-Dahhak, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Waki, Ibn Yaman, Abu Sinan and Thabit: the poll tax. Report 16606: the polytheists were driven out of Mecca, which was hard on the Muslims, who said they used to gain trade and provisions from them, and God sent down the command to fight those who do not believe in God and the last day. Report 16607, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: some Muslims used to cultivate the caravans, and when the sura of disavowal came down commanding the fighting of the polytheists wherever found and lying in wait for them at every ambush, Satan cast into the believers' hearts the thought of how they would live when told to fight the caravan people; God knew what He knew and said obey Me, go on with My command and obey My messenger, for I will enrich you from My bounty, and He took it upon Himself for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 195}

Report 16608, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the believers said they used to gain from the polytheists' markets, and God promised to enrich them from His bounty in exchange for not letting them approach the Sacred Mosque; this verse is with the start of the sura of disavowal in the reading and with its end in meaning, the command to fight those who do not believe in God and the last day up to "humbled", when Muhammad and his Companions were ordered on the Tabuk expedition; report 16609 is similar through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj, and a further report from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, says that when God expelled the polytheists from the Sacred Mosque it was hard on the Muslims, who used to profit from what the sellers brought, and He enriched them with this tribute, the poll tax paid by them month after month and year after year, so no polytheist may approach the Sacred Mosque after their year in any case, except one who pays the poll tax or the slave of a Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 196}

Report 16610, from Jabir ibn Abdullah, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Jurayj and Abu al-Zubayr: except a slave or one of the protected peoples; report 16611, from Qatada: except one who pays the poll tax or a Muslim's slave; report 16612, from Jabir: except a slave or one of the poll-tax people; report 16613, from Qatada: God enriched them with the poll tax running month by month and year by year; report 16614, from Jabir, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Abbad ibn al-Awwam, al-Hajjaj and Abu al-Zubayr: after this year neither a polytheist nor a protected person approaches the Sacred Mosque. Report 16615, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: people said the markets would be cut off from them, trade ruined and the benefits they gained lost, and God said that if they feared poverty He would enrich them from His bounty by another route if He willed, up to "humbled", so that in this was compensation for the fear of losing those markets, God recompensing them for what He cut off of polytheism with what He gave of the necks of the People of the Book through the poll tax. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 197}

As for "God is knowing, wise", the sense is that God knows what your selves told you, believers, of fear of want if the polytheists were barred from approaching the Sacred Mosque, and of other benefits to His servants, and wise in His management of them and of all His creation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 198}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
