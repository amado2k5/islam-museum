## Summary
Verse 46 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 46 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The two constructions of the next clause (pp. 430-431, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says the clause about the Jews can be taken in two ways: first, as a relative clause attached to "those who were handed a portion of scripture", which was the view of most Kufan grammarians; second, as meaning "among the Jews are those who alter", with the relative word dropped because the partitive preposition indicates it, as Arabs say that among us are some who say this and some who do not. He cites a line of Dhu al-Rumma about a company some of whom wept freely while others held back their tears (described, not reproduced), and the verse at [[passage/quran-37-164|37:164]]. Most Basran grammarians took the same course but said the dropped word is "people", citing a line of al-Nabigha comparing the addressee to one of the camels of Banu Uqaysh, where the dropped word is "camel"; the Kufans deny that anything but "who" or its like can be dropped after the partitive. The editor says Dhu al-Rumma's line is in his Diwan with variant readings, that the print altered a word and the manuscript agrees with the Diwan, and explains that holding back the tears means checking their flow; that he had traced al-Nabigha's line earlier and forgot to refer it here; he refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani, and in note 4 says the print added a word wrongly and the manuscript had a corrupt phrase from a scribe's omission, the right form being from al-Farra. The scan of the couplets is partly garbled by OCR, so they are not transcribed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 431}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 432, note 1).** Abu Ja'far says the better view in his opinion is of those who take the clause as attached to those handed a portion of scripture, since both statements and both descriptions are of one kind of people, the Jews whom God described first; this is also what the interpreters said, so there is no need to suppose anything dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 432}

**The sense of altering the words (p. 432, reports 9691-9692, note 1).** Al-Tabari says their distorting means they change the meaning of the words and move them from their interpretation; "words" is the plural of "word". Mujahid used to say it means the Torah. Muhammad b. Amr related from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that this is the Jews' altering of the Torah (9691); al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, the same (9692). Al-Tabari adds that "from their places" means from their positions and the aspects that are their aspects. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the word for distortion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 432}

**Their claim that they heard the word and went against the order (p. 433, reports 9693-9696).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that some of the Jews say to the Prophet that they heard his word and disobeyed his command. Ibn Humayd related from Hakkam, from Anbasa, from Muhammad b. Abd al-Rahman, from al-Qasim b. Abi Bazza, from Mujahid that the Jews said: we heard what you say and we do not obey you (9693); Muhammad b. Amr from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, the same (9694); al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, the same (9695); Yunus from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd: they said we have heard, but we do not obey you (9696). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 433}

**The phrase of address that denies hearing (pp. 433-435, reports 9697-9702, note 1).** Al-Tabari says this is a report from God about the Jews around the Prophet's place of emigration in his day, who abused him and hurt him with vile speech, saying to him a phrase like that of a man to someone he is cursing: hear, and may God not let you hear. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd that this was the saying of the People of the Book, the Jews, like a man saying to another "hear, may you not hear", an affront to the Messenger, abuse and mockery (9697); al-Munjab, to whom the chain is given as "I was told from", from Bishr b. Umara, from Abu Rawq, from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas: they say to you hear, may you not hear (9698). Al-Tabari then says it was reported from Mujahid and al-Hasan that they took the phrase as "hear, though not accepted from you", and answers that if this were the meaning, one would say "not heard"; the meaning is "hear, and may you not hear", and God described them as twisting speech with their tongues and attacking religion by abusing the Prophet. As for what Mujahid said, al-Qasim related from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Mujahid that it means "not listened to", and Ibn Jurayj added from al-Qasim b. Abi Bazza, from Mujahid, that it means what you say is not accepted (9699); al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, the same (9700); al-Hasan b. Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Hasan that it is like saying "hear, though not heard from you" (9701); Musa b. Harun from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that some of them said it in the sense "hear, not one who is humbled" (9702). The editor says the print had another word instead of "humbled" and the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 435}

**The word with two meanings (pp. 435-436, reports 9703-9707, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says its sense is "give us your hearing, understand us and make us understand", and that he explained it in Surat al-Baqara with its proofs ([[passage/quran-2-104|2:104]]). God then reports that they say it to the Messenger twisting their tongues, that is moving them to turn its meaning to the worse of its two meanings, as a slight on the Prophet's right and an attack on the religion. Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada that the Jews used to say it to the Prophet mockingly, and that it was a vile word among the Jews, the twisting being their moving their tongues with it (9703); "I was told from" al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman, from al-Dahhak that a man among the polytheists would say a nearby form, twisting his tongue so as to alter its sense (9704); Muhammad b. Sa'd from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas that they mocked, twisted their tongues at the Messenger and attacked religion (9705); Yunus from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd that the word was their attack on religion and their twisting tongues to invalidate and deny it, and that the "ra'in" is error in speech (9706); al-Munjab from Bishr, from Abu Rawq, from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas that twisting is distortion by lying (9707). The editor says that in 9703 the manuscript and print have a sentence that cannot stand and what he put is certainly right; he refers to earlier explanations of twisting, of the word being vile among the Jews, and of the "ra'in". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 436}

**If they had spoken well (pp. 436-437, reports 9708-9711, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: had these Jews told the Prophet that they heard his word and obeyed his command and accepted what he brought from God, and asked him to hear them and wait until they understood, it would have been better for them with God and more upright and sound in speech; the word for "more upright" is from uprightness, as at [[passage/quran-73-6|73:6]]. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd that they would say: hear us, we have heard and obeyed, and give us time, do not rush us (9708); al-Qasim from al-Husayn, from Abu Tumayla, from Abu Hamza, from Jabir, from Ikrima and Mujahid that the word of waiting means "hear us" (9709); al-Qasim from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Mujahid: make us understand (9710); Muhammad b. Amr from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, the same (9711). Abu Ja'far then says that what Mujahid and Ikrima said, taking the word as "hear from us", and Mujahid's "make us understand", is not known to him in Arab usage, unless Mujahid meant "wait for us so that you understand what you say" or "wait for us until we speak so that you hear from us", which is an intelligible sense though not an interpretation of the word; and he knows the word in Arab usage only as "wait for us" or "look at us". For "wait" he cites a line of al-Hutay'a about waiting for people in the hope that their milk would come with stroking and soothing sounds (described, not reproduced), and for "look at us" a line of Ubaydallah b. Qays al-Ruqayyat about beautiful women gazing as gazelles gaze toward the arak tree (described, not reproduced). The editor says he added a particle in one sentence because it is more idiomatic, and that the print and manuscript differ in another place, where he chose the manuscript's reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 437}

**The editor's note on the lines of al-Hutay'a and Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat (pp. 437-439, notes 5 and 1).** The editor says al-Hutay'a's line is in his Diwan and in al-Mubarrad's al-Kamil, that al-Tabari's version of it has a mix-up beyond doubt which he left as it stands because it shows al-Tabari's occasional haste in writing his commentary as well as his memory of poetry, that the poem is the one in which al-Hutay'a lampooned al-Zibriqan b. Badr, who complained of him to Umar, who imprisoned him, and that the line which al-Tabari should have cited is the one he gave in Surat al-Baqara. He then explains the words: stroking the udder so the she-camel gives milk, the flow of milk, and the shepherd's sound that soothes the animal so the milking is easy; the sense is that the poet was gentle with them, trying to draw out their goodness with tender praise, and found none. The manuscript has a wrong reading of one word. On Ibn Qays al-Ruqayyat's line he says it is in his Diwan, in a poem boasting of Quraysh and praising Mus'ab b. al-Zubayr and recalling the women of the Abd Shams clan, with variation in the transmission, and that "sarw" means nobility and generosity of stock. (The text of these notes is partly garbled in the scan, so only what is legible is given.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 439}

**The close of the verse: being driven away and little belief (p. 439, report 9712, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says God disgraced these Jews whose description is in the verse, putting them far from right guidance and from following the truth because of their denial of Muhammad's prophethood and of the guidance and clear proofs he brought from his Lord, so they do not affirm Muhammad or what he brought, nor acknowledge his prophethood, except in small measure. Al-Hasan b. Yahya related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada that they do not believe except a few (9712). Al-Tabari says he explained the point with its reasons in Surat al-Baqara ([[passage/quran-2-88|2:88]]). The editor refers to the parallel word of waiting in al-Baqara, to the explanation of cursing, and to the parallel verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 439}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 439}
