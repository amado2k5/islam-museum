**Commentary on verse 31 (pp. 446-451)**

On "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to, nay, to God belongs the matter altogether", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its meaning. Some said its meaning is: "they disbelieve in the Merciful", "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved", that is, they would disbelieve in God even if the mountains were moved for them by this Qur'an. They said this is of the postponed that is meant to be put first, and made the answer of "if" come before it, because the speech {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 446}

means what they said: if this Qur'an were to move mountains or split the earth, they would still disbelieve in the Merciful. Report 20399, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to", he said, these are the polytheists of Quraysh, who said to the Messenger of God: if you widened the valleys of Mecca for us and moved its mountains so that we could plough them, and revived those of us who died, and the earth were split by it or the dead spoken to; so God, exalted, said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to, nay, to God belongs the matter altogether". Report 20400, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: this is the saying of the disbelievers of Quraysh to Muhammad: move our mountains so that our land widens for us, since it is narrow, or bring Syria near us, for we trade there, or bring our fathers out of the graves so that we may speak to them; so God, exalted, said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to"; the editor notes that "so God, exalted, said" is missing from the manuscript though required. Report 20401, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: similarly. Report 20402, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: likewise; Ibn Jurayj said, and Abd Allah ibn Kathir said, they said if you had cleared these mountains away for us, or run rivers for us, or spoken to the dead by it; and that was revealed; Ibn Jurayj said, and Ibn Abbas said, they said, move the mountains with the Qur'an, split the earth with the Qur'an, bring out our dead by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 447}

Report 20403, from Ibn Kathir, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they said, if you had cleared the mountains away for us, or run rivers for us, or spoken to the dead by it, and "have those who believed not despaired" was revealed. Others said the meaning is rather that "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved" is a new sentence cut off from "while they disbelieve in the Merciful"; the answer to "if" is omitted, the hearers' knowledge of what is meant sparing its mention. They said the Arabs often do this, as in the saying of Imru' al-Qays in a verse saying that if it were a soul that died all at once, and then the consequence is left unstated because the hearer knows what is meant; and as another said in a verse swearing that if his messenger had come to them with something other than what he came with, they would still have found no way to turn it aside. The editor notes in the footnotes the differing transmissions of Imru' al-Qays's verse and that the printed edition of his diwan left them out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 448}

Report 20404, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to", it was mentioned to us that Quraysh said, if it pleases you, Muhammad, that we follow you, then move the mountains of Tihama for us, or add to our sanctuary so that we may take estates in which to spend the autumn, or revive for us so-and-so and so-and-so, people who died in the Jahiliyya; so God revealed "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved"; He says, if this were done for a Qur'an before your Qur'an, it would be done for your Qur'an. Report 20405, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the disbelievers of Quraysh said to the Prophet, take away from us the mountains of Tihama so that we may make them tillage and they become lands for us, or bring so-and-so and so-and-so to life to tell us what you say is true; so God said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to, nay, to God belongs the matter altogether"; He says, if that were done with any of the Books in the past, it would have been. Report 20406, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: the disbelievers of Quraysh said to Muhammad, move the mountains for us as they were subjected to David, or split the earth for us as it was split for Solomon, who went forth with it a month in the morning and returned with it a month in the evening, or speak to the dead for us as Jesus used to speak to them; He says, I did not send down a book for this, but it was something I gave My prophets and My messengers. Report 20407, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 449}

on "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved", the verse, he said, they said to the Prophet, if you are truthful, move these mountains from us and make them tillage like the land of Syria, Egypt and the other countries, or raise our dead and let them tell them, for they died on what we are on; God said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to", this was never done with any Qur'an or book, so would it be done with this Qur'an? Then al-Tabari comments on "have those who believed not despaired that, if God willed, He would guide all people?": the scholars of Arab speech differed over the meaning of "a-fa-lam yay'as". One of the Basrans claimed that it means "did they not know and see clearly", citing in support a verse of Suhaym ibn Wathil al-Riyahi, in which he says to them in the mountain pass as they take him captive, have you not known that I am the son of Zahdam's horseman; it is also transmitted as "yaysirunani", and whoever narrates it so means they divide me up, from "maysir", as a camel is divided, while whoever narrates "ya'sirunani" means taking captive, and he said that by "have you not despaired" he meant have you not known. They also recited a verse attributed to Malik ibn Awf and to Riyah ibn Adi, saying, have the peoples not known that I am his son, though I am far from the land of my clan; the editor notes the sources of the two verses and the attribution of the first to Suhaym or to Jabir ibn Suhaym. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 450}

They interpreted "a-fa-lam yay'as" as "did he not know and see clearly?" It is reported from Ibn al-Kalbi that this is the dialect of a clan of Nakha' called Wahbil, who say "a-lam tay'as kadha" meaning "did you not know it?"; and from al-Qasim ibn Ma'n that it is the dialect of Hawazin, who say "ya'istu kadha" for "I knew". Some of the Kufans denied that, claiming they had never heard any Arab say "ya'istu" for "I knew"; he says it is meant in that sense even if not heard, since it was put to the believers that had He willed He would have guided all people, so He said "did they not despair knowingly", meaning knowledge makes them despair, as though "knowledge" were implied, as one says "I despaired of you that you would not succeed, knowingly", as if it were said "I knew it knowingly". He said, and the saying of the poet Labid in his Mu'allaqa, describing the hunting of the wild cow, "until when the hunters despaired and sent slack-eared trained dogs", means until when they despaired of everything possible except what appeared to them, they sent. The editor notes that this is the text of al-Farra's words in Ma'ani al-Qur'an and that the "ghudf" are hunting dogs with drooping ears. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 451}

We stop here at the foot of p. 451, in the middle of the commentary on verse 31 (in the Kufan's citing of Labid's verse for the sense of "ya'isa"); the next batch begins at p. 452 (marker 460). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 451}
