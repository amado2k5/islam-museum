## Summary
Verse 31 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 31 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 31 (pp. 446-458)**

On "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to, nay, to God belongs the matter altogether", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its meaning. Some said its meaning is: "they disbelieve in the Merciful", "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved", that is, they would disbelieve in God even if the mountains were moved for them by this Qur'an. They said this is of the postponed that is meant to be put first, and made the answer of "if" come before it, because the speech {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 446}

means what they said: if this Qur'an were to move mountains or split the earth, they would still disbelieve in the Merciful. Report 20399, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to", he said, these are the polytheists of Quraysh, who said to the Messenger of God: if you widened the valleys of Mecca for us and moved its mountains so that we could plough them, and revived those of us who died, and the earth were split by it or the dead spoken to; so God, exalted, said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to, nay, to God belongs the matter altogether". Report 20400, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: this is the saying of the disbelievers of Quraysh to Muhammad: move our mountains so that our land widens for us, since it is narrow, or bring Syria near us, for we trade there, or bring our fathers out of the graves so that we may speak to them; so God, exalted, said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to"; the editor notes that "so God, exalted, said" is missing from the manuscript though required. Report 20401, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: similarly. Report 20402, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: likewise; Ibn Jurayj said, and Abd Allah ibn Kathir said, they said if you had cleared these mountains away for us, or run rivers for us, or spoken to the dead by it; and that was revealed; Ibn Jurayj said, and Ibn Abbas said, they said, move the mountains with the Qur'an, split the earth with the Qur'an, bring out our dead by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 447}

Report 20403, from Ibn Kathir, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they said, if you had cleared the mountains away for us, or run rivers for us, or spoken to the dead by it, and "have those who believed not despaired" was revealed. Others said the meaning is rather that "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved" is a new sentence cut off from "while they disbelieve in the Merciful"; the answer to "if" is omitted, the hearers' knowledge of what is meant sparing its mention. They said the Arabs often do this, as in the saying of Imru' al-Qays in a verse saying that if it were a soul that died all at once, and then the consequence is left unstated because the hearer knows what is meant; and as another said in a verse swearing that if his messenger had come to them with something other than what he came with, they would still have found no way to turn it aside. The editor notes in the footnotes the differing transmissions of Imru' al-Qays's verse and that the printed edition of his diwan left them out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 448}

Report 20404, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to", it was mentioned to us that Quraysh said, if it pleases you, Muhammad, that we follow you, then move the mountains of Tihama for us, or add to our sanctuary so that we may take estates in which to spend the autumn, or revive for us so-and-so and so-and-so, people who died in the Jahiliyya; so God revealed "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved"; He says, if this were done for a Qur'an before your Qur'an, it would be done for your Qur'an. Report 20405, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the disbelievers of Quraysh said to the Prophet, take away from us the mountains of Tihama so that we may make them tillage and they become lands for us, or bring so-and-so and so-and-so to life to tell us what you say is true; so God said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to, nay, to God belongs the matter altogether"; He says, if that were done with any of the Books in the past, it would have been. Report 20406, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: the disbelievers of Quraysh said to Muhammad, move the mountains for us as they were subjected to David, or split the earth for us as it was split for Solomon, who went forth with it a month in the morning and returned with it a month in the evening, or speak to the dead for us as Jesus used to speak to them; He says, I did not send down a book for this, but it was something I gave My prophets and My messengers. Report 20407, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 449}

on "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved", the verse, he said, they said to the Prophet, if you are truthful, move these mountains from us and make them tillage like the land of Syria, Egypt and the other countries, or raise our dead and let them tell them, for they died on what we are on; God said "if there were a Qur'an by which mountains were moved, or the earth were split, or the dead were spoken to", this was never done with any Qur'an or book, so would it be done with this Qur'an? Then al-Tabari comments on "have those who believed not despaired that, if God willed, He would guide all people?": the scholars of Arab speech differed over the meaning of "a-fa-lam yay'as". One of the Basrans claimed that it means "did they not know and see clearly", citing in support a verse of Suhaym ibn Wathil al-Riyahi, in which he says to them in the mountain pass as they take him captive, have you not known that I am the son of Zahdam's horseman; it is also transmitted as "yaysirunani", and whoever narrates it so means they divide me up, from "maysir", as a camel is divided, while whoever narrates "ya'sirunani" means taking captive, and he said that by "have you not despaired" he meant have you not known. They also recited a verse attributed to Malik ibn Awf and to Riyah ibn Adi, saying, have the peoples not known that I am his son, though I am far from the land of my clan; the editor notes the sources of the two verses and the attribution of the first to Suhaym or to Jabir ibn Suhaym. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 450}

They interpreted "a-fa-lam yay'as" as "did he not know and see clearly?" It is reported from Ibn al-Kalbi that this is the dialect of a clan of Nakha' called Wahbil, who say "a-lam tay'as kadha" meaning "did you not know it?"; and from al-Qasim ibn Ma'n that it is the dialect of Hawazin, who say "ya'istu kadha" for "I knew". Some of the Kufans denied that, claiming they had never heard any Arab say "ya'istu" for "I knew"; he says it is meant in that sense even if not heard, since it was put to the believers that had He willed He would have guided all people, so He said "did they not despair knowingly", meaning knowledge makes them despair, as though "knowledge" were implied, as one says "I despaired of you that you would not succeed, knowingly", as if it were said "I knew it knowingly". He said, and the saying of the poet Labid in his Mu'allaqa, describing the hunting of the wild cow, "until when the hunters despaired and sent slack-eared trained dogs", means until when they despaired of everything possible except what appeared to them, they sent. The editor notes that this is the text of al-Farra's words in Ma'ani al-Qur'an and that the "ghudf" are hunting dogs with drooping ears. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 451}

It is in the sense of "until they knew that there was no way but the one they saw and their knowledge reached its limit, so that what was other than it was despair". As for the interpreters, they interpreted it in the sense "did they not know and become clear". Report 20408, from an unnamed freedman, through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Abu Ishaq al-Kufi: Ali used to read "a-fa-lam yatabayyan alladhina amanu"; the editor notes that Abu Ishaq al-Kufi is Abd Allah ibn Maysara, who is weak and flimsy in hadith, and that the chain is flimsy. Report 20409, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Wahhab, Harun, Hanzala and Shahr ibn Hawshab: "a-fa-lam yay'as", he says, "a-fa-lam yatabayyan". Report 20410, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Yusuf, al-Qasim, Yazid, Jarir ibn Hazim, al-Zubayr ibn al-Khirrit or Ya'la ibn Hakim, and Ikrima: he used to read it "a-fa-lam yatabayyan alladhina amanu", saying that the scribe wrote the other while he was drowsy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 452}

In a long note the editor says that the narrators of this report are reliable and its chain sound with no flaw; that, even so, he found none of the great compilers, such as Ahmad in his Musnad, al-Hakim in the Mustadrak or al-Haythami in Majma' al-Zawa'id, who transmitted this report; that Ibn Kathir mentioned Ibn Abbas's reading but passed over this report; that the wording of the versions transmitted without a chain differs from the wording of Abu Ja'far with its chain; that studying the report as it should be studied removes the troubling meaning that comes to mind on first reading it; that he sets beside it the saying of Aisha that the scribe made an error, and denies that the surface sense is meant; that orientalists and their associates take these two reports and their like as a way to attack the Qur'an; and that he wrote a comprehensive treatise on the sense of the Prophet's saying that the Qur'an was sent down in seven ahruf, concluding that all of them remain in the recitations of the reciters, in the unusual readings and in the transmission of the letter forms, unlike what al-Tabari held in the introduction to his commentary, and that he had meant to make it an introduction to the sixteenth volume but chose instead to publish it separately as a book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 453}

Report 20411, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Hajjaj ibn Muhammad: in the first reading, Ibn Kathir and others claimed, "a-fa-lam yatabayyan". Report 20412, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "have those who believed not despaired", he says, has it not become clear. Report 20413, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya ibn Salih and Ali: he says, know. Report 20414, from Mujahid, through Imran ibn Musa, Abd al-Warith and Layth: has it not become clear. Report 20415, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: has it not become clear to those who believed. Report 20416, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: have those who believed not known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 454}

Report 20417, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: have those who believed not known. Al-Tabari says: the correct view is what the interpreters said, that the sense is "has it not become clear and have they not known", by the consensus of the interpreters on it and the verses we recited for it. So the meaning of the speech is: had any Qur'an other than this one had mountains moved by it, they would be moved by this Qur'an, or the earth split by it, it would be split by this, or the dead spoken to by it, they would be spoken to by this; but that was never done with a Qur'an before this one so that it would be done with this. "Nay, to God belongs the matter altogether": all of that is His and in His hand, guiding whom He wills to faith and enabling him to it, and leading astray whom He wills and abandoning him. Has it not become clear to those who believed in God and His Messenger, when they hoped for My answer to what their prophet was asked, of moving the mountains from them, bringing the land of Syria near to them and reviving their dead, that had God willed He would have guided all people to faith in Him without producing a sign or bringing about any of what they asked to be brought about? He says, mighty be His praise: what then is the sense of their loving that, knowing that guidance and misguidance are Mine and in My hand, whether I send down a sign or not, so that I guide whom I will without sending a sign and lead astray whom I will even with one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 455}

On the rest of the verse, "those who disbelieve will not cease to be struck by a calamity for what they did, or it will settle near their home, until God's promise comes; surely God does not fail the appointment", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: those of your people who disbelieved will not cease, Muhammad, to be struck for what they did, their disbelief in God, their denying you and driving you out from among them, by a "qari'a", which is what strikes them of trial, punishment and vengeance, by killing at times, by wars at times and by drought at times, "or it will settle", you, Muhammad, he says, or you will alight, "near their home" with your army and Companions, "until God's promise comes" which He promised you about them, which is your prevailing over them, your conquering their land and your subduing them by the sword; "surely God does not fail the appointment" he says, God will fulfil for you, Muhammad, what He promised you of prevailing over them, for He does not fail His promise. The interpreters said the same: report 20418, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Dawud, al-Mas'udi, Qatada and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "those who disbelieve will not cease to be struck for what they did by a calamity", he said, a raiding party; "or it will settle near their home", he said, Muhammad; "until God's promise comes", he said, the conquest of Mecca; the editor notes that the head of this chain is missing and that he supplied what is added from an earlier chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 456}

Report 20419, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', his father, al-Mas'udi, Qatada and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: similarly but without mentioning "a raiding party". Report 20420, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abu Qatan and al-Mas'udi: the "calamity" is the raiding party; "or it will settle near their home", he said, is Muhammad; "until God's promise comes", he said, the conquest of Mecca. Report 20421, from Ikrima, through al-Muthanna, Abu Ghassan, Zuhayr and Khusayf: it was revealed in Medina about the raiding parties of the Messenger of God; "or it will settle", you, Muhammad, "near their home". Report 20422, from Ikrima, through Ibn Waki', his father and al-Nadr ibn Arabi: "a calamity", a raiding party; "or it will settle near their home", you, Muhammad. Report 20423, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "a calamity", he says, a punishment from heaven coming down on them; "or it will settle near their home", meaning the Messenger of God's coming down on them and fighting them. Report 20424, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 457}

"to be struck for what they did by a calamity", a raiding party of theirs is struck or a misfortune strikes them, "or Muhammad settles near their home", and of "until God's promise comes" he said, the conquest. Report 20425, from Abd Allah ibn Abi Najih, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj and Hammad ibn Zayd: "or it will settle near their home" means the Prophet. Report 20426, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: like the preceding report. Report 20427, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays, Khusayf and Ikrima: "calamity", he said, the raiding parties. Report 20428, from Mujahid, through Abd al-Aziz, Abd al-Ghaffar and Mansur: "calamity", a misfortune from Muhammad; "or it will settle near their home", you, Muhammad; "until God's promise comes", the conquest. Report 20429, from Mujahid, through Isra'il and Khusayf: "calamity", a battalion. Report 20430, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Abd al-Aziz, Amr ibn Thabit and his father: "to be struck for what they did by a calamity", he said, a raiding party; "or it will settle near their home", you, Muhammad. Report 20431, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "those who disbelieve will not cease to be struck by a calamity for what they did", that is, for their works, their evil works; of "or it will settle near their home", you, Muhammad, "until God's promise comes", God's promise is the conquest of Mecca. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 458}

We stop here at the foot of p. 458, in the middle of the commentary on verse 31 (after report 20431, on "qari'a"); the next batch begins with report 20432, which starts at the foot of p. 458 (marker 466) and continues on p. 459. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 458}
