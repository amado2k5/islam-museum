## Summary
Verse 181 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 181 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Occasion (p. 441).** Al-Tabari says it was mentioned that this verse and those after it were sent down about some of the Jews who lived in the time of the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 441}

**Reports 8300-8309 (pp. 441-444).** Ibn 'Abbas (8300), through Abu Kurayb from Yunus ibn Bukayr from Muhammad ibn Ishaq from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, client of Zayd ibn Thabit, from 'Ikrima: Abu Bakr al-Siddiq entered the house of study and found many Jews gathered around a man called Finhas, one of their scholars and rabbis, with a rabbi called Ashtiy' (as printed). Abu Bakr told Finhas to fear God and submit, since he knew that Muhammad was God's messenger, who had come with the truth and was found written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. Finhas answered with words that ascribed need to God and wealth to the Jews, saying that had God been free of need of them He would not have asked a loan of them, as Abu Bakr's companion claimed, and that He forbids usury yet gives it to them. Abu Bakr was angry and struck Finhas's face hard, saying that but for the covenant between them he would have struck his neck; the report has him call Finhas an enemy of God and challenge him to deny it if he was truthful. Finhas went to the Prophet to complain, and the Prophet asked Abu Bakr what had moved him; Abu Bakr said he had been angry for God's sake at what Finhas said. Finhas denied having said it, and the verse came down answering him and confirming Abu Bakr. On Abu Bakr's anger and what reached him, the report names what is in [[passage/quran-3-186|3:186]]. The first note says the manuscript had a gap here which the printed edition filled from al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 105); the editor kept it because it agrees with Ibn Kathir's commentary (2: 308) although it differs in some wording from Ibn Hisham's biography. The second note says reports 8300 and 8301 are in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 207-208) and follow report 7695, among what al-Tabari transmitted from Ibn Ishaq's biography. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 442}

Ibn Ishaq (8301), through Ibn Humayd from Salama from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad from 'Ikrima, client of Ibn 'Abbas: Abu Bakr entered, and he told it similarly, except that the phrase on need and wealth is worded differently, then gave the rest of the report likewise; the note refers to Finhas's story in report 8316. Al-Suddi (8302), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: it was said by Finhas the Jew of a tribe whose name is not clear in the scanned edition and appears in two forms in the report itself; Abu Bakr met him and told him to fear God, believe, affirm and lend God a goodly loan. Finhas asked whether he claimed their Lord was poor and borrowed their money, since only the poor borrow from the rich, and said that if this were true God would be poor. The verse came down, and Abu Bakr said that had there not been a truce between the Prophet and those people he would have killed him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 443}

Mujahid (8303), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: Abu Bakr struck a man of those who said it, who had asked how God could ask for a loan if He were rich; they were Jews. Ibn Abi Najih (8304), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl, has the same question, and Shibl said it reached him that it was Finhas the Jew, who was also the one who made the statements recorded in [[passage/quran-5-73|5:73]] and about God's hand in [[passage/quran-5-64|5:64]], as the report has it. Al-Hasan (8305), through Ibn Humayd from Yahya ibn Wadih from 'Ata': when the verse on the goodly loan came down ([[passage/quran-2-245|2:245]], [[passage/quran-57-11|57:11]]) the Jews said your Lord borrows from you, and this verse came down. Al-Hasan al-Basri (8306), through Ibn Humayd from Hakkam from 'Amr from 'Ata', has it in the same sense, adding that the Jews marvelled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 443}

Qatada (8307), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that it came down about Huyayy ibn Akhtab, who on the loan verse's descent said that our Lord asks us for a loan and only the poor ask the rich. Qatada (8308), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar, has it similarly as a saying of the Jews. Ibn Zayd (8309), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: these are the Jews; the note says the printed edition words this differently and the editor kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 444}

**Interpretation and readings (pp. 444-446).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God heard the saying of those among the Jews who said He was in need of them and they free of need of Him, and that He will record what they said of falsehood and slander against their Lord, and their killing of their prophets without right. The reciters differed: those of the Hijaz and most of Iraq read the verb with a nun at its start and the word for the killing in the accusative, while some Kufan reciters read it with a ya' in the passive with the killing in the nominative, taking guidance from a reading said to be 'Abd Allah's of the following verb, which in it is passive. Al-Tabari says the one who reads this way missed the right course and went against the authority of the reciters of Islam, since the following verb is joined to the first, so they should be read together either both passive or both active; reading one passive and the other active with no meaning that compels it is a choice outside fluent Arabic. The notes say the passage is certainly al-Farra's (Ma'ani al-Qur'an 1: 249), whose copies have a gap that the printed edition's correctors did not notice, completed by al-Tabari's report of the 'Abd Allah reading that Hamza took as his guide; and that al-Tabari's phrasing "forced him upon it" departs from the usual idiom by implied sense, which the editor judges fluent and a rarity he had not found in a book, though he recalls reading it in one of al-Shafi'i's books without remembering where. Al-Tabari's choice is the reading with the nun and the accusative, because the following verb is in the first person, and with the passive it would have been worded to match. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 446}

**The killing of the prophets (p. 446).** Al-Tabari raises a question: how is the killing of prophets ascribed to these people when the reports say those meant were Jews of the Prophet's time, none of whom had killed a prophet, since none reached one? He answers that the meaning is not as supposed: those meant were pleased with what their forebears did in killing prophets and followed their way in holding it lawful, so God ascribed the act of those on their path to all of them, being one community and creed, all approving what the doer among them did, as he showed of similar cases earlier. The note refers to places in volume 2 and its index (p. 611) on ascribing ancestors' acts to descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 446}

**Close of the verse (pp. 446-447).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God will say on the Day of Resurrection to those who made that statement and killed His prophets without right: taste the punishment of a blazing, flaming fire. The third note says al-Tabari's explanation of the word for burning is hardly to be found in the dictionaries, and it continues on the next page. Al-Tabari says fire is a general name for the flaming and the non-flaming, and the burning is a qualifier of what is meant to be consuming, as one says painful for hurting and pain-giving for causing pain. The rest of the note (p. 447): the word means the kindling and flaring of fire and also the flame, and the editor refers to Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 110), which says fire is a general name that may be burning or not, and when it flares it is burning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 447}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 447}
