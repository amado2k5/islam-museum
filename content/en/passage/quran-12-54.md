**Commentary on verse 54 (pp. 146-148)**

On "the king said, bring him to me, I will make him exclusively mine; and when he spoke with him he said, you are today established with us and trusted", al-Tabari says "the king" is the great king of Egypt, who according to Ibn Ishaq was al-Walid ibn al-Rayyan (report 19446, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama). When Joseph's excuse became clear and his trustworthiness and knowledge were known, he said to his companions "bring him to me, I will make him exclusively mine", that is, I will make him one of my intimates above others. On "when he spoke with him", he says: when the king spoke with Joseph and knew his innocence and the greatness of his trustworthiness, he said to him: you, Joseph, are "established and trusted with us", that is, established in whatever you wish and need from us because of the height of your rank with us, and trusted over whatever you are entrusted with. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 146}

Report 19447, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: when the king found an excuse for him he said bring him to me, I will make him mine alone. Report 19448, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: I will take him for myself. Report 19449, from Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Sufyan and Abu Sinan: the king said to him, I want to make you mine alone, except that I disdain that you eat with me; and Joseph said, I have more right to disdain, I am the son of Ishaq, or the son of Ismail (al-Tabari is unsure here, and in his own book it is Ishaq, the one offered to God, son of Ibrahim, God's intimate friend). Report 19450, through Ibn Waki', is similar, except that it says I am the son of Ibrahim, God's friend, the son of Ismail, the one offered to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 147}

Report 19451, from Abd Allah ibn Abi al-Hudhayl, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Abu Sinan: the Aziz said to Joseph, there is nothing I do not like you to share with me, except that I would rather you not share my wife and that my slave not eat with me; and he said, do you disdain that I eat with you? I have more right to disdain than you, I am the son of Ibrahim, God's friend, the son of Ishaq, the one offered, and the son of Ya'qub, whose eyes went white from grief. Report 19452, from Abu Maysara, through Abu Kurayb, Sufyan ibn Uqba, Hamza al-Zayyat and Ibn Ishaq: when the Aziz saw Joseph's deftness, cleverness and refinement he called him and he took lunch and dinner with him rather than with his servants; when what happened between him and the wife happened, she said to him, you keep this one near! tell him to eat with the servants. He told him, go and eat with the servants, and Joseph said to his face, you disdain to eat with me? By God, I am Joseph son of Ya'qub, prophet of God, son of Ishaq, the one offered to God, son of Ibrahim, God's friend. The editor notes that "anifa" and "istankafa" mean the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 148}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
