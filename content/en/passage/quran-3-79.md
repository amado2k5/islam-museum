## Summary
Verse 79 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 79 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 79: what is not fitting for a human being.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that it is not fitting for any human being. The word for human being is a collective for the children of Adam without a singular from its own root, like "people" and "creation", and it can also be a name for one person. He explains God granting him scripture as God sending His scripture down on him, the judgement as His teaching him the discernment of wisdom, and prophethood as His granting it; then saying to the people to be servants to him apart from God means calling people to the worship of himself and not of God after God has given him what He gave. Al-Tabari says that if God gives him this, he calls them only to knowledge of God and urges them to know the laws of His religion and to be leaders in knowing God's command and prohibition and exemplars in obeying and worshipping Him, by being teachers of the book and students of it. The editor refers to earlier places for the sense of giving, of judgement and of prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 538}

**The occasion of revelation as transmitted (reports 7296 and 7297).** Al-Tabari reports that some of the People of the Book said to the Prophet: do you invite us to worship you? In the report of Ibn Humayd from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, from Ikrima or Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas, Abu Rafi al-Qurazi said, when the rabbis of the Jews and the Christians of Najran gathered with the Prophet and he invited them to Islam: do you want us to worship you, Muhammad, as the Christians worship Jesus son of Mary? A Christian man of Najran called al-Rabis said: is that what you want from us and to it you call us? The Prophet answered that God forbid that anything other than God be worshipped or that its worship be commanded, that he was not sent with this and was not commanded it, and God sent down the verse on account of what they said, up to the words that mention their being Muslims. Abu Kurayb relays it from Yunus ibn Bukayr, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad the client of Zayd ibn Thabit, from Said ibn Jubayr or Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, in similar terms. The editor says that Abu Rafi al-Qurazi is Sallam ibn Abi al-Huqayq the Jew; that the printed text has a different form of the name and the manuscript an unpointed word, and that the correct one is al-Rabis as in the Sira of Ibn Hisham, the chief of the Samaritans being called by that title; and that the dual wording in Ibn Hisham is better and what stands in al-Tabari may be the copyist's slip. He notes that both reports complete the earlier reports in Ibn Hisham, with small differences of wording.

**The words of Qatada, al-Rabi and Ibn Jurayj (7298-7300).** Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that the sense is that it does not befit a human being to whom God gave what He gave to command his servants to take him as a lord apart from God. Al-Muthanna relays the same from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi. Al-Qasim relays from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj that some Jews made servants of people apart from their Lord by distorting God's book from its place, so God showed that it does not befit one to whom He gave His book to command people to other than what He sent down in it.

**The sense of rabbaniyyun: wise scholars and jurists (7301-7317).** Al-Tabari says that the sense is that the Prophet tells them to be rabbaniyyun, the word 'say' being dropped because the speech indicates it, and that the interpreters differed over the word. Some said it means wise scholars. This is what Ibn Bashshar, Abu Kurayb and Ibn Humayd (by two routes) relay from Mansur from Abu Razin; Abd al-Razzaq relays from Mamar, from Mansur, from Abu Razin with the order scholars and wise, and Mamar added that Qatada said the same. Yaqub ibn Ibrahim relays from Hushaym, from Awf, from al-Hasan that they are jurists and scholars. From Mujahid that they are jurists, by several routes: Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih; al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih; al-Qasim from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from al-Qasim; and Yunus from Ibn Wahb, from Sufyan, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that they are the jurists and scholars and are above the rabbis. From Qatada: Bishr from Yazid from Said, jurists and scholars. From al-Suddi through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn: the wise jurists. From Ibn Abbas through Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father: wise jurists; and likewise through Ibn Sinan al-Qazzaz, from al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan al-Ashqar, from Abu Kudayna, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas. Al-Munjab relays by two chains, one from Abu Hamza from Yahya ibn Aqil on the same word in another place of the Quran, that they are the jurists and scholars, the other from Bishr, from Abu Ruq, from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas the same. Al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj relays from Abu Muadh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman that al-Dahhak called them jurists and scholars.

**The wise and pious.** Others said that they are the wise and pious, with the report of Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbui from Fudayl ibn Iyad, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib, from Said ibn Jubayr (7318).

**Those who govern and lead the people.** Others said that they are those who govern the people and lead them. Yunus ibn Abd al-Ala relays from Ibn Wahb that Ibn Zayd (7319) said they are those who bring people up, the rulers of this matter, who take charge of them, and he cited another verse of the Maida sura to show that the rabbaniyyun are the rulers and the rabbis the scholars.

**Al-Tabari's choice of the sense, with his reasoning from language.** Al-Tabari says that the most correct view in his opinion is that rabbaniyyun is the plural of rabbani, which is attributed to the rabban who rears people, that is, mends their affairs and takes them in hand. He cites a verse of Alqama ibn Abada for the sense of taking charge of a matter and putting it right, in which the poet describes those who held his affair before the one he praises as not mending it so that it was lost (the line is not quoted here). He gives the morphology: it is said that someone reared my affair, he rears it, and he is its rabb; and when exaggeration in praise is meant, one says rabban, as one says na'asan from the verb meaning to doze; most nouns of this pattern come from verbs whose past is of the form fa'ila, like sakran, atshan and rayyan, though some come from fa'ala yaf'ulu, like na'asa and rabba. So, since the one learned in jurisprudence and wisdom who reforms rears the affairs of people by teaching them good and calling them to what profits them, and so too the wise one who fears God and the ruler who governs on the path of the just, all of them belong among those the word covers; the rabbaniyyun are the pillars of people in jurisprudence, knowledge and the affairs of religion and of this world. For this reason Mujahid said they are above the rabbis, because the rabbis are the scholars and the rabbani is he who joins to knowledge and jurisprudence a clear sight of government and management and of what mends people in this world and the next. The editor refers to the discussion of rabb in volume 1, corrects the printed text in a note by supplying a word that fell from the manuscript, and then praises the explanation, saying it is rarely found in books of language, is among the best he has read on the sense of rabbani, and asks mercy on Abu Jafar.

**The two readings of the verb of teaching and study.** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed. Most reciters of the Hijaz and some Basrans read it with an open first letter and a light second letter, meaning your knowing the book, your study and your reading, and argued that if it were with a doubled letter and a closed first letter, the other verb would be with closed first letter and a doubled letter too. Most Kufan reciters read it with a closed first letter and a doubled middle letter, meaning your teaching people the book, and argued that to describe them as teachers is to describe them as knowing, since none teaches until he knows, whereas one described as knowing is not necessarily one who teaches; so the better reading is the stronger in praise. Al-Muthanna relays from Ishaq, from Yahya ibn Adam, from Ibn Uyayna, from Humayd al-Araj that Mujahid read it with the light form, and Ibn Uyayna said they did not teach it until they knew it (7320). The editor corrects the printed text by supplying a conjunction that fell out.

**Al-Tabari's choice of reading and the sense of study.** Al-Tabari prefers the reading with the closed first letter and doubled middle letter, since God described the people as pillars of the people in their religion and their world, as those who mend and rear, and then said that they became so by teaching them their Lord's book. He takes study to be recitation of the book, notes that it has also been said to be jurisprudence, and holds recitation to be closer since the word is joined to teaching and the book is the Quran, so the study meant is of it and not of jurisprudence, which has not been mentioned. Al-Muthanna relays from Ishaq, from Yahya ibn Adam, from Abu Zakariya that Asim read it with the light form, and explained teaching as the Quran and study as jurisprudence (7321). The editor expresses doubt about the flow of this passage and fears that the copyists dropped something. Al-Tabari sums up the sense: the Prophet tells people to be masters and leaders of the people in their religion and world as rabbaniyyun, by their teaching them God's book and the lawful and unlawful, the obligatory and recommended and the rest of what it contains, and by their reciting and studying it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 546}
