## Summary

**Commentary on verse 187 (pp. 291-300)**

On "they ask you about the Hour, when will it come to anchor? Say: knowledge of it is only with my Lord; none will reveal it at its time but He", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over whom "they ask you about the Hour" means. Some said it means the Messenger's people of Quraysh, who had asked the Messenger of God about it, and report 15462, from Qatada, says Quraysh said to Muhammad that there is kinship between us and you, so tell us in secret when the Hour is, and God said "they ask you as though you were well informed of it"; the editor refers to report 15481. Others said it means some of the Jews, and report 15463, from Ibn Abbas, says Jabal ibn Abi Qushayr and Shamwal ibn Zayd said to the Messenger of God: Muhammad, tell us when the Hour is if you are a prophet as you say, for we know when it is; so God revealed "they ask you about the Hour" up to "but most people do not know". The editor notes that the first name is unpointed in the manuscript and that he follows Ibn Hisham's Sira, in which the report is found. Report 15464, from Tariq ibn Shihab: the Prophet kept mentioning the matter of the Hour until "they ask you about the Hour, when will it come to anchor" was revealed. The editor identifies Ismail ibn Abi Khalid al-Ahmasi as trustworthy and sure, and Tariq ibn Shihab as one who saw the Prophet and narrated from him in a mursal form, and says Ibn Kathir gave it calling the chain good and strong, and that al-Nasa'i gave it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 291} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 292}

Then al-Tabari says: the right view is that some people asked the Messenger of God about the Hour and God revealed this verse; they may have been from Quraysh and they may have been from the Jews, and we have no report that allows settling which. So the sense of the verse is: the people who ask you about the Hour ask "when will it come to anchor?", that is, when will it arise. "Ayyan" means "when" in Arab speech, as in the verse of the rajaz poet asking when his need will be met, the time for its success having come; the editor explains "ibban" as the time and season suited to a thing, and says the line is in the Majaz and the Lisan and that he did not know its author. "Mursaha" means its arising, from one saying "God moored it" and "the people moored it" when they held it, and "it moored". The interpreters said the like, and report 15465, from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 293}

Report 15465, from al-Suddi: "when will it come to anchor?" means when will it arise; report 15466, from Qatada, the same. Others said it means its end, which is close to the sense of those who said its arising, since its ending is its reaching its time, and he has shown that the root is detention and standing; report 15467, from Ibn Abbas: it means its end. As for "say: knowledge of it is only with my Lord; none will reveal it at its time but He", it is a command from God to His prophet Muhammad to answer those who ask about the Hour that none knows the time of its arising but God who knows the unseen, that He does not disclose it at its time and none but He knows it. Report 15468, from Qatada: its knowledge is with God, He reveals it at its time and none knows that but God; reports 15469 and 15470, from Mujahid: "reveals" means brings it, none brings it but He; report 15471, from al-Suddi: none sends it at its time but He. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 293} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 294} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 295}
Then al-Tabari says on "it weighs heavily in the heavens and the earth; it will not come to you but suddenly": the interpreters differed over its sense. Some said it means the Hour is heavy on the people of the heavens and earth, that they should know its time and coming, because it is hidden from them and God has kept its knowledge to Himself; report 15472, from al-Suddi: it is hidden in the heavens and earth, so no angel brought near or sent prophet knows when it will arise; report 15473, from some interpreters: its knowledge weighs on the people of the heavens and earth, who do not know. Others said it means that it will be grave on its coming for the people of the heavens and earth; report 15474, from al-Hasan: when it comes it will weigh on the people of heaven and earth, meaning grow grave for them; report 15475, from Ibn Jurayj: when it comes the sky will split, the stars scatter, the sun be wrapped up, the mountains be moved, and what God said will be, and that is its weight; report 15476, from al-Suddi: some said of "it weighed" that it was great. Others said "in the heavens and the earth" means "upon the heavens and earth", report 15477, from Qatada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 295} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 296}
Al-Tabari chooses: the best view to my mind is that of those who said the sense is that the Hour is heavy in the heavens and earth on their people, that they should know its time and arising, because God hid that from His creation and let none of them know it, since God said this after "say: knowledge of it is only with my Lord; none will reveal it at its time but He" and said after it that it comes only suddenly, so what lies between should likewise be a report of the concealment of its knowledge from creation, as what precedes and follows it is. As for "it will not come to you but suddenly", he says the Hour comes only unexpectedly, without your sensing its coming. Report 15478, from al-Suddi: its arising takes them by surprise, coming upon them in heedlessness; report 15479, from Qatada: God decreed that it will come only suddenly, and it was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God used to say the Hour will break on people while a man is mending his trough, a man watering his animals, a man setting out his wares in the market, and a man lowering and raising his scales. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "suddenly". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 296} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 297}
Then al-Tabari says on "they ask you as though you were well informed about it; say: knowledge of it is only with God, but most people do not know": these people ask you about the Hour as though you were "hafi" about it, and the interpreters differed over the sense of "hafi 'anha". Some said they ask you about it as though you were affectionate to them, and said "about it" is placed forward though it comes later. Report 15480, from Ibn Abbas: as though there were affection between you and them, as though you were their friend; when people asked Muhammad about the Hour they asked as people who thought Muhammad was fond of them, so God revealed to him that knowledge of it is with Him alone, He kept its knowledge to Himself and let no angel or messenger know it. Report 15481, from Qatada: Quraysh said to Muhammad that there is kinship between us, so tell us in secret when the Hour is, and God said "they ask you as though you were well informed about it"; report 15482, from Qatada: that is, affectionate to them, Quraysh saying tell us the knowledge of the Hour in secret because of our kinship with you; report 15483, from Mujahid and Ikrima: affectionate to them when they ask you; report 15484, from Ibn Abbas: close to them and you show regard to them, and Abu Malik said as though you were fond of them, so you tell them, the editor saying he did not know who Abu Malik is in this report; report 15485, from al-Suddi: as though you were their friend. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 297} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 298} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 299}
Others said the sense is: as though you had pressed the question about it and so knew it. Reports 15486 and 15487, from Mujahid: you pressed the question about it until you knew its time; reports 15488 and 15489, from al-Dahhak: as though you knew it; report 15490, from al-Dahhak: they ask you about the Hour as if you had knowledge of it; say: knowledge of it is only with my Lord; report 15491, from some of them: as though you knew it; report 15492, from Ibn Zayd: as though you knew it, and he said its knowledge is hidden from His creatures, and recited [[passage/quran-31-34|31:34]] to the end of the surah. Report 15493, from Ibn Abbas: as though their question pleased you, say: knowledge of it is only with God, and "as though you were hafi about it" means kindly toward it. The editor says this last sentence is not part of Ibn Abbas's words in the preceding report so he set it apart, and fears something fell out through the copyist before it, since al-Tabari mentioned only two views. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 299} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 300}
These took the sense of "as though you were hafi about it" to be "hafi with it", and said the Arabs say "I showed regard to him in asking" and "I showed regard about him", and so it is said "we came to so-and-so to ask by him", meaning to ask about him. Then al-Tabari says: the better of the two views is that of those who said the meaning is as though you were pressing the question about it and so knew it. If someone asks how it came to be said "hafi 'anha" and not "hafi biha" if that is the sense, he answers: because "hafawa" occurs in asking, which is welcoming the one asked and asking him much, and "asking" is joined with "about" at times and with "by" at times, so one says "I asked about him" and "I asked by him"; when "hafi" was put in the place of "asking" it was joined with the commoner of the two particles that go with asking, "about", as the poet says, whom the editor identifies as al-Mumatil al-Mudhill, the citation continuing on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 300}
We stop here at the foot of p. 300, in the commentary on 7:187 at al-Tabari's citing a poet for joining "hafi" with "about"; the next batch begins at p. 301. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 300}
