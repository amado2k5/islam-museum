**Commentary on verse 133: destruction, replacement and the warning against betrayal (pp. 297-299)**

Al-Tabari says the address in the verse is to people: if God wills He can take you away by destruction and ruin, and bring a different people to support and aid His Prophet; God is able to do so. He rebukes in these verses those who betrayed the armour described earlier, and warns the Prophet's companions not to be like them, or to do as the apostate among them did in going over to the polytheists. Whoever does so harms only himself, since all creatures need God and God needs none of them. He identifies the apostate as Tu'ma ibn Ubayriq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 297}

Report 10676, from the Prophet through Suhayl ibn Abi Salih, his father and Abu Hurayra, says that when the verse was revealed he struck Salman on the back and said: these are his people, meaning the Persians. The editor notes that Abd al-Aziz ibn Muhammad al-Darawardi and Suhayl ibn Abi Salih are criticised narrators; that al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-Manthur and ascribes it to al-Tabari and others; and that al-Tabari will give other chains in the commentary on sura Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 299}

Report 10677, from Qatada through Sa'id and Bishr ibn Mu'adh, says that God, our Lord, is able to destroy whom He wills of His creatures and bring others after them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 299}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
