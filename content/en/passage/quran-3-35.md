## Verse 35 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 328-331)

**Imran's wife and her lineage.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-35|3:35]]) says the word for 'when' is tied to 'hearing' in the preceding verse; the editor says the first publisher thought a piece had dropped out of the sentence, but it is complete. Imran's wife, he says, is the mother of Mary the daughter of Imran, the mother of Jesus, and her name, as reported, was Hanna daughter of Faqudh ibn Qubayl. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq (6856) gives her lineage, and another narrator gives 'Faqud' with a dal; the editor says the manuscript and printed text have 'Qatil' and that he adopted 'Qubayl' from al-Tabari's Tarikh. Then al-Tabari gives the lineage of her husband Imran as ibn Yashim, ibn Amun, ibn Minsha, ibn Hizqiya, up through Ahziq, Yutham, Azariya, Amsiya, Yawash, Ahziyahu, Yaram, Yahfashat, Asabir, Abiya, Rahba'am, to Solomon son of David son of Isha (6857), again from Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq. The editor compares the forms of the names in the manuscript, the printed text and the Tarikh (vol. 2, p. 13), notes that some are undotted, and that the manuscript and print merged two names into one. This lineage is as Ibn Ishaq transmitted it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 329}

**The sense of the vow, and the grammar.** Al-Tabari says her vow meant that she made what was in her womb for the worship of her Lord, devoted to His service and the service of His sanctuary in the church, released from the service of everything else and set apart for Him. He takes the word for 'freed' as an accusative of circumstance from the pronoun in the prepositional phrase. At length the editor says the printed text made it a circumstance of the relative 'ma', changing the manuscript, which wrongly ascribes to al-Tabari a grammar he did not hold; al-Tabari's view is that it is a circumstance of the pronoun inside the phrase 'in my womb', the governing verb being 'is settled', and the two analyses differ plainly; he refers to the commentaries of Abu Hayyan and al-Alusi, and says the corrector was confused by the term 'sifa', which here means the preposition. Then al-Tabari says 'accept it from me' means accept what I vowed to You, Lord, for You hear what I say and ask and know what I intend within myself, and neither my secret nor open affair is hidden from You. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the vow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 330}

**The occasion of the vow.** Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq (6858) says Zechariah and Imran married two sisters, the mother of John being with Zechariah and the mother of Mary with Imran; Imran died while Mary's mother was pregnant. She had, they claim, been barren until she grew old, and they were a house of standing with God. Once, in the shade of a tree, she saw a bird feeding its chick, and her heart stirred for a child; she prayed God to give her one, and conceived Mary, and Imran died. When she knew there was a child in her womb she dedicated it to God as a vow, meaning she would have it worship God and keep it confined to the church, with no benefit taken from it in worldly affairs. This is as Ibn Ishaq narrated it, who introduces it with 'as they claim'. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (6859) says the vow meant making it set free for the worship of God with no worldly use; the editor says Ibn Hisham's text is slightly shorter and that he left al-Tabari's version as it stands, and that it is the remainder of the reports ending at 6850. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 330}

**What the early authorities said about 'freed'.** Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad al-Tufawi, from Muhammad ibn Rabi'a, from al-Nadr ibn Arabi, from Mujahid (6860) says a servant of the place of worship, and Abu Kurayb, from Jabir ibn Nuh, from al-Nadr, from Mujahid (6861) says a servant of the church. Abu Kurayb, from Jabir ibn Nuh, from Isma'il, from al-Shabi (6862) says she set it free for worship; Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid, from al-Shabi (6863) says she placed it in the church and freed it for worship; al-Muthanna, from Amr ibn Awn, from Hushaym (6864) gives the same. From Mujahid (6865-6866) it is for the church, to serve it, and Ibn Waki, from his father, from Sufyan, from Khusayf, from Mujahid (6867) says pure, with nothing of worldly affairs mixed in. The editor describes Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad ibn al-Ma'mun, a client of Banu Hashim from Baghdad, from whom al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and al-Tabari narrated, and Muhammad ibn Rabi'a al-Kilabi al-Ru'asi, a cousin of Waki and trustworthy, and says 'bi'a', with a kasra on the ba, is a church of the Christians or Jews. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 331}
