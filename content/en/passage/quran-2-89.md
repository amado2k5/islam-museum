## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when there came to them a Book from God confirming what they had", al-Tabari says those meant are the Jews of the Children of Israel and the Book is the Qur'an, confirming the Torah and the Gospel, citing Qatada and al-Rabi'. On "and before that they used to seek victory over those who disbelieved", seeking victory (istiftah) means seeking help: they used to ask for God's help through Muhammad against the Arab polytheists before his mission. He reports from Ibn Ishaq, from elders of the Ansar, that the verse was revealed about them and their Jewish neighbours: the Jews had the upper hand in the Jahiliyya and would say that a prophet's time was at hand and that they would kill the Ansar with him as 'Ad and Iram were killed; when the prophet was sent from Quraysh and the Ansar followed him, the Jews rejected him (the editor judges the report to have the force of a raised report and to be connected). From Ibn Abbas he reports that Mu'adh ibn Jabal and Bishr ibn al-Bara' reminded the Jews of this, and that Sallam ibn Mishkam of the Banu al-Nadir replied that he had brought nothing they knew and was not the one they used to mention, whereupon the verse came down. He also reports from Ibn Abbas that they rejected and envied him when they saw he was from other than themselves, and from 'Ali al-Azdi that they used to pray "O God, send us this prophet to judge between us". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 331} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 332} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 333} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 334}

Al-Tabari continues with the reports on seeking victory. From Qatada and Abu al-'Aliya: the Jews used to say "O God, send this prophet whom we find in the Torah", and when he was sent from others they rejected him out of envy of the Arabs although they knew he was God's messenger. From al-Suddi: the Arabs used to harass them, so they asked God to send him that they might fight the Arabs with him. Similar reports come from 'Ata', Mujahid, Ibn Abbas and Sa'id ibn Jubayr. From Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak: they claimed to be backing him against the Arabs, "and they were not so, they lied". From Ibn Zayd: they supposed he would be one of them, so rejected and envied him when he was not; by this God benefited the Aws and Khazraj, who had heard from them that a prophet was coming. On the answer to "and when there came to them", he gives two views of the grammarians: that it is omitted because the hearers know it, as in al-Ra'd 13:31, or that it is the "fa" in "when there came to them what they knew, they rejected it". On "so God's curse is on the disbelievers", he says it means God's disgrace and distancing of those who deny what they knew, and the verse shows plainly that they chose disbelief deliberately once the proof was established. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 334} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 335} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 336} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 337}
