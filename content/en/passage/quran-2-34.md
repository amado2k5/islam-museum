## Summary
Verse 34 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We said to the angels, Prostrate to Adam" al-Tabari takes the verse as joined to what precedes, a reminder to the Jews of Medina of God's favours: He created for them all that is on earth, honoured their father Adam with His knowledge, and had the angels prostrate to him. He holds that excepting Iblis from them shows that Iblis was among those commanded to prostrate with them, and cites the verses of surah al-A'raf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 502}
He then records that the interpreters differed over whether Iblis was an angel. He reports from Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, that he was of a tribe of the angels called the Jinn, created from the fire of the simoom, named al-Harith and a keeper of Paradise, while the jinn named elsewhere in the Qur'an were created from a smokeless flame. From Ibn Abbas by way of Tawus he reports that his name was Azazil, a dweller on earth, among the most devout and learned of the angels, and that this led him to pride. From al-Suddi he reports that he was set over the lowest heaven and that they were called Jinn because they were keepers of the gardens (janan), and from Ibn Jurayj, through Ibn Abbas, that he was of the noblest angelic tribe and was called after the gardens as a man is called Makki or Madani. Each is attributed to its narrator, and the editor notes the standing of some narrators in the chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 502} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 503}

Al-Tabari continues the reports that Iblis was an angel: from Ibn Abbas that he belonged to a tribe of the Jinn among the angels and managed what lies between heaven and earth; from Said ibn al-Musayyib that he was chief of the angels of the lowest heaven; and from Qatada that he was over its treasury, with Ibn Abbas saying that had he not been an angel he would not have been commanded to prostrate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 504} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 505}
He gives Ibn Ishaq's account that the Arabs call "jinn" whatever is veiled and unseen, and the angels are veiled and unseen, so "he was of the jinn" means he was of the angels, citing a verse of surah al-Saffat and two lines of al-A'sha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 505} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 506}
He then records the other view: al-Hasan al-Basri said he was never an angel for the blink of an eye and is the origin of the jinn as Adam is of humankind (the editor quotes Ibn Kathir calling this chain sound); Shahr ibn Hawshab that he was one of the jinn whom the angels drove off and one of them captured; Sa'd ibn Mas'ud that he was taken captive as a child when the angels fought the jinn and worshipped with them; and Ibn Zayd that he is the father of the jinn as Adam is of humans. The reasoning of this group is that God says He created him from the fire of the simoom and from a smokeless flame, calls him one of the jinn, and that he has offspring while angels do not procreate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 506} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 507}
In a note the editor quotes Ibn Kathir that many such reports are Israelite material, passed on for consideration, and that the Qur'an makes them unnecessary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 505}

Al-Tabari gives a report from Ibn Abbas, by a chain with an unnamed man, that God created a creation and told them to prostrate to Adam, and they refused and a fire burned them; then another, who also refused; then these, who agreed, and Iblis was of those who refused. The editor quotes Ibn Kathir that it is strange and its chain hardly sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 508}
Al-Tabari comments that the arguments of those who exclude Iblis from the angels show weak knowledge, which is his own view: it is not implausible that God made kinds of angels from different things, light, fire and others; silence on what the angels were made of does not take Iblis out of them; offspring is not ruled out given the desire placed in him; and anything hidden from sight may be called jinn, so he and the angels are among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 508}
On the meaning of "Iblis" he says it has the form "if'il" from "iblas", despair of good, remorse and grief. He reports from Ibn Abbas that God made him despair of all good and a stoned devil as punishment for his disobedience, and from al-Suddi that his name was al-Harith and he was called Iblis when he was struck dumb in bewilderment, citing al-Ajjaj, Ru'ba and a verse in surah al-An'am. He explains that the name is not declined because it has no match among Arabic names and so is treated like foreign names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 509} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 510}
On "he refused and was arrogant" he says refusing means declining to prostrate and arrogance means holding himself above obeying God. He reads the report as a reproach to his like, including the Jews of Medina and their scholars who knew the Prophet and his description yet were too proud, from envy and transgression, to acknowledge his prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 510} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 511}
On "and was of the disbelievers" he says he was of those who denied God's favours to him, as the Jews denied His favours, and that he is counted with them in religion though not in kind, as the hypocrites are said to be "of one another"; the sense is that he was then, upon refusing, among the disbelievers. He reports from Abu al-Aliya that it means "of the disobedient". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 511}
