## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We said to the angels, Prostrate to Adam" al-Tabari takes the verse as joined to what precedes, a reminder to the Jews of Medina of God's favours: He created for them all that is on earth, honoured their father Adam with His knowledge, and had the angels prostrate to him. He holds that excepting Iblis from them shows that Iblis was among those commanded to prostrate with them, and cites the verses of surah al-A'raf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 502}
He then records that the interpreters differed over whether Iblis was an angel. He reports from Ibn Abbas, through al-Dahhak, that he was of a tribe of the angels called the Jinn, created from the fire of the simoom, named al-Harith and a keeper of Paradise, while the jinn named elsewhere in the Qur'an were created from a smokeless flame. From Ibn Abbas by way of Tawus he reports that his name was Azazil, a dweller on earth, among the most devout and learned of the angels, and that this led him to pride. From al-Suddi he reports that he was set over the lowest heaven and that they were called Jinn because they were keepers of the gardens (janan), and from Ibn Jurayj, through Ibn Abbas, that he was of the noblest angelic tribe and was called after the gardens as a man is called Makki or Madani. Each is attributed to its narrator, and the editor notes the standing of some narrators in the chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 502} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 503}
