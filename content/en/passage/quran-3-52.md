## Summary
Verse 52 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 52 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of perceiving.** Al-Tabari says the verb for perceiving means finding, so the verse means that Jesus found among the Israelites to whom God sent him a denial of his prophethood, a rejection of his word and a turning away from what he called them to. He cites the same verb in [[passage/quran-19-98|19:98]], says the related verb without the initial alif means destroying and killing and cites the use in [[passage/quran-3-152|3:152]], and that it also means tenderness and softening, giving a line of al-Kumayt about weeping over abandoned dwellings in which the phrase means that the dwelling softens for him. The editor refers to al-Farra (1:217), the Majalis of Thalab, the Islah al-Mantiq and Lisan al-Arab, explains the word for 'continuous' in the line as the constantly flowing, and says the poet marvels at one who weeps over the ruins of his loved ones and calls his act foolish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 442} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 443}

**The meaning of the preposition here.** Al-Tabari says Jesus' question, who will be my helpers against those who deny God's proof, turn from His religion and reject the prophethood of His prophet, means: who are my helpers together with God. He says that when the Arabs join one thing to another and then wish to speak of the two as one added to the other, they sometimes put the preposition 'to' where they would say 'with', giving the example 'herd to herd is camels', when one is added to the other; but when a thing is simply with another they do not use 'to', so one cannot say 'so-and-so came and to him is wealth' for 'and with him is wealth'. The editor refers to earlier pages (1:299) and to al-Farra (1:218). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 443} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 444}

**The reports of al-Suddi and Ibn Jurayj.** Report 7120 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says it means 'with God', and report 7121 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says the same. The editor records that Ibn Abi Hatim called Muhammad ibn al-Husayn ibn Musa ibn Abi Hunayn al-Kufi truthful, and that Abu Hatim said of Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal al-Qurashi al-Umawi al-Kufi al-Hafari that he was truthful and one of the heads of the Shia; he also names their teachers and those who narrated from them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 444}

**Why Jesus asked for helpers: the report of al-Suddi.** Al-Tabari says the scholars differ on the reason. One group says it was what is related in report 7122 (Musa ibn Harun, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi), a long story given here as transmitted. When God sent Jesus and commanded him to call, the Israelites cast him out, and he and his mother wandered the land; they lodged with a man in a town who treated them well. The town's king was a tyrant who set each man a day on which to feed him and his troops and give them wine, and the man came home grieved because his turn had come; his wife told Mary, Mary told Jesus, and he said that doing it would bring evil, then told the man to fill his pots and jars with water and tell him. When he had filled them, Jesus prayed and what was in the pots became meat, broth and bread and what was in the jars became wine of a kind no one had seen. The king ate and drank and asked where the wine came from, was given confused answers, and was told of the young man who is given whatever he asks of God. The king's dearest son had died days earlier; he asked Jesus to pray for his revival, Jesus said that if he lived it would be evil, the king insisted, and Jesus made him agree to let him and his mother go where they wished; he prayed and the boy lived. When the people of the kingdom saw him alive they called each other to arms, fearing he would devour them as his father had, and they fought; Jesus and his mother left. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 444} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 445} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 446}

**The Jewish companion.** The report goes on that a Jew joined them with two loaves while Jesus had one, and they agreed to share; while Jesus slept, the Jew ate the other loaf bit by bit, and when asked denied it and said he had only one. They passed a shepherd, Jesus asked for a sheep, the Jew fetched it, they slaughtered, roasted and ate it without breaking a bone, and Jesus put the bones in the skin, struck it with his staff and said rise by God's leave; the sheep rose bleating. The shepherd asked who he was, heard 'Jesus son of Mary', called him a sorcerer and fled. Jesus asked the Jew, by Him who revived the sheep, how many loaves he had, and he swore it was one. The same happened with a calf. The editor notes in the margins that the manuscript has a different form of the word for 'give us a sheep' and that the printed edition is right, meaning a sheep fit for slaughter, and that the pronouns shift between Jesus and his companion and then all three, a construction he finds acceptable in the figurative range of Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 446} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 447}

**The sick king.** Next they reached a town; the Jew took a staff like Jesus' and said that he would now revive the dead. The town's king was gravely ill; the Jew went about offering a cure and promising to revive him if he died. He was told that the king's illness had defeated physicians before him and that any physician who failed to cure him was ordered crucified. He was let in, struck the king with the staff until he died, and kept striking and saying 'rise by God's leave', and was seized to be crucified; word reached Jesus, who came to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 447}

**The treasure and the four men.** The report goes on that Jesus went with the Jew to a treasure; four men came upon it and gathered around, and two sent the other two to buy food, drink and animals to carry it. Each pair plotted to kill the other, the first pair put poison in the food, the second killed those who brought it and then ate from it, and all died. Jesus knew and told the Jew to bring it out so they might divide it; Jesus divided it in three, and when the Jew asked what the three were for, said one was his, one was the Jew's and the third was for the one who owned the loaf. The Jew admitted it was he, and Jesus said take my share, yours and that of the owner of the loaf, which is your share of this world and the next; when he lifted it and walked a little, the earth swallowed him. Then Jesus passed the disciples fishing, asked what they were doing, and said 'will you not walk until we fish for people'; they asked who he was, believed him and went with him, and the report ends that this is what the verse refers to. The editor notes that the printed text has a different wording and the manuscript is right, explains 'a little' with two lines by Salim ibn Wabisa al-Asadi and Umar ibn Abi Rabia, and says it is a rarity of the language that dictionaries overlooked. In another note he says a word in the manuscript is unpointed and that the printed edition makes it a form meaning 'and him, as food'; he found no sense he could accept, observed that al-Thalabi in Qisas al-Anbiya, al-Baghawi and al-Durr al-Manthur omit it from al-Suddi's report, thinks it likely that it was 'and he prepared food', and hopes another will establish the right reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 445} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 447} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 448}

**The reports of al-Hasan and Mujahid.** Report 7122 (second, Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan) says he asked for help and the disciples helped him and he prevailed over them, which is the view that the reason was the asking for support. Others say the reason was that those he asked had wanted to kill him; report 7123 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Mujahid) says they disbelieved and wanted to kill him, and that was when he asked his people for help. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 449}

**Helpers and disciples.** Al-Tabari says the word for helpers is the plural of 'helper', as 'nobles' is the plural of 'noble' and 'witnesses' of 'witness'. On why the disciples were so called, the scholars differ. Some say for the whiteness of their clothes, and report 7124 (Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Muharibi, from Qays ibn al-Rabi, from Maysara, from al-Minhal ibn Amr, from Said ibn Jubayr) says so. Others say they were fullers who whitened clothes, and report 7125 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Abu Arta) says the disciples were the washers who bleach cloth. Others say they were the elect of the prophets: report 7126 (Yaqub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn Ulayya, from Rawh ibn al-Qasim) says Qatada mentioned a man among the Prophet's companions as one of the disciples and, asked who they are, said those fit for the caliphate; and report 7127 (from al-Munjab, from al-Husayn, from Bishr, from Umara, from Abu Ruq, from al-Dahhak) says the chosen of the prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 449} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 450}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the likeliest of these views is that they were named for the whiteness of their clothes and for being fullers, since 'hawar' means intense whiteness among the Arabs; hence white refined flour is called by the related word, and a man with very white eyeballs is called ahwar and a woman hawra. He allows that Jesus' disciples may have been named for whitening cloth and being fullers, were known by their companionship with Jesus and his choice of them as companions and helpers, and the name then came to be used for each special companion and helper of a man. The editor explains the word for what is refined of grain and the whiteness of flour. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 450} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 451}

**The saying and the line of Abu Jilda.** Al-Tabari gives, without a chain, report 7128, that every prophet has a disciple and that al-Zubayr is his, and says he means his special companion. The editor says it is a sound hadith, recorded by al-Bukhari in several places of the Fath and by Muslim (15:188), and that the printed text has a different wording and the right one is as he gave it, with another version dropping the first particle. Al-Tabari adds that the Arabs call the women of towns and cities hawariyyat because whiteness predominates in them, citing a line of Abu Jilda al-Yashkuri that tells those women to weep for others; the editor says it was spoken to rouse people to fight the people of Syria and refers to al-Amidi's Mu'talif, the Aghani, the Wahshiyyat, the Hamasa of Ibn al-Shajari and Lisan al-Arab. He describes Abu Jilda as a poet of the Umayyad period, among al-Hajjaj's closest men, who left him, rose with Ibn al-Ash'ath and became one of the fiercest agitators against al-Hajjaj, and gives the remark al-Hajjaj is said to have made when his head was set before him. Al-Tabari then begins to comment on the disciples' declaration of belief and their asking Jesus to bear witness to their submission, and the text breaks off as he begins to say that this is a report from God that Islam is His religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 451}

We stop in the middle of p. 451, where al-Tabari begins to say this is a report from God that Islam is His religion with which He sent His messengers, and continue from p. 451 (end) and p. 452.
