## Summary
Verse 63 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 63 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 63 (pp. 45-48)**

Al-Tabari says: by "and He joined their hearts" God means He joined the hearts of the believers of the Aws and Khazraj, after division and scattering, on His true religion, making them one by it after they were scattered, and brothers after they were enemies. On "had you spent all that is on earth you could not have joined their hearts" he says to His Prophet: had you spent, Muhammad, everything on earth of gold, silver and goods, you could not have joined their hearts by your own power, but God joined them on guidance so they united, as strengthening, support and help for you against your enemy; and He who did that and caused it, so that they became helpers, supporters and one hand against whoever wished you harm, is He who, if an enemy seeks to harm you, will suffice you against his plot and give you victory over him; so trust Him, carry out His command and rely on Him. The editor explains the word for "power" as strength. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 45}

Report 16256, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: these are the Ansar, whose hearts He joined after a war that had been between them; the editor notes the manuscript has a gap filled in red between two phrases. Report 16257, from Bashir ibn Thabit, a man of the Ansar, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far and Shu'ba: it means the Ansar. Report 16258, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: He joined their hearts on the guidance with which He sent you to them; He joined them by His religion on which He gathered them, meaning the Aws and Khazraj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 46}

Report 16259, from Mujahid, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Yaman, Ibrahim al-Khuzi and al-Walid ibn Abi Mughith: when two Muslims meet and shake hands they are forgiven; the narrator asked, for a handshake they are forgiven? and Mujahid said, have you not heard Him say "had you spent all that is on earth you could not have joined their hearts"? al-Walid said, you know better than I. Report 16260, from Mujahid, through Abd al-Karim ibn Abi Umayr, al-Walid, Abu Amr al-Awza'i and Abda ibn Abi Luba: when two who love each other for God's sake see each other and one takes the other's hand and smiles at him, their sins fall away as the leaves fall from a tree; Abda said, that is easy, and he said, do not say so, for God says "had you spent all that is on earth you could not have joined their hearts"; Abda said, I knew he had more understanding than I. The editor gives biographies of the men in the two chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 47}

Report 16261, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Muhammad ibn Khalaf, Ubayd Allah ibn Musa, Fudayl ibn Ghazwan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: this verse was sent down about those who love one another for God's sake. The editor says al-Hakim gave it in the Mustadrak, calling it sound by the conditions of the two shaykhs, al-Dhahabi agreeing; that al-Haythami gave it in Majma' al-Zawa'id by another route, saying al-Bazzar gave it and its men are the men of the Sahih save Junada ibn Salm, who is reliable; and that al-Suyuti gave it in al-Durr al-Manthur, ascribing it also to Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn Abi al-Dunya, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Abi Hatim, Abu al-Shaykh, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 47}

Report 16262, from Umayr ibn Ishaq, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Ibn Awn: we used to be told the first thing taken from people is affection. Report 16263, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, Ayyub ibn Suwayd, al-Awza'i and Abda ibn Abi Luba, like the report of Abd al-Karim from al-Walid. Report 16264, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Ibn Waki, Abu Usama, Ibn Numayr, Hafs ibn Ghiyath, Fudayl ibn Ghazwan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas: they are those who love one another for God's sake. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 48}

On "He is mighty, wise" he says: God who joined the hearts of the Aws and Khazraj after their word was scattered and they were enemies, and made them your helpers, is "mighty", overcome by nothing and his decree turned back by none, executing His judgement on His creatures; so rely on Him and trust Him; "wise" in the ordering of His creation. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "mighty" and "wise". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 48}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
