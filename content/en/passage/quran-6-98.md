## Summary
Verse 98 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 98 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "it is he who brought you into being from a single soul; so there is a resting-place and a repository; we have detailed the signs for a people who understand" al-Tabari says: your God, you who hold others equal to God, "who brought you into being", meaning who began your creation from nothing and brought you into existence when you were nothing, "from a single soul", meaning Adam. 13613, from al-Suddi: Adam; 13614, from Qatada: from Adam. As for "a resting-place and a repository" the interpreters differed on its interpretation. Some said the sense is: it is he who brought you into being from a single soul, so among you is one resting in the womb and among you one deposited in the grave until God raises him for the resurrection. 13615, from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, on [[passage/quran-11-6|11:6]], "he knows its resting-place and its repository": its resting-place in the wombs and its repository where it dies; 13616 and 13617, from him: the repository is where it dies and the resting-place what is in the womb; 13618, from Ibrahim: its resting-place in the wombs and its repository in the earth where it dies; 13619, from Miqsam: its resting-place in the loins where it takes shelter and its repository where it dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 563}

The editor refers for "brought into being" to an earlier passage.

Others said the repository is what was in the loins of fathers, and the resting-place what was in the wombs of women and the belly of the earth or on its back. 13620 and 13621, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they are deposited while in men's loins, and when they settle in women's wombs or on the earth's back or in its belly they have come to rest; 13622, from Ibn Abbas on the verse of Hud: the repository is in the loins and the resting-place what is on the face of the earth or in the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 564}

The editor identifies, in 13622, al-Mughira ibn al-Nu'man al-Nakha'i, who related from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and from whom Shu'ba, al-Thawri and Mis'ar related, trusted.

Others said rather the sense is a resting-place on the earth on its surface and a repository with God. 13623, from Ibn Abbas: the resting-place is the earth and the repository is with the Merciful; 13624, from Mujahid: the resting-place is the earth and the repository is with your Lord; 13625, from Abdullah: its resting-place in this world and its repository in the next; 13626, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the repository in the loins and the resting-place in the next world and on the face of the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 565}

The editor says in 13623 that "al-Mughira" is al-Mughira ibn Miqsam al-Dabbi, a well-known leader; that "Abu al-Jabr ibn Tamim ibn Hudhlam" was in print here and in 13637 and 13629 "Abu al-Khayr Tamim ibn Hidham", and in the manuscript "Abu al-Hurr Tamim ibn Hudhlam" unpointed and without "ibn", an error, for Tamim ibn Hudhlam al-Dabbi's kunya is Abu Salama or Abu Hudhlam, a companion of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud who met Abu Bakr, an early Follower, and al-Mughira does not relate from him but through his son here and through Ibrahim al-Nakha'i; his son Abu al-Jabr's name is Abd al-Rahman ibn Tamim ibn Hudhlam al-Dabbi, from whom Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani and al-Mughira related, so he corrected the manuscript and print and added "ibn"; that "Abu al-Jabr" is with jim and ba, and is given in most books as "Abu al-Khayr", an error that Abd al-Ghani in al-Mu'talif wa al-Mukhtalif, Ibn Makula and al-Dulabi corrected.

Others said the sense is a resting-place in the womb and a repository in the loins. 13627, from Ibn Abbas: a resting-place in the womb and a repository in a loin, not yet created but to be created; 13628, from Ikrima: the resting-place is the one that has settled in the womb and the repository the one deposited in the loins; 13629, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas said: ask! and I said "a resting-place and a repository?" and he said the resting-place in the womb and the repository what was deposited in the loins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 565}

The editor says the printed text has "and will be created" with an unneeded "and", and that "Yahya al-Jabir" in 13628 is Yahya ibn Abd Allah ibn al-Harith ibn al-Mujbir al-Taymi, the printed "Yahya al-Jabiri" being a plain error.

13630, from Ibn Abbas: the resting-place is the womb and the repository what is with the Lord of the worlds of what he will create and has not created; 13631, from him on the verse of Hud: the resting-place what was in the womb, living or dead, and the repository what is in the loins; 13632, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas said to me, before my face had grown hair: have you married, Ibn Jubayr? I said no, and I do not want that today! He said: still, there will come out what is in your loins of those deposited; 13633, the like: he struck my back and said what is deposited in your back will come out; 13634, from Ibn Abbas: the resting-place in the wombs and the repository in the loins, not yet created and he is its creator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 566}

The editor says the printed text has an erroneous form of Abu al-Jabr's name and the manuscript's unpointed form is wrong too, and that the phrase "before my face had grown hair" means before his beard sprouted, a rare expression whose explanation is not found in the dictionaries and works on figures.

13635, from Ibn Abbas: the resting-place in the womb and the repository what is deposited in the loins of men and beasts; 13636, from Mujahid: the resting-place what settled in the womb and the repository what was deposited in the loins; 13637, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas, the like. 13638, from Kurayb: Ibn Abbas called me and said write: in the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate, from Abdullah ibn Abbas to so-and-so, rabbi of Tayma, peace on you, for I praise to you God, there is no god but he; to proceed. I said, will you begin with "peace on you"? He said God is peace. Then he said write: peace on you, to proceed, tell me of "a resting-place and a repository". He sent me with the letter to the Jew and I gave it to him, and when he looked at it he said: welcome to the letter of my friend of the Muslims! He took me to his house, opened large chests of his, and began throwing those things aside, paying them no heed; I said, what is the matter? He said these are things the Jews wrote! until he took out a scroll of Moses; he looked at it twice and said: the resting-place is the womb; then he read [[passage/quran-22-5|22:5]], "we settle in the wombs what we will", and read [[passage/quran-2-36|2:36]] and [[passage/quran-7-24|7:24]], "you have on earth a resting-place and enjoyment". He said: He said: its resting-place above the earth, its resting-place in the womb, and its resting-place beneath the earth until it goes to Paradise or the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 568}

The editor identifies, in 13638, Kurayb ibn Abi Muslim al-Hashimi, client of Ibn Abbas, a trusted Follower.

13639 and 13640, from Ata: the resting-place is what settled in the wombs of women and the repository what was deposited in the loins of men; 13641 to 13645, from Ata and Mujahid: the resting-place is the womb and the repository the loins, what settled in the womb and what was deposited in the loin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 568}

13646, from Ibn Awn: we came to Ibrahim at evening and they told us he had died, and we said did anyone ask him about anything? they said Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad asked him about the resting-place and the repository, and he said the resting-place is in the womb and the repository in the loins; 13647 to 13649, the like; 13650, from al-Ala ibn Harun: I reached Ibrahim's house when he died and said to them did anyone ask him about anything? they said Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad asked him about a resting-place and a repository, and he said as for the resting-place, it is what settled in the wombs of women, and the repository what is in the loins of men. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 569}

The editor identifies, in 13650, Ubaydallah ibn Muhammad ibn Harun al-Firyabi, a teacher of al-Tabari, Damra ibn Rabi'a al-Filastini, and al-Ala ibn Harun al-Wasiti, who lived at Ramla and related from Ibn Awn, trusted, and fears, even thinks it likely, that this report is by al-Ala ibn Harun from Ibn Awn.

13651, from Mujahid: the resting-place is the womb and the repository the loin; 13652, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas said to me: will you not marry? then said: I say this to you knowing that God will bring out of your loins what is in them of the deposited; 13653, from al-Suddi, 13654, from Ibn Abbas, 13655, from Qatada, 13656, from al-Dahhak, 13657, from Ibn Zayd and 13658, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibrahim: the resting-place in the womb and the repository in the loin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 571}

The editor says the printed text has "what was in it as a deposit", differing from the manuscript to no point.

Others said the resting-place is in the grave and the repository in the world. 13659, from Qatada: al-Hasan used to say a resting-place in the grave and a repository in the world, and it is near to joining its companion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 571}

Abu Jafar said: the best of the interpretations on this for being right is to say that God covered by "a resting-place and a repository" all his creatures whom he brought into being from a single soul, as resting or deposited, and did not single out any one sense; there is no doubt that among the children of Adam are those at rest in the womb and deposited in the loin, and those at rest on the earth's back or belly and deposited in men's loins, and those at rest in the grave and deposited on the earth's back; so each resting-place or repository of any of these senses comes within the generality of "a resting-place and a repository" and is meant, unless a report comes that must be accepted that a particular sense is meant to the exclusion of another and a particular rather than general. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 571}

The readers differed in the reading of "a resting-place and a repository". Most readers of Medina and Kufa read "mustaqarr" with fatha and "mustawda'" with fatha, meaning some God made rest in their place and so are made to rest, and some God deposited where he deposited them and so are deposited in it; and some readers of Medina and some of Basra read "mustaqirr" with kasra on the qaf, meaning some have settled in their place and so are settled in it. The better of the two readings in my view, though both have a sound sense, is "mustaqarr", meaning God made it rest in its resting-place, so that the sense is in agreement for it and for "mustawda'", in that neither has its doer named, and in attributing the report to God that he is the one who makes this rest and deposits this; for all agree in reading "mustawda'" with fatha on the dal, passive, so keeping the first the same is likelier than turning from it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 572}

As for "we have detailed the signs for a people who understand" he says: we have made the proofs plain and distinguished the evidences and signs and made them firm for a people who understand the places of proofs and the occasions of lessons and grasp the signs and the reminder; for when they take heed of what I called them to attend, my bringing humans into being from a single soul, the people they see, and my creation of wonders of colours and forms from it, they know that this is the act of one who has no equal and no partner whom they may associate in worshipping him. 13660, from Qatada: we have made the signs plain for a people who understand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 572}

The editor refers for "detailing" and "understanding" to earlier passages.

Verse 98 closes here on p. 572, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-99|6:99]].

The editor says that Abu al-Jabr ibn Tamim came earlier at 13629 and 13623 with the correction, and that "asfat" is the plural of "safat", a vessel like a sack, the report showing here that they used it to keep books and scrolls.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
