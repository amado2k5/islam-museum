## Summary
Verse 87 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 87 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse (p. 592, notes 2-7).** Al-Tabari says God here means the one worshiped, to whom alone worship is due, to whom belongs the worship of everything and the obedience of every obedient one (the editor refers to earlier explanations of worship and of this word; the page numbers are garbled and not given). The assembling for the day of rising means He will raise them after their death and assemble them all at the place of reckoning where people are repaid for their deeds and judgement is made between the people of obedience and disobedience and of belief and unbelief; and the negation is of any uncertainty about the reality of what He reports, that He will gather them after their death. The editor says the print misread the manuscript in one place. Al-Tabari then explains the question about the most truthful: know the truth of this report, for He will gather you for recompense, presentation, reckoning, reward and punishment, certainly, so do not doubt its authenticity or dispute its reality; His word is the truth with no lie, His promise the truth with no breach; and what speaker could be more veracious in speech? (The editor says the print has another word and he kept the manuscript.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 592}

**The argument that falsehood is impossible (p. 593, note 1).** Al-Tabari says a liar lies only to bring some benefit to himself or avert harm from himself; God is the creator of harm and benefit, so a lie from Him is impossible since nothing calls Him to bring benefit to Himself or avert harm; and there is no one other than Him who is free of any such motive, so that he might be His equal in the impossibility of lying; hence He said what He said about the most veracious in speech and report. The editor says the words between brackets are his addition because the print's sentence is crooked and the manuscript's is jumbled, showing the copyist dropped something from al-Tabari's words, so he made an effort to place this addition to make the wording sound, and added the word "so He said" in brackets because the sentence needs it; he explains that al-Tabari put the first argument in the preceding sentence and then followed it with an account of the comparative and the fact that nothing is God's equal in it. This is given as al-Tabari's and the editor's; the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 593}

**End of volume 8 (p. 594).** This page closes the eighth part of the edition and announces that part nine opens with the commentary on verse 88 of the same sura ([[passage/quran-4-88|4:88]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 594}
