## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "Or the sky will fall as you claimed for us, eclipse, or bring Allah and angels to the tribe": Reciters differed in reading the phrase "eclipse," so most reciters of Kufa and Basra read it with a sukun on the 'sin,' meaning: or the sky falls as you claimed for us as eclipse. This is because in Arabic speech, 'kasfa' is the plural of 'kasfa,' which is the plural of many numbers for the gender, just as the lote tree is pluralized with Sidr, and dates with dates. It is narrated from the Arabs by hearsay: Give me a piece of this garment: meaning a piece of it. It is said: He brought us a piece of bread: meaning a piece of bread. It is also possible that if it is read as 'Kisfa' with a sukun on the 's', it is meant as the verbal noun from 'eclipse'. As for 'al-kasfa' with a fatha on the 'sin', it is the combination of three to ten; it is said: one kasfa, three eclipses, and likewise up to ten. In the maqta', statements are transmitted from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Jurayj.
