## Summary
Verse 43 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 43 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "and We shall remove whatever rancour is in their breasts, beneath them rivers flow": We removed from the breasts of these whom He described, and told us are the companions of Paradise, the grudge, malice and enmity some of them bore toward others in the world, and placed them in Paradise on couches facing one another, none envying another for anything with which God favoured him, rivers of Paradise flowing beneath them. He gives (14658) from al-Dahhak that "ghill" is enmity; (14659) from Qatada that it is the grudges; (14660) and (14661) from Ali that the verse was revealed about them, the people of Badr; and (14662) from Qatada that Ali said he hoped to be, with Uthman, Talha and al-Zubayr, among those God spoke of in it, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 438}

and (14663) from al-Suddi that when the people of Paradise are driven to it they find at its gate a tree with two springs at its foot; they drink from one and the rancour in their breasts is removed, which is the pure drink, and wash in the other and the radiance of bliss comes over them, so they are never dishevelled or soiled again; and (14664) from Abu Nadra that the people of Paradise are held back before it until accounts are settled between them, so that they enter and none claims from another even a nail-paring wrongly taken, and the people of the Fire are held before it in the same way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 439}

Then, on "and they said, Praise be to God who guided us to this; we would not have been guided had God not guided us": these who believed and did righteous deeds said, when admitted to Paradise and seeing God's honour and the punishment turned from them: praise be to God who enabled us to the work that earned us this, and we would not have been led to it had God not led us and enabled us by His grace and bounty. He gives (14665) from Abu Hurayra a report from the Prophet that every one of the Fire's people sees his place in Paradise and says, had God guided us, which becomes a regret for them, and every one of Paradise's people sees his place in the Fire and says, had God not guided us, which is their thanks; the note explains that attributing it to Abu Sa'id in the text is an error and Abu Hurayra is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 440}

(14666) from Ali is a long report on the entry of the people of Paradise: they find a tree from whose foot two springs flow, wash in one so that the radiance of bliss comes over them, their hair is not dishevelled and their skin does not change, and drink from the other so that all harm and filth in their bellies comes out; then the gate of Paradise is opened to them and they are greeted, and the youths receive them; they then bring the news to their wives, who come to the threshold, and they enter to find houses of pearl with mansions, raised couches, set cups, ranked cushions and spread carpets, embrace their wives and say praise be to God who guided us to this. The notes treat its narrators, its sources, and Asim ibn Damra, and point to the oddity of the mention of Umar in its setting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 441}

Then, on "the messengers of our Lord came with the truth, and they were called: that is the Paradise you were made to inherit for what you used to do": he reports that these say on entering Paradise and seeing God's honour, while God's enemies are in the Fire: the messengers of our Lord had come to us in the world with the truth, with news of God's promise to the people of obedience and His threat to the people of disobedience. As for "they were called", it means a caller called to them that this is the Paradise that My messengers described to you, which God made you inherit from those who denied His messengers, for your belief and obedience. He gives (14667) from al-Suddi that there is no unbeliever or believer without a place in both Paradise and the Fire, so Paradise is raised for the people of the Fire to see their places and they are told these would be yours had you obeyed God, and then they are divided among the people of Paradise; and (14668) and (14669) from al-Aghar that the caller calls that they may be well and never ill, live and never die, and enjoy and never be wretched. He then notes that the Arabists differed over the "an" that comes with "tilkum". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 443}

One of the Basra grammarians said it is the heavy "anna" made light, with a pronoun understood in it, and that it cannot be the light "an" because a noun follows it and the light one is not followed by nouns; he cites two lines, one by al-A'sha and one whose poet is unknown, and the note explains that Sibawayh's version of al-A'sha's line is joined from two lines. Its meaning for him is "that both of us", and it is like "that we have found" in place of "that is" and "that perform", and it is not the "an" that acts on verbs, since one says "it angered me that he rose", where it falls on verbs without acting on them, and so in "the chiefs among them went off: that go on", meaning go on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 444}

Some of the Kufans denied this, saying it is not allowed for an understood pronoun to go with "an" here, since "an" enters speech to shield what follows it; and this "an" that goes with "tilkum" is the one in which what resembles quotation, without being the wording of quotation, occurs, as in "I called that you are standing", so it is followed by the whole of the speech and "an" is a shield, because calling is like thinking and what resembles saying, so what follows it is left intact as what follows saying is. As for "ayy", it is not on "an", because it is not the answer of the speech. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 445}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
