## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "The Day they see the angels, that Day there will be no glad tidings for the criminals and they will say, 'A stone is barren'": the day those who said: "Why did the angels not come down to us or we would see our Lord" by believing the angels by Muhammad, then there will be no good news for them that day. "They say a stone is a stone," meaning the angels say to the criminals, a stone that is confined. Forbidden to you today is the glad tidings that you will have from God and from the stone. The one who seeks to say to it is a forbidden stone, except those hills. From this is their saying: the judge's stone is upon so-and-so, and so-and-so's stone is upon his family, and from it is the stone of the Kaaba, because he does not enter it during Tawaf, but only circumambulates behind it, and from it is the other person's statement. So I wished to throw a stone to her, for a similar one is thrown at her, meaning a similar stone is thrown to him, meaning a similar one rides the pilgrim. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Al-Hasan.
