## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari notes that the exegetes differed over whom "those are upon guidance from their Lord" points to. Some said both groups already described, the Arab believers in the unseen and those who believe in what was sent down to Muhammad and before him, citing al-Suddi's report from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Masud and other Companions. Others said the God-fearing who believe in the unseen are the very people who believe in what was sent down to Muhammad and before him. Others said it means the believers among the People of the Book alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 247} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 248}
He sets out the grammatical options for the second "alladhina": four ways of reading it as nominative and two as genitive, according to which view is taken of the four opening verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 248}
He prefers that "those" points to both groups together, the God-fearing and those who believe in what was sent down to Muhammad, arguing from God's justice: He would not single out one of two groups for praise when they are equal in what earned the praise, just as they could not be equal in deeds and have only one rewarded. By "upon guidance from their Lord" he understands that they stand on light from their Lord, proof, uprightness and soundness, through God's guiding and enabling them, and he gives Ibn Abbas's report in this sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 248} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 249}
