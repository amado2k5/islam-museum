## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "our Lord, send among them a messenger from among them who will recite to them Your signs" al-Tabari says this is the prayer of Abraham and Ishmael for our Prophet Muhammad in particular, the prayer of which the Prophet used to say "I am the prayer of my father Abraham and the glad tidings of Jesus"; he relates from Khalid ibn Ma'dan that some of the Companions said, Messenger of God, tell us about yourself, and he said I am the prayer of my father Abraham and the glad tidings of Jesus. In a footnote the editor says the chain is mursal, since Khalid ibn Ma'dan does not say he had it from a Companion, and that it is established as connected through Ibn Ishaq in al-Hakim's collection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 82}

On this prayer al-Tabari relates a report from al-'Irbad ibn Sariya, who said he heard the Messenger of God say: "I am with God in the Mother of the Book the seal of the prophets, while Adam was still prostrate in his clay, and I shall tell you the meaning of that: I am the prayer of my father Abraham, the glad tidings of Jesus to his people and the vision of my mother", and then relates it by two other chains from al-'Irbad. In his footnotes the editor gives a detailed account of its sources and of the differences in the chains, the name of one transmitter and a Successor missing from one, and judges the chains sound despite the differences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 82} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 83} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 84} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 85}

A number of interpreters said, as al-Tabari does, that Muhammad is meant: from Qatada, God did so and sent among them a messenger from themselves whose face and lineage they knew, bringing them out of darkness into light; from al-Suddi, he is Muhammad; from al-Rabi', he is Muhammad, and it was said to him that it was answered and that it is at the end of time. He takes "recite to them Your signs" to mean read to them Your Book which You reveal to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 85} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 86}

On "and teach them the Book and the wisdom" he says the Book is the Qur'an, from Ibn Zayd. He then sets out the disagreement on "wisdom": some said it is the Sunna, from Qatada; some, knowledge of the religion, understanding of it and following it, from Malik ibn Anas, whom Ibn Wahb asked; and from Ibn Zayd, wisdom is the religion that they would not know except through him, and understanding in the religion, something God puts in the heart so that it is lit by it, citing "and whoever is given wisdom has been given much good" and what is said of Jesus, "and He will teach him the Book and the wisdom". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 86} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 87}

Al-Tabari says the correct meaning of "wisdom" for him is knowledge of God's rulings which can be known only through the Messenger's explanation, together with knowing them and what is like them, and that it is taken from "hukm" in the sense of separating truth from falsehood, so that one says someone is "hakim" when his word and deed are plainly right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 87}
