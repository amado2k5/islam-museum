## Summary
Verse 112 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 112 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "thus We made for every prophet an enemy: devils of humans and jinn, inspiring one another with embellished speech to deceive", al-Tabari says God speaks to His prophet Muhammad, consoling him for what he met from the unbelievers of his people for God's sake and urging him to patience: as We tried you, Muhammad, by making for you among the polytheists of your people enemies who are devils, inspiring one another with embellished speech to turn people, through their disputing with you, away from following you and believing in you and in what you brought from your Lord, so We tried the prophets and messengers before you by making for them enemies from their own people who harmed them with argument and quarrels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 50}

So this trial with which I tested you was not set for you alone among them; I included them with you, to try and test them, though I am able to prevent those who harmed them, and I did it only to know the resolute among them from the others; so be patient as the resolute among the messengers were patient. "Devils of humans and jinn" are their rebels (the editor refers to the earlier commentary on "shaytan"), and the accusative of "enemy" and "devils" depends on "We made" (he refers to al-Farra'). "Inspiring one another with embellished speech to deceive" means that the one among them who casts the speech casts, to his fellow, a saying he has adorned and beautified with falsehood so that whoever hears it is deceived and strays from the way of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 51}

The interpreters then differed over "devils of humans and jinn". Some said it means the devils of humans who are with humans and the devils of jinn who are with jinn, and that humans have no devils of their own kind. Report 13765, from al-Suddi: the devils of humans are those that lead humans astray and the devils of jinn those that lead jinn astray; the two meet, and each says, "I led my companion astray by such and such, and you led yours astray by such and such", so they teach one another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 51}

Report 13766, from Ikrima: there are no devils among humans, but the devils of the jinn inspire the devils of humans and the devils of humans inspire the devils of the jinn (the editor notes that Sa'id ibn Masruq al-Thawri is trustworthy); 13767, from al-Suddi: the human has a devil and the jinni has a devil; the human's devil meets the jinni's devil, and they inspire one another with embellished speech to deceive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 52}

Al-Tabari says: Ikrima and al-Suddi, in this interpretation of theirs, made the enemies of the prophets whom God mentions the children of Iblis, not the children of Adam nor the jinn, and made those described as inspiring one another with embellished speech the offspring of Iblis, so that those of his offspring who are with the sons of Adam inspire those of his offspring who are with the jinn. This interpretation has no intelligible basis, he holds, because God made Iblis and his offspring enemies of the sons of Adam, so every one of his offspring is an enemy to every one of Adam's; and in this verse God specifies the prophets in reporting that He made devils enemies to them. If the devils meant were those al-Suddi mentions, the children of Iblis, there would be no sense in the specification of the prophets in the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 52}

He holds that the meaning is rather as he said: that God made the rebels of humans and jinn an enemy to every prophet, inspiring one another with the kind of speech with which they harm them. A report from the Messenger of God came in the sense we gave. Report 13768, from Abu Dharr (through Humayd ibn Hilal, from a man of Damascus, from Awf ibn Malik): the Messenger of God said, "Abu Dharr, have you sought refuge in God from the evil of the devils of humans and jinn?" I said, "Messenger of God, do humans have devils?" He said, "Yes." Report 13769, from Abu Dharr: I came to the Messenger of God at a sitting where he had stayed long, and he said, "Have you prayed?" I said no; he said, "Stand and pray two rak'as." Then I came and sat with him and he said, "Have you sought refuge in God from the evil of the devils of humans and jinn?" I said, "Do humans have devils?" He said, "Yes, worse than the devils of the jinn." The footnote on 13768 says Humayd ibn Hilal al-Adawi is trustworthy though spoken of, that he heard from Awf ibn Malik but narrates here through an unknown man of Damascus, and cites Ibn Kathir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 53}

In the footnote on report 13769 the editor shows that its chain in the manuscript has a gross error from the copyist's haste and puts it right (Abu Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Ayyub should be Abu Abd al-Malik Muhammad ibn Ayyub al-Azdi, as in al-Bukhari's al-Tarikh al-Kabir, and Ibn A'idh is Abd al-Rahman ibn A'idh); he says that Ibn Kathir cites the report and calls it broken, whereas the editor holds that it is not, and that Ibn Kathir gives other routes, among them one in the Musnad of Ahmad (5:178-179), and says that together they show its strength and soundness. Then report 13770, from Qatada: it reached me that Abu Dharr stood one day praying, and the Prophet said to him, "Seek refuge, Abu Dharr, from the devils of humans and jinn", and he said, "Are there devils among humans?" He said, "Yes" (the editor calls this report broken in its chain). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 54}

Others said of it what we said, that God is reporting that the devils of humans and jinn inspire one another. Report 13771, from Qatada: some of the jinn are devils and some of humans are devils, inspiring one another, and he relays the Abu Dharr report; 13772, from Qatada: we were told that Abu Dharr stood one day praying and the Prophet of God said to him, "Seek refuge in God from the devils of jinn and humans", and he said, "Do humans have devils like the devils of the jinn?" He said, "Yes; or have I lied about it?" (the footnote explains this as the Prophet's rejection of Abu Dharr's question, since the text of the revelation shows it); and 13773, from Mujahid: the unbelievers among the jinn are devils who inspire the devils of humans, the unbelievers among humans, with embellished speech to deceive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 55}

As for "embellished speech to deceive", it is that which is adorned with falsehood; one says "he embellished his speech and his testimony" when he beautified it with falsehood and gilded it. Report 13774, from Ikrima: the adorning of falsehood with tongues. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 55}

Al-Tabari goes on with the interpreters on "embellished speech to deceive". Report 13775, from al-Suddi: they embellished it, adorned it; 13776 and 13777, from Mujahid: the adorning of falsehood with tongues; 13778, from Ibn Abbas: they made the speech seem fair to one another so that they would follow them in their trial; 13779, from Ibn Zayd: "zukhruf" is the adorned, in that this deception was made fair for them, as Iblis made fair to Adam what he brought him and swore he was a sincere adviser to him, and he recited al-Fussilat 41:25 ("We assigned them companions who made things fair to them"). "Ghurur" is what deceives a person and tricks him, turning him from the right to the wrong and from truth to falsehood, a verbal noun from "I deceived so-and-so, I deceive him with ghurur and gharr" (the editor refers to the earlier commentary on "ghurur"); and report 13780, from al-Suddi: they deceive with it humans and jinn. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 56}

He then explains "had your Lord willed, they would not have done it; so leave them and what they invent": had you willed, Muhammad, that those who were enemies of My prophets, the devils of humans and jinn, believe, so that their scheming did not touch them and they were safe from their treachery and harm, I would have done it; but I did not will it, in order to try some of them by others, each group deserving what had gone before for it in the earlier Book. "So leave them" means: let them be, namely the devils who dispute with you with falsehood among the polytheists of your people and quarrel with you through what their allies among the devils of humans and jinn inspire in them; "and what they invent" means what they fabricate of falsehood and lies. He says to him: be patient with them, for I am behind their punishment for their inventing against God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 57}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
