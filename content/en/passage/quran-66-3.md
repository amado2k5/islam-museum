## Al-Tabari's commentary

His statement, “And when the Prophet confided a matter to one of his wives, and she divulged it and Allah made it known to him, he informed her of part of it and ignored part. And when he informed her of it, she said, ‘Who told you this?’ He said, ‘The All-Knowing, the All-Aware told me,’” explains: “And when the Prophet,” Muhammad, “confided a matter to one of his wives,” and in the words of Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Zayd ibn Aslam, his son Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd, al-Sha’bi, and al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, it is Hafsa. We have already mentioned the narration regarding this. His statement, “a conversation,” refers to the conversation he confided in her, according to these scholars. It was his statement to one of his wives that he had forbidden himself his concubine, or what he had forbidden himself of what God, may His praise be exalted, had made lawful for him, and his oath to that effect, and his statement, “Do not mention this to anyone.” His statement, “So when she informed [him] of it,” means, as God, may He be exalted, says: When she informed her companion of the conversation that the Messenger of God had confided in her, “and God revealed it to him,” means: God revealed to His Prophet Muhammad that she had informed her companion of it. The passage also includes statements attributed to al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd.
