## Summary
Verse 93 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 93 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 93: what was lawful for the Children of Israel (p. 7).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that nothing of food had been forbidden to the Children of Israel, the descendants of Jacob son of Isaac son of Abraham, before the Torah came down; all of it was lawful for them except what Jacob forbade himself, which his sons then forbade in imitation of their father, without God's forbidding it to them in revelation, in a sending down, or on the tongue of a messenger before the Torah came down. He then says that the interpreters differed over whether the prohibition of it for them was sent down in the Torah or not.

**First view: the Torah forbade it (7399, pp. 7-8).** Some said that when God sent down the Torah He forbade them of that what they had been forbidding before it came. Muhammad ibn al-Husayn relays from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that the Jews said: we forbid only what Israel forbade himself; he forbade the veins; sciatica seized him by night and left him by day, and he swore that if God cured him of it he would never eat a vein; so God forbade it to them. Then God told His prophet to challenge them to produce the Torah and read it out, and that no one but He had forbidden this to them, because of their transgression, as in [[passage/quran-4-160|4:160]] (7399). Al-Tabari explains that the sense on this view is that every food was permitted before the Torah except what Israel forbade himself, of which God forbade them in the Torah what Israel had forbidden, on account of their wronging themselves; and if they deny that, they are told to produce the Torah and read it out if they are honest that God did not forbid it to them in it. The editor says that the veins are the blood vessels of the flesh, the hollow ones in which blood runs, while the non-hollow ones are the sinews.

**Second view: the Torah did not forbid it (7400-7402, pp. 8-10).** Others said that none of it was forbidden to them nor did God forbid it to them in the Torah, but that it was something they forbade themselves in following their father and then attributed the prohibition to God; so God declared them liars in attributing it to Him, and ordered His prophet to ask them to produce the Torah and read it out so that they could see whether it is in it, making their lie plain to those who do not know their affair. Al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj relays from Abu Muadh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman, from al-Dahhak that Israel is Jacob; sciatica seized him so that he could not rest at night for the pain, though it did not trouble him by day; he swore that if God healed him he would never eat a vein, and that was before the Torah came down to Moses; the Prophet asked the Jews about it and they said the Torah had come down forbidding what Israel forbade; so God ordered His prophet to demand the Torah from them, and al-Dahhak said they lied and invented, for the Torah did not come down with it (7400). Al-Tabari says that on this view the sense is that every food was permitted to the descendants of Israel before the Torah came down and after it came down, except what Israel forbade himself before it, with the meaning 'but', that is, that Israel forbade himself some of it, and that al-Dahhak seems to take it as what the grammarians call the detached exception. The editor says the printed text has a reading that is nothing, because the copyist of the manuscript had put too many dots on the letters of the word, which confused the publisher; that the verb meaning to spend the night does not mean to sleep, since the lexicographers say it means to enter the night, and whoever says it means to sleep has erred; and that the phrase means he could not settle or rest at night. He refers to the earlier use of the particle 'illa' in the sense of 'but' in volume 3.

**Third view: Israel forbade it to his children by his own prohibition (7401 and 7402).** Others said the sense is that every food was permitted to the descendants of Israel except what Israel forbade himself before the Torah came down, so that it is forbidden to his children by Israel's forbidding it to them, without God having forbidden it to Israel or his children. Muhammad ibn Sad relays from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas that he forbade himself the veins; he suffered from sciatica and could not sleep at night; he said: by God, if God spares me it, no child of mine will eat it; it is not written in the Torah; the Prophet asked some of the People of the Book, and they said it is forbidden to us from before the Book (7401). Al-Qasim relays from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, who said Ibn Abbas said that sciatica seized him and he could not rest at night from the severity of the pain, though it did not trouble him by day; he swore that if God healed him he would never eat a vein, and that was before the Torah came down; the Jews said to the Prophet that the Torah came down forbidding what Israel forbade; God ordered His prophet to demand the Torah from them, and they lied, for it is not in the Torah (7402). The editor corrects the printed wording by the manuscript and refers to his earlier note.

**Al-Tabari's choice (7403 and 7404).** Al-Tabari says the most correct view in his opinion is that of those who said the sense is that every food was permitted to the descendants of Israel before the Torah came down except what Israel forbade himself without God forbidding it to him, so that it was forbidden to them by their father Israel's forbidding it, without God forbidding it to them in a sending down or a revelation before the Torah, until the Torah came down and God forbade them in it what He willed and permitted what He willed. He says this is the saying of a group of the interpreters and the sense of the saying of Ibn Abbas that he gave before. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that Israel is Jacob, and that every food was permitted before the Torah came down except what Israel forbade himself, and when God sent down the Torah He forbade them in it what He willed and permitted what He willed (7403); Ammar relays from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Qatada in similar terms (7404).

**What did Israel forbid himself? The veins (7405-7414).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over what Israel forbade. Some said the veins. Yaqub ibn Ibrahim relays from Hushaym, from Abu Bishr, from Yusuf ibn Mahak that a Bedouin came to Ibn Abbas and said that he had made his wife forbidden to himself; Ibn Abbas said: she is not forbidden to you. The Bedouin said: why, when God says in His book such and such? (alluding to the verse); Ibn Abbas laughed and said: what do you know of what Israel forbade himself? Then he turned to the people and said: sciatica attacked Israel and wore him down, and he vowed to God that if He lifted it from him he would not eat a vein, and that is why the Jews remove the veins from meat (7405). The editor says that 'anasa' is the plural of 'nasa', the vein that comes out of the hip, runs inside the thighs and goes down to the ankle, which the known sickness seizes. Ibn Bashshar relays from Muhammad ibn Jafar, from Shu'ba, from Abu Bishr, from Yusuf ibn Mahak the same, that the Bedouin mentioned a man who forbade his wife and Ibn Abbas said she is not forbidden (7406). Yaqub ibn Ibrahim relays from Ibn Ulayya, from Sulayman al-Taymi, from Abu Mijlaz that Jacob was seized by the pain of sciatica and vowed to God, or swore, or took an oath, not to eat it of animals, and that all the veins follow that vein (7407); the editor says the wording in the manuscript and printed text is not sound and that he inferred what he put from the verb meaning 'swore' in the next report. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, who said: it was mentioned to us that sciatica seized him one night and kept him awake, so he swore that if God cured him he would never eat a sciatic vein, and his sons then searched out the veins and drew them out of meat (7408); Ammar relays from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Qatada the same, adding that what he forbade himself before the Torah came down was the veins (7409). Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada that Israel complained of sciatica and said: God has cured me, so I will forbid the veins, and he forbade them (7410). Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Sufyan al-Thawri, from Habib ibn Abi Thabit, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas that sciatica seized Israel and he spent the night crying out, and vowed to God that if He cured him he would not eat the veins; Sufyan said that the crying out is shouting (7411). Muhammad ibn Amr relays from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that he complained of sciatica and forbade the veins (7412); al-Muthanna relays it from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (7413); and Ibn Humayd from Jarir, from Mansur, from Habib ibn Abi Thabit, from Ibn Abbas that he would spend the night crying out and forbade himself to eat a vein (7414). The editor explains in a note that the cry is the sound of one weeping and his shouting.

**The other view: camel flesh and milk (7415-7418).** Others said that what Israel forbade himself was camel flesh and milk. Al-Qasim relays from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abd Allah ibn Kathir, who said: we heard that he complained of an illness and they said it was sciatica; he said: Lord, the food dearest to me is camel flesh and milk, and if You heal me I forbid them to myself; and Ibn Jurayj said that Ata ibn Abi Rabah said that Israel forbade camel flesh and milk (7415). Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan that Israel forbade himself camel flesh, that they claimed to find this in the Torah, and that it was before the Torah came down, so God told them to produce the Torah, and that they will not find in it Israel's forbidding himself camel flesh (7416); the editor says the manuscript and printed text read 'except camel flesh', which does not stand, and that he thought it a corruption of the word for 'that is' and put it so. Ibn Bashshar relays from Yahya ibn Said, from Sufyan, from Habib ibn Abi Thabit, from Said, from Ibn Abbas that sciatica seized Israel and he spent the night crying out, which is to say shouting; he vowed that if God cured him of it he would not eat it, meaning camel flesh, and the Jews forbade it; and Ibn Abbas recited the verse to show that this was before the Torah (7417). Abu Kurayb relays from Yahya ibn Isa, from al-A'mash, from Habib, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas that he forbade the veins and camel flesh; he had sciatica and ate of its flesh, spent a night crying out, and swore never to eat it (7418). (These are views and reports al-Tabari transmits, and the museum presents them as they were reported, without a verdict.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 14}

**The rest of the views and the report of Ibn Abbas (7419 and 7420, p. 15).** Abu Kurayb relays from Waki, from Isra'il, from Jabir, from Mujahid that Israel forbade the flesh of livestock (7419); the editor says the printed text has 'flesh' in the plural and that he put the manuscript's reading. Al-Tabari says the most correct of these views is that of Ibn Abbas which al-A'mash relays from Habib, from Said, from him, that it was the veins and the flesh of camels, because the Jews to this day agree on the prohibition of both as their forebears held it. A report from the Prophet to a similar effect has also been transmitted: Abu Kurayb relays from Yunus ibn Bukayr, from Abd al-Hamid ibn Bahram, from Shahr ibn Hawshab, from Ibn Abbas that a band of Jews came to the Prophet and said: Abu al-Qasim, tell us which food Israel forbade himself before the Torah came. The Prophet said: I put you on oath by the One who sent the Torah down to Moses, do you know that Israel, who is Jacob, fell gravely ill and his sickness dragged on, so he vowed a vow to God that if God cured him of his sickness he would forbid himself the food and drink he loved most, and the food he loved most was camel flesh and the drink he loved most was its milk? They said: by God, yes (7420). The editor says this is an abridgement of an earlier report and that its chain is sound and its sources were given there.

**The sense of the demand to produce the Torah (pp. 15-16).** Al-Tabari says the sense of the command is: say, Muhammad, to those of the Jews who claim that God forbade them the veins and the flesh and milk of camels in the Torah: bring the Torah and read it, so that it becomes plain to anyone to whom their lying and false statement about God was hidden that this is not among what He sent down in the Torah; and the sense is that if you are right in your claim that God sent down the prohibition of it in the Torah, bring it to us and read out from it its prohibition on us. This is a report from God about their lying, since they will never bring it as it is; God made His prophet aware of their lie against Him and made that knowledge a proof for him against them. For this matter was hidden from many of the people of their community, and Muhammad, who was unlettered and not of their community, would have been all the less likely to know it had God not informed him by revelation from Himself, so it was for him one of the greatest proofs against them that he is God's prophet to them; for it was one of the reports of their forebears, among the hidden knowledge of theirs that none knows but their elite, except one whom the One from whom nothing is hidden informs, of a prophet or messenger, or one whom God lets in on His knowledge from among whom He wills of His creation. (This is al-Tabari's argument, presented by the museum as his, without a verdict.) The editor says the 'in' in the manuscript's and the printed text's wording is an addition from a slip of the copyist's pen.
