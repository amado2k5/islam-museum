**Commentary on verse 14 (pp. 397-403)**

On "to Him belongs the call of truth; those they call besides Him respond to them with nothing, like one stretching out his hands to water so that it may reach his mouth, and it will not reach it; and the prayer of the disbelievers is only in error", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: to God, from His creation, belongs the call of truth, the "call" being the "truth", as "the abode" is added to "the hereafter" in "the abode of the hereafter" [[passage/quran-12-109|12:109]], {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 397}

as we have explained earlier. By the call of truth is meant only God's oneness and the testimony that there is no god but God. The interpreters took it so: report 20280, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima: "the call of truth", he said, is "there is no god but God". Report 20281, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: the testimony that there is no god but God. Report 20282, from Ali, through Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Hashim, Sayf, Abu Ruq and Abu Ayyub: the affirmation of oneness; the editor notes that this is the ruined chain that has occurred many times. Report 20283, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "there is no god but God". Report 20284, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "there is no god but God". Report 20285, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "there is no god but God", which is not fitting for anyone other than Him; it should not be said, so-and-so is the god of Banu so-and-so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 398}

On "those they call besides Him", he says, mighty be His praise: the gods the polytheists call lords and gods. "Besides Him" means below God; by "min dunihi" the gods are meant, in that they fall short of Him and cannot be a god, and none but the One, the Subduer, may be a god; from it is the saying of Jarir in a verse in which he threatens his opponent that his hands will fall short of him. On "they respond to them with nothing" he says: these gods whom the polytheists call gods do not answer them with anything they want, of benefit or the repelling of harm, "like one stretching out his hands to water": the caller on the gods gains nothing from calling them except what the one who stretches out his hands to water gains, stretching them to it without lifting it to himself in a vessel, but so that it will rise to him by his calling it, pointing at it and gripping it. The Arabs coin a proverb for one who strives for what he cannot reach with the one who grips water; one of them, Dabi' ibn al-Harith al-Burjumi, said in a verse composed in his prison describing his longing for his family, that he was like one clasping water, which holds nothing, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 399}

meaning that of that he has in his hand only what is in the hand of one clasping water, for one who clasps water has nothing in his hand. Another, al-Ahwas ibn Muhammad al-Ansari, said in a verse that he had become, from the love there was between him and her, like one clasping water in the hand. The interpreters said the same. Report 20286, from Ali, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Sayf, Abu Ruq and Abu Ayyub: "like one stretching out his hands to water so that it may reach his mouth, and it will not reach it", he said, like a thirsty man stretching his hand to a well so that the water rises to him, and it will not reach him; the editor notes this is the ruined chain mentioned above. Report 20287, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "like one stretching out his hands to water", he calls the water with his tongue and points to it with his hand, and it never comes to him. Report 20288, from Mujahid, through Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and al-A'raj: "so that it may reach his mouth", he calls it to come to him and it will not come; so too he who is below Him does not respond. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 400}

Reports 20289 to 20292, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: the same. Report 20293, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it does not reach him until his neck is torn and he dies of thirst; God, exalted, says "and the prayer of the disbelievers is only in error"; this is a parable God struck: this one who calls on this idol and this stone besides God receives no answer ever, no good is driven to him and no harm repelled from him until death comes to him, like this one who stretched out his arms to the water to reach his mouth and it does not reach his mouth until he dies of thirst. Others said the meaning is "like one stretching out his hands to water" to take hold of his reflection in it, and he will not reach it. Report 20294, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 401}

"like one stretching out his hands to water so that it may reach his mouth", he said, this is the parable of the polytheist who sets another beside God: he is like the thirsty man who looks at his reflection in water from afar and wants to take hold of it and cannot. Others said what Muhammad ibn Sa'd relates in report 20295, from Ibn Abbas: the parable of the idols and those who worship them besides God is like a man whom thirst has reached until death distresses him, with his palms placed in the water, not reaching his mouth; God says the gods neither answer nor benefit those who worship them until this man's palms reach his mouth, and they never will. Report 20296, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: they benefit them with nothing except what this one gains with his palms, stretching them toward what is never reached. Others said what Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la relates in report 20297, from Qatada, through Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the water does not reach his mouth as long as he stands with his palms outspread, not closing them, "and it will not reach it; and the prayer of the disbelievers is only in error"; he said, this is a parable God struck for whoever takes a god besides God, that it will not benefit him or repel harm from him until he dies in that state. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 402}

On "and the prayer of the disbelievers is only in error" he says: the calling of the one who disbelieved in God on the idols and gods is only in error, that is, without uprightness or guidance, for he associates others with God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 403}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
