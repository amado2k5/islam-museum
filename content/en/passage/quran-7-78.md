## Summary
Verse 78 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 78 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-78|7:78]], al-Tabari says: the "rajfa" seized those of Thamud who hamstrung the camel, and the rajfa is the cry. The noun comes from the saying "rajafa bihi", to shake and rock him, and he cites verses of al-Akhtal, said about Yazid ibn Muawiya, in which the poet mourns lost youth and describes how women turned from him once he went grey; the editor remarks that the lines are full of feeling and sorrow and worth memorising. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 544}

By "rajfa" here he means the cry that shook them and moved them toward destruction, since Thamud perished by the cry, as scholars report. (14830), (14831) and (14833) from Mujahid that the rajfa is the cry; (14832) from al-Suddi that it is the cry. "They were in their dwelling place, fallen" means those whom God destroyed lay in their land and town where they perished; that is why "dar" is in the singular rather than "duur", though the plural may be meant with the singular standing for the whole, as in [[passage/quran-103-2|103:2]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 545}

"Jathimin" means fallen prostrate, motionless, for there is no life in them; the Arabs call one kneeling on his knees "jathim", and Jarir has a line comparing the soot-blackened hearthstones of a cooking pot to crouching kites. (14834) from Ibn Zayd that jathimin means dead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 546}
