## Summary
Verse 144 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 144 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 144, "and of the camels two" and what follows: al-Tabari says the sense of this half is that of the half before it, on sheep and goats; these are four more pairs, as he explained of the earlier four, which makes the eight pairs God described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 188}

On "or were you witnesses when God charged you with this", he takes it as a command from God to His prophet to say to these ignorant idolaters, whose stories were told in the verses just past: which of these eight pairs that I asked you to explain the prohibition of did your Lord forbid you? If they answer about any of them, let him say: who told you God forbade this, did a messenger come to you from your Lord, or did you witness your Lord and see Him, and He charged you with this that you say and forge against God? The editor's note records that the printed edition and the manuscript read one word differently and that he kept the reading he judged correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 188}

He continues that what they say, reporting from God that something is forbidden, cannot be known except by revelation from Him with a messenger He sends to His creatures, or by hearing it from Him; by which of the two ways did they know? Neither was the case. He then explains "who does greater wrong than he who forges a lie against God": whoever conjectures falsehood against God and ascribes to Him a prohibition He did not make, or a permission He did not give, wrongs himself most and is furthest from the truth; "to lead people astray without knowledge" means to turn them from His path; and "God does not guide the wrongdoing people" means He does not grant right guidance to one who forged against Him and ascribed to Him what He did not forbid, out of disbelief in God and denial of His prophet's mission. The editor's notes point back to the earlier commentary on the words for witnessing, charging, forgery, going astray, guidance and wrongdoing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 189}

He gives (14077) from Ibn Zayd that "were you witnesses when God charged you with this" means with this that you say; and (14078) from al-Suddi that those who made the bahira and sa'iba used to say that God had commanded this, so God said "who does greater wrong than he who forges a lie against God, to lead people astray without knowledge". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 189}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
