**Commentary on verse 62 (pp. 156-157)**

On "and he said to his young men, put their goods into their saddlebags so that they recognise them when they return to their family, perhaps they will come back", al-Tabari says: Joseph said to his "fityan", his servant boys (report 19470, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: his boys), "put their goods in their saddlebags", that is, put the price of the food that you took from them into their saddlebags. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "fata" and "bida'a". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 156}

"Rihal" is the plural of "rahl", a plural of abundance; the plural of fewness is "arhul", which covers three to ten. The interpreters say the same of the meaning of "goods": report 19471, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: their silver pieces (the editor explains that "awraq" is the plural of "waraq", silver or money of any kind). Report 19472, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: he then ordered their goods, with which they had paid for the food they were given, to be put in their saddlebags without their knowing. Report 19473, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: he said to his boys as he was measuring for them, put their goods in their saddlebags so that they recognise them when they return to their family, perhaps they will return to me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 157}

If someone asks: for what reason did Joseph order his boys to put his brothers' goods in their saddlebags? It is said that this allows several explanations. One is that he feared his father might have no dirhams, the year being one of drought and dearth, so that taking that from them would harm him, and he wished them to come back to him. Or he wished his father and brothers to have ample means, with little need of him, and returned it to them without their knowing the reason, out of generosity and kindness. The editor notes that the manuscript lacks some words, which he supplied for the sense. The discussion continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 157}

We stop here at the foot of p. 157, in the middle of the commentary on verse 62 (after the first two explanations of why Joseph had the goods put in the saddlebags); the next batch begins at p. 158. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 157}
