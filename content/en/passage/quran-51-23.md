## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "By the Lord of the heavens and the earth. Indeed, the like of what you speak is true": Allah Almighty says, dividing by His creation Himself: "By the Lord of the heavens and the earth. Indeed, what I have said to you, O people, is indeed in the heaven your provision and what you are promised is true, just as it is true that you speak." And Qad: from Awf, from Al-Hasan, regarding His saying: "By the heavens and the earth, indeed it is true like what you say," he said: I heard that the Messenger of Allah said: "Allah fought a people to whom their Lord swore by Himself, but they did not believe Him." Al-Farra' said: There are two ways to combine "ma" and "inna" in this context: the first is that this is similar to the Arabs combining two nouns and particles, as the poet said about nouns: Among the people of Lai, when they fear the vile, the door rings rattle, combining the Lai and the 'Dhin', and one of them is separate from the other, as the other said in the tools: 'As soon as he saw and quotes in the passage from al-Hasan.'
