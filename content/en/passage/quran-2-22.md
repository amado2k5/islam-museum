## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari says "who made the earth a bed for you" is joined to the first "who", and both describe "your Lord": worship the Lord who created you and made the earth a smoothed expanse and a place of rest to settle on. He reads God as recalling His favours to His servants so they return to obeying Him, out of kindness and mercy, not out of any need for their worship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 365}
He reports from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that it is a bed one walks on, the expanse and the resting place, and from Qatada and al-Rabi' ibn Anas that "bed" means a smoothed expanse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 365} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 366}
On "and the sky as a structure" he says the sky (sama') is so called for its height over the earth and those on it; whatever is above something else is its sama', which is why a house's roof is called a samawa, and why one says "sama fulan lifulan" when he rises toward another from above. He cites lines by al-Farazdaq and al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani for this usage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 366}

Al-Tabari reports from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that the sky's structure over the earth is like a dome, a roof over it, and from Qatada that God made the sky a roof for people. God names sky and earth among His favours because their food, provision and livelihood come from them, so He alone is owed obedience and thanks, not idols. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 367}
On "and sent down water from the sky, bringing forth fruits as provision for you" he says God sent rain that brought forth fruits from what they sowed and planted, as food and sustenance, reminding them of His power and favours and that He is the creator and provider, not those they set up as rivals. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 367} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 368}
On "so do not set up rivals to God" he says "andad" is the plural of "nidd", an equal or like, and cites a line of Hassan ibn Thabit. He reports from Qatada and Mujahid that it means "equals", and from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that these are peers among men whom people obey in disobedience of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 368}

On "rivals", al-Tabari reports from Ibn Zayd that they are the gods people set up with God and gave what they gave Him, from Ibn Abbas that they are "likenesses", and from Ikrima that it is like saying "were it not for our dog, the thief would have entered the house". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 369}
He says God forbade them to associate anything with Him or to make any peer to Him in obedience: as He has no partner in creating them, providing for them or in His favours, so obedience and worship should be His alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 369} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 370}
On "while you know" he records disagreement over who is addressed: all polytheists, Arab and People of the Book, or only the People of the Torah and Gospel. He reports from Ibn Abbas that it was revealed about both groups, unbelievers and hypocrites, meaning that they know no other lord provides for them and that the tawhid the Messenger calls to is true; and from Qatada that they know God created them and the heavens and earth, yet set up rivals. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 370}
He gives Mujahid's reading that they know He is one God in the Torah and Gospel, and thinks Mujahid took it as addressed to the People of the Book because he supposed the Arabs did not know God was their creator and provider. Al-Tabari disagrees: God reports in His book that they affirmed His oneness as creator and provider while associating others in worship, and he refers to verses in surat al-Zukhruf and surat Yunus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 371}
His preferred view is that the Arabs' knowledge of God's oneness matched that of the People of the Book and that the address is general, since God challenged all people, so it means every accountable person who knows God's oneness, Arab or non-Arab, literate or unlettered, as Ibn Abbas and Qatada said. This is his own choice between the views. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 372}
