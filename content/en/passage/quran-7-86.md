## Summary
Verse 86 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 86 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-86|7:86]], al-Tabari means by "do not sit on every road threatening" do not sit on every path threatening the believers with death. They used, it is said, to sit on the road of anyone going to Shuayb meaning to believe in him, threaten and frighten him and say he is a liar. (14846) from Qatada that they threatened anyone who came to Shuayb and wanted Islam; (14847) from Ibn Abbas that "sirat" is the road, and they frightened people from going to Shuayb; (14848) from him that they sat on the road and told those passing that Shuayb was a liar, so do not let him draw you from your religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 556}

(14849) and (14850) from Mujahid that "sirat" is a road and they threatened on every true path; (14851) from al-Suddi that they sat on every road threatening the believers; (14852) from him that the meaning is the tax-collectors. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 557}

(14853) from Abu Hurayra or another, Abu Jafar al-Razi being in doubt: on the night of his journey the Prophet came on a log in the road which tore any cloth that passed it and pierced anything; he asked, what is this, Gabriel? He said, this is the likeness of people of your community who sit on the road and cut it, and then he recited the verse. Al-Tabari says this report shows that for Abu Hurayra the meaning was that Shuayb forbade his people highway robbery, and that they were highway robbers. The editor notes that the report is an abridgement of a long one al-Tabari will give in the commentary on al-Isra, that its narrators are trustworthy, and that al-Haythami and al-Suyuti cite it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 558}

It is said "bi-kulli sirat"; in speech outside the Qur'an "fi kulli sirat" would be equally fluent, because a road is not a specific place, as one says "he sat for him at such a place, on such a place, in such a place". And he says "tuidun" not "tidun", because the Arabs say "awadtuhu" with the alif for a threat they leave vague, and when they make it clear they say "wadtuhu khayran" or "wadtuhu sharran" without the alif, as in [[passage/quran-22-72|22:72]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 559}

As for "and you bar from God's way whoever believes in Him", it means you turn away from God's path, which is turning from belief in God and acting in obedience to Him, those who believe in God and affirm His oneness; "and seek to make it crooked" means you look for those who take God's path a deviation from the straight and true toward straying and error. (14854) and (14855) from Mujahid that they bar its people and look for its deviation; (14856) from Qatada that you seek to bend the path away from the truth; (14857) from al-Suddi that you bar from Islam and seek the path's ruin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 559}

We stop here at verse 86 of Surat al-A'raf on printed p. 559, after report 14857 and before "remember when you were few"; the next batch begins on printed p. 560 (marker 564).
