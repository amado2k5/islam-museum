## Summary
Verse 5 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 5 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 5 of sura al-Ma'ida opens by making lawful the good things and the food of the People of the Book. Al-Tabari says 'today the good things are made lawful for you' means: today the lawful of slaughtered animals and foods is made lawful for you, believers, without the foul ones among them, and 'the food of those given the Book' means the slaughtered animals of the People of the Book, the Jews and Christians, those on whom the Torah and Gospel came down and who follow both or one of them; their slaughter is lawful for you to eat, unlike the slaughter of the rest of the idolaters who have no Book, the Arab idolaters and worshippers of idols and images. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 572}

Whoever of those does not affirm God's oneness and follow the religion of the People of the Book, their slaughter is forbidden to you. Then they differed over whom God meant by 'the food of those given the Book' among the People of the Book. Some said He meant the slaughter of every follower of a Book, whether those on whom the Torah and Gospel came down or those who entered their community and followed their religion, forbidding what they forbade and allowing what they allowed, from among them or other nations. Report 11220, from Ikrima: Ibn Abbas was asked about the slaughter of the Christians of Banu Taghlib, and he recited the verse forbidding taking Jews and Christians as allies up to 'whoever allies with them is of them'; the editor notes this report is placed after the next one in the manuscript. Report 11221, from Ibn Abbas, is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 573}

Report 11222, from Qatada: al-Hasan and Ikrima saw no harm in the slaughter of the Christians of Banu Taghlib or in marrying their women, and they recited the same verse; report 11223, from al-Hasan and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, is the same. Report 11224, from al-Sha'bi: he saw no harm in the slaughter of the Christians of Banu Taghlib, and recited the verse 'your Lord is never forgetful'. Report 11225, from Ibn Shihab on the slaughter of Arab Christians: it is eaten because they are People of the Book in religion and mention God's name. Report 11226, from Ata: they only profess the religion of that Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 574}

Report 11227, from Shu'ba: I asked al-Hakam, Hammad and Qatada about the slaughter of the Christians of Banu Taghlib and they said there is no harm in it, and al-Hakam recited the verse 'and among them are unlettered ones who do not know the Book'. Report 11228, from Ibn Abbas: eat the slaughter of Banu Taghlib and marry their women, for God said in His book the verse about alliance; if they were of them only by alliance, they would be of them. Report 11229, from Qatada: al-Hasan saw no harm in the slaughter of Banu Taghlib's Christians and said: they adopted a religion, and that is their religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 574}

Others said that by 'those given the Book' in this verse He meant those on whom the Torah and Gospel came down from the Israelites and their descendants, and that anyone who joined them from other nations, following their religion without being Israelite, is not meant here and his slaughter is not lawful to eat, since he is not of those given the Book before the Muslims. This is what Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i said, as al-Rabi' related to us, and by it he explained the disapproval of the slaughter of Arab Christians by Companions and Followers. Report 11230, from Ubayda: Ali said: do not eat the slaughter of the Christians of Banu Taghlib, for they hold to Christianity only by drinking wine; the editor lists those who recorded it and cites Ibn Hajar that its chains are sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 575}

Reports 11231 and 11232, from Ubayda on Ali, say the same: they hold to nothing of their religion but drinking wine. Report 11233, from Abu al-Bakhtari: Ali forbade us the slaughter of Arab Christians; report 11234, from Muhammad ibn Ali: he disliked the slaughter of the Christians of Banu Taghlib; report 11235, from Ibn Abbas: do not eat the slaughter of Arab Christians or Christians of Armenia. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 576}

Al-Tabari says these reports from Ali show only that he forbade the slaughter of Banu Taghlib's Christians because they were not truly on Christianity, having left what Christians allow and forbid except wine; and whoever claims a faith but holds to none of it is nearer to disowning it than to joining it and its people. That is why Ali forbade eating their slaughter, not because they were not Israelites. Since it is the consensus of the authorities that there is no harm in the slaughter of any Christian or Jew who follows the Christian or Jewish religion, allowing what they allow and forbidding what they forbid, Israelite or otherwise, the error of al-Shafi'i's explanation is clear, and the contrary view is right: the slaughter of every Jew and Christian is lawful, of whatever kind of the children of Adam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 576}

As for the 'food' in 'the food of those given the Book', it is the slaughtered animals, and the interpreters said the same. Reports 11236 to 11240, from Mujahid: 'the food of those given the Book' is the slaughtered animals, their slaughter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 577}

Reports 11241 to 11247, from Mujahid and Ibrahim, say the same: 'the food of those given the Book' is their slaughter; report 11248, from Ibn Abbas: their slaughter; report 11249, from al-Hasan, the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 578}

Report 11250, from Qatada: that is, their slaughter; report 11251, from al-Suddi: as for their food, it is the slaughtered animals. Report 11252, from al-Dahhak: God made lawful for us their food and their women; report 11253, from Ibn Abbas: He made lawful for us their food and their women. Report 11254, from Ibn Zayd, asked about what is slaughtered for churches with God's name said over it: God made lawful for us the food of the People of the Book and excepted nothing from it. Report 11255: Umayr ibn al-Aswad asked Abu al-Darda about a ram slaughtered for a church called Jirjis, given as an offering to it, whether they might eat from it; Abu al-Darda said: God forgive us! They are People of the Book, their food is lawful for us and ours for them; and he told him to eat. In a long footnote the editor sets out the difficulty of this chain, thinks something has dropped out of the sources, and tells apart men with similar names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 579}

As for 'and your food is lawful to them', it means your slaughtered animals, believers, are lawful for the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 580}

Then 'and the chaste women of the believers and the chaste women of those given the Book before you, when you have given them their dowries'. Al-Tabari says it means: made lawful for you, believers, are the chaste women of the believing women, the free among them, for you to marry, and the chaste women of those given the Book, the Jews and Christians who followed what is in the Torah and Gospel before you, that is, the free women, when you give those you marry their wages, their dowries. The interpreters differed over which 'muhsanat' God meant. Some said He meant free women only, whether chaste or unchaste, and those who held this allowed marrying a free woman, believing or from the People of the Book, Jews and Christians, of whatever people, whether chaste or not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 581}

They forbade marrying the slave women of the People of the Book in any case, since God made faith a condition for marrying slave women in the verse in sura al-Nisa'. Reports 11256 and 11257, from Mujahid: 'the chaste of those given the Book' means free women. Report 11258, from Tariq ibn Shihab: a man divorced his wife and his sister was proposed to, though she had done wrong; he came to Umar and told him, and Umar said: what did you see of her? He said: nothing but good; Umar said: marry her off and tell nothing. Report 11259, from al-Sha'bi: a woman of ours from Hamdan fornicated and the Messenger's collector flogged her the prescribed punishment, then she repented; they came to Umar asking whether to marry her off, how bad was what she did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 582}

Umar said: if I hear that you have mentioned any of that, I will punish you severely. Report 11260, from Tariq ibn Shihab: a man wanted to marry off his sister, who said: I fear I will disgrace my father, I have transgressed; he came to Umar, who said: has she not repented? He said: yes. He said: then marry her off. Report 11261, from al-Sha'bi: Nubayshah, a woman of Hamdan, transgressed and tried to cut her own throat; they reached her and treated her and she recovered; they told Umar and he said: marry her as a chaste Muslim woman is married. Report 11262, from Amir: a man of Yemen had a sister who committed an indecency and drew a razor across her throat; she was reached and her wound treated until it healed; her uncle then moved with his family to Medina, where she read the Qur'an and worshipped until she was among their most devout women; she was asked for in marriage, and her uncle disliked deceiving the suitor and disliked exposing his brother's daughter; he went to Umar and told him, and Umar said: had you exposed her I would have punished you; when a righteous man comes whom you accept, marry her to him. Report 11263, from Amir, gives the story of Nubayshah similarly. Report 11264, from Amir: a man came to Umar and said: a daughter of mine was buried alive in the Age of Ignorance and I took her out before she died; she reached Islam, and after becoming Muslim she incurred one of God's prescribed punishments, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 583}

and she took a razor to cut her own throat; I reached her when she had cut some of her neck veins, and treated her until she healed; then she came with good repentance, and she is being asked for in marriage, Commander of the Believers, shall I tell what happened? Umar said: Would you tell her story? You would go to what God has covered and uncover it! By God, if you tell anyone about her I will make you an example for the cities; marry her as a chaste Muslim woman is married. Report 11265, from al-Sha'bi, is similar. Report 11266, from Abu al-Zubayr: a man asked a man for his sister and was told she had done wrong; this reached Umar ibn al-Khattab, who beat the man and said: what has it to do with you? Marry and be silent. Report 11267, from al-Hasan: Umar said: I had resolved not to let anyone who committed an indecency in Islam marry a chaste woman; Ubayy ibn Ka'b said: Commander of the Believers, idolatry is graver than that, and it is accepted from a person who repents. The editor remarks in a footnote that these reports show a fine discipline of the religion, and that those who came later went to excess in their horror at a believer's slip. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 584}

Others said God meant by 'the chaste women of the believers and the chaste of those given the Book before you' the chaste of both groups, slave women or free; those who held this allowed marrying the slave women of the People of the Book who follow their religion, on the strength of this verse, and forbade the prostitutes among the believers and the People of the Book. Reports 11268 and 11269, from Mujahid: the chaste. Reports 11270 to 11274, from al-Sha'bi: the chastity of the Jewish or Christian woman is that she does not commit adultery, that she washes after sexual impurity, and that she guards her chastity from fornication; report 11275, from Sufyan: the chaste. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 585}

Report 11276, from al-Suddi: as for 'al-muhsanat', they are the chaste. Report 11277, from Qatada: a woman took her slave as a husband and said: I interpreted God's Book, 'what your right hands possess'; she was brought to Umar, and some of the Prophet's companions told him she had interpreted a verse of God's Book wrongly; he exiled the slave and cut his hair and said to her: after him you are forbidden to every Muslim; the editor explains that cutting the head here means cutting the hair, not killing. Report 11278, from Ibrahim, on a woman who fornicates before the marriage is consummated: she has no dowry and the two are separated. Reports 11279 to 11281, from al-Sha'bi, Jabir and al-Hasan, on an unmarried woman who fornicates: she is flogged a hundred lashes, exiled for a year, and returns to her husband what she took from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 586}

Report 11282, from al-Hasan: if a man sees an indecency in his wife and is certain of it, he should not keep her. Report 11283, from Abu Maysara: the slave women of the People of the Book are like their free women. The interpreters then differed over the ruling in 'and the chaste women of those given the Book before you': is it general or particular? Some said it is general for the chaste among them, since 'muhsanat' are the chaste, so a Muslim may marry any free or slave woman of the People of the Book, from enemy territory or under protection, relying on the apparent sense of the verse. Others said those meant are the free women among them and the verse is general over all of them, so marrying all free Jewish and Christian women is permitted, whether from enemy territory or under protection, of whatever kind of Jews and Christians; this is the view of a group of earlier and later scholars. Report 11284, from Qatada: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and al-Hasan saw no harm in marrying Jewish and Christian women and said: God made it lawful knowingly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 587}

Others said it means marriage to the women of the People of the Book among the Israelites only, not other peoples who followed Judaism and Christianity; this is the view of al-Shafi'i and those who follow him, and the editor gives his text from the Umm in a footnote. Others said it means the women of the People of the Book who have a pact and covenant with the Muslims, while the women of the people at war are forbidden to the Muslims. Report 11285, from Ibn Abbas: some women of the People of the Book are lawful for us and some are not; then he recited the verse on fighting those who do not believe in God until they pay the jizya: whoever pays it, their women are lawful for us, and whoever does not pay it, their women are not lawful for us; al-Hakam said: I told Ibrahim, and he liked it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 588}

Al-Tabari then weighs in: the soundest view is that what is meant by 'the chaste women of the believers and the chaste women of those given the Book before you' is the free women of the believers and the People of the Book, because God did not permit free men to marry slave women, in the circumstance in which He allowed them to marry, unless they are believers, as in the verse of sura al-Nisa', so He allowed only the believing ones. Had 'muhsanat' meant the chaste, the chaste among their slave women would come within the permission, and the unchaste among their free women and the free women of the believers would be left out; yet God has made free believing women lawful even if they committed an indecency, in the words 'marry the unmarried among you'. So marriage to free Muslim women and free women of the People of the Book is lawful for believers, whether or not they committed an indecency, under treaty or from enemy territory, provided it is in a place where the husband does not fear for his child being forced into unbelief. As for the view that the Israelite women of the Book alone are meant, it deserves no time to answer, being anomalous and outside what the scholars of the community hold in making lawful the women of all Jews and Christians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 589}

As for 'when you give them their wages', the wage is the compensation the husband gives the woman for enjoying her, which is the dowry. Report 11286, from Ibn Abbas: that is, their dowries. Then 'being chaste, not fornicators and not taking lovers'. Al-Tabari says it means: lawful for you are the chaste women of the believers and of those given the Book before you, while you are chaste, not fornicators and not takers of secret lovers; 'muhsinin' are those who are chaste, 'not musafihin' are those who do not openly fornicate with every fornicating woman, and not takers of lovers are those who do not keep to one woman who has become his companion and he hers, taking her as a friend with whom he fornicates. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 590}

Report 11287, from Ibn Abbas: that they marry them with a dowry and declaration, not fornicating openly, nor taking lovers in secret; the editor remarks in a footnote that he thinks 'declaration' here means announcing the marriage, and that he is unsure of all of it. Report 11288, from Qatada: God made lawful for us two kinds of chaste women, a believing one and one of the People of the Book; the taker of lovers is one with a single lover, a single companion. Report 11289, from al-Hasan: a man asked him whether a man may marry a woman of the People of the Book; he said: what has he to do with the People of the Book when God has multiplied Muslim women? If he must, let him choose a chaste one who is not a fornicator; the man asked: what is a fornicator? He said: one who follows a man when he glances at her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 591}

Then 'and whoever disbelieves in faith, his work has come to nothing, and in the Hereafter he is among the losers'. Al-Tabari says it means whoever denies what God ordered belief in, God's oneness and the prophethood of Muhammad and what he brought from God; the reward of his work in this world, by which he hoped to gain a station with God, has been voided, and in the Hereafter he is among the lost, who cheated themselves of their share of God's reward by their disbelief in Muhammad and their working without obedience to God. It is also said that the phrase refers to the People of the Book and was revealed for people who felt uneasy about marrying women of the People of the Book when told it was lawful. Report 11290, from Qatada: we were told some Muslims said: how can we marry their women when they are not of our religion? and God sent down this verse, making their marriage lawful knowingly. Report 11291, from Ata: 'in faith' means in God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 592}

Report 11292, from Ata: faith is the affirmation of God's oneness; reports 11293 to 11298, from Mujahid: whoever disbelieves in God. Report 11299, from Ibn Abbas: God declared that faith is the firmest handhold, that He accepts no deed without it, and forbids Paradise only to whoever leaves it. If someone asks why those who read it as 'whoever disbelieves in God' interpret it so, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 593}

the answer is that faith is belief in God and His messengers and what He sent them with of His religion, and disbelief is denying it; so they say 'disbelief in faith' means denial of God and denial of His oneness, and they explained the sense of the phrase by what was meant, setting aside its literal sense in the recitation. If asked what the literal sense is, it is: whoever refuses faith in God and holds back from His oneness and obedience to Him in what He commanded and forbade, his work has come to nothing, for disbelief in Arabic is denial and faith is affirmation and declaration, and whoever refuses to affirm God's oneness and declare it is among the disbelievers; that is the meaning of the words in their proper sense. With this the ninth volume of al-Tabari's commentary ends, and the tenth begins with the sixth verse, [[passage/quran-5-6|5:6]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 594}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
