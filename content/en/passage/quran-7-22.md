## Summary
Verse 22 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 22 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "so he led them on with delusion; and when they tasted of the tree, their nakedness appeared to them, and they began to cover themselves with leaves of Paradise", he says: "so he led them on with delusion" means he deceived them with delusion; one says "so-and-so goes on leading so-and-so on with delusion", meaning goes on deceiving him with delusion and speaking to him with false adornment of speech. "When they tasted of the tree" means when Adam and Eve tasted the fruit of the tree, that is, savoured it; "their nakedness appeared to them" means their private parts were uncovered to them, because God {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 351}

...stripped them of the clothing with which He had clothed them before the sin and the error, and took it from them because of the error they made and the disobedience they committed; "and they began to cover themselves with leaves of Paradise" means they set about fastening on themselves leaves of Paradise to cover their private parts. He gives (14397) from Ibn Abbas that they began taking leaves of Paradise and putting them on their private parts; and (14398) from Ubayy ibn Ka'b that the Prophet said Adam was like a tall palm with much hair on his head, and when he fell into the error his nakedness appeared to him, which he had not seen before, and he ran off, and a tree caught him and held him by his hair; he said to it, let me go, and it said, I will not let you go; and his Lord called to him, Adam, are you fleeing from Me? He said, no, but I am ashamed before You. The note says Abu Bakr al-Hudhali is weak and that Ibn Kathir says the report stopping with Ubayy is sounder than the one raised to the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 352}

He gives (14399) from Ibn Abbas that the tree God forbade Adam and his wife was the wheat ear, and when they ate of it their nakedness appeared to them, and what had covered their private parts was their nails, and they began to cover themselves with leaves of Paradise, fig leaves, sticking them together; Adam went off through Paradise and a tree seized his head and called, Adam, are you fleeing from Me? He said, no, but I am ashamed before You, Lord. He said, did you not have, in what I gave you of Paradise and made lawful to you, enough room away from what I forbade you? He said, yes, Lord, but by Your might I did not think anyone would swear falsely by You. That, he said, is God's saying "he swore to them: I am for you one of the sincere advisers". He said, then by My might I will send you down to earth and you will get your living only by toil. So he was sent down from Paradise, where they had eaten in plenty, and they were sent down without plenty of food and drink; he was taught the craft of iron and commanded to plough, and he ploughed, sowed, watered, and when it ripened reaped, threshed, winnowed, ground, kneaded, baked and ate it. The note says al-Hasan ibn Umara in the chain is abandoned and his hadith forged. He gives (14400) and (14401) from Mujahid that "they stitched" means they patched like a garment; and (14402) from Qatada that they had not seen them before, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 353}

and (14403) from al-Hasan, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, that Adam was a tall man like a tall palm with much hair on his head, and when he fell into the error that befell him his nakedness appeared to him, which he had not seen, and the note says this is the version stopping with Ubayy and sounder in chain than the raised one; (14404) and (14405) from Ibn Abbas that the leaves were fig leaves; and (14406) from Qatada and Abu Bakr that Adam's clothing in Paradise was entirely nail, and when he fell into sin it was scraped off and his nakedness appeared, and the note says Husam ibn Misakk is very weak and prone to error and Abu Bakr is also weak, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 354}

and (14407) from Qatada that they could not see their nakedness; and (14408) from Wahb ibn Munabbih, on God's words in [[passage/quran-7-27|7:27]], "stripping them of their garments", that the garment of Adam and Eve was a light on their private parts, so neither saw the other's nakedness, and when they fell into the error their nakedness appeared to them; the note says Ibn Kathir calls the chain to him sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 355}

Then, on "and their Lord called to them: did I not forbid you this tree, and tell you that Satan is your clear enemy?", he says: their Lord called Adam and Eve: did I not forbid you to eat the fruit of the tree whose fruit you ate, and tell you that Iblis is your clear enemy, that is, he made his enmity to you plain by refusing to prostrate to Adam out of envy and transgression. He gives (14409) from Muhammad ibn Qays that He said to Adam, why did you eat it when I forbade you? He said, Lord, Eve fed me. He said to Eve, why did you feed him? She said, the serpent told me to. He said to the serpent, why did you tell her? It said, Iblis told me to. He said, cursed and driven away! As for you, Eve, as you made the tree bleed you will bleed every month; as for you, serpent, I will cut off your legs and you will walk on your face, and whoever meets you will crush your head; go down, enemies of one another. The note says the report appeared at length by this chain as no. 752. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 356}

He gives (14410) from Ibn Abbas that when Adam ate of the tree he was asked, why did you eat of the tree I forbade you? He said, Eve told me to. He said, then I have made it that she conceives only reluctantly and gives birth only reluctantly. Eve then wailed, and she was told, wailing is on you and your offspring. The note says "ranat" means she cried out in grief and distress. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 356}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
