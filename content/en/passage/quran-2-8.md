## Summary
Verse 8 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and of the people" al-Tabari gives two analyses: "al-nas" as a plural with no singular of its own form, the singular being "insan", or as originally "unas" with its hamza dropped and the article added; he mentions a view that it is a separate form from "unas", argued from the diminutive "nuways". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 268}
He says all the exegetes agree that the verse was revealed about a group of hypocrites and that the description is theirs. He gives Ibn Abbas that they are the hypocrites of the Aws and Khazraj and those who followed their course, adding that he left out the names to avoid lengthening the book; Qatada that the verses through "and they were not guided" concern the hypocrites; and Mujahid that this verse and the twelve after it describe the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 268} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 269}

He cites al-Suddi, al-Rabi' ibn Anas and Ibn Jurayj that the verse is about the hypocrites; in Ibn Jurayj's words the hypocrite's speech differs from his deed, his secret from his open conduct, his entrance from his exit, and his presence from his absence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 270}
Al-Tabari gives a setting: once the Prophet was settled in his city of hijra and Islam had spread there, the rabbis of its Jews showed hostility out of envy and transgression, and a small group of the Ansar who had hardened in their former polytheism privately sided with them, supporting them in secret for fear for their lives; they told the believers "we believe" and told their fellows, when alone with them, "we are with you". He says he omits their names to avoid lengthening the book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 270} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 271}
He takes "the Last Day" as the Day of Resurrection, called last because no day follows it: in Arab usage a day takes its name from the night before it and no night follows that one, which is why it is called a barren day. Denying them the name of belief is a refutation of what they claimed to hold. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 271} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 272}
