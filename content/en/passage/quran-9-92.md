**Commentary on verse 92 (pp. 421-423)**

Al-Tabari says: nor is there a way against the group who, when they came to you for mounts, asking you for conveyance so as to reach where they would fight, striving against God's enemies with you, Muhammad, you said to them "I find no mount to carry you on", "they turned back", meaning they went away from you, "their eyes overflowing with tears", weeping in grief that they found nothing to spend and with which to equip themselves for striving in God's way. The editor refers to earlier explanations of turning away and of tears flowing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 421}

Some said this verse came down about a group of Muzayna. Report 17080, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: they are of Muzayna; report 17081, from him, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdallah and Warqa': they are Banu Muqarrin of Muzayna; report 17082, from him, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak and Ibn Jurayj: they are Banu Muqarrin of Muzayna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 421}

Report 17083, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr, Warqa' and Ibn Abi Najih: they are Banu Muqarrin of Muzayna. Report 17084, from Ibn Mughaffal al-Muzani, through Urwa, Abu al-Aliya, al-Rabi' ibn Anas and Abu Ja'far: he was one of the band about whom "nor those who, when they came to you for mounts" came down, the verse. Report 17085, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr, Ibn Uyayna and Ibn Jurayj: "they turned back, their eyes overflowing with tears in grief", he said, of them was Ibn Muqarrin, and Sufyan said people said of them was al-Irbad ibn Sariya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 422}

Others said rather that it came down about al-Irbad ibn Sariya. Report 17086, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Amr al-Sulami and Hujr ibn Hujr al-Kala'i, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Abu Asim, Thawr ibn Yazid and Khalid ibn Ma'dan: we went in to al-Irbad ibn Sariya, he being the one about whom "nor those who, when they came to you for mounts" was sent down, the verse; report 17087 is similar through al-Muthanna, Sulayman ibn Abd al-Rahman, al-Walid and Thawr. Others said rather that it came down about seven men from various tribes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 422}

Report 17088, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b and others, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Ma'shar: some of the Companions of the Messenger of God came asking him for mounts, and he said "I find nothing on which to carry you", and God sent down "nor those who, when they came to you for mounts", the verse; he said they were seven men: of Banu Amr ibn Awf, Salim ibn Umayr; of Banu Waqif, Harami ibn Amr; of Banu Mazin ibn al-Najjar, Abd al-Rahman ibn Ka'b, called Abu Layla; of Banu al-Mu'alla, Salman ibn Sakhr; of Banu Haritha, Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid, Abu Abla, who gave his honour in charity and God accepted it from him; of Banu Salima, Amr ibn Ghanama; and Abdallah ibn Amr al-Muzani. Report 17089, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: up to the words "in grief", they are the weepers, who were seven. The editor notes that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and that Ibn Ishaq's count is seven men of the Helpers and others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 423}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
