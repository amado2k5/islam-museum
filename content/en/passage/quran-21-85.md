## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 85 to 86 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "And Isma'il, Idris, and Dhul-Kifl, each of the patient": He means mentioning him as Isma'il ibn Ibrahim, the truthful in promise, and Idris: Enoch, and Dhul-Kifl: a man who was appointed by some people, either as a prophet or as a king among the righteous kings in a certain deed, and he fulfilled it after him. God praised him for his good loyalty to what he undertook and made him among His enumerated servants, along with those who praised his patience in obeying God. And with what we have said about his matter, reports have come from the early scholars. The narration is mentioned from them: And from Al-A'mash, from Al-Minhal ibn Amr, from Abdullah ibn Al-Harith: A prophet among the prophets said: Who is responsible for me to fast during the day and stand up at night without getting angry? A young man stood up and said: I am. He said: Sit down, then he returned and said: Who will guarantee me to stand up at night and fast during the day without getting angry? That young man stood up and said: I am. In the segment, he quotes from Mujahid and Al-A'mash.
