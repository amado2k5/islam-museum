**Commentary on verse 67: "O Messenger, convey what has been sent down to you", pp. 467-469**

Al-Tabari says: this is God's command to His prophet Muhammad to convey to the Jews and Christians, whose faults, boldness against their Lord and alteration of His book God has set out in this surah, and to the other polytheists, what was sent down about them: the faults, the commands and the prohibitions. He is not to feel fear for himself so long as he carries out God's order, nor be alarmed by their numbers and the fewness of those with him, nor guard himself against anyone in God's cause, for God suffices him against every person. God also tells him that if he fell short in conveying anything of what was sent down, however little, he would stand as one who had conveyed none of it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 467}

Report 12270, from Ibn Abbas, gives the sense: if you conceal a verse of what was sent down, you have not conveyed My messages. Report 12271, from Qatada, says God told His prophet He would suffice him against the people and protect him, and that he was once told to withdraw behind a screen but replied that he would show himself to people as long as he lived. Report 12272, from Mujahid, says that when the command to convey came down he said that he was one man and the people would gather against him, and the words "if you do not" were then sent down. Report 12273, from Said ibn Jubayr, says that when it came down he told his Companions not to guard him, for his Lord had protected him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 468}

Reports 12274 to 12277, from Abdullah ibn Shaqiq, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Aisha, say the Companions took turns guarding the Prophet, and when "God protects you from the people" came down he put his head out of the tent and sent them away, saying God had protected him. The editor, Mahmud Shakir, notes that some of these chains are mursal, that Aisha's report is given by al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim, and explains the word for taking turns on guard.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 469}

**Commentary on verse 67, continued: the occasion and the meaning of protection, pp. 470-472**

Al-Tabari notes that the interpreters differed on the occasion. Some said it came down over a bedouin who meant to kill the Prophet, and God spared him from him. Report 12278, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, says that the Prophet used to rest under a shady tree when he halted; a bedouin drew his sword and asked who would defend him, the Prophet said "God", the man's hand trembled and the sword fell. The editor points to weakness in some narrators of this report and to variant versions.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 470}

Others said it came down because he feared Quraysh and was then reassured. Report 12279, from Ibn Jurayj, says he used to dread Quraysh, and when it came down he lay back and said let whoever wishes forsake him. Reports 12280 to 12283, from Masruq on Aisha, say that whoever claims the Prophet hid anything of the revelation has invented a great lie against God, and she then recited the verse. The editor traces these four as one report with several chains, given by al-Bukhari.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 471}

Al-Tabari says "protects you from the people" means keeps them from harming you; the word comes from the cord that binds a waterskin, and he cites a line of poetry for the sense of restraining. As for "God does not guide the unbelieving people", it means God does not grant right guidance to one who turned from the way of truth, rejected what the Prophet brought, and did not stop at God's command.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 472}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
