## Summary
Verse 14 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 14 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The recompense of whoever disobeys and transgresses (pp. 71-72, report 8794).** Abu Jafar says the sense is: whoever goes against God and against His Messenger in doing what they commanded of dividing the inheritances as they commanded them to be divided among those entitled, and other obligations of God, opposing their command by what they forbade, and goes beyond the separations of obedience that God made a division between it and disobedience, to what He forbade of dividing the estates of their dead among their heirs and other of His limits (the editor notes that the printing has the singular "his heir" and that the right reading is the manuscript's), God will make him enter a fire in which he remains for ever, neither dying nor leaving it, and for him is a punishment of shame, one that disgraces the one who is punished with it (the editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word for humiliating). The interpreters spoke along these lines: al-Muthanna (8794, from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that it concerns the inheritances mentioned before, and Ibn Jurayj said that the disobedience meant is committing sins for which God punishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 71} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 72}

**A question on staying for ever in the fire, and al-Tabari's answer (pp. 72-73).** Abu Jafar puts the question of an objector: is the one who goes against God and against His Messenger in dividing inheritances kept in the fire for ever? He answers that he is, if he adds to his disobedience in this a doubt that God obliged him with what He obliged His servants in the two verses, or knew it and opposed God and His Messenger in their command. He bases this on what Ibn Abbas relates of the saying of some when God's ruling about children was revealed to the Messenger of God, through the end of the two verses: shall one who neither rides a horse, nor fights the enemy, nor takes booty be given half the property or all of it? They were objecting to what God had assigned to the dead man's young children, his wives and his daughters. So whoever opposes God's division of the inheritance among its heirs as He divided it in His book, and opposes His ruling and His Messenger's in that, objecting to their ruling as those whom Ibn Abbas mentioned objected, the hypocrites among the Companions of the Messenger of God about whom this verse was revealed and those like them, is of those who stay in the fire for ever, because by objecting to God's ruling in that he becomes one who disbelieves in God and has left the community of Islam. This is Abu Jafar's own statement and argument as he gives it, and the museum gives no verdict on it. The editor's notes: the printing has a verb form where the manuscript's right reading is given; the report of Ibn Abbas meant is the earlier report no. 8726, of which Abu Jafar gives the sense here, not the wording; the phrase "of those who opposed God's division" is the relative clause of "so he opposed God and His Messenger in their command", and he marked the intervening passage between dashes; and the thread of all these sentences continues on the next page: "Yes, if he adds to their disobedience ... then he is of those who stay in the fire for ever". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 72} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 73}
