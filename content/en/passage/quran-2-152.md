## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "remember Me and I will remember you" al-Tabari says: remember Me, believers, by obeying Me in what I command and forbid, and I will remember you with My mercy and forgiveness, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 211} Some took it as remembrance by praise and commendation; from al-Rabi', God remembers whoever remembers Him, increases whoever thanks Him and punishes whoever disbelieves in Him; from al-Suddi, no servant remembers God but God remembers him, no believer remembers Him but He remembers him with mercy, and no unbeliever but He remembers him with punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 211} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 212}

On "and be grateful to Me and do not disbelieve" he says: be grateful to Me, believers, for the favour I gave you of Islam and guidance to the religion I prescribed for My prophets and chosen ones, and do not deny My kindness so that I take away My favour, for I promised My creation that whoever is grateful I will increase and whoever disbelieves I will deprive. He notes that the Arabs say "I advised for you and thanked for you" and rarely "I advised you", though they sometimes say "I thanked you and advised you", citing verse. He has shown earlier that gratitude is praising a man for his praiseworthy deeds and that disbelief is covering a thing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 212} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 213}
