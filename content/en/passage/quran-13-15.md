**Commentary on verse 15 (pp. 403-405)**

On "to God prostrates whoever is in the heavens and the earth, willingly or unwillingly, and their shadows in the morning and the evening", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: if these who call on idols and images as God's partners refuse to give Him alone obedience and sincere worship, then to God prostrate those in the heavens, the noble angels, and those in the earth who believe in Him, willingly; as for the disbelievers, they prostrate to Him unwillingly when they are forced to prostrate. Report 20298, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: the believer prostrates obediently and the disbeliever unwillingly. Report 20299, from Sufyan, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd and Ibn al-Mubarak: when al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym recited this verse he said, yes, my Lord. Report 20300, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: whoever entered Islam obediently, that is "willingly", and "unwillingly" is whoever entered only by the sword. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 403}

On "and their shadows in the morning and the evening" he says: the shadows of everyone who prostrates, willingly or unwillingly, also prostrate in the mornings and evenings, because every person's shadow shifts back in the evening, as He, mighty be His praise, said "have they not looked at what God has created of anything, its shadows inclining to the right and the left, prostrating to God in humility?" [[passage/quran-16-48|16:48]]. A number of interpreters said the same. Report 20301, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "and their shadows in the morning and evening" means when the shadow of one of them shifts to his right or left. Report 20302, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and Sufyan: the shadow of the believer prostrates willingly and he is willing, and the shadow of the disbeliever prostrates willingly while he is unwilling; the editor explains that the shadow is of God's creation and devout to Him, prostrating willingly, while the owner of the shadow, the disbeliever, is unwilling to prostrate. The report numbered 20304 in the printed text, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: it was mentioned that the shadows of all things prostrate to Him, and he recited "prostrating to God in humility", saying those shadows prostrate to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 404}

"Al-asal" is the plural of "usul", which is the plural of "asil", and "al-asil" is the evening, between the afternoon and sunset; al-Tabari cites in evidence a verse of Abu Dhu'ayb that swears an oath and mentions the evening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 405}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
