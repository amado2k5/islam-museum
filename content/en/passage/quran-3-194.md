## Summary
Verse 194 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 194 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 194: why ask for what was promised? (pp. 483-485).** Al-Tabari raises a question: what is the point of these people asking their Lord to give them what He promised, when they know He fulfils His promise and that breaking it is not possible? He says the people of speculation (the speculative theologians, per the editor's note) differed. First view: the saying took the form of a request but its sense is a statement, the wording meaning for them "so that You give us what You promised through Your messengers"; it is not that they said "if You take us with the abrar, fulfil for us", since they knew God does not break His appointment, and what He promised through His messengers He does not give because of supplication but graciously began it and will then fulfil it. Second view: it is a request and supplication that He make them among those He gave the honour He promised through His messengers, not that they had already deserved a standing of honour and then asked that He not break His promise; they said that had they asked their Lord to give them what He promised the abrar they would have declared themselves pure and testified that they deserve His honour and reward, which is not the description of people of merit. Third view: it is a request and desire that He give them what He promised of victory over their enemies from the unbelievers, triumph over them and the raising of the word of truth over falsehood, and hasten it; they said it is impossible that they lacked certainty that He does not break the appointment, but they had been promised victory with no time fixed for hastening it. The editor's notes on p. 483 weigh the printed text against the manuscript and explain the speculative theologians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 484}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 484-485).** Al-Tabari says the soundest view for him is that this is the description of those of the Prophet's companions who emigrated from their homeland and house, leaving the polytheists for God and His messenger, and of others among his followers, who wished that their help against those who opposed God and them be hastened, and said: give us what You promised us of Your help against them now, for You do not break the appointment, but we have no patience with Your forbearance toward them, so hasten their disgrace and give us victory over them. He takes support from the end of the next verse, which mentions the emigrants, those driven from their homes, those harmed, who fought and were killed. The editor's note says the printed text has a word that the manuscript gives undotted except for a dot over the ha, that the right reading is "their disgrace", and that he added "to them" in brackets from the clue "and to us victory over them". He then answers the other two views: in Arabic it is not found that "do for us thus, Lord" means "so that You do for us thus"; otherwise one could say to another "come to me and talk to me" meaning "so that you talk to me", which is unknown; likewise "give us what You promised" is unknown in the sense of "make us among those You gave it to", though anyone given something exalted becomes the equal of those given the like, and that is not the apparent sense even if the meaning comes to it. The editor's notes say the wording of the printed text and manuscript is jumbled here and meaningless, that his reading is certain because this is a reply to the first view, and that the last remark replies to the second of the three views. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 485}

**Al-Tabari's reading of the sentence (p. 485, report 8366).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: Lord, give us what You promised through Your messengers, that You will raise Your word, the word of truth, by supporting us against whoever disbelieved in You, opposed You and worshipped another, and hasten it for us, for we know that You do not break Your appointment; and do not expose us on the Day of Resurrection by the sins that went before, but expiate them for us and forgive them. Ibn Jurayj (8366), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: he is asking God to fulfil what He promised through His messengers. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 485}
