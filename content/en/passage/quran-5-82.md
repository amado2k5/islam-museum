**Commentary on verse 82: "You will find the strongest in enmity to the believers the Jews and the polytheists", pp. 498-501**

Al-Tabari says: God tells His prophet that he will find the people most hostile to those who believed him and followed him, the people of Islam, are the Jews and the polytheists, the idol-worshippers, and that he will find those nearest in affection to the believers are those who say "we are Christians", because among them are priests and monks and they are not arrogant about accepting and following the truth. The word for affection is a noun of place-form from "I loved something".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 498}

It was said that this verse and the next came down about a group of Christians of Abyssinia who came to the Prophet and, on hearing the Qur'an, became Muslims and followed him; and it was said that it concerned the Negus, king of Abyssinia, and companions who accepted Islam with him. Report 12315, from Said ibn Jubayr, says the Negus sent a delegation to the Prophet, who recited to them and they accepted Islam and the verse came down; they went back to the Negus and told him, and he too accepted Islam, and when he died the Prophet told his Companions to pray for their brother and prayed over him in Medina. Report 12316, from Mujahid, says that they were the delegation who came with Ja'far and his companions from Abyssinia.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 499}

Report 12317, from Ibn Abbas, says that while in Mecca the Prophet feared for his Companions from the polytheists and sent Ja'far ibn Abi Talib, Ibn Mas'ud and Uthman ibn Maz'un with a party to the Negus; the polytheists sent Amr ibn al-As with a party, who told the Negus that a man who had made fools of Quraysh's minds had claimed to be a prophet and had sent a party to corrupt his people. When the Companions came, they greeted him without the royal greeting and said they had greeted him with the greeting of the people of Paradise. He asked what their companion said of Jesus and his mother, and they said that he calls him God's servant, a word from Him cast to Mary and a spirit from Him, and her a pure virgin; the Negus took a twig from the ground and said Jesus and his mother did not exceed what their companion had said by the weight of this twig, and the polytheists disliked this and their faces changed. Then the delegation recited in the presence of priests and monks, who recognised the truth and wept, and "that is because among them are priests and monks" came down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 500}

Report 12318, from al-Suddi, says the Negus sent twelve men from Abyssinia, seven priests and five monks, to look at the Prophet and question him; when he recited what God sent down they wept and believed and verses were sent down about them, and they returned to the Negus, who emigrated with them and died on the road, and the Prophet and the Muslims prayed over him and asked forgiveness for him. Report 12319, from Ata', says they were people of Abyssinia who believed when the emigrant believers came to them. Others said it describes people who were on the law of Jesus as believers and believed in Muhammad when God sent him; report 12320, from Qatada, says that they were people of the Book on a true law brought by Jesus who believed him and, when God sent His prophet, accepted him and knew that what he brought was true.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 501}

Al-Tabari holds that God described a people who said "we are Christians", whom the Prophet would find nearest in affection to those who believe in God and His messenger, without naming them for us; it may be that the companions of the Negus are meant, and it may be a people who followed a law.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 501}

We stop here at the foot of p. 501, in al-Tabari's choice on who the Christians nearest in affection are; the rest of verse 82 follows in the next batch. The pages of commentary end at p. 585, before the indexes beginning at p. 587.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
