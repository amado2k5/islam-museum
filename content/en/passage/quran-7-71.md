## Summary
Verse 71 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 71 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-71|7:71]], al-Tabari says: Hud told his people that punishment and wrath from God had come down on them. Abu Amr ibn al-Ala, as reported, held that "rijz" and "rijs" are one word with the s turned into a z, as in "sitt" (six), which comes from "sidas", and as in "qarbus" and "qarbut". He adduced two lines of rajaz verse: in one the poet curses a clan of Banu Yarbu and writes "nat" for "nas" (people) and "akyat" for "akyas" (shrewd), turning the s into a t; the other is by Ruba. The editor tells the tale of the she-ghoul said to have married Amr ibn Yarbu and notes that the lines are variously transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 521}

Then (14808) from Ibn Abbas that "rijs" means wrath; the editor notes that the printed edition wrote "rijz" and that "rijs" is correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 522}

As for "do you dispute with me about names you and your fathers gave", he says: do you quarrel with me over names you gave to idols that neither harm nor benefit? "God sent down no authority for them" means God gave you no proof for worshipping them and no excuse to plead, since worship belongs only to one who harms and benefits, rewards obedience and punishes disobedience, provides and withholds. Stone, iron and copper have no benefit or harm in them, and nobody who worships them besides God has any argument, because God never allowed it; nor can they be hoped for or feared, now or later. "Wait, I am waiting with you" means: wait for God's judgment between us and you, and the decision of His ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 523}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
