## Summary
Verse 55 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 55 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The time clause and the scholars on the word for taking back.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: and God planned against the people who tried to kill Jesus, despite their disbelief in God and their denial of Jesus in what he brought them, when God told Jesus that He would take him and raise him to Himself, and so took him and raised him; the time word is in his view an attachment to what precedes. The scholars then differ on the meaning of the taking. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 455}

**First view: the taking of sleep.** Some say it is the taking of sleep, so the sense is 'I am putting you to sleep and raising you in your sleep'. Report 7133 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdallah ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) says it is the taking of sleep and God raised him in his sleep; then the report gives al-Hasan saying that the Prophet told the Jews that Jesus has not died and will return to them before the Day of Resurrection. The editor says this is a mursal report, which al-Suyuti recorded in al-Durr al-Manthur (2:36) and attributed to Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim, and Ibn Kathir gave with Ibn Abi Hatim's chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 455}

**Second view: taking up alive.** Others say it means 'I am taking you from the earth and raising you to Me', the word meaning to seize, as one says 'I took in full what was owed me by so-and-so'; the sense is taking him from the earth alive to His neighbourhood and raising him from among the idolaters and those who disbelieved in him, without death. Report 7134 (Ali ibn Sahl, from Damra ibn Rabia, from Ibn Shawdhab, from Matar al-Warraq) says 'taking you from this world, and not by the taking of death'; report 7135 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from al-Hasan) says 'from the earth'; report 7136 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says that His raising him to Himself is His taking him and cleansing him of those who disbelieved. The editor records that Ali ibn Sahl al-Ramli is trustworthy; that Ibn Sad said of Damra ibn Rabia al-Filastini al-Ramli that he was trustworthy, secure and good, and Adam ibn Abi Iyas said he had seen no one with better judgement of what came from his head; that Ibn Shawdhab is Abdallah ibn Shawdhab al-Khurasani, trustworthy; and that Matar al-Warraq is Matar ibn Tahman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 455} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 456}

**The report of Kab al-Ahbar.** Report 7137 (al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from Muawiya ibn Salih) says Kab al-Ahbar said God would not have Jesus die, having sent him as a caller and bearer of good news; when Jesus saw how few followed and how many denied him he complained to God, who revealed that He would take him and raise him, that he who is raised is not raised dead, and that He would send him against the one-eyed Dajjal to kill him, after which he would live twenty-four years and then be given the death of a living man; Kab said this confirms the Prophet's saying about how a community with himself at its start and Jesus at its end could perish. The editor says al-Suyuti recorded it, attributed it to al-Tabari alone and called its chain sound, that the saying of the Prophet in it is mursal, and that whatever the soundness of the chain, the narration of Kab al-Ahbar is, in his view, not to be relied on; he adds Muawiya's saying, recorded by al-Bukhari, that Kab was among the most truthful of those who narrate from the People of the Book though they still tested him for lies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 456} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 457}

**Reports of Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Zayd and al-Hasan.** Report 7138 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) glosses the word as 'taking'. Report 7139 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says 'your taker' and 'your raiser' are one, that Jesus has not yet died until he kills the Dajjal and then dies, and he cites the verse on his speaking to people in the cradle and in maturity, [[passage/quran-3-46|3:46]], saying he was raised before maturity and will descend in maturity. Report 7140 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says God raised him to Himself and he is with Him in heaven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 457}

**Third view: the taking of death.** Others say it means the taking of death. Report 7141 (al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from Muawiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) says 'the one who makes you die'. Report 7142 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from one not suspect, from Wahb ibn Munabbih al-Yamani) says God took Jesus son of Mary for three hours of the day until He raised him. Report 7143 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq) says the Christians claim He took him for seven hours of the day and then revived him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 457} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 458}

**Fourth view: an inverted order.** Others say the sense is 'raiser of you to Me, cleanser of you of those who disbelieved, and taker of you after I send you down to the world', which they take as a case of putting first what is last and last what is first. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 458}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the soundest of these in his view is that of those who say it means taking him from the earth and raising him to God, because of the continuous transmission from the Prophet that Jesus will descend and kill the Dajjal, then remain on earth for a period whose length the reports differ on, then die, and the Muslims will pray over him and bury him. Report 7144 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from al-Zuhri, from Hanzala ibn Ali al-Aslami, from Abu Hurayra) has the Prophet say that God will send Jesus down as a just ruler and fair leader who breaks the cross, kills the swine, lifts the poll tax and makes wealth overflow until none takes it, and who will pass through al-Rawha for pilgrimage or lesser pilgrimage or both. The editor says Salama is Ibn al-Fadl al-Abrash and refers to his authentication, that Hanzala ibn Ali al-Aslami is a well-known trustworthy follower, that Ahmad recorded the hadith in the Musnad (no. 7890 among others), at length and in brief, from Yazid ibn Harun, Sufyan, al-Zuhri and Hanzala and by several routes, and that the reading 'or both' is the one fixed in the manuscript while the printed edition has a senseless wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 458} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 459}

**The rest of the report on the descent of Jesus (report 7145).** Al-Tabari gives it (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from al-Hasan ibn Dinar, from Qatada, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Adam, from Abu Hurayra): the Prophet said that the prophets are half-brothers, their mothers different and their religion one; that he is the nearest of people to Jesus son of Mary because no prophet came between them; and that Jesus will descend, so whoever sees him should know him: a man of middle build, of ruddy and white complexion, with lank hair that seems to drip though no water has touched it, between two lightly dyed garments. He will break the cross, kill the swine, make wealth overflow and fight people over Islam until God destroys all the creeds in his time, and destroys the Dajjal. Security will settle on the earth until lions graze with camels, leopards with cattle and wolves with sheep, and children play with snakes without harm. He will stay forty years, then die, and the Muslims will pray over him and bury him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 459}

**The editor's notes on the report.** Shakir grades the chain "very weak", because it includes al-Hasan ibn Dinar al-Basri, whom he calls a liar not to be relied on; he says Abd al-Rahman ibn Adam is a trustworthy Successor whom Muslim cites. He states that the base of the report is sound and will come by another sound chain through Said ibn Abi Arouba from Qatada, and he cites Ahmad's Musnad (no. 9259, through Affan and Hammam from Qatada), al-Hakim with al-Dhahabi's agreement, and Ibn Kathir's collection of its routes. He judges the phrase "he is my successor over my community" in this version to be anomalous, found only with one unreliable narrator. He glosses "half-brothers" with Ibn al-Athir (children of different mothers and one father, meaning one faith and differing laws) and explains the descriptive terms. He adds that Muslims do not differ over the descent of Jesus at the end of time, because sound reports of it are continuously transmitted, that it is known from the religion by necessity, and that one who denies it is not counted a believer; this is the editor's position. He also notes that the manuscript reading in the preceding report is correct and the printed one is wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 459} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 460}

**Al-Tabari's argument.** Al-Tabari says it is known that had God made him die, He would not make him die a second death, joining two deaths, since God told His servants that He creates them, makes them die and then gives them life, as in [[passage/quran-30-40|30:40]]. So the sense, to him, is that God told Jesus He is taking him from the earth, raising him to Himself and cleansing him of those who denied his prophethood. He adds that although this is cast as a statement, it contains God's argument against the Najran delegation who disputed with the Prophet about Jesus: that Jesus was neither killed nor crucified as the Christians and the Jews who conceded it claim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 460} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 461}

**Reports 7146 to 7148: cleansing from the deniers.** Report 7146 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says the Prophet told the delegation, refuting what they had granted the Jews about the crucifixion, how God raised and cleansed him. Al-Tabari says the cleansing means purifying him and freeing him from those who disbelieved in him and denied the truth he brought, among the Jews and other communities. Report 7147 (same chain) says he was cleansed when they intended against him what they intended. Report 7148 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says he was cleansed from Jews, Christians, Magians and the disbelievers of his own people. The editor notes that the printed text altered a phrase in 7146, that his reading is the manuscript's and also that of Ibn Hisham (Sira 2:231), and that 7146 and 7147 continue the reports ending with 7130. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 461} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 462}

**The followers placed above the deniers.** Al-Tabari says God will place those who followed Jesus' way and the religion and natural disposition of Islam above all communities who denied his prophethood and opposed the course of his followers, so that they end up prevailing over them. The editor adds a bracketed word to restore a sentence garbled in both manuscript and print. The reports: 7149 (Bishr ibn Muadh, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says they are the people of Islam, who followed his natural disposition, creed and practice, and remain dominant over their opponents until the Hour; 7150 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is similar; 7151 and 7152 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) say God is the helper of those who followed him in Islam against the deniers; 7153 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says the followers are the believers, and it is also said that they are the Romans; 7154 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says the Muslims are placed above those who abandoned Islam. The editor says the printed text replaced al-Suddi's wording with a denial that they are the Romans, whereas the manuscript has the Romans, who were then the Christians, and that "believers" means Muslims who did not alter their faith nor say of Jesus what the Christians later said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 462} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 463}

**Another view: Christians above Jews.** Al-Tabari notes others who say the meaning is that those of the Christians who followed him are set above the Jews. Report 7155 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says the deniers are the disbelievers among the Israelites, the followers are the Israelites and others who believed in him, and the Christians are above the Jews: in no land with Christians in it, east or west, are they not above the Jews, who are humbled everywhere. This is Ibn Zayd's statement as al-Tabari relates it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 463}

**The close of the verse and the shift of address.** Al-Tabari says return to God means the destination of those who differed about Jesus, when He will judge among them truthfully on his matter. He sees a turn from speaking of an absent party to addressing them, since the report concerned Jesus' followers and those who denied him; the sense, to him, is that both groups return to God and He judges between them, but the speech reverts to address because of the preceding quotation. He compares the shift in [[passage/quran-10-22|10:22]]. The editor corrects a misprint in the word for "preceding", notes that al-Tabari uses it often, refers back to al-Tabari's account in volume 1 of the Arab practice in quoted speech, and explains that the closing words concern those who differed about Jesus (Jews, Christians and others) as well as those who said the truth, that he is God's servant and messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 464}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
