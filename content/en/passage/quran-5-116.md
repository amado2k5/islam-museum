## Summary
Verse 116 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 116 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says: "on the day God gathers the messengers and asks what answer they were given", "when God said: Jesus son of Mary", in [[passage/quran-5-116|5:116]]. It was said that God said this to Jesus when he raised him to himself in the world; report 13028, from al-Suddi: when God raised Jesus to himself the Christians said what they said and claimed Jesus had told them to; God asked him about his words and he said "glory to you, it is not for me to say what I have no right to" to "you are witness over all things". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 234}

Others said: this is God's report that he will say it to Jesus on the day of resurrection. Report 13029, from Ibn Jurayj: the people hear it, and he answers as you have seen and acknowledges servanthood for himself, so whoever said of Jesus what he said knows that he was speaking falsehood. Report 13030, from Maysara: God said, Jesus, did you say that to the people? and his joints shook, fearing he might have said it, and he said glory to you, if I said it you knew it. Report 13031, from Qatada: when will that be? On the day of resurrection; do you not see that he says, in [[passage/quran-5-119|5:119]], "this is a day when the truthful profit from their truth"? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 234}

On this interpretation of Ibn Jurayj, "wa idh" must mean "wa idha", as in [[passage/quran-34-51|34:51]], "if you could see when they are terrified" meaning when they are terrified, and as Abu al-Najm says in a line that prays for God's reward of gardens of Eden in the high chambers, meaning when he rewards, and as al-Aswad ibn Ya'fur says in a line about women who, when the old man jested with them, say that he has not gone his way, meaning when he jested. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 235}

The editor traces Abu al-Najm's line to Ibn al-Anbari's al-Addad, al-Sahibi and Lisan al-Arab, explains "'alali" as plural of "'illiyya", a high chamber, which he says means "'illiyyin"; and says al-Aswad ibn Ya'fur al-Nahshali was the Nahshal tribe's A'sha, and that the line, in the Diwan of the two A'shas and in al-Addad, comes from a poem most of which is lost except this line and five more.

Those who follow Ibn Jurayj seem to take the verse to mean: whoever disbelieves after this among you, I shall punish him with a punishment I inflict on none in the worlds in this world, and I shall punish him also in the next, "when God said: Jesus son of Mary". Al-Tabari holds the sounder of the two views to be al-Suddi's, that God said it to Jesus when he raised him to himself and that the report is of what has passed, for two reasons. One is that "idh" in most known Arabic usage goes with the past tense, though it sometimes comes where a report of what will happen is meant when hearers know its sense, which is neither common nor eloquent, and taking God's words in the better-known sense where a way is found is better than the less known and stranger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 236}

The other is that neither Jesus nor any prophet doubted that God does not forgive one who dies a polytheist, so how could it be imagined of Jesus that he would say in the next world, answering his Lord: if you punish whoever took me and my mother as gods besides you, they are your servants, and if you forgive them, you are the mighty, the wise. If someone asks what was the point of God's asking Jesus "did you say this to the people" when he knows Jesus did not, the answer is that it allows two explanations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 236}

The editor refers for "idh" and "idha" to earlier volumes 1, 3, 6, 7 and 9 and to p. 317 further on.

One is to warn Jesus against saying it and forbid him, as one says to another "did you do such and such?" when the other knows the speaker thinks the thing grave, by way of forbidding the act and threatening him over it. The other is to let him know that his people he had left had broken his covenant and changed their religion after him, so that it joins informing him of their state after him and warning him against saying it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 237}

As to the sense of the words: "take me and my mother as two gods", that is, as two objects of worship besides God. Jesus said, glorifying you, Lord, and holding it too great for me to do or speak it: "it is not for me to say what I have no right to", that is, I have no right to say it, being a created servant and my mother your handmaid; how could a servant and a handmaid claim lordship? "If I said it you knew it" means nothing is hidden from you and you know I did not say it and did not tell them to. Al-Tabari then begins on "you know what is in my soul and I do not know what is in yours, you are the knower of the unseen": God reports of his prophet Jesus that he clears himself before him of what the unbelieving Christians said of him and his mother, that he called them to it or ordered it, so he said "glory to you, it is not for me to say what I have no right to; if I... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 237}

The editor prefers from the manuscript "and a warning to him of saying it", refers for "glory" to earlier volumes, and corrects the reading "how can a servant and handmaid" from the manuscript.

Then he said "you know what is in my soul", that is, Lord, nothing is hidden from you of what my soul concealed and I did not utter or show with my limbs, so how much less what I have uttered and shown? If I had said that to the people, you would have known it, for you know the hidden thoughts of souls that are not uttered, let alone what has been uttered; "and I do not know what is in yours" means I do not know what you have kept from me and not told me, for I know of things only what you have taught me; "you are the knower of the unseen" means the knower of hidden matters that none but you is told of. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 238}

The editor refers for "knower of the unseen" to p. 211 and for "the unseen" to volumes 1, 10 and 6.

Verse 116 closes here at p. 238, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-5-117|5:117]].
