## Summary
Verse 71 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 71 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: shall we call, apart from God, on what neither benefits nor harms us, and be turned back on our heels after God has guided us, like one whom the devils lured in the land, bewildered, who has companions calling him to guidance, 'come to us'?" al-Tabari says: this is God's alerting his prophet to his argument against the polytheists of his people who worship idols. He says to him: say, Muhammad, to those who hold idols and rivals equal to their Lord and tell you to follow their religion and worship the images with them: shall we call, apart from God, on a stone or wood that cannot benefit or harm us, singling it out for worship apart from God and leaving the worship of him in whose hand are harm and benefit, life and death, if you have sense and distinguish good from evil? You surely know that serving what is hoped for in benefit and feared for harm is worthier than serving what is not hoped for nor feared. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 450}

"And be turned back on our heels" says: be turned back on our backs, going backward without gaining what we sought; al-Tabari has explained the sense of "turned back on the heels" and that the Arabs say of anyone seeking a need he did not gain that he was turned back on his heels; and what is meant here is: being turned from Islam to unbelief "after God guided us" and gave us success, so that we are like the man whom the devil drew along, falling bewildered through the land. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 450}

The editor refers for "turned back on the heels" to an earlier passage and to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an.

"Istahwathu" is the "istaf'ala" form of "hawa fulan ila kadha yahwi", and from it comes [[passage/quran-14-37|14:37]], "make the hearts of some people incline to them", meaning draw towards them and want them. "Hayran" is the "fa'lan" form of "hara fulan fi al-tariq", he is bewildered in it, in "hayra", "hayaran" and "hayrura", when he goes astray and is not guided to the path. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 451}

"Who has companions calling him to guidance" says: this bewildered one whom the devils lured in the land has companions on the path and the straight way who call him to the road of guidance they are on, saying to him come to us. "Hayran" is left uninflected because it is a "fa'lan" form, and every noun on "fa'lan" whose feminine is "fa'la" is not declined in Arab speech, definite or indefinite. This is a parable God gave for one who disbelieved in God after believing and followed the devils among the polytheists, and his companions who were his companions when he was a Muslim, who remain in the true religion, call him to the guidance they hold while he has left it, saying come to us and be with us on a straight guided path, and he refuses and follows the devil's callers and worships gods and idols. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 451}

The editor says "hayrura" is a verbal noun like "sayrura" that the dictionaries do not give, so it is something learned from Abu Jafar and added to the dictionaries.

A group of interpreters said the like of this and another group differed. 13422, from al-Suddi: the polytheists said to the believers, follow our way and leave the religion of Muhammad, and God said "say: shall we call, apart from God, on what neither benefits nor harms us", these gods, "and be turned back on our heels after God has guided us" so that we are like the one whom the devils lured in the land; he says: your likeness if you disbelieve after believing is that of a man who was with people on the road and lost it, the devils bewildered him and lured him, while his companions were on the road and called him to them saying come to us, we are on the road, and he refused to come; that is the likeness of the one who would follow you after knowing Muhammad, Muhammad who calls to the road, the road being Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 452}

13423, from Ibn Abbas: this is a parable God gave for the gods and those who call to them, and for the callers who call to God: like a man who lost the road, wandering astray, when a caller called him "so-and-so son of so-and-so, come to the road", while he has companions calling him "so-and-so, come to the road"; if he follows the first caller he goes off with him until he throws him into destruction, and if he answers the one who calls him to guidance he is guided to the road. This caller calling in the wilds is one of the ghouls. He says: the likeness of one who worships these gods apart from God is that he thinks he is on something until death comes to him and he faces destruction and regret. The words "whom the devils lured in the land" are the ghouls, who call him by his name, his father's name and his grandfather's, and he follows them and thinks he is on something, and in the morning they have thrown him into destruction, perhaps eaten him, or thrown him into a wasteland where he dies of thirst; that is the likeness of one who answers the gods worshipped apart from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 453}

The editor says "wandering astray" dropped out of the printed text and is in the manuscript, and that the right reading is "that is the likeness".

13424, from Qatada: they led him astray in the land, bewildered; 13425, from Mujahid: "what neither benefits nor harms us" is the idols; 13426, from Mujahid: a bewildered man whom his companions call to the road, the likeness of one who goes astray after being guided; 13427, from Mujahid: this is a parable God gave for the unbeliever, bewildered, whom the Muslim calls to guidance and who does not answer; 13428, from Qatada: God taught it to Muhammad and his companions to argue with the people of error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 453}

Others interpreted it differently: 13429, from Ibn Abbas: he is the man who does not respond to God's guidance, a man who obeyed Satan, did wrong in the land, strayed from the truth and went astray, and he has companions who call him to guidance and claim that what they bid him is guidance; God says this to their friends among men: God's guidance is the guidance, and error is what the jinn call to. Ibn Abbas on this report seems to hold that the companions of this bewildered man call him to error and claim it is guidance, and that God gave them the lie by "say: God's guidance is the guidance". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 454}

Al-Tabari holds this an interpretation with a case, were it not that God called what the bewildered one's companions call him to "guidance", the report being of the companions who call him to what they call him to, so that they were the ones who named it; but God named it guidance and reported that the bewildered one's companions call him to it, and it is not allowed that God call error guidance, since that would be a lie and it is not allowed to describe God as lying. This could be directed to a right sense only if it were God's report of the callers that they said to him come to guidance; but as he says "calling him to guidance" it cannot be that they were calling him to error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 454}

As for "come to us", it means they say come to us, come here, the saying being dropped as the speech shows it. It is reported that Ibn Mas'ud read "calling him to guidance, clear"; 13430, from Abu Ishaq, on Abdullah's reading; 13431, from Mujahid, on Ibn Mas'ud's reading, that the "guidance" is the road and that it is clear. Read so, "clear" is an attribute of "guidance" and its accusative is by "qat'" from guidance, as if it were said "calling him to the clear guidance", then "clear" is put in the accusative when the article is dropped and it becomes indefinite describing a definite. This reading supports those who say guidance here is guidance in truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 455}

The editor refers for "qat'" to the indexes of terms, and says this is a plain statement that "qat'" is an indefinite that has become an attribute of a definite.

On "say: God's guidance is the guidance, and we are commanded to submit to the Lord of the worlds" he says: say, Muhammad, to those who hold idols equal to their Lord and say to your companions follow our way and we shall bear your sins, for we are on guidance: it is not as you claim; "God's guidance is the guidance" says: the road of God that he showed us and made plain, his way that he told us to keep to, and his religion that he laid down for us and made clear, is the guidance and the straightness about which there is no doubt, not the worship of idols and images {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 455}

We stop here at the foot of p. 455, in the middle of al-Tabari's sentence on "say: God's guidance is the guidance", and he continues on p. 456.
