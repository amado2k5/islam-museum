## Summary
Verse 17 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum

Then, on "Then I shall come at them from before them and from behind them, from their right and from their left, and You will not find most of them thankful", he says the commentators differ. Some said "before them" means from the side of the Hereafter, "behind them" from the side of the world, "from their right" from the side of the truth and "from their left" from the side of falsehood; he gives (14369) from Ibn Abbas that it means I shall make them doubt their Hereafter, make them desire their world, confuse the matter of their religion for them and make sins attractive to them. A contrary interpretation is also reported from Ibn Abbas by the same chain, which is (14370) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 338}

that "before them" is from the world, "behind them" from the Hereafter, "from their right" from the side of their good deeds and "from their left" from the side of their evil deeds; and the other report confirms it, (14371) from Ibn Abbas, that before them is from their side, behind them is the matter of their Hereafter, from their right is from their good deeds and from their left is from their evil deeds; and (14372) from Qatada that he came at them from before them and told them there is no resurrection, no Paradise and no Fire, from behind them from the matter of the world, which he adorned for them and called them to, from their right from their good deeds, which he made them slow to do, and from their left he adorned sins and disobedience and called them to them and ordered them to them; then he said, he came at you, son of Adam, from every direction except above you, for he could not come between you and God's mercy. Others said rather that "before them" is from the side of their world and "behind them" from the side of their Hereafter, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 339}

and he gives (14373) from Ibrahim and (14374) and (14375) from al-Hakam that "before them" is from their world, "behind them" from their Hereafter, "from their right" from their good deeds and "from their left" from their evil deeds, with 14375 adding that he adorns the world for them, makes them slow toward the Hereafter, turns them away from the truth and makes falsehood attractive to them; (14376) from al-Suddi that "before them" is the world, to which he calls them and for which he makes them eager, "behind them" the Hereafter, about which he makes them doubt and which he makes distant to them, "from their right" the truth, about which he makes them doubt, and "from their left" falsehood, which he lightens for them and makes them desire; and (14377) from Ibn Jurayj the like. Others said the meaning is from where they see and from where they do not see, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 340}

and he gives (14378) and (14379) from Mujahid that "before them and from their right" is where they see, and "behind them and from their left" where they do not see; (14380) that Mujahid said it is as he said, he comes at them from before them, behind them, their right and their left, with Ibn Humayd adding that he said he comes at them from there; and (14381) the like. Al-Tabari says the soundest of these views to him is that of those who said the meaning is: then I shall come at them from all aspects of the truth and of falsehood, to turn them away from the truth and make falsehood fair to them. That is because it follows His words "I will sit in wait for them on Your straight path", which report that he will sit for the children of Adam on the road God commanded them to take, the true religion of God, and so he comes at them in it from all its aspects: from the aspect God commanded them, to turn them from it, which is "from before them and from their right", and from the aspect God forbade them, adorning it for them and calling them to it, which is "from behind them and from their left". He did not say "from above them", because God's mercy descends on His servants from above them, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 341}

and he gives (14382) from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, that He did not say "from above them" because mercy descends from above them. As for "and You will not find most of them thankful", he says: and You will not find, Lord, most of the children of Adam thankful to You for Your blessing that You bestowed on them, like Your honouring their father Adam with the honour You gave him by making Your angels prostrate to him and preferring him; and their thanks to Him is their obedience to Him by acknowledging His oneness and following His command and prohibition. Ibn Abbas used to say of this, (14383), that it means monotheists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 342}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
