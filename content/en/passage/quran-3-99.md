## Summary
Verse 99 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 99 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The address and the meaning of turning away (p. 53).** Al-Tabari says the address here is to the Jews of the Children of Israel and others who profess belief in God's scripture, asking why they turn people away from God's path and highway, which He laid down for His prophets, His friends and the people of faith, that is, whoever believes in God, His messenger and what came from Him. He explains that the feminine pronoun returns to "the path", a feminine word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 53}

**The meaning of "seeking" it (p. 53).** Al-Tabari glosses the verb as seeking crookedness for it, and cites a line of Suhaym, the slave of the Banu al-Hashas, for the sense of seeking (the editor refers to where its source was traced earlier in the work). He then distinguishes two forms of the verb: with a joining alif it means seek something for me, with a cutting alif it means help me seek it, and he gives similar cases such as milking. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 53}

**The meaning of crookedness (p. 54).** For al-Tabari crookedness is bending and inclining, here going astray from guidance: they seek for God's religion a deviation from its ways and its straightness. The wording passes through "the path" while the meaning is its people, those on the way of truth. He adds that the form with the vowel i belongs to religion and speech, and the form with the vowel a to walls, spears and anything standing upright; the editor cites Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 54}

**Witnesses and God's awareness (p. 54).** Al-Tabari explains that they are witnesses that what they turn people from is true, which they know and find in their books. God is not heedless of what they do of what He does not approve for His servants and other deeds, until He hastens punishment for them or delays it until they meet Him and He repays them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 54}

**The reported occasion (p. 54).** Al-Tabari notes it was said that these verses and those after them up to [[passage/quran-3-105|3:105]] were revealed about a Jewish man who tried to stir up the two clans of the Aws and the Khazraj after Islam so that they would return to the enmity and hatred of the pre-Islamic period; God rebuked him, condemned his act, and also admonished the Companions, forbidding division and dispute and commanding unity and harmony. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 54}

**The report of Shas ibn Qays (7524, pp. 55-56).** Al-Tabari relates from Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from "the trustworthy one", from Zayd ibn Aslam, that Shas ibn Qays, an old man advanced in years, deep in disbelief, bitter in grudge and envy toward the Muslims, passed a group of the Aws and Khazraj talking in a gathering. He was angered by their togetherness and the mending of their relations after the enmity of the pre-Islamic period, and said the notables of the Banu Qayla gathering in this land left no settled place for them. The editor explains that the notables are the chiefs and nobles, and that Banu Qayla are the Ansar, named for an ancient mother, Qayla bint Kahil. He told a young Jewish man with him to sit among them, remind them of the day of Bu'ath, a day on which the Aws and Khazraj fought and the Aws prevailed, and recite some of what was said in verse about it. He did so, the people quarrelled and boasted until two men leapt up on their knees: Aws ibn Qayzi of the Banu Haritha of the Aws and Jabbar ibn Sakhr of the Banu Salima of the Khazraj. One said to the other that if they wished they would return it now fresh as a young animal (the editor: meaning the war renewed as it began). Both sides grew angry, called for arms and agreed to meet at the Zahira, that is the lava field; they went out to it and each side drew together on its pre-Islamic claims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 55} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 56}

The news reached the Prophet, who went out to them with those of the Emigrants who were with him and reminded them of God's favour in guiding them to Islam and joining their hearts, ending the matter of the pre-Islamic period. The people recognised a prompting of Satan and the plot of their enemy, threw down their weapons, wept and embraced, and went back with the Prophet hearing and obeying. In the report God revealed about Shas ibn Qays and what he did this verse and the one before it, and about Aws ibn Qayzi, Jabbar ibn Sakhr and those with them [[passage/quran-3-100|3:100]] and what follows up to [[passage/quran-3-105|3:105]]. The editor says the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira, that it is the remainder of earlier reports on the episode (he points to 7333-7334), that al-Tabari's text is fuller than Ibn Hisham's in places, and that the copyist dropped a word at the start of the two verses by oversight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 56}

**Second view: the Jews and Christians hiding the Prophet's description (7525-7528, p. 57).** It was also said that the Jews of the Children of Israel in Medina at the time of revelation, and the Christians, are meant, and that their turning away consisted in telling anyone who asked whether they found the Prophet mentioned in their books that they did not find his description. Al-Suddi (7525, via Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat) said that when asked whether they found Muhammad, they said no, and so turned people away. Qatada (7526, via Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid, Sa'id) said they bear witness in what they read of God's book that Muhammad is God's messenger and that Islam is the religion God accepts from no one else, which they find written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. Al-Rabi gave the like (7527, via al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, his father). Al-Hasan (7528, via Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr, Abbad) said they are the Jews and Christians, forbidden to turn Muslims aside while wishing to lead people to error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 57}

**Al-Tabari's comment (pp. 57-58).** Al-Tabari says that on al-Suddi's view the Jews keep people from Muhammad and from the believers' following him by hiding his description which they find in their books, so "the path" would be Muhammad and the sense that they seek his destruction. The other reports and views accord with what he explained, that the path is Islam and what Muhammad brought of truth from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 57} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 58}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 58}
