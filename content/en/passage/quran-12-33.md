**Commentary on verse 33 (p. 87)**

On "he said: my Lord, prison is dearer to me than what they call me to" al-Tabari says this report from God shows the Aziz's wife returned to soliciting Joseph and threatened him with prison unless he did what she asked, and he chose prison over it; had she not returned and threatened him, it would be impossible for him to say it, since he was not being called to anything nor threatened with jail. "Al-sijn" is the jailing itself, the house of jail, and the readers of all the cities read it with a kasra on the sin. The Arabs put nouns of place derived from verbs in the place of the verbs, saying "the sun rose a rising and set a setting". The editor explains that by "verbs" the author means verbal nouns. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 87}

They take them, though nouns, as replacements for verbal nouns; so too "sijn", and when the sin takes a fat-ha it is a true verbal noun. It is reported of some early reader that he read it with a fat-ha; al-Tabari says he does not allow reciting so, since the authoritative readers agree against it. The meaning: Joseph said, my Lord, confinement in prison is dearer to me than what they call me to of disobeying You and the indecency they solicit me for. Report 19246, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: from adultery. Report 19247, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Joseph said, having turned to his Lord and begged His help in what had befallen him, that prison was dearer to him than doing what God dislikes. The editor says the right word in the note is "adaf", meaning he feared and was anxious and so took refuge in his Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 88}

On "unless You turn their scheming away from me, I will incline to them" he says: if You do not ward off from me, Lord, what they do to me in soliciting me, I will lean toward them and follow them in what they want from me, as one says "so-and-so inclined to such-and-such". The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "turning away" and "scheming", and says the verse al-Tabari cites is by Yazid ibn Dabba al-Thaqafi, the opening of a love poem to Hind found in al-Aghani and Majaz al-Qur'an. Reports 19248 to 19250: from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, "I follow them"; from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama, what I fear from them; from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb, if help and protection are not from You they will not be from me or with me. On "and be among the ignorant" he says: through my inclination to them I would be among those who ignored Your right and went against Your command and prohibition; report 19251, from Ibn Ishaq: ignorant when I commit disobedience to You. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 89}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
