**Commentary on verse 1 (pp. 225-228)**

On [[passage/quran-11-1|11:1]] al-Tabari says he has already set out the interpreters' differences on 'Alif Lam Ra' and what is right, which spares repeating it here. His words 'a Book whose verses are made firm' mean this Book God sent down on His prophet Muhammad, the Qur'an; 'Book' is nominative with the sense 'this is a Book', while for those who claim 'Alif Lam Ra' stands for all the letters, the Book is nominative through it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 225}

The interpreters differed on 'made firm, then set out in detail'. Some said its verses were made firm by command and prohibition and then set out by reward and punishment. Reports 17915, 17916 and 17917, from al-Hasan, say so; the editor says he could not identify Abu Muhammad al-Thaqafi in the first, and that Abd al-Karim b. Muhammad al-Jurjani in the second, judge of Jurjan, died about 170 so that it is doubtful Ibn Humayd met him. The opposite is reported of al-Hasan in report 17918: made firm by reward and punishment, set out by command and prohibition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 226}

Others said: its verses were made firm against falsehood and then set out, with the lawful and the forbidden made plain. Report 17919, from Qatada: God made them firm against falsehood and set them out by His knowledge, making plain what is lawful and forbidden, obedience and disobedience; report 17920 from him: made firm against falsehood, then set out, made clear. Al-Tabari says the sounder of the two views is that God made His verses firm against flaw, defect and falsehood and then set them out by command and prohibition, since making a thing firm is putting it right and securing it, and making the Qur'an's verses firm is securing them against any flaw or falsehood by which the crooked-hearted might find fault; setting out is distinguishing them one from another by explaining the lawful and forbidden, command and prohibition in them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 227}

Some commentators explained 'set out' as 'explained', close to what al-Tabari said. Reports 17921, 17922, 17923 and 17924, from Mujahid: explained; two further reports, 17925 and 17926, from him say likewise. Qatada said: made clear. As for 'from One wise and aware', it means wise in the ordering and measuring of things and aware of what their outcomes become; report 17927, from Qatada: from One wise and aware. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 228}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
