## Summary
Verse 185 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 185 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 452).** Al-Tabari says the destination of these Jews who fabricated against God and belied His messenger, and of all others of His creatures, and the return of all of them, is to Him, because He has decreed death on everyone. So He told His Prophet not to be grieved by the belying of those who belied him, since messengers were belied before him who came with signs and proofs like his own, and he has an example in them; the destination of those who belied him and of others is to God, who will pay every soul the repayment of its work on the Day of Resurrection. By the paying of wages he understands the wages of deeds, good for good and evil for evil. The one who is drawn away from the Fire and kept far from it has been saved and obtained his need; one says "so-and-so won his aim" when he attains it. The note refers to the explanation of the verb of being removed in volume 2 (p. 375). He then says the pleasures, lusts, adornments and ornaments of this world are an enjoyment that deception gives, a fading deceit with no reality when tested and no soundness when tried; people take pleasure in what deception gives them, then it turns on them with calamities, misfortunes and things they hate, so they should not rely on the world, for they are in it in deception, enjoying it, and will depart from it after a little. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 453}

**Ibn Sabit's report and al-Tabari's reply (p. 453, report 8314).** 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Sabit (8314), through al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Jarir from al-A'mash from Bukayr ibn al-Akhnas: it is like the shepherd's provision, a handful of dates, or a little flour, or a little something to drink milk over. Al-Tabari takes Ibn Sabit to have understood the world as a small provision that does not carry its owner to his destination nor suffice for his journey, and says this is one possible interpretation, but the sound one in his view is what he said himself, because the word for the deception means deceit in Arabic, so there is no reason to turn it to smallness, for a thing may be small without its owner being in deception, whereas for one who is in deception neither little nor much of what he is in is sound for him. He says the word with a damma on its first letter is a verbal noun from "so-and-so deceived me", and with a fatha it is an attribute of the deceiving Satan who deceives the son of Adam until he brings him into disobedience by which he deserves punishment. The note refers to the explanation of enjoyment in volume 3 and in volume 1 (pp. 539-540). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 453}

**Report 8315 (pp. 453-454).** Abu Hurayra (8315), through Abu Kurayb from 'Abda and 'Abd al-Rahim from Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu Salama: the Prophet said that a whip's place in Paradise is better than the world and all in it, and told them to recite, if they wished, what is in [[passage/quran-3-185|3:185]]. The notes say 'Abda is Ibn Sulayman al-Kilabi of Kufa and 'Abd al-Rahim is Ibn Sulayman al-Marwazi al-Ashall. The report is in Ahmad's Musnad (no. 9649) from Yahya ibn Sa'id from Muhammad ibn 'Amr with this chain; in al-Tirmidhi (4: 85) from 'Abd ibn Humayd and others, who called it good and sound; in al-Hakim's Mustadrak (2: 299) via Shuja' ibn al-Walid, who said it met Muslim's condition though they did not extract it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; and Ibn Kathir (2: 311) gives it from Ibn Abi Hatim from his father from Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah al-Ansari from Muhammad ibn 'Amr, then says it is established in the two Sahihs by another route without this addition (the mention of the verse), while Ibn Hibban in his Sahih and al-Hakim in his Mustadrak gave it with it. Al-Suyuti (2: 107) adds Ibn Abi Shayba, Hannad and 'Abd ibn Humayd, and al-Mundhiri in al-Targhib wa-l-Tarhib (4: 277) gives it from al-Tirmidhi's version in the sense of the versions of Ahmad, al-Bukhari, al-Tabarani in al-Awsat (with a chain of narrators of the sound collections) and Ibn Hibban. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 454}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 454}
