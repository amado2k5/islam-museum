## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "but they will never wish for it, because of what their hands have sent ahead; and God knows the wrongdoers", al-Tabari says this reports their dislike of death and refusal of the challenge, since they knew the threat would fall upon them, and knew Muhammad as a messenger though they denied him, so they feared God's punishment for what their hands had earned. He reports from Ibn Abbas (through Ibn Ishaq) that they refused to pray for death on the falser of the two parties, "because of what they know of what they have of knowledge of you and their disbelief in it"; from al-Dahhak, that were they truthful they would have wished for it; and from Ibn Jurayj, that the Jews were the most averse to death. "What their hands have sent ahead" means what they have put forward, a figure the Arabs use, ascribing the offence to the hand even when another limb committed it, since most of people's offences are done by hands; here it means their disbelief and disobeying God's command to follow Muhammad though they found him written in the Torah, and what their hearts held of envy, transgression and denial; Ibn Jurayj reports that they knew he was a prophet and concealed it. "God knows the wrongdoers" means He knows the wrongdoing of the children of Adam, Jews, Christians and the other communities alike, and the wrong of the Jews was disbelieving by disobeying His command to follow Muhammad after having sought victory through him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 367} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 368} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 369}
