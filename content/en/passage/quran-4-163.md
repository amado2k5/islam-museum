**Commentary on verse 163: the revelation to Muhammad as to Noah and the prophets (pp. 399-405)**

Al-Tabari says 'We have revealed to you as We revealed to Noah and the prophets after him' means: We have sent you, Muhammad, with prophethood as We sent Noah and all the prophets, those named to you and those not named. Report 10839, from al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym: he was revealed to as all the prophets before him were revealed to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 400}

The verse is said to have been revealed when God exposed the Jews by the signs in their request for a book from heaven: the Prophet recited it to them, and they said: God has sent nothing to a human after Moses. Report 10840, from Ibn Abbas: Sakkin and Adi ibn Zayd said: Muhammad, we know that God has sent nothing to a human since Moses; so God revealed that. The editor notes that 'Adi ibn Zayd appears in the printed and manuscript texts as 'Adi ibn Thabit, which is an error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 400}

Others say that when the earlier verses about them were revealed they said: God sent nothing to a human, nor to Moses or Jesus; so God revealed: they did not measure God with the measure due. Report 10841, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: when the verses were recited and he informed them of their deeds, they denied all that God sent down, and one of them loosened his seat and said: nor to anyone! So God revealed the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 401}

On 'We gave David the Psalms', the readers differ: most readers of the cities read the z with a fatha, as a singular name of the book David was given; some Kufan readers read it with a damma, as plural of zabr, meaning written scrolls. Al-Tabari prefers the fatha, as the name of the book David was given, as the Torah is the name of Moses' book, the Gospel of Jesus' book, and the Criterion of Muhammad's book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 402}

On 'and messengers We have related to you before', al-Tabari says the accusative of 'messengers' comes because the nouns before it, though in the genitive, have the sense of the accusative, so the messengers are coordinated on the sense of the nouns before them. It may also be accusative as attached to the verb: and We have related to you messengers before. Report on Ubayy's reading gives the nominative, with a returning pronoun in 'We related them to you'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 403}

On 'God spoke to Moses with speech', al-Tabari says it means God addressed Moses with His speech. Report 10842, from Nuh ibn Abi Maryam: asked how God spoke to Moses, he said: face to face. Report 10843, from Ka'b through Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Rahman and Juzay ibn Jabir: when God spoke to Moses He spoke in all languages before His speech; Moses said: Lord, I do not understand until He speaks to me in the last of the tongues; God said: is this how Your speech is? No, and if you heard My speech it would be nothing. Moses asked: is there anything in Your creation like Your speech? God said: none; the nearest of My creatures in resemblance to My speech is the most intense thunderclap people hear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 404}

Report 10844, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: Moses asked: what have you likened your Lord's speech to among what He created? He said: the pouring thunder. The editor notes that the printed and manuscript texts read 'the still thunder' and the correct word is 'the pouring', meaning the continuous and flowing sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 405}

Report 10845, from Juzay ibn Jabir al-Khath'ami through Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri, from Ka'b: Moses said, by God, Lord, I do not understand this, until He spoke to him in the last of the tongues with a voice like His; and the nearest of His creatures in resemblance to His speech is the most intense thunderclap people hear. Reports 10846 and 10847 give the same chain in nearly the same words. The editor notes that the printed text has 'the rabbis' instead of 'Ka'b al-Ahbar', and that reports of this kind may not be taken for God's attributes; the matter of those is God's alone, as His Messenger conveyed it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 406}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
