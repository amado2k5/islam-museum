## Summary
Verse 44 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 44 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of the seeing formula at the start of the verse (pp. 426-427, notes 1-5).** The interpreters differed: some said it means "have you not been told?", others "have you not known?". Al-Tabari says the correct sense is: have you not seen with your heart, Muhammad, knowledge of those who were handed a portion of scripture, because report and knowledge do not bring seeing with the eye but sight of the heart through knowledge. The giving of a share means they were given a portion of God's book and knew it. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the formula and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and corrects two places in the manuscript and print. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 427}

**Who is meant (pp. 427-428, reports 9687-9690, notes 1-4).** It was mentioned that God meant a group of Jews around the Prophet's place of emigration. Bishr b. Mu'adh related from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada that they are the Jews, whom he calls God's enemies, who chose misguidance (9687); al-Qasim from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ikrima that the passage was revealed about Rifa'a b. Zayd b. al-Sa'ib the Jew (9688); Abu Kurayb from Yunus b. Bukayr, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad b. Abi Muhammad, client of Zayd b. Thabit, from Sa'id b. Jubayr or Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas that Rifa'a b. Zayd b. al-Tabut, one of their notables, when he spoke with the Messenger would twist his tongue with an ambiguous word of address that sounded like asking for a hearing, then speak against Islam and fault it, and the passage was sent down (9689); Ibn Humayd from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq with his chain from Ibn Abbas, the same (9690). The editor says the name appears in the manuscript as in print "b. al-Sa'ib" and in the next report "b. al-Tabut", that Jewish names are difficult and he could not decide that either is wrong, perhaps al-Sa'ib being his grandfather's name and al-Tabut his nickname; that in 9689 both manuscript and print read "Abu Ishaq" for "Ibn Ishaq", a gross error he corrected; that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and follows an earlier report in this work (its number is unreadable in the scan); and that "Abu Ishaq" in 9690 is an error of the print alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 428}

**The sense of choosing misguidance (pp. 428-429, note 4).** Al-Tabari says it means the Jews handed a portion of scripture choose error, which is taking a way other than the truth and riding a path other than right guidance while knowing the straight road. God described them so for their persistence in denying Muhammad and leaving belief in him, though they know belief in him is the true way, and though they affirm what they found of his description in their books. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the words for buying and for error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 429}

**Their wish to mislead the believers, and the warning (p. 429).** Al-Tabari says these Jews want the companions of Muhammad who believe in him to leave the straight way and the highway of truth, deny Muhammad and become astray like them; this is a warning from God to His believing servants not to take counsel from any of those he calls enemies of Islam (his wording) in a matter of their religion, nor to listen to any of their attacks on the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 429}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 429}
