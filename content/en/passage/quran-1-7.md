## Summary
Verse 7 of [[passage/quran-1|Surat al-Fatiha]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 7 of 7 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
Under "the interpretation of 'the path of those You have favoured'" al-Tabari says the phrase explains which straight path it is, since every road of truth is a straight path; so Muhammad was told to say "Guide us, our Lord, on the straight path, the path of those You have favoured with obedience to You and worship of You, among Your angels, prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous". It matches [[passage/quran-4-66|4:66-69]], which promises guidance on the straight path and the company of those God favoured among the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous to whoever obeys God and the Messenger. So the guidance asked for is guidance to the road whose description God gave, the road of those He described as He did, and whoever follows it, obedient to God and His Messenger, God has promised to bring to where they arrive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 177} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 178}
He reports from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak that it means the way of those You have favoured with obedience to You and worship of You among the angels, prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous; from al-Rabi' that they are the prophets; from Ibn Abbas that they are the believers; from Waki' that they are the Muslims; and from Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd that it is the Prophet and those with him. The editor explains the weakness of the first chain and the break in the third, and quotes Ibn Kathir's remark that the earlier report from Ibn Abbas is the wider and fuller one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 178} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 179}
Al-Tabari says the verse is a plain proof that those who obey God reach obedience only by God's favouring them with it and giving them success, for it ascribes all their guidance, obedience and worship to His favour. Asked where the rest of the statement is, and what the favour is, he answers that the Arabs rely on part of a sentence when the stated part points to the unstated, and the favour is the upright way and straight path already explained, so the stated words, with the two clauses so close, make repeating it needless. He gives the example of a line of al-Nabigha describing a man as a timid camel with an old waterskin rattling behind its legs, where only the camel is named, and one by al-Farazdaq mocking a people who wear the tethering ropes, where the pronoun is dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 179} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 180}
Under "the interpretation of 'not of those who incurred wrath'" he says the readers agree on reading "ghayr" in the genitive, which has two explanations. It may qualify "those", though "those" is definite and "ghayr" indefinite, because "those who" with its clause is not a fixed definite like a proper name but like an unspecified indefinite such as "the man" or "the camel", as in "I sit only with the learned, not the ignorant"; if "those whom You have favoured" were a fixed definite it could not be qualified by an indefinite. Or "ghayr" is in the genitive with the sense of repeating "path", so "the path of those You have favoured, the path of other than those who incurred wrath". The two come close in sense, since whoever God has favoured and guided to the true religion is safe from wrath and rescued from error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 180} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 181}
He says no listener can doubt that those favoured with guidance to the path are not under their Lord's wrath nor astray, since it is impossible for pleasure and anger to meet in one person at one time, or for guidance and error to be together, so the description they were given already shows them to be so even if it does not say it; and this holds if we take "not those who incurred wrath nor those astray" to refer to others. If we take it as a qualifier of "those whom You have favoured", the listener has no need to infer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 182}
He adds that "ghayr" may be read in the accusative, although he dislikes the reading because it departs from the readers; a reading that departs from what the community has transmitted widely, he says, is a view against the truth and against the way of the Muslims, though it would have an explanation if it were allowed. Read in the accusative, it qualifies the pronoun in "alayhim", which is in an accusative position as object of "You favoured": "the path of those You guided as a favour from You, not under wrath", as "ghayr" is cut off in "I passed by Abdullah, not the generous", since Abdullah is a fixed definite and "not the generous" is indefinite. He reports that some Basran grammarians took the accusative as an exception, "except those who incurred wrath, whom You have not favoured in their religion, so do not make us among them", and he cites a line of al-Nabigha about halting at ruins where the exception sense appears. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 182} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 183}
Al-Tabari continues the Basran grammarians' view of "ghayr" in the accusative as an exception, with a line of exception from outside the kind, and then gives the Kufan grammarians' objection: if it were an exception, "nor those astray" would be wrong, since "la" is a negation and a negation is joined only to a negation, and they found no exception in Arab speech joined by a negation, so "ghayr" means negation, not exception. Al-Tabari says he set out the ways of parsing, though his aim in this book is the meaning of the verses, because differences in parsing come from differences in meaning, and the sound view for him is the first: reading "ghayr" in the genitive as a qualifier of "those whom You have favoured", or with the sense of repeating "path"; both are sound and good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 184}
Then an inquirer asks who those who incurred wrath are, from whom we were told to ask not to be counted. He says they are those God described in [[passage/quran-5-60|5:60]] as those God cursed and was angry with and turned some into apes and swine; God told us what befell them for their disobedience and then taught the way to be spared the like. As evidence he gives hadiths of Adi ibn Hatim, with chains the editor calls sound: the Prophet said those who incurred wrath are the Jews, and in another account, when Adi asked him about the verse, he said they are the Jews. The editor notes that the hadith comes from the story of Adi's coming to Islam and was also reported by Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi. This is how al-Tabari transmits it, and the museum gives it as transmitted and passes no judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 185} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 186}
He reports from Abdullah ibn Shaqiq that a man came to the Prophet as he besieged Wadi al-Qura and asked who the people he was besieging were, and he said these are those who incurred wrath, the Jews; four chains, some without the Companion's name and some joined with a Companion who is not named; the editor says the joined version is accepted, that Ibn Kathir mentions an account naming Abdullah ibn Amr, and that another report from Abu Dharr in Ibn Mardawayh explains "the astray" as the Christians. From Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak: the Jews, with whom God was angry; and from al-Suddi, Mujahid, al-Rabi', Ibn Jurayj through Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd and his father: the Jews. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 186} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 187} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 188}
He then says there is disagreement about the nature of God's wrath. Some say it is His bringing down punishment on one He is angry with, in this world or the next, as in [[passage/quran-43-55|43:55]] and [[passage/quran-5-60|5:60]]; some say it is His blame of them and their acts and a rebuke in words; and some say wrath is an understood meaning drawn from what is known of wrath, yet unlike the wrath of human beings, which disturbs, moves and pains them, since the essence of God is not subject to afflictions, but it is an attribute of His, like knowledge and power, in the sense understood by affirmation, though its meaning differs from the knowledge of servants. The editor says this affirmation is the way of Ahl al-Sunna in affirming God's attributes as He described Himself without reinterpretation. The editor also records that the Egyptian manuscript at Dar al-Kutub ends this section with the copyist's blessing and a note of a hearing session on a Thursday seven nights before the end of Ramadan in 346 AH, attended by Ahmad, Muhammad and al-Hasan, sons of Abdullah ibn Ahmad al-Farghani, and by Muhammad ibn Muhammad al-Tusi; the museum describes this note and does not reproduce its wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 188} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 189}
Under "the interpretation of 'nor those astray'" he says a Basran scholar held that "la" before "the astray" was added to round off the sentence and carries no meaning, citing lines of al-Ajjaj, Abu al-Najm and al-Ahwas and [[passage/quran-7-12|7:12]]; and it is reported of him that he took "ghayr" in the sense of "other than", so the sense is "the path of those You have favoured, who are other than those who incurred wrath and the astray". The editor says the speaker is Abu Ubayda and that al-Farra alludes to him. A Kufan grammarian objected to this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 190}
Al-Tabari completes the Kufan reply to the Basran: if "ghayr" with "those who incurred wrath" meant "other than", it would be wrong to join it with "la", since "la" is joined only to a negation before it, as it is wrong to say "I have other than your brother and not your father"; he holds that "ghayr" here means negation and that "la" does not come at the start with the sense of being dropped, with no earlier negation, or else "I wanted not to honour your brother" could mean "I wanted to honour him". He reads the "la" in the line of al-Ajjaj as a true negation, and the remaining lines as having an earlier negation at the start. Al-Tabari says this second view is the sounder: "ghayr" has three senses in Arabic speech, exception, negation and "other than", and "nor those astray" can only be joined to "not those who incurred wrath" with the sense of negation, so the speech means: guide us on the straight path, the path of those You have favoured, not those who incurred wrath, nor the astray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 191} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 192}
He then asks who the astray are, from following whom we were told to seek God's protection, and says they are those described in [[passage/quran-5-77|5:77]] as a people who had gone astray before and led many astray and strayed from the right way. As proof he gives the same hadiths of Adi ibn Hatim and of Abdullah ibn Shaqiq as before, with the same chains, saying the astray are the Christians; likewise from Mujahid, and from Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak: other than the way of the Christians whom God led astray for their lie against Him, that is "inspire us with Your true religion, there is no god but God alone with no partner, so that You are not angry with us as You were with the Jews nor lead us astray as You did the Christians". Also from Ibn Jurayj, al-Suddi, al-Rabi', Ibn Zayd and his father that they are the Christians. In a note the editor judges that "Urwa ibn Abdullah ibn Qays" in one chain is a scribal corruption of "Qushayr". This is what al-Tabari transmits; the museum gives it as transmitted and holds no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 193} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 194} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 195}
Al-Tabari says anyone who turns aside from the right course and takes a way other than the straight road is "astray" among the Arabs, because he has missed the face of the road, and for that God called the Christians astray, for missing the right path in religion. Asked whether this is also true of the Jews, he says yes: both groups are astray and under wrath, but God marked each group with a description by which His servants know it when He mentions it, and named neither except by what is truly an attribute of it, although it also has further attributes of blame. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 195}
Then he answers some Qadariyya who thought that God's describing the Christians as astray, ascribing the straying to them and not their being led astray to Himself, supports what their fellows say: he calls this ignorance of the breadth of Arabic speech, and says that if it were as they think, "the tree moved" when the wind moves it and "the earth shook" when the quake moves it would be wrong. He cites [[passage/quran-10-22|10:22]], which ascribes sailing to the ships although another makes them sail, and [[passage/quran-45-23|45:23]], which says plainly that He is the One who leads astray and guides. The Qur'an came down in the Arabs' tongue, he says, and they ascribe an act sometimes to the one from whom it came and sometimes to its cause, so an act the servant earns and God brings into being as a new thing is rightly ascribed to the one who earns it, as earning it by power and choice, and to God by bringing it into being and ordering it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 195} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 197}
Al-Tabari closes the commentary on al-Fatiha with "a question asked by the people of impiety who attack the Qur'an", putting the objection in their mouth: you wrote at the start of your book that the highest speech is the one that most fully explains the speaker's need and is nearest to the hearer's understanding, and that God's speech is first in this; so why lengthen the discourse on the mother of the Qur'an with seven verses, when all its meanings are in two, "Master of the Day of Judgement" and "You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help", since whoever knows the Master of that Day knows Him by His fair names and attributes, and whoever obeys God follows the way of those He favoured? What wisdom is in the remaining five? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 198}
His answer is that God gathered for His Prophet and his community, in the Book He sent down to him, meanings He never gathered in a book sent to any earlier prophet; each earlier book came with only some of the meanings that the Book given to Muhammad contains: the Torah is exhortations and detail, the Psalms praise and glorification, the Gospel exhortation and reminder, none of them a miracle that bears witness for the one it was sent to, while the Qur'an holds the meanings of all and adds to them. Among the noblest things by which it excels is its wondrous composition, its strange arrangement and its marvellous construction, which orators, the eloquent and the poets could not match even in its smallest surah, besides what it holds of encouragement and warning, command and prohibition, stories, argument and parable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 198} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 199}
So the reason for the length in the mother of the Qur'an, and in the similar surahs, is, on his account, that God wished to gather with its wondrous composition, set apart from the metres of poetry, the rhymed speech of soothsayers, the sermons of preachers and the letters of the eloquent, which all people cannot equal, the evidence of the prophethood of Muhammad; with its praise, glorification and thanks to Him to make servants mindful of His greatness, power and kingdom so that they remember His favours and thank Him and so deserve more; with its description of those He favours with knowledge of Him, to teach His servants that every favour they have in religion and the world comes from Him, so they turn their longing to Him; and with its account of what He has done to those who disobeyed Him, to warn His servants off disobedience. That, he says, is the far-reaching wisdom and the complete proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 199} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 200}
He ends with four reports from Abu Hurayra and Jabir ibn Abdullah: when the servant says "praise be to God, Lord of the worlds", God says "My servant has praised Me"; when he says "the Compassionate, the Merciful", God says "My servant has extolled Me"; when he says "Master of the Day of Judgement", God says "My servant has glorified Me", and in Jabir's report God has divided the prayer between Himself and His servant in two, and the servant has what he asks. The editor says the hadith counts as raised to the Prophet in meaning though it is halted in wording in two chains, that Malik and Muslim reported it raised through al-Ala ibn Abd al-Rahman, that Jabir's chain is good and sound, and that Ibn Kathir calls it strange from that route. On p. 201 the commentary on the Surat al-Fatiha ends with the closing line "end of the commentary on the surah, the Opening of the Book"; two blank pages and the title page of Surat al-Baqara follow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 200} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 201}
