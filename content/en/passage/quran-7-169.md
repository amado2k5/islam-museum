**Commentary on verse 169 (pp. 209-216)**

Al-Tabari says "a successor came after them": a bad successor arose after the people whose description he has given, a bad replacement for them. One says "a successor of truth" and "a successor of evil", and the usual practice is a fathah on the letter lam in praise and a silent lam in blame, though the lam can be vowelled in blame and silent in praise. For the silent lam in praise he cites a line by Hassan ibn Thabit from his elegy for Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and the martyrs of the day of Banu Qurayza, and the editor gives its sources and explains "the first precedence" as the precedence of the Ansar in Islam. Al-Tabari supposes that the sense of corruption is taken from the saying "the milk went sour", when milk turns sour from being left long in the skin until it spoils, or from "the mouth of the fasting person changed", when its smell changes; for the silent lam in blame he cites a line by Labid in his elegy for Arbad, saying that he remained among a succession like the skin of a mangy camel after those under whose protection people lived had gone, and the editor gives the poem and explains its words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 209} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 210}

It is said that the successors God mentions in this verse are the Christians; report 15313, from Mujahid: the Christians. Al-Tabari holds that the sound view is that God is describing them as a bad, worthless successor to the people whose stories He told in the preceding verses; He does not tell us in His book that they are Christians, and their story is closer to the Jews' stories than the Christians'. What comes before is an account of the Children of Israel and what comes after is the same, so what lies between is more likely an account of them too, since the verse gives no evidence for turning the account toward others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 210} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 211}

So, in his view, the meaning is: a bad replacement took their place who inherited the Book of God and then lost it, neglecting to act by it and opposing its ruling; they take bribes in God's judgment from the goods of "this lower world", meaning the nearer as against the farther life to come, and say when they do so that God will forgive them their sins, wishing vain wishes on God, as He said of them in [[passage/quran-2-79|2:79]]. "And if the like offering comes to them they take it" means that if another forbidden thing like the bribe is offered them after that, they take it and treat it as lawful and do not hold back; He tells us that they persist in their sins and are not people of turning back or repentance. The people of interpretation said the same, though their wording varies. The editor gives the manuscript reading of a word in the passage and discusses the wording of another phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 211}

Reports 15314 to 15317, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they commit a sin, then ask God's forgiveness, and if that sin presents itself again they commit it; the "offering" in the verse means the sins. Report 15318, from Mujahid: whatever of this world appears to them in a day, lawful or forbidden, that they desire, they take, and hope for forgiveness; and if they find the like the next day they take it. Reports 15319 and 15320 are similar, with the addition that they wish for forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 212}

Report 15321, from Qatada: yes, by God, a bad successor, who inherited the Book after their prophets and messengers, whom God had raised and made a covenant with; and God said in another verse, [[passage/quran-19-59|19:59]]. They wished wishes on God that deceived them; nothing distracted them from anything or stopped them, and whenever some worldly thing appeared to them they ate it, not caring whether lawful or forbidden. Report 15322, from Qatada, is similar. Report 15323, from al-Suddi: the Children of Israel never appointed a judge who did not take bribes in his judgments, and their best gathered and took pledges from each other that they would not do so and not take bribes; yet when a man of them was made judge he took bribes, and when asked what was the matter with him taking bribes in judgment, he said: I will be forgiven. The rest of them reproached him, but when he died or was removed a man who had reproached him was put in his place, and he too took bribes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 213}

Report 15324, from Ibn Abbas: they take what they get and leave what they please, lawful or forbidden, saying "we shall be forgiven". Report 15325, from Ibn Zayd: "the goods of this lower world" is the book that they wrote; "and they say, we shall be forgiven" means we associate nothing with God; "and if the like comes they take it": the man in the right comes to them with a bribe and they bring out the book of God and judge for him by the bribe; and when a wrongdoer came with a bribe they brought out "al-mathnat", the book they had written, and judged for him by what is in it, for the bribe, so that he was in the right by it but a wrongdoer under the Torah; so God said what He said in the rest of the verse. Report 15326, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they commit sins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 213} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 214}

He then turns to "was not the covenant of the Book taken from them, that they would say nothing about God but the truth, and they studied what is in it; and the home of the hereafter is better for those who are God-fearing; will you not understand". He says: was not the covenant of the Book taken from these bribe-takers in their judgments, who say "God will forgive us this deed" when they are reproached for it, that is, God's taking covenants from the Children of Israel to uphold the Torah and act by what ... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 214}

Al-Tabari says God said to those whose story is told in this verse, rebuking them for opposing His command and breaking His covenant and pledge: was not the covenant of His Book taken from them that they would say nothing about God but the truth, would attribute to Him only what He sent down to His messenger Moses in the Torah, and would not lie against Him? Report 15327, from Ibn Abbas: this concerns what they claim God owes them of forgiveness for their sins, to which they keep returning without repenting. The words "and they studied what is in it" are joined to "they inherited the Book" and mean that they read what is in it: they inherited the Book, learned what is in it and studied it, then wasted it, left off acting by it and opposed God's covenant with them. Report 15328, from Ibn Zayd: "they taught it", they taught what is in the Book that God mentions, and he recited [[passage/quran-3-79|3:79]]. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "covenant" and "study". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 214} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 215}

On "and the home of the hereafter is better for those who are God-fearing", he says: what is in the home of the hereafter, that is, in the return to God, of what He has prepared for His friends, who act by what He sent down in His Book and keep its limits, is better for those who fear God and dread His punishment, who observe Him in His commands and prohibitions and obey Him in all this in their worldly life. "Will you not understand?": do these people who take the goods of this lower world for their judgments and say "we shall be forgiven" not understand that what God has in the home of the hereafter for the God-fearing who are just between people in their judgments is better than this paltry offering that they hasten after in this world against God's command, judging between people unjustly? The editor notes that al-Tabari's reading is with the letter ya (a third-person form), as his commentary shows, and that he did not mention this reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 215} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 216}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
