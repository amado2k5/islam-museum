## Summary
Verse 52 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 52 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening, desiring his face; you are not accountable for anything of theirs, nor are they accountable for anything of yours, that you should drive them away and be among the wrongdoers" al-Tabari says: it is reported that this verse came down on the Messenger of God concerning a group of the weaker Muslims, of whom the polytheists said to him: if you drove these away from you we would come to you and attend your assembly! Report 13255, from Ibn Mas'ud: the nobles of Quraysh passed the Prophet with Suhayb, Ammar, Bilal, Khabbab and the like, weaker Muslims, by him, and said: Muhammad, are you content with these among your people? Are these the ones God has favoured among us? Are we to follow these? Drive them from you! Perhaps if you drive them away we shall follow you! So this verse came down, "do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening, desiring his face", "and so we have tried some of them by others", to the end of the verse. 13256 and 13257 have the same from Ibn Mas'ud about the passing of the nobles of Quraysh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 374}

In his notes the editor identifies Abu Zubayd Abthar ibn al-Qasim al-Zubaydi, trusted, correcting "Abu Zayd" in print; Ash'ath ibn Siwar, trusted; and Kardus al-Tha'labi, a trusted Follower; he says Ahmad related it in the Musnad in short form through Asbat, that al-Haythami traced it and said Ahmad's men are the men of the Sahih except Kardus, who is trusted, and that al-Suyuti traced it in the Durr al-Manthur; at 13256 he puts dots at the head of the chain because Abu Jafar does not relate directly from Jarir ibn Abd al-Hamid al-Dabbi, his teachers do; and at 13257 he says "from Kardus, from Ibn Abbas" is without doubt an error since the report is related from no one but Ibn Mas'ud.

Report 13258, from Khabbab ibn al-Aratt: al-Aqra' ibn Habis al-Tamimi and Uyayna ibn Hisn al-Fazari came and found the Prophet sitting with Bilal, Suhayb, Ammar and Khabbab among a number of weak believers; when they saw them around him they despised them, and came to him and said: we would like you to give us a place with you by which the Arabs know our excellence, for Arab delegations come to you and we are ashamed that the Arabs see us with these slaves, so when we come, make them withdraw from us, and when we have finished sit with them if you wish! He said: yes! They said: write us a document to that effect. He called for the page and called Ali to write; we were sitting in a corner when Gabriel came down with this verse, "do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening, desiring his face; you are not accountable for anything of theirs nor are they accountable for anything of yours, that you should drive them away and be among the wrongdoers"; then he said "so we have tried some of them by others, that they should say: are these the ones God has favoured among us? Is not God best informed of the thankful?"; then he said "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you, your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself". The Messenger of God threw the page from his hand, then called us and we came to him as he was saying "peace upon you, your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself"! We sat with him, and when he wished to rise he rose and left us; so God revealed [[passage/quran-18-28|18:28]], "and keep yourself patient with those who call on their Lord morning and evening, desiring his face, and let not your eyes pass beyond them, desiring the adornment of the present life". He said: the Messenger of God used to sit with us after that, and when the time came at which he rose we rose and left him until he rose. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 376}

Report 13259, from Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, like the account of al-Husayn ibn Amr, except that he says: when they saw them around him they drove them away, and came to him and were alone with him; and he also says: he called us and we came to him as he said "peace upon you", and we drew so near him that day that we put our knees on his knees; the rest of the report is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 377}

In his notes the editor identifies al-Husayn ibn Amr al-Anqazi, weak and soft, and his father Amr ibn Muhammad al-Anqazi, trusted; Asbat ibn Nasr al-Hamdani, whom Ahmad weakened though my brother judged him trustworthy; al-Suddi Isma'il ibn Abd al-Rahman, trusted; Abu Sa'id al-Azdi, reader of the Azd, who is Abu Sa'd al-Azdi or al-Arhabi, whom Ibn Hibban listed among the trusted; and Abu al-Kanud al-Azdi, whose name is disputed and from whom, of the six books' authors, only Ibn Majah related this very report; he says Ibn Majah related it by this route and that the Zawa'id calls its chain sound and its men trusted, and that Ibn Kathir, giving the report from Ibn Abi Hatim's commentary, said this is a strange report, for this verse is Meccan while al-Aqra' ibn Habis and Uyayna became Muslims long after the emigration, and he traces it to al-Suyuti's Durr al-Manthur.

Report 13260, from Qatada and al-Kalbi: some of the unbelievers of Quraysh said to the Prophet: if it would please you that we follow you, drive away from us so-and-so and so-and-so, some of the weaker Muslims! And God said "do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening, desiring his face". 13261, from Qatada: some people said to the Messenger of God: Muhammad, if it pleases you that we follow you, drive away so-and-so, so-and-so, people who were lower than them in the world and whom the polytheists despised, and God revealed this verse to its end. 13262, from Mujahid: Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd used to sit with Muhammad, and Quraysh, despising the two, said: but for them and their like we would sit with him! He was forbidden to drive them away. 13263, from Sa'd: this verse was revealed about six of the Prophet's companions, among them Ibn Mas'ud; he said: we used to get ahead to the Prophet, come near him and listen to him, and Quraysh said: he draws these near and not us! So it was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 378}

In his notes at 13263 the editor identifies al-Miqdam ibn Shurayh ibn Hani' ibn Yazid al-Harithi, trusted, and his father Shurayh, who reached the Messenger of God without seeing him and related from his father, Umar, Ali, Bilal, Sa'd and Abu Hurayra, and Sa'd, who is Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, correcting "Sa'id" in print and manuscript; he says Muslim related it in his Sahih by two routes, Ibn Majah with different wording, and al-Suyuti traced it in the Durr al-Manthur.

Report 13264, from Ikrima: Utba ibn Rabi'a, Shayba ibn Rabi'a, Mut'im ibn Adi, al-Harith ibn Nawfal and Qurta ibn Abd Amr ibn Nawfal, among the nobles of Banu Abd Manaf who were unbelievers, came to Abu Talib and said: Abu Talib, if your brother's son would drive away from him our clients and allies, who are our slaves and hired servants, it would be weightier in our hearts and he would be obeyed more by us and we would be nearer following and trusting him! Abu Talib came to the Prophet and told him what they said, and Umar ibn al-Khattab said: if you did that until you see what they want and where their words lead? So God revealed this verse, "warn by it those who fear being gathered to their Lord ... and do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening" to "is not God best informed of the thankful?". They were Bilal, Ammar ibn Yasir, Salim the freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, Subayh the freedman of Usayd, and of the allies Ibn Mas'ud, al-Miqdad ibn Amr, Mas'ud ibn al-Qari, Waqid ibn Abdallah al-Hanzali, Amr ibn Abd Amr Dhu al-Shimalayn, Marthad ibn Abi Marthad and Abu Marthad of Ghani, ally of Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib, and their like among the allies. "And so we have tried some of them by others, that they say: are these the ones God has favoured among us?" came down about the leaders of unbelief of Quraysh, and the clients and allies; when it came down Umar ibn al-Khattab came and excused himself for his words, and God revealed "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 379}

The editor explains "al-'usafa'" as plural of "'asif", a slave or a hired man despised, keeps the manuscript's form of the Companions' names, which is right, and says he found in al-Isaba, under Subayh, a report from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj that they were three, Ammar ibn Yasir, Salim freedman of Abu Hudhayfa and Subayh; if so "Bilal" is an error, though he cannot decide on it.

The editor identifies Mas'ud ibn Rabi'a al-Qari, ally of Banu Zuhra; Waqid ibn Abdallah al-Hanzali al-Tamimi, ally of Banu Adi; and Amr ibn Abd Amr ibn Nadla al-Khuza'i, Dhu al-Shimalayn, ally of Banu Zuhra, and recalls Ammar's words that three companions of the Messenger of God were "adbat", those who work with both hands.

Report 13265, from Ibn Zayd: a man said to the Prophet: I am ashamed before God that he should see me with Salman, Bilal and their kind, so drive them away from you and sit with so-and-so, so-and-so! The Qur'an came down "do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening, desiring his face", and he recited to "so you would be among the wrongdoers"; there is nothing between you and being among the wrongdoers but that you drive them away. Then he said "so we have tried some of them by others, that they should say: are these the ones God has favoured among us? Is not God best informed of the thankful?", then he said: and to these whom they told you to drive away, convey my peace, give them good news and tell them I have forgiven them! He recited "when those who believe in our signs come to you, say: peace upon you, your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself" to "so we explain the signs and so that the way of the sinners be plain", and said: so that you know it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 380}

The editor says "dhawihim" means their companions and likes, and that annexing "dhu" to a pronoun occurred in prose early, this report being among the proofs.

The interpreters differed on the calling by which this group, whom God forbade his prophet to drive away, called on their Lord. Some said it is the five prayers. 13266, from Ibn Abbas: "who call on their Lord morning and evening" means worship their Lord morning and evening, the prescribed prayer. 13267, from Ibrahim: it is the five prescribed prayers; and if it were what the storytellers say, whoever did not sit with them would perish. 13268, from Ibrahim: it is prayer. 13269, from Mujahid: the prescribed prayer, the dawn and the afternoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 381}

The editor explains "al-qussas" as plural of "qass", one who takes the front in a mosque or elsewhere and preaches to people and recalls the reports of the past, in whose tales additions and omissions may enter; it is in the hadith that the storyteller awaits hatred, and that the children of Israel perished when they told tales, meaning when they added to the report and lied; and he refers to reports 13270 and 13282.

Report 13270, from Hamza ibn Isa: I went in to al-Hasan and said: Abu Sa'id, what of God's word, [[passage/quran-18-28|18:28]], "keep yourself patient with those who call on their Lord morning and evening"? Are they these storytellers? He said no, but those who keep the prayers in congregation. 13271 and 13272, from Mujahid and al-Dahhak: the prescribed prayer. 13273, from Qatada: the two prayers, the dawn prayer and the afternoon prayer. 13274, from Abdullah ibn Umar: they are those who attend the prescribed prayers. 13275 and 13276, from Mujahid and Ibrahim: the five prayers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 382}

At 13270 the editor says he found no mention of Muhammad ibn Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Kindi, al-Tabari's teacher, that the printed text dropped "Muhammad ibn", a poor liberty, identifies Husayn al-Ju'fi and Hamza ibn al-Mughira al-Makhzumi, the devout, and says he found among the narrators no one named Hamza ibn Isa and thinks the copyist erred.

Report 13277, from Mujahid: the believers who pray, Bilal and Ibn Umm Abd; Ibn Jurayj said Abdallah ibn Kathir told me from Mujahid: I prayed the dawn prayer with Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, and when the imam gave the greeting the people rushed to the storyteller, and Sa'id said: how quickly they go to this assembly! Mujahid said: I said, they take the sense of what God said. He said: and what did he say? I said "do not drive away those who call on their Lord morning and evening". He said: is it about this? It is about the prayer we have just left, it is about prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 383}

At 13274 the editor says al-Suyuti traced it in the Durr al-Manthur and its chain is sound.

Report 13278, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Amra: the prescribed prayer. 13279 and 13280, from Amir: it is prayer. 13281, from Qatada: the dawn prayer and the afternoon prayer. 13282, from Mujahid: Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Amra prayed in the Prophet's mosque, and when he had prayed he stood and leaned on the Prophet's room, and the people streamed to him, and he said: people, keep back! A man invoked mercy on him and said: they came only for this verse, [[passage/quran-18-28|18:28]], "keep yourself patient with those who call on their Lord morning and evening". He said: is this what is meant by it? It is about prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 384}

The editor identifies at 13278 Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Amra ibn Mihsan ibn Tha'laba al-Ansari, who related from his father, Uthman ibn Affan and Ubada ibn al-Samit, whom Ibn Sa'd called trusted and a great narrator; he says 13282 is the long form of 13278 and that "anthala alayhi al-nas" means they came streaming to him from every side. He comments that this report is evidence for him of the earlier imams' right knowledge of their religion and their revealed book, and of the error into which some of our scholars and writers fell who dealt with God's book out of desire, until what the imams rejected was made a proof used by ignorant Sufis and charlatans for claims of sainthood and miracles; he asks God to keep him far from ignorance. He adds that most of these reports, in which al-Tabari explained the verse of Surat al-Kahf 28, are not related in his commentary on al-Kahf, which is one of the ways he abridged this commentary.

Others said: it is prayer, but the men did not ask the Messenger of God to drive these weak ones from his assembly or put them behind, only to put them behind the first row so that they would be behind them in the row. Report 13283, from Ibn Abbas: "and so we have tried some of them by others" were people who were with the Prophet, poor men, and some of the noble said: we shall believe in you, and when we pray put back these who are with you so that they pray behind us! {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 385}

Others said: their calling was their remembrance of God. 13284 and 13285, from Ibrahim and Mansur: "who call on their Lord morning and evening" are the people of remembrance, and 13286, from Ibrahim: do not drive them away from remembrance. Others said it was their learning the Qur'an and reciting it; 13287, from Abu Jafar: he used to make them recite the Qur'an; who is it who narrates to the Prophet? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 385}

At 13287 the editor says the printed text dropped "who is it who narrates to" from the manuscript, that the passage is two separate sentences, "he used to make them recite the Qur'an" and the question "who is it who narrates to the Prophet", both an answer to those who took the verse to mean the storytellers and preachers, as in reports 13282, 13277, 13270 and 13267; that if the storytellers were meant the Prophet would have been ordered to keep himself patient with whoever sat preaching to him and reminding him of God; and that the question means: who is this who would preach to the Messenger of God and remind him of God and of God's days?

Others said: by their calling on their Lord is meant their worship of him. 13288, from al-Dahhak: "who call on their Lord morning and evening" means worship, do you not see that he said "no doubt that what you call me to", meaning worship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 386}

The editor says the verse came so in the manuscript and print, and that he is almost certain it is a slip of a narrator or of Abu Jafar himself, and prefers that he meant [[passage/quran-40-66|40:66]], "say: I am forbidden to worship those you call on besides God", the verse put in its place, [[passage/quran-40-43|40:43]], being one in which calling cannot mean worship.

Al-Tabari holds the right view to be that God forbade his prophet to drive away a people who called on their Lord morning and evening; calling on God may be by remembering him, glorifying and praising him in speech, and may be by working for him with the limbs, the acts that were obligatory for them and other voluntary acts that please the one for whom the worshipper works; it may be that the people combined all these senses, so God described them as calling on him morning and evening, since God has called worship "calling", saying, [[passage/quran-40-60|40:60]], "your Lord said: call on me, I shall answer you; those who are too proud for my worship will enter Hell abased"; and it may be a particular kind of calling; no view is sounder than describing the people as God described them, as calling on their Lord morning and evening, taking the description in the general sense in which their Lord gave it and not singling out one part of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 387}

So the sense of the words is: Muhammad, warn with the Qur'an I sent you those who know that they will be gathered to their Lord and who, from fear of coming before God, having no intercessor or helper but him, are constant in working for him, when those of your people who deny God and the last day turn from your warning and from listening to what God sent you, in pride against God; and do not drive them away or banish them, lest you be one who put banishing in the wrong place and banished and drove away one he had no right to drive away or banish, and brought near one whom it was not for him to bring forward by nearness; for those I forbade you to drive away are those who call on their Lord, asking his pardon and forgiveness by their good deeds, the fulfilment of the obligations he laid on them and their voluntary acts, and their remembrance of him with their tongues morning and evening, seeking by it nearness to God and approach to his pleasure; "you are not accountable for anything of theirs" says: you are not accountable for anything of the provision I gave them, "nor are they accountable for anything of yours" of the provision I gave you, "that you should drive them away" for fear that I call you to account for what I granted them in the world of provision. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 388}

The editor keeps "who please the one who works for them, worshipping" after correcting manuscript and print, and "constant" for "lasting".

The words "that you should drive them away" answer "you are not accountable for anything of theirs and they are not accountable for anything of yours", and "and be among the wrongdoers" answers "do not drive away those who call on their Lord". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 388}

Verse 52 closes here at p. 388, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-53|6:53]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
