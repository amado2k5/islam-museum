## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 3 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "follow what has been sent down to you from your Lord, and do not follow patrons other than Him; little do you remember", God tells His Prophet to say to these idolaters of your people who worship idols and images: follow, people, what came to you from your Lord with clear proofs and guidance, act on what your Lord commanded you, and follow nothing besides Him, meaning nothing other than what your Lord sent down to you; that is, do not follow the commands of your patrons who order you to associate others with God and worship idols, for they lead you astray and do not guide you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 298}

If someone asks, how do you say the sense is "say: follow" when no mention of saying appears in the speech? The answer is that although it is not mentioned outright, the speech points to it, in His words "so let there be no constraint in your heart about it, so that you may warn with it"; in "so that you may warn" is a command to warn, and in the command to warn is a command to speak, since warning is speech; so the sense is, warn the people and say to them, follow what was sent down to you from your Lord. Had it been said, so that you warn with it and remind the believers, saying to them follow what was sent down to you, that would not be rejected. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 298}

Some Arabists said "follow" is an address to the Prophet, the sense being: a Book sent down to you, so let there be no constraint in your heart about it, follow what was sent down to you from your Lord; and he held it comparable to [[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]], where the address begins with the Prophet and then the verb is made plural, since God's command to His Prophet is a command to his whole community, as one says to a man addressed alone, meaning him and his followers or clan, will you not fear God, are you not ashamed before God, and the like. Al-Tabari says that although this is an acceptable view, his own choice is better in sense, because the apparent wording points to it; the editor refers to al-Farra's words. "Little do you remember" means little do you take admonition and reflect so as to return to the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 299}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
