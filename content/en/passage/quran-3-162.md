## Summary
Verse 162 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 162 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 162: the disagreement on the comparison (8169-8171, pp. 365-366).** Abu Ja'far: the scholars of interpretation differed. Some said it means: is the one who sought God's pleasure by leaving ghulul like the one who returned with God's anger on account of what he defrauded? Al-Dahhak (8169), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn 'Uyayna, from Mutarrif: whoever did not defraud, like the one who defrauded. Al-Dahhak (8170), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna, from Mutarrif ibn Tarif: whoever paid the fifth, like the one who incurred God's anger. Others said, Ibn Ishaq (8171): is the one who sought God's pleasure over what people love and dislike like the one who returned with God's anger for the sake of people's pleasure and displeasure? He says: is the one who is on My obedience, whose reward is Paradise and his Lord's pleasure, like the one who deserved His wrath and whose destination is hell and an evil end? Are the two examples equal? Know it, then (from Ibn Hisham 3: 124, the continuation of reports ending at 8168, with slight differences of wording). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 365} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 366}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 366).** Abu Ja'far holds the better of the two interpretations to be that of al-Dahhak ibn Muzahim, because the verse comes after God's threat against ghulul and His prohibition of it, and then He asks: are the one who obeys God in what He commanded and forbade and the one who disobeys Him in that equal? That is, they are not equal and their states are not equal with Him, since for the one who obeyed is Paradise and for the one who disobeyed the Fire. So his reading is: is the one who left ghulul and the sins God forbade, and acted in obedience in that and in His other duties, following God's pleasure in all of it and avoiding His anger, like one who returned bearing God's anger and wrath, and so deserved to dwell in hell? They are not alike. As for the closing words, he means by them the destination to which the one who incurred God's anger goes and returns, which is hell. In the footnotes the editor refers to earlier explanations of 'ba'a' (2: 138 and 345, 7: 116) and of 'masir' (6: 128 and 317, 3: 56), and notes that the sentence structure makes what lies between the two places an explanation of 'masir'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 366}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 366}
