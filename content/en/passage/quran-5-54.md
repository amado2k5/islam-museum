## Summary
Verse 54 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 54 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 54: "Believers, whoever of you turns back from his religion, God will bring a people He loves and who love Him", pp. 409-415**

Al-Tabari says God addresses the believers who trust God and His messenger and accept what their prophet brought: whoever of you goes back from the true religion he is on today, changing it by entering unbelief, whether Judaism, Christianity or another kind, will not harm God at all, and God will bring in their place the believers who did not change or turn back, a people better than those who turned back, whom God loves and who love Him. This warning was for those whom God knew would turn back after the death of His prophet, and likewise His promise to the believers was for those whom He knew would not change their religion. When God took His prophet, some tent-dwellers and some townsfolk did turn back, and God replaced them with better, kept His promise to the believers and carried out His threat on those who turned.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 410}

The interpreters said likewise. Report 12177, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, says that 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, then governor of Medina, sent for him one day and said a verse had kept him awake the night before, the one from "whoever of you turns back from his religion" to "fearing no one's blame"; Muhammad said: governor, God meant by the believers the rulers from Quraysh who might turn from the truth. The editor says 'Abd Allah ibn 'Ayyash al-Qitbani in the chain is not firm though reliable, and that Abu Sakhr is Humayd ibn Ziyad al-Kharrat.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 410}

They then differed over who the people are whom God brought in place of those who turned. Some said Abu Bakr al-Siddiq and his companions, who fought the people of the apostasy until they brought them back in by the door they had left by. Reports 12178 to 12182, from al-Hasan with various chains, say this is Abu Bakr and his companions. The notes say al-Fadl ibn Dalham al-Wasiti the butcher is disputed, and that Abu Musa is Isra'il ibn Musa al-Basri, who settled in India, reliable.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 411}

Report 12183, from al-Dahhak, says it is Abu Bakr and his companions: when some Arabs left Islam he struggled against them until he returned them to it. Report 12184, from Qatada, says God sent the verse knowing that some would turn back; when He took His prophet, most of the Arabs left Islam except three mosques, the people of Medina, of Mecca and of Bahrain from 'Abd al-Qays, who said: we pray but do not give zakat, and our property will not be taken. Abu Bakr was told that if they were taught they would pay; he said, by God, I will not separate what God joined, and had they withheld a hobbling rope of what God and His messenger imposed we would fight them for it. A note explains the "rope" as a year's zakat or the cord that tied the animal due, and that the report is also told with a kid.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 412}

Qatada goes on: God sent a band with Abu Bakr and he fought for what the Prophet fought for, taking captives, killing and burning with fire those who had turned and withheld zakat, until they admitted the zakat, humbled and abased. Delegations of the Arabs came and he gave them the choice between a shameful settlement or a devastating war; they took the shameful one, which was to admit that their dead were in the fire and the believers' dead in paradise, to return the Muslims' property they had taken, and that what the Muslims took of theirs was lawful. The editor comments on the words for the humbled and abased and on a text in al-Baladhuri's Futuh al-Buldan that supports his reading of the terms.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 413}

Report 12185, from Ibn Jurayj, says they turned back when the Messenger of God died and Abu Bakr fought them. Report 12186, from 'Ali, with Sayf ibn 'Umar in the chain, says God knew the believers, and the sense of evil fell on the riffraff among them of hypocrites and those He knew would turn; then He said "whoever of you turns back" and He will bring a people He loves, by Abu Bakr and his companions. The editor notes that number 12187 was skipped in the numbering by oversight, and another note says the manuscript marks here the end of a part in an older division of the book, with a closing prayer formula, and begins a new part with the opening invocation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 414}

Others said a people of Yemen is meant, and some said the clan of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, 'Abd Allah ibn Qays. Reports 12188 to 12192, from 'Iyad al-Ash'ari, say that when the verse came down the Messenger of God gestured toward Abu Musa with something he held and said: they are this man's people, in close wordings through Shu'ba and Simak ibn Harb. The editor introduces 'Iyad ibn 'Amr al-Ash'ari, a Successor whose Companionship is disputed, who reported from the Prophet in a mursal chain and from whom al-Sha'bi and Simak ibn Harb reported, and says Ibn Sa'd gave the report in the Tabaqat and al-Hakim in the Mustadrak.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 415}

**Commentary on verse 54, continued: "humble toward the believers, mighty toward the unbelievers", pp. 416-422**

Report 12193, from 'Iyad al-Ash'ari, or Ibn 'Iyad in doubt over the narrator, says they are the people of Yemen. Report 12194, from Shurayh ibn 'Ubayd, says that when God sent down the verse 'Umar asked: are my people and I they, Messenger of God? He said: no, this man and his people, meaning Abu Musa al-Ash'ari. The editor identifies the transmitters: Abu Sufyan al-Himyari, truthful but of middling standing; Husayn ibn 'Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami, reliable; Muhammad ibn 'Awf al-Ta'i, al-Tabari's teacher, a reliable memoriser; Abu al-Mughira of Homs, reliable; and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Jubayr al-Hadrami, a reliable Successor.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 416}

Others said they are all the people of Yemen. Reports 12195 and 12196, from Mujahid, say some people of Yemen; report 12197 from him says the people of Saba'; report 12198, from Shahr ibn Hawshab, says the people of Yemen; report 12199 says 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, as governor of Medina, sent to Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi to ask about it, and he said they are the people of Yemen; 'Umar said: would that I were of them, and he said: amen. Others said the Ansar of the Messenger of God; report 12200, from al-Suddi, says they are the Ansar.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 417}

Al-Tabari explains that on the view that Abu Bakr and his companions are meant, in their war on the people of apostasy, the verse means: believers, whoever of you turns back from his religion will not harm God, and God will bring a people He loves and who love Him who will take revenge on them at their hands; report 12201, from 'Ali, says so. On the view that the people of Yemen are meant, God will bring to the believers who did not turn back a people He loves and who love Him as helpers and supporters; report 12202, from Ibn 'Abbas, says it is a warning from God that whoever turns back will be replaced by better. On the view of the Ansar the meaning is the same as on the view of Abu Bakr and his companions.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 418}

Al-Tabari then says the best view in his opinion is what the report from the Messenger of God relates, that they are the people of Yemen, the people of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari; without that report his view would only be that they are Abu Bakr and his companions, because nobody fought people who had shown Islam in the Prophet's time and then turned back except Abu Bakr and those with him; but he leaves that view for the report, since the Prophet is the source of clear explanation of what God sent down. A note explains "source", the place where the origin and beginning of a thing is.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 419}

He raises a question: if the people are those of Yemen, were the people of Yemen in the days of Abu Bakr's war on the apostates his helpers against them, since there is no breaking of God's promise? He answers that God did not promise the believers to replace the apostates that day with better ones who would fight the apostates, but only that He would bring better ones in their place, and He did so soon: He brought them in the time of 'Umar, and their place in Islam and its people was the finest, they were helpers of the Muslims and more useful to them than the rabble of Bedouins and rough desert folk who turned back after the Messenger of God and had been a burden on Islam's people, not a benefit.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 420}

The reciters differed over "whoever of you turns back". The people of Medina read it with the doubling shown, two dals with the last jussive, as in their copies; the people of Iraq read it merged, with one dal and a vowel, on the model of the dual, since a jussive verb whose doubling shows in the singular is merged in the dual, and he gives examples with the verb "give back". Both usages are fluent and well known. Al-Tabari chooses what is in their own copies and those of the East, one doubled dal with a vowel, for the reason described.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 421}

On "humble toward the believers, mighty toward the unbelievers", al-Tabari says "humble toward the believers" means soft and merciful to them, from the saying that someone was humble to another when he submits to him, and "mighty toward the unbelievers" means severe and harsh to them, from the saying that someone showed himself mighty to another when he showed pride and hardness. Report 12203, from 'Ali, says gentle toward their own religion's people and harsh toward those who differ with them; report 12204, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the humble are the merciful; report 12205, from Ibn Jurayj, says merciful among themselves and stern toward them; report 12206, from al-A'mash, says weak before the believers. The notes correct names in the chains and record the manuscript's wording.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 422}

We stop here at the foot of p. 422, at the start of "they strive in the way of God and fear no one's blame"; the rest of verse 54 follows in the next batch, from p. 423.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
