**Commentary on verse 54: "Believers, whoever of you turns back from his religion, God will bring a people He loves and who love Him", pp. 409-415**

Al-Tabari says God addresses the believers who trust God and His messenger and accept what their prophet brought: whoever of you goes back from the true religion he is on today, changing it by entering unbelief, whether Judaism, Christianity or another kind, will not harm God at all, and God will bring in their place the believers who did not change or turn back, a people better than those who turned back, whom God loves and who love Him. This warning was for those whom God knew would turn back after the death of His prophet, and likewise His promise to the believers was for those whom He knew would not change their religion. When God took His prophet, some tent-dwellers and some townsfolk did turn back, and God replaced them with better, kept His promise to the believers and carried out His threat on those who turned.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 410}

The interpreters said likewise. Report 12177, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, says that 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz, then governor of Medina, sent for him one day and said a verse had kept him awake the night before, the one from "whoever of you turns back from his religion" to "fearing no one's blame"; Muhammad said: governor, God meant by the believers the rulers from Quraysh who might turn from the truth. The editor says 'Abd Allah ibn 'Ayyash al-Qitbani in the chain is not firm though reliable, and that Abu Sakhr is Humayd ibn Ziyad al-Kharrat.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 410}

They then differed over who the people are whom God brought in place of those who turned. Some said Abu Bakr al-Siddiq and his companions, who fought the people of the apostasy until they brought them back in by the door they had left by. Reports 12178 to 12182, from al-Hasan with various chains, say this is Abu Bakr and his companions. The notes say al-Fadl ibn Dalham al-Wasiti the butcher is disputed, and that Abu Musa is Isra'il ibn Musa al-Basri, who settled in India, reliable.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 411}

Report 12183, from al-Dahhak, says it is Abu Bakr and his companions: when some Arabs left Islam he struggled against them until he returned them to it. Report 12184, from Qatada, says God sent the verse knowing that some would turn back; when He took His prophet, most of the Arabs left Islam except three mosques, the people of Medina, of Mecca and of Bahrain from 'Abd al-Qays, who said: we pray but do not give zakat, and our property will not be taken. Abu Bakr was told that if they were taught they would pay; he said, by God, I will not separate what God joined, and had they withheld a hobbling rope of what God and His messenger imposed we would fight them for it. A note explains the "rope" as a year's zakat or the cord that tied the animal due, and that the report is also told with a kid.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 412}

Qatada goes on: God sent a band with Abu Bakr and he fought for what the Prophet fought for, taking captives, killing and burning with fire those who had turned and withheld zakat, until they admitted the zakat, humbled and abased. Delegations of the Arabs came and he gave them the choice between a shameful settlement or a devastating war; they took the shameful one, which was to admit that their dead were in the fire and the believers' dead in paradise, to return the Muslims' property they had taken, and that what the Muslims took of theirs was lawful. The editor comments on the words for the humbled and abased and on a text in al-Baladhuri's Futuh al-Buldan that supports his reading of the terms.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 413}

Report 12185, from Ibn Jurayj, says they turned back when the Messenger of God died and Abu Bakr fought them. Report 12186, from 'Ali, with Sayf ibn 'Umar in the chain, says God knew the believers, and the sense of evil fell on the riffraff among them of hypocrites and those He knew would turn; then He said "whoever of you turns back" and He will bring a people He loves, by Abu Bakr and his companions. The editor notes that number 12187 was skipped in the numbering by oversight, and another note says the manuscript marks here the end of a part in an older division of the book, with a closing prayer formula, and begins a new part with the opening invocation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 414}

Others said a people of Yemen is meant, and some said the clan of Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, 'Abd Allah ibn Qays. Reports 12188 to 12192, from 'Iyad al-Ash'ari, say that when the verse came down the Messenger of God gestured toward Abu Musa with something he held and said: they are this man's people, in close wordings through Shu'ba and Simak ibn Harb. The editor introduces 'Iyad ibn 'Amr al-Ash'ari, a Successor whose Companionship is disputed, who reported from the Prophet in a mursal chain and from whom al-Sha'bi and Simak ibn Harb reported, and says Ibn Sa'd gave the report in the Tabaqat and al-Hakim in the Mustadrak.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 415}

We stop here at the foot of p. 415, after report 12192 from Iyad al-Ash'ari that the people are the people of Abu Musa; the rest of verse 54 follows in the next batch, from p. 416.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
