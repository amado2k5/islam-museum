## Summary
Verse 101 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 101 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Explaining "O you who believe, do not ask about things which, if made plain to you, would vex you", al-Tabari notes that this verse came down on the messenger of God because of questions that some people put to him, sometimes to test him and sometimes in mockery; one would say, who is my father, and another, whose camel had strayed, would say, where is my camel? So God told them not to ask about things of that kind, like Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa's question about who his father was, "if made plain to you they would vex you", that is, if We showed you the truth of what you ask about, showing it would vex you. Report 12794, from Abu al-Juwayriya: Ibn Abbas said to a bedouin of Banu Sulaym, do you know about what this verse was revealed? Some people used to ask the messenger of God in mockery, one saying, who is my father, and another whose camel had strayed, where is my camel, and God revealed this verse about them (the editor says al-Bukhari relates it and Ibn Kathir says only al-Bukhari reports it). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 98}

Report 12795, from Anas: people asked the messenger of God until they pressed him with questions, and one day he went up on the pulpit and said: ask me nothing but I will make it plain to you! Anas said: I began to look right and left and saw every man wrapping his garment around himself weeping; then a man who, when he quarrelled, was called by someone other than his father, spoke up and said: messenger of God, who is my father? He said: your father is Hudhafa! Then Umar spoke and said: we are content with God as Lord, with Islam as religion and with Muhammad as messenger, and I seek refuge with God from the evil of trials! The messenger of God said: I have never seen the evil and the good as I saw them today; paradise and the fire were shown to me until I saw them beyond this wall. Qatada used to mention this report at this verse (the editor traces it to Muslim's Sahih and the Durr al-manthur). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 99}

Report 12796, from Anas: a man said, messenger of God, who is my father? He said: your father is so-and-so; and the verse "O you who believe, do not ask about things..." came down (the editor says al-Bukhari, Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i relate it). Report 12797, from Qatada from Anas: the people asked the messenger of God until they pressed him with questions, and he went out to them one day and went up on the pulpit and said: ask me nothing today but I will make it plain to you! The Companions of the messenger of God feared that some matter had come upon him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 100}

Anas said: I did not turn right or left without finding everyone wrapping his head in his garment weeping; then a man who used to quarrel and be called by someone other than his father spoke up and said: prophet of God, who is my father? He said: your father is Hudhafa! Then Umar stood and said: we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion, Muhammad as messenger, seeking refuge in God from the evil of trials! And the messenger of God said: I have never seen the good and the evil as I saw them today; paradise and the fire were shown to me until I saw them on this side of the wall. Report 12798, from Ikrima: that was the day the Prophet stood among them and said, ask me nothing but I will tell you; a man stood up, and the Muslims disliked his standing that day, and said, messenger of God, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa, and this verse came down. Report 12799, from Tawus: it came down about a man who said, messenger of God, who is my father? He said, your father is so-and-so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 101}

Report 12800, from Qatada: they asked the Prophet until they overdid it; he stood angry, preaching, and said: ask me! By God, you will ask me nothing as long as I am in this place but I will tell you! A man stood and said, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa! His anger grew and he said, ask me! When the people saw that their weeping increased, and Umar knelt on his knees and said: we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion, Muhammad as messenger. The messenger of God said: by Him in whose hand is my soul, paradise and the fire were shown to me just now across this wall, and I never saw good and evil as I have today. Al-Zuhri said: the mother of Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa said, I never saw a more disobedient son than you! Were you not afraid your mother may have done what the people of the pre-Islamic time did and you would shame her before the people? He said: by God, if he had attached me to a black slave I would have accepted it. Report 12801, from al-Suddi: the messenger of God was angry one day and stood preaching, saying: ask me, for you will ask me nothing but I will tell you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 102}

A man of Quraysh from Banu Sahm, called Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa, who was slandered, stood up and said: messenger of God, who is my father? He said: your father is so-and-so, and he named him for his father. Umar stood up to him and kissed his foot and said: messenger of God, we are content with God as Lord, with you as prophet, with Islam as religion and with the Qur'an as guide; pardon us, God pardon you! He went on until he was content, and that day he said: the child belongs to the bed, and to the adulterer the stone. Report 12802, from Abu Hurayra: the messenger of God came out angry, his face red, until he sat on the pulpit; a man stood and said, where is my father? He said, in the fire; another stood and said, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa; Umar stood and said: we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion, Muhammad as prophet and the Qur'an as guide; we, messenger of God, are newly out of the pre-Islamic time and polytheism, and God knows who our fathers are! His anger subsided and the verse came down (the editor says the chain includes Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban, whom they called a liar, though Ibn Kathir says the chain is good). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 103}

Others said this verse came down on the messenger of God because of a questioner who asked about something in the matter of the pilgrimage. Report 12803, from Ali: when "pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to God by people who are able to make their way there" was revealed, they said, messenger of God, every year? He was silent; they said again, every year? He was silent; then he said: no; if I had said yes it would have been obligatory! And God revealed this verse (the editor notes that Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim relate it, and that al-Tirmidhi calls it good and unusual). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 104}

We stop here at the foot of p. 104, in the middle of the commentary on verse 101, after report 12803 on the occasion concerning the pilgrimage.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
