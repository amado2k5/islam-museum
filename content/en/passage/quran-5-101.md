## Summary
Verse 101 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 101 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Explaining "O you who believe, do not ask about things which, if made plain to you, would vex you", al-Tabari notes that this verse came down on the messenger of God because of questions that some people put to him, sometimes to test him and sometimes in mockery; one would say, who is my father, and another, whose camel had strayed, would say, where is my camel? So God told them not to ask about things of that kind, like Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa's question about who his father was, "if made plain to you they would vex you", that is, if We showed you the truth of what you ask about, showing it would vex you. Report 12794, from Abu al-Juwayriya: Ibn Abbas said to a bedouin of Banu Sulaym, do you know about what this verse was revealed? Some people used to ask the messenger of God in mockery, one saying, who is my father, and another whose camel had strayed, where is my camel, and God revealed this verse about them (the editor says al-Bukhari relates it and Ibn Kathir says only al-Bukhari reports it). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 98}

Report 12795, from Anas: people asked the messenger of God until they pressed him with questions, and one day he went up on the pulpit and said: ask me nothing but I will make it plain to you! Anas said: I began to look right and left and saw every man wrapping his garment around himself weeping; then a man who, when he quarrelled, was called by someone other than his father, spoke up and said: messenger of God, who is my father? He said: your father is Hudhafa! Then Umar spoke and said: we are content with God as Lord, with Islam as religion and with Muhammad as messenger, and I seek refuge with God from the evil of trials! The messenger of God said: I have never seen the evil and the good as I saw them today; paradise and the fire were shown to me until I saw them beyond this wall. Qatada used to mention this report at this verse (the editor traces it to Muslim's Sahih and the Durr al-manthur). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 99}

Report 12796, from Anas: a man said, messenger of God, who is my father? He said: your father is so-and-so; and the verse "O you who believe, do not ask about things..." came down (the editor says al-Bukhari, Muslim, al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i relate it). Report 12797, from Qatada from Anas: the people asked the messenger of God until they pressed him with questions, and he went out to them one day and went up on the pulpit and said: ask me nothing today but I will make it plain to you! The Companions of the messenger of God feared that some matter had come upon him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 100}

Anas said: I did not turn right or left without finding everyone wrapping his head in his garment weeping; then a man who used to quarrel and be called by someone other than his father spoke up and said: prophet of God, who is my father? He said: your father is Hudhafa! Then Umar stood and said: we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion, Muhammad as messenger, seeking refuge in God from the evil of trials! And the messenger of God said: I have never seen the good and the evil as I saw them today; paradise and the fire were shown to me until I saw them on this side of the wall. Report 12798, from Ikrima: that was the day the Prophet stood among them and said, ask me nothing but I will tell you; a man stood up, and the Muslims disliked his standing that day, and said, messenger of God, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa, and this verse came down. Report 12799, from Tawus: it came down about a man who said, messenger of God, who is my father? He said, your father is so-and-so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 101}

Report 12800, from Qatada: they asked the Prophet until they overdid it; he stood angry, preaching, and said: ask me! By God, you will ask me nothing as long as I am in this place but I will tell you! A man stood and said, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa! His anger grew and he said, ask me! When the people saw that their weeping increased, and Umar knelt on his knees and said: we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion, Muhammad as messenger. The messenger of God said: by Him in whose hand is my soul, paradise and the fire were shown to me just now across this wall, and I never saw good and evil as I have today. Al-Zuhri said: the mother of Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa said, I never saw a more disobedient son than you! Were you not afraid your mother may have done what the people of the pre-Islamic time did and you would shame her before the people? He said: by God, if he had attached me to a black slave I would have accepted it. Report 12801, from al-Suddi: the messenger of God was angry one day and stood preaching, saying: ask me, for you will ask me nothing but I will tell you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 102}

A man of Quraysh from Banu Sahm, called Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa, who was slandered, stood up and said: messenger of God, who is my father? He said: your father is so-and-so, and he named him for his father. Umar stood up to him and kissed his foot and said: messenger of God, we are content with God as Lord, with you as prophet, with Islam as religion and with the Qur'an as guide; pardon us, God pardon you! He went on until he was content, and that day he said: the child belongs to the bed, and to the adulterer the stone. Report 12802, from Abu Hurayra: the messenger of God came out angry, his face red, until he sat on the pulpit; a man stood and said, where is my father? He said, in the fire; another stood and said, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa; Umar stood and said: we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion, Muhammad as prophet and the Qur'an as guide; we, messenger of God, are newly out of the pre-Islamic time and polytheism, and God knows who our fathers are! His anger subsided and the verse came down (the editor says the chain includes Abd al-Aziz ibn Aban, whom they called a liar, though Ibn Kathir says the chain is good). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 103}

Others said this verse came down on the messenger of God because of a questioner who asked about something in the matter of the pilgrimage. Report 12803, from Ali: when "pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to God by people who are able to make their way there" was revealed, they said, messenger of God, every year? He was silent; they said again, every year? He was silent; then he said: no; if I had said yes it would have been obligatory! And God revealed this verse (the editor notes that Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim relate it, and that al-Tirmidhi calls it good and unusual). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 104}

Report 12804, from Abu Hurayra: the messenger of God said: God has prescribed the pilgrimage for you! A man said, every year, messenger of God? He turned away from him until he repeated it two or three times, then said: who is the questioner? He said, so-and-so! He said: by Him in whose hand is my soul, had I said yes it would have been obligatory, and had it been obligatory on you, you could not have borne it, and had you left it you would have disbelieved! So God revealed this verse (the editor weakens the chain through Ibrahim ibn Muslim al-Hajari and traces the report in al-Jassas, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti). Report 12805, from Abu Hurayra: the messenger of God preached to us and said, people, God has prescribed the pilgrimage for you. Muhsin al-Asadi stood up and said, every year, messenger of God? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 105}

He said: had I said yes it would have been obligatory, and had it been obligatory and you then left it you would have gone astray; be silent about me as long as I am silent about you, for those before you were destroyed only by their questions and their disagreements with their prophets! And God revealed "O you who believe, do not ask about things..." to the end of the verse. The editor traces the report at length in Ahmad, Muslim, al-Bukhari and al-Bayhaqi, and notes that in the sound chains the questioner is al-Aqra' ibn Habis, whereas al-Husayn ibn Waqid differs on the questioner's name, making him Muhsin al-Asadi or Ukkasha ibn Muhsin, and that there are additions in his reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 106}

Report 12806, from Abu Hurayra, is the same except that he says Ukkasha ibn Muhsin al-Asadi stood up. Report 12807, from Abu Umama al-Bahili: the messenger of God stood among the people and said: the pilgrimage has been prescribed for you! A bedouin stood and said, every year? The speech of the messenger of God stuck and he fell silent and was angered; he stayed a long time, then spoke and said: who is the questioner? The bedouin said, here I am! He said: woe to you! What assures you that I would not say yes, and had I said yes it would have been obligatory, and you would have disbelieved! Those before you were destroyed only by the leaders of hardship; by God, if I made lawful to you everything on the earth and forbade you a space the size of a foot, you would fall into it! And God revealed the verse at that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 107}

In his notes the editor explains the pointing of the words for "stuck", "fell silent" and "was angered", says that the "leaders of hardship" are those who begin to ask about things that then become forbidden to people because of their asking, and describes the narrators of the chain (Zakariya ibn Yahya al-Misri, Abu Zayd Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi al-Ghamr and others), noting that al-Haythami called the chain good and Ibn Kathir weakened it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 108}

Report 12808, from Ibn Abbas: the messenger of God made a proclamation to the people, saying: people, the pilgrimage has been prescribed for you! A man of Banu Asad stood and said: messenger of God, every year? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 109}

The messenger of God was made very angry and said: by Him in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, had I said yes it would have been obligatory, and had it been obligatory you could not have borne it and would then have disbelieved; so leave me as long as I leave you, and when I command you something do it and when I forbid you something desist! And God revealed "O you who believe, do not ask about things...", forbidding them to ask the kind of question the Christians asked about the table, so that they became disbelievers on account of it; God forbade that and said: do not ask about things which, if the Qur'an came down with severity about them, would vex you, but wait, and when the Qur'an comes down you will not ask about anything but find its explanation. Report 12809, from Ibn Abbas: when the pilgrimage verse was revealed, the Prophet called out to the people: people, God has prescribed the pilgrimage for you, so make it! They said, one year or every year? He said: no, one year; had I said every year it would have been obligatory, and had it been obligatory you would have disbelieved; then God said "O you who believe, do not ask..."; they asked the Prophet about things and he admonished them and they desisted. Report 12810, from Mujahid: the messenger of God mentioned the pilgrimage and was asked, is it obligatory every year, messenger of God? He said: no; had I said it, it would have been obligatory, and had it been obligatory you could not have borne it, and had you not borne it you would have disbelieved. Then he said: ask me, and no man in this gathering of mine will ask me about anything but I will tell him, even if he asks about his father! A man stood and said, who is my father? He said, your father is Hudhafa ibn Qays. Umar stood and said: messenger of God, we are content with God as Lord, Islam as religion and Muhammad as prophet, and we seek refuge in God from His anger and the anger of His messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 110}

Others said this verse came down because they asked the messenger of God about the bahira, the sa'iba, the wasila and the ham. Report 12811, from Ibn Abbas: "things" are the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and ham; do you not see that He says after it "God did not appoint any bahira or sa'iba"? (the speaker is Khusayf). As for Ikrima, he said they used to ask him about signs, and were forbidden to; then He said "a people before you asked about them, and then became disbelievers because of them." Khusayf says: I said, Mujahid reported to me from Ibn Abbas something other than this, so why do you say this? He said: hayh! (the editor explains Ikrima's word and Mujahid's poor opinion of Ikrima). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 111}

The rest of report 12811: Khusayf says of Ikrima that it was he who asked the messenger of God, who is my father? And Sa'id ibn Jubayr said: they were those who asked the messenger of God about the bahira and the sa'iba. Al-Tabari's choice: the verse came down because of the many questions put to the messenger of God, like Ibn Hudhafa's question about who his father was, and the question of the one who asked, when He said that God made the pilgrimage obligatory on you, whether every year, and the like, because reports of this are widespread from the Companions, the Successors and most of the interpreters. As for what Mujahid related from Ibn Abbas, it is not far from right, but the widespread reports from the Companions and Successors are against it, so he dislikes holding it; still it is not implausible that the question about the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and ham was among the questions they put to the Prophet that God disliked their asking, as He disliked their question about the pilgrimage and Ibn Hudhafa's question about his father; the verse came down forbidding all such questions, and each reporter told of some of the things for which it came down and for other things. He holds this the soundest view, since the chains for all the meanings mentioned are sound, and directing them to the right sense in each is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 112}

Next "but if you ask about them while the Qur'an is being revealed, they will be made plain to you; God has pardoned them, and God is Forgiving, Forbearing". God says to the Companions of the messenger of God whom He forbade to question him about what He forbade them to ask, about obligations He had not imposed on them, permitting matters He had not permitted, and forbidding things He had not forbidden before the Qur'an came: believers, do not ask about what I have sent down neither in a book nor in revelation, for if the explanation of it were shown to you by revelation it would vex you, since revelation on it, when it comes to you, comes with your testing and trial, either by making an act obligatory on you and imposing a duty, which is hardship and burden, or by forbidding what, had no revelation come forbidding it, you would have been free to venture into, or by permitting what you hold forbidden, which grieves you because it moves you from what you thought right to what you thought wrong; but if you ask about them after the Qur'an has come down on them, and after the start of making their matter plain in My book to My messenger, what I sent down to him of the explanation of My book will make it easy for you. He cites report 12813, from Abu Tha'laba al-Khushani: God has laid down obligations, so do not neglect them, forbidden things, so do not violate them, set limits, so do not transgress them, and pardoned things, not from forgetfulness, so do not enquire into them (the editor notes that al-Suyuti has it as a saying of the Prophet). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 113}

Reports 12814 and 12815, from Ubayd ibn Umayr: God made things lawful and forbade things; make lawful what He made lawful and avoid what He forbade, and He left things that He neither permitted nor forbade, and that is a pardon from God; then he recited "O you who believe, do not ask..." As for "God has pardoned them", it means God has pardoned you your asking about the things you asked the messenger of God about, which He disliked for you, so as not to hold you to account or punish you for it, knowing your repentance and turning back; "God is Forgiving" means covering the sins of whoever repents of them and leaving off exposing him in the next life, and "Forbearing" means slow to punish him for them, since He covers the repentant with His mercy and pardon from punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 114}

A report in line with what al-Tabari says has come from Ibn Abbas, report 12816: "do not ask about things" means do not ask about things which, if the Qur'an came down on them with severity, would vex you, but wait, and when the Qur'an comes down you will not ask about anything but find its explanation. The reports on verse 101 end here, on p. 115. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 115}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
