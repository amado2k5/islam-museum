**Commentary on verse 42 (pp. 499-500)**

On "those before them plotted, but to God belongs all plotting; He knows what every soul earns, and the disbelievers will know for whom is the final abode", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: those before these polytheists of Quraysh, of the nations that have passed away, plotted against God's prophets and messengers; "but to God belongs all plotting", that is, to God belong the means of plotting entirely, in His hand and to Him, and the plotting of any plotter harms no one except the one whom He wills to harm by it, so the plotters harmed none by their plotting but those God willed to be harmed by it, and they harmed only themselves by it, because by it they angered their Lord against themselves until He destroyed them and saved His messengers. So too these polytheists of Quraysh plot against you, Muhammad, and God will save you from their plotting and attach the harm of their plotting to them and not to you. On "He knows what every soul earns" he says: your Lord, Muhammad, knows what these polytheists of your people do and the plotting against you in which they strive, and knows all the deeds of all creation, none of it hidden from Him; "and the disbelievers will know for whom is the final abode" he says, they will know, when they come before their Lord on the Day of Resurrection, whose is the outcome of the abode of the hereafter, when they enter the Fire and the believers in God and His Messenger enter Paradise. Al-Tabari says: the readers differed in reading it. The readers of Medina and some of Basra read "wa-sa-ya'lamu al-kafir" in the singular, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 499}

while the readers of Kufa read it "al-kuffar" in the plural. Al-Tabari says: the correct reading is the plural, "wa-sa-ya'lamu al-kuffar", because the report before it ran about them as a group and was followed afterward by a report about them, that is, His saying "whether We show you part of what We promise them or We cause you to die" and after it "those who disbelieve say, you are not a messenger". It is mentioned that in Ibn Mas'ud's reading it is "al-kafirun" and in Ubayy's "alladhina kafaru", all of which is evidence for the soundness of the reading we chose. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 500}
