**Commentary on verse 12 (pp. 569-572)**

On [[passage/quran-12-12|12:12]], 'send him with us tomorrow to enjoy himself and play; we shall guard him', the readers differ. Most readers of Medina read 'yarta'i' with kasra on the 'ayn and ya' in 'yarta'i wa yal'ab', of the pattern 'ifta'ala' from grazing, 'I grazed, I graze', as if they took the sense to be send him with us tomorrow to graze the camels and play. Most readers of Kufa read 'yarta' wa yal'ab', with ya' in both and sukun on the 'ayn, from 'so-and-so roamed in his wealth' when he took pleasure and comfort in it and spent it on his desires, and from this the proverb 'the shackle and the pasture', whose origin the editor tells, and he cites a verse of al-Qutami. Some of Basra read 'narta' wa nal'ab' with nun in both and sukun on the 'ayn; report 18813, from Abu 'Amr: he used to read with the nun, and I said to him, how could they say 'we play' being prophets? He said, they were not prophets then. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 569}

Al-Tabari says: the best reading with me is of whoever read both with ya' and put sukun on the 'ayn of 'yarta'', because the men were asking their father to send Joseph with them and deceived him by speaking of their request in terms of Joseph's gladness, joy and zest on going out into the open desert and playing there, not in terms of themselves. The interpreters' sense agrees; report 18814, from Ibn Abbas: he runs about and is lively; 18815, from him: he amuses himself, is lively and runs about; 18816, 18817 and 18818, from Qatada: lively and amusing himself, running and amusing himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 570}

Reports 18819 and 18820, from al-Dahhak: he amuses himself and plays; 18821 and 18822, from al-Suddi: lively and playing, amusing himself; 18823, from Qatada: lively and playing; 18824, from al-Dahhak: runs and is lively, the editor giving notes on one narrator and citing Ibn Hajar. Those who read 'yarta'i' with kasra seem to take it in the sense of report 18825, from Ibn Zayd: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 571}

he pastures his flock and watches and uses his mind and learns what a man learns. Mujahid said in report 18826 that 'narta'i' means some of us guard others, we watch over each other and keep guard; reports 18827, 18828, 18829 and 18830, from Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj, are similar, the editor explaining that 'natakalla'' is from 'kala'ahu', he guarded, tended and kept watch over him. Al-Tabari says: so the sense is send him with us tomorrow to amuse ourselves, play, enjoy and be lively in the desert, and we are his guardians against anything he dislikes or that harms him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 572}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
