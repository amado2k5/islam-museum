**Commentary on verse 142: the hypocrites' deception of God, and their show in prayer and remembrance (pp. 329-332)**

Al-Tabari says the hypocrites' deception of their Lord means that by their hypocrisy they secure their lives and property. God deceives them by the judgement He makes concerning them, protecting their lives because of what they show with their tongues of faith, though He knows their hearts; this is a gradual leading on in this world, until they meet Him and He sends them to the fire of hell for what they conceal of unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 329}

Report 10721, from al-Suddi through Asbat, says that on the Day of Resurrection God gives them a light by which they walk with the Muslims, as they were with them in this world, then He takes the light away and extinguishes it, so they stand in darkness, and a wall is set between them. Report 10722, from Ibn Jurayj through Hajjaj, says the verse was revealed about Abd Allah ibn Ubayy, Abu Amir ibn al-Nu'man and the hypocrites. The editor identifies the one called the monk Abu Amir as Amr ibn Sayfi ibn al-Nu'man, and notes that the Prophet said: do not call him the monk; call him the wicked. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 329}

Report 10723, from al-Hasan, says every believer and hypocrite is given a light by which to walk, and when they reach the bridge the hypocrites' light is put out, while the believers go on with their light. The hypocrites call to them asking to wait so they can borrow from their light, and are told to go back and seek a light. Al-Hasan says this is God's deception of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 330}

Al-Tabari says their standing lazily to prayer means that the hypocrites do none of the deeds God requires of believers in order to draw near to Him, since they do not believe in the return, reward or punishment; they do only outward deeds to keep themselves safe, out of fear that the believers will kill them or take their property; so they stand lazily to prayer to show the believers that they are among them, though they are not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 331}

Report 10724, from Qatada: by God, were it not for people the hypocrite would not pray, and he prays only for show and reputation; report 10725, from Ibn Zayd: they are the hypocrites; were it not for show they would not pray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 331}

On 'they remember God only a little', al-Tabari says the sense is that they remember God only for show, to ward off from themselves killing, captivity and the taking of property, not with the remembrance of one who is certain and affirms God's oneness; that is why it is called little, because it is not meant for God or sought as nearness to Him, and so it is like a mirage with a surface but no water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 331}

Report 10726, from Abu al-Ashhab: al-Hasan read 'only a little' and said it is little because it was for other than God. Report 10727, from Qatada: the hypocrite's remembrance is little because God does not accept it; whatever God rejects is little, and whatever God accepts is much. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 332}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
