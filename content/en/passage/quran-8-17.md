## Summary
Verse 17 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 17 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 17 (pp. 441-449)**

Then al-Tabari says on "you did not kill them, but God killed them; and you did not throw when you threw, but God threw, and that He might try the believers with a fair trial from Him; God is Hearing, Knowing" [[passage/quran-8-17|8:17]]: God says to those who believe in Him and His Messenger, among those who were at Badr with the Messenger of God and fought the enemies of his religion among the unbelievers of Quraysh: you did not kill the polytheists, believers, but God killed them. He attributed their killing to Himself and denied it of the believers who fought the polytheists, since He is the cause of their killing and the believers' fighting them was by His command. In that is the clearest proof of the falsity of the saying of those who deny that God has a hand in the acts of His creatures by which they reach them. Likewise His words to His prophet "and you did not throw when you threw, but God threw": He attributed the throwing to the Prophet of God and then denied it of him and said of Himself that He is the thrower, since He is the One who brought what was thrown to those at whom it was thrown among the polytheists and the cause of the throw for His Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 441} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 442}

So it is said to those who deny what we have mentioned: you know God's attributing His prophet's throwing at the polytheists to Himself after describing His prophet with it and attributing it to him, and that is one act, from God its causing and directing and from the Messenger of God the flinging and sending; what do you deny of other acts of creatures that are acquired being likewise, from God the bringing into being and accomplishing by causing, and from the creature the acquiring by his powers? They will say nothing of one of the two without being bound to say the like of the other. The editor gives the reading of the manuscript and printed edition and his correction of the word for "those who deny", the copyist having joined the letter ra to ya and nun. The interpreters said the like: report 15817, from Mujahid: "you did not kill them" is said to the companions of Muhammad when each said, I killed this one, I killed that one, and "you did not throw when you threw" is said to Muhammad when he pelted the unbelievers with gravel; report 15818, from Mujahid, in the same sense; report 15819… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 442}

…report 15819, from Qatada: "you did not throw when you threw, but God threw", he said, the Messenger of God threw them with gravel on the day of Badr; report 15820, from Ikrima: none of it fell but in a man's eye; report 15821, from Hisham ibn Urwa: when the Messenger of God came to Badr he said, these are their places of falling; the polytheists found the Prophet had got to it first and halted there, and when they appeared before him, they claimed, the Prophet said, this is Quraysh, come with their clamour and pride, opposing you and calling your messenger a liar; God, I ask You what You promised me; when they advanced he faced them and threw a handful in their faces, and God routed them; report 15822, from Hakim ibn Hizam: on the day of Badr we heard a sound fall from the sky like a pebble falling in a basin, and the Messenger of God made that throw and we were routed. The editor explains "jalaba" as the commotion of people gathering and shouting to one another, says the printed edition made an ugly change and the manuscript agrees with the History, identifies the men of the chains, and says Hakim's report is weak because of Abd al-Aziz ibn Imran al-Zuhri; Ibn Kathir mentions it calling it strange by this route, al-Haythami mentions it saying al-Tabarani gave it in the Great and Middle collections with a good chain, and al-Suyuti traces it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 442} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 443} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 444}
Report 15823, from Muhammad ibn Qays and Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: when the two sides drew near each other the Messenger of God took a handful of earth and threw it in the faces of the people and said, may the faces be disfigured; it entered all their eyes, and the companions of the Messenger of God went on killing and taking them captive, their rout being in the Messenger of God's throw, and God sent down the verse; report 15824, from Qatada: it was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God took three stones on the day of Badr and threw them at the faces of the unbelievers, and they were routed at the third stone; report 15825, from al-Suddi: the Messenger of God said to Ali when the two hosts met on the day of Badr, give me some gravel from the ground, and he handed him gravel with earth on it, which he threw in the faces of the people, and no polytheist was left without some of that earth in his eyes, and then the believers followed them up, killing and capturing them; report 15826, from Ibn Zayd: the Messenger of God took three pebbles and threw one at the right of the people, one at their left and one in their midst, and said may the faces be disfigured, and they were routed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 444} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 445}
Report 15827, from Ibn Abbas: the Messenger of God raised his hand on the day of Badr and said, Lord, if this band is destroyed You will never be worshipped on earth; Gabriel said to him, take a handful of earth; he took a handful of earth and threw it in their faces, and there was no polytheist whose eyes, nostrils and mouth did not get earth from that handful, and they turned and fled; report 15828, from Ibn Ishaq: God said of the Messenger of God's throwing gravel from his hand at the polytheists "you did not throw when you threw, but God threw", that is, that was not by your throw, were it not for what God put in it of your help and what He cast into the hearts of your enemy from it when God routed them. A saying contrary to these sayings is reported from al-Zuhri, report 15829: Ubayy ibn Khalaf al-Jumahi came to the Prophet with a decayed bone, crumbling it, and said, will God bring this to life, Muhammad, when it is rotten? and the Prophet said, God will bring it to life, then make you die, then enter you into the Fire; when the day of Uhud came he said, by God I shall kill Muhammad when I see him; that reached the Prophet and he said, rather I shall kill him, God willing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 445} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 446}
The editor says he fears something has dropped out here, for Ibn Kathir in his commentary mentions what he ascribes to Ibn Jarir, two other sayings very strange: one from Abd al-Rahman ibn Jubayr, that on the day of Ibn Abi al-Huqayq at Khaybar the Messenger of God called for a bow, and a weak bow was brought, and he said bring me another; a stout bow was brought and he shot at the fortress and the arrow flew until it killed Ibn Abi al-Huqayq in his bed, and God sent down the verse, Ibn Kathir saying this is strange though its chain is good; and the other from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and al-Zuhri that it was revealed about the Prophet's throwing a spear at Ubayy ibn Khalaf at Uhud, Ibn Kathir saying they perhaps meant the verse takes it in by its generality, not that it was revealed specially about it. The editor gives what al-Suyuti brought out in al-Durr al-Manthur on it and says this makes it likely that something has dropped from Abu Ja'far's reports at this place unless they come in another place, that the report about Ubayy ibn Khalaf has already come in the report of al-Suddi, and that al-Hakim gave the first in the Mustadrak calling it sound on the condition of the two shaykhs, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 446} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 447} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 448}
As for "and that He might try the believers with a fair trial from Him", the sense is: so that He might bestow favour on the believers in God and His Messenger by victory over their enemies, give them as spoils what they had and write for them the rewards of their works and their striving with the Messenger of God; and that fair trial is God's throwing at these polytheists, "fair trial" meaning the fair and beautiful favour, which is what was described and what is like it. Report 15830, from Ibn Ishaq: that He might make the believers know His favours to them in making them prevail over their enemy despite the enemy's many and their own few, so that they might know His right by it and give thanks for His favour. As for "God is Hearing, Knowing", it means, believers, that God hears the prayer of the Prophet and his entreating his Lord and asking Him for the destruction of his enemy and yours… The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "trial". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 448}
…and for your saying and the saying of all His creatures; "Knowing" of all that and of what is for your good and the good of His servants and other things, encompassing it; so fear Him and obey His command and the command of His Messenger. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "hearing" and "knowing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 448} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 449}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
