## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "it is prescribed for you, when death approaches one of you, if he leaves wealth, to make a bequest for parents and kin, in what is customary, a duty on the God-fearing" al-Tabari says "kutiba" means "was made obligatory": the bequest was made obligatory when death approaches one of you, if he leaves good, which is wealth, for the parents and kin who do not inherit from him, "in what is customary", that is what God allowed, not exceeding a third and without the testator meaning to wrong his heirs; "a duty on the God-fearing" means an obligatory duty on whoever fears God and obeys Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 384}

Al-Tabari asks whether it was made obligatory on a man of wealth to bequeath to his parents and kin who do not inherit from him, and answers yes, and that one who neglects it has wasted an obligation, on the strength of "kutiba 'alaykum", as in "fasting is prescribed for you", and none disputes that one who can fast and does not has wasted an obligation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 385} He mentions that a group of scholars said the bequest for parents and kin is abrogated by the verse of inheritance and another group said it is firm; al-Tabari says he does not judge it abrogated without a proof, since the ruling of this verse and the verse of inheritance can hold together. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 385} Among those holding this he cites al-Dahhak, that whoever dies without bequeathing to his kin ends his deeds in disobedience; Masruq, to a man who bequeathed unsuitable things, bequeath to your kin who do not inherit from you and leave the property as God divided it; and Abu Mijlaz and Lahiq ibn Humayd, that the bequest is a duty on whoever leaves good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 385} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 386} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 387}

He then reports that scholars differed on the verse's ruling in three positions. Some said God abrogated nothing of it; it is general in appearance, and meant are the parents and kin who do not inherit and not those who do; from Jabir ibn Zayd and al-Hasan, for a man who bequeathed to a non-relative while having needy relatives, two thirds of the third go back to his relatives and one third of the third to the beneficiary, and from Tawus that it is taken from them and returned to his needy kin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 387} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 388} Others said it was binding and acted upon for a time, then God abrogated by the verse of inheritance the bequest to the testator's parents and kin who inherit and kept the bequest for those who do not, from Qatada, Ibn 'Abbas, al-Hasan, Tawus, al-Rabi' and 'Ali ibn Abi Talha; from Qatada, a bequest to an heir is not allowed, and from Ibn 'Abbas, God sent down after it the verse of al-Nisa' on the parents' inheritance and kept the bequest for kin within a third of the estate; and from Muslim ibn Yasar, al-'Ala' ibn Ziyad and Iyas ibn Mu'awiya, the verse concerns kin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 388} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 390} Others said God abrogated all of it and set the fixed shares and inheritance, so no bequest is obligatory for anyone for anyone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 390}

Among those holding that the bequest is wholly abrogated is Ibn Zayd: God abrogated all of that and set the fixed shares; from Ibn 'Abbas, that he preached, recited al-Baqara, and on reaching this verse said it was abrogated, and elsewhere that the shares for parents and kin abrogated the bequest; from Ibn 'Umar, al-Hasan, 'Ikrima, Shurayh, Qatada, Mujahid and al-Suddi, that the inheritance verse abrogated it; from Shurayh that a man used to bequeath all his wealth until the inheritance verse came; and from al-Suddi that when it was revealed people had no fixed inheritance and a man bequeathed to his father and family and it was divided among them until al-Nisa' abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 390} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 391} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 392} He reports that Ibn 'Umar made no bequest, that when someone said to al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym "bequeath me your Qur'an copy" he looked at his son and said "those related by blood are nearer to one another in God's Book", and from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, on Talha and Zayd being strict about bequests, that the Prophet died without a bequest and Abu Bakr made one, and whichever you do is good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 392} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 393}

The "good" which, when left, makes the bequest due is wealth, from Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Suddi, al-Rabi', 'Ikrima, al-Dahhak and 'Ata', Mujahid saying "good" throughout the Qur'an is wealth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 393} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 394} He then reports their differences on the amount of wealth at which the bequest binds. Some said a thousand dirhams, from Qatada, and 'Ali ibn Abi Talib is reported to have told a man who left seven to nine hundred "do not bequeath, you have not left good", and told another the same, and 'A'isha, of a man who left four hundred dinars with many children, "I see no merit in it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 394} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 395} Some said between five hundred dirhams and a thousand, from Aban ibn Ibrahim al-Nakha'i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 395}

Some said the bequest is obligatory from a little wealth or much, from al-Zuhri: God made the bequest a duty from what is little of it or much. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 395} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 396} Al-Tabari chooses al-Zuhri's view, because "good" applies to little wealth and much, God set no limit and specified nothing, so whoever is facing death with any wealth, little or much, must bequeath from it to those of his fathers, mothers and kin who do not inherit, in what is customary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 396}
