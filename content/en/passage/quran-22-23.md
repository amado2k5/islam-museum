## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 23 to 24 in a single passage. He explains His saying: "Indeed, Allah will admit those who believe and do righteous deeds into gardens beneath which rivers flow, where they will be adorned with bracelets of gold and pearls, and their garments therein will be silk." As for those who believe in Allah and His Messenger and obey them with what Allah has commanded them of righteous deeds, Allah will admit them to gardens of Eden beneath which rivers flow, and will adorn them therein with bracelets of gold and pearls. Readers differed in the pronunciation of the phrase "walu'a" (pearl pearls), so the majority of the reciters of Medina and some people of Kufa read it as accusative with those in the angels, meaning: they adorn them with bracelets of gold and pearls, with the conjunction of the pearl to the place of the bracelets, because although the bracelets are reduced to include 'man' in them, they are in the accusative sense. They said: they are counted in the script of the Qur'an with an alif, which is evidence of the correct reading of the accusative in it. The general recitation of the Qur'an and the Egyptians read it as "wa'lulu" as a drop, in conjunction with the apparent grammatical grammatical of the bracelets. In the passage, he quotes statements from Ibn Abbas.
