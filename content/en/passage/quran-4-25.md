## Summary
Verse 25 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 25 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of verse 25: the meaning of 'tawl', the view that it is wealth and ampleness (pp. 182-183, reports 9049-9056).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed over the meaning of the word for means in this verse. Some said it is bounty, wealth and ampleness. Muhammad ibn Amr (9049, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it is wealth; al-Muthanna (9050, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih) reports the same from Mujahid; al-Muthanna (9051, from Abdallah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is whoever has no ampleness; Bishr ibn Mu'adh (9052, from Yazid from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that it is ampleness; al-Qasim (9053, from al-Husayn from Hushaym from Abu Bishr) reports from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that it is wealth; al-Muthanna (9054, from Hibban ibn Musa from Ibn al-Mubarak from Hushaym from Abu Bishr) reports from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that it is ampleness, and the editor notes that the printed text added "ibn" in al-Muthanna's chain, which the manuscript lacks and which is the right reading, with references to earlier and later places; Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (9055, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that it is ampleness of wealth; Yunus (9056, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that it is not finding what to marry a free woman with. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 183}

**The view that it means desire, and reports on a free man marrying a slave woman (pp. 183-184, reports 9057-9061).** Others said that here it means desire. Yunus (9057, from Ibn Wahb from Abd al-Jabbar ibn Umar) reports from Rabi'a that a man may marry a slave woman if his desire is for her; the editor corrects the narrator's name to Abd al-Jabbar ibn Umar al-Ayli and refers to his biography. Yunus (9058, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that Rabi'a softened this somewhat, holding that a man may marry a slave woman if he fears for himself because of loving her, even if he could marry another. Al-Muthanna (9059, from Hibban ibn Musa from Ibn al-Mubarak from Hammad ibn Salama from Abu al-Zubayr) reports from Jabir that he was asked about a free man marrying a slave woman and said that if he has means, no; asked what if love for her falls into his heart, he said that if he fears hardship let him marry her. Ibn Humayd (9060, from Jarir from Mansur from Ubayda) reports from al-Shabi that a free man should not marry a slave woman unless he finds none, while Ibrahim used to say there is no harm in it. Al-Muthanna (9061, from Hibban ibn Musa from Ibn al-Mubarak from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ata that they did not object to a man of means marrying a slave woman today if he feared being made miserable by her; the editor notes that the printed text reads the last word differently from his reading of the manuscript and ties it to the mention of hardship in the earlier reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 184}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the meaning of 'tawl' (pp. 184-185).** Abu Jafar holds that the sounder of the two views is that the word here means ampleness and wealth, because all agree that God did not forbid anything and then make it lawful because desire overcame a person or for the sake of pleasure; rather He made the forbidden lawful in necessity, when the person in need fears for his life, as with carrion for the desperate. If that is agreed for everything except the marriage of slave women by one who can afford a free woman, then the marriage of slave women by a man able to marry a free woman is the same: it does not become lawful because desire overpowers him, since that is satisfaction of pleasure and not a case of necessity that lifts the concession. He sees in the agreement that a man overcome by desire for a woman, free or slave, may have her only by marriage or purchase as God allowed, evidence against those who explained the word as desire and allowed a man able to marry a free woman to marry slave women. The meaning of the verse for him is therefore: whoever among you lacks the means for marrying free women, let him marry from the slave women he owns. The root sense of the word is bestowal of favour; the verb is used in that sense with one vowelling, and with another for length as the opposite of shortness. In footnotes the editor says the first publisher found the two sentences after the agreement hard to follow, explains that they mean God did not make the forbidden lawful because of desire but because of necessity, notes where he preferred his reading over the printed text and the manuscript, and notes that one phrase is the object of "God allowed His servants". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 185}

**The explanation of the clause on marrying believing free women (p. 185).** Abu Jafar begins to explain the next clause: whoever of you, people, lack, meaning of the free, the means to marry the muhsanat, who are the free women, the believing ones, meaning those who have, and the sentence breaks off at the end of the page. The editor refers in a footnote to the earlier explanation of the word muhsanat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 185}

**The rest of the explanation of 'believing free women', with reports from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, al-Suddi and others (pp. 186-187, reports 9062-9068).** Abu Jafar completes his sentence: the believing women are those who affirmed God's oneness and the truth the messenger brought, and the interpreters spoke of the muhsanat much as he did. Al-Muthanna (9062, from Abdallah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that the word means free women, so whoever cannot marry them should marry from the believers' slave women; Muhammad ibn Amr (9063, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that the muhsanat are free women and that one should marry a believing slave woman; al-Muthanna (9064, from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl) reports the same from Mujahid; Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (9065, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the young women here are your slave women; al-Qasim (9066, from al-Husayn from Hushaym from Abu Bishr) reports from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that whoever cannot find the means to marry a free woman marries a slave woman, and the editor says the printed text has the verb in another form and that he kept the manuscript; Yunus (9067, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that such a man marries the slave woman and keeps chaste with her, and her owners spare him her upkeep, that God made this lawful to no one unless he lacks the means to marry and support a free woman, and that it is not lawful for him until he fears hardship, the editor preferring the manuscript over the printed text in two places; al-Muthanna (9068, from Hibban ibn Musa from Ibn al-Mubarak from Sufyan from Hisham al-Dastuwa'i from Amir al-Ahwal) reports from al-Hasan that the Messenger of God forbade marrying a slave woman on top of a free wife and a free woman on top of a slave wife, and said that whoever has the means for a free woman should not marry a slave woman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 187}

**The readers' differences over the vowel of 'muhsanat', and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 187-188).** Abu Jafar says the readers differed over this word. A group of the readers of Kufa and Mecca read it with a kasra on the letter sad here and in its parallels in the Qur'an, except the first place in [[passage/quran-4-24|4:24]], which they read with a fatha, taking it to mean women protected by their husbands; elsewhere they took the kasra to mean women who protected themselves by chastity. Most readers of Medina and Iraq read all of them with a fatha, meaning that some were protected by their husbands and some by their freedom or their Islam. Some early readers read all of them with a kasra, meaning they were chaste and guarded themselves; this reading is reported from Alqama, with differing transmission from him. The editor observes that Abu Jafar did not mention this reading or this difference in his commentary on verse 24 earlier, which he counts as evidence of how much he abridged that commentary. Abu Jafar's choice is that both readings are widespread among the readers of the regions and agree in meaning, so whichever a reader uses is right, except the first place in [[passage/quran-4-24|4:24]], where he does not allow the kasra because the regions agree on the fatha; had the kasra been as widespread there as the fatha, it too would be right, given the varied senses of the word for protection he explained, and the meaning would then be the chaste among women. The editor adds that none of this was mentioned in the first commentary and that the meaning of protection came a little earlier. Abu Jafar then says the word for young women is the plural of a word for a girl, meaning young women, that every slave woman of age or youth is called by it, and the male slave by the masculine form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 188}

**The dispute over marrying slave women who are not believers (pp. 188-189, reports 9069-9072).** Abu Jafar says the scholars differed over marrying young women who are not believers, and whether God's description of them as believing means a prohibition of the others or is guidance for the believers. Some said it indicates that marrying the slave women of polytheists and the People of the Book is forbidden. Ibn Bashshar (9069, from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it is not fitting to marry a Christian slave woman; Ibn Waki (9070, from his father from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that a free Muslim should not marry a slave woman of the People of the Book; Ali ibn Sahl (9071, from al-Walid ibn Muslim) says he heard Abu Amr (al-Awza'i), Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz, Malik ibn Anas and Abu Bakr ibn Abdallah ibn Abi Maryam say that a Christian slave woman is not lawful to a free Muslim or a Muslim slave, because the verse carries the description of belief, meaning for marriage. Others said it is guidance and recommendation, not prohibition, and among them are a group of the people of Iraq; (9072, from Ibn Humayd from Jarir from Mansur from Mughira) Abu Maysara said that the People of the Book are in the position of free women. Among them are Abu Hanifa and his companions, who argued from the verse of al-Ma'ida, [[passage/quran-5-5|5:5]], which made the chaste women of the People of the Book lawful, saying no one may single out a slave woman or a free woman among them, and that the description of belief here means only women who are not idol worshippers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 189}

**The editor's notes on report 9071 (p. 189).** The editor identifies al-Walid ibn Muslim al-Dimashqi and refers to his earlier biography, says Abu Amr is al-Awza'i, and that the name stood in both printed text and manuscript as "Abu Amr Sa'id", as if one man, which he corrected to two men; for Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Tanukhi, Abu Muhammad, he refers to the earlier biography. Abu Bakr ibn Abdallah ibn Abi Maryam al-Ghassani was, he says, one of the diligent worshippers, with many hadith but weak; he quotes Abu Hatim that he was weak in hadith, that thieves broke in and took his belongings and he became confused, that he died in 156, and that the Tahdhib al-Tahdhib errs in the year of his death, referring to Ibn Sa'd's Tabaqat. He adds that printed text and manuscript read "and Malik ibn Abdallah ibn Abi Maryam", that no narrator has that name, and that he corrected it to Abu Bakr ibn Abi Maryam, from whom al-Walid ibn Muslim transmitted as from the others named before. In another note he explains that "among them Abu Hanifa and his companions" is joined to the earlier mention of a group of the people of Iraq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 189}

**Al-Tabari's choice in the dispute (p. 190).** Abu Jafar holds that the sounder view is that the description indicates a prohibition of marrying the slave women of the People of the Book, who are lawful only by ownership, because God made marriage to slave women lawful under conditions, and where the conditions are not all met a Muslim may not marry them. To the objection that the verse of al-Ma'ida shows they are permitted by marriage, he replies that its ruling has been shown to concern a particular class of their chaste women, their free women and not their slaves, by the description of belief here; neither verse sets aside the other, and one would set aside the other only if their rulings could not both stand rightly; since both can, neither is judged to override the other without a proof from a report or an analogy, and there is none; the verse allows his reading, that the chaste women meant are the free women of the People of the Book and not their slaves. In footnotes the editor says he preferred the manuscript's reading over the printed one in three places, including the dual form in place of the singular for the word for ruling, and that the first publisher misread one spot in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 190}

**The clause on God's knowledge of people's faith, a transposed clause (p. 191).** Abu Jafar says this is a case of postposing whose sense is preposing, and the meaning is: whoever among you cannot afford the dowers of believing free women, let one of you marry from another, meaning let this man marry this man's girl. He explains that the word for "some" is in the nominative by the sense of the sentence, since the earlier clause means "let him marry from those his hands own", and "some of you" is then joined to that sense. He explains that God knows best the faith of those who believed in Him, in His messenger and in what the messenger brought, and affirmed all of it; so the poor man who cannot afford a free woman should marry from the wealthy man his believing girl who has shown and displayed her faith, and they leave their inner states to God, whose knowledge it is, not yours; He knows your inner states and theirs. The editor notes that the manuscript completes the verse here with an extra clause and that the first publisher did well to drop it, since the commentary treats the clause on God's knowledge of faith as coming before the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 191}

**The clause on marrying with the families' permission and giving the wages (p. 192, report 9073).** Abu Jafar says the command to marry them means to wed them; with their families' permission means with the permission of their owners, their command to you to marry them and their consent; and giving them their wages means giving them their dowers. Yunus (9073, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the wages are the dower. The word for fairness he explains as in what you agree on, of what God has made lawful and allowed to be made a dower for them. The editor refers in footnotes to the earlier explanations of the words for marrying, permission, giving, wages and fairness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 192}

**The description of chaste women who are not open fornicators or takers of lovers (p. 193, reports 9074-9075).** Abu Jafar says the muhsanat here are the chaste, the musafihat are the fornicating women, and the takers of lovers are women who take friends for illicit union. He says it was put so because the fornicating women among the Arabs in the Jahiliyya were those who announced their fornication, and the takers of lovers were those who confined themselves to one companion and friend for debauchery in secret and not openly. Al-Muthanna (9074, from Abdallah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that the sense is: marry them chaste, not fornicators in secret or in public, and that the akhdan are close friends. Muhammad ibn Sa'd (9075, from his father from his uncle from his father from his grandfather) reports from Ibn Abbas that the musafihat are the women who announce their fornication, that the taker of lovers is the one with a single companion, and that the people of the Jahiliyya forbade open fornication and allowed what was hidden, saying that what showed was baseness and what was hidden did no harm, and God then sent down a verse forbidding indecencies, open and hidden, which is [[passage/quran-6-151|6:151]] (the printed number is smudged). The editor refers to the earlier explanations of muhsanat and sifah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 193}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 193 in the commentary on verse 25 of sura 4, after report 9075 in the explanation of the clause on chaste women who are neither open fornicators nor takers of secret lovers, and continue from p. 194. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 193}
