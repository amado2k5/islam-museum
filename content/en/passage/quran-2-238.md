## Summary
Verse 238 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 238 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says the command to guard means keeping to the obligatory prayers at their times, tending and holding to them, and the middle one among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 167} Masruq: guarding them is guarding their time and not neglecting them, and neglect is leaving their time (5378, 5379). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 168} They then differed over the middle one; some said it is the afternoon prayer, from 'Ali (5380). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 168} The editor says the chain of this report is stopped at 'Ali and very weak because of al-Harith al-A'war, and that the report comes from 'Ali by chains some of which are sound; this is the editor's grading, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 168}

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) who said the middle prayer is the afternoon prayer: Ibn 'Abbas, from a man the narrator does not name (5381); 'Ali b. Abi Talib by several chains (5382, 5383, 5384, 5385); in the report of Abu al-Sahba' al-Bakri he asked 'Ali, who said it is the afternoon prayer and it is the one that distracted Sulayman son of Dawud (5386). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 169} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 170} Abu Hurayra (5387, 5388, 5390). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 171} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 172} Ibn 'Umar reports from the Prophet that whoever misses the afternoon prayer is as if his family and property were taken from him, and Ibn 'Umar saw it as excellent for this reason, as being the middle one (5389, 5391). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (5392). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} Humayda, the freedwoman of 'A'isha, says she found in 'A'isha's codex an added gloss after the mention of the middle prayer saying it is the afternoon prayer (5393); Umm Humayd bint 'Abd al-Rahman from 'A'isha, that they used to read it in the first script in the Prophet's time with that addition (5394, 5395); 'A'isha, through al-Qasim b. The editor gives his gradings of the chains in the notes: report 5381 is weak because of the unnamed narrator; 5382 is at least good and 5383 sound as a corroboration; the reports through al-Harith al-A'war are weak; 5386 is sound once the narrators are identified; 5389 is very sound and its raised form is in Ahmad and the six books; 5392 is broken because al-Hasan did not hear from Abu Sa'id, who is also reported to have said it is the noon prayer and the afternoon prayer; Ibn Hazm transmits some of these in al-Muhalla; and the conjunction in some of 'A'isha's codex versions joins a description, not a different prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 169} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 174} These gradings and biographies are the editor's, not al-Tabari's.

The reports differ over whether a conjunction stands before the mention of the afternoon prayer in the version attributed to the codices, which the editor sets out; the view that the afternoon prayer is meant is given under the names of those who held it.

More of the reports on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) about the afternoon prayer: al-Hasan (5419). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 182} Al-Tabari then gives the grounds of those who held this view, the reports of the Battle of the Trench: from 'Abd Allah b. The report of al-Bara' is given as transmitted, a report of the abrogation of a wording, and the museum gives no verdict on it.

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) who said it is the afternoon prayer: al-Hasan, mursal from the Prophet (5441); a report from a man who, as a boy, was sent by Abu Bakr and 'Umar to ask the Prophet, who counted the prayers on his fingers and left the middle one, which is the afternoon (5442); al-Rabi', that the polytheists kept them on the day of the Confederates from the afternoon prayer until sunset (5443); 'Ali from the Prophet on his prayer against the polytheists (5444); Abu Malik al-Ash'ari from the Prophet, that it is the afternoon prayer (5445). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 197} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 198} The editor says 5441 is mursal and its sense sound from what came before; 5442 has an unknown chain in his view because he could not identify two narrators, and had they been known it would be a good report since the man sitting with 'Abd al-'Aziz b. Others said the middle one is the sunset prayer, from Qabisa b.
