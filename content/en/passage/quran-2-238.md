## Summary
Verse 238 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 238 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) begins on p. 167. Al-Tabari says the command to guard means keeping to the obligatory prayers at their times, tending and holding to them, and the middle one among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 167} Masruq: guarding them is guarding their time and not neglecting them, and neglect is leaving their time (5378, 5379). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 168} They then differed over the middle one; some said it is the afternoon prayer, from 'Ali (5380). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 168} The editor says the chain of this report is stopped at 'Ali and very weak because of al-Harith al-A'war, and that the report comes from 'Ali by chains some of which are sound; this is the editor's grading, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 168}

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) who said the middle prayer is the afternoon prayer: Ibn 'Abbas, from a man the narrator does not name (5381); 'Ali b. Abi Talib by several chains (5382, 5383, 5384, 5385); in the report of Abu al-Sahba' al-Bakri he asked 'Ali, who said it is the afternoon prayer and it is the one that distracted Sulayman son of Dawud (5386). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 169} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 170} Abu Hurayra (5387, 5388, 5390). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 171} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 172} Ibn 'Umar reports from the Prophet that whoever misses the afternoon prayer is as if his family and property were taken from him, and Ibn 'Umar saw it as excellent for this reason, as being the middle one (5389, 5391). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (5392). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} Humayda, the freedwoman of 'A'isha, says she found in 'A'isha's codex an added gloss after the mention of the middle prayer saying it is the afternoon prayer (5393); Umm Humayd bint 'Abd al-Rahman from 'A'isha, that they used to read it in the first script in the Prophet's time with that addition (5394, 5395); 'A'isha, through al-Qasim b. Muhammad, that it is the afternoon prayer (5396); Hisham b. 'Urwa from his father, that 'A'isha's codex had that addition (5397). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 174} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 175} These are reports of a reading attributed to 'A'isha's codex; the museum does not say it is part of the Qur'an, and the reports themselves describe it as an addition or explanation.

The editor gives his gradings of the chains in the notes: report 5381 is weak because of the unnamed narrator; 5382 is at least good and 5383 sound as a corroboration; the reports through al-Harith al-A'war are weak; 5386 is sound once the narrators are identified; 5389 is very sound and its raised form is in Ahmad and the six books; 5392 is broken because al-Hasan did not hear from Abu Sa'id, who is also reported to have said it is the noon prayer and the afternoon prayer; Ibn Hazm transmits some of these in al-Muhalla; and the conjunction in some of 'A'isha's codex versions joins a description, not a different prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 169} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 174} These gradings and biographies are the editor's, not al-Tabari's.

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) who said the middle one is the afternoon prayer: Umm Salama, who told 'Abd Allah b. Rafi' to write a codex for her and to let her know when he reached the verse about prayer, and then dictated to him the same explanatory addition (5398). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 176} Al-Hasan (5399). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 176} 'A'isha through Abu Ayyub (5400, 5401); Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (5402); al-Rabi' from 'Ali (5403, 5411); Sa'id b. The editor writes in the notes: the narrators of 5398 are reliable and 'Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Abi Dawud give it with a conjunction in some chains; 5399 has a weak, unknown chain; 5405 is broken between Abu Bishr and Salim; 5406 is broken because Nafi' relayed from Hafsa as a mursal, and he corrects an earlier grading of his own on that account; Thuwayr in 5414 is very weak; 5417 is good, al-Tirmidhi called it good and elsewhere sound, and there is a long discussion over al-Hasan's hearing from Samura, the preferred view being that he heard from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 176} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 177} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 178} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 180} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 181} These are the editor's gradings, not al-Tabari's. The reports differ over whether a conjunction stands before the mention of the afternoon prayer in the version attributed to the codices, which the editor sets out; the view that the afternoon prayer is meant is given under the names of those who held it.

More of the reports on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) about the afternoon prayer: al-Hasan (5419). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 182} Al-Tabari then gives the grounds of those who held this view, the reports of the Battle of the Trench: from 'Abd Allah b. Mas'ud that the polytheists kept the Prophet from the afternoon prayer until the sun turned yellow or red, and he called on God to fill their bellies and graves with fire because they had kept him from the prayer (5420, 5421, 5430). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 182} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 183} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 188} From 'Ali, that the Prophet said on the day of the Confederates that they kept him from the prayer until the sun had set and called down the same on them, with a doubt in Shu'ba's report between houses and bellies (5422); from 'Ali that they used to think it the dawn prayer until he heard the Prophet on the day of the Confederates say what shows it is the afternoon prayer (5423); from Shutayr b. Shakal from 'Ali (5424, 5426), in which, in 5426, he then prayed it between the two night prayers, meaning between the sunset and the evening prayer; from Yahya b. al-Jazzar from 'Ali that the Prophet said it at a gap in the trench (5425); from 'Ubayda al-Salmani from 'Ali that the Prophet did not pray the afternoon prayer on the day of the Trench until after sunset (5427); a report of Zirr b. Hubaysh from 'Ali that this happened during the fighting with the people of Khaybar, which the editor calls irregular because all the sound reports place it at the Confederates (5428); from 'Ali by way of Qatada (5429); and through Masruq and Talha from Murra, that the Prophet said it on the day of the Trench (5431). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 183} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 184} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 185} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 186} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 187} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 188} The editor gives the sources of these reports in Muslim, Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah, al-Bukhari and others, and says that some of the chains are sound and some weak, such as 5431 for being a mursal and for the weakness of Sahl b. 'Amir; these are his gradings, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 183} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 184} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 185} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 186} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 188}

More reports on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) about the afternoon prayer: Abu Hurayra, raised to the Prophet, that it is the afternoon prayer (5432); Ibn 'Abbas, that the Prophet went out on a campaign and the polytheists held him from the afternoon prayer until evening, and he called on God to fill their houses and bellies with fire (5433), with another chain from him that this was on the day of the Confederates until the sun set (5434, 5435). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 190} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 191} Kuhayl b. Harmala relates that Abu Hurayra was asked about it and said: we disagreed over it as you have, at the courtyard of the Prophet's house, and among us was Abu Hashim b. 'Utba, who asked leave and went in and came out and told us it is the afternoon prayer (5436). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 191} Al-Bara' b. 'Azib, by a chain through Shaqiq b. 'Uqba, said that the verse was first revealed with wording that names the afternoon prayer outright after the prayers, and they recited it so in the Prophet's time as long as God willed, then God abrogated it and sent down the wording that names the middle one instead; a man with Shaqiq asked, then is it the afternoon prayer, and he said, I have told you how it was revealed and how God abrogated it, and God knows best (5437). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 193} Samura from the Prophet, that it is the afternoon prayer (5438, 5439). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 194} Umm Habiba from the Prophet on the day of the Trench (5440). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 195}

The editor says in the notes: report 5432 is raised to the Prophet with a sound chain, and the raising by a reliable narrator is accepted; the narrators of 5433 are reliable and Ahmad relates it; Ibn Abi Layla's report 5434 is open to criticism but has other routes; the narrator Sulayman al-Jurashi of 5436 is weak but not alone in it and Ibn Kathir called it very strange by this route; report 5437 is in Muslim's collection and the editor corrects the printed form of a verb of reciting; report 5440 has a sound chain on Muslim's standard, which he found in no other source, and he discusses whether the narrator was Hafsa and not Umm Habiba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 189} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 190} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 191} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 192} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 193} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 195} These are the editor's, not al-Tabari's. The report of al-Bara' is given as transmitted, a report of the abrogation of a wording, and the museum gives no verdict on it.

More of those on ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) who said it is the afternoon prayer: al-Hasan, mursal from the Prophet (5441); a report from a man who, as a boy, was sent by Abu Bakr and 'Umar to ask the Prophet, who counted the prayers on his fingers and left the middle one, which is the afternoon (5442); al-Rabi', that the polytheists kept them on the day of the Confederates from the afternoon prayer until sunset (5443); 'Ali from the Prophet on his prayer against the polytheists (5444); Abu Malik al-Ash'ari from the Prophet, that it is the afternoon prayer (5445). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 197} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 198} The editor says 5441 is mursal and its sense sound from what came before; 5442 has an unknown chain in his view because he could not identify two narrators, and had they been known it would be a good report since the man sitting with 'Abd al-'Aziz b. Marwan would be a Companion; Ibn Kathir called it very strange; the narrator of 5444 is very weak but the report came earlier with a sound chain; 5445 has a weak chain though Ibn Kathir said it is acceptable; and he defends Shu'ba's transmission of the Umm Habiba report and notes that al-Tabari took care to cite his teacher Abu Musa's remark. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 197} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 198} These are the editor's, not al-Tabari's.
