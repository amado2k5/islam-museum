From Abu Basra al-Ghifari, that the Prophet led them in the afternoon prayer and said that it was prescribed for those before you, who became lax and left it, and whoever prays it among you has his reward doubled, and there is no prayer after it until the Witness appears, meaning the star (5493); in another report they were at al-Mukhammas and whoever guards it is given its reward twice (5494). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 223} From Buraydah, the Prophet said pray early on a cloudy day, for whoever misses the afternoon prayer, his work comes to nothing (5495). Al-Tabari adds, without a chain, the Prophet's saying that whoever misses the afternoon prayer is as if robbed of family and property (5496), and his saying that whoever prays before sunrise and before sunset will not enter the Fire (5497). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 223} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 224} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 226} The editor notes that 5493 is in Muslim and Ahmad; he corrects the spelling of the place name and supplies two narrators missing from the chain of 5495 so that it runs; that al-Bukhari relates the report with a chain differing from al-Awza'i's; that Ibn Kathir took al-Awza'i's as sounder, which he thinks a slip; and that 5497 is in Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 222} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 223} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 224} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 225} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 226} These are the editor's gradings and estimates, not al-Tabari's.

Al-Tabari then says the Prophet urged guarding it with an urging he gave to no other prayer, though guarding all of them is required, so it is plain that the one God singled out for urging after commanding all the obligatory prayers is the one the Prophet followed him in, singling it out beyond the others, warning his community against losing it as earlier nations did, and promising a double reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 226} He then gives his own surmise, not a report: God made the night for rest, when people are quiet and free to guard God's obligations, and the dawn is a time when few go out for earning; noon is the time of people's midday rest; there are two daytime periods known for livelihood, the first from morning to the midday heat, when God lightened the burden and did not make a prayer obligatory though He urged the forenoon prayer, and the second at the end of the day, in which the afternoon prayer is obligatory and He urged guarding it lest they lose it by preferring the means of this world; he says he will expand on this in his larger book on the rulings of the laws. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 226} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 227}

He explains the name of the middle one as being in the middle of the five obligatory prayers, with two before it and two after, a feminine form of the word for middlemost, from the saying I went into the middle of the people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 227} The discussion of the middle prayer ends here, and on p. 228 he begins the last clause of ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) on standing before God in devotion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 228}

Al-Tabari begins the last clause of ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]) by saying that interpreters differed over the meaning of devotion in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 228} Some said it is obedience, the sense being that they stand to God in their prayer obedient to what He ordered and forbade there: al-Sha'bi (5498, 5499); Jabir b. Zayd (5500); 'Ata' (5501); Sa'id b. Jubayr (5502, 5503, 5510); al-Dahhak, that the word in the Qur'an means obedience and that the people of every religion stand to God disobedient, so let Muslims stand obedient (5504 to 5507); Ibn 'Abbas (5508, 5509, 5517); al-Hasan (5511); Mujahid (5512, 5513); Qatada (5514); 'Atiyya, that they used to ask for their needs in prayer until the verse came down and they left speaking, and that obedient is meant (5515, 5516); a report from the Prophet through Abu Sa'id that every occurrence of the word in the Qur'an means obedience (5518); Sa'id b. 'Abd al-'Aziz (5519); Tawus (5520). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 228} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 229} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 231} The editor says the chain of 5518 includes Dirraj, Abu al-Haytham and Ibn Lahi'a, that al-Haythami weakened it on account of Ibn Lahi'a, and that the editor does not consider Ibn Lahi'a weak; that is his judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 231}

Others said that in this verse the word means silence, the sense being that they stand to God silent about what God forbade them to say in their prayer: al-Suddi (5521); 'Abd Allah b. Mas'ud, that they used to speak in prayer and a man asked his neighbour about his need, until he came, greeted them and they did not reply, and when the Prophet finished his prayer he said that what kept him from returning the greeting was that they had been ordered to stand silent in prayer (5522); from him also, that the Prophet did not reply to his greeting and when he finished said that God had brought a new ruling that no one should speak in prayer (5523); Zayd b. Arqam, that they used to talk in prayer in the Prophet's time until the verse came down and they were ordered to be silent (5524); 'Ikrima, that they used to speak and were forbidden speech (5525); 'Abd Allah b. Mas'ud by another chain, that the Prophet said God has brought a new ruling that none should speak in prayer except in remembrance of God and fitting praise and glorification (5526); Ibn Zayd, that the one in devotion is the person praying who does not speak (5527). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 231} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 232} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 233} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 234} The editor says the chain of 5523 is very weak; that 5524 is in al-Bukhari, Muslim and Ahmad; that the chain of 5526 is sound; and that the underlying meaning from Ibn Mas'ud is established in the Musnad and the two Sahihs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 232} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 233} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 234} These are the editor's gradings, not al-Tabari's.

Others said that here the word means stillness and humility in prayer, the sense being that they stand humble, lowering their wings, not fidgeting or playing: Mujahid, that devotion includes a long bowing, lowering the eyes, lowering the wing and humility from awe of God, and that the scholars stood in awe of the Merciful, not turning, nor turning pebbles, nor toying with anything, nor talking to themselves of worldly matters except by forgetfulness (5528). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 234}

More of the views on the meaning of devotion in ([[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]]): Mujahid, that devotion includes stillness, humility and lowering the wing from awe of God, and that the jurists among the Prophet's Companions, when one stood to pray, did not turn, turn pebbles or speak to himself of worldly matters except by forgetting (5529, 5530, 5531); al-Rabi', that devotion is stillness, meaning standing in prayer and holding oneself upright for it (5532). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 235} Others said it means supplication here, the sense being that they stand to God longing in their prayer, with the report of Abu Raja' from Ibn 'Abbas that he made the standing supplication before bowing in the dawn prayer (5533); the editor thinks "supplicating" would be a better word, though "longing" is sound in meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 235}

Al-Tabari then makes his choice: the soundest view is that it means obedient, because the root of the word is obedience, and obedience in prayer may be keeping silent about the speech God forbade there, which is why some took the meaning as silence, except for reciting the Qur'an or remembering God as He deserves, and he cites the saying of al-Nakha'i and Mujahid that they used to speak in prayer, then the verse came and they cut off speech, and that the word is silence and the word is obedience (5534). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 236} Obedience may also be humility, lowering the wing, a long standing and supplication, since each of these is either what the person praying is ordered or what is recommended, and in all of them the servant is obedient to God; the word's root is obedience to God and it is then used for whatever the servant obeys God by. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 236} The sense of the clause for him is that those praying stand in prayer obedient to God, by not talking to one another and by leaving other speech aside from reciting the Qur'an, remembering God or supplicating, not disobeying by neglecting their limits or falling short of what is due. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 236} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 237} This is al-Tabari's choice; the other views are given under their own names.
