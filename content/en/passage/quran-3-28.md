## Summary
Verse 28 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Verse 28 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 313-314)

**The prohibition of taking unbelievers as supporters.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-28|3:28]]) says this is God's prohibition to the believers against taking the unbelievers as helpers, supporters and backers. He explains that the verb is vowelled with a kasra because it is in a jussive position after a prohibition, the kasra on the last letter being for the meeting of two quiet letters; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. Al-Tabari gives the sense: do not befriend the unbelievers in their religion, back them against the Muslims, or point out the Muslims' weak points; whoever does so has, in his view, disowned God and been disowned by God through apostasy from his religion and entry into unbelief. The exception he explains as the case of those under the unbelievers' authority who fear for themselves: such a person shows them friendship with the tongue and hides enmity, does not follow them in their unbelief, and does not help them against a Muslim by any act. The editor refers in his notes to earlier discussions of 'wali' and 'awliya'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 313}

**The reports.** Al-Muthanna, from Abdullah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya ibn Salih, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas (6825), says God forbade the believers to be gentle with the unbelievers or to take them as intimates in preference to the believers, unless the unbelievers are dominant over them, in which case they show them kindness while differing from them in religion. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, from Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas (6826) gives an occasion: al-Hajjaj ibn Amr, an ally of Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, with Ibn Abi al-Huqayq and Qays ibn Zayd, had become intimate with some Ansar men to turn them from their religion; Rifa'a ibn al-Mundhir ibn Zanbar, Abdullah ibn Jubayr and Sa'd ibn Khaythama told those men to avoid these Jews and beware of keeping company with them, but the men refused, and the verse was sent down. The editor says he did not find this report in the Sira of Ibn Hisham as we have it, explains the phrase 'became intimate with' as being close to a person and aware of his private affairs, and corrects 'Zubayr' in the printed text to 'Zanbar' from Ibn Hisham and al-Isaba. Al-Hasan, via Muhammad ibn Sinan (6827), says the believer does not make an unbeliever his friend in preference to the believers; the editor refers to his note at 6822. Al-Suddi, via Musa, Amr and Asbat (6828), says the allegiance meant is to ally with them in their religion and show them the weak points of the believers, and that whoever does this is a polytheist and God has disowned him, unless he guards himself by outwardly showing allegiance in their religion while disowning the believers. These statements are given as transmitted, with no verdict from the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 314}

**What was said about the exception.** Al-Muthanna, from Qabisa ibn Uqba, from Sufyan, from Ibn Jurayj, from someone who told him, from Ibn Abbas (6829), says the word means speaking with the tongue while the heart is at rest in faith. Al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Hafs ibn Umar, from al-Hakam ibn Aban, from Ikrima (6830), says it holds so long as no Muslim's blood is shed and his property is not treated as lawful. From Mujahid (6831-6832) it is conciliation in worldly matters and good manners; the editor explains the verb used as living with people according to their character, shaping oneself to them and being agreeable. Al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi (6833), says Abu al-Aliyah held that precaution is by the tongue and not by deed. Al-Dahhak (6834) and, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd's chain of his father, uncle, father and grandfather, Ibn Abbas (6835) say that precaution is by the tongue: whoever is compelled to say something that is disobedience to God and says it for fear for himself while his heart is at rest in faith, no harm comes to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 315}

**The second view: ties of kinship.** Al-Tabari reports that others said the sense is: unless there is kinship between you and the unbeliever. Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada (6836), says God forbade the believers to love the unbelievers or take them as allies in preference to the believers, and that the exception concerns kin among the polytheists, without allying with them in their religion, so that he may keep ties with a relative among them. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada (6837), says it is not lawful for a believer to take an unbeliever as an ally in his religion, and the exception is that there is kinship between them and he keeps the tie. Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan (6838), says: keep their company in this world with kindness, with kin and others, but not in religion. The editor notes that the printed text has one form of the word where the manuscript, by a pen stroke, has the other form with a doubled letter, which he adopted, it being one of the two readings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 316}

**Al-Tabari's weighing.** Al-Tabari says Qatada's explanation has a possible aspect but is not the one the apparent wording of the verse points to; the more common sense of the expression is 'unless you fear them', so the precaution God mentions is precaution against the unbelievers and not others. Qatada took it to mean guarding oneself against God for the sake of kinship and so keeping ties, but that is not the dominant sense, and interpretation in the Qur'an follows the dominant apparent sense of Arab usage. This is al-Tabari's weighing, and the statements are given as transmitted with no museum verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 317}

**The two readings.** Al-Tabari says most reciters of the cities read the word on the pattern 'fu'ala', like 'tukhama', 'tu'ada' and 'tuka'a', from 'ittaqaytu', while others read it on the pattern 'fa'ila'. He chooses the first reading, since its proof is established by widespread transmission that excludes error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 317}

**The warning and the return.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is that God makes the believers fear His punishment, should they commit disobedience or ally with His enemies, for to Him is their return after death and on the day of gathering for reckoning; when they come to Him having gone against His commands and done what He forbade, including taking unbelievers as allies, they will meet a punishment they cannot bear, so they should guard themselves. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word for 'destination'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 317}
