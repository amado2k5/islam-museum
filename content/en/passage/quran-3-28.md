## Verse 28 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 313-314)

**The prohibition of taking unbelievers as supporters.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-28|3:28]]) says this is God's prohibition to the believers against taking the unbelievers as helpers, supporters and backers. He explains that the verb is vowelled with a kasra because it is in a jussive position after a prohibition, the kasra on the last letter being for the meeting of two quiet letters; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. Al-Tabari gives the sense: do not befriend the unbelievers in their religion, back them against the Muslims, or point out the Muslims' weak points; whoever does so has, in his view, disowned God and been disowned by God through apostasy from his religion and entry into unbelief. The exception he explains as the case of those under the unbelievers' authority who fear for themselves: such a person shows them friendship with the tongue and hides enmity, does not follow them in their unbelief, and does not help them against a Muslim by any act. The editor refers in his notes to earlier discussions of 'wali' and 'awliya'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 313}

**The reports.** Al-Muthanna, from Abdullah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya ibn Salih, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas (6825), says God forbade the believers to be gentle with the unbelievers or to take them as intimates in preference to the believers, unless the unbelievers are dominant over them, in which case they show them kindness while differing from them in religion. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, from Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas (6826) gives an occasion: al-Hajjaj ibn Amr, an ally of Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, with Ibn Abi al-Huqayq and Qays ibn Zayd, had become intimate with some Ansar men to turn them from their religion; Rifa'a ibn al-Mundhir ibn Zanbar, Abdullah ibn Jubayr and Sa'd ibn Khaythama told those men to avoid these Jews and beware of keeping company with them, but the men refused, and the verse was sent down. The editor says he did not find this report in the Sira of Ibn Hisham as we have it, explains the phrase 'became intimate with' as being close to a person and aware of his private affairs, and corrects 'Zubayr' in the printed text to 'Zanbar' from Ibn Hisham and al-Isaba. Al-Hasan, via Muhammad ibn Sinan (6827), says the believer does not make an unbeliever his friend in preference to the believers; the editor refers to his note at 6822. Al-Suddi, via Musa, Amr and Asbat (6828), says the allegiance meant is to ally with them in their religion and show them the weak points of the believers, and that whoever does this is a polytheist and God has disowned him, unless he guards himself by outwardly showing allegiance in their religion while disowning the believers. These statements are given as transmitted, with no verdict from the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 314}
