## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "or were you witnesses when death came to Jacob" al-Tabari says "am" is a question beginning anew after earlier speech, as in "Alif Lam Mim. The sending down of the Book, no doubt in it... or do they say he has invented it", and that "shuhada'" is the plural of "shahid". The sense for him: were you, company of Jews and Christians who deny Muhammad and reject his prophethood, present and witnesses when death came to Jacob? That is, you were not present at that, so do not falsely claim things of God's prophets and messengers and ascribe Judaism and Christianity to them, for God sent His friend Abraham, his sons Isaac and Ishmael and their descendants with the upright, submitting faith, and with it they charged their sons. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 97}

Al-Tabari says these verses came down as God's denial of the claim of the Jews and Christians about Abraham and his son Jacob, that they were of their religion, so He said to them "or were you witnesses when death came to Jacob", so that you would know what he said to his sons and they to him; from al-Rabi', the people of the Book are meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 97} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 98}

On "when he said to his sons: what will you worship after me" he says the second "when" repeats and replaces the first, and "what will you worship after me" means what will you worship after my death? They said "we shall worship your God and the God of your fathers, Abraham, Ishmael and Isaac, one God", meaning we shall be sincere to Him in worship and affirm His Lordship as one, associating nothing with Him. "And to Him we are submitting" means humble to Him in servitude and obedience; it can be a circumstantial clause, we worship Him as ones submitting, or a new statement, we are now and always submitting to Him, and he holds the first is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 98} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 99}

It was said that Ishmael is named before Isaac because he was older, from Ibn Zayd. Some early readers read "and the God of your father Abraham", supposing that since Ishmael was Jacob's uncle he could not be counted among his fathers; al-Tabari says this is from little knowledge of how the Arabs speak, for they do not hesitate to use paternal uncles in the sense of fathers, and the right reading for him is "the God of your fathers", on the consensus of the readers. "Ilahan" is accusative as a circumstantial of "ilahaka". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 100}
