**Commentary on verses 13-14 (pp. 539-542)**

On "those who disbelieved said to their messengers: we shall surely drive you from our land, or you shall return to our creed; so their Lord revealed to them: We shall surely destroy the wrongdoers, and We shall settle you in the land after them; that is for whoever fears My standing and fears My threat", al-Tabari says, mighty be His mention: those who disbelieved in God said to their messengers who were sent to them, when they called them to God's oneness, to devoting worship to Him and to leaving the worship of gods and idols, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 539}

"we shall surely drive you from our land", they mean from our country, expelling you from it, "or you shall return to our creed", they mean unless you return to our religion, which we hold, of worshipping idols. A lam entered in "la-ta'udunna", being in the sense of a condition, as if an answer to the oath; the sense of the speech is: we shall drive you from our land or you return to our creed. The sense of "aw" here is that of "illa" or "hatta", as it is said in speech "la-adribannaka aw tuqirra li", I shall strike you unless you admit to me. Some of the Arabs make what follows "aw" in such a place a conjunction on what precedes: if what precedes is jussive they make it jussive, if accusative accusative, and if it has a lam they put a lam in it, since "aw" is a conjunction; and some of them put what follows "aw" in the accusative in every case, so it is known from its accusative that it is cut off from what precedes, as Imru' al-Qays said in a verse in which his companion wept on seeing the pass before him, certain that they were headed for Caesar, and he said to him, let your eye not weep, we are seeking a kingdom or we will die and be excused; he put "namut fa-nu'dhara" in the accusative while "nuhawil" is in the nominative, because he meant "unless we die" or "until we die"; and so the saying of another, I cannot give up loving her unless love does to me other than what it did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 540}

On "so their Lord revealed to them: We shall surely destroy the wrongdoers", he means those who wronged themselves and so made God's punishment due for themselves by their disbelief. It is also possible that they were called "the wrongdoers" for worshipping what it is not allowed to worship of idols and gods, so that by putting worship where it does not belong, which is wrong, they were so named. On "and We shall settle you in the land after them", this is a promise from God to those of His prophets who were promised victory over the disbelievers of their peoples. He says: when the nations of the messengers persisted in disbelief and threatened their messengers with harming them, God revealed to them the destruction of those of their nations who disbelieved in them and promised them victory. All that was from God a warning and a threat to the polytheists of our Prophet Muhammad's people for their disbelief in him and their boldness against his Prophet, a strengthening for Muhammad and a bidding to patience over what he met of unpleasantness in it from the polytheists of his people, as those before him, the resolute of His messengers, were patient, and letting him know that the end of whoever disbelieves in him is destruction and his end is victory over them, which is God's way with those who passed before. Report 20611, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and We shall settle you in the land after them", he said, He promised them victory in the world and Paradise in the hereafter. On "that is for whoever fears My standing and fears My threat" He says, mighty be His praise: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 541}

so is My doing for whoever fears his standing before Me and fears My threat and so fears Me by obeying Him and avoids My wrath: I help him against those of My enemies who wished him ill and sought unpleasantness for him, destroy his enemy, disgrace him and make him inherit his land and homes. He said "for whoever fears My standing", and its meaning is as I said, that it is for whoever fears his standing before Me, where I make him stand for reckoning, as He said "and you make your provision that you deny" [[passage/quran-56-82|56:82]], meaning "you make your being provided by Me that you deny"; for the Arabs ascribe their acts to themselves and to what they were done to, saying "I was glad at seeing you" and "at my seeing you"; so it is here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 542}
