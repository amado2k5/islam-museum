## Summary
Verse 88 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 88 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Opening commentary (p. 7, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari explains the clause as: what is the matter with you, believers, that you have become two differing camps over the people of hypocrisy, when God returned them to the rulings applied to idolaters, namely that their blood may be shed and their children taken captive. The word for casting back means returning, and he cites a line of Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt. The editor says the line is not in the poet's collection in exactly this form, that the collection has a line in a different metre, and that he found nothing with Abu Ja'far's wording elsewhere. Al-Tabari notes that either verb form may be used, and that a reading without the initial alif is reported from Abd Allah and Ubayy. The editor points to the earlier treatment of the word for a party, to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, and to a later passage of this volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 7}

**The occasion: five views (pp. 8-13).** First view (pp. 8-9, notes 1-2): the verse concerns a dispute among the Prophet's Companions about those who turned back from Uhud to Medina, the people of [[passage/quran-3-167|3:167]]. Report 10049 (al-Fadl ibn Ziyad al-Wasiti, from Abu Dawud, from Shu'ba, from Adi ibn Thabit, from Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-Ansari, from Zayd ibn Thabit): when the Prophet set out for Uhud a group turned back; the Companions split into two camps, one saying "kill them" and the other "no"; the verse came down, and the Prophet said Medina is Tayba and expels its impurity as fire expels the dross of silver. Report 10050 (Abu Kurayb, from Abu Usama, from Shu'ba by the same chain) is similar, and 10051 (Zurayq ibn al-Sakhtal, from Shababa, from Adi ibn Thabit by the same chain) says that the hypocrites were mentioned before the Prophet and two groups disagreed. The editor says he does not know who al-Fadl ibn Ziyad al-Wasiti is (the nearest he found is al-Fadl ibn Ziyad al-Tassas al-Baghdadi), identifies Abu Dawud as al-Tayalisi and Abu Usama as Hamad ibn Usama, and found Zurayq only in al-Dhahabi's al-Mushtabih. He judges that a link has dropped out of the chain of 10051 before Adi ibn Thabit: Shababa ibn Sawwar died in the early 200s and Adi in 116, some ninety years apart, and the likely missing words are "from Shu'ba". He calls Adi reliable and well known, and the grandson of Abd Allah ibn Yazid al-Khatmi, a Companion who attended al-Hudaybiyya as a boy. He traces the report to Ahmad's Musnad, al-Bukhari, Muslim (who has only the closing words about Tayba), Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, and notes that it is absent from the printed Musnad of al-Tayalisi because that edition is incomplete. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 13}

Second view (pp. 9-11, notes 1-3): people came from Mecca to Medina, showed Islam, then went back to Mecca and showed idolatry. Report 10052 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid: they left Mecca claiming to be emigrants, then apostatized and asked the Prophet's leave to go to Mecca for merchandise. The believers disagreed about them; God exposed their hypocrisy and ordered fighting them. They returned with their goods heading for Medina and were met by Ali ibn Uwaymir or Hilal ibn Uwaymir al-Aslami, who had an alliance with the Prophet and was the one whose chest was constricted at fighting either the believers or his own people; he shielded them by saying they were making for Hilal, with whom the Prophet had a treaty. Report 10053 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid is similar and adds that they were not fought that day. The editor says the printed edition dropped part of the report, which he restored from the manuscript, and that the manuscript and al-Durr al-Manthur have the correct wording in one clause.

Third view (pp. 10-11): the people were idolaters who showed Islam in Mecca and helped the idolaters against the Muslims. Report 10054 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather) from Ibn Abbas: they had spoken the words of Islam in Mecca while backing the idolaters, left on an errand and said there would be no harm if the Prophet's Companions met them. When the believers heard, one party said to ride out and kill them for aiding the enemy, and the other asked whether people who said what they said should be killed for not having emigrated, their blood and property made lawful. The Prophet was with them and forbade neither side, and the verse came down. Report 10055 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: two Qurayshi men with the idolaters in Mecca had spoken of Islam without emigrating; some Companions met them and disputed whether their blood and property were lawful; the verse came down, up to a clause in [[passage/quran-4-90|4:90]]. Report 10056 (al-Qasim, from Abu Sufyan, from Ma'mar ibn Rashid, who says "it reached me") says people of Mecca wrote to the Prophet that they had become Muslims, falsely, and the Muslims differed over whether their blood was lawful. Report 10057 (a narrator al-Tabari does not name, from al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd ibn Sulayman) from al-Dahhak: people stayed behind in Mecca, announced belief and did not emigrate; some Companions befriended them and others disowned them; God named them hypocrites, cleared the believers of befriending them and told them not to do so until they emigrated.

Fourth view (p. 12, notes 1-4): people of Medina. Report 10058 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: some hypocrites wished to leave Medina, saying illnesses had struck them there and the place did not agree with them, and that they might go out to the open country until they recovered and then return. The Companions then disagreed: one party called them hypocrites and enemies of God whom they longed to fight if the Prophet allowed it, the other said they were brothers distressed by Medina who had gone out to the open air to recover. On pp. 12-13 the editor glosses the verb for finding the place unhealthy, saying the dictionaries list a related form but not this one, though it is sound by the rules of Arabic; he explains the terms for raised ground and low ground; and he says the printed edition changed a phrase in the report, while the unpointed manuscript reading, supported by al-Farra', is correct.

Fifth view (p. 13, notes 1-2): the affair of the slander. Report 10059 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd: the verse concerns Ibn Ubayy when he said what he said about Aisha (see [[passage/quran-24-11|24:11]]). Report 10060, also from Ibn Zayd: when it came down, Sa'd ibn Mu'adh said he disowned before God and His Messenger the party of Abd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. The editor says the printed edition merged 10059 and 10060 and dropped the opening of the second; he restored it from the manuscript and blames al-Suyuti's way of quoting al-Tabari in al-Durr al-Manthur.

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 13-14).** Al-Tabari holds, as his own view, that the most correct opinion is that the verse concerns the Companions' disagreement over a group of Meccans who apostatized after having become Muslims. His reasoning: the views reduce to two, that they were from Mecca or from Medina, and the instruction against befriending them until they emigrate is the clearest proof that they were not from Medina, since emigration in the Prophet's time meant going from the lands of disbelief to his home and city, whereas a hypocrite or idolater living in the abode of emigration was at home and under no duty to emigrate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 14}

**The grammar of the accusative (pp. 14-15, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari reports that the grammarians differed on why the word for the two camps is in the accusative. Some Basrans say it is a circumstantial clause, like "what is the matter with you, standing?". Some Kufan grammarians say it is governed by the verb sense in the phrase and that it does not matter whether the accusative is definite or indefinite; they allow "what is the matter with you, the one travelling with us?" because the construction is like the verbs that take an accusative complement, such as kana and zanna. The editor says this is an abridgement of al-Farra' in his Ma'ani and prefers a manuscript reading in one phrase. Al-Tabari sides with the Kufan view, his own preference, because what is being asked about in such a phrase is the standing, which puts it with kana and its sisters and zanna and its sisters; the editor says the printed text is better than the manuscript in one of his phrases. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 15}

**The meaning of casting back (pp. 15-16, note 1).** The commentators differed. Some said it means returned them, as al-Tabari does: report 10061 (al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Ata' al-Khurasani, from Ibn Abbas. Others said it means made them fall, as in 10062 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah, from Mu'awiya, from Ali ibn Abi Talha) from Ibn Abbas. Others said it means led them astray and destroyed them: 10063 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Abu Sufyan, from Ma'mar) and 10064 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar), both from Qatada, and 10065 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi. Al-Tabari says he has already explained the word earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 16}

**The question about guiding and the path (p. 16, notes 2-3).** Al-Tabari reads the question as addressed to the party who defended these hypocrites: do you seek the guidance of people whom God abandoned and kept from the truth and from following Islam, by blocking believers who wish to fight them? He says that for anyone God has abandoned in his religion and in acknowledging God and His Prophet, the Prophet will find no path by which to guide him to what he was kept from and no way to reach what he was denied. The editor refers to the language indexes for the meanings of guidance, error and path and says a bracketed addition is required by the context. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 16}


These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 16}
