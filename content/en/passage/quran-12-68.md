**Commentary on verse 68 (pp. 167-168)**

On "and when they entered from where their father had told them, it did not avail them anything against God, except for a need in Ya'qub's soul which he fulfilled; he was possessed of knowledge because We had taught him, but most people do not know", al-Tabari says: when Ya'qub's sons entered as their father told them, that is, entering Egypt by different gates, their entering it so did not avail them anything against God's decree which He had decided among them and made binding, except for a need in Ya'qub's soul which he fulfilled, that is, they satisfied a wish of Ya'qub by not entering by one road for fear of the eye on them, so his soul was reassured. Reports 19495 to 19498, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; and Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr and Waraqa, all from Ibn Abi Najih: fear of the eye on his sons. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 167}

Report 19499, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "a need in Ya'qub's soul which he fulfilled" is what he feared for his sons from people's eyes, because of their appearance and number. On "he was possessed of knowledge because We had taught him", he says: Ya'qub had knowledge by Our teaching him. It is also said that it means he was keeper of the knowledge We had lodged in his heart. There is a difference reported from Qatada: report 19500, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: that is, of what We taught him; report 19501, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Ibn Abi Arouba: he acted on what he knew; report 19502, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Sufyan: "he had knowledge of what We taught him", and he said that one who does not act is not a scholar. "But most people do not know", he says: but many people other than Ya'qub do not know what he knows, because We withheld that from them and they did not learn it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 168}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
