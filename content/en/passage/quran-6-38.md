## Summary
Verse 38 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 38 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "there is no creature on the earth nor bird flying with its wings but they are communities like you; we have neglected nothing in the book; then to their Lord they will be gathered" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say to these who turn from you and deny God's signs: people, do not suppose that God is heedless of what you do or will not repay you for what you earn! How could he be heedless of your deeds or leave off repaying you for them when he is not heedless of the deed of anything that crawls on earth, small or great, nor the act of any bird that flies on its wings in the air, but made all that classes classified and kinds sorted, known as you are known and acting in what they were made to serve as you do, with what they do for themselves or against themselves kept for them and all written down of their deeds in the mother of the book, then he will make them die and then raise and repay them on the day of resurrection for their deeds. He says: the Lord who did not waste keeping the deeds of beasts and creatures on earth and birds in the air, keeping their movements and acts and recording it in the mother of the book, gathering them and repaying them for what they did in the abode of trial, is the likelier not to waste your deeds or neglect to keep your acts that you commit, people, until he gathers you and repays you for them all, good with good and evil with evil, since he has singled you out with favours and spread on you of his bounty what he did not spread on others in the world, and you are the more entitled to thank him and the readier to know what is owed him, because of the reason he gave you, by which you distinguish things, and the understanding he did not give the beasts and the birds, by which you tell your benefits from your harms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 344}

The editor refers for "creature" to p. 275 of volume 3 and for "community" to volume 10.

The interpreters said so: reports 13211 and 13212, from Mujahid, on "communities like you": kinds sorted, known by their names. 13213, from Qatada: birds are a community, men a community, jinn a community. 13214, from al-Suddi: only creatures like you. 13215, from Ibn Jurayj: the ant and what is above it of the kinds of creatures God made. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 345}

As for "we have neglected nothing in the book", it means we did not waste the recording of anything in it; report 13216, from Ibn Abbas: we left nothing but we wrote it in the mother of the book. 13217, from Ibn Zayd: we did not leave the book heedless; there is nothing but it is in the book. 13218, from him: all of them are written in the mother of the book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 345}

The editor notes that the printed text dropped "the book" in 13217, which stands in the manuscript.

As for "then to their Lord they will be gathered", the interpreters differed on the meaning of their gathering that God means here. Some said their gathering is their death. 13219, from Ibn Abbas: the death of the beasts is their gathering. 13220, from him: by gathering he means death. 13221, from al-Dahhak: by gathering he means death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 346}

Others said gathering here means the assembly for the raising at the Hour and the resurrection. Report 13222, from Abu Hurayra: God will gather all creation on the day of resurrection, beasts, animals, birds and everything, and it will come to such justice of God that day that he takes for the hornless from the horned, then says "be dust"; so the unbeliever says, [[passage/quran-78-40|78:40]], "would that I were dust". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 347}

In his notes the editor identifies Ja'far ibn Burqan al-Kilabi, trusted, and Yazid ibn al-Asamm, a trusted Follower, says al-Hakim related it in the Mustadrak and called it sound by Muslim's condition, al-Dhahabi agreeing, traces it to Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti's Durr al-Manthur, and explains "jamma'" as the sheep without horns and "qarna'" as the one with large horns.

Report 13223, from Abu Dharr: while I was with the Messenger of God... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 347}

The rest of 13223: while I was with the Messenger of God two she-goats butted one another, and the Messenger of God said: do you know why they butted? They said: we do not know! He said: but God knows, and he will judge between them. 13224, from Abu Dharr: two sheep butted one another in the Prophet's presence and he said to me: Abu Dharr, do you know why they butted? I said: no! He said: but God knows and will judge between them! Abu Dharr said: the Messenger of God left us and there was no bird turning its wings in the sky but he had given us some knowledge of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 348}

In his notes the editor identifies Ishaq ibn Sulayman al-Razi al-Abdi, trusted, Fitr ibn Khalifa al-Qurashi, trusted, correcting "Matr" in print, and Mundhir al-Thawri, Mundhir ibn Ya'la, trusted, little hadith, who related from Followers and did not meet the Companions; he says Ahmad related it in the Musnad, short and long, by several routes, whose chains are either broken, like Abu Jafar's, or contain unknown men, and that Ahmad related it in another wording by a good connected chain from Abu Dharr: the Messenger of God was sitting when two sheep were locked together and one butted the other and made it miscarry; he laughed, and was asked what made him laugh, and said: I marvelled at it; by him in whose hand is my soul, it will be paid back on the day of resurrection.

Al-Tabari holds the right view to be that God reported that every creature and bird is gathered to him, and it may be the gathering of resurrection, or the gathering of death, or both, and neither the plain wording nor a report from the Messenger shows which is meant, since "hashr" in Arab speech is gathering, as in [[passage/quran-38-19|38:19]], "and the birds gathered, all turning to him", meaning collected; since gathering is hashr, and God gathers his creatures to himself on the day of resurrection and gathers them by death, the soundest saying is to take the verse in the general sense God gave it by its plain wording, and to say that every creature and bird is gathered to God after perishing and after the resurrection, since by "then to their Lord they will be gathered" he made it general and did not single out one gathering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 349}

If someone asks what is the point of "no bird flying with its wings", when does a bird fly except with its wings, the answer is that he showed before that God sent down this book in the tongue of a people, in their languages and what they know among themselves and use in speech, and addressed them so; since it is their way when they mean emphasis to say "I spoke to so-and-so with my mouth", "I walked to him on my foot" and "I struck him with my hand", he addressed them with the like of what they know in their speech, and from this is [[passage/quran-38-23|38:23]], "this brother of mine has ninety-nine ewes". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 349}

The editor notes that the printed text gave the verse as in our copies, which is not where Abu Jafar's evidence lies, the manuscript being right as he gave it, which is Abdullah ibn Mas'ud's reading, and refers to what Abu Jafar said in the commentary on Surat Sad on the Arabs' emphasis of a word.

The editor's supplement (Ahmad Shakir, from the "completion of the tracing"; not al-Tabari's words) on reports 13224 and 13223: the first concerns the two goats that butted each other; the second repeats that and adds Abu Dharr's saying that the Messenger left them with no bird flapping its wings in the sky of which he had not given them some knowledge. Both chains are broken, as his brother Mahmud Shakir explained, because Mundhir ibn Ya'la al-Thawri did not meet Abu Dharr and narrates only from the Followers. The sense of the first, the butting goats, is given by Ahmad in the Musnad through Abd al-Rahman ibn Tharwan, from al-Hudhayl ibn Shurahbil, from Abu Dharr, with a sound chain. The sense of the second comes by another sound route in Ibn Hibban's Sahih, through Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Fitr ibn Khalifa and Abu al-Tufayl from Abu Dharr, a sound connected chain which the editor found connected only in Ibn Hibban, where he set out its tracing in detail. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 590}

Verse 38 closes here at p. 350, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-39|6:39]].
