**Commentary on verse 35 (pp. 521-526)**

Al-Tabari says on "their prayer at the House was nothing but whistling and clapping; so taste the punishment for your unbelief" [[passage/quran-8-35|8:35]]: what is there for these polytheists that God should not punish them while they bar from the Sacred Mosque those who pray to God in it and worship Him, not being God's guardians, whereas His guardians are those they bar from the Sacred Mosque and who do not pray in it; "their prayer at the House" meaning God's ancient House "was nothing but whistling", that is a whistle. One says, he whistled, whistling and a whistle; it was said that "muka" is a man's joining his hands, putting them in his mouth and crying out, and one says the animal's rear whistled when it blew out wind, hence the rear is called "mukwa". Al-Tabari cites two verses, one by Antara from his famous ode describing a slain man whose wound made sounds like a whistle, the other by al-Tirimmah describing a thrust, and the editor explains their words such as "husband", "fair one", "flank-muscle", "split-lipped", "the angry" and "gaping". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 521} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 522}

As for "clapping", it is hand-clapping; one says, he clapped, clapping, and he struck, and he struck palm on palm, in one sense. The interpreters spoke likewise; report 16022, from Hajar ibn Anbas: whistling is the whistle and clapping is the clapping, the editor identifying Musa ibn Qays al-Hadrami and Hajar ibn Anbas, a trustworthy Kufan Follower who was at the Camel and Siffin with Ali; reports 16023 and 16024, from Ibn Abbas: the prayer of the polytheists at the House was whistling, meaning the whistle, and clapping, meaning the clap; report 16025, from Atiyya: the clap and the whistle. In reports 16026 to 16033, from Atiyya al-Awfi from Ibn Umar, whistling is the whistle and clapping the clap, with the opposite in 16026, and in 16029 Atiyya showed how Ibn Umar did it, whistling, tilting his cheek and clapping his hands; report 16030, from Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, describes blowing a whistle into the cupped hands; report 16031 is from Mujahid from Ibn Abbas. The editor mentions Salama ibn Sabur in the chain of 16032, whom Ibn Ma'in weakened and Ibn Hibban held trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 522} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 523} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 524}

Report 16034, from Ibn Abbas: Quraysh used to circle the House naked, whistling and clapping, and God sent down [[passage/quran-7-32|7:32]] and they were ordered to wear clothes; report 16035, from Sa'id: Quraysh used to oppose the Prophet at the circling, mocking him, whistling and clapping; report 16036, from Mujahid: they blew into their hands; reports 16037, 16038 and 16039, from Mujahid: whistling is putting their fingers in their mouths and clapping is the clap, confusing Muhammad's prayer by this, a group of Banu Abd al-Dar; reports 16040 and 16041, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: whistling was that they interlaced their fingers and whistled through them, and he showed the place where they whistled, on the side of Abu Qubays; the editor explains "flicking" and "Abu Qubays", the mountain over the hollow of Mecca. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 524} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 525}

Report 16042, from Abu Salama: whistling is blowing, and he pointed with his palm before his mouth, and clapping is the clap; reports 16043 and 16044, from al-Dahhak: whistling is the whistle and clapping the clap; report 16045, from Qatada: whistling is clapping with the hands and clapping is a cry with which they opposed the Qur'an; report 16046, from Qatada, the other way round; report 16047, from al-Suddi: whistling is a whistle like a white bird called the whistler found in the land of Hijaz, which the editor says is a bird like the lark; report 16048, from Ibn Zayd: whistling was a whistle the people of the Jahiliyya made openly, and he also said a whistle in their hands, and play. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 525} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 526}

We stop here at the foot of p. 526, after report 16048 in the commentary on 8:35; the next batch begins at p. 527. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 526}
