**Commentary on verse 162: the firm in knowledge, the believers, and those who establish prayer (pp. 393-398)**

Al-Tabari says this is God's exception from the Jews described in the earlier verses, who asked you to send down a book from heaven. The 'firm in knowledge' among them are those firmly rooted in knowledge of God's rulings that His prophets brought, who have mastered it and know its truth. The believers in God and His messengers believe in the Qur'an sent down to you, Muhammad, and in the books sent before you; and they do not ask you for a sign, since from the books of God they read and from the reports of their prophets they know that you are God's messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 393}

Report 10836, from Qatada, says God excepted a group of the People of the Book, some of whom believed in God and in what was sent down to them and to God's prophet, and affirmed and knew that it was the truth from their Lord. The commentators differ on whether 'those who establish prayer' are the firm in knowledge or others, though their grammatical case is different from 'the firm'. Some say the wording is a slip of the scribe, and it should read: the firm in knowledge among them and those who establish prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 394}

Report 10837, from Hammad ibn Salama through al-Zubayr: he asked Aban ibn Uthman why it was written so; Aban said that the scribe wrote 'the firm in knowledge among them' and, on reaching the next word, asked what to write, and was told to write 'those who establish prayer', and he wrote what he was told. Report 10838, from Hisham ibn Urwa through his father: he asked A'isha about this place and others, and she said: nephew, that is the scribe's work, they erred in the writing; and it is said that in the reading of Ibn Mas'ud it is 'those who establish prayer' in the nominative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 395}

Others, including some grammarians of Kufa and Basra, say that 'those who establish prayer' is an attribute of the firm in knowledge, but since the sentence became long with words coming between them, 'those who establish prayer' is put in the accusative as praise; the Arabs do this with the description of one thing when it is long with praise or blame, varying the case of its beginning and middle and then returning to the case of the first. They cite lines of verse on 'those who fulfil their covenant' in another verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 395}

Others say 'those who establish prayer' is an attribute of other than the firm in knowledge in this place, though in another place it describes them. All these say its case is genitive. Some say it is coordinated with the 'what' in 'what was sent down to you and what was sent before you', so the sense is: they believe in what was sent down to you and in those who establish prayer; others say those who establish prayer are the angels, whose establishment of prayer is their glorifying their Lord and asking forgiveness for those on earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 396}

Those who held this view rejected the accusative of praise for 'those who establish prayer', because the Arabs use the accusative of praise only after the predicate is complete, and the predicate of 'the firm in knowledge' is 'those We will give a great reward'; so the accusative of praise in the middle of the sentence, before the predicate of the subject is complete, is not allowed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 397}

Al-Tabari judges that the most sound view is that 'those who establish prayer' is in the genitive, coordinated with the 'what' of 'what was sent down to you and what was sent before you', and that its sense is directed to the angels: the believers among them believe in the book sent down to you and what was sent before you, and in the angels who establish prayer; then the sentence returns to describe the firm in knowledge, saying: but the firm in knowledge among them, and the believers in the books, and those who give the alms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 397}

He gives as his reason that the reading ascribed to Ubayy ibn Ka'b, 'those who establish prayer', agrees with his codex, and that if it were a scribal error it would appear in all the codices; that our codex agrees with Ubayy's, which shows its soundness; and that had it been an error, the companions from whom the Qur'an was taken would have corrected it orally and taught the nation the correct form; the transmission of the whole community of the reading as it is written is the best proof that it was not the scribe's doing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 398}

The reading of 'those who establish prayer' as a reference to establishing prayer is a claim without proof from the apparent text or a sound report, and it is not lawful to turn the apparent text to a hidden sense without proof. 'Those who give the alms' is coordinated with 'the believers believe', meaning those who give the zakat of their wealth to those God has made entitled to it, and 'the believers in God and the Last Day' are those who affirm His oneness, His godhead, the resurrection, reward and punishment; 'We will give them a great reward' means recompense for their obedience to God, and a great reward, which is the garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 399}
