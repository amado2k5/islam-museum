**Commentary on verse 135: standing firm for justice and witness for God (p. 301)**

Al-Tabari says this is an instruction from God to His believing servants not to do as those did who went to the Messenger about the matter of the Banu Ubayriq, defending them, speaking for them and presenting their case as people of need and poverty. God commands the believers to be upright in justice, that is, in fairness, as witnesses for God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 301}

Al-Tabari says 'standing firm' (shuhada') is in the accusative as a detached phrase from 'upright' applied to those who believe, meaning: stand for justice for God at your testimony, or at the time of it. 'Even against yourselves' means even if your testimony is against yourselves, your parents or your kin; stand by justice in it, give it by saying the truth, and do not incline in it to a rich man for his wealth or to a poor man for his poverty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 302}

God, who made the judgement between rich and poor equal, is more worthy of both than you, since He owns them and knows what suits each; so He commanded you to equalise them in testimony for or against them. The sense of 'do not follow desire lest you deviate' is: do not follow your own desires by leaning in testimony towards either party. He answers the question of how a witness can testify against himself: when someone owes another a right and acknowledges it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 302}

Al-Tabari sees the verse as a discipline from God to the believers, not to do what those did who defended the Banu Ubayriq in their theft and betrayal before the Messenger and vouched for their goodness. He commands them to speak justly in testimony even against themselves, parents, mothers and kin, and not to let the wealth, poverty or kinship of those they testify for lead them into false witness or to conceal the truth. It was also said to have come down as a discipline to the Prophet himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 303}

Report 10678, from al-Suddi through Asbat, says two men disputed before the Prophet, one rich and one poor; he leaned to the poor man, thinking a poor man would not wrong the rich, but God insisted on justice for both. Al-Tabari also gives a view that the verse concerns testimony, ordering believers to treat rich and poor equally when they testify. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 304}

Report 10679, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha and Mu'awiya: God commanded believers to speak the truth even against themselves, parents or children, not to favour a rich man for his wealth, nor pity a poor man for his need, and so leave the truth and do wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 304}

Report 10680, from Ibn Shihab through Yunus and Ibn al-Mubarak, on a father's testimony for his son and kin: this was the practice of the early Muslims; the righteous were not suspected in testimony of a father for a son, a son for a father, a brother for a brother, or a man for his wife. Later people did things that led rulers to suspect them, so the testimony of those suspected for their kin was rejected. The editor remarks that Ibn Shihab would be astonished at what our time has become. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 304}

Report 10681, from Ibn Zayd: do not let this man's poverty move you to pity him so that you do not give testimony against him; he says this to the witness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 304}

Report 10682, from Qatada through Sa'id and Bishr ibn Mu'adh, says the verse is about testimony: establish testimony, son of Adam, even against yourself, your parents, your kin or your people's honour, for testimony belongs to God and not to people; by justice the strong is restrained from the weak, the liar from the truthful, and the wrongdoer from the right; through it people are set right. It is also reported that Moses asked his Lord what was least placed on earth, and was told: justice. The editor explains 'yarankhuhu' as humbling and weakening him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 305}

Al-Tabari explains that 'both' (bihima) is dual because the rich and the poor have been mentioned; some scholars say the rich and the poor are unknown, so the reference may be singular, dual or plural. Ubayy's reading is 'God is more worthy of them'; 'or' is taken to mean 'and'; and there is an implied 'whoever'. The sense of 'do not follow desire lest you deviate' is: do not follow your desires so as to abandon establishing the testimony in truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 305}

On the words 'if you distort or turn away', al-Tabari reports different readings. Some say the address is to judges: if they distort the judgement for one of the two parties over the other, or turn away. Ibn Abbas, through Qabus ibn Abi Zabyan from his father, says these are two men sitting before the judge, whose bending and turning away towards one of them is meant (10683). The editor notes the criticism of Qabus and his father by Ibn Ma'in and Ibn Hibban. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 307}

Others say the address is to witnesses: if they distort their testimonies and alter them, or turn away from them and leave them. Report 10684, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha and Mu'awiya, says distorting with the tongue in testimony, or turning away from it; report 10685, from Ibn Abbas through Ibn Sa'd, his father, uncle and grandfather, gives the same sense of twisting and turning away. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 307}

Al-Tabari explains 'twisting' (lajlaja) and 'turning away' (i'rad): twisting is when a person twists his tongue away from the truth so that he does not give the testimony as it should be given; turning away is leaving it. Report 10686, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih: 'twist' means altering the testimony, and 'turn away' means concealing it; report 10687, from Mujahid through Shibl, gives alteration and concealment; report 10688, through Sufyan, says distorting or abandoning it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 308}

Report 10689, from Qatada, says faltering or concealing, and this is about testimony. Report 10690, from al-Suddi, says 'twist' is twisting the testimony so as not to give it, and 'turn away' is turning from it to conceal it, saying: I have no testimony. Report 10691, from Ibn Zayd, says he conceals the testimony, or twists part of it, or turns away from it, refusing to testify against someone he pities for his poverty, or sparing a rich man in hope of what he has. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 308}

Report 10692, from Mujahid: distorting. Report 10693, from Ata: faltering and so spoiling it, or leaving it. Report 10694, from al-Dahhak: not giving it as it should be given; turning away is concealing the testimony. Report 10695, from Qatada through Shaybān: faltering, and leaving it so as not to testify. Report 10696, from al-Dahhak: a man twisting his tongue away from the truth in testimony. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 309}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that 'twisting' is the witness twisting his testimony for or against the party by altering it with his tongue and not giving it, so as to invalidate it; and that 'turning away' is his leaving its performance and standing by it, so that he does not testify. This view is better because God commanded them to stand firm for justice as witnesses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 309}

Al-Tabari sets out the readings. Most readers outside Kufa read two waws, from 'lawa' meaning to delay a debt; some Kufan readers read one waw, and he gives two ways of reading that and rejects both. He prefers the two-waw reading, meaning that you push away standing by the testimony as it should be stood by for the one who is owed it, altering or changing it, or turning away from it so that you leave off standing for him, as a man delays another's debt. He quotes a line of al-Ashsha on delaying a debt. The editor notes that Tabari's explanation of the waw of the plural here contains an evident error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 310}

Al-Tabari ends that God is aware of what you do: of standing by the testimony, of altering it, or of concealing it; that is, He knows it, keeps it against you, and will repay you for it in the Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 311}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
