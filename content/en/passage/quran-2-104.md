## In al-Tabari's commentary

The interpreters differed on the meaning of "ra'ina", which the believers were forbidden to say. Some said it means "do not say what is contrary", from Ata and Mujahid. Others said it means "lend us your hearing", that is listen to us and we will listen to you, from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid; al-Dahhak said a man among the polytheists would say "lend me your hearing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 459} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 460}

They then differed on the reason for the prohibition. Some said it was a word the Jews used in mockery and abuse, so the believers were forbidden to say it to the Prophet, from Qatada, Atiyya, al-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas, and Ibn Zayd (who linked it to the verse in Surat al-Nisa and glossed "al-ra'in" as the one who errs; the editor says he found this nowhere else and that others derived it from ru'una, folly, ignorance and slackness). Others said it was a usage of the Ansar in the pre-Islamic period and they were forbidden it in Islam, from Ata; Abu al-Aliya reported that the Arab polytheists said it in conversation and were forbidden, and Ibn Jurayj that it is the speech of the mocker. Others said it was the speech of one particular Jew, Rifa'a ibn Zayd (of Banu Qaynuqa in al-Suddi's report, where al-Tabari observes that the correct name is Ibn al-Tabut, not Ibn al-Sa'ib), who addressed the Prophet with it abusively, and the Muslims took it from him, so God forbade them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 460} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 461} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 462} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 463}

Al-Tabari holds the right view is that it was a word God disliked them to say to His Prophet, like what is reported from the Prophet, "do not call the grape karm, but say habla", and "do not say 'my slave', but say 'my lad'", two words of one meaning where one is disliked and the other preferred in address (the editor gives the routes of both reports in the Musnad and the two Sahihs). He then puts the objection of what the prohibition of "ra'ina" signifies, once the reason for the grape and the slave is known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 463}
