**Commentary on verse 14 (pp. 38-39)**

He then explains "Then We made you successors in the land after them, to see how you would act". Then We made you, people, successors after these generations We destroyed for their wrongdoing, replacing them on earth and living there after them, "to see how you act", that is, that your Lord may see where your conduct stands against that of the nations that perished before you for their sins and their disbelief in their Lord: will you follow their example and so earn the punishment they earned, or take a different road, believing in God and His messenger and affirming the resurrection after death, and so earn the great reward from your Lord. The editor refers to the gloss on "successors" in the thirteenth volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 38}

Report 17579, from Qatada: it was mentioned to us that Umar ibn al-Khattab said, our Lord spoke truly, He made us successors only to see how our deeds are, so show God good deeds by night and day, in secret and in the open. Report 17580, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: Awf ibn Malik told Abu Bakr that he had seen in a dream a rope let down from heaven by which the messenger of God was drawn up, then it was let down and Abu Bakr was drawn up, then people were measured around the pulpit and Umar was ahead of them by three cubits; Umar then said to leave his dream aside. When Umar became caliph he asked Awf for the dream and Awf told it; Umar explained the three cubits as that he would be caliph, that he would not fear the blame of a blamer in God's cause, and that he would be a martyr; he recited "Then We made you successors" and said he had been made a successor, so let him see how he acts, and as for martyrdom, how could he have it with the Muslims around him, ending with "God is able to do what He wills". The editor explains the verbs for "drawn up" and "measured". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 39}

In the note the editor says that Zayd ibn Awf al-Qutai, Abu Rabi'a, known as Fahd, is abandoned as a narrator, that the printed text and Ibn Kathir's commentary both gave his name wrongly, and that Ibn Sa'd transmitted the report in different wording with a good chain in the Tabaqat. This is the editor's judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 39}
