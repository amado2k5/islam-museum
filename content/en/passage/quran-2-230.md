## Summary
Verse 230 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 230 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari then moves to what God says in ([[passage/quran-2-230|2:230]]) about the condition in which a woman is not lawful to the man who divorced her until she marries another husband. The interpreters differed over what it indicates. Some said it indicates that if a man divorces his wife the third time after the two divorces mentioned, then his wife is not lawful to him after the third until she marries another husband, other than the divorcer. From Qatada: God made divorce three. When he divorces her once he has more right to her until her waiting period ends, which is three menses; if the period ends before he takes her back, she is separate from him by one divorce, has more right to herself and he becomes one of the suitors. When a man wanted to divorce his wife he would wait for her menses, and when she was pure, divorce her once at the beginning of her waiting period, before two just witnesses; if he then wished to take her back he did so while she was in her waiting period, and if he left her until it ended she was separate from him by one divorce. If he wished to divorce her again after the first while she was in her waiting period, he would wait for her menses and, when she was pure, divorce her again at the beginning of her waiting period; if he wished to take her back he did, and she was with him on one divorce remaining; and if he wished to divorce her he divorced her the third time when she was pure, and this is the third after which she is not lawful to him until she marries another husband (4881). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 585} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 586} From Ibn 'Abbas, if he divorces her three times she is not lawful until she marries another husband (4882); from al-Dahhak, when he divorces once or twice he has the right to take her back until her period ends, and the third leaves him with no right over her until she marries another husband (4883, 4884); and from al-Suddi, after the two divorces, and this is the third (4885). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 586} The editor notes that the manuscript gives the sense of the verse and not its wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 586}

Others said that the clause indicates what is required of one who releases his wife kindly after the two divorces, and that if he releases her thus, she is not lawful to him except after another husband. From Mujahid, it goes back to the keeping or releasing (4886, 4887). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 586} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 587} Al-Tabari chooses Mujahid's view, because of the report he narrated from the Prophet answering the man who asked where the third divorce is, in which the Prophet said that the third is the release with kindness. If release with kindness is the third divorce, then it is clear that this clause has nothing to do with indicating the third divorce, and is rather a statement of what is lawful for one who released his wife kindly after the two divorces and what is forbidden to him of her, and the state in which he may marry her, and a notice to God's servants that after release as described the man has no right to take his wife back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 587} The editor notes that the old division of the copy from which his copy was made ends here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 587} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 588}

Al-Tabari then asks which kind of marriage God means, the intercourse or the marriage contract? He says both: if a woman marries a man by a contract and he does not have intercourse with her but divorces her, she is not lawful to the first husband; likewise if someone has intercourse with her without a marriage, she is not lawful to the first, by consensus of the community. So the interpretation of the clause is that she marries another husband in a sound marriage, he has intercourse with her in it, and then divorces her. The proof that intercourse is meant, though it is not named in the Book of God, is the consensus of the whole community; and further, were she to marry another husband right after the divorce, before her waiting period ended, she would be marrying in a way other than that which God permitted, even though the clause does not mention the waiting period, since His words about divorced women waiting show it; and likewise where intercourse is not named, the revelation to the Messenger and his explanation to the people show it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 588} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 589} He then gives the reports from the Prophet on this: from 'A'isha, the Prophet was asked about a man who divorced his wife, who then married another man, who consummated the marriage but divorced her before intercourse; is she lawful to her first husband? The Prophet said, she is not lawful to her first husband until the other tastes her little sweetness and she tastes his (4888). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 589} The editor says this hadith and nine after it are ten chains for 'A'isha's hadith that consummation is required for a woman divorced three times to be lawful to her first husband, a matter agreed on; the second husband must desire the woman and intend lasting companionship, and if he marries her intending to make her lawful to the first, he is the hallil whom the Prophet cursed, together with the one for whom it is done, and his marriage is void; he traces the hadith to Ahmad's Musnad, Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 589}

To continue the reports from the Prophet that tasting is required: from 'A'isha, through Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father, like the above (4889). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 589} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 590} From 'A'isha, the wife of Rifa'a al-Qurazi came to the Prophet and said, I was with Rifa'a and he divorced me with the final divorce, and I married 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Zubayr, but he has only something like the fringe of a garment, meaning he is unable with her; he said to her, do you want to return to Rifa'a? No, not until you taste his little sweetness and he tastes yours (4890, 4891, 4892). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 590} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 591} In another version from 'Urwa from 'A'isha, the Prophet smiled and said to her, perhaps you want to return to Rifa'a; no, not until you taste his sweetness and he tastes yours; Abu Bakr was sitting with the Prophet and Khalid ibn Sa'id ibn al-'As was at the door of the room, not yet admitted, and began calling Abu Bakr, will you not check this woman from what she says openly before the Messenger of God? (4893). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 591} From al-Qasim from 'A'isha, the Prophet said, no, not until he tastes of her sweetness what the first tasted (4894, 4895); and that a man divorced his wife three times and she married another who divorced her before touching her, and the Prophet was asked whether she is lawful to the first; he said, no, not until he tastes her sweetness as the first did (4896). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 591} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 592} From Umm Muhammad from 'A'isha from the Prophet, when a man divorces his wife three times she is not lawful to him until she marries another and each tastes the other's sweetness (4897). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 592} From Abu Hurayra from the Prophet, until he tastes her sweetness (4898), and about the woman her husband divorces three times who marries another who divorces her before consummating, and the first wants to take her back, he said, no, until he tastes her sweetness (4899). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 593} From Anas ibn Malik from the Prophet, about a man who divorced his wife three times and another married her and divorced her before consummating, can she return to her first husband, he said, no, until he tastes her sweetness and she tastes his (4900). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 594} From Ibn 'Abbas, al-Ghumaysa' or al-Rumaysa' came to the Prophet complaining of her husband, claiming that he did not reach her; soon her husband came and claimed that she was lying and wanted to return to her first husband; the Prophet said, it is not for you until a man other than him tastes your sweetness (4901). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 595}

In the notes the editor gives the sources of these reports: 'A'isha's hadith is in Muslim, Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah; he notes that 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Zubayr is a well-known Companion; he reports Ibn al-Athir's explanation that the diminutive of the word for sweetness points to the small amount by which it becomes lawful; he says the chain from Abu Hurayra includes Abu al-Harith al-Ghifari, a Follower in whom al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim mention no criticism, and he disagrees with Ibn Kathir's statement that he is unknown; and he judges the narrators of the Anas chain reliable, citing Ahmad, al-Bayhaqi and al-Haythami's Majma' al-Zawa'id. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 590} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 591} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 592} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 593} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 594}
