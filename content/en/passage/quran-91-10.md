## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 9 to 15 as a single unit. He interprets the phrase “And he who corrupts it has failed” as meaning: he has failed in his pursuit, not attaining the righteousness he sought and desired for himself. “He who corrupts it” means: he whose soul God corrupts, burdening and diminishing it, by abandoning it to guidance until it commits sins and forsakes obedience to God. It has also been said that “dassaha” is the same as “dassasaha,” with one of its “s” letters being changed to a “y,” as in the saying of al-Ajjaj: “The swooping of the falcon when the falcon breaks,” meaning: “the swooping.” And I thought this matter, meaning: I thought, and the Arabs do that a lot, so they change some of the letters of the doubled letter, sometimes a ya, and sometimes a waw. And from it is the saying of another: He takes me in poetry, every art, until he repels the thought from me. He means: the thought. And he quotes in the passage sayings of Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatada, Ibn Zaid, and Sufyan.
