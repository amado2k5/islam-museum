## Summary
Verse 91 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 91 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-91|7:91]], al-Tabari says: the "rajfa" seized the unbelievers of Shuayb's people; he has already explained its meaning, a shaking by God's punishment; "and by morning they were in their dwellings fallen" means kneeling, dead and destroyed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 566}

(14868) from al-Suddi: God sent Shuayb to Midian and to the people of the Wood, the thicket of trees, who in addition to unbelief cheated in measure and weight. He called them, they denied him, and when they defied and denied him they asked for the punishment. God opened for them a door of the doors of Hell, and its heat destroyed them, and neither shade nor water helped them. Then He sent a cloud with a pleasant wind; they felt its coolness and pleasantness and called one another to the "shadow", all going out to it; when they gathered under it, it blazed with fire on them and destroyed them, which is the sense of [[passage/quran-26-189|26:189]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 566}

(14869) from Ibn Ishaq: the story of Shuayb and his people is what God tells in the Qur'an; they were cheats in the measures and weights they gave people, besides their unbelief and denial of their prophet. He called them to worship God and to leave wrongdoing, and spoke, sincerely and truthfully, what [[passage/quran-11-88|11:88]] points to. When the Prophet spoke of Shuayb he used to say, "that is the orator of the prophets", for the beauty of his replies to his people about what was meant for them. When they denied him and threatened him with stoning and exile from their land, the punishment of the day of the shadow seized them. Ibn Ishaq says a man of Midian called Amr ibn Jalha, on seeing the cloud, spoke verses calling his people to see that Shuayb was a messenger and to leave their two soothsayers, Sumayr and Imran ibn Shaddad; he said he saw a rain-cloud rising and calling with a roar over the hard ground in the valley, and that by the next forenoon they would see in it only al-Raqim, their dog, walking among the uplands. The editor explains "ghabya", "sumana" and "anjad". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 567}

Then (14870) from Ibn Ishaq that it reached him, and God knows best, that God sent the heat on them until it cooked them, and then raised for them (the report is not yet finished). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 567}

The rest of report 14870: then He raised for them a shadow like a black cloud, and when they saw it they hurried to it, seeking relief in its coolness from the heat they were in, until, once under it, it closed on them and they all perished; God saved Shuayb and those who believed with him by His mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 568}

(14871) from Abu Abd Allah al-Bajali: "Abu Jad", "Hawwaz", "Hutti", "Kalmun", "Saafas" and "Qarashat" were names of kings of Midian, and their king on the day of the shadow in Shuayb's time was Kalmun. His sister wept for him in verses saying that Kalmun had broken her support and that his death came in the midst of the camp, that death came to the lord of the people as fire amid a shadow, and that the fire was borne upon them and their home became like something vanished. The editor notes that Abu Abd Allah al-Bajali is unknown, that al-Tabari gives the like in his history through Yahya ibn al-Ala al-Bajali, of whom Ahmad said he was a liar who forged reports, and that al-Baghawi and al-Thalabi record the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 568}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
