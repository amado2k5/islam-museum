## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when you said: O Moses, we will not endure one kind of food" al-Tabari explains that "sabr" is holding the soul back from something, so the sense is: we cannot bring ourselves to keep to one food, which was what God gave them in the wilderness, the quail, so ask your Lord to bring forth for us of what the earth grows. On why they asked, he reports from Qatada that in the wilderness they had been shaded by the cloud and given manna and quail, tired of it and recalled their life in Egypt, so asked Moses and were told to go down to a town; from Abu al-Aliya that their food was the quail and their drink the manna; from Ibn Zayd that their drink was honey from the sky and their food a bird, and they knew no bread or anything else; and from Mujahid and Ibn Abi Najih that they wanted to exchange the manna and quail for herbs and what was named with them. He adds that "min" in "of what the earth grows" is partitive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 124} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 125} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 126}
