## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when you said: O Moses, we will not endure one kind of food" al-Tabari explains that "sabr" is holding the soul back from something, so the sense is: we cannot bring ourselves to keep to one food, which was what God gave them in the wilderness, the quail, so ask your Lord to bring forth for us of what the earth grows. On why they asked, he reports from Qatada that in the wilderness they had been shaded by the cloud and given manna and quail, tired of it and recalled their life in Egypt, so asked Moses and were told to go down to a town; from Abu al-Aliya that their food was the quail and their drink the manna; from Ibn Zayd that their drink was honey from the sky and their food a bird, and they knew no bread or anything else; and from Mujahid and Ibn Abi Najih that they wanted to exchange the manna and quail for herbs and what was named with them. He adds that "min" in "of what the earth grows" is partitive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 124} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 125} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 126}

Al-Tabari notes that some took "min" in "of what the earth grows" as redundant, citing the Arab saying "I saw not from anyone", while a group of grammarians denied that and said its presence shows that part of what is named is meant; so for him the sense is: ask your Lord to bring forth for us some of what the earth grows of its herbs and cucumbers. Herbs, cucumbers, lentils and onions are well known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 126} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 127}
On "fum" the commentators differ: some say wheat and bread, from Ata, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Hasan, Abu Malik, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd and Ibn Abbas, and from Ibn Abbas that in the tongue of Banu Hashim it is wheat, citing a line of Uhayha ibn al-Julah, with the editor remarking on the weakness of the chain of some of these reports. Others say it is garlic, from Mujahid and al-Rabi', and in some readings it is "thumiha", attributed by some to Ibn Mas'ud. Al-Tabari holds that calling wheat and bread "fum" belongs to an old dialect, and that if the reading with "tha" is sound it is one of the interchanged letters, since "fa" and "tha" are close in articulation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 127} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 128} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 129} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 130}
On "would you exchange what is lower for what is better" he says Moses asked them whether they would take the baser in worth and value of life in place of what is higher; "istibdal" is leaving one thing for another in its place, and "adna" means baser, lower and smaller in worth, though some took it as "nearer". He reports the sense of baser from Qatada and others, and notes the dialect form with hamza in "dani'". For him there is no doubt that whoever exchanges manna and quail for herbs, cucumbers, lentils, onions and garlic exchanges a humble life for a higher one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 130} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 131}
