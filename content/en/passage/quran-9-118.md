## Summary
Verse 118 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/repentance-of-the-three-accepted|Repentance of the Three Accepted]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 118 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 118 (pp. 542-557)**

Al-Tabari says: God has turned to the Prophet, the emigrants and the helpers, and to the three who were left behind. These three, whom God describes in this verse, are the others of whom He said in verse 106 of this surah that they were deferred to God's command, and he has already given those who said this, so there is no need to repeat it. The sense is: God turned to the three whom God held back from repentance and so deferred them behind those who repented among the ones who stayed behind from the Messenger. Reports 17431 and 17432 are from Ikrima and Qatada: they were held back from repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 543}

"Until the earth, for all its vastness, closed in on them" means in its breadth, through grief and regret at staying behind from jihad with the Messenger; "their own souls closed in on them" by the anguish and distress they felt; "they were sure there was no refuge" means they were certain in their hearts that nothing could shelter them from the trial that came from God's command because of their staying behind, nor from the punishment they feared, except God. Then He granted them the turning to obedience and a return to what pleases Him, so that they would turn to Him, return to obedience and hold to His command and prohibition. "God is the Accepter of repentance, the Merciful" means He gives His servants the turning to obedience and gives success to whom He likes among them, and is merciful in not punishing them after repentance or abandoning one who wishes repentance without turning to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 544}

Report 17433 is from Jabir, through Ibn Waki', Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Abu Sufyan: the three left behind are Ka'b ibn Malik, Hilal ibn Umayya and Murara ibn Rabi'a, all of the helpers; report 17434 is similar through Ubayd ibn Muhammad al-Warraq and Abu Usama, with Murara ibn al-Rabi' or ibn Rabi'a, Abu Usama being unsure. The editor says the well-known form is Murara ibn al-Rabi', refers to the same variation in reports 17177, 17178 and 17183, cites Ibn Kathir on what Muslim has, and gives a notice on Ubayd ibn Muhammad al-Warraq, trustworthy, died 255, whom al-Tabari also cites in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 544}

Report 17435 is from Ikrima and Amir, through Ibn Waki', Isra'il and Jabir: they were deferred in the middle of Bara'a; report 17436 is from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: those deferred in the middle of Bara'a in the words about others deferred to God's command, Hilal ibn Umayya, Murara ibn Rib'i and Ka'b ibn Malik; reports 17437 to 17439 are similar, one adding that all were of the helpers; report 17440 is from Sa'id: Ka'b ibn Malik, who was a poet, Murara ibn al-Rabi' and Hilal ibn Umayya, all helpers; report 17441 is from al-Dahhak, the same. The editor gives the manuscript's reading of the names and refers back to his earlier note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 545}

Report 17442 gives the same from al-Dahhak through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hashim and Juwaybir. Report 17443 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: Ka'b ibn Malik, Hilal ibn Umayya and Murara ibn Rabi'a stayed behind at Tabuk; it was mentioned to us that Ka'b ibn Malik tied himself to a pillar and said he would not loose himself until the Messenger of God released him, and the Messenger said he would not release him until his Lord released him if He willed; another had stayed behind for the sake of a garden of his whose fruit had ripened and made it a charitable gift in God's way, saying he would never eat of it; the third rode through wastelands after the Messenger, one land lifting him and another dropping him, his feet streaming blood. The editor explains the walled garden and the streaming. Reports 17444 and 17445 are from Abu Malik and Ikrima with the three names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 546}

Report 17446 is from Ka'b ibn Malik, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Ibn Awn and Umar ibn Kathir ibn Aflah: I was never in a campaign better provided with mount and means than in that one. When the Messenger set out I said I would prepare tomorrow and catch up; I busied myself with my preparation and evening came without finishing, and the same on the third day, and I said, "Far away, the people have gone three days," and I stayed. When the Messenger returned, people began to make excuses to him; I came and stood before him and said I had never been better provided than in this campaign, and he turned from me, and the people were told not to speak to us, and our women told to withdraw from us. One day I climbed over a garden wall and found Jabir ibn Abdullah, and I said, "I ask you by God, do you know that I ever cheated God and His Messenger?" and he was silent and did not speak to me. One day I heard a man on the pass calling "Ka'b, Ka'b" until he reached me, saying, "Give glad tidings to Ka'b." The editor gives a notice on Umar ibn Kathir ibn Aflah and says Ahmad gave it in his Musnad by the same route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 547}

Report 17447 is from Ibn Shihab, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the Messenger of God campaigned at Tabuk, intending the Byzantines and the Arab Christians in Syria; when he reached Tabuk he stayed there some ten nights, the deputation of Adhruh and the deputation of Ayla met him and he made peace with them on the jizya, then he returned without going beyond it, and God revealed the verse; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 547}

The rest of report 17447, from Ibn Shihab: the three who were left behind were a party including Ka'b ibn Malik of the Banu Salima, Murara ibn Rabi'a of the Banu Amr ibn Awf and Hilal ibn Umayya of the Banu Waqif; they stayed behind from the Messenger of God on that campaign among some eighty men. When he returned to Medina these three told the truth and admitted their faults, while the rest lied and swore that only an excuse had kept them; he accepted the outward words of those, took their pledge and left their inner thoughts to God, and forbade speaking to the three who were left behind, telling them, when they told their story and admitted their faults, that they had spoken truly and should rise until God decided about them. When God revealed the Qur'an, He turned to the three, and said of the others what is in verses 95 and 96 of this surah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 548}

Then Ibn Shihab reports from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abdullah ibn Ka'b ibn Malik, from Abdullah ibn Ka'b, who led his father when he went blind: I heard Ka'b ibn Malik tell his story of staying behind on the Tabuk campaign. He said he had never stayed behind from any campaign of the Messenger except Tabuk, apart from Badr, where no one who stayed behind was reproached, since the Muslims had set out for a Quraysh caravan until God brought them together with their enemy without appointment; and he had been with the Messenger on the night of al-Aqaba when they pledged to Islam, and would not exchange it for presence at Badr, though Badr is more spoken of. His story was that he had never been stronger or better off than when he stayed behind on that campaign; he had never before owned two mounts until then. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 548}

The Messenger campaigned in fierce heat, facing a long journey, deserts and a numerous enemy, so he made clear to the Muslims their matter so they could prepare, and told them where he was heading; the Muslims with him were many, and no register held them together, meaning a roll. Ka'b said any man wishing to stay away would think it would stay hidden unless a revelation came about him; and the Messenger campaigned when the fruit and shade were good and he was drawn to them. The Messenger prepared and the Muslims with him; I set out each morning to prepare with them and came back having done nothing, telling myself I could do it whenever I wished, and this went on until the others were fully in earnest; then the Messenger left with the Muslims in the morning and I had done nothing of my preparation, until they were far ahead and the campaign was out of reach. I meant to set off and catch them, and wish I had, but it was not decreed for me. When I went out among the people after his departure it grieved me that I saw no example for myself but a man accused of hypocrisy or one of the weak whom God excused. The editor explains several words and shows what the manuscript dropped, supplied from the Sahih of Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 549}

The Messenger did not mention me until he reached Tabuk, where, sitting among the people, he asked what Ka'b ibn Malik had done; a man of the Banu Salima said his two cloaks and admiring his own flanks had held him back, and Mu'adh ibn Jabal said, "What a bad thing to say; by God, Messenger of God, we know nothing of him but good." The Messenger was silent, and then he saw a man in white, shimmering in the mirage, and said, "Be Abu Khaythama," and it was Abu Khaythama al-Ansari, who had given a measure of dates in charity and been mocked by the hypocrites. Ka'b said: when I heard that he was returning from Tabuk, my anxiety came upon me and I began to think of lies, asking how I would escape his anger tomorrow, and taking counsel from every thoughtful person of my family; when it was said that he had arrived, falsehood left me and I knew I could never escape him by anything, so I resolved on the truth. When he came back from a journey he began at the mosque, praying two rak'as and sitting with people, and when he did this the stay-behinds came, making excuses and swearing, some eighty men. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 550}

He accepted their outward words, took their pledge, asked forgiveness for them and left their inner thoughts to God, until I came and greeted him, and he smiled the smile of an angry man and said, "Come." I walked and sat before him, and he asked what held me back, and whether I had not bought my mount. I said, "Messenger of God, were I sitting with anyone else of this world I would expect to escape his anger with an excuse, for I have been given skill in argument; but I know that if I tell you a lie today to please you, God will soon make you angry with me, and if I tell you the truth you will be angry with me but I hope for God's good end; by God I had no excuse, and I was never stronger or better off than when I stayed behind." He said, "This man has told the truth; rise until God decides about you." Men of the Banu Salima followed me and said they knew of no earlier sin of mine and that I might at least have excused myself as the others did, since the Messenger's forgiveness would have been enough for my sin; they kept reproaching me until I almost went back to call myself a liar. Then I asked if anyone else had met the same, and they said two men had said what I said and been told what I was told: Murara ibn Rabi'a and Hilal ibn Umayya al-Waqifi, two righteous men who had been at Badr, an example for me; so I went on when they named them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 551}

The Messenger forbade the Muslims to speak to us three alone among those who stayed behind, and people shunned us and changed toward us until the land itself seemed strange to me, not the land I knew. We stayed so for fifty nights. My two companions gave in and stayed in their houses weeping, but I was the youngest and hardiest, so I went out, attended prayer, walked in the markets and no one spoke to me; I would greet the Messenger in his seat after prayer and wonder whether his lips moved in answer, then pray beside him and steal glances, and when I turned to my prayer he looked at me and when I looked toward him he turned away. When the Muslims' coldness grew long for me, I walked and climbed over the wall of Abu Qatada's garden, my cousin and the dearest of people to me, and greeted him, and by God he did not return it; I asked him in God's name whether he knew I love God and His Messenger, and he was silent, and again, and the third time he said God and His Messenger know best, and my eyes overflowed and I turned away and climbed back over the wall. The editor explains "you three", notes that a phrase in the printed text was added and is not in the manuscript or Muslim, and discusses the variation in Murara's name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 552}

As I was walking in the Medina market, a Nabataean farmer from Syria who had come to sell food asked who would direct him to Ka'b ibn Malik, and people pointed to me, until he came and gave me a letter from the king of Ghassan. I could read, and it said that it had reached them that my companion had been harsh with me, that God had not placed me in a house of humiliation and neglect, and that I should join them and they would console me. When I read it I said this too is a trial, and I took it to the oven and burned it there. When forty of the fifty nights had passed and revelation was slow, the Messenger's messenger came and told me the Messenger commanded me to keep apart from my wife. I asked whether I should divorce her or what, and he said no, only keep apart and not go near her; and the same was sent to my companions. I told my wife to go to her family and stay with them until God decided. Hilal's wife went to the Messenger and said that Hilal was an old man with no servant, and asked if he would dislike her serving him; he said no, only that he must not come near her. She said he had no movement toward anything, and that he had wept since all this happened. The editor explains the words for resolving, the oven and its firing, and cites the versions of Muslim and al-Bukhari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 553}

Some of my family said I should ask permission about my wife as was given to Hilal's wife; I said I would not, for I could not know what he would say if I asked about her, and I was a young man. I stayed ten more nights, completing fifty from when speaking to us was forbidden. After the dawn prayer on the fiftieth morning, as I sat on the roof of one of our houses in the state God described, with my soul closed in on me and the earth closed in for all its breadth, I heard the voice of a crier on the mountain of Sal' calling at the top of his voice, "Ka'b ibn Malik, rejoice." I fell down in prostration and knew that relief had come. The Messenger announced God's turning to us after the dawn prayer, and people went to bring us the news; messengers went to my two companions, a man rode his horse to me and a man of Aslam ran and climbed the mountain, and the voice was faster than the horse. When the one whose voice I heard came to me, I took off my two garments and clothed him with them for his good news; by God I owned no others that day; I borrowed two garments, put them on and went to the Messenger; the report continues on the next page. The editor explains the climbing and the announcing, and compares the wording of Muslim and al-Bukhari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 554}

The rest of Ka'b's account, summarised in our own words: people met him in crowds congratulating him on the acceptance of his repentance; when he entered the mosque the Prophet was sitting with people around him, and Talha ibn Ubaydillah hurried up, shook his hand and congratulated him, the only one of the emigrants to do so, which Ka'b never forgot. When he greeted the Prophet, whose face was bright with joy, he was told to rejoice at the best day he had known since his mother bore him; he asked whether it came from the Prophet or from God and was told it came from God, and the Prophet's face, when pleased, shone like a piece of the moon. Ka'b offered to give up his wealth in charity as part of his repentance, and the Prophet advised him to keep some of it as better for him, so he kept his share at Khaybar; he vowed to speak only the truth for the rest of his life and says he knew of no one whom God tested better in truthfulness, and that he never deliberately lied after that. The editor explains the congratulation and its wording, Talha's gesture by the bond of brotherhood mentioned by Ibn Hajar, and the sense of divesting oneself of wealth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 555}

Then God revealed the verses, and Ka'b said that after guiding him to Islam, no favour was greater to him than his truthfulness to the Prophet, so that he did not lie and perish as those did who lied, for God said of those who lied the harshest words, which are in verses 95 and 96. He added that the "leaving behind" of the three named in the verse is not about staying back from the campaign but about God deferring their case while accepting the excuses and oaths of the others. The editor compares the wording of the manuscript, the printed text, Muslim and al-Bukhari, says al-Tabari will give Ka'b's account by other routes, and that this one has the isnad of Muslim's Sahih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 556}

Reports 17448 to 17450 are the same account of Ka'b by other routes: through Aqil from Ibn Shihab; through Ma'mar from al-Zuhri from Abd al-Rahman ibn Ka'b from his father; and through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq from Ibn Shihab. The editor notes that al-Bukhari and Ahmad gave the first at length, Ahmad the second, and Ibn Hisham in his biography the third. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 557}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
