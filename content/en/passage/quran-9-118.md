## Summary
Verse 118 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/repentance-of-the-three-accepted|Repentance of the Three Accepted]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 118 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 118 (pp. 542-547)**

Al-Tabari says: God has turned to the Prophet, the emigrants and the helpers, and to the three who were left behind. These three, whom God describes in this verse, are the others of whom He said in verse 106 of this surah that they were deferred to God's command, and he has already given those who said this, so there is no need to repeat it. The sense is: God turned to the three whom God held back from repentance and so deferred them behind those who repented among the ones who stayed behind from the Messenger. Reports 17431 and 17432 are from Ikrima and Qatada: they were held back from repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 543}

"Until the earth, for all its vastness, closed in on them" means in its breadth, through grief and regret at staying behind from jihad with the Messenger; "their own souls closed in on them" by the anguish and distress they felt; "they were sure there was no refuge" means they were certain in their hearts that nothing could shelter them from the trial that came from God's command because of their staying behind, nor from the punishment they feared, except God. Then He granted them the turning to obedience and a return to what pleases Him, so that they would turn to Him, return to obedience and hold to His command and prohibition. "God is the Accepter of repentance, the Merciful" means He gives His servants the turning to obedience and gives success to whom He likes among them, and is merciful in not punishing them after repentance or abandoning one who wishes repentance without turning to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 544}

Report 17433 is from Jabir, through Ibn Waki', Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Abu Sufyan: the three left behind are Ka'b ibn Malik, Hilal ibn Umayya and Murara ibn Rabi'a, all of the helpers; report 17434 is similar through Ubayd ibn Muhammad al-Warraq and Abu Usama, with Murara ibn al-Rabi' or ibn Rabi'a, Abu Usama being unsure. The editor says the well-known form is Murara ibn al-Rabi', refers to the same variation in reports 17177, 17178 and 17183, cites Ibn Kathir on what Muslim has, and gives a notice on Ubayd ibn Muhammad al-Warraq, trustworthy, died 255, whom al-Tabari also cites in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 544}

Report 17435 is from Ikrima and Amir, through Ibn Waki', Isra'il and Jabir: they were deferred in the middle of Bara'a; report 17436 is from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: those deferred in the middle of Bara'a in the words about others deferred to God's command, Hilal ibn Umayya, Murara ibn Rib'i and Ka'b ibn Malik; reports 17437 to 17439 are similar, one adding that all were of the helpers; report 17440 is from Sa'id: Ka'b ibn Malik, who was a poet, Murara ibn al-Rabi' and Hilal ibn Umayya, all helpers; report 17441 is from al-Dahhak, the same. The editor gives the manuscript's reading of the names and refers back to his earlier note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 545}

Report 17442 gives the same from al-Dahhak through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hashim and Juwaybir. Report 17443 is from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: Ka'b ibn Malik, Hilal ibn Umayya and Murara ibn Rabi'a stayed behind at Tabuk; it was mentioned to us that Ka'b ibn Malik tied himself to a pillar and said he would not loose himself until the Messenger of God released him, and the Messenger said he would not release him until his Lord released him if He willed; another had stayed behind for the sake of a garden of his whose fruit had ripened and made it a charitable gift in God's way, saying he would never eat of it; the third rode through wastelands after the Messenger, one land lifting him and another dropping him, his feet streaming blood. The editor explains the walled garden and the streaming. Reports 17444 and 17445 are from Abu Malik and Ikrima with the three names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 546}

Report 17446 is from Ka'b ibn Malik, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Ibn Awn and Umar ibn Kathir ibn Aflah: I was never in a campaign better provided with mount and means than in that one. When the Messenger set out I said I would prepare tomorrow and catch up; I busied myself with my preparation and evening came without finishing, and the same on the third day, and I said, "Far away, the people have gone three days," and I stayed. When the Messenger returned, people began to make excuses to him; I came and stood before him and said I had never been better provided than in this campaign, and he turned from me, and the people were told not to speak to us, and our women told to withdraw from us. One day I climbed over a garden wall and found Jabir ibn Abdullah, and I said, "I ask you by God, do you know that I ever cheated God and His Messenger?" and he was silent and did not speak to me. One day I heard a man on the pass calling "Ka'b, Ka'b" until he reached me, saying, "Give glad tidings to Ka'b." The editor gives a notice on Umar ibn Kathir ibn Aflah and says Ahmad gave it in his Musnad by the same route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 547}

Report 17447 is from Ibn Shihab, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the Messenger of God campaigned at Tabuk, intending the Byzantines and the Arab Christians in Syria; when he reached Tabuk he stayed there some ten nights, the deputation of Adhruh and the deputation of Ayla met him and he made peace with them on the jizya, then he returned without going beyond it, and God revealed the verse; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 547}
