**Commentary on verse 126 (pp. 579-581)**

Al-Tabari says: the readers differed over "do they not see". Most readers of the cities read it with ya, meaning do these people with sickness of hypocrisy in their hearts not see; Hamza read it with ta, meaning do you, believers, not see that they are tried. The right reading to him is the ya, as God's rebuke of them, since the authority of the readers of the cities agrees on it and its meaning is sound. So the sense is: do these hypocrites not see that God tests them every year once or twice, that is, in some years once and in some twice, and still they do not repent, meaning that despite the affliction from God and the test that befalls them they do not return from hypocrisy or repent of unbelief, nor do they take heed of the proofs of God they see and signs they witness, but persist in hypocrisy. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of trial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 579}

The interpreters differed over the trial God mentions with which these hypocrites are tried. Some said it is God's testing them with drought and hardship. Reports 17490 to 17493 are from Mujahid through several chains: tried by famine and hunger, tested by punishment once or twice every year. Others said it means they are tested by campaigning and jihad; reports 17494 and 17495 are from Qatada and al-Hasan: tried by campaigning in God's way once or twice every year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 580}

Others said it means they are tested by the lies the polytheists spread about the Messenger and his companions, with which those with sickness in their hearts are led astray. Reports 17496 and 17497 are from Hudhayfa, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sharik, Jabir and Abu al-Duha, and through Ibn Waki' and his father: every year we would hear a lie or two by which many groups of people were led astray. Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that God made His believing servants marvel at these hypocrites and rebuked the hypocrites for how little they take heed and how poorly they attend to the admonitions with which God admonishes them. It may be that these admonitions are the hardships of hunger and drought He sends, or what He shows them of His Messenger's victory over the unbelievers and the triumph of his word over theirs, or what is shown to Muslims of their hypocrisy and hidden foulness in their reliance on the rumours of the polytheists about the Messenger and his companions; no report establishes one of these over the others, and there is nothing better than accepting the plain statement of God: do they not see that they are tested once or twice a year in a way that should deter them, and yet they are neither deterred nor warned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 581}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
