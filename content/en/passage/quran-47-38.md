## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "Behold, these are you calling to spend in the way of Allah. Among you is he who is stingy, and whoever is stingy is only withholding from himself. And Allah is the Self-sufficient, while you are the poor. And if you turn away, He will replace you with another people, and then they will not be like you": Allah Almighty says to the believers: "Here you are," O people, "these are the ones you call to spend in the way of Allah," meaning: you are calling to spend in the struggle of the enemies of Allah and in support of His religion, "so among you is he who is stingy" with spending in it, and the "ha" is included in Two places, because when the Arabs wanted approximation, they placed the pronoun between "ha" and "dhā," saying: "Here you are with this, standing," because approximation is the answer to the word. Sometimes they repeated "ha" with "this," or sometimes they exclusioned with the former, and the second was omitted. They do not put "yütü" before "ha," because "ha" is an answer, so do not bring it closer after the word. Some grammarians of Basra said: The note was placed in two places for emphasis. In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah and Ibn Zayd.
