## In al-Tabari's commentary

Here al-Tabari says God ordered them to strike the dead man with part of the cow so that he would live, and neither the verse nor any report on which an argument stands shows which part. The reports differ: the thigh, from Mujahid, 'Ikrima, Qatada and 'Ubayda; the piece between the shoulders, from al-Suddi; a bone, from Abu al-'Aliya; some limb, from Ibn Zayd. By his own reasoning al-Tabari holds it could be the thigh, the tail, the shoulder cartilage or another part, and that not knowing does no harm, nor knowing any good. The point was that he would live and tell Moses and the disputants who killed him; the words saying so are omitted as the sentence implies them, as in "Strike the sea with your staff, and it parted". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 229} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 231} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 232}
On "thus God gives life to the dead" he says it addresses believers and argues against the polytheists who denied resurrection, bidding them take a lesson from the revival in this world of the Israelite slain man, and that revival on the Day of Resurrection is likewise; the Arab polytheists were unlettered, but those of the Israelites who knew this were among them. "His signs" are his marks and proofs of his messenger's prophethood, so that "you may reason", understand that he is true and believe in him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 232} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 233}
