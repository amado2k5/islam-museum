## Summary
Verse 270 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 270 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-270|2:270]]), pp. 580 to 581. Al-Tabari explains the close of ([[passage/quran-2-269|2:269]]): those who take admonition from what God admonished in these verses, in which He admonished those who spend their wealth and others, so that they remember His promise and warning, refrain from what their Lord forbade and obey what He ordered, are the people of understanding who grasped God's command and prohibition; admonitions benefit only those of sense and reason. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 580} The editor notes a correction of a sentence in print and manuscript by adding a conjunction, the sentence's syntax, and refers to earlier commentary on the words for understanding and spending. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 580}

Al-Tabari then explains ([[passage/quran-2-270|2:270]]): whatever spending you spend, that is whatever charity you give, or whatever vow you vow, a vow being what a person makes obligatory on himself as piety in obeying God and drawing near to Him, of charity or good work, God knows all of it, nothing small or great escapes Him, and He counts it against them until He repays them; whoever's spending, charity and vow were to seek God's pleasure and out of firmness of soul He repays with the doubling He promised, and whoever's spending was to be seen by people and whose vows were to the devil He repays with the punishment He threatened; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 580} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 581} Reports 6193 and 6194, Mujahid: He knows it and counts it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 581}

Al-Tabari adds that God threatens those whose spending is for show and whose vows are obedience to the devil that they have no helpers, the word for helpers being the plural of one who helps as the nobles is the plural of noble, meaning those who help them against God on the day of resurrection by warding off His punishment with power or ransom; he had shown that the wrongdoer is one who puts a thing in the wrong place, and the one who spends for show and the one who vows in disobedience are called wrongdoers for putting their spending and vows in the wrong place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 581} The editor notes a difference between manuscript and print in one place, giving the right reading, and refers to earlier commentary on help, helper and wrongdoing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 581} We stop at the end of p. 581. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
