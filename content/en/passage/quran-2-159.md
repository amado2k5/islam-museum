## Summary
Verse 159 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "those who conceal what We sent down of clear proofs and guidance after We made it clear to the people in the Book" al-Tabari says it means the Jewish scholars and rabbis and the Christian scholars, for concealing from people the matter of Muhammad and not following him though they found him written in the Torah and Gospel; the "clear proofs" are what was made clear of his prophethood, mission and description in the two books, and the "guidance" is what He made plain of his affair in the books He sent down on their prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 249} He reports from Ibn 'Abbas that Mu'adh ibn Jabal, Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Kharija ibn Zayd asked some Jewish rabbis about what is in the Torah, and they concealed it and refused to tell them, and the verse was revealed about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 249} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 250} From Mujahid, they are the People of the Book; from al-Rabi', they concealed Muhammad though they found him written with them, out of envy and wrongdoing; from Qatada, they concealed Islam, which is God's religion, and concealed Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 250}

On "after We made it clear to the people in the Book" he says "the people" means some people, since knowledge of Muhammad's prophethood, description and mission was only with the People of the Book, and the Book is the Torah and Gospel. Though the verse was revealed about a particular group, it is meant for anyone who conceals knowledge God obliged him to make clear to people, and he cites what is reported from the Prophet, "whoever is asked about knowledge he has and conceals it will be bridled on the Day of Resurrection with a bridle of fire", {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 251} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 252} and Abu Hurayra saying that were it not for a verse in God's book he would not have narrated to you, and reciting this verse; in another report, were it not for two verses God sent down in His book he would not narrate anything, this one and the verse on the covenant taken from those given the Book to make it clear to people, from Al 'Imran. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 252}

On "those, God curses them and the cursers curse them" al-Tabari says: those who conceal what God sent down about Muhammad, his description and the matter of his religion, after God made it clear to them in their books, He curses for that concealment and for leaving it unexplained to people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 254} "La'na" from "la'anahu Allah" means He put him far away and removed him; the root of "la'n" is expelling, and he cites a verse of al-Shammakh; so the sense is that God keeps them away from Himself and His mercy, and the cursers ask their Lord to curse them, since the cursing of the sons of Adam and the rest of creation is to say "O God, curse him". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 254}

He then notes the interpreters' differing views on who "the cursers" are. Some said the beasts and creeping things of the earth; from Mujahid and 'Ikrima, beetles, scorpions and animals say we are denied rain because of the sins of the sons of Adam, and from Mujahid it is camels, cattle and sheep that curse the sons of Adam's disobedient when the land is barren. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 254} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 255} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 256} He asks why non-rational beings take the masculine sound plural, and answers that it is the Arabs' practice, when they describe an animal by what belongs to people, to pluralise it like their males, as in "they said to their skins, why did you testify against us" and "O ants, enter your dwellings". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 256} Others said they are the angels and believers, from Qatada and al-Rabi' ibn Anas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 256} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 257} Others said everything except the sons of Adam and the jinn, and from al-Suddi he reports al-Bara' ibn 'Azib that when an unbeliever is placed in his grave an animal with an iron rod comes and strikes him once between the shoulders and he cries out, and whoever hears his cry curses him except men and jinn; al-Dahhak has something similar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 257}

Al-Tabari chooses the view that the cursers are the angels and believers, because God described the unbelievers as those on whom lies the curse of God, the angels and all people in "those who disbelieved and died as disbelievers, on them is the curse of God, the angels and all people", so the curse on the other party is God's curse and that of those named. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 257} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 258} As for the view that they are beetles, scorpions and creeping things, he says its truth cannot be known except by a report from God or the Prophet and there is none, so it is not permissible to affirm that God meant the animals without a decisive report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 258} Note on the text: al-Suddi's report no. 2374m (of an Ansari friend, Tha'laba ibn Ghanama, asking a Jewish man whether they find Muhammad among them) breaks off in the printed text after the question, and the editor remarks that the bracketed words in the following paragraph were dropped by the copyist without doubt; only what is visible of the report is therefore given here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 251}
