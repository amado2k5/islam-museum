## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "whatever verse We abrogate", al-Tabari says it means whatever We transfer of a verse's ruling to another, replacing and changing it, so that the lawful becomes forbidden, the forbidden lawful, the permitted barred and the barred permitted; this happens only in command and prohibition, bar and release, denial and permission, whereas reports have neither abrogating nor abrogated. The root of "naskh" is copying a book, moving it from one copy to another, so abrogating a ruling is moving it and transferring its wording to another; so whether the verse's text is left in place or its trace is erased, it is abrogated in both cases, and the new ruling to which the servants' obligation is moved is the abrogator. He relates from al-Hasan al-Basri in agreement that your Prophet was made to recite a Qur'an and then forgot it, and that some of the Qur'an has been abrogated and you still recite it. He then records that the interpreters differed on "ma nansakh". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 471} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 472}

On "ma nansakh" al-Tabari gives the interpreters' words: from al-Suddi, that its abrogation is its taking back; from Ibn Abbas, "what We replace"; from the companions of Ibn Mas'ud and Mujahid, "We keep its writing and change its ruling". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 472} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 473}

On "aw nunsiha" he says the reciters differed. The people of Medina and Kufa read it with damma on the nun, which has two senses. The first is forgetting: whatever We abrogate, changing its ruling, or cause to be forgotten; he notes that in Ibn Mas'ud's codex it reads "whatever We cause you to forget of a verse or abrogate, We bring the like of it". A group said this: Qatada (a verse was abrogated by one after it, and the Prophet would recite a verse or more which was then forgotten and lifted, God causing His Prophet to forget what He willed and abrogating what He willed), Ubayd ibn Umayr and al-Rabi' ("We lift it"), and al-Hasan al-Basri (the Prophet was made to recite a Qur'an and then forgot it). Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas took it likewise but read it "aw tunsaha", addressing the Prophet, that is you, Muhammad; al-Qasim ibn Rabi'a reports he told Sa'd that Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab reads it "nunsiha", and Sa'd said the Qur'an was not sent down upon al-Musayyab nor his family, and cited verses from al-A'la and al-Kahf by reference. The editor notes al-Hakim's remark on the soundness of this report's chain and the notes of Abu Hayyan and Ibn Khalawayh on the reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 473} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 474} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 475} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 476}

The other sense is leaving, as in the verse of al-Tawba "they forgot God so He forgot them", meaning they left God and He left them; the verse then means whatever We abrogate, changing its ruling and replacing its obligation, or leave without replacing, We bring better than it or its like, from Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi and al-Dahhak. From Ibn Zayd, "We erase it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 476}

Others read "nansa'uha", with fatha on the nun and a hamza after the sin, meaning "We postpone it", from "nasa'tu this matter" when delayed, citing the verse of Tarafa ibn al-Abd. This is reported from Ata (you postpone it), Ibn Abi Najih (We defer it), Mujahid (defer and postpone), Atiyya (We postpone it and so do not abrogate it) and Ubayd ibn Umayr (deferral and postponement), al-Tabari correcting a name in one chain (Ali al-Azdi, not Ubayd al-Azdi). He says it was the reading of a group of the Companions and Successors and of the reciters of Kufa and Basra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 476} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 477}
