## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "whatever verse We abrogate", al-Tabari says it means whatever We transfer of a verse's ruling to another, replacing and changing it, so that the lawful becomes forbidden, the forbidden lawful, the permitted barred and the barred permitted; this happens only in command and prohibition, bar and release, denial and permission, whereas reports have neither abrogating nor abrogated. The root of "naskh" is copying a book, moving it from one copy to another, so abrogating a ruling is moving it and transferring its wording to another; so whether the verse's text is left in place or its trace is erased, it is abrogated in both cases, and the new ruling to which the servants' obligation is moved is the abrogator. He relates from al-Hasan al-Basri in agreement that your Prophet was made to recite a Qur'an and then forgot it, and that some of the Qur'an has been abrogated and you still recite it. He then records that the interpreters differed on "ma nansakh". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 471} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 472}
