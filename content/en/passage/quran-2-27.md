## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "those who break God's covenant after its confirmation" al-Tabari says it describes the transgressors whom alone God misleads by the parable. He records disagreement over what the covenant is: some say it is God's charge to His creatures, His commands and prohibitions in His books and through His Messenger, and breaking it is leaving off acting on it; others say these verses came down about the unbelievers among the People of the Book and their hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 410}

Al-Tabari sets out three views on the covenant. The first is what God took from the people of the Torah: to act on it, to follow Muhammad when he was sent and believe in him, and their breaking it is denying him after knowing the truth and concealing it after pledging to make it known. The second is that the verse covers all polytheists, unbelievers and hypocrites, God's covenant with them in tawhid being the evidence He set for His lordship, and in command and prohibition the miracles He gave His messengers, and breaking it is refusing to acknowledge them. The third is the covenant taken when He brought them out of Adam's loins, mentioned in surat al-A'raf, and breaking it is not fulfilling it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 410} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 411}
He chooses the view that these verses came down about the unbelieving Jewish rabbis near the Prophet's place of emigration and the hypocrites who held to polytheism, yet apply to everyone in a like error: what fits the hypocrites covers all hypocrites, and what fits the rabbis covers their like. He argues that the passage was revealed about them from its start to the end of their story, and that the call to keep the covenant in the Children of Israel verse is addressed to them specifically. This is his preferred reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 411} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 413}
He explains "after its confirmation" as after God secured the covenants of loyalty from them, and says the ruling also takes in anyone with their traits of breaking covenants, cutting kinship ties and spreading corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 414}
He reports from Qatada: beware of breaking this covenant, for we know of no sin about which God threatened as He did for breaking the covenant. He begins al-Rabi' ibn Anas's report that these are six traits among the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 414}
