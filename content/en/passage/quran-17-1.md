## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "Glory be to Him who took His servant by night from the Sacred Mosque to the Al-Aqsa Mosque, whose surroundings We have blessed to show him of Our signs. Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing." Abu Ja'far Muhammad ibn Jarir al-Tabari said: Allah means mentioning Him by His words: "Glory be to Him who took His servant by night," it is a purification of the One who took His servant by night and his absolution from what the polytheists say about Him having a partner in His creation, that He has a wife and a son, and that He is exalted and glorified above what they attributed to Him and attributed to Him from their ignorance and mistaken statements. I have previously explained that the phrase 'Subhan' is a noun placed in the verbal noun, so its position is accusative, which makes it unnecessary to repeat it in this place. Some have said it is accusative because it is not described, and the Arabs use it in tasbih in various places. Among them is prayer. Many interpreters interpreted God's saying: 'If he were not among those who glorify Allah,' if not for him being among those who pray. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Al-Hasan.
