## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 40 to 41 in a single passage. He explains His saying, "Our saying to something, when We intend to do so, is to say to him, 'Be,' and it is": If We intend to raise someone who dies, there is no burden upon us in reviving them, nor in anything else that We create, is, and transmit. When We intend to create and create it, We only say to it, 'Be,' and it is, without suffering or burden upon us. Reciters differed in the reading of the phrase 'yakun' (it will be), but most reciters of the Hijaz and Iraq read it as the beginning, and that the phrase 'Our saying of something, if we want it to be to it, is a complete statement that is self-sufficient for what comes after it, then it begins and is said: 'faykun,' as the poet said: he wants to parse it and make it a foreign word. Some reciters from the people of the Levant and some later Kufan readers read this as 'fiyyakun' as accusative, in conjunction with the phrase 'to say to him.' In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Jurayj.
