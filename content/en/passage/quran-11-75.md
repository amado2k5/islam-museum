**Commentary on verse 75 (p. 406)**

On [[passage/quran-11-75|11:75]] al-Tabari says: the Arabs hardly answer 'lamma', when a past verb follows it, except with a past verb, saying 'when he stood I stood' and hardly 'when he stood I stand'; it may be allowed for a verb that takes long, such as disputation, quarrel and fighting, so they say 'when I met him I fight him', meaning I began to fight him. His words 'Abraham was forbearing, tender-hearted, repentant' mean slow to anger, humble to his Lord, submissive and obedient to His command, 'repentant', one who returns to obedience to Him. Report 18349, from Mujahid: 'awwah munib' is the devout, the returning. Al-Tabari has explained the sense of 'awwah' earlier with the differences and the evidence for the sound view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 406}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
