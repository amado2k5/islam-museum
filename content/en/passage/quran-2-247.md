## Summary
Verse 247 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 247 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-247|2:247]]) begins on p. 306. Al-Tabari says that their prophet Shamwil told the notables that God had granted what they asked and sent Talut (Saul) as king; they objected that he should rule over them, being of the tribe of Benjamin son of Jacob, a tribe with neither kingship nor prophecy, and said they were likelier to have the kingship, being of the tribe of Judah, and that he had not been given much wealth, being a water-carrier, and it was said a tanner. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 306}

Al-Tabari relates from Wahb b. Munabbih (5636) that God told Shamwil to look at the horn holding the oil, and when a man came in and the oil stirred he would be the king of the Children of Israel, and Shamwil was to anoint his head with it; he waited. Talut was a tanner of the tribe of Benjamin who went out seeking a beast of his that had strayed, with a servant of his; they went in to the prophet to ask about it, the oil stirred, the prophet rose, anointed him and said, you are the king of the Children of Israel; his name in Syriac was Shaul son of Qays. The notables of Israel came to the prophet objecting, and he said God had preferred him over them and increased him in knowledge and in bodily strength. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 306} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 307} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 308} In the margin the editor explains the horn, the stirring and the hides and compares the lineage names with the People of the Book's scriptures; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 307} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 308} From Wahb in another report (5637): they asked Ashmwil to send a king; he said God has spared you the fighting; they said they feared those around them and wanted a king to turn to; God inspired him to send Talut and anoint him with the oil of holiness. Donkeys of Talut's father had strayed, so he sent him with a servant to seek them; they came to the prophet, who told him God had sent him as king; he said, how, my tribe is the lowest of the tribes, my clan the lowest of my tribe's clans and my house the lowest of its houses; he said, your sign is that you will return and your father will have found his donkeys; then he anointed him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 308} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 309}

From al-Suddi (5638): when they called Sham'un a liar and asked for a sign, he prayed to God and a staff was brought of the length of the man to be sent as king; they measured themselves against it and none matched; Talut was a water-carrier on a donkey that had strayed; when they measured him he matched, and the prophet said God had sent him as king; the people said, you have never lied more than now, we are of the tribe of kingship and he is not and has no wealth; the prophet said God had preferred him and increased him in knowledge and bodily strength. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 309} From 'Ikrima, Talut was a water-seller (5639). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 309} From Qatada (5640, 5641): among the Children of Israel were two tribes, one of prophecy, Levi, from which Moses came, and one of kingship, Judah, from which David and Solomon came; Talut was of Benjamin, which had neither, so they objected. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 309} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 310} From al-Dahhak (5642, 5643) similarly, with two tribes of prophecy and of rule. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 310} These reports come from Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-247|2:247]]) from p. 310: al-Rabi' (5644): when Talut was sent to the Children of Israel as king they objected and wondered, since he was neither of the tribe of prophecy nor of the tribe of kingship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 310} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 311} Ibn 'Abbas (5645): in the Children of Israel were two tribes, one with prophecy and one with kingship, and none was sent save from the tribe of prophecy and none ruled save from the tribe of kingship; God sent Talut though he was of neither, chose him over them and increased him in knowledge and body, which is why they objected. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 311} Ibn 'Abbas (5646): this was when the Torah was taken up and the believers driven out, and when the Ark came to them, the two tribes being those of prophecy and of rule. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 311} It was also said that "kingship" here means command of the army, from Mujahid (5647, 5648). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 312} Al-Tabari refers back to what was said earlier on the meaning of two words, and the editor gives the places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 312}

He then explains God's choosing him as choosing him over them (5649 Ibn 'Abbas, 5650 al-Dahhak, 5651 Ibn Zayd). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 312} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 313} He explains the increase in knowledge and body as God giving him more knowledge than others of those addressed, it being reported that revelation came to him, and in body an increase in height not given to others: Wahb (5652), that he stood above them from the shoulders upward; al-Suddi (5653), the staff report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 313} Others said the sense is that He added to the choosing an increase in knowledge and body, from Ibn Zayd (5654). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 313}

On God giving His kingship to whom He wills, al-Tabari says that kingship is His and in His hand alone and He places it with whom He wills, so do not object that Talut was sent though not of the house of kingship, for kingship is not an inheritance from forefathers but in God's hand, and they should not make their own choice against Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 314} Wahb (5655): kingship is in God's hand and He places it where He wills and they have no choice in it; Mujahid, that his kingship is His authority (5656, 5657). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 314} God's being all-encompassing and all-knowing means He is wide in bounty and gives it to whom He loves, and knows who is fit for the kingship He gives and gives it from His knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 314} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 315} The editor notes that the manuscript has a gap of about a word here which he could not fill. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 315}
