## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "those who are sure that they will meet their Lord" (literally "who think") al-Tabari raises the question how God could describe as humble those who merely "think" they will meet Him, since thinking is doubt. He answers that the Arabs call certainty "thann" and also doubt "thann", just as they call both darkness and light "usdfa" and both the one who helps and the one who cries for help "sarikh", citing lines of Duraid ibn al-Simma and Umayra ibn Tariq for "thann" as certainty; the editor explains the verses in his notes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 17} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 18}

Al-Tabari adds that instances of "thann" meaning certainty in Arab usage are too many to count, including the verse in surah al-Kahf, and that the commentators explained it so: from Abu al-Aliya, thann here is certainty; from Mujahid, every thann in the Qur'an is certainty or knowledge; from al-Suddi, that they are sure; from Ibn Jurayj, that they knew they would meet their Lord, like "I knew that I would meet my reckoning" in surah al-Haqqa; and from Ibn Zayd that, since they had not seen it, their thann was certainty rather than doubt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 18} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 19} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 20}
On "that they will meet their Lord" he asks why the active participle "mulaqu" is added to "their Lord" when the full form with the nun is normal for a future sense, and answers that the grammarians all allow the construct form without the nun, differing only on the reason: the Basrans say the nun is dropped for lightness while still intended, citing "taster of death" and "senders of the she-camel" and two lines; the Kufans say it has the share of nouns in construct because it has a noun's form, so the construct follows the form and the full form follows the meaning. The editor refers to Sibawayh and the Khizana for the lines. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 20} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 21}
For him the meaning of the verse is: seek help to keep My covenant through patience and prayer, and prayer is heavy except for those who fear My punishment, are humble before My command and are sure of meeting Me and returning to Me. It is heavy except for such people because for one with no certainty of a return, reward or punishment prayer is toil and error, since he hopes for no benefit or protection from harm by it, while it is light for believers for what they hope from it and fear from neglecting it. God commanded the rabbis of the Children of Israel to be among those who establish it hoping for its reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 22}
On "and that they are returning to Him" he says "they" refers to the humble and "Him" to the Lord, and then lists the differences over the meaning of the return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 22}

On the meaning of the return in "and that they are returning to Him", al-Tabari reports from Abu al-Aliya that they are certain they will return to Him on the Day of Resurrection, and another view that they return to Him by dying. His own choice is Abu al-Aliya's, because God said in the earlier verse "then to Him you will be returned" after being raised and brought back to life, which is the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 23}
