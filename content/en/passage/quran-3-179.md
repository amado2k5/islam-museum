## Summary
Verse 179 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 179 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 179 (pp. 424-427): the first part.** Al-Tabari explains that God would not leave the believers as they were, with the believer confused with the hypocrite and the one not told from the other, until He separates the hypocrite who hides disbelief from the sincere believer of true faith by trials and tests, as He separated them at Uhud when the enemy was met. The notes refer to earlier passages on the words for "leave", "corrupt" and "good" (numbers garbled). He says the interpreters differed over who is meant by the corrupt one. Some said what he said: Mujahid (8268), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: at Uhud He separated the hypocrite from the believer; Ibn Jurayj (8269), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: to show the one true in his faith from the false, and Mujahid said that at Uhud He separated them, hypocrite from believer; Ibn Ishaq (8270), through Ibn Humayd from Salama: the hypocrites (the note: Ibn Hisham, 3: 128, part of report 8265 and completing the reports before it, the printed text having the singular where the manuscript, the earlier report and Ibn Hisham have the plural). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 424} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 425}

Others said the sense is that the believer should be told from the unbeliever by emigration and fighting: Qatada (8271), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: by the unbelievers he means that God would not leave the believers in the error in which they were, until He separated them in fighting and emigration; Qatada (8272), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: until the wicked is told from the believer; and al-Suddi (8273), through Muhammad from Ahmad from Asbat: they said that if Muhammad was truthful he should tell them who believes in God and who disbelieves, so God sent this down, until the believer is brought out from the unbeliever. Al-Tabari prefers the first interpretation because the verses before it concern the hypocrites and this one follows in their sequence, so that it fits them better than others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 425} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 426}

**The second part: knowledge of the unseen (pp. 426-427, reports 8274-8276).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some, such as al-Suddi (8274), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat, said that God would not have made Muhammad aware of the unseen but chose him and made him a messenger. Others, such as Ibn Ishaq (8275), through Ibn Humayd from Salama, took it to mean in what God intends to try them with, so that they beware of what will come upon them in it, and that He teaches whom He chooses of His messengers what He wills (the note: Ibn Hisham, 3: 128, completing the reports ending at 8270, the printed text having "ya'lamuhu" with a ba' which is corrected from Ibn Hisham, the manuscript being undotted). Al-Tabari's choice is that God would not have shown them the inner thoughts of His servants so that they would know the believer from the hypocrite and the unbeliever, but distinguishes them by trials, as He distinguished them by hardship at Uhud, by fighting His enemy and the like, so that they know believer, unbeliever and hypocrite; yet He selects whom He wills of His messengers and shows him some of what is in the hearts of some of them, by his revelation and message. Mujahid (8276), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: He makes them pure for Himself. His reasoning is that the verse opens by God telling that He will not leave His servants without trials until He tells their believer from their unbeliever and hypocrite by testing, and then follows with the denial of showing the unseen; so in what it opens with, the exposure of the hypocrite's hypocrisy and the unbeliever's disbelief, there is a clear sign that the meaning is that He would not show them what is hidden of their inner secrets other than by what He mentioned, save for those of His messengers He excepted and singled out for His knowledge. The second note says the printed text and the manuscript have a sentence that does not run, that the copyist seems to have dropped "because" and that the editor prefers what he printed, though the syntax alone would justify it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 426} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 427}

**The close of verse 179 (p. 428, report 8277).** Al-Tabari says the last part means that if they believe him whom God chose among His messengers and told of the hypocrites among them, and fear their Lord by obeying what their Prophet has commanded and forbidden, they will have a great reward for that; Ibn Ishaq (8277), through Ibn Humayd from Salama: you return and repent. The notes say a phrase "for that" is an intrusion in the manuscript and printed text, a slip of the copyist's pen, which the editor dropped, and that the report is in Ibn Hisham (3: 128) completing the reports ending at 8275. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 428}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 428}
