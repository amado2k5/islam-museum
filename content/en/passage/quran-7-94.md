## Place in the museum

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-94|7:94]], al-Tabari says, addressing his prophet Muhammad, making known to him God's way with the nations before his community and reminding those of Quraysh who disbelieved in him, so that they might desist from the polytheism and denial in which they persisted: "We sent no prophet to any town" before you "but We seized its people with misery and hardship"; "basa" is want and the narrowness and hardness of living, and "darra" is harm and a bad state in the means of their worldly life; "that they might humble themselves" means We did it so that they would turn humbly to their Lord, submit to Him and return by leaving their unbelief and repenting of denying their prophets. (14878) from al-Suddi that the two mean poverty and hunger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 572}

Al-Tabari has already cited the evidence for the meanings of these two words. "Yadda'run" is said where the sense is "yatadarra'un", the ta being assimilated into the dad because their points of articulation are close. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 573}
