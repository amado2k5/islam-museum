**Commentary on verses 28-29 (pp. 432-437)**

On "those who believed and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God; surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest. Those who believed and did righteous deeds, tuba for them and a good return", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: He guides to Himself, by repentance, those who believed. "Those who believed" is in the accusative, in apposition to "man", since "those who believed" are "whoever turned", expressed by it. On "and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God" he says: their hearts become still and feel at home in the remembrance of God. Report 20358, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God", he says, they became still toward the remembrance of God and felt at home with it. On "surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest" he says: surely in the remembrance of God the hearts of the believers grow still and feel at home. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 432}

It was also said that He meant by this the hearts of the believers among the Companions of the Messenger of God. Report 20359, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest", for Muhammad and his Companions. Reports 20360 and 20361, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, and Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa: for Muhammad and his Companions. Report 20362, from Sufyan ibn Uyayna, through Ahmad ibn Yunus: "and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God", they are Muhammad's Companions. On "those who believed and did righteous deeds" he says: the righteous deeds are the deeds, that is, acting on what their Lord commanded them, "tuba for them". "Tuba" is in the nominative, "lahum" being its predicate. Some of the Basrans and Kufans said it is a nominative as one says in speech "woe to Amr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 433}

Al-Tabari says: the nominative is preferred in "tuba" because of the goodness of annexation in it without a lam, since one says "tubaka" as one says "waylaka" and "waykaka"; were it not good to annex it without a lam, the accusative would be better and more eloquent, as the accusative in their saying "ta'san li-Zayd, bu'dan lahu, suhqan" is better, since annexation without a lam is not good in those. The interpreters differed over the meaning of "tuba lahum". Some said its meaning is: how good is what they have. Report 20363, from Ikrima, through Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Marwazi of the people of Kufa, Abu Zakariya al-Kalbi and Umar ibn Nafi': Ikrima was asked about "tuba lahum" and said, how good is what they have; the editor notes in a footnote that Ja'far ibn Muhammad is al-Tabari's teacher, that Abu Zakariya al-Kalbi is Yahya ibn Mus'ab, neighbour of al-A'mash and truthful, and that Umar ibn Nafi' has been spoken against. Report 20364, from Ikrima, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Umar ibn Nafi': "tuba lahum", he said, how good is what they have. Report 20365, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Umar ibn {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 434}

Nafi': "tuba lahum", he said, how good is their destination. Others said its meaning is: a blessing for them. Reports 20366 to 20368, from al-Dahhak, through Abu Hisham, Abu Khalid al-Ahmar and Juwaybir; al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra'; and Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym: "tuba lahum", he said, a blessing for them. Others said its meaning is joy and coolness of the eyes. Report 20369, from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Dawud, al-Muthanna ibn Ibrahim, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: "tuba lahum", he says, joy and coolness of the eyes. Others said its meaning is: a good state for them. Report 20370, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "tuba lahum", he says, a good state for them, and it is a word from the speech of the Arabs. Report 20371, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: this is an Arabic word; a man says "tuba lak", meaning you have gained good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 435}

Others said its meaning is: good for them. Report 20372, from Ibrahim, through Abu Hisham, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan and Mansur: good for them. Report 20373, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mansur: "tuba lahum", he said, the good and honour God gave them. Others said "tuba lahum" is one of the names of Paradise, and the sense of the speech is: Paradise is theirs. Report 20374, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Yaman, Ash'ath, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "tuba lahum", he said, the name of Paradise in Abyssinian. Report 20375, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Hisham, Ibn Yaman, Ash'ath, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the name of the land of Paradise in Abyssinian. Report 20376, from Sa'id ibn Mashjuj, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Ja'far: "tuba" is the name of Paradise in Indian. Report 20377, from Sa'id ibn Mashjuj, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Dawud ibn Mihran, Ya'qub and Ja'far ibn Abi al-Mughira: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 436}

the name of Paradise in Indian is "tuba"; the editor notes that the name Sa'id ibn Mashjuj appears in different forms in the manuscript, in Ibn Kathir and in al-Durr al-Manthur, and that he found no mention of him in the books on narrators. Report 20378, from Ikrima, through Abu Hisham, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan and al-Suddi: "tuba lahum", he said, Paradise. Reports 20379 and 20380, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih, and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: Paradise. Report 20381, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "those who believed and did righteous deeds, tuba for them and a good return", he said, when God created Paradise and finished it He said "those who believed and did righteous deeds, tuba for them and a good return", and that was when it pleased Him. Report 20382, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sharik and Layth: "tuba lahum", he said, Paradise. Others said "tuba lahum" is a tree in Paradise. Report 20383, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Qurra ibn Khalid and Musa ibn Salim: "tuba lahum", a tree in Paradise; the editor notes that Musa ibn Salim is reliable and his report from Ibn Abbas is mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 437}

We stop here at the foot of p. 437, in the middle of the commentary on verses 28 and 29 (after report 20383, on "tuba"); the next batch begins at p. 438 (marker 446). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 437}
