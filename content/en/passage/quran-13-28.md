## Summary
Verse 28 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 28 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 28-29 (pp. 432-444)**

On "those who believed and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God; surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest. Those who believed and did righteous deeds, tuba for them and a good return", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: He guides to Himself, by repentance, those who believed. "Those who believed" is in the accusative, in apposition to "man", since "those who believed" are "whoever turned", expressed by it. On "and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God" he says: their hearts become still and feel at home in the remembrance of God. Report 20358, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God", he says, they became still toward the remembrance of God and felt at home with it. On "surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest" he says: surely in the remembrance of God the hearts of the believers grow still and feel at home. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 432}

It was also said that He meant by this the hearts of the believers among the Companions of the Messenger of God. Report 20359, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "surely in the remembrance of God hearts find rest", for Muhammad and his Companions. Reports 20360 and 20361, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, and Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa: for Muhammad and his Companions. Report 20362, from Sufyan ibn Uyayna, through Ahmad ibn Yunus: "and whose hearts find rest in the remembrance of God", they are Muhammad's Companions. On "those who believed and did righteous deeds" he says: the righteous deeds are the deeds, that is, acting on what their Lord commanded them, "tuba for them". "Tuba" is in the nominative, "lahum" being its predicate. Some of the Basrans and Kufans said it is a nominative as one says in speech "woe to Amr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 433}

Al-Tabari says: the nominative is preferred in "tuba" because of the goodness of annexation in it without a lam, since one says "tubaka" as one says "waylaka" and "waykaka"; were it not good to annex it without a lam, the accusative would be better and more eloquent, as the accusative in their saying "ta'san li-Zayd, bu'dan lahu, suhqan" is better, since annexation without a lam is not good in those. The interpreters differed over the meaning of "tuba lahum". Some said its meaning is: how good is what they have. Report 20363, from Ikrima, through Ja'far ibn Muhammad al-Marwazi of the people of Kufa, Abu Zakariya al-Kalbi and Umar ibn Nafi': Ikrima was asked about "tuba lahum" and said, how good is what they have; the editor notes in a footnote that Ja'far ibn Muhammad is al-Tabari's teacher, that Abu Zakariya al-Kalbi is Yahya ibn Mus'ab, neighbour of al-A'mash and truthful, and that Umar ibn Nafi' has been spoken against. Report 20364, from Ikrima, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Umar ibn Nafi': "tuba lahum", he said, how good is what they have. Report 20365, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Umar ibn {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 434}

Nafi': "tuba lahum", he said, how good is their destination. Others said its meaning is: a blessing for them. Reports 20366 to 20368, from al-Dahhak, through Abu Hisham, Abu Khalid al-Ahmar and Juwaybir; al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra'; and Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym: "tuba lahum", he said, a blessing for them. Others said its meaning is joy and coolness of the eyes. Report 20369, from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Dawud, al-Muthanna ibn Ibrahim, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: "tuba lahum", he says, joy and coolness of the eyes. Others said its meaning is: a good state for them. Report 20370, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "tuba lahum", he says, a good state for them, and it is a word from the speech of the Arabs. Report 20371, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: this is an Arabic word; a man says "tuba lak", meaning you have gained good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 435}

Others said its meaning is: good for them. Report 20372, from Ibrahim, through Abu Hisham, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan and Mansur: good for them. Report 20373, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Mansur: "tuba lahum", he said, the good and honour God gave them. Others said "tuba lahum" is one of the names of Paradise, and the sense of the speech is: Paradise is theirs. Report 20374, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Yaman, Ash'ath, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "tuba lahum", he said, the name of Paradise in Abyssinian. Report 20375, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Hisham, Ibn Yaman, Ash'ath, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the name of the land of Paradise in Abyssinian. Report 20376, from Sa'id ibn Mashjuj, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Ja'far: "tuba" is the name of Paradise in Indian. Report 20377, from Sa'id ibn Mashjuj, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Dawud ibn Mihran, Ya'qub and Ja'far ibn Abi al-Mughira: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 436}

the name of Paradise in Indian is "tuba"; the editor notes that the name Sa'id ibn Mashjuj appears in different forms in the manuscript, in Ibn Kathir and in al-Durr al-Manthur, and that he found no mention of him in the books on narrators. Report 20378, from Ikrima, through Abu Hisham, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan and al-Suddi: "tuba lahum", he said, Paradise. Reports 20379 and 20380, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih, and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: Paradise. Report 20381, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "those who believed and did righteous deeds, tuba for them and a good return", he said, when God created Paradise and finished it He said "those who believed and did righteous deeds, tuba for them and a good return", and that was when it pleased Him. Report 20382, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sharik and Layth: "tuba lahum", he said, Paradise. Others said "tuba lahum" is a tree in Paradise. Report 20383, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Qurra ibn Khalid and Musa ibn Salim: "tuba lahum", a tree in Paradise; the editor notes that Musa ibn Salim is reliable and his report from Ibn Abbas is mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 437}

Report 20384, from Abu Hurayra, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, al-Ash'ath ibn Abd Allah and Shahr ibn Hawshab: "tuba lahum" is a tree in Paradise to which God says, split open for My servant what he wishes, and it splits open for him horses with their saddles and bridles, camels with their halters and whatever clothing he wishes; the editor notes that the report is a statement of Abu Hurayra and that Shahr ibn Hawshab was considered reliable though spoken against. Report 20385, from Shahr ibn Hawshab, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Ja'far: "tuba" is a tree in Paradise from which all the trees of Paradise come, its branches being beyond the wall of Paradise. Report 20386, from Abu Hurayra, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd ibn Nasr and Ibn al-Mubarak: like report 20384. Report 20387, from Shamir ibn Atiyya, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abd al-Jabbar, Marwan and al-Ala': it is a tree in Paradise called "tuba". Report 20388, from Mughith ibn Sumayy, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Sufyan, Mansur and Hassan Abu al-Ashras: "tuba" is a tree in Paradise, and there is no dwelling in Paradise without a branch of it; a bird comes {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 438}

and lands, and he calls it and eats of one side of it dried meat and of the other roast, then says "fly" and it flies. Report 20389, from a man of Syria, through Abu Salih and Mu'awiya: your Lord took a pearl and put it on His palms, shaped it between His hands, planted it in the midst of the people of Paradise and said to it, extend until you reach My good pleasure; it did, and when it stood straight the rivers of Paradise burst from its roots; that is "tuba". Report 20390, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, through al-Fadl ibn al-Sabbah, Isma'il ibn Abd al-Karim al-San'ani and Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil: in Paradise there is a tree called "tuba", in whose shade a rider travels a hundred years without crossing it; its blossoms are fine cloths, its leaves striped cloaks, its branches ambergris, its plain ruby, its soil camphor and its mud musk; from its roots flow rivers of wine, milk and honey, and it is a gathering place for the people of Paradise. While they are in their gathering, angels from their Lord come leading choice camels bridled with gold chains, their faces like lamps in beauty and their fur as soft as the finest down, bearing saddles with planks of ruby and sides of gold, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 439}

and clothes of silk brocade; they make them kneel and say, our Lord has sent us to you that you visit Him and greet Him. They ride them, swifter than a bird and smoother than bedding, a man travelling beside his brother talking and conversing with him, no beast's ear touching another's ear, until the very tree moves aside from their path so as not to come between a man and his brother. They come to the Merciful, the Compassionate, who unveils His noble face to them so that they look on Him, and when they see Him they say, O God, You are Peace and from You is peace, and to You belong majesty and honour. Then He, blessed and exalted, says: I am Peace and from Me is peace, and My mercy and love are due to you; welcome to My servants who feared Me unseen and obeyed My command. They say, our Lord, we did not worship You as You deserve and did not esteem You as You are due, so permit us to prostrate before You. God says: this is not an abode of toil and worship but an abode of dominion and bliss, and I have lifted from you the toil of worship, so ask Me what you wish, for each of you has his wish. They ask Him, until the one with the humblest wish says, Lord, the people of the world vied with one another over their world and were cramped in it; Lord, give me everything they had in it from the day You created it to when the world ended. God says: your wish fell short today, and you asked less than your rank; this is yours from Me, and I will favour you with My own rank, for in My giving there is no stinginess and no scarcity. Then He says, show My servants what their wishes did not reach and what never occurred to them; and they are shown until they have fulfilled the wishes in their hearts {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 440}

and among what is shown to them are pairs of mounts, on every four of which is a couch of a single ruby, on each couch a poured gold dome, in each dome furnishings of Paradise laid one on another, and in each dome two houris, each wearing two garments of Paradise containing every colour in Paradise and perfumed with every fragrance; the light of their faces passes through the thickness of the dome so that whoever sees them thinks they are outside it, and the marrow of their legs is seen above the shin like a white thread in a red ruby; they see his excellence over his companion as the sun's over stone, or more, and he sees theirs likewise. Then he goes in to them, they greet him, kiss him and embrace him, and say, by God, we did not think God would create the like of you. Then God orders the angels, who lead them in ranks through Paradise until each of them reaches the dwelling prepared for him. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir related this report, saying before it that Ibn Jarir related from Wahb ibn Munabbih a strange and wondrous account, and after it that it is a strange narrative and a wondrous report, parts of which have witnesses. Report 20391, from Hammad, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Ali ibn Jarir: a tree in Paradise, in the house of every believer a branch of it. Report 20392, from Mughith ibn Sumayy, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Mansur and Hassan ibn Abi al-Ashras: "tuba" is a tree in Paradise; if a man rode a young camel around it he would die of old age before reaching the place he set out from, and there is no dwelling of the people of Paradise without a branch of that tree hanging down over them; when they wish to eat of the fruit it bends down to them {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 441}

and they eat of it as they wish, and the bird comes and they eat of it dried meat and roast as they wish, and then it flies. A report has been related from the Messenger of God like what those who said it is a tree said. Report 20393, from Utba ibn Abd al-Sulami, through Sulayman ibn Dawud al-Qumsi, Abu Tawba al-Rabi' ibn Nafi', Mu'awiya ibn Sallam, Zayd, Abu Sallam and Amir ibn Zayd al-Bikali: a bedouin came to the Messenger of God and said, Messenger of God, is there fruit in Paradise? He said, yes, and in it is a tree called "tuba", which corresponds to Firdaws. He said, which tree of our land does it resemble? He said, it resembles no tree of your land, but have you been to Syria? He said, no, Messenger of God. He said, it resembles a tree called the walnut, which grows on one trunk and then spreads out at the top. He said, how great is its root? He said, if a young camel of your family set out to circle its root, it would not encompass it before its collarbones broke from age. The editor says this is a good chain and that Ahmad narrated it at length in his Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 442}

Report 20394, from Qurra, father of Mu'awiya ibn Qurra, through al-Hasan ibn Shabib, Muhammad ibn Ziyad al-Jazari and Furat ibn Abi al-Furat: the Messenger of God said, "tuba lahum wa husn ma'ab" is a tree God planted with His own hand and breathed into from His spirit, which grows with ornaments and robes, and its branches are seen from beyond the wall of Paradise; the editor says this report is ruined in its chain, enough being what is in it about Muhammad ibn Ziyad, whom he calls a malicious liar who forged hadith. Report 20395, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Amr ibn al-Harith, Darraj and Abu al-Haytham: a man said to the Messenger of God, what is "tuba"? He said, a tree in Paradise a hundred years' journey across; the garments of the people of Paradise come out of its sheaths. The editor notes that Darraj has been spoken against and that Ahmad said his reports from Abu al-Haytham from Abu Sa'id carry weakness, the best that can be said of the chain being that it is of the sort used as supporting evidence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 443}

Al-Tabari says: on this interpretation, which we reported from the Messenger of God, the account of the nominative in "tuba lahum" must differ from what we related from the Arabic scholars. The report from the Messenger of God is that "tuba" is the name of a tree in Paradise; that being so, it is a proper noun like Zayd and Amr, and so "wa husn ma'ab" can only be in the nominative, conjoined to "tuba". As for "and a good return", he says it means a good place to turn back to. Report 20396, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Juwaybir: "and a good return", he said, a good destination. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 444}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
