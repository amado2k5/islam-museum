## Summary
Verse 280 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 280 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-280|2:280]]), pp. 28 to 30. Al-Tabari explains the verse: if one of those from whom you collect your capital, among your debtors, is in hardship over your capital that was owed you before charging usury, then give them time until they are at ease. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 28} He notes that the word for one in hardship is in the nominative, either because kana is complete there or because its predicate is omitted, the Arabs omitting predicates of indefinites, so that if kana is taken as the complete verb the sense is also sound, namely if one in hardship among your debtors is found owing your capital; and it is reported that this is in Ubayy ibn Ka'b's reading with the accusative, meaning if the debtor is in hardship, which is allowed in Arabic but which he holds impermissible as a reading because it differs from the written copies of the Muslims' codices; this is al-Tabari's choice and reasoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29} The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29}

As for the deferral to ease, he says it means you must give him time until ease, as in the verse on the fidya for illness at ([[passage/quran-2-196|2:196]]), and he refers to earlier discussion of the nominative; the word for ease is a maf'ala form from yusr, and the sense is that you must give him time until he has ease by means of the debt owed you, so that he becomes one of the people of ease through it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29} The editor corrects a phrase in manuscript and print, saying letters were joined in the old source the copyist misread, and thinks another reading would be right in sense; this correction is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29}

The interpreters said likewise. 6277, Ibn Abbas: it came down about usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} (No report numbered 6276 is visible in the pages read.) 6278, Ibn Sirin: a man disputed with another before Shurayh, who ruled against him and ordered him imprisoned, and a man beside Shurayh said he is in hardship and God speaks in His Book about the one in hardship, and Shurayh said that is about usury, and God said in His Book that He orders giving trusts back to their people and judging with justice among people, referring to the verse at ([[passage/quran-4-58|4:58]]), and God does not command us something and then punish us for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} 6279, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i: that is about usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} 6280, al-Sha'bi: al-Rabi ibn Khuthaym was owed a right by a man and used to come and stand at his door and say, so-and-so, if you are at ease, pay, and if you are in hardship, then to ease; the editor corrects the chain and the name in manuscript and print. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} These accounts of Shurayh, al-Nakha'i and others are what they relate and are not a legal ruling by the museum. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's choice and reasoning are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.

Report 6281, Muhammad (Ibn Sirin): a man came to Shurayh and kept saying that someone was in hardship; the narrator took him to be speaking about a prisoner, and Shurayh said usury had existed in this clan of the Ansar and God then sent down the ruling on deferral ([[passage/quran-2-280|2:280]]), adding that God also commands the return of trusts to their people, so He would not command something and then punish for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 31} 6282, Qatada: the deferral is on the capital. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 31} 6283, Ibn Abbas (through his family chain): the command to defer the one in hardship is about usury; there is no deferral in a trust, which is returned to its people; the editor flags a copyist's misreading in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 31} 6284, al-Suddi: the deferral is on the capital and ease means wealth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 31} 6285, Ibn Jurayj from Ibn Abbas: this concerns usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 31} 6286, al-Dahhak: it concerns usury, which the people of the Jahiliyya traded in, and when some of them accepted Islam they were ordered to take only their capital; the editor corrects the name of a transmitter from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 31}

6287, Ibn Abbas (through Ali ibn Abi Talha): the one meant is the debtor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 32} 6288, Abu Ja'far (Muhammad ibn Ali) answered with the word death, and 6289 gives the same from Muhammad ibn Ali; neither report says what he meant by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 32} 6290 and 6292, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i: this is about usury; 6291, Ibrahim on a man who marries with the dower deferred until ease: until death or separation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 32} 6293 and 6294, Mujahid: the creditor defers him and does not add to the debt, whereas in the Jahiliyya, when a debt fell due and the debtor had nothing, the creditor added to it and deferred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 32}

Others said the verse is general, covering anyone owed a right by a person in hardship, whatever its origin, a lawful debt or usury. 6295, al-Dahhak: every debt on a Muslim is the same; whoever knows his brother's hardship may not jail him or press him until God eases him, and deferral was set for lawful debts, so debts follow that rule. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 33} 6296, Ibn Abbas (through Mujahid): it came down about debt; for the teacher of this chain (Ali ibn Harb) the editor reports al-Nasa'i's word "salih" and Abu Hatim's "saduq" and a death date of 265, which is the editor's note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 33}

Abu Ja'far (al-Tabari) holds that the right view is that the verse concerns the debtors of people who accepted Islam in the Prophet's time and were owed debts on which they had charged usury in the Jahiliyya, Islam reaching them before they collected; God ordered the remaining usury set aside, the capital taken from the well-off and the one in hardship deferred until his ease. The same applies to anyone who accepts Islam owed usury: Islam voids the surplus for the debtor and obliges him to repay the capital if he is well-off. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 33} He adds that although the verse came down about them, the ruling to defer the one in hardship is binding for anyone whose debt is due and who cannot pay. His reasoning is that the creditor's right lies in the debtor's property and obligation, not in his person. A creditor's money can only be in one of three places. If it were in a specific property, the debt would vanish when that property perished, which no one says. If it were in the debtor's person, it would vanish at his death even if he left many times the amount, which no one says either. So it is in his obligation, paid from his property; when he has none there is no claim on his person and no basis for jailing a man who is not withholding a right he can pay. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 34} This argument and choice are al-Tabari's. The editor comments on differences between manuscript and print in this passage (for example the words for "his delaying" and "his person") and praises al-Tabari's strong argument and logic; these are the editor's remarks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 35}

On the next clause, the one about giving charity, al-Tabari says it means that remitting your capital to this debtor is better than deferring him until you can collect it, for those who know the merit of charity and the reward God set for whoever lifts the debt from a debtor in hardship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 35} The interpreters differed. Some said it means giving the capital as charity to the rich and poor among them: 6297, Qatada, that they keep their capital without profit or surplus and that giving up the principal is better for them; 6298 gives the same; 6299, 6300 and 6301, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, that it is out of their capitals. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 36} Others said it means charity to the one in hardship: 6302, al-Suddi, giving the capital to the poor, and al-Abbas did give his up; 6303, al-Rabi', that if you give him your capital as charity it is better for you; 6304, al-Dahhak, that it applies to the debtor in hardship and not the well-off, from whom the capital is taken, taking from the poor being lawful but charity better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 36} 6305, al-Dahhak: better than deferral, so God preferred charity over deferral; 6306, Ibn Zayd: better than the deferral; 6307, al-Dahhak: deferral is obligatory, charity is better and is for every debtor in hardship, not the well-off. The editor adds notes on a rare verbal noun and on the idiom "he chose one over another" meaning he preferred it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 37} Al-Tabari prefers the second view, charity to the one in hardship, because it follows the nearest mention of the ruling, and attaching a clause to what is next to it is dearer to him than attaching it to what is distant; this is his choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 37}

He then notes it was said that these verses on usury are the last revealed in the Qur'an. 6308, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib: Umar ibn al-Khattab said the last thing revealed was the usury verse and the Prophet was taken before explaining it, so leave usury and what raises doubt; 6309, 'Amir (al-Sha'bi): Umar stood, praised God and said he did not know whether they might command something unsuitable or forbid something suitable, that the usury verses were among the last revealed and the Prophet died before clarifying them, so leave what doubts you for what does not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 38} The editor locates the first report in Ahmad's Musnad and Ibn Majah and in Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti, and grades its chain weak because of a break, since Sa'id did not hear from Umar; he likewise grades the second chain weak because al-Sha'bi did not meet Umar, quoting Ibn Hajar's Fath al-Bari that it is broken. He then cites Ibn Kathir's version from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri through Hayyaj ibn Bistam, on whom the critics differ (Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in and Ibn Abi Hatim weakened him; al-Makki ibn Ibrahim and al-Dhuhli authenticated him), and concludes, on a route through al-Khatib, that this chain is sound. These gradings are the editor's and not the museum's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 38} 6310, Ibn Abbas (through al-Sha'bi): the last revealed to the Prophet was the usury verse, and they command things without knowing whether they harm. The editor notes that al-Bukhari gives only its first part, and quotes Ibn Hajar that "last" means the late revelation of the Baqara verses on usury, while the prohibition of usury itself came much earlier, as the verse in Al Imran in the Uhud account shows ([[passage/quran-3-130|3:130]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 39}

These reports are given as they were transmitted; al-Tabari's choice and argument are his, and the documentation and gradings are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.
