## Summary
Verse 280 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 280 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-280|2:280]]), pp. 28 to 30. Al-Tabari explains the verse: if one of those from whom you collect your capital, among your debtors, is in hardship over your capital that was owed you before charging usury, then give them time until they are at ease. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 28} He notes that the word for one in hardship is in the nominative, either because kana is complete there or because its predicate is omitted, the Arabs omitting predicates of indefinites, so that if kana is taken as the complete verb the sense is also sound, namely if one in hardship among your debtors is found owing your capital; and it is reported that this is in Ubayy ibn Ka'b's reading with the accusative, meaning if the debtor is in hardship, which is allowed in Arabic but which he holds impermissible as a reading because it differs from the written copies of the Muslims' codices; this is al-Tabari's choice and reasoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29} The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29}

As for the deferral to ease, he says it means you must give him time until ease, as in the verse on the fidya for illness at ([[passage/quran-2-196|2:196]]), and he refers to earlier discussion of the nominative; the word for ease is a maf'ala form from yusr, and the sense is that you must give him time until he has ease by means of the debt owed you, so that he becomes one of the people of ease through it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29} The editor corrects a phrase in manuscript and print, saying letters were joined in the old source the copyist misread, and thinks another reading would be right in sense; this correction is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 29}

The interpreters said likewise. 6277, Ibn Abbas: it came down about usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} (No report numbered 6276 is visible in the pages read.) 6278, Ibn Sirin: a man disputed with another before Shurayh, who ruled against him and ordered him imprisoned, and a man beside Shurayh said he is in hardship and God speaks in His Book about the one in hardship, and Shurayh said that is about usury, and God said in His Book that He orders giving trusts back to their people and judging with justice among people, referring to the verse at ([[passage/quran-4-58|4:58]]), and God does not command us something and then punish us for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} 6279, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i: that is about usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} 6280, al-Sha'bi: al-Rabi ibn Khuthaym was owed a right by a man and used to come and stand at his door and say, so-and-so, if you are at ease, pay, and if you are in hardship, then to ease; the editor corrects the chain and the name in manuscript and print. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 30} We stop at 6280; report 6281 begins at the foot of p. 30. These accounts of Shurayh, al-Nakha'i and others are what they relate and are not a legal ruling by the museum. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's choice and reasoning are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
