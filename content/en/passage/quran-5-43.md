**Commentary on verse 43: "how do they make you judge when they have the Torah with God's ruling in it", pp. 336-337**

Al-Tabari says: how can these Jews, Muhammad, make you judge between them, accepting you as arbiter, when they have the Torah I sent down to Moses, which they confess to be true and My book, and know that what ruling is in it is My ruling, without denying it, and that My ruling in it on the married adulterer is stoning; yet despite knowing this they turn away, that is, leave judging by it, in boldness against Me and disobedience. Though addressed to the Prophet this is a reproach to the Jews: how can you accept the judgement of My prophet Muhammad while denying his prophethood, when you leave My ruling that you confess is true and binding on you, which Moses brought; you are all the more likely to leave My ruling that My prophet reports, given your denial of his prophethood.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 336}

Then God reports on these Jews and on their like who deviate from His ruling: "they are not believers", that is, whoever turns away from God's ruling in the book He sent down on His prophet is not one who believes God and His messenger and confesses His oneness and His prophet's prophethood, for this is not the act of the people of faith. The root of turning away is withdrawing from something. Report 12002, from 'Abdallah ibn Kathir, says their turning away is what they left of God's book; report 12003, from Ibn 'Abbas, says God's ruling means God's penalties; report 12004, from Qatada, says it is God's clarification of what they quarrelled over about their slain man; report 12005, from al-Suddi, says it is stoning.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 337}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
