## Summary
Verse 105 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 105 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "thus We vary the signs, and so that they may say 'you have studied', and so that We make it clear to a people who know", al-Tabari says: as I have varied for you, people, the signs and proofs in this sura and set them out, making you know them in the matter of my oneness and of the truth of my messenger and my book, and brought you to stand upon them, so I make clear to you my signs and proofs in whatever you were ignorant of in my command and prohibition. Report 13705, from al-Suddi: We vary the signs for those who set up equals to their Lord, as We varied them in this sura, so that they may not say "you have studied". The editor notes a corrected word in the print. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 25}

The readers differed over "darasta". Most of the readers of Medina and Kufa read "darasta", meaning "you have read", without the alif; a group of the early readers, among them Ibn Abbas (with differing reports from him), a group of the Followers and some of the readers of Basra read "darasta" with an alif ("darasta"), meaning "you have read with and learned from the People of the Book"; Qatada is reported to have read "durisat", "it has been read and recited", and al-Hasan "darasat", "it has been effaced" (al-Farra' glossed it as "it has grown old"). The editor corrects the print against the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 26}

Al-Tabari chooses: the best of the readings in my view is the reading "darasta" with the meaning "you have read and learned", because the polytheists used to say that to the Prophet, and God reported their saying in al-Nahl (16:103), where they say that a human being teaches him; so they were saying that Muhammad merely learns what he brings you from someone else. Hence the reading "darasta", in the sense of "you learned from the People of the Book", is closer to the truth and the sounder choice than the reading "darasta" with an alif, "you read with them and disputed with them", and the other readings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 26}

The interpreters differed according to the readers' differences. Of those among the early authorities who read "darasta" and took it as "you learned and read": report 13706, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha: they said "you have read and learned", this being said by Quraysh; report 13707, from Mujahid: you read and learned; report 13708, from Ibn Abbas (through Abu Ishaq, from al-Tamimi): you read and learned; report 13709, from al-Suddi: you read the books; report 13710, from al-Dahhak: you learned and read; and report 13711, from al-Tamimi: I said to Ibn Abbas, "What do you say of the word 'darasta'?" and he said, "You read and learned". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 27}

Al-Tabari continues with those among the early authorities who read "darasta" with an alif and took it as "you disputed". Report 13712, from Ibn Abbas, is the same as before; 13713, from Ibn Abbas: you read with them; 13714, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, that Ibn Abbas read it so ("I think he said: you read with the People of the Book"); 13715 and 13716, from al-Tamimi, from Ibn Abbas: you read with them and learned; 13717 and 13718, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, that Ibn Abbas used to read it with the alif, the sin unvowelled and the ta vowelled with fatha; and 13719, from Amr ibn Kaysan, that Ibn Abbas read it the same way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 28}

The rest of report 13719 gives Ibn Abbas's gloss: you recited, disputed, argued; report 13720 gives the same from him; 13721 and 13722, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: you read with them; 13723 says he read it so with the sense "you copied from them"; 13724, 13725 and 13727, from Mujahid: you read to the Jews and they read to you (13724 adds "you discussed matters with them"); 13726, from al-Dahhak: that is, the People of the Book; and 13728, through Ibn Sa'd from Ibn Abbas: they said "you studied with the People of the Book and read the books and learned them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 29}

Next those who read "durisat", "it was recited and read", in the passive: report 13729, from Qatada: that is, it was read and learned; 13730, from him: it was read, and in the letter (codex reading) of Ibn Mas'ud it is "darasa". Then those who read "darasat", "it has been effaced and has grown old", meaning that what you recite to us has long passed us by and its time has stretched on (the editor refers to al-Farra'): report 13731, from Qatada, that al-Hasan used to read "darasat", "it was effaced"; 13732, from Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani, on Ibn Mas'ud's reading "darasat" without an alif; and 13733, from Amr ibn Dinar: I heard Ibn al-Zubayr say that some boys here read "darasta" but it is "darasat". The editor corrects a word in the print and manuscript and explains an old usage of "waqf" as "sukun". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 30}

Report 13734, from al-Hasan: it has grown old and been effaced. Others read "darasa", from "darasa the thing", to recite it: report 13735, from Harun, that in the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b and Ibn Mas'ud it is "darasa", meaning that the Prophet read; one may say now "darasta" and now "darasa", addressing him at one time and speaking of him at another, because of the saying. Al-Tabari says he has already shown which of these readings is best and the proof of his choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 31}

On "and so that We make it clear to a people who know", he says: as We varied the signs, lessons and proofs in this sura for those who set up equals to their Lord, so We vary the signs for them elsewhere, so that they do not say to Our messenger, "you merely learned what you bring us from the People of the Book", and so be restrained from denying him and from inventing falsehood against him; and so that, by varying the signs, We make the truth clear to a people who know the truth when it is made plain to them, follow it and accept it, unlike those who, when it is made plain to them, are blind to it, fail to grasp it and grow further from understanding it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 31}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
