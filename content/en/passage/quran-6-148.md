## Summary
Verse 148 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 148 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 148, "those who set up partners will say: had God willed, we would not have set up partners": al-Tabari says they are those who made idols and images equal to God, among the idolaters of Quraysh. They said it as a refuge from submitting to the truth by a false argument, when the truth had become clear to them and they knew the falsehood of the polytheism and of the forbidding of tillage and livestock in which they persisted, as the earlier verses had set out. Their claim: had God wanted of us faith in Him and singling Him out for worship rather than idols, and making lawful what we forbade of the bahira, the sa'iba and the rest, we would not have made a partner for Him, nor our fathers before us, nor forbidden what we forbid, since He is able to come between us and that: either by compelling us to faith, to leaving polytheism and to declaring lawful what we forbade, or by dealing gently with us through His granting of success, so that we would come to affirm His oneness, leave the worship of equals and make lawful what we forbade. But He approved of what we are upon and willed what we forbid, and did not come between us and it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 208}

God said, belying them in their claim that He approved the polytheism they were upon and the prohibitions they made, and answering the falseness of their argument: "thus those before them denied", meaning: as these idolaters denied what you brought of truth and clear exposition, so those before them among the sinning nations, who were insolent against their Lord, denied what their prophets brought them of God's signs and clear proofs and rejected their counsel, "until they tasted Our might", that is, until they angered Us and We became angry with them and sent down Our might on them; they tasted it, were ruined, failed and lost this world and the next. These later ones are being led along their path if they do not turn back, believe and affirm what you brought from your Lord. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "tasted". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 209}

He gives (14129) from Ibn Abbas on "had God willed, we would not have set up partners, nor our fathers", then "thus those before them denied", then "had God willed, they would not have set up partners": that they said "we worship them only that they bring us near to God", and God told them they do not bring them near; and the sense of "had God willed, they would not have set up partners" is that had He willed He would have gathered them all on guidance. And (14130) and (14131) from Mujahid that "nor forbidden anything" is the saying of Quraysh, without certainty, that God forbade this bahira and sa'iba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 209}

If one asks what proof there is that God was denying the idolaters' claim that He was pleased with their worship of idols and wanted them to forbid what they forbade of crops and cattle, rather than denying their saying "had God willed, we would not have set up partners" and their description of Him as having willed their shirk, their fathers' and their forbidding, al-Tabari answers that the proof is the words "thus those before them denied". God reports that they followed, in denying their Prophet what he brought them (the prohibition of worshipping anything but God and of forbidding what God did not forbid in His book and by His Messenger), the way of their predecessors among the nations that denied God and His messenger. Denial is of something denied; had the verse reported their lying in what they said, it would read "lied" with the dhal unmarked by doubling and would charge them with lying against God, not with denial; and there are many further reasons that would lengthen the book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 210}

Then God addresses His Prophet in "say: do you have any knowledge that you can bring forth to us?": say, Muhammad, to these idolaters who equate idols with their Lord, who forbid what they forbid of crops and cattle and who say that God is pleased with what they do: do you have, for your claim that God is pleased with your shirk and your forbidding, certain knowledge from the report of one whose report removes all excuse, or a proof that gives us certainty? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 210}

"Bring it forth to us" means show and make it plain, as the errors of your words and deeds were made plain to you, together with their contradiction and impossibility in reason and in what is heard. "You follow only conjecture" means that what you say, the idols you worship and the crops and cattle you forbid rest only on a supposition that it is true and that you are on the right, while it is false. "You only guess" means you only attribute falsehood to God by conjecture, with no certain knowledge or clear proof. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "bringing forth" and on "guessing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 211}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
