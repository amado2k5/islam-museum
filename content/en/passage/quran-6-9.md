## Summary
Verse 9 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 9 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "had we made him an angel we would have made him a man" al-Tabari says: had we made our messenger to these who hold others equal to me, who say "why has no angel been sent down with Muhammad to confirm him", an angel sent down to them from heaven to bear witness to Muhammad's truth and bid them follow him, we would have made him in the form of a man, since they cannot see an angel in his own form; so whether I sent them an angel or a man is the same, as an angel comes down in human form, and my arguments in both cases stand that you are truthful and what you brought them is true. Report 13084, from Ibn Abbas: he would have come to them only in the form of a man, since they cannot look at angels. 13085, from Mujahid: in the form of a man, in a man's build. 13086, 13087 and 13088, from Qatada: in the form of a human; 13089, from Ibn Zayd: we would have made that angel in the form of a man and not sent him in angelic form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 269}

Then "and we would have confused them in what they confuse": had we sent an angel from heaven confirming you, Muhammad, and witnessing for you before these who hold others equal to me and deny your signs, to the truth of your prophethood, and made him in the form of a man of Adam's children since they cannot bear to see an angel in the form I created him in, his case would be confused to them and they would not know whether he is an angel or a human; they would not be sure he is an angel nor believe in him, and would say this is no angel; and we would confuse them in what they confuse for themselves of the truth of your matter, the soundness of your proof and the witness to your prophethood. One says "labastu alayhim al-amr albisuhu" of mixing a matter for them, and "labistu al-thawb" of wearing, and "labus" is a name for clothes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 270}

The editor keeps "human" from the manuscript, refers for "confusion" and "garment" to volumes 6, 3 and 1, and notes that another report on this verse in volume 1 is not repeated here, one of the ways he abridged his commentary.

The interpreters said the like: 13090, from Ibn Abbas: we would have made it seem otherwise to them. 13091, from Qatada: no people confuse themselves but God confuses them, and the confusion is from the people. 13092, from al-Suddi: we made seem to them what they make seem to themselves. Another view is reported from Ibn Abbas: 13093, "we would have confused them in what they confuse" are the People of the Book, who left their religion, denied their messengers and shifted words from their places. 13094, from al-Dahhak: it means distortion; they are the People of the Book, who divided their books and religion and denied their messengers, so God confused them in what they confused for themselves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 271}

Al-Tabari has already shown that these verses from the start of the surah are likelier to concern the idol-worshipping polytheists than the Jews and Christians of the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 271}

Verse 9 closes here at p. 271, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-10|6:10]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
