## Summary
Verse 172 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 172 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 172 (pp. 399-403, reports 8233-8241).** Al-Tabari says the verse concerns the believers who answered the call of God and His Messenger after the wounds and cuts that had struck them: those who followed the Prophet to Hamra' al-Asad in pursuit of the enemy, Abu Sufyan and the Qurayshi polytheists with him, as they withdrew from Uhud. When Abu Sufyan withdrew, the Prophet went out after him as far as Hamra' al-Asad, eight miles from Medina, so that people would see that he and his companions had strength against their enemy. The note refers to the earlier explanation of the word for "wound" (the reference is printed as 237 and 7). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 399}

**'Ikrima's report on the setting out (8233, pp. 399-400).** Through Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from Husayn ibn 'Abdallah from 'Ikrima: Uhud fell on a Saturday in mid-Shawwal; on the next day, Sunday, sixteen nights after Shawwal had begun, the Prophet's caller announced pursuit of the enemy and that none should go out with them except those who had been present the day before. Jabir ibn 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr ibn Haram spoke to him, saying his father had left him in charge of seven sisters and told him it was not fitting to leave them without a man, nor would he prefer him over himself in fighting with the Messenger; so he had stayed with them. The Prophet gave him leave and he went out with him. The Prophet went out to frighten the enemy, to let them know he had come out after them so that they would think him strong and that what befell him had not weakened him against them. The notes say that the word "day" at the start is an addition from Ibn Hisham's Sira and al-Tabari's Tarikh; that the manuscript and printed text read "Hassan ibn 'Abdallah", an error, the correct form being from the Tarikh, Husayn ibn 'Abdallah ibn 'Ubaydallah ibn al-'Abbas ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib, who related from 'Ikrima and from whom Hisham ibn 'Urwa, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn al-Mubarak and Ibn Ishaq related, and who is weak in hadith according to the editor, with an entry in the Tahdhib; and that the account is in Ibn Hisham (3: 106-107) and the Tarikh (3: 28). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 399} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 400}

**The man of Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal (8234, p. 400).** Through Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from 'Abdallah ibn Kharija ibn Zayd ibn Thabit from Abu al-Sa'ib, freedman of 'A'isha bint 'Uthman: a man among the Prophet's companions of Banu 'Abd al-Ashhal who was at Uhud said that he and a brother returned wounded; when the Prophet's caller summoned them to pursue the enemy, he said to his brother, or his brother said to him, that an expedition with the Messenger must not pass them by, though they had no mount and each was heavily wounded. They went out; he was the more lightly wounded, and whenever his brother was overcome he carried him for a stretch and the brother walked a stretch, until they reached where the Muslims had arrived. The Prophet reached Hamra' al-Asad, eight miles from Medina, stayed three days, Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday, and then returned to Medina. The note explains "'uqba" as the distance a walker can go as far as he is able, here carrying him a leg and walking a leg; the account is in Ibn Hisham (3: 107-108) and the Tarikh (3: 28). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 400}

**Reports 8235-8238 (pp. 401-402).** Ibn Ishaq (8235): the verse is about those who travelled with the Prophet on the morning after Uhud to Hamra' al-Asad despite the pain of their wounds (Ibn Hisham, 3: 128). Qatada (8236), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: this was on the day of Uhud after the killing and wounds, once the polytheists, Abu Sufyan and his companions, had gone; the Prophet asked his companions whether there was no band to volunteer for God's cause and seek out its enemy, which would be more telling on the enemy and farther for the hearing, and a band went off with what God knows of their hardship. The note says the printed text has "tashudd" and the manuscript "tashaddad", which mean nothing and are certainly corruptions of the form the editor adopted ("tantadib"), which is the wording that recurs often in the Hamra' al-Asad reports. Al-Suddi (8237), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: Abu Sufyan went off from Uhud and, some way along, they regretted it and said that they had done badly, having killed the Muslims and then left them when only fugitives remained; they should return and wipe them out. God cast terror into their hearts and they were routed; God informed His Messenger, who pursued them to Hamra' al-Asad, after which they went back; the verse came down about them. The note says the manuscript has a different wording by oversight and the printed text is right, and that the report came earlier as 8003 (see the note there). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 401}

Ibn 'Abbas (8238), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather: God cast terror into Abu Sufyan's heart on the day of Uhud after what he had done, and he went back to Mecca. The Prophet said that Abu Sufyan had gained something from them, then gone back with terror cast into his heart. Uhud was in Shawwal, and traders used to come to Medina in Dhu al-Qa'da and camp at Badr al-Sughra once a year; they came after the battle, when the believers were wounded and complained of it to the Prophet, for what had befallen them was hard on them. The Prophet called people to set out with him and to follow what they had been following, saying that the traders would now depart and come to the pilgrimage and not find the like until the following year. Satan came and frightened his allies by saying that people had massed against them, and the people refused to follow; the Prophet said he would go even if no one followed him, to urge the people on. Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, 'Umar, 'Uthman, 'Ali, al-Zubayr, Sa'd, Talha, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman and Abu 'Ubayda ibn al-Jarrah, seventy men in all, volunteered, and they went after Abu Sufyan as far as al-Safra', and the verse was revealed. The note on p. 402 says the word is "li-ahuddid" in the manuscript, the printed text and Ibn Kathir (2: 298), that al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 101) omits it, that the editor judges "li-yuhaddid" to be the likely form, and that he has no doubt this wording is not the Prophet's own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 402}

**Reports 8239 and 8241 from 'A'isha, and 8240 from Ibn Jurayj (pp. 402-403).** 'A'isha (8239), through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim from Hashim ibn al-Qasim from Abu Sa'id from Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father: she told 'Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr, "Nephew, your father and your grandfather", meaning Abu Bakr and al-Zubayr, were among those about whom the verse came down. The editor's note (begun on p. 402 and finished on p. 403) says Hashim ibn al-Qasim is Abu al-Nadr, an imam and hafiz, teacher of Ahmad, Ishaq and Ibn al-Madini, trustworthy, firm and a proof, of whom the people of Baghdad were proud; Abu Sa'id is al-Mu'addib, Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Abi al-Wadda' al-Qudai, trustworthy and reliable; al-Hakim relates it in the Mustadrak (2: 298) through al-'Abbas ibn Muhammad al-Duri from Hashim ibn al-Qasim by this chain, the printed Mustadrak having "Hisham ibn al-Qasim", which the editor takes as a printer's error; al-Hakim called it sound on the conditions of the two shaykhs, who did not extract it, and al-Dhahabi agreed. The report is in the Sahihayn, as will come at 8241; the editor suggests al-Hakim and al-Dhahabi may have counted it among the additions because here she addresses 'Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr while the later report has her addressing 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, who are brothers and the speech is the same; al-Hakim also relates it a second time like Muslim's version. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 402} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 403}

Ibn Jurayj (8240), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: he was told that when Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and his companions left on the day of Uhud, the Muslims told the Prophet that they were heading for Medina. He said that if they rode the horses and left the baggage animals they were heading for it, but if they sat on the baggage animals and left the horses, God had terrified them and they were not. They rode the baggage animals, and God terrified them. Then the Prophet called some people to follow them so that they would see strength in them, and they followed for two or three nights, and the verse came down. The note on p. 403 says the printed text has "ar'abahum" and the manuscript "ra''abahum", as the editor adopted, and that this is correct, since the lexicographers state that one says "ra'abahu" or "ra''abahu" but not "ar'abahu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 403}

'A'isha (8241), through Sa'id ibn al-Rabi' from Sufyan from Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father: 'A'isha told him that both his parents were among those who answered the call after the wounds, meaning Abu Bakr and al-Zubayr. The note says Sa'id ibn al-Rabi' al-Razi is al-Tabari's teacher, from whom he narrated before (3791), and the editor found no entry for him; that the report repeats 8239 but with the address to 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr rather than his brother 'Abdallah, both being sons of her sister Asma' bint Abi Bakr; and that Muslim relates it (2: 241) by several chains through Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father, and through Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid from al-Bahi, that is 'Abdallah al-Bahi, freedman of Mus'ab ibn al-Zubayr, from 'Urwa, to similar effect. This note continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 403}
