## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 3 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of the commentary on verse 3: the first view (pp. 531-533, reports 8456-8461).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said the sense is: if you fear, guardians of orphans, that you will not be fair over their dowry and bring it to the dowries of women like them, then do not marry them, and marry others, the women outside the family whom God made lawful to you, from one to four; and if you fear injustice when you marry more than one, keep to one or to the other category the verse names, women under ownership. 'A'isha (8456), through Ibn Humayd from Ibn al-Mubarak from Ma'mar from al-Zuhri from 'Urwa: nephew, she is the orphan girl in her guardian's care whose property and beauty attract him and who wants to marry her for less than the customary dowry; they were forbidden to marry them unless they dealt fairly with them in completing the dowry, and were ordered to marry other women. The editor's note says al-Tabari narrates the report, at length and in brief, with seven chains (8456-8461), that it is firmly established in the two Sahihs and elsewhere, that this chain is Ibn al-Mubarak from Ma'mar from al-Zuhri and 8460 will come through 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar referring back to this wording, that al-Bukhari narrated it in his Sahih twelve times, which he will point out, and that al-Bayhaqi narrated it in al-Sunan al-Kubra (7: 141-142) with several chains. 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr (8457), through Yunus ibn 'Abd al-A'la from Ibn Wahb from Yunus ibn Yazid from Ibn Shihab, asked 'A'isha, wife of the Prophet, about the verse, and she said much the same, including that they were forbidden to marry them unless they dealt fairly and brought them to the highest of their customary dowry; then Yunus ibn Yazid said Rabi'a said of it: leave them, for I have made four lawful to you. The note says al-Layth's narration from Yunus comes at 8459; that al-Bukhari (5: 94-95, Fath) and Muslim (2: 398-399) narrate it, Muslim's longer than here but without Rabi'a's closing words, which are not part of the report proper; that al-Bukhari also narrates it (9: 91, Fath) through Hassan ibn Ibrahim from Yunus, abridged and without Rabi'a's word; that the phrase "the highest of their custom in the dowry" is as established in Sahih Muslim and the manuscript has another word, apparently a copyist's slip. 'Urwa (8458), through al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd and Sa'id ibn Maslama from Isma'il ibn Umayya from Ibn Shihab: I asked 'A'isha, Mother of the Believers, and she said much the same, that they were forbidden to marry them unless they completed the dowry and then ordered to marry others. On this chain the editor notes: al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd ibn Abi Ja'far al-Bazzar al-Baghdadi is reliable, Ibn Khuzayma took from him in his Sahih, and Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Khatib mention him without impugning him; al-Mizzi names him "al-Husayn", and Ibn Hajar followed but stated that the vowels are the ones for al-Hasan, which is right; Sa'id ibn Maslama ibn Hisham ibn 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan is weak, al-Bukhari saying "there is something in him" and that he has objectionable reports, Ibn Ma'in "nothing" and Abu Hatim "weak and objectionable in hadith"; the printed edition reads "and Abu Sa'id", an error from writing a short form for "he told us"; Isma'il ibn Umayya al-Umawi's biography passed at 2615; and the weakness of this chain because of Sa'id does not prevent the report being sound in itself through other routes. 'Urwa (8459), through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih from al-Layth from Yunus from Ibn Shihab, like Yunus's report from Ibn Wahb; 'A'isha (8460), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar, like Ibn Humayd's from Ibn al-Mubarak, the note saying these two repeat 8456 and 8457. 'A'isha (8461), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj from Hisham from his father: it came down about the orphan girl with a man, she having property; perhaps he marries her for her property while she does not please him, then beats her and treats her badly; so he was admonished. The note says al-Qasim is ibn al-Hasan, al-Husayn is ibn Dawud with a nickname the scan renders unclearly (he refers to 8398), and Hajjaj is ibn Muhammad al-Misisi al-A'war, whose biography passed at 1691 and whom his brother Mahmud also treated in volume 6, p. 548, note 3. These statements, including their rulings on marriage and dowry, are given as transmitted with their chains and the museum gives no verdict.

**The close of the note on report 8461 (p. 534).** Here the editor continues his note from p. 533: al-Bukhari narrates it (8: 179, Fath) through Hisham ibn Yusuf from Ibn Jurayj, similarly, but its wording suggests it came down about one named person; the Hafiz said that what is known from Hisham ibn 'Urwa is the general sense, and so al-Isma'ili narrated it through Hajjaj ibn Muhammad from Ibn Jurayj, which is the narration of Hajjaj found in al-Tabari; al-Bukhari also narrates it in a second place in Fath (the page number is unclear in the scan) through Abu Usama from Hisham ibn 'Urwa, in the right form; Muslim (2: 399) through Abu Usama; al-Bukhari (9: 119) through 'Abda ibn Sulayman from Hisham; it comes again at 8477 through Waki' from Hisham, where he will trace it; and he then lists its other routes in the two Sahihs: al-Bukhari (5: 94-95) and Muslim (2: 399) through Salih from al-Zuhri from 'Urwa; al-Bukhari (5: 292; 9: 169-170; 12: 298) through Shu'ayb from al-Zuhri; (9: 117 and 169-170) through 'Uqayl from al-Zuhri; and (9: 162) through Abu Mu'awiya from Hisham ibn 'Urwa, abridged. Ibn Kathir mentions 'A'isha's report (2: 342-343) from two of al-Bukhari's narrations and adds nothing to the tracing; al-Suyuti (2: 118) gives it in three wordings, long and short, and adds 'Abd ibn Humayd, al-Nasa'i, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim.

**Second view: the ban on more than four, to protect the orphans' property (pp. 534-535, reports 8462-8465).** Al-Tabari says that on the previous view the answer to the condition is the command to marry. Others said the sense is rather the prohibition of marrying more than four, out of caution for the orphans' property lest their guardians waste it: a Qurashi man would marry ten women, or more or fewer, and when he became poor turned to his ward's property and spent it or married with it; they were forbidden this and told: if you fear for your orphans' property that you will spend it, then do not be equitable in it because of your need and the cost of your wives' upkeep, so do not exceed four in the number of women, and if you fear even with four that you will not be equitable with their property, keep to one or to ownership. The editor's note: the printed edition has "caution for the orphans' property" and he set the manuscript's reading. 'Ikrima (8462), through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba from Simak: a man of Quraysh would have several wives and some orphans, his money would go and he would turn to the orphans' money, and this verse came down. 'Ikrima (8463), through Hannad ibn al-Sari from Abu al-Ahwas from Simak: a man would marry four, five, six or ten, saying what stops me from marrying as so-and-so married, and take his ward's money and marry with it; so they were forbidden to marry above four. Ibn 'Abbas (8464), through Sufyan ibn Waki' from his father from Sufyan from Habib ibn Abi Thabit from Tawus: men were limited to four because of the orphans' property. Ibn 'Abbas (8465), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father from his uncle from his father from his father: a man would marry with the orphan's money as many as God willed, and God forbade that.

**Third view: as you fear for orphans, fear for women (pp. 535-539, reports 8466-8474).** Others said the people felt scruple about the orphans' property, lest they be unfair in it, but none about women, so they were told: as you fear being unfair to orphans, fear it with women, and marry of them only from one to four, no more; and if you fear being unfair also in going beyond one, marry only the one you do not fear to wrong: one, or what is under ownership. Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8466), through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim from Ibn 'Ulayya from Ayyub: the people were on their pre-Islamic ways except what they were ordered or forbidden; they mentioned orphans and the verse came down; as you feared not being just to orphans, so fear it with women. Al-Suddi (8467), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: they were strict over orphans and not over women, one marrying several wives without treating them alike; God said: as you fear not being equitable among orphans, fear it with women, so marry one up to four, and if you fear, one or what is under ownership. Qatada (8468), through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Sa'id, to the end of the verse: as you feared injustice to orphans and it grieved you, so fear it in combining women; in the Jahiliyya a man might marry ten or fewer, and God made four lawful and then limited them to four; if you fear not being equitable among four, then three, or else two, or else one; and if you fear in one, what is under ownership. The notes say the manuscript has "all women" where "combining" is right as in the printed edition, and that the printed edition added a relative pronoun in a phrase where the manuscript is right and good. Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8469), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar from Ayyub: what is lawful to you of women; fear in women what you feared in orphans, that you not be fair to them. Sa'id (8470), through al-Muthanna from al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal from Hammad from Ayyub: Islam came while people were on their old ways, except what they were ordered and followed or forbidden and avoided, until they asked about orphans and the verse came down. Sa'id (8471), through al-Muthanna from Abu al-Nu'man 'Arim from Hammad ibn Zayd from Ayyub: God sent Muhammad while people were on their old ways except what they were ordered or forbidden; they used to ask him about orphans and it came down; as you fear unfairness to orphans, fear it with women. Ibn 'Abbas (8472), through al-Muthanna from 'Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: in the Jahiliyya they married ten of the unmarried women and treated the matter of the orphan as grave; they lost part of their religion over the orphan and gave up what they used to marry (p. 538), so God forbade them what they used to marry in the Jahiliyya. The editor's note, which carries the number 8473 in the printed edition but treats the chain of 8472, says: 'Abd Allah ibn Salih, al-Layth ibn Sa'd's scribe, was declared reliable at 186; Mu'awiya ibn Salih al-Hadrami was declared reliable at 186 and has entries in al-Tahdhib, al-Bukhari's two histories, Ibn Sa'd, Ibn Abi Hatim, the history of the judges of Cordoba and al-Nubahi's judges of al-Andalus; 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, he showed at 1833, did not hear from Ibn 'Abbas, so the chain is broken and weak for the break; al-Bayhaqi narrates it in al-Sunan al-Kubra (7: 150) through 'Uthman ibn Sa'id from 'Abd Allah ibn Salih with this chain; the Hafiz alludes to it in Fath (8: 179-180) when explaining 'A'isha's report, saying her interpretation came from Ibn 'Abbas likewise and that al-Tabari narrated it; and al-Suyuti (2: 118) gives it and attributes it only to Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim. Al-Dahhak (8473), in what was told from al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj from Abu Mu'adh from 'Ubayd ibn Sulayman: in their pre-Islamic days they took nothing of the orphan's property while marrying ten women and marrying their fathers' wives, so they lost part of their religion over women; God admonished them about orphans and women: for orphans, the passage at [[passage/quran-4-2|4:2]]; for women, this same verse; and elsewhere, [[passage/quran-4-22|4:22]]. Al-Rabi' (8474), in what was told from 'Ammar from Ibn Abi Ja'far from his father: as you feared injustice to orphans and it grieved you, so fear it in combining women (the note says both copies have "all" and "combining" is right, referring to p. 536, note 1); a man in the Jahiliyya married ten or fewer, God made four lawful and limited them to four; if you fear, one, and if you fear in one, what is under ownership.

**Fourth view: fear of fornication (p. 539, reports 8475-8476).** Others said the sense is: as you fear in the matter of orphans, so be wary with women of committing fornication with them, but marry in a good marriage. Mujahid (8475), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: if you felt scruple over the guardianship of orphans and eating their property, out of faith and belief, then feel the same scruple over fornication, and marry women in a good marriage, from two to four; and if you fear being unfair, one or what is under ownership. Mujahid (8476), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih, likewise.

**Fifth view: do not marry the orphan girls you are guardian of (pp. 539-540, reports 8477-8478).** Others said the sense is: if you fear not being fair to the orphan girls whose guardians you are, do not marry them, and marry others lawful to you. 'A'isha (8477), through Sufyan ibn Waki' from his father from Hisham ibn 'Urwa from his father: it came down about the orphan girl who is with a man who is her guardian, she having no other guardian and no one to dispute him, and he marries her not for her property but beats her and treats her badly. In the note the editor says the chain is weak because of Sufyan ibn Waki', whose weakness he has explained often, first at 142-143, but the report is sound in itself, as at 8456-8461 and in the routes he traced from the two Sahihs, and is firmly established through Waki' without his son Sufyan: al-Bukhari (9: 160, Fath) narrates it, longer than here, through Yahya from Waki' with this chain; the Hafiz says in Fath that Yahya, al-Bukhari's teacher, is ibn Musa or ibn Ja'far as he showed in the introduction, and the introduction (p. 236) says Ibn al-Sakan named him Yahya ibn Musa. Al-Hasan (8478), through Humayd ibn Mas'ada from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Yunus: what is lawful to you among your orphans who are your kin.

**Al-Tabari's choice and argument (pp. 540-541).** Abu Ja'far says the most fitting of the views is that of those who said: as you fear for orphans, fear for women, and marry of them only those you do not fear to wrong, from one to four; if you fear injustice even with one, do not marry her, and keep to what is under ownership, which is likelier to keep you from wronging them. His argument: God opened the preceding verse with the ban on eating orphans' property wrongly and mixing it (he is pointing to [[passage/quran-4-2|4:2]]), then told them that if they feared God in that and took care, the scruple required over women is like that required over orphans; and He taught them how to be free of injustice towards women as He had taught the way out in the orphans' property: marry, if you are secure from injustice, whom I have made lawful, two, three or four; and if you also fear injustice over one, in that you cannot treat her fairly, do not marry her, but take concubines from your slaves, for you are likelier not to wrong them, since they are your possessions and property and what is due them is not what is due to free wives, so this is nearer to safety from sin and injustice. This is al-Tabari's choice and argument, given as transmitted, and the museum gives no verdict. He holds that the speech has an omitted part which the apparent wording indicates, the sense being: if you fear not being equitable in the orphans' property, so fear not being equitable in the rights of women that God has made obligatory on you, and marry of them only those with whom you are secure from injustice; if you fear in that, one; if you fear in one, what is under ownership, the clause "so fear likewise" being omitted because the stated wording points to it. Should someone ask where the answer to the first condition is, it is the command to marry, but what shows that al-Tabari's reading is meant is the closing part of the verse urging reduction so as to escape injustice. He has shown earlier that "iqsat" in Arab speech is justice and fairness and "qist" is injustice and wrong (note: 6: 77 and 270), and that "orphans" here is the plural for males and females (note: p. 524, note 3). On p. 540 the editor suggests the better wording "and He told them how the way out is for them", like the phrase after it.

**The good among women, and why "ma" (p. 542, reports 8479-8481m).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: marry those lawful to you, not those forbidden to you. Abu Malik (8479), through Ibn Humayd from Ibn al-Mubarak from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: what is lawful to you; Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8480), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar from Ayyub: what is lawful to you. If someone asks why "ma" was said and not "man", since "ma" is used for what is not persons, the answer is that the sense is not as he supposes but: marry in a good marriage. Mujahid (8481), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: marry women in a good marriage; Mujahid (8481m), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl, likewise. So what is meant is the act, not the persons of the women, hence "ma" and not "man", as one says "take of my slaves what you like" meaning take your wish, whereas if he meant "take whom you like" he would say it with "man"; likewise the phrase on what is under ownership means ownership itself. The note refers to al-Farra, Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 253-254).

**The sense of the command to marry and the deflected number forms (pp. 543-546).** Al-Tabari says the command to marry means that each of you marries a number from these, as is said in [[passage/quran-24-4|24:4]] about flogging; he then explains that the three number forms in the verse are left without tanwin and inflection because they are deflected from "two", "three" and "four", as 'Umar is deflected from 'Amir and Zufar from Zafir, and so "ahad", "thuna'" and "mawhad", and the others, are not inflected for the same reason; and that the evidence that masculine and feminine are alike in them is their use in this sura and in Fatir (35:1) as describing wings, that they are not added to what the definite numbers are added to and do not take the article, so they are a definite name of number; were they indefinite they would take the article and be added as "the three" and "the four" are. The note refers to al-Farra, Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 254-255). He cites a line of Tamim ibn Ubayy ibn Muqbil about blue flies that plague horses and make them start, in which "ahad" and "muthna" appear uninflected because they describe a definite noun; the line is described, not reproduced. The editor's note says it is from a long old poem, traced in al-Farra, al-Hayawan (7: 233) and Lisan al-'Arab, it recurs in this commentary (7: 184, Bulaq), the verses describe the poet's horse, the line's transmissions vary over the words for the flies, "green" and "tick", and he glosses its rare words for the fly, the chest, the striking down and the neighing, and the line after it. Al-Tabari says the Arabs sometimes treat the form as indefinite and inflect it, and he points to a line whose author the editor does not know, about killing among a pair and a single man with four of you, a fifth and a sixth (p. 544); the editor says the line was in both copies a patchwork of two lines, which he restored from al-Farra, that what al-Tabari says here is al-Farra's own wording, and that "sadin" means sixth. Another witness, by Sakhr ibn 'Amr al-Sulami, brother of al-Khansa', that "thuna'" and "ahad" are not inflected, is described by the editor and traced in Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 115), al-Aghani (13: 139), al-Mukhassas, al-Jawaliqi's commentary on Adab al-Katib, al-Batalyawsi and al-Khizana (4: 474), recurring in the commentary (22: 76, Bulaq); he notes that Ibn Qutayba in Adab al-Katib gives a different wording which the publisher of the commentary followed here though the manuscript is right, that the two lines concern the killing of Duraid ibn Hirmila al-Murri and are followed by a line about a wide wound, and that al-Jawaliqi cites other lines of Yazid ibn 'Amr al-Sa'iq al-Kilabi (pp. 544-545). Then a line by 'Amr Dhu al-Kalb (p. 545) wishing that his enemy may meet him alone in a lawful month; the editor says the poet, brother of the Banu Kahil, was a neighbour of Hudhayl, that Abu 'Ubayda in the Majaz wrongly ascribes it to Sakhr al-Ghayy al-Hudhali, traces it in Diwan al-Hudhaliyyin (3: 117), the Majaz (1: 115), al-Ma'ani al-Kabir, al-Mukhassas and al-Aghani, whose author erred in ascribing it to Sakhr ibn 'Amr and in reading "the sacred month"; the poet had met him earlier in a sacred month and could not raise a weapon, and the next line says the fight between them will last only as long as a hand turns from right to left. Al-Tabari says nothing beyond "four" in these forms is heard from the Arabs: no "khumas", "mukhammas", "suba'" or "musabba'", except in a line of al-Kumayt which gives "'ushar" for ten, praising a man whom people hoped for before he was ten years old; the editor traces it in the Majaz (1: 116), al-Aghani, Lisan, al-Mukhassas, al-Jawaliqi, al-Batalyawsi and al-Khizana (1: 82-83) from al-Kumayt's poem in praise of Aban ibn al-Walid ibn 'Abd al-Malik, says the printed edition has "the line of al-Kumayt" with a different word and the manuscript is right, takes al-Tabari's "he means ten, ten" to mean the many qualities in which the man surpassed other men, and explains the words for shedding the milk teeth, odd or even, and watching. The whole section, the note adds, is in al-Farra (1: 254-255) and Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz (1: 114-116).

**The accusative and nominative of "one", and the number four (pp. 546-547).** Al-Tabari says the accusative of "one" means: if you fear not to fulfil what justice obliges you towards more than one wife you hold by marriage, in what God made due to them from you, marry one of them; and had the reading come with the nominative it would be allowed, meaning "one suffices" or "one is enough", as in [[passage/quran-2-282|2:282]]. The notes: both copies read the relative phrase with a faulty form, which he mended, and refer to al-Farra (1: 255). He then puts an objector's question: you know that the lawful number of free women is four, so how is it said in forms which in number amount to nine? (the note refers to al-Nahhas's al-Nasikh wa-l-Mansukh, p. 92). He answers that the meaning is: marry whom you wish, two if you are secure from injustice, three if you do not fear it, or four if you are secure with them; what proves this is the condition that follows, since the sense is: if you fear with two, marry one; and if you fear also with one, what is under ownership. A second objection: God's command and prohibition are obligatory until a proof shows they are for training and guidance, and the verse is in the form of a command, so is there evidence that it is not for obligation? He answers yes, the condition that follows; so it is known that the command to marry is an indication of the prohibition of marrying a number of women one fears injustice with, and not a command to marry, the sense being: if you fear not being equitable over the orphans and felt scruple about them, feel the same scruple with women, and marry only those you are secure from wronging, from one to four. He has shown elsewhere (note: 2: 293-294) that the Arabs put speech in the form of a command with the meaning of prohibition, threat or warning, as in [[passage/quran-18-29|18:29]] and in [[passage/quran-16-55|16:55]] and [[passage/quran-30-34|30:34]]; so the command to marry here has the sense of prohibition: do not marry except whom is right for you. And in the same sense on the phrase about what is under ownership the interpreters spoke.

**The reports on ownership (p. 548, reports 8482-8485).** Qatada (8482), through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid from Sa'id: if you fear not to be fair in one, what your hand owns. Al-Suddi (8483), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: concubines. Al-Rabi' (8484), in what was told from 'Ammar from Ibn Abi Ja'far from his father: like Qatada. Al-Dahhak (8485), through Yahya ibn Abi Talib from Yazid from Juwaybir: the fairness meant is in intercourse and in affection.

**The sense of "ta'ulu" (pp. 548-549).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: if you fear not to be fair among two, three or four and so marry one, or fear not to be fair with one and so take a concubine, that is nearer to your not wronging or inclining. One says "the man 'ala, ya'ulu, 'awlan and 'iyalatan" when he inclines and wrongs; and from it comes the "'awl" of the inheritance shares, because when the portions add up to more, a shortfall enters them (a sense given in the language; the museum gives no verdict on inheritance questions). From need, one says "'ala the man 'aylatan" when he needs; he cites a line of Uhayha ibn al-Julah on the poor not knowing when he will be rich and the rich not knowing when he will be poor, described, not reproduced. The editor's notes: both copies read "he means by the saying of the Exalted" at the start of the sentence and the sense requires what he set; he refers to the explanation of "nearer" (6: 78); the line is in Jamharat Ash'ar al-'Arab, al-Farra (1: 255), Ibn Duraid's Jamhara (2: 193), Ibn al-Athir's history (1: 278) and Lisan, recurs in the commentary (30: 149 and 10: 75, Bulaq), and is from a poem composed in a war between his people of the Aws and the Banu al-Najjar of the Khazraj in which his brother was killed; his wife Salma bint 'Amr ibn Zayd al-Najjariyya warned her people of Uhayha's and his people's coming, and he struck her until he broke her hand and divorced her; the line after it, its companion, says that a man who has settled on a matter does not know in which land his noon rest will overtake him; and the manuscript has a wrong letter by the copyist, which he mended.

**The reports on the sense of "'awl" (pp. 549-551, reports 8486-8501).** Al-Hasan (8486), through Humayd ibn Mas'ada from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Yunus: "'awl" is inclining among women. Mujahid (8487), through Ibn Humayd from Hukkam from 'Anbasa from Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Rahman from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza: do not incline; Mujahid (8488), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih; and Mujahid (8489), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl. 'Ikrima (8490), through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna from Abu al-Nu'man Muhammad ibn al-Fadl from Hushaym from Dawud ibn Abi Hind: not to incline, and he added: have you not heard Abu Talib's saying, in a line describing a scale of justice whose weight does not wrong? 'Ikrima (8491), through al-Muthanna from Hajjaj from Hammad ibn Zayd from al-Zubayr from Hurayth: not to incline, and he recited a line that he claimed Abu Talib said, of a scale of justice that does not shortchange a barley grain and a true weigher whose weight is not unjust; al-Tabari says this line is also transmitted in another form, of a true scale that does not steal a grain, with a witness from itself that is not unjust. The lines are described, not reproduced. Ibrahim (8492), through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim from Hushaym from Mughira: not to incline; Ibrahim (8493), through al-Muthanna from 'Amr ibn 'Awn from Hushaym from Mughira. In the editor's note on p. 550: the line is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (1: 296) and many other works, from the poem that is said to be Abu Talib's, with which he confronted Quraysh about the Messenger of God, saying that he would not give him up or leave him for anything until he perished in his defence; before the line is a line calling for swift punishment on 'Abd Shams and Nawfal; another transmission has a different wording; he explains the barley grain as the smallest unit of gold and silver weights, a medium unpeeled grain with its extending ends cut, referring to the explanation of the "habba" (4: 582, note 2), where he recorded a sense the dictionaries do not, and explains the verbs for reducing and for stealing or betraying. Abu Ishaq the Kufan (8494), through al-Muthanna from 'Amr ibn 'Awn from Hushaym: 'Uthman ibn 'Affan wrote to the people of Kufa about something they had reproached him for: I am not a scale that does not incline. Abu Malik (8495), through Abu Kurayb from 'Ittham ibn 'Ali from Isma'il ibn Abi Khalid: do not incline; in the note the printed edition reads "'Abbad ibn 'Ali", the copyist of the manuscript having written "'Abbad" and then turned the last letter into a different one without dotting the word, which confused the publisher; the right reading is "'Ittham ibn 'Ali al-'Amiri", Abu Kurayb's teacher, who has passed hundreds of times, his biography at 337. Qatada (8496), through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid from Sa'id: nearer to not inclining; Qatada (8497), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: incline; al-Rabi' (8498), in what was told from 'Ammar from Ibn Abi Ja'far from his father: not to incline; al-Suddi (8499), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: incline; Ibn 'Abbas (8500), through al-Muthanna from 'Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: not to incline; and Ibn 'Abbas (8501), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father from his uncle from his father from his father: nearer to not inclining. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
