## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 3 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of the commentary on verse 3: the first view (pp. 531-533, reports 8456-8461).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said the sense is: if you fear, guardians of orphans, that you will not be fair over their dowry and bring it to the dowries of women like them, then do not marry them, and marry others, the women outside the family whom God made lawful to you, from one to four; and if you fear injustice when you marry more than one, keep to one or to the other category the verse names, women under ownership. 'A'isha (8456), through Ibn Humayd from Ibn al-Mubarak from Ma'mar from al-Zuhri from 'Urwa: nephew, she is the orphan girl in her guardian's care whose property and beauty attract him and who wants to marry her for less than the customary dowry; they were forbidden to marry them unless they dealt fairly with them in completing the dowry, and were ordered to marry other women. The editor's note says al-Tabari narrates the report, at length and in brief, with seven chains (8456-8461), that it is firmly established in the two Sahihs and elsewhere, that this chain is Ibn al-Mubarak from Ma'mar from al-Zuhri and 8460 will come through 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar referring back to this wording, that al-Bukhari narrated it in his Sahih twelve times, which he will point out, and that al-Bayhaqi narrated it in al-Sunan al-Kubra (7: 141-142) with several chains. 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr (8457), through Yunus ibn 'Abd al-A'la from Ibn Wahb from Yunus ibn Yazid from Ibn Shihab, asked 'A'isha, wife of the Prophet, about the verse, and she said much the same, including that they were forbidden to marry them unless they dealt fairly and brought them to the highest of their customary dowry; then Yunus ibn Yazid said Rabi'a said of it: leave them, for I have made four lawful to you. The note says al-Layth's narration from Yunus comes at 8459; that al-Bukhari (5: 94-95, Fath) and Muslim (2: 398-399) narrate it, Muslim's longer than here but without Rabi'a's closing words, which are not part of the report proper; that al-Bukhari also narrates it (9: 91, Fath) through Hassan ibn Ibrahim from Yunus, abridged and without Rabi'a's word; that the phrase "the highest of their custom in the dowry" is as established in Sahih Muslim and the manuscript has another word, apparently a copyist's slip. 'Urwa (8458), through al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd and Sa'id ibn Maslama from Isma'il ibn Umayya from Ibn Shihab: I asked 'A'isha, Mother of the Believers, and she said much the same, that they were forbidden to marry them unless they completed the dowry and then ordered to marry others. On this chain the editor notes: al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd ibn Abi Ja'far al-Bazzar al-Baghdadi is reliable, Ibn Khuzayma took from him in his Sahih, and Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Khatib mention him without impugning him; al-Mizzi names him "al-Husayn", and Ibn Hajar followed but stated that the vowels are the ones for al-Hasan, which is right; Sa'id ibn Maslama ibn Hisham ibn 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan is weak, al-Bukhari saying "there is something in him" and that he has objectionable reports, Ibn Ma'in "nothing" and Abu Hatim "weak and objectionable in hadith"; the printed edition reads "and Abu Sa'id", an error from writing a short form for "he told us"; Isma'il ibn Umayya al-Umawi's biography passed at 2615; and the weakness of this chain because of Sa'id does not prevent the report being sound in itself through other routes. 'Urwa (8459), through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih from al-Layth from Yunus from Ibn Shihab, like Yunus's report from Ibn Wahb; 'A'isha (8460), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar, like Ibn Humayd's from Ibn al-Mubarak, the note saying these two repeat 8456 and 8457. 'A'isha (8461), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj from Hisham from his father: it came down about the orphan girl with a man, she having property; perhaps he marries her for her property while she does not please him, then beats her and treats her badly; so he was admonished. The note says al-Qasim is ibn al-Hasan, al-Husayn is ibn Dawud with a nickname the scan renders unclearly (he refers to 8398), and Hajjaj is ibn Muhammad al-Misisi al-A'war, whose biography passed at 1691 and whom his brother Mahmud also treated in volume 6, p. 548, note 3. These statements, including their rulings on marriage and dowry, are given as transmitted with their chains and the museum gives no verdict. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
