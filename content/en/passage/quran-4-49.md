## Summary
Verse 49 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 49 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Those who declare themselves pure and the dispute about how (pp. 452-453, reports 9733-9737, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the Prophet is told to look with his heart at the Jews who clear themselves of sins and purify themselves, and that the interpreters differed about the manner of this self-purification. Some said it was their claim to be sons of God and His beloved. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9733, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that they cleared themselves by a matter they had not reached, saying that and that they had no sins; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9734, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from al-Hasan that they were Jews and Christians who said this and said only a Jew or Christian enters paradise (see [[passage/quran-2-111|2:111]]; for the first claim [[passage/quran-5-18|5:18]]); al-Qasim (9735, from al-Husayn, from Abu Tumayla, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) from al-Dahhak that the Jews said they had no sins beyond those of their children on the day they are born, and if the children had sins, they had the same, and that God then spoke of their forging lies against Him ([[passage/quran-4-50|4:50]]); Yunus (9736, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd that the People of the Book said these things and that they were on what God loves, and God denied them when they claimed they would enter paradise; and Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9737, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi that it came down about the Jews, who said they teach their children the Torah when small so the children have no sins, and their sins are like their children's, what they do by day being expiated for them by night. The editor's notes refer to the earlier discussion of seeing with the heart (p. 426) and of purification (p. 369). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 453}

**The view that they put children forward to lead prayer (pp. 453-454, reports 9738-9742, note 1).** Others said their self-purification was putting their children forward as prayer-leaders, claiming the children had no sins. Muhammad b. Amr (9738, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) and al-Muthanna (9739, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) report this from Mujahid, and al-Qasim (9740, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from al-A'raj) from Mujahid that they put boys ahead in supplication and prayer; Ibn Jurayj said they were Jews and Christians. Ibn Waki' (9741, from his father, from Sufyan, from Husayn) reports from Abu Malik that it came down about the Jews; Ibn Waki' (9742, from his father, from Abu Makin) from Ikrima that they put forward boys not yet of age to pray with them. The editor notes that Abu Makin is Nuh b. Rabi'a al-Ansari, their client, with a biography in al-Tahdhib. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 454}

**The view that their children would intercede (p. 454, report 9743).** Others said it was their claim that their children would intercede for them and purify them. Muhammad b. Sa'd (from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father) reports from Ibn Abbas that the Jews said their children had died, were a means of nearness to God for them and would intercede, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 454}

**The view that they purified one another (pp. 454-455, report 9744, notes 1-2).** Others said it was some of them purifying others. Yahya b. Ibrahim al-Mas'udi (from his father, from his grandfather, from al-A'mash, from Qays b. Muslim, from Tariq b. Shihab) reports from Abdullah b. Mas'ud that a man sets out in the morning with his religion and returns with nothing of it; he meets a man who holds no benefit or harm for him and says to him, "by God, you are such and such", and perhaps returns without achieving any of his need, having angered God; then Ibn Mas'ud recited the verse. The editor notes that the print has a wording he calls an error without doubt, the right one being in the manuscript; that the negated phrase means "did not obtain anything of what he sought" and is used only with negation; that the doubled word for "such and such" is one of the allusive expressions; that Yahya al-Mas'udi was treated earlier (5379); that Qays b. Muslim al-Jadali al-Adwani narrated from Tariq b. Shihab and al-A'mash, al-Thawri and others from him, Ahmad calling him trustworthy in hadith though a Murji'i, and quoting Sufyan on his reverence for God; and that Tariq b. Shihab al-Ahmasi was narrated from by the four compilers, saw the Prophet and narrated from him as a mursal, and narrated from the four caliphs, Bilal, Hudhayfa and a maternal uncle whose name is only partly legible in the scan. The note breaks off at the page end and appears to continue on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 455}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 455).** Abu Ja'far says the soundest view in his opinion is that of those who said their self-purification was describing themselves as without sins or errors and as God's sons and beloved, as God reported them saying, since that is the most evident sense and God reported that they purified only themselves; the view that it was putting their children forward for prayer is an interpretation whose correctness is not reached except by an authoritative report that yields knowledge. He then begins explaining the clause saying that God is the one who purifies, calling it His denial of those among the Jews and Christians who clear themselves of sins, and has God say to them that the matter is not as... and the page breaks off here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 455}

**Al-Tabari completes his explanation: God is the purifier (p. 456).** Al-Tabari finishes the sentence that broke off on the previous page: the matter is not as you claim, that you have no sins or errors and are innocent of what God dislikes; rather you are people of fabrication and lying about God. The purified one is not whoever purifies himself but whoever God purifies, and God purifies and clears of sins those on whom His will falls among His creatures by granting them success in avoiding His disobediences and in obeying Him. He gives as his reason what follows, the denunciation of their fabrication against God by their claim to be His sons and beloved and that He had cleansed them of sins ([[passage/quran-4-50|4:50]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 456}

**The denial of wronging, for the self-purifiers and others (p. 456).** Al-Tabari says God does not wrong those He has reported as purifying themselves, nor anyone else of His creation: He does not cut anything from their rights in not purifying one He did not purify or in purifying one He purified, and puts nothing in the wrong place. Rather He grants success to those on whom His will falls and abandons those He wills among the disobedient; all this is His and in His hand, and in all of it He wrongs no one, purified or not, by so much as a fatil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 456}

**The fatil: what comes out between the fingers (pp. 456-458, reports 9745-9751, notes 1-3 on p. 457).** The interpreters differed over the meaning of the fatil. Some said it is the dirt that comes out between the fingers or palms when one is rubbed against the other. Sulayman b. Abd al-Jabbar (9745, from Muhammad b. al-Salt, from Abu Kudayna, from Qabus, from his father) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is what comes out between your fingers; Ibn Humayd (9746, from Hukkam, from Anbasa, from Abu Ishaq al-Hamdani, from al-Taymi) that he asked Ibn Abbas and he said: what you twist between your fingers; Ibn Waki' (9747, from his father, from Yazid b. Dirham Abu al-Ala', from Abu al-Aliya) from Ibn Abbas that it is what comes out between a man's fingers; Muhammad b. Sa'd (9748, by his chain to Ibn Abbas) that it is what comes out when you rub your fingers; Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9749, from Hushaym, from Husayn) from Abu Malik that it is the dirt that comes out between the palms; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9750, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi that it is what you twist with your hands and dirt comes out; and Ibn Humayd (9751, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Mujahid) from Ibn Abbas that it is what we rub in our hands and it comes out between them. In the editor's note on 9745: Sulayman b. Abd al-Jabbar b. Zurayq the tailor was treated earlier (5994), as were Muhammad b. al-Salt and Abu Kudayna Yahya b. al-Muhallab; the chain was defective in the print, the mention of Muhammad b. al-Salt having dropped out (as in 7964 and 5994, and as the same chain recurs at 9799), since Sulayman did not meet Abu Kudayna; Qabus b. Abi Zabyan is weak and not used as proof, as Ibn Sa'd said, and Ibn Hibban said he had a poor memory and was alone in reporting from his father what has no basis; his father Husayn b. Jundab, Abu Zabyan, narrated from Umar, Ali, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Umar and other Companions and tabi'un and is trustworthy, with a biography in al-Tahdhib. In the note on 9747: Yazid b. Dirham Abu al-Ala' is the brother of Muhammad b. Dirham, narrated from Anas and al-Hasan, also from Abu al-Aliya though the biographers did not mention it, and Waki' and Abd al-Samad b. Abd al-Warith narrated from him; al-Fallas called him trustworthy and Ibn Ma'in said he was nothing, and Ibn Hibban listed him among the trustworthy and said he errs much; the editor refers to Ibn Abi Hatim, Lisan al-Mizan and report 9811, and says the print has "Zayd b. Dirham" while the manuscript has the right name. In the note on 9748 the editor says the print added a word that is not in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 458}

**The view that it is what is inside the date-stone (pp. 458-459, reports 9752-9762, note 1 on p. 459).** Others said it is what lies in the belly of the date-stone. Al-Muthanna (9752, from Abdullah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha) reports this from Ibn Abbas; Ibn Waki' (9753, from his father, from Talha b. Amr) and Yunus (9754, from Ibn Wahb, from Talha) from Ata b. Abi Rabah the same; al-Qasim (9755, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abdullah b. Kathir) and Ibn Bashshar (9756, from Muhammad b. Sa'id, from Sufyan b. Sa'id, from Mansur) from Mujahid; al-Hasan b. Yahya (9757, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from Qatada; a report given in the passive "I was told" form (9758, from al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) from al-Dahhak; Yunus (9759, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9760, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) from al-Dahhak; al-Muthanna (9761, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid; and Ibn Bashshar (9762, from Abu Amir, from Qurra) from Atiyya. The editor notes that Abu Amir is Abd al-Malik b. Amr (treated at 4143; the scan reads his epithet unclearly), that Qurra b. Khalid narrated from Abu Raja', Ibn Sirin and al-Hasan and that Shu'ba, Yahya b. Sa'id al-Qattan, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi and others narrated from him, with a biography in al-Tahdhib, and that Atiyya is Ibn Sa'd b. Junada, treated at 305. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 459}

**Al-Tabari's choice and argument (pp. 459-460).** Abu Ja'far says the word fatil is originally "that which is twisted", turned from the pattern for the passive participle to the pattern fa'il, as sari' and dahin are said for masru' and madhun. Since God's purpose was to report that He does not wrong His servants in the smallest things of no weight, how much less in things of weight, and since the dirt between the fingers or palms on twisting is like what lies in the cleft and belly of the date-stone and other twisted things of no worth, he holds that all of it falls within the meaning, except what is excluded by something that must be conceded from the apparent sense of the revelation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 460}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 460}
