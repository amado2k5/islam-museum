## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "Safa and Marwa are among the signs (sha'a'ir) of God" al-Tabari says "al-Safa" is the plural of "safat", the smooth rock, citing a line of al-Tirimmah; some said "safa" is singular, its dual "safwan" and plural "asfa'" and "sufiy", citing a line of a rajaz poet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 224} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 225} "Al-Marwa" is the small pebble, its few being "marawat" and its many "al-marw", and he cites a line of al-A'sha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 225}

Al-Tabari cites a line of Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali for "marw" as the small rocks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 226} He says God meant by "Safa and Marwa" the two mountains of those names in His sanctuary, not any smooth rocks or pebbles, which is why the definite article was added. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 226} On "among the signs of God" he says these are God's landmarks that He made for His servants as places to worship Him, by supplication, remembrance or performing the duties laid on them, citing al-Kumayt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 226} He reports from Mujahid that the "sha'a'ir" are "from the report He tells you of", which al-Tabari finds far-fetched; the sense for him is God informing the believers that the running between them is among the rites of the pilgrimage that He prescribed and commanded His friend Abraham when he asked to be shown the rites, its form being a report though a command is meant, since God commanded His Prophet to follow the creed of Abraham and made Abraham a leader for those after him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 227} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 228}

On "whoever performs hajj to the House or 'umra" he says "hajj to the House" means to come to it, returning to it after beginning, and anyone who often goes to a thing "performs hajj" to it, citing a line of al-Mukhabbal al-Sa'di; the pilgrim is called "hajj" because he comes to the House before the standing, returns for the circumambulation on the day of sacrifice, then for the farewell circumambulation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 228} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 229} The one performing "'umra" is so called because after circumambulating he leaves after visiting it; "i'timar" is visiting, and anyone who aims for something is a "mu'tamir" of it, citing al-'Ajjaj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 229}

On "there is no blame on him in going round them" he says there is no harm or sin in going round them. He puts the question of how "among the signs of God" can be a command to go round while "no blame" is said, and answers that for some the meaning is that when the Prophet made the 'umra of the Qada' people were wary because in the Jahiliyya they used to go round them for two idols on them, and said how can we go round them when we know honouring idols is polytheism; and the verse was revealed about that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 230}

Al-Tabari completes his answer: God said "Safa and Marwa are among the signs of God", meaning going round them is among them, with the mention of going round left out since they themselves are named; so whoever performs hajj to the House or 'umra should not be wary of going round them because of what the people of the Jahiliyya did for the two idols, for the polytheists went round them in unbelief while you go round them in faith, affirming My messenger and obeying My command. "Junah" is sin; from al-Suddi, no sin on him, but he has a reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 230} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 231}

He gives the reports of the early generations: from al-Sha'bi, in the Jahiliyya there was an idol on Safa called Isaf and one on Marwa called Na'ila, and when they circled the House they touched the two idols; when Islam came and the idols were broken the Muslims said the running was only for the sake of the two idols and is not among the signs, and the verse was revealed; in another version God made it a voluntary good deed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 231} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 232} From Anas ibn Malik, they disliked going round between them because they were among the rites of the Jahiliyya, until the verse was revealed, and when Islam came they stopped them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 232} From Ibn 'Abbas, by a chain the editor calls weak, there were idols by them and when these were forbidden they refrained until the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 233} From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, people were reluctant to go round between them, so God told them they are among His signs and going round between them is dearer to Him, and the sunna passed on going round them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 234} From al-Suddi on Ibn 'Abbas, in the Jahiliyya demons played music all night between Safa and Marwa and there were gods between them, so when Islam came the Muslims said we will not go round them, it is polytheism we did in the Jahiliyya, and the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 234}

From Mujahid, the Ansar said running between these two stones belongs to the Jahiliyya, and the verse was revealed; from Ibn Zayd, the people of the Jahiliyya had put an idol on each and the Muslims disliked going round them because of the two idols, so God revealed the verse and the Prophet made going round them a sunna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 235} From Anas, asked whether they disliked going round them with the idols, he said yes until the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 235}

He reports from Anas that Safa and Marwa were among the rites of Quraysh in the Jahiliyya, and when Islam came they left them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 235} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 236} Others said the verse was revealed about people who in the Jahiliyya did not run between them and who, when Islam came, feared running between them as they had feared it before; from Qatada, a tribe of Tihama did not run between them in the Jahiliyya, so God told them they are among His signs and that going round between them was a sunna of Abraham and Ishmael. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 236}

From 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, he asked 'A'isha about the verse and said there is no blame on anyone who does not go round them; she said, "what an evil thing you said, my nephew: if it were as you interpreted it would say there is no blame on him not to go round them. It was revealed about the Ansar, who before they became Muslims used to cry the talbiya for Manat, the idol they worshipped at al-Mushallal, and whoever did so felt reluctant to go round between Safa and Marwa; when they asked the Prophet, God revealed the verse." 'A'isha added that the Prophet had made going round them a sunna, so no one may leave it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 236} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 237} In another report, men of the Ansar said we did not go round them out of honour to Manat; al-Zuhri says he mentioned this to Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith ibn Hisham, who said this is knowledge, and that he had heard men of learning say that when going round the House was revealed and going round Safa and Marwa was not, people told the Prophet we used to go round them in the Jahiliyya, is there any blame in our not doing so, and the verse was revealed, and that he understood it to have been revealed about both groups. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 238}

Al-Tabari says the correct view for him is that God made going round between Safa and Marwa one of His signs just as He did circumambulating the House. As for "there is no blame on him in going round them", it may have been said to both groups, those wary because of the two idols named by al-Sha'bi and those wary from disliking it in the Jahiliyya as reported from 'A'isha. Whichever it was, the verse is no indication that blame was lifted from those who went round because it had been forbidden and then allowed, as all agree God never forbade it at any time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 239} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 240}

He then notes that the scholars differ in several ways on the ruling for one who leaves it: some held that whoever leaves it has left a rite of his pilgrimage which only making it up itself covers, saying these are two circuits, God commanding one around the House and the other between Safa and Marwa; some held that a compensatory sacrifice covers it, like leaving some of the stone-throwing, the standing at the sacred monument or the farewell circuit; and others held that going round them is voluntary, good if done and with nothing required if left. He begins with those who say the running is obligatory and no compensation covers it, including 'A'isha, who said "by my life, whoever does not run between Safa and Marwa has not performed the pilgrimage, because God said they are among His signs". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 240}
