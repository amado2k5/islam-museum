## Summary
Verse 128 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 128 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 194).** Abu Ja'far says the sense is: God helps you so that He may destroy a party of the unbelievers, or shame them, or accept their repentance, or punish them, for they are wrongdoers, and the Prophet has no say in their affair. The third verb is joined to what precedes it, though it could also mean "you have no say until He accepts their repentance", the verb then taking the subjunctive in the sense of "until"; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 234). Abu Ja'far prefers the first view because nothing of the creatures' affair belongs to anyone but their Creator, before the unbelievers' repentance and punishment and after. The denial means, for him, that the Prophet has nothing of the matter of God's creatures except to carry out his Lord's command among them and end in obedience to Him; their affair is God's and the judgment on them is in His hand, He judging as He wills, whether acceptance of repentance from those who disbelieved and disobeyed, or punishment in this world by killing and ruinous afflictions or in the next by what He prepared for the unbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 194}

**Ibn Ishaq's report (p. 195).** From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7804): then He said to Muhammad: you have no judgment over My servants beyond what I commanded you about them; either I accept their repentance by My mercy, and if I wish I do it, or I punish them for their sins, for they deserve that by their disobedience to Me. The editor notes slight differences in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 115) and that the report follows those ending with 7801. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 195}

**Occasion: what befell the Prophet at Uhud (pp. 195-200).** It is said that God sent the verse down on His Prophet because, when the polytheists did to him at Uhud what they did, he said, as though despairing of their guidance or return to the truth: how can a people prosper who did this to their prophet? In many reports in nearly these words:
- From Humayd ibn Mas'ada, Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, Humayd al-Tawil, Anas (7805): the Prophet said at Uhud, his incisor broken and his head cut, wiping the blood from his face: how can a people prosper who stained their prophet with blood while he called them to their Lord? Then the verse was sent down. In his note the editor says al-Tabari gives it with five chains (7805-7808 and 7810) from Humayd al-Tawil from Anas through Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, Ibn Abi Adi, Hushaym, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash and Ibn Ulayya, and 7809 as al-Hasan's mursal; that Ahmad has it in the Musnad via Hushaym, Sahl ibn Yusuf, Yazid ibn Harun and Ibn Abi Adi; that al-Tirmidhi (4: 83) has it from Ahmad ibn Mani' and Abd ibn Humayd from Yazid ibn Harun and calls it good and sound; that al-Nahhas has it in al-Nasikh wa-l-mansukh (p. 90); that Ahmad also has a similar one through Thabit from Anas, and Muslim through Hammad ibn Salama; that al-Bukhari mentions it suspended from both routes; that al-Hafiz in al-Fath explains Humayd's route was joined by Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Ishaq in the Maghazi and Thabit's by Muslim; that Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti mention it, the latter crediting further compilers; and he refers to reports 7818-7821. He adds that the word for the broken tooth means the four teeth next to the incisors, between the incisor and the canine. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 195}
- From Ibn Bashshar, Ibn Abi Adi, Humayd, Anas, from the Prophet, similarly (7806). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 196}
- From Ya'qub, Hushaym, Humayd al-Tawil, Anas, from the Prophet, similarly (7807). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 196}
- From Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbu'i, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, Humayd al-Tawil, Anas (7808): when he was cut on the forehead and his incisor broken the Prophet said: a people who did this to their prophet will not prosper; then God revealed the verse to him. The editor notes that al-Nasa'i weakened Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbu'i (earlier, no. 421) and that he leans to his being trustworthy since Ibn Abi Hatim gave his biography without any criticism. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 196}
- From Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Ibn Awn, al-Hasan (7809): the Prophet said at Uhud: how can a people prosper who made his prophet's face bleed while he called them to God? Then the verse came. For the editor this is al-Hasan's mursal version; he mentions another flawed mursal report from al-Hasan in al-Suyuti, credited to Abd ibn Humayd alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 197}
- From Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Humayd, Anas, from the Prophet, to that effect (7810). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 197}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7811): we were told that the verse came down at Uhud when the Prophet's face was wounded and some of his incisor broken, and, while Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, washed the blood from his face, he said: how can a people prosper who stained their prophet's face with blood while he called them to their Lord? Then God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 197}
- From Ibn Humayd, Yahya ibn Wadih, al-Husayn ibn Waqid, Matar, Qatada (7812): the Prophet was hurt at Uhud, his incisor broken and his brow split; he fell wearing two coats of mail with blood flowing; Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, passed, sat him up and wiped his face, and he came to saying: what of a people who did this to their prophet while he calls them to God? Then God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 197}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' ibn Anas (7813): the verse came down at Uhud when the Prophet's face was cut and his incisor broken; he meant to pray against them and said: how can a people prosper who made their prophet's face bleed while he calls them to God and they call him to Satan, he calls them to guidance and they call him to error, he calls them to Paradise and they call him to the Fire? He meant to pray against them, then the verse came down and he held back from praying against them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 198}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7814): Abu Sufyan came from the Hawl angry at what was done to his men at Badr and fought the Prophet's Companions fiercely at Uhud until as many of them were killed as there were prisoners at Badr; then the Prophet said a word that God knew was mixed with anger: how can a people prosper who stained their prophet's face with blood while he calls them to Islam? Then the verse came. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 198}
- From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, Qatada (7815): the Prophet's incisor was broken at Uhud by Utba ibn Abi Waqqas, who also cut his face, while Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, washed the blood from him and the Prophet said: how can a people prosper who did this to their prophet? Then God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 198}
- From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, al-Zuhri and Uthman al-Jazari, Miqsam (7816): the Prophet prayed against Utba ibn Abi Waqqas at Uhud when he broke his incisor and bruised his face, saying: O God, let the year not turn on him before he dies an unbeliever; and the year did not turn before he died an unbeliever. The editor explains the word for bruising as a blow that bruises skin and flesh without breaking the bone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 198}
- From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Abbas (7817): the Prophet was cut at the parting of his brow and his incisor was broken; Ibn Jurayj said we were told that when he was wounded Salim, freedman of Abu Hudhayfa, washed the blood from his face while the Prophet said: how can a people prosper who stained their prophet's face with blood while he called them to God? Then God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 199}

**Another occasion: his prayer against certain men (pp. 199-200).** Others said the verse came down because he had prayed against some people, and God sent down that the matter was not for him concerning them.
- From Yahya ibn Habib ibn Arabi, Khalid ibn al-Harith, Ibn Ajlan, Nafi', Ibn Umar (7818): the Prophet used to pray against four men, then the verse came down; he said: and God guided them to Islam. In his note the editor says Khalid ibn al-Harith al-Hujaymi is trustworthy, firm and an imam; that Ahmad has it from Yahya ibn Habib, al-Tabari's teacher here, and al-Tirmidhi too, who calls it good, strange and sound, strange by this route from Nafi' from Ibn Umar; that Ahmad has it by other routes including Harun ibn Ma'ruf from Ibn Wahb from Usama ibn Zayd from Nafi' from Ibn Umar, which is a sound corroboration of Ibn Ajlan's report and makes it no longer strange; and that Ibn Kathir, al-Hafiz in al-Fath and al-Suyuti (crediting al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Jarir and Ibn Abi Hatim) mention it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 199}
- From Abu al-Sa'ib Salm ibn Junada, Ahmad ibn Bashir, Umar ibn Hamza, Salim, Ibn Umar (7819): the Prophet said: O God, curse Abu Sufyan; O God, curse al-Harith ibn Hisham; O God, curse Safwan ibn Umayya; then the verse came down. The editor's note says Ahmad ibn Bashir Abu Bakr al-Kufi is trustworthy and was used by al-Bukhari, denies that the criticism attributed to Ibn Ma'in concerns him, and says his father's name in the manuscript and printed text is an error he corrected from al-Tirmidhi's version; that Umar ibn Hamza ibn Abd Allah ibn Umar is held trustworthy since Muslim used him and al-Hakim said his reports are all upright; that Ahmad has it with a closing addition that God accepted their repentance and al-Tirmidhi with the addition that they became Muslim and made good their Islam, al-Tirmidhi calling it good and strange; and that al-Zuhri's route from Salim from his father is in Ahmad and in al-Nahhas and Ibn al-Mubarak's from Ma'mar. The note is cut at the foot of p. 200 and continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 200}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 200 in the commentary on verse 128 and continue from p. 201. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 200}
