**Commentary on verse 83 (pp. 213-214)**

On "he said, rather your souls have beguiled you into something; so patience is fitting; perhaps God will bring them all to me; He is the Knowing, the Wise", al-Tabari says: something is left out of the speech, namely: Benjamin's brothers returned to their father and Rubil stayed behind, and they told him his news; when they told him that he had stolen, "he said, rather your souls have beguiled you into something", that is, your souls made attractive to you something you had set your mind on and wanted; "so patience is fitting" means my patience over what has befallen me of the loss of my child is a fine patience with no lament in it. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "taswil". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 213}

On "perhaps God will bring them all to me", he means all my children, returning them to me; "He is the Knowing" of my loneliness, of their loss and my grief over them, and of whether what they say is true or false, "the Wise" in managing His creation. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "patience is fitting", "al-alim" and "al-hakim". The interpreters said the same: report 19644, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "your souls have beguiled you into something" means made attractive; and "perhaps God will bring them all to me" means Joseph, his brother and Rubil. Report 19645, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when they brought this to Ya'qub, meaning Rubil's words to them, he suspected them and thought it like what they did to Joseph, then said "your souls have beguiled you into something; so patience is fitting; perhaps God will bring them all to me", that is, Joseph, his brother and Rubil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 214}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
