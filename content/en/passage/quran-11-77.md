**Commentary on verse 77 (pp. 407-411)**

On [[passage/quran-11-77|11:77]] al-Tabari says: when Our angels came to Lot he was distressed by their coming, the verb being from 'su'', evil, 'and felt constrained for them' by their coming, 'in his arm's reach', that is, his soul was straitened with grief at their coming, because he did not know in the state in which their coming distressed him that they were God's messengers, and knew what his people did of shameless acts and feared for them, so he was constrained by their coming, knowing he would need to defend his guests, and therefore said 'this is a hard day'. Report 18350, from Ibn Abbas: he thought ill of his people and was straitened by his guests. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 408}

Report 18351, from Hudhayfa: when the messengers came to Lot they came while he was on a piece of his land working it, and they had been told, and God knows best, do not destroy them until Lot bears witness; they came and said, we shall be your guests tonight; he went with them and when he had walked a while he turned and said, do you not know what the people of this town do? By God I know no more evil people on the face of the earth; he went on with them, then said it a second time; and when his wicked wife saw them she went and warned them; report 18352, likewise. Report 18353, from Qatada: the angels came to Lot while he was in a field of his, and God had said to the angels, if Lot bears four witnessings against them I have permitted you their destruction; they said, Lot, we wish to be your guests tonight; he said, has their matter reached you? They said, what is their matter? He said, I testify by God that it is the worst town on earth in deeds, saying it four times, so Lot bore four witnessings against them, and they entered his house with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 408}

Report 18354, from al-Suddi: the angels left Abraham for the town of Lot and arrived at midday; when they reached the river of Sodom they met a daughter of Lot drawing water for her family, he having two daughters, the elder named Ri'ya and the younger Zaghra; they said, girl, is there a lodging? She said yes, stay where you are, do not enter until I come to you, for she feared for them from her people; she came to her father and said, father, two youths are at the town gate, I have not seen better faces, let your people not seize them and shame them; his people had forbidden him to host a man and said, leave us to host the men; he brought them and none knew but the household of Lot, but his wife went out and told her people that there were men in Lot's house whose faces she had never seen the like of, and his people came to him rushing. Report 18355, from Ibn Ishaq: as the people of the Torah claim, the messengers left Abraham for Lot at al-Mu'tafika, and when they came Lot was distressed and straitened for them out of fear that his people would shame him before his guests, and said 'this is a hard day'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 409}

As for 'and he said, this is a hard day', Lot is saying that this day is a day of severe evil and great trial. One says 'our day was hard' of 'asaba, yas'ibu, 'asban', and of this is a line of Adi b. Zayd in a poem he made in the prison of al-Nu'man b. al-Mundhir about a hard day, a rajaz line of a day that binds the heroes, another line of a hard day in Iraq, and a line of Ka'b b. Ju'ayl about groups familiar with a hard day; the editor explains the lines and their rare words. Al-Tabari then says that the interpreters said as he did, and the reports follow on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 410}

Reports 18356 from Mujahid, 18357 and 18359 from Qatada, and 18360 from Ibn Abbas give 'hard' as 'severe'; report 18358, from Ibn Ishaq, a day of trial and hardship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 411}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
