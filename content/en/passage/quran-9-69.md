**Commentary on verse 69 (pp. 340-344)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet: say, Muhammad, to these hypocrites who said "we were only chatting and playing": was it at God, the verses of His book and His Messenger that you mocked? You are like those before you among the nations who did as you do, so God destroyed them and hastened their disgrace in this world along with the punishment and exemplary penalty prepared for them in the hereafter; beware that there befall you of God's punishment the like of what befell them, for they were stronger than you in might and force and richer in wealth and children; "they enjoyed their portion", meaning they took their share and lot of their world and their religion, and were content with that portion in this world in place of their portion in the hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 340}

You, hypocrites, have followed their way in enjoying your portion, meaning you did with your religion and your world as the nations before you did, whom I destroyed for opposing My command; "and you plunged" into lying and falsehood about God "as they plunged", as those nations did before you. The editor refers to earlier explanations of enjoyment, portion and plunging. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 341}

Report 16930, from Abu Hurayra, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Abu Ma'shar and Sa'id al-Maqburi: the Prophet said "you will take as the nations before you took, cubit by cubit, span by span and arm's length by arm's length, until if one of them entered a lizard's hole you would enter it"; Abu Hurayra said, recite if you wish the verse "like those before you, they were stronger than you in might and richer in wealth and children"; they said "Messenger of God, as the Persians and Romans did?" and he said "and who are the people if not they?". The editor says its chain is weak because Abu Ma'shar Najih ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Sindi is rejected in hadith, but that the report has a root in al-Bukhari's Sahih through Sa'id al-Maqburi from Abu Hurayra in other wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 341}

Report 16931, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Umar ibn Ata and Ikrima: how like tonight is to last night; "like those before you" are these, Banu Israel, to whom we are likened; by Him in whose hand is my soul you will follow them until if one of them entered a lizard's hole you would enter it. The editor notes that Umar ibn Ata is Ibn Waraz, who is weak, so the chain is weak, but the report has a root in the Sahih. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 342}

Report 16932, from Abu Hurayra, through Ibn Jurayj, Ziyad ibn Sa'd, Muhammad ibn Zayd ibn Muhajir and Sa'id al-Maqburi: the Messenger of God said "by Him in whose hand is my soul, you will follow the ways of those before you, span by span, cubit by cubit and arm's length by arm's length, until if they entered a lizard's hole you would enter it"; they said "who are they, Messenger of God, the People of the Book?", and he said "what else?". The editor notes that Ziyad ibn Sa'd and Muhammad ibn Zayd are reliable and the chain sound, and that the last word is in the manuscript an interrogative meaning "who else", for which he cites the Lisan. Report 16933, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "who else"; the editor traces Abu Sa'id's report to al-Bukhari and Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 343}

Report 16934, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "they enjoyed their portion", he said, their religion. Report 16935, from al-Rabi', through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Ja'far and his father: the Messenger of God said "I warn you against introducing a novelty into Islam", knowing that some people of this community would do it, and God said about it "they enjoyed their portion"; they supposed the trial that befell Banu Israel before them would not befall them, but the trial returns as it began. The editor says the manuscript's wording is unreadable and that the printed text seems taken from al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 343}

As for "those, their deeds have come to nothing", it means these who said "we were only chatting and playing" and did as the perished nations before them did; "their deeds have come to nothing" means their deeds went for naught, so they have no reward but the Fire, since they were in what angers and displeases God; "and those are the losers" means they are the ones cheated in their bargain, selling the bliss of the hereafter for their small, paltry portion of this world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 344}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
