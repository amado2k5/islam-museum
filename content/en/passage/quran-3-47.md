## Summary
Verse 47 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 47 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Mary's question and the answer.** Al-Tabari says Mary spoke when the angels told her what they told her: my Lord, from what direction will I have a child, from before a husband I marry, or will his creation begin in me without husband or male and without a human touching me? God answered her that He creates from her a child of hers in this way, without any human touching her, making him a sign and lesson for people; He creates what He wills and does what He wishes, giving a child to whom He wills with no male or from a male and withholding it from whom He wills among women even if she has a husband, because nothing He wishes to create is hard for Him: He commands what He wills and it comes into being as He wills, from what He wills and as He wills. The editor refers to the earlier volumes for the interrogative word, notes that the manuscript has one particle and the print another where he preferred the one he gave, and that the bracketed words are added from the context. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 421}

**The report of Ibn Ishaq.** Report 7079 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says God does what He wills and creates what He wills, human or not, and when He decrees a thing it is as He wishes and however He wishes. The editor says it is in the Sira of Ibn Hisham (2:230), among the remainder of the reports whose last was 7072, and that he removed a superfluous particle from the print and manuscript and dropped a repeated last verb, since it comes later, and that the Sira's wording 'as He wished' is also correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 421} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
