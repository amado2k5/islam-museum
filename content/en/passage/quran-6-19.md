## Summary
Verse 19 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 19 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: what thing is greatest in testimony? Say: God is witness between me and you" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these polytheists of your people who deny and reject your prophethood: what thing is greater and mightier in testimony? Then tell them the greatest of things in testimony is God, in whose testimony cannot occur what may occur in the testimony of any of his creatures, forgetfulness, error, mistake and lying; then say to them: he who is greatest in testimony is witness between me and you as to which of us is in the right and which in falsehood, and which of us is rightly guided in act and word and which foolish, and we are content with him as judge between us. Report 13116, from Mujahid: Muhammad was ordered to ask Quraysh and then to tell them: God is witness between me and you; 13117, from him, the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 289}

The editor says the bracketed addition is needed for the sense.

Then "this Qur'an was revealed to me that I may warn you by it and whoever it reaches": say to these polytheists who deny you, God is witness between me and you, and this Qur'an was revealed to me that I may warn you by it of his punishment and warn by it whoever else it reaches, of all other people, if they do not stop and act on what is in it, treating its lawful as lawful and its forbidden as forbidden and believing in all of it, of the coming down of God's vengeance. Report 13118, from Qatada: we are told that the Prophet used to say: people, convey, even one verse of God's book, for whoever receives one verse of God's book has received God's command, whether he takes it or leaves it. 13119, from him: convey from God; whoever receives a verse of God's book has received God's command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 290}

The editor explains "coming down" as accusative, object of "warn whoever it reaches", refers for "revelation" to p. 217, and notes that in the manuscript "taking it or leaving it" can be read in either form.

Report 13120, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: whoever the Qur'an reaches, it is as though he saw the Prophet; then he recited "and whoever it reaches, do you indeed bear witness". 13121, from Mujahid: wherever the Qur'an comes, it summons and warns. 13122 and 13123, from Mujahid: "whoever it reaches" are those who accept Islam among the non-Arabs and others. 13124, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b: whoever the Qur'an reaches, Muhammad has reached him. 13125, from Ibn Abbas: "that I may warn you by it" means the people of Mecca, "and whoever it reaches" whoever this Qur'an reaches, to him it is a warner. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 291}

Report 13126, from Mujahid: "that I may warn you" the Arabs, "and whoever it reaches" the non-Arabs. 13127, from al-Suddi: whoever the Qur'an reaches, it is a warner to him. 13128, from Ibn Zayd: whoever this Qur'an reaches, I am his warner; and he recited [[passage/quran-7-158|7:158]], "people, I am God's messenger to you all", so whoever the Qur'an reaches, God's messenger is his warner. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 292}

So the sense for al-Tabari is: that I may warn you with the Qur'an, polytheists, and warn all other people it reaches; "man" is accusative as the object of "I warn", with "reached" its relative clause, and the pronoun returning to "man" dropped, as the Arabs do in the relative clauses of "man", "ma" and "alladhi". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 292}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

Then "do you really testify that there are other gods with God? Say: I do not testify; say: he is but one god and I am free of what you associate": say to these polytheists who reject your prophethood and hold another lord equal to God: do you, polytheists, testify that there are other gods with God, that is, other objects of worship, idols and images? He said "ukhra", feminine, and not "ukhar", though "alihah" is a plural, because plurals take the feminine, as in [[passage/quran-20-51|20:51]], "what of the former generations" with the feminine, not "al-awwal" or "al-awwalin". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 292}

Then he said to his prophet: say, Muhammad, "I do not testify" to what you testify, that there are other gods with God, but I reject and deny it; "say: he is but one god", one object of worship with no partner in what he deserves of worship from his creatures, "and I am free of what you associate": say, I am free of every partner you call on beside God, add to his sharing and worship with him; I worship nothing but God and call on no other as god. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 293}

It has been said that this verse was revealed about particular Jews, by a route whose soundness is not established; report 13129, from Ibn Abbas: Nahham ibn Zayd, Qardam ibn Ka'b and Bahri ibn Amr came and said: Muhammad, do you know any god with God besides him? The Messenger of God said: there is no god but God, I was sent with that and call to it; so God revealed about them and their saying "say: what thing is greatest in testimony" to "they do not believe". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 293}

The editor refers for "ukhra" and "ukhar" to volumes 6 and 3, says report 13129 is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and follows report 12284, that this chain has occurred hundreds of times as Abu Jafar's chain to Ibn Ishaq and then to Ibn Abbas, and that this is the first time Abu Jafar says this chain is not established for him.

Verse 19 closes here at p. 293, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-20|6:20]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
