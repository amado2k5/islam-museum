**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 233-239)**

On [[passage/quran-11-5|11:5]] the readers differed on 'they fold their breasts': most readers of the cities read 'yathnuna' in the sense of 'they do', from 'thanaytu', with 'breasts' in the accusative. These then differed on its meaning. Some said it was the deed of certain hypocrites, who when they passed the Messenger of God covered their faces and bent their backs. Reports 17938, 17939 and 17940, from Abd Allah b. Shaddad: when one of them passed the Prophet he bent his chest, lowered his head and wrapped himself in his cloth so the Prophet would not see him. The editor explains the phrase about the cloth on the face as taking his cloth and trying to cover his face with it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 233}

Others said they did it out of ignorance of God, thinking what their breasts hid would be hidden from God if they did so. Reports 17941 to 17945, from Mujahid: they fold their breasts in doubt and misgiving about the truth, to hide from God if they could, their breasts tightened with doubt. Report 17946, from al-Hasan: out of their ignorance of Him; God said 'when they cover themselves with their garments', in the darkness of night inside their houses, He knows at that hour what they conceal and what they show; report 17947, from Abu Razin: one of them used to bend his back and wrap himself in his cloth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 235}

Others said they did it so as not to hear God's Book. Reports 17948 and 17949, from Qatada: they bowed their breasts so as not to hear God's Book, and that is the most hidden a son of Adam can be, when he bows his chest, wraps himself in his cloth and keeps his worry in his soul, yet God does not miss that. Others said this is God telling His prophet of the hypocrites who hid enmity and hatred toward him and showed him love and friendship, claiming to be with him and on his religion, that they fold their breasts over disbelief to hide from God, and then that their secrets and what they show are not hidden from Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 236}

Others said they did so when they whispered to one another; report 17950, from Ibn Zayd, says so. It is reported of Ibn Abbas that he read 'tathnawni sudurahum', on the pattern of 'tahlawli al-thamara'; report 17951, from Ibn Abi Mulayka: I heard Ibn Abbas read it so, and he said they would not go to women or to relieve themselves except covered with their clothes, disliking to expose their private parts to the sky; report 17952, from Muhammad b. Abbad b. Ja'far, to the same effect: some people were shy to relieve themselves and so be exposed to the sky. Another saying is reported from Ibn Abbas on its meaning, which follows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 236}

Report 17953, from Ikrima, that Ibn Abbas read 'tathnawni sudurahum' and said it means doubt about God and doing wrong; 'they cover themselves with their garments' means he waxes proud or hides from God, and God sees him and knows what they hide and what they show; report 17954 is to the same effect. Al-Tabari says the right reading is that of the readers of the cities, 'yathnuna sudurahum', in the pattern of 'they do', with 'breasts' in the accusative, meaning they bend their breasts and conceal them. Report 17955, from Ibn Abbas: they conceal; report 17956, from him: they hide what is in their hearts; 'when they cover themselves with their garments' He knows what they did by night and day; report 17957, from al-Dahhak: 'tathnawni sudurahum'. Al-Tabari says that al-Dahhak's interpretation follows Ibn Abbas's reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 238}

Al-Tabari says: since the reading we mentioned is the sounder of the two, through the agreement of the readers who are authority, the soundest interpretation is that of those who said they did this out of ignorance of God, thinking that what their souls hide, or what they whispered among themselves, would be hidden from Him. He says so because 'to hide from him' means to hide from God, the pronoun going back to God's name, and Muhammad has not been mentioned before it for it to refer to him, and it stands in the report about God; and so it is clear they only thought of hiding from God through their ignorance of Him. He told them that the secret of their affairs and their open acts are not hidden from Him in whatever state they were, wrapped in their clothes or out in the open. One says 'he covered himself with his cloth', as in [[passage/quran-71-7|71:7]], and he cites a line of al-Khansa mourning her brother Sakhr in which she says she watches the stars through the night and wraps herself in her worn clothes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 238}

His words 'He knows what they conceal' mean He knows what those ignorant of their Lord conceal, who think God misses what their breasts hide when they bend over what is in them, and what they whisper and keep secret among themselves; 'and what they show': the secrets of His servants and their open acts are alike to Him; 'He knows what is in the breasts' means He is aware of all that the breasts of His creatures hide, of belief and disbelief, truth and falsehood, good and evil, and what they will conceal that has not yet been concealed. Report 17958, from Ibn Abbas: they cover their heads. Al-Tabari says: take care lest your Lord look upon you while you hold in your breasts doubt of any of His oneness, command or prohibition, or of what He required you to believe and affirm, so that you perish through that belief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 239}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
