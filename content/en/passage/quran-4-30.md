## Summary
Verse 30 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 30 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The difference over what 'that' points to (p. 230, report 9167 and the views after it).** The people of interpretation differed over the act on which the threat rests. Some said the meaning is whoever kills himself, that is, kills his believing brother in aggression and wrong, whom God will bring into a fire. Al-Qasim (9167, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj) reports that he asked Ata whether it applies to all that went before or to the prohibition on killing selves, and Ata said it applies to the prohibition on killing selves. Others said the meaning is whoever does what God forbade from the start of this sura to this point: marrying those whose marriage He forbade, overstepping His limits, wrongfully eating orphans' property, and killing a forbidden soul without right. Others said it means whoever eats the property of his Muslim brother wrongfully without his goodwill and kills his believing brother wrongfully, whom God will bring into a fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 230}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 230-231).** Abu Jafar says the sound view in his opinion is that the meaning is whoever does what God forbade from the verse that forbids inheriting women against their will ([[passage/quran-4-19|4:19]], identified by the editor from its being named in al-Tabari's sentence) to this clause: marrying forbidden women, keeping women from their right to marry, eating property wrongfully, and killing believers whose killing is forbidden, because God promised punishment on every one of these. If someone asks what prevented him from taking the pointer to cover everything threatened from the start of the sura, he answers that every section up to the end of the eighteenth verse ([[passage/quran-4-18|4:18]]) was joined with a threat, in the painful punishment, and that no punishment is mentioned after that for what is forbidden in the verses that follow until this one; so the pointer is better taken as covering what was not joined with a threat, though all agree that God threatened over all of it, than as covering what had already been joined with the threat in its prohibition. The editor says in a note that this is a clear argument and a proof of Abu Jafar's fine understanding of the Qur'an's meanings and purposes and a sound method of linking the verses, seldom matched outside this commentary; he also kept the manuscript's wording where the printed text differs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 231}

**The meanings of the words, and the absence of difficulty for God (pp. 231-232).** Abu Jafar says the first word means going beyond what God permitted him into what He forbade him, the second means doing it without God's permission and committing what He forbade; that bringing him into a fire means making him enter it and burn in it; and that burning him in it presents no difficulty to God, because the man cannot refuse his Lord what He wants to do to him. Fulfilling a threat is hard only for the one threatening against a person who can resist when he tries to carry it out; but whoever is in the grip of the one who threatened him, it is easy for that one to execute his judgement on him. The editor refers to the earlier language indexes on transgression and wrong and to an earlier explanation of the word for burning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 232}

**The end of the sixth part of the manuscript (note on p. 232).** At this point the editor says the sixth part of his manuscript ends, and he gives the closing note at its end: a formula of completion with thanks to God and blessings on the Prophet and his family and companions, a statement that the next part opens with the commentary on the verse on the major sins that al-Tabari takes up next, that the copying was finished in one of the months of the year 715 of the Hijra, with a prayer for forgiveness for its owner, its copyist, its author and all Muslims, and below it a later reader's note in a North African hand, saying that Muhammad b. Mahmud b. Muhammad b. Husayn al-Jaza'iri al-Hanafi read it on the second of Rabi al-Awwal of the year 1239. The editor says this al-Jaza'iri is the one whose marginal note was printed in volume 5 in its place, and that the seventh part of the manuscript then begins with the basmala and a request for help.
These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 232}
