## Summary
Verse 1 of [[passage/quran-10|Surat Yunus]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 1 of 109 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 1 (pp. 9-12)**

Al-Tabari opens the commentary on Surat Yunus with the letters at the head of the surah and says the interpreters differed about them. Some said the meaning is "I am God, I see"; he gives report 17518, from al-Dahhak, through Yahya ibn Dawud al-Wasiti, Abu Usama and Abu Rawq, and report 17519, from Ibn Abbas to the same sense, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sharik, Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Abu al-Duha. The editor identifies Yahya ibn Dawud al-Wasiti as a teacher of al-Tabari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 9}

Others said these are letters of God's name al-Rahman. He gives report 17520, from Ibn Abbas, that these letters are the letters of al-Rahman separated; report 17521, from Salim ibn Abd Allah, that the name al-Rahman is split up in such openings and then pronounced whole; report 17522, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, to the same sense; and report 17523, from al-Sha'bi (Amir), who was asked about these letters and said they are names of God split into spelled letters, which become one of His names when joined. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 10}

Still others said it is one of the names of the Qur'an, with report 17524 from Qatada. Al-Tabari says that he set out the people's disagreement about such openings, each speaker's view and what he holds to be right, at the start of Surat al-Baqara, so he does not repeat it here; he gave the reports only because they differ from what was said there about the letters of the first of them, and for those who reconciled all these meanings he has already given their view and need not repeat it. The editor refers to the first volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 10}

He turns to "these are the verses of the wise Book", on which they also differed. Some said "these" means the verses of the Torah: report 17525, from Mujahid, says the Torah and the Gospel, and report 17526, from Qatada, the books that came before the Qur'an. Others said they are the verses of the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 11}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that they are verses of the Qur'an, "that" being used in the sense of "this", a usage he explained in Surat al-Baqara; "verses" are signs, and "the Book" is a name of the Qur'an. In a note the editor says the printed text has a different form of a word where the manuscript reading is correct, and he follows the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 11}

His reason is that neither the Torah nor the Gospel has been mentioned before this place, nor are they recited after it, so the statement cannot be turned to them; the sense is therefore "these are the verses of the wise Qur'an". "Wise" here means "made firm", a form turned from one pattern to another, as in the phrase "a painful punishment" meaning one that pains. He cites a line of poetry by Amr ibn Ma'dikarib al-Zubaydi for this use (described here, not quoted), and the editor refers to its earlier tracing and explanation in the first volume. So the meaning is the verses of the Book God made firm and made clear to His servants, as at the opening of Surat Hud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 12}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
