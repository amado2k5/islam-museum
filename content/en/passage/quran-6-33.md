## Summary
Verse 33 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[person/abu-jahl|Abu Jahl]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 33 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "we know that what they say grieves you; it is not you they deny, but the wrongdoers reject God's signs" al-Tabari says: we know, Muhammad, that what the polytheists say grieves you, namely their saying that he is a liar, "yet it is not you they deny". The readers differed: a group of Kufan readers read "la yukdhibunaka" with light dhal, meaning they do not call you a liar in what you brought them of God's revelation and do not reject its being true, for they know its truth but deny its reality in words, so do not believe. One scholar of Arab speech used to report from the Arabs that they say "akdhabtu al-rajul" when you say he brought a falsehood and reported it, and "kadhdhabtuhu" when you say he is a liar. A group of readers of Medina, the two Iraqs, Kufa and Basra read "la yukadhdhibunaka", meaning they do not deny you in knowledge, for they know you are truthful, but deny you in words, out of obstinacy and envy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 331}

The editor says the bracketed addition in the preceding sentence is missing from manuscript and print and is needed, that he inferred it from the attribution of this reading, which is that of Ali, Nafi' and al-Kisa'i, and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and Abu Hayyan's commentary.

Al-Tabari holds the right view to be that these are two well-known readings, each read by a group of readers, each with an understandable route to soundness. For there is no doubt that among the polytheists were some who called the Messenger of God a liar and rejected the prophethood God had given him, some saying he is a poet, some a soothsayer, some a madman, all denying that what he brought was revelation from heaven or a sending-down from the Lord of the worlds in speech; and among them some had seen his case clearly and knew the truth of his prophethood, yet stubbornly rejected it, out of envy and wrong. So the reader of "la yukdhibunaka" means that those who knew the reality of your prophethood and the truth of your words deny, in speech, that what you recite to them is God's sending down, knowing with sound knowledge that it is from God, and he is right in what we said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 331}

And in God's word in this surah, [[passage/quran-6-20|6:20]], "those to whom we gave the book know him as they know their sons", is the clearest proof that among them was the obstinate who rejected his prophethood while knowing him and the truth of his prophethood. Likewise the reader of "la yukadhdhibunaka" means that they deny the Messenger of God only out of obstinacy, not ignorance of his prophethood or the truth of his speech, and is right in what we said. A group of interpreters went to each of these two interpretations. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 332}

Reports 13190 and 13191, from Abu Salih: Gabriel came to the Prophet one day as he sat grieving and asked what grieved him; he said, these have denied me! Gabriel said, they do not deny you, they know you are truthful, "but the wrongdoers reject God's signs". 13192, from Qatada: they know you are God's messenger and reject it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 333}

Report 13193, from al-Suddi: on the day of Badr al-Akhnas ibn Shariq said to Banu Zuhra: Banu Zuhra, Muhammad is your sister's son, so you are most entitled to hold back from him; if he is a prophet you will not fight him today, and if he is a liar you are the most entitled to hold back from your sister's son; stay here until I meet Abu al-Hakam, and if Muhammad is beaten you will return safe, and if he prevails your people will do nothing to you. On that day he was named al-Akhnas, his name having been Ubayy. Al-Akhnas met Abu Jahl and took him aside and said: Abu al-Hakam, tell me about Muhammad, is he truthful or a liar? No one of Quraysh is here but I and you to hear our talk! Abu Jahl said: woe to you, by God Muhammad is truthful and has never lied, but if the sons of Qusayy take the banner, the keeping of the Ka'ba's door, the watering of pilgrims and prophethood, what is left for the rest of Quraysh? That is God's word "it is not you they deny, but the wrongdoers reject God's signs", the signs of God being Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 333}

The editor notes that Ibn Kathir's commentary here has "you are the most entitled to defend him", and explains "al-Akhnas" as from "khanasa yakhnisu khunusan" when one withdrew from something, drew back and returned.

Report 13194, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they do not deny Muhammad, but they reject God's signs. Those who said the sense is that they do not deny you but deny what you brought: in 13195 and 13196, from Najiya ibn Ka'b, Abu Jahl said to the Prophet: we do not accuse you, but we accuse what you brought! and God revealed "it is not you they deny, but the wrongdoers reject God's signs". Others said the sense is that they do not make false what you brought them; 13197, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b: they do not nullify what is in your hands. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 334}

As for "but the wrongdoers reject God's signs", he says: but the polytheists reject God's proofs, the verses of his book and his messenger, denying the soundness of all that. Al-Suddi used to say that "signs" here means Muhammad, and the report of that from him has been given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 334}

Verse 33 closes here at p. 334, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-34|6:34]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
