## Summary
Verse 119 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 119 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 119, first clauses: leading astray, arousing desires and the command to slit the cattle's ears (pp. 213-214, notes 1-3 on p. 214).** Al-Tabari says that the rebellious devil's words reported in this verse mean three things. First, that he will turn the determined portion he takes from God's servants away from the highway of guidance to error, and from Islam to disbelief. Second, that he will make them swerve, through the desires he puts in their souls, from obeying God and affirming His oneness to obeying him and associating partners with God. Third, that he will command that portion of God's servants to worship idols and rivals instead of God, until they sacrifice to them, declare things forbidden and permitted for them, and lay down a law other than the one God laid down, so that they follow him and oppose God. The word for slitting is cutting, and here it is the cutting of the ear of the bahira so that it is known as a bahira; what the accursed one meant is that he calls them to the bahira, and they answer him and practise it in obedience to him. Three reports are given. Report 10445 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says that the slitting concerned the bahira and the sa'iba, whose ears they slit for their idols. Report 10446 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says they split the ears and make the animals bahira. Report 10447 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza, from Ikrima) says it was a religion Iblis laid down for them, like the bahiras and the sa'ibs. The editor's notes on p. 214: that the verb for sacrificing means to slaughter a sacrificial animal, with references to earlier explanations; that the bahira was one of the beliefs of the people of the Jahiliyya which Islam voided, a sheep or she-camel whose ear is slit and which is then left alone so that no one touches it; and that the sa'iba was an animal a man vowed to set loose when he returned from a long journey, recovered from illness or was delivered from hardship or war, so that its back was not used, it was not kept from water or pasture and it was not ridden. The editor adds a remark on the plural form of the second word, noting that the manuscript has the correct form and the printer altered it needlessly, though the printer's form is also correct, since such reports are themselves evidence for the language. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 214}

**Verse 119, the alteration clause: castration, the religion of God, tattooing and the like (pp. 215-223, note 1 on p. 215, note 1 on p. 216, notes 1-2 on p. 217, note 1 on p. 219, note 1 on p. 220, notes 1-2 on p. 221, note 1 on p. 222 and notes 1-3 on p. 223).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the clause about the devil's command to change what God created, and the reports fall into three views, each given as transmitted. The first view is that the sense is castrating animals. Report 10448 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Hammad ibn Salama, from Ammar ibn Abi Ammar) has Ibn Abbas disliking castration and saying the clause was revealed about it; 10449 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abdallah ibn Dawud, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas) and 10450 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Abu Ja'far, from al-Rabi', from Anas ibn Malik) give the same for Anas. Report 10451 (Ibn Waki', from Ibn Fudayl, from Mutarrif) has an unnamed man tell that Ibn Abbas called castrating animals mutilation and then recited the clause. Report 10452 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Abu Ja'far al-Razi, from al-Rabi') says castration is among the changes; 10453 (the same chain through Ja'far ibn Sulayman) has Shubayl hear Shahr ibn Hawshab give this meaning, and, when Shubayl had Abu al-Tayyah ask al-Hasan about gelding sheep, al-Hasan said there was no harm in it. Reports 10454 and 10455 pass through al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza: Mujahid told him to ask Ikrima, who said castration. In 10455 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Abd al-Jabbar ibn al-Ward) Mujahid reacts by cursing Ikrima and saying he knew the meaning was other than castration; when al-Qasim asked again, Ikrima answered by pointing to the verse of sura 30 about the natural disposition, [[passage/quran-30-30|30:30]], saying it means God's religion, and Mujahid, on hearing this, said God disgrace him. Further reports give castration for Ikrima (10456, 10457, and 10462, in which he is said to have disliked it and to have said the clause came down about it), for Abu Salih (10458), for Anas (10459) and again for Ibn Abbas (10460, 10461). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 223}

The second view is that the sense is the religion of God. It is reported from Ibn Abbas (10463, al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali), from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (10464 to 10467, 10481), from Mujahid (10468 to 10474, among them 10469, in which, when Ikrima's view is mentioned to him, he calls Ikrima a liar, and 10470, which gives the religion of God for both Mujahid and Ikrima), from Qatada and al-Hasan (10475, 10476), from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza (10477), from al-Suddi (10478), from al-Dahhak (10479 and the letter he wrote to Kathir, the freedman of Ibn Samura, in 10482) and from Ibn Zayd (10480). Several of these, 10471, 10472, 10473, 10479 and 10480, tie the reading to the verse of sura 30 on the natural disposition, [[passage/quran-30-30|30:30]], where the natural disposition and the straight religion are named together. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 223}

The third view is that the sense is tattooing, and the reports bring in a related group of acts. Reports 10483 to 10485 give al-Hasan al-Basri saying tattooing; in 10486 (Ahmad ibn Hazim, from Abu Nu'aym, from Abu Hilal al-Rasibi) he is asked about a woman who peeled her face and says that God cursed her and that she changed what God made. Reports 10487 to 10489 are sayings of Abdallah (that is, Ibn Mas'ud), passed through Ibrahim and, in two chains, Alqama, that God cursed women who space their teeth, pluck their eyebrows, tattoo themselves, and file their teeth for beauty, as changers of what God made; in 10489 Shu'ba says he thinks the closing phrase was included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 223}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: the most correct of the views is that the sense is the religion of God, because the other verse, on the natural disposition, shows this meaning. On that meaning, he says, there enters every act God forbade, castrating what may not be castrated, tattooing and filing teeth where forbidden, and other acts of disobedience, and also the abandonment of everything God commanded, since the devil calls to all disobedience and forbids all obedience; this is what is meant by his commanding his portion to change what God made of His religion. So, al-Tabari says, there is no sense in taking the clause to mean a promise to command the changing of some forbidden things and not others. As for the one who limited it to castration and tattooing, he may have done so because he took the sense to be bodily change; but the devil's words about slitting the cattle's ears already announce a bodily change, in detail, so there is no reason to repeat that in general terms afterward, since the fuller Arabic style explains the general by the detailed and the broad by the specific, not the reverse, and the book of God is better read in the fuller style where that can be done. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 223}

The editor Shakir adds notes. On p. 215 he remarks that the old jurists use a word for castration which the dictionaries do not record, that al-Mutarrizi in his lexicon calls it an error, and that this is a point of difficulty, since the many reports below, including words of Ibn Abbas, show the word in use and the lexicographers themselves draw on such reports. On p. 216 he gives biographical notes on Shubayl ibn Azra (a reliable narrator used once by Abu Dawud, a master of Arabic and brother-in-law of Qatada; al-Jahiz is quoted as counting him among Kharijite scholars after seventy years as a Rafidi, while al-Baladhuri is quoted as saying he was not a Kharijite and composed such verses as concealment) and Abu al-Tayyah (Yazid ibn Humayd, a firm and reliable narrator). On p. 217 he notes that Abd al-Jabbar ibn al-Ward is reliable, and that Mujahid's curse on Ikrima reflects his poor opinion of him for inconsistency in transmission, as Malik ibn Anas also held; he gives from Ibn Hajar a report that al-A'mash found Ikrima inconsistent about the great seizure, and refers to Ibn Hajar's long treatment of Ikrima's reliability and the many imams who narrated from him. He also notes that 10461 is a continuation of 10460 numbered separately by mistake. On pp. 219 to 221 his notes refer back to the note on 10455, identify the narrators (Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh al-Anbari, Yazid ibn Harun, and the brothers Nuh ibn Qays and Khalid ibn Qays, the latter reliable) with manuscript readings against the print, and explain the face-peeling of 10486, an old treatment with saffron or a mixture, which he says was forbidden because it removed the top of the skin, citing a saying that God cursed the woman who peels and the woman who is peeled. On p. 222 he says that 10487 to 10489 are a sound report in al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari 10:317 and following) and in the Musnad of Ahmad, notes that Alqama is missing from one chain, as Ibn Hajar also observes of some of al-A'mash's pupils, and that Ahmad's wording includes the tattooed women, so that he fears a word dropped from the copyist's text of 10489, and defines the terms: the woman who spaces her teeth by filing, who plucks her eyebrows, who tattoos by pricking the skin and filling it with a dye, and, in a note running onto p. 223, who sharpens her teeth with a file. On p. 223 he records the manuscript and print variants (including a line dropped from the manuscript, where he thinks the print's reading right and is unsure whether it is a copyist's repair or al-Tabari's own words), and adds a closing remark in his own voice that Muslim women of his day do all this in imitation of those who disbelieved, that he sees the punishment warned of as having come upon the community for its disobedience, and a prayer for guidance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 223}

**Verse 119, last clause: taking the devil as an ally (p. 224, notes 1-3 on p. 224).** Al-Tabari says this is a report from God about the state of the determined portion of Satan, those who broke away from God and His Messenger after the guidance became clear to them. Whoever follows the devil and obeys him in disobedience and opposition to God's command, and takes him as an ally and helper to the exclusion of God, has perished and has cheated himself of his share, and the loss is plain because the devil has no power to give him help against God when God punishes him for that disobedience, but abandons him when he needs him. The editor's notes: that the print adds a word "from" before those who broke away, against the manuscript; that both the manuscript and the print omit a "from" before "God", which he restored on the strength of the verse; and references to earlier explanations of the words for loss and for clear (p. 199, note 2). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 224}
