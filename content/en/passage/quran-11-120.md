**Commentary on verse 120 (pp. 539-543)**

On [[passage/quran-11-120|11:120]] al-Tabari says: 'all that We relate to you', Muhammad, 'of the news of the messengers' who were before you, 'to strengthen your heart with', so do not be distressed at the denial of those of your people who denied you and rejected what you brought them, and let your chest not tighten so as to leave out some of what I sent down to you because they said 'if only a treasure were sent down to him or an angel came with him', when you know what the messengers before you met from their nations; report 18741, from Ibn Jurayj: so that you know what the messengers before you met from their peoples. The grammarians differ on the reason for the accusative 'kullan'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 539}

One of the Basran grammarians said it is accusative in the sense 'and We relate to you of the news of the messengers what strengthens your heart, all of it', taking 'all' as an accusative verbal noun of 'relate', that is, We relate to you all the stories; some grammarians rejected that and said it is not allowed, saying 'kullan' is accusative by 'relate' because 'kull' is built on annexation whether or not an annexed noun is with it, meaning 'We relate all of it to you', making 'what We strengthen' a restatement of 'kullan'. As for 'and in this has come to you the truth', the interpreters differ; some said it means in this chapter the truth has come to you; reports 18742 and 18743, from Abu Musa: in this chapter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 540}

Reports 18744, 18745, 18746, 18747 and 18748, from Ibn Abbas: in this chapter, among them that Ibn Abbas read this chapter to the people until he reached the verse and said in this chapter, and that he read the verse from the pulpit and said in this chapter; 18749, 18750, 18751 and 18752, from Mujahid, and 18753, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: in this chapter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 541}

Reports 18754 and 18755, from Abu al-'Aliya, 18756, 18757, 18758 and 18762, from al-Hasan, 18759, from Mujahid, and 18760 and 18761, from Qatada: in this chapter. Others said the sense is that in this world the truth has come to you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 542}

Report 18763, from Qatada: in this world; 18764, from al-Hasan: in the world. Al-Tabari says: the better of the two interpretations is that of whoever said that in this chapter the truth has come to you, for the consensus of the authorities among the interpreters that this is its sense. If someone asks, did the truth not come to the Prophet in the other chapters of the Qur'an, so that it would be said in this chapter the truth came? The answer is, yes, it came to him in all of them. If he asks, what is the point of singling this chapter out? The answer is that the sense is the truth came to you in this chapter along with what came to you in the other chapters of the Qur'an, not that the truth came to you in this chapter apart from the others. 'And an admonition' means an admonition came to you that admonishes those ignorant of God and shows them the lesson of those who disbelieved in Him and denied His messengers, 'and a reminder for the believers', a reminder that reminds the believers of God and His messengers so that they do not neglect what they owe God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 543}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
