## Summary
Verse 138 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 138 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**What "this" points to (pp. 231-232).** The interpreters differed on what is meant by "this". Some said it means the Qur'an.
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7873): this Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 231}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7874): it is this Qur'an; God made it an exposition for people in general, and guidance and admonition for the God-fearing in particular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 232}
- From al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7875): for the God-fearing in particular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 232}
- From al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Ibn Jurayj (7876): for the God-fearing in particular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 232}
Others said "this" points to the preceding command to travel and look at the patterns of the nations that passed: that is, what I have taught you, Companions of Muhammad, is an exposition for people. From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq to that effect (7877). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 232}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 232).** Abu Ja'far says the sounder of the two views in his opinion is that "this" points to what precedes the verse, God's reminding the believers, teaching them His limits and urging them to hold to obedience and to patience in fighting His enemies and theirs, because "this" points to something present, seen or heard, and here it is heard in the preceding verses; the sense is: what I have made clear and made known to you is an exposition, meaning explanation and interpretation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 232}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7878): this is an interpretation for people, if they accept it. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 116), the continuation of the reports ending at 7870. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 233}
- From Ahmad ibn Hazim and al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Sufyan, Bayan, al-Sha'bi (7879): this is an exposition, from blindness. From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri, al-Sha'bi, the same (7880). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 233}

**Guidance and admonition (p. 233).** Abu Ja'far says guidance is pointing to the path of truth and the course of the religion, and admonition is the reminder toward what is right and sound. The editor refers to the language indexes for "guidance" and to the earlier explanation of "admonition" (2: 180 and 6: 14, as printed).
- From Ahmad ibn Hazim and al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Sufyan, Bayan, al-Sha'bi (7881): guidance away from error, and admonition away from ignorance. From al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri, Bayan, al-Sha'bi, the same (7882). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 233}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7883): for the God-fearing, that is for whoever obeyed Me and knew My command. The editor traces it to Ibn Hisham (3: 116) and says it seems that what Ibn Hisham gives for this verse dropped out of Ibn Ishaq's text in al-Tabari, namely his words before the part al-Tabari transmitted: "light and discipline for the God-fearing"; what Abu Ja'far transmitted interprets the God-fearing, and here it explains guidance and admonition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 233}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 233}
