## Summary
Verse 124 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 124 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 124: who enters Paradise, the grain-groove and the word "of" (pp. 248-250, note 1 on p. 248, notes 1-3 on p. 249 and note 1 on p. 250).** Al-Tabari says that this is God speaking to those to whom He said that the matter is not by their wishes: only the one who does righteous deeds, among their males and females and among His servants' males and females, while believing in God and His Messenger Muhammad and affirming His oneness and Muhammad's prophethood and what he brought, will enter Paradise and enjoy it in the hereafter; the polytheists who deny the Messenger are not to hope to take the place of the believers while they are unbelievers. Report 10535 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says that God refused to accept faith without righteous work and refused to accept Islam without doing good. On the last words of the verse, he says that God does not wrong those who do righteous deeds of the reward of their deeds by so much as the naqir, the groove on the back of a date-stone, which is the smallest thing, so how by anything greater; this is told to the servants so that they know He does not diminish the reward of their deeds, little or much, but gives it in full as He promised. Reports 10536 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Mujahid) and 10537 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abu Amir, from Qurra, from Atiyya) give the naqir as the groove on the back of the date-stone and, for Atiyya, the one in its middle. He then answers a question about why the word "of" appears before righteous deeds in the verse. There are two reasons. One is that God knew His believing servants could not do all righteous deeds, so He made His promise binding for whoever does what he can and did not deprive him of His grace for what his strength fell short of. The other is that He made the promise binding for whoever avoids the major sins and performs the obligations, even if he falls short in some duty, as a favour to the believers, since grace is more fitting for Him and forgiving the people of faith more worthy of Him. Some of the grammarians, he adds, claimed that the word was added in this place with the sense of omission, so that the clause means whoever does the righteous deeds; he judges that impermissible, because its entry has a meaning and so it cannot mean omission. The editor notes that the passage from "what we have said on the sense of naqir" to the end of 10537 is missing from the manuscript, and that the two reports are not among those listed in the earlier place where the word is explained (volume 8, pp. 472-475), a case of al-Tabari's abridging of his own commentary; he explains that in the discussion the manuscript has a marker over the word for "his strength" showing that the reading was already so in the copyist's source, that the print has the plural "his powers" which is also good, and that the word for "some people" before the grammarians' view is absent from the manuscript but harmless, the view being that of some of the Basran grammarians, as al-Tabari has often indicated; and refers to earlier explanations of the added "of" (note on p. 250). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 250}
