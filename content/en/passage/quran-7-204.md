## Summary
Verse 204 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 204 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 204 (pp. 344-352)**

God says to those who believe in Him and trust His book, for whom the Qur'an is guidance and mercy: "when the Qur'an is recited" to you, believers, "listen to it", meaning lend it your hearing to understand its verses and take warning from its admonitions, "and be silent" for it to grasp it and ponder it and not babble over it so that you do not grasp it, "that you may be shown mercy" meaning that your Lord may show you mercy through your taking admonition from His admonitions, taking lesson from its lessons and acting on what your Lord made clear to you of His obligations in its verses. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "listen". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 344} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 345}

Then the interpreters differed over the situation in which God commanded listening to the reciter of the Qur'an when he recites and silence for him. Some said it is the situation of the one praying behind an imam whom he follows and he hears the imam's recitation, who must listen to his recitation; and they said it was revealed about that. Report 15581, from al-Musayyab ibn Rafi: Abd Allah used to say we used to greet one another in prayer, peace on so-and-so, peace on so-and-so, and the verse [[passage/quran-7-204|7:204]] came; report 15582, from Abu Hurayra: they used to talk in prayer and when this verse and the other came down they were commanded to be silent. The editor identifies Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash and Asim ibn Abi al-Najud as trustworthy, and al-Musayyab ibn Rafi al-Asadi as a trustworthy Follower who did not meet Ibn Mas'ud, so the first report has a break in its chain, which Ibn Kathir mentions; he adds that report 15582 will come by another chain with other wording as report 15601 and that Ibrahim al-Hajari is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 345}

Report 15583, from al-Zuhri: this verse was revealed about a young man of the Ansar who, whenever the Messenger of God recited something, recited it too. Report 15584, from Bashir ibn Jabir: Ibn Mas'ud prayed and heard people reciting with the imam, and when he finished he said, is it not time that you understood, is it not time that you reasoned? and recited [[passage/quran-7-204|7:204]] as God commanded you; the editor notes that the manuscript has an unpointed name he could find no reading for. Report 15585, from Talha ibn Ubayd Allah ibn Kariz: I saw Ubayd ibn Umayr and Ata ibn Abi Rabah talking while the storyteller was telling stories and I said, will you not listen to the remembrance and earn what is promised? They looked at me and turned back to their talk; I repeated it three times, and they said that is only in prayer, and recited the verse. The editor identifies Talha as Abu al-Mutarrif al-Misri, trustworthy and of few hadiths. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 345} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 346}
Report 15586, from Abu Hurayra: it was revealed about raising voices behind the Messenger of God in prayer. The editor says the report is weak in chain because of the weakness of Abd Allah ibn Amir al-Aslami, whom Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in, Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim weakened; that al-Wahidi gave it in Asbab al-Nuzul; that Abu Ayad Umar ibn al-Aswad al-Ansi was a trustworthy devotee of the people of Syria; that the preceding report 15582 is weak because of the weakness of Ibrahim al-Hajari; and that al-Bayhaqi gave it and al-Suyuti added Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, Abu al-Shaykh and Ibn Mardawayh. Reports 15587 to 15593, from Mujahid, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Dahhak, Ibrahim and al-Hakam: in prayer, some saying in the obligatory prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 346} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 347} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 348}
Report 15598, from Qatada: they used to talk in their prayer about their needs when it was first made obligatory for them, so God sent down the verse; report 15599, from Qatada: a man would come while they were praying and ask them how many they had prayed and how many remained, so God sent down the verse, and another said they used to raise their voices in prayer when they heard mention of Paradise and the Fire so God sent it down; report 15600, from al-Zuhri: the Prophet was reciting and a man was reciting and it was revealed; report 15601, from Abu Hurayra: they used to talk in prayer and when it was revealed he said this is in prayer, the editor referring to 15582; report 15602, from Amir: in the obligatory prayer; report 15603, from al-Suddi: when it is recited in prayer; report 15604, from Ibn Abbas: he means in the prescribed prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 348} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 349}
Report 15605, from Mujahid: this is in prayer, and he disliked that when the imam passes a verse of fear or mercy anyone behind him should say anything, the thing being silence, and said there is no harm when a man reads outside prayer in speaking; report 15606, from Ibn Zayd: this is when the imam stands for prayer; report 15607, from al-Zuhri: one does not recite behind the imam in what he recites aloud, the imam's recitation suffices them even if his voice does not reach them, but they recite in what he does not recite aloud, silently to themselves, and it is not right for anyone behind him to recite with him in what he recites aloud, silently or openly, and he cited the verse; report 15608, from Ibn Abbas: this is in the obligatory prayer, and as for stories or recitation besides, they are voluntary; the Prophet of God recited in an obligatory prayer and his companions recited behind him and confused him, so the Qur'an came down with the verse, and so this is in the obligatory prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 349} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 350}
Others said the verse rather means the command to be silent for the imam in the sermon when he recites the Qur'an in his sermon. Report 15609, from Mujahid: silence for the imam on Friday; report 15610, from Mujahid: in the sermon of Friday. Others said it means silence in the prayer and in the sermon. Report 15611, from Mujahid: in prayer and the Friday sermon; report 15612, from Ata: silence is due in two cases, with a man reciting the Qur'an while praying and with the imam while he preaches; report 15613, from Mujahid: silence is due in two, in prayer while the imam recites and on Friday while the imam preaches; report 15614, from al-Hasan: in the obligatory prayer and at the remembrance; report 15615, from Mujahid: silence is due in two, in prayer and on Friday; report 15616, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: silence on the Day of Sacrifice, the Day of Breaking the Fast and Friday, in what the imam recites aloud of the prayer, the editor saying Thabit ibn Ajlan al-Ansari al-Sulami is spoken of, some holding him trustworthy and others weak; report 15617, from al-Hasan: in prayer and at the remembrance; report 15618, from Ata: the verse made silence obligatory on Friday, and in prayer likewise. The editor mentions what he corrected in the chains of the printed edition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 350} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 351} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 352}
Al-Tabari chooses: the best of the views is that of those who said they were commanded to listen to the Qur'an in prayer when the imam recites and the one behind him following him hears it, and in the sermon. He says this is best because of the soundness of the report from the Messenger of God that he said when the imam recites, be silent, and the agreement of all that whoever hears the sermon of the imam and is obliged to attend the Friday prayer must listen and be silent for it, with the succession of reports commanding that from the Messenger of God, and that there is no time when it is obligatory for anyone to listen to the Qur'an and be silent for the one who hears it from its reciter except in these two cases, with a disagreement over one of them, which is the case of being behind an imam one follows. The report has come sound from the Messenger… The editor refers to the tracing of the report in al-Bayhaqi's Great Sunan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 352}
We stop here at the foot of p. 352, in the commentary on 7:204 in the middle of al-Tabari's sentence at "the report has come sound from the Messenger"; the next batch begins at p. 353. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 352}
