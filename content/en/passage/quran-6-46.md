## Summary
Verse 46 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 46 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: do you see, if God took away your hearing and your sight and set a seal on your hearts, what god other than God would bring it back to you? See how we vary the signs, yet they turn away" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these who hold idols and images equal to me and deny you: do you see, polytheists who join others with God, if God made you deaf and took your hearing, made you blind and took your sight, and set a seal on your hearts and stamped them so that you could not understand a word, see a proof or grasp what is to be grasped, what god other than God, to whom belongs the worship of every worshipper, would bring it back to you, that is, give back what God took from you of hearing, sight and understanding, so that you would worship him or join him in worship with your Lord who is able to take that from you and to give it back to you when he wills? This is God's teaching his prophet the argument against those who join others with him: he tells him to say to them that those they worship besides God own no harm or benefit for you, and only he deserves your worship in whose hand are harm and benefit, withholding and giving, who is able to do all he wills, and not the helpless who can do nothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 365}

Then he said to his prophet Muhammad: "see how we vary the signs", meaning see how we follow arguments upon them and set examples and lessons for them so that they take heed and remember and turn back; "yet they turn away" means that despite our following arguments upon them and warning them with lessons, they turn from remembering and taking heed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 365}

The editor refers for "sealing the heart" to pp. 258 to 262 of volume 1 and for "varying" to pp. 275 and 276 of volume 3.

The rest: one says "sadafa fulan anni bi-wajhihi, so he yasdifu sudufan wa-sadfan", that is, turned aside and away, from which comes a line of Ibn al-Riqa' about women who, when they remember a story, tell its best part and who turn away from every evil that is to be guarded against, and a line of Labid in his poem describing men of ease and luxury who drink at night and turn away from drink by day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 366}

The editor says he did not find Ibn al-Riqa's line or know the poem, and that Labid's line is in his Diwan, poem 12, line 22, among verses in which he praises himself well; he gives the lines before it, explains "al-mu'abbad" as the trodden road, "al-tajr" as the sellers of wine, "al-fudal" as its dregs and "al-qawamih" as those who dislike and refuse drink, and says the poet describes the men as people of luxury and ease when night comes.

If someone asks how it was said "what god other than God would bring it back to you" with a singular pronoun, when the mention before it was plural, "if God took away your hearing, your sight and set a seal on your hearts", the answer is that the pronoun may refer to "hearing", so it is singular as hearing is singular; or the sense may be "what god other than God would bring you back what he took from you of hearing, sight and hearts", singular as "what" is singular; and the Arabs do this, using a singular pronoun when they refer to acts, however many the acts referred to, as in "your coming and going pleases me". It has also been said that the pronoun in "bihi" refers to "guidance". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 367}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an for these two explanations.

The interpreters said as we said on "they turn away": 13244 and 13245, from Mujahid: turn away. 13246, from Ibn Abbas: turn aside. 13247, from Qatada: turn away from them. 13248, from al-Suddi: block. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 368}

Verse 46 closes here at p. 368, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-47|6:47]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
