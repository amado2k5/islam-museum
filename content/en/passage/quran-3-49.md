## Summary
Verse 49 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 49 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The messenger to the Israelites.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that He makes him a messenger to the Children of Israel, the verb of making being omitted because the speech indicates it, as the poet says in a line about a woman who sees her husband in battle wearing sword and spear, the line and its sources being given at 1:140 (the editor). The sign, he says, is the mark that confirms Jesus' word and verifies his report that he is a messenger from their Lord to them. The editor explains that the passage from 'and We make him a messenger' to 'my warner' is God's account to Mary, after which al-Tabari begins to explain the words of Jesus; that the print and the manuscript have a faulty phrase in one place and he gave the correct one, and that the print has a different word where the manuscript is right. Report 7085 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says his prophethood is confirmed by it and that he is God's messenger to them; the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham (2:230), completing the reports whose last was 7084. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 423} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 424}

**The clay and the bird.** Al-Tabari says God then makes plain what the sign is: that he makes for them a bird-shape from clay. He says the plural is the plural of the singular word for bird, and that the readers differed: some Hijazis read the singular in both places and others the plural in both. The reading he likes best is the plural in both, because this was a description of Jesus that he does it by leave of his Lord and because it matches the written form of the codex; following the codex's script with sound sense and wide currency is better to him than departing from it, and this is his own choice. The editor explains the reading of the manuscript and print in a note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 424} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 425}

**The account of Ibn Ishaq on the bird.** Report 7086 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq) says Jesus sat one day with boys of the scribes' school and took clay and asked whether he should make them a bird from it; they asked whether he could, and he said yes, by leave of his Lord; he shaped it, then, when he had made it the shape of a bird, he blew into it and told it to be a bird by his Lord's leave, and it went off flying between his palms. The boys went out and told their teacher, and spread the news among people. He grew up and the Israelites intended harm to him, so when his mother feared for him she put him on a small donkey of hers and left, fleeing with him. The editor explains the vocalisation of the diminutive of donkey. Al-Tabari also mentions that when he wished to create the bird he asked them which bird is hardest to make and was told the bat; report 7087 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says he asked which bird is hardest in creation, they said the bat, which is only flesh, and he did it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 425} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 426}

**The grammar of the blowing.** Al-Tabari asks how it can be said he blows 'into it' when the preceding words speak of making the shape of a bird, and answers that the sense is blowing into the bird, and that 'into them' would have been sound too, as in the table surah [[passage/quran-5-110|5:110]], where the blowing is into the form; the editor says the manuscript and print carry a wording that differs from the recitation there, a slip of the scribe who had just copied the Al Imran verse, which the publishers followed. He mentions that in one reading the preposition is dropped, and that the Arabs do this, saying 'many a night I spent it and spent in it'; the editor says this is the text of al-Farra (1:214) and that al-Tabari has adapted and abridged it. Al-Tabari cites a line of Ibn Mufarrigh al-Himyari (as the editor identifies him) in which he says of one he mourns that no collar was torn and no wailing woman stood for him, meaning stood over him; the editor gives the story of Ubaydullah ibn Ziyad, killed by the army of al-Mukhtar at the battle of al-Zab, and of Ibn Mufarrigh's satire on him, and explains the word for spoils. He also cites a line by another poet whom the editor could not identify, about a woman of the Banu Ayidh Allah gone until the blowing of the Horn, and the editor explains its words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 426} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 427}

**Healing and the meaning of the word for the born-blind.** Al-Tabari says the verb means 'I cure': one says God cured the sick when He healed him, and the sick man recovered, in two known forms. The commentators differed about the word for the one afflicted in sight. Some said it is one who cannot see at night but sees by day: report 7088 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the one who sees by day and not at night, and 7089 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) the same; the editor says the Arabs say 'he went out wandering' of one who goes out bewildered. Others said he is the blind person whom his mother bore so: report 7090 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says they used to be told that he is the one born blind with his eyes covered, as does 7091 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from Qatada), and 7092 (al-Tabari, from al-Munjab, from Bishr, from Umara, from Abu Ruq, from al-Dahhak, from Ibn Abbas) says he is the one born blind; the editor says the printed text has 'closed eyes' and that he kept 'covered', meaning hidden. Others said he is simply the blind: 7093 (Musa ibn Harun, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi), 7094 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abbas), 7095 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) and 7096 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan). Others said he is the weak-sighted: report 7097 (printed 7017 in the OCR, numbered here from context) (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Hafs ibn Umar, from al-Hakam ibn Aban, from Ikrima). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 428} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 429}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says what is known among the Arabs of the sense of the root is blindness, one saying that his eye went blind and I blinded it, and cites a line of Suwayd ibn Abi Kahil about eyes turned white and a line of Ruba using the same verb; the editor explains Suwayd's poem and his enemy and refers to the Mufaddaliyyat (405) and the lexicon. He then says God reports Jesus saying this to the Israelites as an argument from these lessons and signs for his prophethood, because blindness and leprosy have no treatment by which a physician could cure them, so this was among the proofs of the truth of his saying that he is God's messenger, being among the miracles. He then begins on what Ikrima said, that the word means weak-sighted, and on what Mujahid said, and the text breaks off there at the end of p. 430. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 430}

**Reply to the sayings of Ikrima and Mujahid.** Al-Tabari says what Ikrima said, that the word means weak sight, and what Mujahid said, that it is poor sight at night, have no sense, because God does not argue against His creatures with a proof they could dispute. Had Jesus' proof of his prophethood against the Israelites been that he heals the weak-sighted or the one who sees by day and not at night, they could have contested him by saying what proof is that for you when there are people among us who treat that and are neither prophets nor messengers. In this he finds clear evidence that the word means the blind who see nothing, night or day, and it is closer to Qatada's saying, that he is one born so, since no human claims to treat such a thing except one God gave what He gave Jesus, and the same holds for treating the leper. The editor adds explanations of words in the poem of Suwayd cited earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 430} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 431}

**Raising the dead.** Al-Tabari says Jesus' raising of the dead was by prayer to God: he prayed for them and was answered. Report 7098 (Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn Askar, from Isma'il ibn Abd al-Karim, from Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil) says he heard Wahb ibn Munabbih say that when Jesus was twelve God inspired his mother, who was in Egypt, where she had fled from her people when she bore him, to go up with him to Syria; she did, and stayed there until he was thirty; his prophethood lasted three years and then God raised him to Himself. Wahb claimed that sometimes as many as fifty thousand sick gathered around him in one gathering; whoever could reach him did, and for whoever could not, Jesus walked to him, and he treated them only by prayer to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 431} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 432}

**Telling what is eaten and stored.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: I inform you of what you eat that I did not see or witness with you when you ate it, and of what you lift up and hide and do not eat. This, he says, is part of Jesus' proof of his prophethood, together with the miracles he brings, the creating of the bird from clay, the healing of the blind and the leper and the raising of the dead by leave, which no human can do except one to whom God gave it as a sign of his truth, and his informing of the unseen which no human of his kind has any way to reach. The editor explains the grammar of the passage. Al-Tabari then raises an objection: what proof is in this, when we have seen astrologers and soothsayers report such things often and be right? He answers, and this is his own argument, that it is known of the astrologer and soothsayer to those they tell that they report what they derive by some means leading to the knowledge; this was not so of Jesus or the other prophets and messengers of God, who reported it without deriving it or seeking to know it by contrivance, but by God's informing them to begin with, with no earlier premise they followed, built on or resorted to, as the astrologer resorts to his computation and the soothsayer to his familiar; the editor says the familiar is a follower from the jinn whom the soothsayer sees, who becomes his companion and tells him falsehoods about the unseen. That, for al-Tabari, is the dividing line between the prophets' knowledge of the unseen and that of those who lie against God or claim such knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 432} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 433}

**The boy in the school.** Report 7099 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq) says that when Jesus was nine or ten or so his mother put him in the school, they claim, with a teacher of boys, and whenever the teacher started to teach him anything Jesus was ahead of him in knowing it, so the teacher said: do you not marvel at the widow's son? Whatever I start to teach him I find he knows better than I. The editor explains the word for the teacher. Report 7100 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says that when he grew his mother gave him over to learn the Torah, and he played with the boys of the village and told them what their fathers were doing. Report 7101 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Isma'il ibn Salim, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says that in the school he told them what they ate in their houses and what they stored; 7102 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hushaym, from Isma'il ibn Salim, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr) says he would say to a boy: your family have hidden such and such food for you, will you feed me from it? Al-Tabari says this is how the prophets and their proofs are: they bring what could be reached by some trick but not in the way others bring it, rather in a way creation knows cannot be reached by trick except through God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 433} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 434}

**The reports of the commentators.** Report 7103 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says the sense is what you ate last night and what you hid of it, Jesus saying it, and 7104 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) says the same. Report 7105 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) has Ata ibn Abi Rabah say it is the food and things they store in their houses, an unseen God taught him, and 7106 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says as Mujahid did. Report 7107 (Musa ibn Harun, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says Jesus told the boys with him in the school what their fathers did, what they put aside for them and what they ate; he would say to a boy, go, your family have put aside such and are eating such, and the boy would go and cry at his family until they gave him it, and they asked who told him and he said Jesus; so they kept their children from him, saying do not play with this sorcerer, and gathered them in a house; Jesus came looking, they said they were not there, and he asked what was in the house; they said pigs, and he said so may they be; they opened it and they were pigs. For this reporter this is the sense of [[passage/quran-5-78|5:78]]. The editor explains that weeping at his family means persisting in weeping. Report 7108 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) explains storing as what you hide for fear that the one who withholds will replace it; the editor says the wording of the print and manuscript has no sense, that he fears the letters are disordered, and that he left the manuscript's text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 434} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 435}

**The view of the table.** Al-Tabari says others held that the sense is what they eat of the table that comes down to them and what they store of it. Report 7109 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says when the people asked for the table it was a table coming down to them wherever they were, with fruit of Paradise, and they were commanded not to be dishonest in it, nor hide nor store for the next day, a trial God set them; whenever they did any of that Jesus told them. The editor says the print has a certainly wrong word and the manuscript is undotted, and that the lexicographers say it is not called a table unless food is on it. Report 7110 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says that when it came down they were bound to eat and not store; they stored and were dishonest and were turned into pigs, and it links this to [[passage/quran-5-115|5:115]]; Ibn Yahya also related from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada, from Khilas ibn Amr, from Ammar ibn Yasir the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 435} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 436}

**The form of the verb.** Al-Tabari says the root of the verb for storing is the reflexive form of the verb for laying by, with the letter doubled as in the verb for remembering, because the two letters, close in articulation, are hard to pronounce together, so one is assimilated into the other and both become a doubled letter midway between them (the editor says this is al-Farra's wording). Some Arabs let the other letter prevail, but the language of the recitation is the first and no other reading is allowed, since its transmission is widespread; it is the 'best' dialect. He cites a line of Zuhayr that the generous man gives freely and is sometimes wronged and so suffers the wrong, which is transmitted with two different letters; the editor says the right wording is that in the Diwan, preceded by lines blaming the miser and praising the generous man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 436} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 437}

**The close of the verse.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that in his creating the bird from clay by God's leave, in healing the blind and the leper, in raising the dead and in telling them what they eat and store with no calculation, astrology, soothsaying or divination, there is a lesson and a subject of thought, so they may conclude that he is right in saying he is a messenger from their Lord to them and know that he is truthful in what he calls them to of God's command; the condition means that they are believers in God's proofs and signs, acknowledging His oneness and His prophet Moses and the Torah he brought them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 437}
