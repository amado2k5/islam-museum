## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 129 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 129 (pp. 587-589)**

Al-Tabari says: if these of your people to whom you brought the truth from your Lord turn away, Muhammad, and turn their backs on you, not accepting the counsel for God you brought and the light and guidance you called them to, then say "God is enough for me", my Lord suffices; "there is no god but Him", no one else is worshipped; "in Him I put my trust", relying on His help and leaning on His support, for He is my helper against whoever opposes me and turns away from me, of you and of others; "and He is the Lord of the Mighty Throne", who owns all besides Him, and all kings are His possessions and servants. By describing Himself as Lord of the Mighty Throne he means that everything besides Him is His servant, in His dominion and authority, because a great throne belongs to kings; He described Himself as possessor of the Throne alone among creation and as the great King alone, and everything else is under His authority and message, subject to His rule and decree. The editor refers to earlier discussion of turning away, sufficiency, trust and the Throne. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 587}

Report 17511 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "if they turn away, say God is enough for me" refers to the unbelievers who turned from the Messenger, and this applies to the believers too. Report 17512 is from Ubayd ibn Umayr, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Uyayna and Amr: Umar would not record a verse in the mushaf until two men witnessed it; a man of the helpers came with these two verses "a Messenger has come to you from among yourselves", and Umar said he would never ask for proof of them, for that is how the Messenger was. Report 17513 is from Abu Salih al-Hanafi, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ahmad ibn Abdullah ibn Yunus, Zuhayr and al-A'mash: the Prophet said that God is merciful and loves every merciful person and places His mercy on every merciful one; they said, "Messenger of God, we are merciful to ourselves and our property," and he said it is not so, rather be as God said, "a Messenger has come to you from among yourselves", and he recited the two verses. The editor says this report is mursal and its men are trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 588}

Reports 17514 and 17515 are from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, through al-Muthanna, Abd al-Samad, Shu'ba, Ali ibn Zayd and Yusuf ibn Mihran from Ibn Abbas, and through Muslim ibn Ibrahim: the last verse revealed of the Qur'an was "a Messenger has come to you from among yourselves" to the end of the verse. Reports 17516 and 17517 are from him: the most recently revealed Qur'an is these two verses, through Ibn Waki', his father, Shu'ba, Ali ibn Zayd and Yusuf ibn Mihran, and through Abu Kurayb, Yunus ibn Muhammad, Aban ibn Yazid al-Attar and Qatada. The editor notes that Ali ibn Zayd ibn Jud'an has a weak memory, that Abdullah ibn Ahmad gave the report in his father's Musnad and al-Haythami cited it in the Majma al-Zawa'id, and that the last report is mursal because Qatada did not narrate from Ubayy ibn Ka'b. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 589}

With this the commentary on Surat at-Tawba ends in this volume; in the manuscript, a closing note by the copyist follows, saying the next volume begins with the commentary on the surah in which Yunus is mentioned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 589}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
