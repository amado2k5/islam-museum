## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when it is said to them: believe in what God has sent down, they say: we believe in what was sent down to us", al-Tabari says those meant are the Jews of the Children of Israel living around the prophet's place of emigration: told to believe in what God sent down of the Qur'an on Muhammad, they say they believe in what was sent down to them, the Torah. On "and they disbelieve in what is beyond it", he says "wara'ahu" here means "other than" and "after", that is, other than the Torah and the books of God after it, reported from Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya and al-Rabi'. On "and it is the truth confirming what they have", he says the Qur'an is meant, from al-Suddi, and that it is called confirming what they have because God's books confirm one another: the Gospel and the Qur'an enjoin following Muhammad and believing in him just as the Torah of Moses does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 348} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 349}

Al-Tabari continues that God told the Jews the Qur'an is the truth, agreeing with the book they have in what they deny, and that their denial of the Torah is like their denial of the Gospel and the Qur'an, from obstinacy and transgression against His messengers. On "say: why then did you kill God's prophets before, if you are believers?" he says those addressed are the Jews who said "we believe in what was sent down to us"; the question both refutes their claim and rebukes them (reported from al-Suddi), since God forbade killing prophets in their own book and commanded following them. To the question why the verb is in the present tense while the sense is past, he gives two grammarians' views: some Basrans say the sense is "why did you kill", citing al-Baqara 2:102 and lines of verse, while some Kufans say the present form is used of the past as a man rebukes another for what he did, and that the killers were their forebears, but they took them as allies and approved, so the deed was ascribed to them. Al-Tabari's choice is that God addressed the Jews who met the Prophet about what their forebears had done and ascribed it to them, as Arabs say "we did such and such to you on that day, and you did to us", meaning their ancestors; "before" means before today, and "if you are believers" means if you believe in what was sent down to you as you claim, then why do you approve the acts of the killers of prophets. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 349} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 350} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 351} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 352} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 353} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 354}
