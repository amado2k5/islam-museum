## Summary
Verse 133 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 133 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 133, "your Lord is the Self-sufficient, full of mercy; if He wills He can take you away and make whatever He wills succeed you, as He raised you from the offspring of another people", al-Tabari says: your Lord, Muhammad, who commanded His servants as He commanded and forbade them as He forbade, who rewarded obedience and punished disobedience, is "the Self-sufficient" without need of His servants, of their deeds and of their worship, while they are the ones in need of Him, because their life and death, their provision and food, their benefit and harm are in His hand; He did not create them, nor command or forbid them, out of need for them or their works, but to show them favour through His mercy and reward them for their good, for He is full of kindness and mercy. As for "if He wills He can take you away", if your Lord, who created His creation with no need of it, wills, He destroys these creatures of the children of Adam, "and makes whatever He wills succeed you" by bringing other creatures and nations to succeed you on earth after your passing and destruction, "as He raised you from the offspring of another people", as He brought you into being after other creatures who were before you. The notes refer to earlier explanations of "al-ghani", "al-rahma" and "idhhab". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 126}

The sense of "min" in this place is succession, as one says "I gave you a robe from your dinar", meaning in place of the dinar a robe, not that the robe is part of the dinar; so for those addressed by "as He raised you", it is not meant that they were raised from the loins of another people, but that they were raised in place of creatures, after other people who had perished before them. "Dhurriyya" is of the pattern "fu'liyya" from "dhara'a Allah al-khalq", meaning He created them, with the hamza later dropped; the editor's note explains "'ubbiyya" and corrects its vocalisation. A reading "dhurri'a" on the pattern "fu''ila" is reported from some early readers, and from another "dhirriyya" on the pattern "'illiyya"; al-Tabari says the reading used by the readers of the cities is "dhurriyya" with damma on the dhal and a doubled ya', on the pattern of "'ubbiyya". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 127}

The editor's note refers to the earlier explanation of "dhurriyya". The root sense of "insha'" is bringing into being; one says "so-and-so began to speak to the people", meaning he started and took it up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 128}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
