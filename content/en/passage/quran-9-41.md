**Commentary on verse 41 (pp. 262-267)**

The interpreters differed over the meaning of the "light" and the "heavy" for which God ordered whoever was one or the other to go out with him. Some said the lightness God meant here is youth and the heaviness old age. Report 16734, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd, Hukkam and Anbasa: the old and the young; report 16735, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki, Hafs and Amr: elders and youths; report 16736, from Abu Talha, through Ibn Uyayna, Ali ibn Zayd and Anas: on "go forth, light and heavy" he said the middle-aged and the young, and that he had never heard God excuse anyone; he went to Syria and fought until he died. The editor says Ali ibn Zayd is weak in hadith. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 262}

Report 16737, from al-Mughira ibn al-Nu'man, through Ibn Humayd, Hukkam and Anbasa: a man of Nakha' who was a stout old man wanted to fight, and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas forbade him, and he said God says "go forth, light and heavy"; Sa'd then let him, and the old man was killed; Umar later asked what had become of the old man who seemed to be of Banu Hashim, and they said he had been killed, Commander of the Faithful. The editor corrects that the man was of Nakha' and not of Banu Hashim. Reports 16738 and 16739, from Abu Salih and Ikrima: the young man and the old; reports 16740 and 16741, from al-Dahhak and Bishr ibn Atiyya: the middle-aged and the young. The editor gives the narrators and notes that Ibn Sa'd, al-Hakim, al-Suyuti and al-Haythami give the report of Anas about Abu Talha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 263}

Report 16742, from Muqatil ibn Hayyan, through al-Walid, Ali ibn Sahl and Bukayr ibn Ma'ruf: the young and the middle-aged. Report 16743, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the young and old, the rich and poor; report 16744, from al-Hasan, through Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id and Qatada: old men and young men. Report 16745, from Hibban ibn Zayd al-Shar'abi, through Sa'id ibn Amr, Baqiyya and Hariz: we went out with Safwan ibn Amr, who was governor of Hims, toward the Jarajima, and I met a very old man of Damascus, his eyebrows fallen over his eyes, on his mount among the raiders, and said uncle, God has excused you; he raised his eyebrows and said nephew, God called us out light and heavy, and whom God loves He tests, then restores and tests again; God tests only those of His servants who give thanks, are patient, remember Him and worship none but God. The editor says the Jarajima are the Nabateans of Syria and that Hariz ibn Uthman is reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 265}

Report 16746, from Abu Salih, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Isma'il: every old man and young man. Others said the sense is those who are busy and those who are not; report 16747, from al-Hakam, through Ibn Bashshar, Ibn Waki, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and Mansur: busy and not busy. Others said go out rich and poor; report 16748, from Abu Salih, through Ibn Humayd, Hukkam and Anbasa: rich and poor. Others said keen and not keen; report 16749, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his grandfather, and report 16750, from Qatada: keen and not keen. Others said riding and on foot; report 16751, from Abu Amr, through Ali ibn Sahl and al-Walid: when the going out was to the passes of Syria, people went out light, riding, and when it was to these coasts they went out light and heavy, riding and on foot. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 266}

Others said with an estate and without; report 16752, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the heavy is the one who has an estate and hates to lose it by going out, and the light is the one without an estate, and God said go forth light and heavy. Report 16753, from a man of Hadramawt, through Ibn Abd al-A'la, al-Mu'tamir and his father: it was told him that some people, one of them perhaps ill or old, said that if he kept away from going out out of reluctance he would be sinful, and God sent down "go forth, light and heavy". Report 16754, from Abu Ayyub, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya, Ayyub and Muhammad: Abu Ayyub was at Badr with the Messenger and then missed no Muslim campaign except one year, saying "go forth, light and heavy" and that he found himself only light or heavy. Report 16755, from Rashid ibn Sa'd, through Ali ibn Sahl, al-Walid ibn Muslim and Hariz, from one who saw al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad, the Messenger's horseman, on one of the money-changers' chests at Hims, his bulk overflowing it; I said God has excused you, and he said the sura of Bahuth, "go forth, light and heavy", refused us. The editor says Ibn Sa'd and al-Hakim give the report of Abu Ayyub and that the name of the sura is doubtful in the manuscript, to be explained later. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 267}

We stop here at the foot of p. 267, in the middle of the commentary on verse 41 (after report 16755); the next batch begins at p. 268. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 267}
