## Summary
Verse 40 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 40 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

The grammarians differed on the sense of "ara'aytakum". A Basran grammarian said: the kaf after the ta comes for address and the ta is left with fatha as it was for one person; it is like the kaf of "ruwaydaka zayd", with no place named by a particle, neither raised nor accusative, and in address like the kaf of "dhaka"; like it is the Arabs' "absiraka zayd", putting in the kaf for address. Others of them said: the sense of "ara'aytakum" is "ara'aytum", the kaf entering for address with emphasis and the ta alone being the noun, as the kaf that distinguishes one, two and many is put in "hadha, dhaka, tilka, ula'ika"; so too their "laysaka thumma illa zayd", "la siyyaka zayd", "bal-aka" for "bala", "la-bi'saka rajulan" and "la-ni'maka rajulan"; they said "unzuraka zayd ma asna'u bihi" and "absiraka ma asna'u bihi", meaning "look at him", and "absirakum" is reported, meaning "look", and from a man of Banu Kilab "ata'lamuka kana ahad ash'ar min Dhi al-Rumma?", entering the kaf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 351}

A Kufan grammarian said: with "ara'aytaka Amran" the usual speech is to drop the hamza, and the kaf in "ara'aytaka" is accusative, as if the origin were "did you see yourself in other than this state?"; this is dualised, pluralised and feminised, so one says "ara'aytuma-kuma", "ara'aytumukum" and "ara'aytunnakunna", putting its verb on himself and asking him about himself, then it grew common until they left the ta single for masculine, feminine, dual and plural, saying "ara'aytakum zaydan ma sana'a" and "ara'aytakunna ma sana'a", making the ta one and dualising and pluralising the kaf as a substitute for the ta, as in [[passage/quran-69-19|69:19]], "here, read my book", "ha'i, man", "ha'uma", then "hakum", the kaf and mim sufficing for what was dualised and pluralised; so the kaf is as if raised, being a substitute for the ta. It is sometimes made single for dual, plural, masculine and feminine, like the saying "'alayka zaydan", the kaf in the genitive and the sense raised. As for what is brought with it, most often it falls on nouns, then comes the interrogative, "ara'aytaka zaydan hal qama", since it came to mean "tell me about Zayd", then he explains what he asks about; this is the commonest speech, and the interrogative does not come right after it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 352}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, corrects "absiraka" with ba where print has nun, and keeps "ara'aytunnakunna" from the manuscript, which matches al-Farra.

They do not say "ara'aytaka hal qumta", because they want to make plain about whom one asks, and then the state asked about is made plain. Sometimes the condition comes without the noun, so they say "ara'ayta in ataytu zaydan hal ya'tina" and "ara'aytaka" too and "ara'aytu zaydan in ataytuhu hal ya'tina", when it means "tell me", and it is said in the three ways. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 353}

The editor says the printed text has "tunayha" for "yaliha", which is wrong, and "sometimes came with the report" where the manuscript has "with the condition", and that the publisher dropped "if I came" through poor judgement.

The sense of the words is: say, Muhammad, to these who hold idols and images equal to God: tell me, if God's punishment comes to you, people, like that which came to the peoples before you, some of whom perished by the quake and some by the thunderbolt, or the Hour comes to you in which you are raised from your graves and resurrected to the station of resurrection, will you call there on other than God to remove the trial that has come on you, or turn to your other gods in alarm to save you from the great trial that has come on you? "If you are truthful" means if you are right in your claim and your supposition that your gods whom you call besides God help or harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 353}

Verse 40 closes here at p. 353, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-41|6:41]].
