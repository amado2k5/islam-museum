## Summary
Verse 118 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 118 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of the commentary on verse 118: what an intimate is and what is forbidden (p. 138).** Al-Tabari says God addresses those who believed God and His messenger and acknowledged what their Prophet brought from his Lord, forbidding them to take as allies and friends, apart from their own religion and community, those who are not believers. The word for lining is used as a figure for a man's close friend, likened to the part of the garment next to the belly, because the friend stands to a man's secrets, which he keeps from his remoter and many of his nearer kin, as the garment stands to the body. God therefore forbade believers to take disbelievers as close friends, then made known their hidden deceit, treachery and wish to do them harm, warned against them and forbade befriending them. In a note the editor says the printed text altered this sentence by merging a letter and dropping another, the manuscript has a conjunction before the preposition, he kept what reads best, unless a verb of forbidding has dropped out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 138} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 139}

**The root of the word for failing to do something, the word for ruin, and the editor's objection (pp. 139-140).** Al-Tabari takes the negated verb to mean that they are unable to do something (the verb is from a root that he glosses as "to be able"), and cites a line of Abu al-Ayyal al-Hudhali describing a creature that cannot see at noon. He then says the intimate circle in question will not leave off its effort in what brings the believers ruin. For the root of that word he gives corruption, a sense used in many ways, and cites an unchained saying of the Prophet (7679): whoever is afflicted with that word or with wounds. As to the last phrase of the verse he takes it as wishing hardship on the believers in their religion and wishing what harms and does not please them, and the editor refers to the earlier treatment of that word in volume 4 (pp. 358-361). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 139} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 140}

The editor's note on p. 139 names the poet as Abu al-Ayyal al-Hudhali, lists the sources of the line (the Hudhalis' collected poems, al-Jahiz's book on animals, al-Ma'ani al-Kabir and the lexicon Lisan al-Arab), and gives the setting: an exchange of verse with Badr ibn Amir al-Hudhali after news reached the poet that a nephew had sided with his opponents, which Badr denied and Abu al-Ayyal rejected. He glosses the feminine adjective for one who cannot see in the sun and the verb for being poor, and explains the lines as saying that praise and verse prove empty once matters come to light. The note ends with a further line of the poem, which is not reproduced here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 139}

In a note on p. 140 the editor objects to al-Tabari: he says al-Tabari went far in the ingenuity of his explanation, that the lexicographers' account of the word is truer and fuller, since they give the sense al-Tabari gives but also say that one phrase means "I did not manage it" and the other "I did not refrain from doing it", and that it is among the words with opposite senses, one meaning to weaken and slacken and the other to exert oneself; he sends the reader to the Arabic lexicons. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 140}

On the saying 7679 the editor notes that al-Tabari gave it without a chain and that Ahmad in the Musnad and al-Bayhaqi in his Sunan have it. Ahmad's route is from his teacher Muhammad ibn Salama al-Harrani and from Yazid ibn Harun, both from Ibn Ishaq, from al-Harith ibn Fudayl, from Sufyan ibn Abi al-Awja al-Sulami, from Abu Shurayh al-Khuza'i, with the wording: whoever is afflicted with bloodshed or with the word for wounds has a choice among three, either retaliation, or taking the blood-money, or pardon; if he wants a fourth, restrain his hand; and if he does any of that and then transgresses again and kills, the fire awaits him, there to remain. This is fiqh as transmitted, and the museum gives no verdict on it. The editor glosses bloodshed as killing a person and the other word as wounds or the severing of a limb, says he left al-Tabari's text as it stands and marked by punctuation that the second word looks like a doubtful alternative wording of the narrator, and judges it likelier that it means "that is, wounds", since the saying itself explains the word by wounds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 140}

**The occasion of revelation and the reports on who the intimates are (7680-7690, pp. 140-144).** Al-Tabari relates that the verse was revealed about some Muslims who mixed with their allies among the Jews and among the hypocrites of the Jews and gave them sincere affection because of ties from the pre-Islamic period, and God forbade them this and forbade them to take them into their confidence in any of their affairs. He then relates the following. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 140}

- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Muhammad ibn Ishaq, Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas (7680): some Muslim men kept up ties with Jewish men because of neighbourliness and alliance in the pre-Islamic period, and the verse was revealed about them forbidding close intimacy for fear of discord from them. The editor notes the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2:207) and follows reports 7644 and 7645 above, and that the printed text has a stray letter at the start of the word for "forbade", the right reading being the manuscript's and Ibn Hisham's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 141}
- From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7681): this concerns the hypocrites of Medina; God forbade the believers to ally themselves with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 141}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7682): God forbade the believers to take hypocrites into their confidence, or to fraternize with them, or to ally with them in preference to believers. The editor glosses the verb as taking them as close friends, says dictionaries omitted that form though it is pure Arabic, and notes a variation between print and manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 141}
- From Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, Ibn Abbas (7683): they are the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 141}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7684): do not take the hypocrites into your confidence by allying with them over the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 142}
- From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7686): the intimates are the hypocrites. From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7687): a believer is not to take a hypocrite into his confidence in preference to his brother. From Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Ibn Zayd (7688): these are the hypocrites, and he cited a later phrase of the same verse in support. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 143}

**The saying of Anas and al-Hasan on the light of polytheists and engraving on rings (7685, pp. 142-143).** From Abu Kurayb and Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Hushaym, al-Awwam ibn Hawshab, al-Azhar ibn Rashid, Anas ibn Malik: the Messenger of God said: do not seek light from the fire of the people of polytheism, and do not engrave Arabic on your rings. Anas says they did not know what that meant until they came to al-Hasan and asked him. He said that engraving means not to engrave the name Muhammad on rings, and that seeking light from the polytheists' fire means not to consult them in any of one's affairs; al-Hasan added that its confirmation is in God's Book and recited this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 142}

The editor's long note on this report (p. 142, completed on p. 143) says that al-Azhar ibn Rashid al-Basri is trustworthy, that al-Bukhari in al-Tarikh al-Kabir and Ibn Abi Hatim give an entry for him with no criticism, and that a different narrator of the same name exists, al-Azhar ibn Rashid al-Kahili of Kufa, later in date: Hushaym's informant al-Awwam ibn Hawshab (d. 148 AH) narrates from the Basran, and Marwan ibn Mu'awiya al-Fazari (d. 193 AH) from the Kufan. He says al-Mizzi erred in Tahdhib al-Kamal in recording that Abu Hatim called the Basran unknown, followed by Ibn Hajar in Tahdhib al-Tahdhib and by al-Dhahabi in al-Mizan, and that it added to the confusion to say Ibn Ma'in called him weak; both sayings concerned the Kufan. He quotes Ibn Abi Hatim's entry for al-Kahili (no. 1180): Ibn Ma'in said the Azhar from whom Marwan ibn Mu'awiya narrated is weak, and his father said he is unknown. The editor adds that Ibn Hajar did not verify and said in the second entry that he feared the two were one, though Ibn Ma'in separated them, and that the difference between them is as clear as the sun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 142}

He goes on to say that Ahmad has the saying in the Musnad (no. 11978) from Hushaym by this chain without al-Hasan's comment, and so does al-Bukhari in al-Tarikh al-Kabir from Musaddad from Hushaym, who explained that the Arabic in question means the words "Muhammad, Messenger of God", that is, do not write what is on the Prophet's seal. Abu Ya'la narrated it at length with al-Hasan's words from Ishaq ibn Isra'il from Hushaym, quoted by Ibn Kathir (2:227), who then says that al-Nasa'i narrated it from Mujahid ibn Musa from Hushaym, that Ahmad has it likewise without al-Hasan al-Basri's interpretation, and that this interpretation is open to question. The editor did not find it in al-Nasa'i's Sunan and thinks it may be in the larger collection. Al-Suyuti (2:66) adds attribution to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi's Shu'ab al-Iman, and does not attribute it to al-Nasa'i or to al-Bukhari's Tarikh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 143}

**Two views on the word for hardship, and a grammatical objection (7689, 7690, pp. 143-144).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the wish for hardship. Some said it means they wish that you go astray from your religion; from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad, Asbat, al-Suddi (7689). Others said it means they wish that you fall into hardship in your religion; from al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7690). The editor refers to the earlier treatment of that word in volume 4. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 144}

Al-Tabari then poses a hypothetical objection: how is the report on the intimates given in the past tense in the place of a circumstantial clause after the report is complete, when such clauses take present or future forms and not past ones? He answers that the phrase is not a circumstantial clause on the intimates but a second statement about them, cut off from the first, so that the meaning is: do not take intimates whose description is such and such, and whose description is such and such, the second not joined to the first though both describe one person. He notes that some grammarians claimed it belongs to the relative clause of the intimates, but that this was already completed by the previous phrase so there is no ground for a second after the noun is completed, and he affirms his own view that it is a fresh predicate on them, neither the same as the first nor a circumstantial clause nor a cut. The editor refers to earlier discussions of the cut and of the relative clause in the indexes of technical terms, and notes that the relative clause is a qualifier of an indefinite. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 144}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 144}

**What it means that hatred has shown from their mouths (p. 145).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that the hatred of those whom the believers are forbidden to take as intimates has shown through their tongues, and what shows from them through their tongues is their persisting in disbelief and their enmity to whoever opposes their error. This is among the firmest causes of their enmity to the people of faith, since it is enmity over religion, and enmity over religion ends only when one of the two parties goes over to the other's creed, which for the one who moves is a move from guidance to what was, in his view, error. Their showing it to the believers and persisting in it was thus the clearest sign to the people of faith of what they held toward them. The editor notes that manuscript and print both read the mouths with a preposition and that he kept the reading that matches the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 145}

**Another view: the showing is between the hypocrites and their disbelieving brothers (7691, 7692, pp. 145-146).** Some said the meaning is that their hatred of the believers showed to their allies among the hypocrites and disbelievers through each informing the other, and those who held this said the verse means the hypocrites, not those who openly professed disbelief among the Jews and polytheists. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7691): hatred showed from the mouths of the hypocrites to their brothers among the disbelievers, from their deceit toward Islam and its people and their hatred of them. From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7692): from the mouths of the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 145}

**Al-Tabari's rejection of that view and his account of who is meant (pp. 145-146).** Al-Tabari holds that Qatada's saying has no sense. His argument is that God forbade the believers only to take as intimates those whom they knew to bear deceit and hatred for Islam and its people, either by clear evidence of that in their character or by those described openly showing enmity and hostility. As for those whose identity was not established, it is not allowed that the believers be forbidden to befriend them unless first made to know them, by person and name or by attributes they knew them by. The hypocrites' showing their hearts to their disbelieving brothers does not let the believers know their state while the hypocrites profess faith to them and court them; so it is clear that those forbidden as intimates are those whose hatred showed through their tongues as God described, whom the believers recognized by that description, and whom God described as people of the fire, among the People of the Book who had a pledge and covenant from the Messenger of God and his Companions. He adds that if they were the hypocrites the matter would be as he had explained, and if they were open enemies at war the believers would not take them as intimates given their different lands and cities, so they are the People of the Book who lived among the believers in the days of the Messenger and held his covenant, from the Jews of the Children of Israel. In a note on p. 146 the editor says print has a meaningless word, the manuscript an unpointed shape, and the right reading is the verb meaning to know a person truly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 145} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 146}

**Ibn Mas'ud's reading and masculine or feminine verbs (pp. 146-147).** Al-Tabari says the word for hatred is a verbal noun and that it is reported that in the reading of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud its verb is masculine; this is allowed though the word is feminine in form, because the femininity of verbal nouns is not obligatory, so what goes with them may be masculine or feminine. He gives verses where the verb is feminine in one and masculine in another: [[passage/quran-11-67|11:67]], [[passage/quran-11-94|11:94]], [[passage/quran-6-157|6:157]], and a verse in al-A'raf for which the editor's bracketed numbers, as they appear in the source, point to [[passage/quran-7-73|7:73]] and [[passage/quran-7-85|7:85]]. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:231). Al-Tabari adds that the hatred showed through their tongues because what is meant is the speech that showed to the believers from their mouths. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 146} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 147}

**What the breasts conceal is greater (7693, 7694, p. 147).** Al-Tabari says what the breasts of those you were forbidden to take as intimates conceal is greater and graver than the hatred that showed to you through their tongues. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7693): what their breasts hide is greater than what they showed with their tongues. From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7694): what their breasts hold is greater than what they showed with their tongues. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 147}

**The close of verse 118: the signs and reasoning (p. 148).** Al-Tabari says God has made clear to the believers the lessons in the affair of these Jews whom they were forbidden to take as intimates, from which they may take heed, if they understand from God His admonitions, commands and prohibitions and know where they benefit and what comes back to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 148}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 148}
