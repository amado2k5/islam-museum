## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and beware of a Day when no soul will avail another in anything" al-Tabari says the sense is: beware of a day on which no soul will avail another at all, and allows also that it means "a day which no soul will make good", with the returning pronoun to "day" dropped, citing a line of rajaz. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 26}

Al-Tabari continues on "no soul will avail another in anything": the omitted word is, for him, the pronoun or "in it", clear from the wording; some grammarians insisted on one or the other, but he holds that omitting what the wording shows is permitted. The sense is a warning from God to His servants of His punishment on the Day of Resurrection, a day when no soul avails another and no father avails his child. He reports from al-Suddi that "tajzi" means "suffices". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 27}
He says the root of "jaza'" in Arabic is settling and compensating, and sets out the linguists' differences between "jaza" and "ajza'a", whether they mean the same, with some attributing "ajza'tu" to the dialect of Tamim. So the meaning is: no soul will settle anything for another or suffice for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 27} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 28}
He explains that today one may pay a child's, parent's or friend's debt, while in the Hereafter the settling of rights is by good and bad deeds. He gives, by three chains, the report from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said: "May God have mercy on a servant who owes his brother a wrong, in honour, wealth or standing, and asks his pardon before it is taken from him, when there is no dinar or dirham there; if he has good deeds they take from them, and if he has none they load him with their bad deeds". The editor calls it sound and traces it to al-Tirmidhi, al-Bukhari and Ahmad. He also gives the report from Ibn Abbas: "Let none of you die in debt, for there is no dinar or dirham there; they only divide good and bad deeds", with a sound chain according to the editor, and then a report from Anas like Abu Hurayra's, of whose chain the editor says he could not resolve a problem. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 28} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 29} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 30}

Al-Tabari adds that one who would be glad to keep a claim over his child or father and be paid it cannot be expected to settle another's debt. He rejects the view of some Basran grammarians that "no soul will avail another" means no soul will be adequate to stand in its place, saying it is not intelligible in Arabic to say "you have not availed me at all" meaning you have not stood in my place, and that the sound wording of the revelation with "anything" shows it wrong; the editor comments on the quality of this linguistic reasoning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 30} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 31}
On "and no intercession will be accepted from it" al-Tabari says "shafa'a" is a verbal noun from "so-and-so interceded for me with so-and-so", meaning asking him to meet a need, and the intercessor is so called because he doubles the one who seeks his help, making him a pair after being single. So the meaning is: beware a day when no soul will pay for another what is owed to God or anyone else, nor will God accept the intercession of any intercessor so as to excuse what is owed. It is said that God addressed the people of this verse so because they were Jews of the Children of Israel who said "we are God's children and beloved, and our fathers will intercede for us", and He told them no soul avails another and no intercession is accepted until everyone entitled has his right in full. He reports from Uthman ibn Affan a saying of the Prophet that the hornless animal will take redress from the horned on the Day of Resurrection; the editor calls the chain weak because of Hajjaj ibn Nusayr, while the meaning is established through Abu Hurayra in Ahmad, Muslim and al-Tirmidhi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 30} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 31} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 32}
Al-Tabari's own view is that God took away the hope they had of being saved by their fathers' intercession while rejecting the truth, unless they repented of disbelief, and made this a rule for all who follow their path; and that the verse is general in wording but particular in meaning, because of the reports that the Prophet said "my intercession is for the people of major sins among my community" and "every prophet was given a prayer, and I have kept mine in reserve as intercession for my community". He gives both without a chain, and the editor traces them through Anas and Jabir in the hadith books. He concludes that God may pardon His believing servants much of the punishment for their wrongs through His prophet's intercession, and that "no intercession will be accepted from it" refers to one who died in disbelief without repenting. He adds that this is not the place for a long treatment of intercession, promise and warning, which he will take up where it belongs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 32} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 33}
On "nor will any ransom be taken from it" he says "'adl" with fatha on the ayn is a ransom in Arab usage. He reports from Abu al-Aliya that it means ransom, from al-Suddi that even if it brought the earth full of gold to ransom itself it would not be accepted, the same from Qatada and Ibn Zayd, from Ibn Abbas "exchange", which is ransom, and, in a report from a man of Banu Umayya, that the Prophet was asked what "'adl" is and said it is ransom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 33}

Al-Tabari adds that a ransom is called "'adl" because it balances the thing though of a different kind and stands as its equal in recompense rather than in form, as in the verse of surah al-An'am. "'Idl" with kasra on the ayn is a match of the same kind, like a camel's load balanced on its back; when its value in a different kind is meant, the ayn takes fatha. He gives a report from a man of Banu Umayya that "'adl" is ransom, which the editor calls weak, being mursal or broken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 33} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 34} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 35}
On "nor will they be helped" he says no helper will help them that day, as no intercessor will intercede, and no ransom be accepted: favouritism is gone, bribes and intercessions are void, and mutual aid and support are lifted, and judgement passes to the just and mighty One before whom intercessors and helpers are of no avail. He cites the verse of surah al-Saffat and reports from Ibn Abbas on "why do you not help one another" that it means why do you not defend us, which is out of reach for you today. He records a view that it means they will have no helper from God to rescue them when He punishes them, and another that it concerns appeal, intercession and ransom. Al-Tabari's own choice is the first, because God told those addressed that the Day of Resurrection has no ransom for one deserving punishment, no intercession and no helper, though these existed for them in the world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 35} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 36}
