## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and beware of a Day when no soul will avail another in anything" al-Tabari says the sense is: beware of a day on which no soul will avail another at all, and allows also that it means "a day which no soul will make good", with the returning pronoun to "day" dropped, citing a line of rajaz. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 26}

Al-Tabari continues on "no soul will avail another in anything": the omitted word is, for him, the pronoun or "in it", clear from the wording; some grammarians insisted on one or the other, but he holds that omitting what the wording shows is permitted. The sense is a warning from God to His servants of His punishment on the Day of Resurrection, a day when no soul avails another and no father avails his child. He reports from al-Suddi that "tajzi" means "suffices". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 27}
He says the root of "jaza'" in Arabic is settling and compensating, and sets out the linguists' differences between "jaza" and "ajza'a", whether they mean the same, with some attributing "ajza'tu" to the dialect of Tamim. So the meaning is: no soul will settle anything for another or suffice for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 27} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 28}
He explains that today one may pay a child's, parent's or friend's debt, while in the Hereafter the settling of rights is by good and bad deeds. He gives, by three chains, the report from Abu Hurayra that the Prophet said: "May God have mercy on a servant who owes his brother a wrong, in honour, wealth or standing, and asks his pardon before it is taken from him, when there is no dinar or dirham there; if he has good deeds they take from them, and if he has none they load him with their bad deeds". The editor calls it sound and traces it to al-Tirmidhi, al-Bukhari and Ahmad. He also gives the report from Ibn Abbas: "Let none of you die in debt, for there is no dinar or dirham there; they only divide good and bad deeds", with a sound chain according to the editor, and then a report from Anas like Abu Hurayra's, of whose chain the editor says he could not resolve a problem. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 28} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 29} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 30}
