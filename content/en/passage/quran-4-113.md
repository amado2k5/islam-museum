## Summary
Verse 113 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 113 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 113: God's favour to the Prophet (pp. 199-201, notes 1-5 on p. 199, notes 1-2 on p. 200 and note 1 on p. 201).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: were it not that God was gracious to you, Muhammad, and protected you by His grant of success and by making plain to you the affair of this traitor, so that you held back from arguing for him and from defending the wrongdoers against those in the right, a group of them, that is of these who betray themselves, would have intended to make you err, to slip from the path of truth, by their confusing the traitor's affair for him, their testimony before him that the traitor was innocent of what was claimed, and their asking him to excuse him and to uphold his excuse among his companions. God says that those who intended to lead the Prophet astray from the due judgement in the affair of the man who betrayed his neighbour's coat of mail only lead themselves astray. If someone asks how they led themselves astray, he answers that they took themselves along paths that God had not allowed. God had earlier in His Book, on His Messenger's tongue, forbidden cooperating in sin and aggression and commanded cooperating in what is right; so for anyone who strove in the affair of the traitors it was due that he help those they wronged, not the traitors against those who contended with them before the Prophet for their rights; their striving in the traitors' help was therefore a path not God's, and this is the misleading of themselves. The words that they will not harm the Prophet at all mean that God keeps him firm and straight and clarifies to him the affair of those who sought to mislead him, and will expose him and them. On the Book and wisdom, al-Tabari says that among God's favours to him He sent down the Book, the Qur'an in which is the clarification of everything, guidance and admonition, and with the Book wisdom, which is what the Book mentions in summary of its lawful and unlawful, command and prohibition, rulings, promise and warning, and taught him what he did not know of the report of the first and last, what was and what is to be; all of this is from God's favour since He created him, so he should thank Him by holding to obedience and hastening to His pleasure, keeping to what was sent down in the Book and wisdom, and opposing whoever tries to lead him from God's way, for God is the one who takes him in charge and protects him from the evil of whoever wishes him harm, as He protected him from the group who intended to mislead him in this matter, and none other can save him from harm if God wills it, should he oppose God in some command or prohibition and follow the whim of one trying to bar him. Al-Tabari adds that this verse is God's warning to His Prophet of the place of his error and a reminder to him of what is due from him to God. The editor's notes: references to earlier explanations of calumny (p. 197, note 4), of the words for grace, for a group (volume 1, p. 141) and for leading astray (volume 8, p. 507), and of wisdom (volume 5, pp. 15 and 211, and volume 3, pp. 87-88); that the printed text had "he explains the matter of his deed" where the manuscript is right; that the printed text and the manuscript read "and what was and what is to be before that is of God's favour", which is not straight, and he corrected it to "so all that"; and that the printed text has a word for "the place of his luck" where the undotted manuscript is read as "his error", which suits the story. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 201} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 201}
