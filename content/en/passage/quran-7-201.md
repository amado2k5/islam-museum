**Commentary on verse 201 (pp. 333-337)**

Al-Tabari says: "those who fear God" among His creatures and so dread His punishment by performing His obligations and avoiding disobedience to Him, "when a touch of Satan touches them, they remember", meaning when a visitation from Satan comes upon them, of anger or other things that turn from what they owe of God's right, they remember God's punishment and reward, His promise and warning, see the truth and act on it, come to obedience to God in what He made obligatory and leave obedience to Satan in it. The reciters differed over "taif": most reciters of Medina and Kufa read "ta'if" on the pattern of "fa'il", and some Meccans, Basrans and Kufans read "taif". The people of knowledge of Arab speech differed over the difference between "ta'if" and "taif": a Basran said they are the same, being like an apparition or something that visits you, and that "taif" may be a lightened form of "tayyif", like "mayyit" and "mayt"; a Kufan said "ta'if" is what goes round you of Satan's whispering, while "taif" is of a touch and contact; another of them said "taif" is a touch and "ta'if" is anything that goes round a person; and it is reported from Abu Amr ibn al-Ala that he said "taif" is whispering. The editor says al-Tabari ascribed the first to the Basrans while the Lisan al-Arab ascribes it to al-Farra, a Kufan, and he did not find it in the printed Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 333} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 334}

Al-Tabari chooses: the better of the two readings is that of those who read "ta'if from Satan", because the interpreters interpreted it as meaning anger and the slip that come from the one going round him; since that is the sense and "taif" is a verbal noun from "he went round", it is a report from God of what touches those who fear Him from Satan, and what touches them is what goes round them of his means, such as anger and whispering; Satan goes round the child of Adam only to make him slip from obedience to his Lord or to whisper to him, and the whispering and the causing to slip is the "ta'if from Satan". There follows in the text a passage on "taif" being an apparition, a verbal noun from "it went round", a speaker saying he had not heard "taif" as a verb and taking it to mean "mayyit" from the letter waw, and the report of the Basrans and some Kufans that they heard "taif" as a verb from the Arabs, and they recite a line by Ka'b ibn Zuhayr asking how the apparition visited you, its visiting being a remembrance and a yearning for you. The editor says this passage, from "as for taif" to the line of verse, was put in the wrong place, since the later "he says I did not hear" belongs to a speaker other than Abu Ja'far without doubt, that he left it where it stands and set it apart though its place was before "Abu Ja'far said: the better of the two readings", that its text is Abu Ubayda's in the Majaz al-Qur'an, and that "shu'uf" is a verbal noun from "love of so-and-so burned his heart". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 334} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 335}

As for the interpretation, they differed. Some said the "ta'if" is anger, and report 15555, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "taif" is anger; reports 15556 to 15559, from Mujahid: it is anger. Others said it is the touch and the slip from Satan, and report 15560, from Ibn Abbas: the "ta'if" is a touch from Satan, "then they see clearly"; report 15561, from Ibn Abbas: an incitement from Satan, they remember; report 15562, from al-Suddi: when they slip they repent. The editor notes that in the reports the manuscript has "ta'if" at times and "taif" at others, the two readings. Then al-Tabari says: these two interpretations are close in sense, because anger is of Satan's causing to slip and the touch is also of sin from him, and all of it is of Satan's "ta'if"; so there is no reason to restrict one sense to the exclusion of another, but right to make it general as God made it general, saying that those who fear God, when something from the means of Satan, whatever it be, comes upon them, remember God's command and come to His command. As for "then they see clearly", it means they see God's guidance, His explanation and obedience in it, and so stop what the visitation of Satan called them to; report 15563, from Ibn Abbas: they stop disobedience, hold to God's command and disobey Satan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 335} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 336} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 337}
