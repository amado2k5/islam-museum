## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "And this worldly life is nothing but amusement and play, and indeed the Hereafter belongs to animals, if only they knew": "And what is this worldly life" that these polytheists enjoy, "except amusement and play," meaning: except the souls are justified by what they delight in, then it is near, with no permanence or permanence. "And indeed, the Hereafter is the animal," meaning: and the Hereafter is encompassed by eternal life that has no perishation, cessation, or death. Also: And from Qatadah, His saying: "And indeed, the Hereafter is the animal, if they knew" is a life in which there is no death. And from Ibn Abi Nujayh, from Mujahid, regarding the phrase "it is the animal," he said: there is no death in it. And he said: Muawiyah narrated to us, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas, regarding the phrase "And the Hereafter is the animal," he says: it is everlasting. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid.
