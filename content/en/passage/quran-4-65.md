## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The denial and the oath (p. 518).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that the matter is not as they claim, of believing what was sent down to you while they take their cases to the taghut and turn from you when called to you; then God opens an oath by your Lord, Muhammad, that they do not truly accept Him, you or what was sent to you until they make you the judge in what has become tangled among their affairs and whose ruling is unclear to them. He explains that the verb is used when people differ in speech or matter, with nouns such as the quarrel. He says that not finding constriction over what he decreed means their souls are not narrowed or burdened with sin by rejecting his decree, doubting obedience to him, or by thinking that what he decided between them is anything but right, which they may not contradict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 518}

**The meaning of constriction (pp. 518-519, reports 9908-9911).** Al-Muthanna (9908, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it means doubt; Ibn Humayd (9909, from Hukkam, from Anbasa, from Muhammad b. Abd al-Rahman, from al-Qasim b. Abi Bazza) from Mujahid, doubt; Muhammad b. Amr (9910, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid likewise; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9911, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) from al-Dahhak, sin. Al-Tabari says full submission means submitting to his decree and judgement in obedience and acknowledgement of his prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 519}

**The view: it came down about al-Zubayr (p. 519, report 9912, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom the verse means and about whom it came down. Some said it came down about al-Zubayr b. al-Awwam and an opponent of the Ansar who brought a matter before the Prophet. Yunus b. Abd al-A'la (9912, from Ibn Wahb, from Yunus and al-Layth b. Sa'd, from Ibn Shihab) reports that Urwa b. al-Zubayr told him, from Abdullah b. al-Zubayr, from al-Zubayr, that he disputed with a man of the Ansar who had been at Badr with the Prophet over channels of water from the Harra with which both watered their palms; the Ansari said, let the water pass, and al-Zubayr refused, and the Prophet said, water your land, Zubayr, then send the water to your neighbour; the Ansari grew angry and said, because he is your aunt's son? and the face of the Prophet changed colour, and the report continues on the next page. In his notes the editor says shiraj is the plural of a word for a water-course from the Harra to the plain; that the Harra is a known place in Medina of black stony ground; that the print wrote the word for pasture without the hamza, a misleading error; that releasing the water means the water ran through al-Zubayr's land before the Ansari's and he held it back to water his own; that "an" here gives a reason, "because he is your aunt's son"; and that al-Zubayr's mother, Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib, was the Prophet's paternal aunt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 519}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 519 in the commentary on verse 65 of sura 4, in report 9912 on the dispute of al-Zubayr and the Ansari over irrigating their palms (the report has not yet ended), and continue from p. 520. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 519}
