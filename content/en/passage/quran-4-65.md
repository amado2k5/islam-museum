## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The denial and the oath (p. 518).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that the matter is not as they claim, of believing what was sent down to you while they take their cases to the taghut and turn from you when called to you; then God opens an oath by your Lord, Muhammad, that they do not truly accept Him, you or what was sent to you until they make you the judge in what has become tangled among their affairs and whose ruling is unclear to them. He explains that the verb is used when people differ in speech or matter, with nouns such as the quarrel. He says that not finding constriction over what he decreed means their souls are not narrowed or burdened with sin by rejecting his decree, doubting obedience to him, or by thinking that what he decided between them is anything but right, which they may not contradict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 518}

**The meaning of constriction (pp. 518-519, reports 9908-9911).** Al-Muthanna (9908, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it means doubt; Ibn Humayd (9909, from Hukkam, from Anbasa, from Muhammad b. Abd al-Rahman, from al-Qasim b. Abi Bazza) from Mujahid, doubt; Muhammad b. Amr (9910, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid likewise; Yahya b. Abi Talib (9911, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) from al-Dahhak, sin. Al-Tabari says full submission means submitting to his decree and judgement in obedience and acknowledgement of his prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 519}

**The view: it came down about al-Zubayr (p. 519, report 9912, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom the verse means and about whom it came down. Some said it came down about al-Zubayr b. al-Awwam and an opponent of the Ansar who brought a matter before the Prophet. Yunus b. Abd al-A'la (9912, from Ibn Wahb, from Yunus and al-Layth b. Sa'd, from Ibn Shihab) reports that Urwa b. al-Zubayr told him, from Abdullah b. al-Zubayr, from al-Zubayr, that he disputed with a man of the Ansar who had been at Badr with the Prophet over channels of water from the Harra with which both watered their palms; the Ansari said, let the water pass, and al-Zubayr refused, and the Prophet said, water your land, Zubayr, then send the water to your neighbour; the Ansari grew angry and said, because he is your aunt's son? and the face of the Prophet changed colour, and the report continues on the next page. In his notes the editor says shiraj is the plural of a word for a water-course from the Harra to the plain; that the Harra is a known place in Medina of black stony ground; that the print wrote the word for pasture without the hamza, a misleading error; that releasing the water means the water ran through al-Zubayr's land before the Ansari's and he held it back to water his own; that "an" here gives a reason, "because he is your aunt's son"; and that al-Zubayr's mother, Safiyya bint Abd al-Muttalib, was the Prophet's paternal aunt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 519}

**Continuation of report 9912 (p. 520, notes 1-4).** The report goes on: the Prophet then said, water, Zubayr, then hold the water until it reaches the barriers, then send it to your neighbour; so he gave Zubayr his full right (in the report's wording al-Tabari says a different verb is the correct one; the editor understands him to mean the correct wording of this report, not to deny that the verb used is sound Arabic, meaning to take a right in full). Before that the Prophet had advised Zubayr a course meant to be gentle with him and with the Ansari; when the Ansari angered him he gave Zubayr his full right in a plain judgement. Zubayr said he thought the verse came down only about this. The editor explains that the barriers are the earthen dividers that hold water and that the verb for "angered" means to make angry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 520}

**The editor's tracing of report 9912 (pp. 520-521, note 4).** The editor, Ahmad Shakir, says the chain reads as Zubayr b. al-Awwam's own account, or possibly Abdullah b. al-Zubayr telling the story; other versions read as Urwa b. al-Zubayr relating the story himself, which looks like a report skipping its source, and others as Urwa relating from his father. He lists those who give it by al-Tabari's own chain through Ibn Wahb: Ibn Abi Hatim (as quoted by Ibn Kathir), Ibn al-Jarud in al-Muntaqa, and al-Isma'ili (as quoted by Ibn Hajar in the Fath); al-Nasa'i, from two teachers, Yunus b. Abd al-A'la and al-Harith b. Miskin, both from Ibn Wahb; all say that Abdullah b. al-Zubayr reported from Zubayr. He lists Ahmad's Musnad version (through al-Layth, from Ibn Shihab, from Urwa, from Abdullah b. al-Zubayr) and says al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn Majah and Ibn Hibban give it through al-Layth b. Sa'd, from al-Zuhri, from Urwa, from Abdullah b. al-Zubayr, as a told story. He quotes al-Bukhari that only al-Layth names "Urwa from Abdullah", and says Ibn Hajar answered him from al-Nasa'i and others, whose version matches al-Tabari's, that Yunus b. Yazid al-Ayli also names it, and that Ibn Wahb added "from his father al-Zubayr". He then gives the version of Ahmad and al-Bukhari through Abu al-Yaman, from Shu'ayb, from al-Zuhri, where Urwa says Zubayr used to relate it, which reads as Urwa from his father directly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 521}

**The editor on Urwa hearing from his father (p. 521).** The editor quotes Ibn Kathir that the Musnad version is broken between Urwa and his father, who he says Urwa did not hear, the sure thing being that he heard it from his brother Abdullah. He replies, as he did in his commentary on the Musnad, that this is Zubayr's report and it is not far-fetched that both sons heard it from him, and that Urwa also heard it from his brother or had his brother confirm it; the claim that Urwa did not hear from his father is refuted by the evidence, since he was an adolescent or adult, about thirteen, when his father was killed. He cites al-Tahdhib quoting Muslim b. al-Hajjaj in his book al-Tamyiz that Urwa performed the pilgrimage with Uthman and memorised from his father and from Companions after him; al-Bukhari's entry on Urwa in al-Tarikh al-Kabir stating that he heard his father, A'isha and Abdullah b. Umar; another report in Ahmad's Musnad through Hisham b. Urwa, from Urwa, "my father Zubayr told me", whose chain he calls sound and which states the hearing outright; and Ibn Hajar's remark in the Fath that al-Bukhari accepted the report despite this difference, relying on the soundness of Urwa's hearing from his father. Urwa also told it on his own account without naming his brother or father, so it appears mursal, as in the next report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 521}

**Report 9913 (pp. 521-522, note 1).** Ya'qub (9913, from Isma'il b. Ibrahim, from Abd al-Rahman b. Ishaq, from al-Zuhri) reports from Urwa that a man of the Ansar disputed with Zubayr over a water channel of the Harra; the Prophet told Zubayr to water and then let the water go. The Ansari, who was of Banu Umayya, said, be just, Prophet of God, even if he is your aunt's son; the face of the Prophet changed until it was seen he was pained by this; then he said, Zubayr, hold the water up to the barrier, or to the ankles, then let it go. The narrator adds that the verse came down. The editor says the print dropped the words "of Banu Umayya", apparently thinking they were Quraysh, whereas they are the Banu Umayya b. Zayd b. Qays b. Amir b. Murra b. Malik of the Aws. He identifies Isma'il as Ibn Ulayya and Abd al-Rahman b. Ishaq as reliable (trusted by Ibn Ma'in and al-Bukhari, and used by Muslim); says the report has the form of a mursal because Urwa, a Successor, tells the story without naming his source; and says Yahya b. Adam gives it in Kitab al-Kharaj from Ibn Ulayya as al-Tabari does, while al-Bukhari gives the mursal form in two places through Ma'mar and in another through Ibn Jurayj, from al-Zuhri, with Ibn Hajar pointing to further versions from al-Zuhri that agree. The editor prefers that Urwa heard it from his father together with his brother Abdullah, perhaps was not sure of his memory because of his youth, and heard it again from his brother, so he told it in several ways; he says al-Bukhari's including the mursal form in two places confirms it was connected, and that Ibn Hajar supports this since the matter concerned Zubayr and his sons had every reason to preserve it. He adds that al-Suyuti mentions the report and attributes it also to Abd al-Razzaq, Abd b. Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 522}

**Report 9914 (pp. 522-523, note 1).** Abdullah b. Umayr al-Razi (9914, from Abdullah b. al-Zubayr, from Sufyan, from Amr b. Dinar, from Salama, a man of the descendants of Umm Salama) reports from Umm Salama that Zubayr took a man to the Prophet, who judged for Zubayr, and the man said, because he is your aunt's son; so God sent down the verse. The editor says he found no entry or mention anywhere for Abdullah b. Umayr al-Razi, al-Tabari's teacher; that Abdullah b. al-Zubayr is al-Humaydi, the reliable imam, a teacher of al-Bukhari, whom Abu Hatim called the most reliable of people on Ibn Uyayna, died 219; that Sufyan is Ibn Uyayna; and that Salama is Salama b. Abdullah b. Umar b. Abi Salama, noted earlier. He adds that Ibn Hajar says the story came by another route, given by al-Tabari and al-Tabarani, from Umm Salama; that al-Haythami mentions a version of al-Tabarani's in which Ya'qub b. Humayd, trusted by Ibn Hibban and weakened by others, appears, which is not in al-Tabari's chain and so is another route; that Ibn Kathir has it from Ibn Mardawayh by this chain but with "a man of the family of Abu Salama" and without Umm Salama; and that al-Suyuti attributes it also to al-Humaydi, Sa'id b. Mansur, Abd b. Humayd and Ibn al-Mundhir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 523}

**The other view on the occasion (pp. 523-524, reports 9915-9917).** Al-Tabari says others held that the verse came down about the hypocrite and the Jew whom God described in the account of taking a dispute to the taghut. Muhammad b. Amr (9915, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it concerns the Jewish man and the Muslim man who took their case to Ka'b b. al-Ashraf; al-Muthanna (9916, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid, likewise; and Ya'qub (9917, from Ibn Ulayya, from Dawud) from al-Sha'bi, similar, except that he said "to the soothsayer". The editor points to another view that al-Tabari recorded earlier, in report 5819, that the verse came down about an Ansari called Abu al-Husayn whose two sons became Christian, and says this is among the proofs that Abu Ja'far abridged his commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 524}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 524-525, notes 2-4).** Al-Tabari says the view that it means the two who took their dispute to the taghut is the more correct (his choice), because the verse stands in the setting of the story of those about whom God began the account and nothing shows that their story is cut off, so joining the parts is better unless an indication of a break arrives. If someone thinks the reports on Zubayr and his son, about his quarrel with the Ansari, suggest that this verse and its story are cut off from the verses before, he says it is not impossible that it came down about those who took their dispute to the taghut and also contains the explanation of what Zubayr and his Ansari opponent took to judgement, since the verse indicates it; and as that is not impossible, joining the meanings is better while the speech runs on one course with consistent meanings, unless an indication of a break appears. The editor says the print read a meaningless phrase here because its publisher read the manuscript poorly and did not know its copyist's habit of writing a certain verb without dots; in two other places he kept the manuscript reading and noted a printing error and a variant clause in the print. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 525}

**Grammar of the coordinated verb (p. 525).** Al-Tabari says the last verb of the verse is put in the subjunctive by coordination with the verb before it, which is itself coordinated with the verb that follows the limit "until". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 525}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 525}
