## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when you said: O Moses, we will not believe you until we see God openly" al-Tabari says the sense is: we will not accept you or acknowledge what you brought until we see God plainly, with the veil between us lifted, as a well is cleared when the mud covering its water is removed; he cites al-Farazdaq. He reports from Ibn Abbas, al-Rabi', Ibn Zayd and Qatada that "jahra" means openly, to the eye. He then says God reminds them of their forebears' disagreement and poor conduct toward their prophets despite the many signs they had seen: at one time asking their prophet for a god other than God, at another worshipping the calf, at another saying they would not believe until they saw God openly, and at another, when called to fight, saying "go, you and your Lord, and fight". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 80} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 81}

Al-Tabari adds that God informed the Jews of Medina, whom these verses address, that in denying Muhammad while knowing the truth of his affair they were no more than their forebears who turned back from their religion again and again and rose against Moses despite God's great favour. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 82}
On "so the thunderbolt took you while you looked on", the commentators differ on what the thunderbolt was: Qatada says they died, al-Rabi' that they heard a sound and were struck, meaning died, al-Suddi that it was fire, Ibn Ishaq that it was the quake, and they all died. Al-Tabari explains that "sa'iqa" originally means any terrifying thing a person sees or meets until it brings destruction, loss of mind or of some bodily faculty, whether sound, fire, earthquake or tremor, and that the one struck may stay alive, as in the verse where Moses fell "sa'iqan", meaning in a swoon; he cites Jarir. "While you looked on" means it took them openly, as they watched it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 82} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 83} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 84}
