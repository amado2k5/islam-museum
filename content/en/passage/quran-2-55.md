## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when you said: O Moses, we will not believe you until we see God openly" al-Tabari says the sense is: we will not accept you or acknowledge what you brought until we see God plainly, with the veil between us lifted, as a well is cleared when the mud covering its water is removed; he cites al-Farazdaq. He reports from Ibn Abbas, al-Rabi', Ibn Zayd and Qatada that "jahra" means openly, to the eye. He then says God reminds them of their forebears' disagreement and poor conduct toward their prophets despite the many signs they had seen: at one time asking their prophet for a god other than God, at another worshipping the calf, at another saying they would not believe until they saw God openly, and at another, when called to fight, saying "go, you and your Lord, and fight". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 80} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 81}
