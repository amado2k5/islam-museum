## Summary
Verse 139 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/sacred-livestock-jahiliyya|the sacred livestock of the Jahiliyya (bahira, sa’iba, wasila, ham)]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 139 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 139, "they said: what is in the bellies of these cattle is reserved for our males and forbidden to our wives, but if it is dead, they share in it", the commentators differed on what is meant by "what is in the bellies of these cattle". Some said it means milk; he gives (13932) Ibn Abbas: milk. The note gives a biography of Abd Allah ibn Abi al-Hudhayl al-Anazi, a reliable Successor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 146}

Report 13933 gives the same from Ibn Abbas; 13934, from Qatada: the milk of the bahira was for males and not women, and if what was born was dead, males and females shared in it; 13935, from Qatada: what is in the bellies of the bahira means their milk, which they made for the men and not the women; 13936, from Amir: of the bahira only men drank the milk, and if any of it died, men and women ate it; and 13937, from Ibn Abbas: it is milk, which they forbade to their females and their males drank; when a ewe bore a male they slaughtered it and it was for the men and not the women, while a female was left and not slaughtered; if it was dead they shared in it, and God forbade that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 147}

Others said what is meant is the foetuses in the bellies of the bahira and sa'iba; he gives (13938) al-Suddi, that whatever of these animals was born alive was reserved for men and not women, and whatever was born dead was eaten by both men and women; and (13939) and (13940) Mujahid: the sa'iba and bahira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 148}

Al-Tabari's choice: the soundest saying is that God reported of these unbelievers that they said of particular cattle, what is in the bellies of these cattle is reserved for our males, apart from our females; milk is among what is in their bellies, and so are their foetuses, and God did not specify in His report of them that they said some of it was forbidden to women and not other parts. So it must be said that they held that what is in the bellies of those cattle, milk and foetus, is lawful for their males alone and not their females, unless the foetus was dead, in which case men and women share in eating it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 148}

The Arabists differed over why "khalisa" is feminine. Some Basran and some Kufan grammarians said it was made feminine to confirm the purity, as if once purity was affirmed for them it resembled abundance, and went the way of "rawiya" and "nassaba"; some Kufan grammarians said it is feminine because "cattle" is feminine, since what is in their bellies is like them, while whoever made it masculine did so for "ma" being masculine, and in the reading of Abd Allah it is "khalisun"; "khalisa" in its feminine can also be a verbal noun, like "'afiya" and "'aqiba", as in "We purified them with a pure quality" (38:46). Al-Tabari's view is that the meaning is intensity in the purity of what is in the cattle's bellies for their males apart from their females, as is done with "rawiya", "nassaba" and "'allama" when emphasis is meant in describing one who has that quality, as one says so-and-so is the pure one of so-and-so, his close confidant. The editor refers to al-Farra' and to the earlier explanation of "al-khalisa". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 149}

As for "forbidden to our wives", they differed on "azwaj": some said women, from Mujahid (13941); others said daughters, from Ibn Zayd (13942), who said the "azwaj" are the daughters, and they said the daughters get nothing of it. Al-Tabari's view: God reported of these idolaters that they said of what is in the bellies of these cattle, this is forbidden to our wives; and "azwaj" in their speech are their women, who are without doubt the daughters of the men whose children they are and the wives of the men whose spouses they are. In "forbidden to our wives" lies a clear proof that "khalisa" is feminine for emphasis, for had it been for the feminine of "cattle", it would have been "muharrama 'ala azwajina"; but since the feminine in "khalisa" was for emphasis and no such emphasis was intended in "muharram", it returned to the masculine of "ma". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 150}

On "but if it is dead, they share in it", the readers differed. Yazid ibn al-Qa'qa' and Talha ibn Musarrif, with others, read "wa in takun maytatun" with ta' and "maytatun" in the nominative (Yazid doubling the ya' and Talha lightening it), as reported in 13943 and 13944; some readers of Medina, Kufa and Basra read "wa in yakun maytatan" with ya', "maytatan" in the accusative and the ya' light, as if he meant: if what is in the bellies of those cattle is dead, making "yakun" masculine for the masculine "ma" and putting "al-mayta" in the accusative as the predicate of "yakun"; those who read "wa in takun maytatun" meant: if what is in their bellies is dead, making "takun" feminine for the feminine "mayta". "They share in it" means men and their wives share in eating it, forbidding it to none of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 150}

Ibn Zayd said (13945) that women eat with the men if what comes out of their bellies is dead, in which case they share in it, and that they said: if we wish we give the daughters a share in it, and if we wish we do not. Al-Tabari remarks that the apparent wording of the recitation is against what Ibn Zayd interpreted, for it shows that they said, if what is in their bellies is dead, we are partners in it, with no condition of will, whereas Ibn Zayd claimed they left it to their will. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 151}

Then "He will repay them for their description; He is wise and knowing". Al-Tabari says: He will repay and requite these forgers of lies against Him in their forbidding what God did not forbid, permitting what He did not permit, and attributing their lie in that to God; "their description" means their describing a lie against God, as He said elsewhere, "their tongues describe the lie" (16:62). "Wasf" and "sifa" are one in Arabic usage and both are verbal nouns, like "wazn" and "zina". He gives (13946) and (13947) Mujahid: their lying speech in that; (13948) Abu al-Aliya: their lie; and (13949) Qatada: their lie. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 152}

As for "He is wise and knowing", al-Tabari says that God, in repaying them for their lying description and false saying about Him, is wise in all His management of His creation and knowing of what is good for them and of their other affairs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 153}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
