**Commentary on verse 49 (pp. 128-132)**

On "then after that will come a year in which people will be given rain, and in which they will press" al-Tabari says this is a report from Joseph to the people of what was not in their king's dream, but of the knowledge of the unseen that God gave him as a sign of his prophethood and a proof of his truthfulness; report 19377, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: then God added to him knowledge of a year they had not asked about. By "in it people are given relief" he means with rain; reports 19378 to 19381, from Qatada, al-Dahhak, Ibn Abbas and Mujahid: with rain, and from Ibn Abbas: he told them something they had not asked him about, which God had taught him; through Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Wasiti, Juwaybir, al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 129}

On "and in it they will press" the interpreters differed. Some said it means they will press grapes, sesame and the like; report 19382, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: grapes and oil; report 19383 from him, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: sesame for oil, grapes for wine and olives for oil; report 19384 from him, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, uncle and father from his father: rain will fall on them and they will press grapes and press oil and press all the fruits. Report 19385 begins at the end of the page, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 129}

Report 19385, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: they press their grapes. Report 19386, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: grapes. Report 19387, from al-Dahhak, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Wasiti and Juwaybir: oil. Report 19388, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they used to press grapes and fruits. Report 19389, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they press grapes, olives and fruits in the fertile year; this was knowledge God gave Joseph that he had not been asked about. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 130}

Others said that "and in it they will press" means "and in it they will milk". Report 19390, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Fudala and Ali ibn Abi Talha: in it they milk. Report 19391, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hammad, al-Faraj ibn Fudala and Ali ibn Abi Talha: Ibn Abbas used to read it with a ta', meaning "you will milk". Al-Tabari then turns to the readers' differences: some readers of Medina, Basra and Kufa read it with a ya', in the sense of pressing grapes and oils, while most of the Kufan readers read it with a ta'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 130}

Some read it in the passive, "they will be rained upon". Al-Tabari says he does not allow reading it so, because it departs from what the readers of the great cities follow. He then says the correct view is that the reader may choose either of the other two readings: with the ya', going back to the report about the people, so that in it the people are relieved and press their grapes and oils; or with the ta', going back to the words "except a little of what you store" and addressing those who were addressed there. Both are widespread among the readers of the cities and agree in meaning although the wording differs, since if the addressees were relieved and pressed, the people around them were too, and the reverse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 131}

Al-Tabari adds that someone without knowledge of the early authorities, who explains the Qur'an by his own opinion on the manner of Arab speech, took "and in it they will press" to mean "in it they will be saved from drought and dearth by rain", deriving it from al-'asr and al-'usra, meaning a refuge, and citing a verse of Abu Zubayd al-Ta'i. The editor identifies him as Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna in his Majaz al-Qur'an, corrects the wording from the manuscript, traces the verse to its sources, notes that it comes from a poem in which the poet mourned his brother al-Lajlaj, who died of thirst on the road to Mecca, and glosses "al-manjud" as the distressed, the overpowered, the perishing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 131}

Al-Tabari says that this interpretation is refuted well enough by the opposite view of all the scholars among the Companions and Successors, and of Ibn Abbas. As for the report of al-Faraj ibn Fudala from Ali ibn Abi Talha (milking), he finds it meaningless, because it goes against known Arabic usage and against what is known of Ibn Abbas. The editor's note gives a second verse, by Labid, on the word "manjud" and explains the story of Qays ibn Juz' ibn Khalid ibn Ja'far, who rode out on a raid and prevailed, then died suddenly on his horse while serving as a lookout in the cold, and notes a different reading in the poet's collection. The verses are described here and not quoted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 132}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
