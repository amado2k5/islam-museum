## Summary
Verse 116 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 116 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**A threat to the defiant group and their like (p. 133).** Al-Tabari says this is a threat from God to the other, defiant group of the People of the Book whom He reported to be defiant and to have incurred His wrath, and to those like them among the people of disbelief in God and His messenger and what Muhammad brought. Those who disbelieved are those who denied Muhammad's prophethood and rejected him and what he brought; neither their wealth, collected in the world, nor their children, whom they raised in it, will avert anything of God's punishment on the Day of Resurrection if He delays it for them, nor in the world if He hastens it. He singled out children and wealth because a man's children are his nearest kin, he has more power over his own property than over others', and his word over it goes further; if his own son and the property he controls do not avail him, other relatives and their property are further still from availing. The editor corrects the printed wording from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 133}

**Companions of the fire and abiding (pp. 133-134).** God then reported that they are the people of the fire, and made them its companions because they are its inhabitants who neither leave nor part from it, like a man's companion who does not leave him and his mate who does not part from him; and His statement that they abide in it shows their companionship has no end, since some things part from their companion in some states and times, whereas the companionship of those who disbelieved with the fire they are consigned to is permanent, with no end. He ends by seeking refuge in God from it and from whatever speech and action brings near to it. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "companions of the fire" and corrects a point in the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 133} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 134}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 134}
