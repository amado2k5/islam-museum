## Summary
Verse 86 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 86 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 86: about whom did it come down?** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over whom this verse refers to and about whom it came down.

**Those who said it concerns al-Harith ibn Suwayd and those who turned back (7360-7367).** Some said it came down about al-Harith ibn Suwayd al-Ansari, who had been Muslim and then turned back after his Islam. Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Buzay al-Basri relays from Yazid ibn Zurayʿ, from Dawud ibn Abi Hind, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas that a man of the Ansar became Muslim, then turned back and joined the idolatry, then regretted it and sent to his people to ask the Messenger of God whether he had any repentance; the verses came down up to the exception for repentance, and his people sent word to him and he became Muslim (7360). Ibn al-Muthanna relays from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud, from Ikrima similarly without taking it back to Ibn Abbas, except that he said that his people wrote to him and he said: my people lied to me, and he returned (7361); Abu Kurayb relays from Hakim ibn Jumay, from Ali ibn Mushir, from Dawud ibn Abi Hind, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas that a man of the Ansar turned back, giving a similar account (7362), and the editor notes that Hakim ibn Jumay al-Kufi has an entry in al-Kabir and al-Jarh. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ja'far ibn Sulayman, from Humayd al-A'raj, from Mujahid that al-Harith ibn Suwayd came and became Muslim with the Prophet, then disbelieved and went back to his people, and God sent down the Quran about him; a man of his people carried it to him and read it to him, and al-Harith said: by God, I know you to be truthful, the Messenger of God is more truthful than you, and God is the most truthful of the three; al-Harith returned and became Muslim and his Islam was good (7363). Musa ibn Harun relays from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that it came down about al-Harith ibn Suwayd al-Ansari, who disbelieved after his belief, and God sent down these verses about him up to the mention of the companions of the fire abiding in it; then he repented and became Muslim, and God lifted it from him by exempting those who repent (7364). Muhammad ibn Amr relays from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that it was a man of Banu Amr ibn Awf who disbelieved after his belief (7365), and al-Muthanna relays it from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid (7366); al-Qasim relays from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Mujahid that it was a man of Banu Amr ibn Awf, and Ibn Jurayj said that Abd Allah ibn Kathir told him from Mujahid that he joined the land of Rum and became Christian, then wrote to his people to send to ask whether he had any repentance; he said: I think he believed, then returned. Ibn Jurayj said that Ikrima said it came down about Abu Amir the monk, al-Harith ibn Suwayd ibn al-Samit and Wahwah ibn al-Aslat, among twelve men who went back from Islam and joined Quraysh and then wrote to their families asking whether they had any repentance, and the exception of those who repented came down (7367).

**Those who said it concerns the People of the Book (7368-7371).** Others said that this verse refers to the People of the Book and came down about them. Muhammad ibn Sad relays from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas that they are the People of the Book who knew Muhammad and then disbelieved in him (7368); Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan that they are the Jews and Christians (7369); Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that al-Hasan used to say they are the People of the Book among the Jews and Christians, who saw Muhammad's description in their book, acknowledged it and testified that he was true, and when he was sent from others they envied the Arabs and denied him, disbelieving after their acknowledgement (7370); and al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from al-Hasan that they are the People of the Book who used to find Muhammad in their book and to seek victory through him, and then disbelieved after their faith (7371).

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says that the nearer of the two views to the outward sense of the revelation is what al-Hasan said, that the verse refers to the People of the Book, but that the reports for the other view are more numerous and those who held it were more learned in the interpretation of the Quran; the editor says this is a good and decisive judgement on this verse and on others over whose meanings people differed. Al-Tabari holds it permissible that God sent down these verses on account of the people who were reported to have turned back from Islam, joining their story with that of those who took their path in turning away from belief in Muhammad, and then made known to His servants His way with them; so included is everyone who believed in Muhammad before he was sent and then disbelieved in him after he was sent, and everyone who was an unbeliever and then became Muslim in his time and then turned back from his Islam while he lived, so that both groups, and others of similar sense, are meant by the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 576}

**The sense of the verse according to al-Tabari.** He says: by what means would God guide to the right and grant success in faith to a people who denied Muhammad's prophethood after they confirmed him and acknowledged what he brought from his Lord, and after they acknowledged that he is truly God's messenger to His creation, when proofs and signs of that came to them from God; and God does not grant success in truth and right to the people of wrongdoing, who are those who exchanged truth for falsehood and chose unbelief over faith. He says he has shown earlier the sense of wrongdoing, that it is putting a thing in the wrong place; the editor refers to volume 1 and the language index.
