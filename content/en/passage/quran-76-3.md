## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 3-4 as a single unit. He interprets the phrase “Indeed, We guided him to the way, whether he be grateful or ungrateful” as meaning, may His praise be exalted, that by saying “Indeed, We guided him to the way,” We have shown him the path to Paradise and made it clear to him, whether he is grateful or ungrateful. If the statement is interpreted in this way, then “either” and “either” are in the sense of a consequence. It is also possible that “either” and “either” have the same meaning, as in the verse “either He will punish them or He will forgive them.” In this case, the phrase “whether he be grateful or ungrateful” would be a circumstantial clause describing the pronoun “him” in “We guided him.” Thus, if the statement is interpreted in this way, the meaning would be: We guided him to the way, whether he be wretched or blessed. Some grammarians of Basra said, as he said, “Either the punishment or the Hour,” as if you hadn’t mentioned “either.” He said, “And if you wish, you can begin what follows it and raise it.” The passage also quotes statements from Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Zayd.
