**Commentary on verse 111 (pp. 498-500)**

Al-Tabari says: God bought from the believers their selves and their wealth for Paradise; "a promise binding on Him in truth" means He promised them Paradise, the Almighty, a promise binding on Him that He will fulfil to them in His revealed books, the Torah, the Gospel and the Qur'an, if they fulfil what they covenanted with God, fighting in His way and in support of His religion against His enemies, killing and being killed; "and who is more faithful to his covenant than God" means who is better in fulfilling what he guaranteed and stipulated than God; "so rejoice" He says to the believers: rejoice, believers who affirmed God in what you covenanted, in your sale of your selves and your wealth for what you sold them to your Lord for, for that is the great triumph; The editor refers to earlier explanations of the words of this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 498}

Report 17266 is from Shamir ibn Atiyya: every Muslim owes God a pledge on his neck, which he fulfils or dies holding, as in the words that God bought from the believers, up to that is the great triumph; then He described them: the repenters, the worshippers, up to give glad tidings to the believers. Report 17267 is from Ibn Abbas: God bought from the believers their selves and their wealth, that is, for Paradise. Report 17268 is from Qatada, who recited the verse and said God priced them and set a high price for them; report 17269 is from al-Hasan, who recited it and said He made a pledge with them and set a high price for them. The editor explains that tathamun is haggling over a price. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 499}

Report 17270 is from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and others: Abdullah ibn Rawaha said to the Messenger of God, "Set the condition for your Lord and for yourself as you wish." He said, "For my Lord, that you worship Him and associate nothing with Him; for myself, that you protect me as you protect yourselves and your wealth." They said, "And what do we get if we do that?" He said, "Paradise." They said, "A profitable sale; we shall neither cancel nor ask to cancel," and the verse was revealed. The editor explains iqala as annulling a sale so that the goods return to their owner and the price to the buyer. Report 17271 is from Ubayd ibn Tufayl al-Absi: a man asked him about the verse, then said, "Shall I not charge the polytheists and fight until I am killed?" He answered, "Woe to you, where is the condition? The repenters, the worshippers." {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 500}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
