## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains "has been prescribed for you" as made obligatory for you, and "fasting" (siyam) as a verbal noun from "I abstained from such a thing", so it means restraining oneself from what God commanded restraint from; hence horses are called "fasting" when they hold back from moving, and he cites a line of al-Nabigha and the phrase "I vowed to the Merciful a fast", meaning silence from speech. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 409} He then reports the differing views of the commentators about who is meant by "those before you" and what the comparison is. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 410} Some said they are the Christians and the comparison is in timing and length; from al-Sha'bi it is reported that Ramadan was prescribed for the Christians and they moved it to a season and then added days until it reached fifty, and the editor notes in a footnote the weakness of a narrator in that chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 410} Others said the comparison is that their fast ran from the last evening prayer to the last evening prayer, which was the first fast prescribed for the believers, agreeing that they were the Christians; in al-Suddi's report they added twenty days to atone for what they had done, making it fifty, and from al-Rabi' the fast ran from the late evening to the late evening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 411} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 412} Others said the People of the Book, from Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 412} Others said it was prescribed for all people, from Qatada: Ramadan was prescribed on people as on those before them, and before it God had prescribed three days of each month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 412}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that the sense is: fasting has been prescribed for you as it was for those before you of the People of the Book, "a limited number of days", which is the whole month of Ramadan, since those after Abraham were commanded to follow him, and the comparison falls on the timing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 412} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 413} By "that you may become mindful" he understands that you may guard against eating, drinking and sexual relations during the fast, as a number of commentators said, among them al-Suddi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 413}
