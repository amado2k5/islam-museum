**Commentary on verse 28 (pp. 77-83)**

On [[passage/quran-10-28|10:28]], al-Tabari says: on the day We gather creation for the place of reckoning all together, We then say to those who associated gods and rivals with God, "stay in your place", that is, remain where you are, you polytheists and your partners whom you worshipped besides God among the gods and idols. The editor refers to the earlier gloss on "gathering". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 77}

"So We separated them" means We set apart the polytheists and what they had associated with God, from the phrase "I separated the thing", said when one parts it from another. He says the verb is in the intensive form to express multiplying and repeating the act. Some are reported to have read it with an alif, as in the same form elsewhere in Surat Luqman; the Arabs sometimes add an alif in place of the doubling of the middle letter, saying the form with alif when the act concerns one person, while for two they hardly say anything else. The editor notes that the bracketed addition in al-Tabari's words is a conjecture from the language texts, since the manuscript ran the passage on as one block, and refers to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 78}

As for "and their partners said, you were not worshipping us": this is when the followed disowned those who followed them, saw the punishment and found the ties cut. When the polytheists were told to follow what they worshipped and their gods were set up before them, they said "we used to worship these", and the gods answered that they had not been worshipping them. Report 17648, from Mujahid: on the Day of Resurrection there is an hour of hardship in which the gods they worshipped are set up; the gods say that they neither heard nor saw nor understood and did not know they were being worshipped; the worshippers insist they were, and the gods reply that God suffices as witness between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 78}

Report 17649, from Ibn Zayd: We separated them; their partners said you were not worshipping us; they replied that they had been; the gods said God suffices as witness, and that they could not hear, see or speak; then God said that there every soul will prove what it sent ahead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 79}

It is also related from Mujahid that he took "gathering" here to mean death (report 17650, through Ibn Waki', from al-A'mash). Al-Tabari says that his own view is the better reading of the verse, because God reports what He will say that day to those who associated, and that is clearly not something that happens in the grave; it is an account of what will be said to them and what they will say at the place of standing after the resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 79}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
