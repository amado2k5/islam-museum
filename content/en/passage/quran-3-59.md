## Summary
Verse 59 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 59 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 59.** Al-Tabari says the likeness of Jesus, whom God created without a father (as the Najran Christian delegation was told), is to God like the likeness of Adam, whom He made from dust and then ordered to be, so he was, without male or female; Jesus' creation from his mother without a father is no stranger than Adam's creation without parents, and God's command, when He commands a thing to be, is that it is. The editor notes that the scribe misplaced a clause and that he moved it back to its place, which makes the sentence run. The people of interpretation say the verse was sent down as an argument for the Prophet against the Najran delegation who disputed with him about Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 467} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 468}

**Reports 7160 to 7162.** Report 7160 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mughira, from Amir) says the people of Najran said the most extreme things about Jesus among the Christians and used to dispute with the Prophet, so the verse was sent down, up to what is in [[passage/quran-3-61|3:61]]. Report 7161 (Muhammad ibn Saad, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) says a group from Najran, including al-Sayyid and al-Aqib, came and asked what he meant by mentioning their companion; "who?", he said; "Jesus, you claim he is God's servant"; he said yes, he is God's servant; they asked whether he had seen or been told of the like of Jesus and left; then Gabriel came with the command of his Lord and told him to say to them, when they returned, what the verse says. Report 7162 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says it was related that the two leaders of Najran and their bishops, al-Sayyid and al-Aqib, met the Prophet and asked about Jesus, saying every human has a father, so what of Jesus who has none, and God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 468} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 469}

**Report 7163 (its start).** Al-Tabari relays from al-Suddi (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) that when the Prophet was sent and the people of Najran heard of him, four of their best came to him: al-Aqib, al-Sayyid, Ma Sarjis and Ma Rikh, and asked what he says about Jesus. The editor discusses the spelling of the names: Mar Sarjis is a known saint of theirs, mentioned in poetry of Jarir and al-Akhtal; he could not establish the last name and did not find the two names in Ibn Hisham's list of the delegation, and he states no conclusion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 469}

We stop at the beginning of report 7163 on p. 469 and continue from p. 470. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
