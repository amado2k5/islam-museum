## Summary
Verse 59 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 59 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 59.** Al-Tabari says the likeness of Jesus, whom God created without a father (as the Najran Christian delegation was told), is to God like the likeness of Adam, whom He made from dust and then ordered to be, so he was, without male or female; Jesus' creation from his mother without a father is no stranger than Adam's creation without parents, and God's command, when He commands a thing to be, is that it is. The editor notes that the scribe misplaced a clause and that he moved it back to its place, which makes the sentence run. The people of interpretation say the verse was sent down as an argument for the Prophet against the Najran delegation who disputed with him about Jesus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 467} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 468}

**Reports 7160 to 7162.** Report 7160 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mughira, from Amir) says the people of Najran said the most extreme things about Jesus among the Christians and used to dispute with the Prophet, so the verse was sent down, up to what is in [[passage/quran-3-61|3:61]]. Report 7161 (Muhammad ibn Saad, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) says a group from Najran, including al-Sayyid and al-Aqib, came and asked what he meant by mentioning their companion; "who?", he said; "Jesus, you claim he is God's servant"; he said yes, he is God's servant; they asked whether he had seen or been told of the like of Jesus and left; then Gabriel came with the command of his Lord and told him to say to them, when they returned, what the verse says. Report 7162 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says it was related that the two leaders of Najran and their bishops, al-Sayyid and al-Aqib, met the Prophet and asked about Jesus, saying every human has a father, so what of Jesus who has none, and God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 468} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 469}

**Report 7163 (its start).** Al-Tabari relays from al-Suddi (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) that when the Prophet was sent and the people of Najran heard of him, four of their best came to him: al-Aqib, al-Sayyid, Ma Sarjis and Ma Rikh, and asked what he says about Jesus. The editor discusses the spelling of the names: Mar Sarjis is a known saint of theirs, mentioned in poetry of Jarir and al-Akhtal; he could not establish the last name and did not find the two names in Ibn Hisham's list of the delegation, and he states no conclusion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 469}

**The end of report 7163.** Al-Tabari completes al-Suddi's report: the Prophet said of Jesus that he is God's servant, His spirit and His word; they replied that rather he is God, who came down from His dominion, entered Mary's womb and came out, showing them His power and command; and they asked whether he had ever seen a human created without a father. God then sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 469} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 470}

**Reports 7164 to 7166.** Report 7164 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ikrima) says the verse was revealed about al-Aqib and al-Sayyid of Najran, both Christians. Ibn Jurayj adds that it reached them that the delegation of Najran's Christians came to the Prophet, among them al-Sayyid and al-Aqib, the two chiefs of Najran then, and said: why do you revile our companion? He asked who their companion was; Jesus son of Mary, they said, whom you claim to be a servant. The Prophet said: yes, he is God's servant and His word and a spirit from Him. They grew angry and said that if he was truthful he should show them a servant who revives the dead, heals the sick and shapes a bird from clay and breathes into it, but that he is God. He fell silent until Gabriel brought him what is in [[passage/quran-5-72|5:72]]; the Prophet told Gabriel that they had asked him for the likeness of Jesus, and Gabriel said his likeness is like Adam's, made from the earth. When morning came they returned, and he recited the verses to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 470}

Report 7165 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) means: if they say Jesus was created without a male, then I created Adam from dust by that same power with neither female nor male, and he was flesh, blood, hair and skin like Jesus, so Jesus' creation without a male is no stranger than this. The editor notes that the manuscript and print read one word where Ibn Hisham's Sira has another, that al-Tabari abridged Ibn Ishaq's words here and gives what he left out in report 7169, and that the report is in the Sira (2:231-232) as a continuation of earlier ones. Report 7166 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says two men of Najran came to the Prophet and asked whether he knew anyone born without a male, so that Jesus would be like him; God sent the verse, with the question: had Adam a father or a mother, as He created this one in this one's womb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 470} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 471}

**Al-Tabari on a grammatical objection.** He raises the question: how is the clause about Adam attached when Adam is a definite noun and definite nouns take no qualifying clause? He answers that the clause about his creation from dust is not a qualifier of Adam but an explanation of his case, interpreting the likeness drawn and how it came about. The editor explains that "qualifier" here means a descriptive clause, which needs an indefinite noun described, a pronoun in the clause linking it, and a declarative clause, and refers to al-Farra's Maani al-Quran (1:219). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 471}

**The tense of the closing verb.** Al-Tabari asks why the last verb is in the present tense when the account began with Adam's creation, a matter already past. He says God is informing His prophet that He brings things into being by His word "be", and the report of Adam ends at that word, so what follows is a fresh statement. The sense, to him: know, Muhammad, that whatever your Lord says "be" to, is. Because the wording shows that the aim is to tell the Prophet and all creation that what He brings about exists without source, beginning or element, the wording is left to suggest it and the present is joined to the past on that meaning. He relays that some Arabic grammarians say the verb is raised as the start of a new clause, meaning "was", as if saying "and it is". The editor calls al-Tabari's explanation a sufficient one rarely found in commentaries, and judges his view more probable than the other, widely repeated in the books of tafsir; this is the editor's opinion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 471} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 472}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
