## Summary
Verse 82 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 82 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "those who believe and do not mix their belief with wrong, for them is security, and they are guided" al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over who said what God reported, "those who believe and do not mix their belief with wrong". Some said this is God's final judgement between Abraham, his friend, and those of his people who argued with him, polytheists, when Abraham said to them "how should I fear what you associate..."; and God said, deciding between him and them: those who believed God and made worship pure for him and did not mix their worship of him and their belief in him with wrong, meaning with associating others, and did not associate anything in worshipping him, and then made their worship of God sincere, have better right to security from his punishment, for the fault of worshipping their Lord, than those who associate idols and images in their worship of him, for they are the ones afraid of his punishment, the fault of their worship; in this world they dread God's anger descending on them, and in the next they are certain of God's painful punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 492}

The editor refers for "mix" and "wrong" to earlier passages and says the printed text dropped the word "his Lord".

13473, from Ibn Ishaq: those who made worship pure as Abraham made it pure, in worship of God and his oneness, "and did not mix their belief with wrong" meaning with associating others, "for them is security and they are guided": security from punishment and guidance in the argument through knowledge and uprightness. God says "that was our argument that we gave Abraham against his people; we raise in degrees whom we will; your Lord is wise, knowing". 13474, from Ibn Zayd: God said and decided between them "those who believe and do not mix their belief with wrong", he said with associating others, "for them is security and they are guided"; as for sins, no one is free of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 493}

Others said this is the answer of Abraham's people to Abraham when he said to them which party has better right to security, and they said to him: those who believed in God and affirmed his oneness have better right to security, as they did not mix their belief with wrong. 13475, from Ibn Jurayj: the one who worships one lord or the one who worships many lords? His people say: those who believe and do not mix their belief with wrong by worshipping idols, and that is Abraham's argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 493}

Abu Jafar said: the better of the two views is that of those who said this is God's report of which of the two parties has better right to security and his final judgement between Abraham and his people, because were it from the saying of Abraham's people, who worshipped idols and associated them in worshipping God, they would have admitted the oneness and followed Abraham in what they disagreed with him over, the oneness; but it is, as I said of its interpretation, first. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 494}

The editor says the printed text has "at first" in a different word and he kept the manuscript's, which is entirely right, meaning "first".

The interpreters differed over the sense God meant by "and did not mix their belief with wrong". Some said: associating others. 13476, from Abdullah ibn Mas'ud: when this verse came down it weighed on the companions of God's messenger, and God's messenger said: do you not see what Luqman said, [[passage/quran-31-13|31:13]], "associating others is a great wrong"? 13477: Ibn Idris related it first from his father from Aban ibn Taghlib from al-A'mash, then heard it from al-A'mash himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 494}

The editor says Abdullah ibn Mas'ud's report through al-A'mash is related by Abu Jafar through chains numbered 13476 to 13483, that al-Bukhari related it in his Sahih and Muslim through Abdullah ibn Idris, Abu Mu'awiya and Waki, al-Tirmidhi in the commentary through Isa ibn Yunus, and Ahmad by several chains in his Musnad, and that Muslim mentioned report 13477 too.

13478, from Abdullah: when it came down it weighed on the Muslims, who said: God's messenger, none of us but wrongs himself? He said: not so; do you not hear Luqman's words to his son, "associating others is a great wrong"? 13479: it weighed on the companions of God's messenger and they said which of us has not wronged himself? He said: it is not as you think, it is only what Luqman said to his son. 13480: it weighed on the people and they said which of us does not wrong himself? He said: it is not what you mean; have you not heard what the righteous servant said, "my son, do not associate others with God, associating others is a great wrong"? It is only associating others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 495}

The editor identifies Aban ibn Taghlib al-Rabi'i, trusted, of whom Ibn Adi said that most of his copies are sound and he is truthful in his reports even if his school was Shi'ism, and he quotes Ibn Hajar in al-Tahdhib that Shi'ism in the early scholars' usage was holding Ali superior to Uthman and right in his wars, while putting the two Shaykhs first, and some held him the best of creatures after God's messenger, and that if he is pious, devout, truthful and diligent his report is not rejected for this, whereas in the later usage it is pure rejection and the report of an extreme rejector is not accepted. He explains why the second report is cited after the first: Ibn Idris related it earlier from al-A'mash directly and earlier from his father, to point out the height of his chain; he identifies in 13478 Isa ibn Uthman ibn Isa al-Ramli and his uncle Yahya ibn Isa, both trusted; and says Ahmad related 13479 and 13480 in the Musnad through Waki and Abu Mu'awiya.

13481 and 13482, from Alqama and Ibrahim: associating others. 13483, from Abdullah: it weighed on the companions of God's messenger, who said which of us has not mixed his belief with wrong? and the Prophet said: not so, have you not heard Luqman's words, "associating others is a great wrong"? 13484 and 13485, from Abu Bakr: "and did not mix their belief with wrong", he said associating others. 13486: Zayd ibn Sawhan asked Salman: Abu Abdullah, a verse of God's book has reached me to the utmost, "those who believe and do not mix their belief with wrong"! Salman said: it is associating others with God. Zayd said: I would not be glad not to have heard it from you, nor to have the like of all I own this evening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 497}

The editor identifies in 13484 Abu Ishaq al-Shaybani, Sulayman ibn Abi Sulayman, Abu Bakr ibn Abi Musa al-Ash'ari, trusted, and al-Aswad ibn Hilal al-Muharibi, Abu Sallam, who lived in the time of Umar but did not meet Abu Bakr; in 13485 that the printed text dropped "from Abu Ishaq" and the report is mursal; in 13486 Sa'id ibn Ubayd al-Ta'i, Abu al-Hudhayl, trusted by Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in, of whom Abu Hatim said his hadith is written, Abu al-Ash'ar al-Abdi, whom al-Bukhari lists in the kunyas saying Khalifa ibn Khalaf related from him, noting that "Isma'il ibn Ubayd" in al-Bukhari's version is probably an error for "Sa'id ibn Ubayd", and he did not know his father; and Zayd ibn Sawhan ibn Hujr al-Abdi, brother of Sa'sa'a, who met the Prophet, is listed among the Companions, was very fond of Salman al-Farisi and was killed at the Camel with Ali, Ibn Sa'd calling him trusted and of few reports. He notes that al-Suyuti mentioned the report in al-Durr al-Manthur, ascribing it to al-Firyabi, Ibn Abi Shayba, al-Hakim al-Tirmidhi, Ibn al-Mundhir, Abu al-Shaykh and Ibn Mardawayh; and that at the end of report 13486 a part of the old division from which his manuscript was copied ends, with a closing in which the copyist prays a blessing on the Prophet and his family.

We stop here at the foot of p. 497, at the end of a part of the old division after report 13486 in the commentary on verse 82, and al-Tabari continues on p. 498.
