## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari notes the interpreters differ on "you were dead and He gave you life, then He causes you to die, then gives you life". He reports from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that it means you were nothing, so He created you, then makes you die, then gives you life on the Day of Rising; from Ibn Mas'ud that the verse in surat Ghafir about two deaths and two lives is like it; and similar from Mujahid, Abu Malik and Abu al-Aliya. From al-Dahhak on Ibn Abbas: you were dust before He created you, which was a death, then He gave you life, then He makes you die and you return to the graves, a second death, then raises you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 418} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 419}
Abu Salih said the meaning is that He gives you life in the grave, then makes you die. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 419}
Qatada said they were dead in their fathers' loins, so God gave them life and created them, then He causes the death that cannot be avoided, then gives life for the resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 419} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 420}
He reports Ibn Zayd that God brought them from Adam's back when He took the covenant, then made them die, then created them in wombs, then made them die, then gives them life on the Day of Rising. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 421}
Al-Tabari says each view has a basis in interpretation. Those who say "you were nothing" follow the Arabs' calling an obscure, forgotten thing "dead" and a well-known one "alive", citing a line by Abu Nukhayla, so the sense is that they were obscure and unmentioned and He gave them life by creating them as people who are remembered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 421}

Al-Tabari goes on to weigh the views. Those who take "causing death" as the soul's departure must take the address as to people in their graves after being revived there; he finds this unlikely because the verse is a rebuke calling people back to obedience, and there is no turning back or repentance in the grave after death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 422}
He explains Qatada's view as that they were sperm without souls, so giving them life is breathing souls into them, causing death is taking the souls, and the next life comes when the trumpet is blown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 422}
He holds that Ibn Zayd's account departs from the plain wording, since the verse names two deaths and two lives and he names three, and that drawing out Adam's offspring and taking the covenant has nothing to do with these two verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 422} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 423}
He mentions another view, that the first death is the sperm's separation from the man's body into the woman's womb, arguing that a living being's death is the soul leaving it and that a severed limb is dead; he says this is a possible reading if any of the trusted authorities on the Qur'an's meaning had held it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 423} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 424}
He chooses the view of Ibn Mas'ud and Ibn Abbas: "dead" in the sense of forgotten and unknown, sperm in their fathers' loins, whom He gave life by forming them as people who are remembered and known, then causes to die by taking their souls so they are unknown in the barzakh until the resurrection, then gives life by returning souls, and to Him they return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 424}
He says the verse is a rebuke to those who said "we believe in God and the Last Day" but were not believers: how can they deny His power to give them life after death when they were lifeless sperm that He formed? One who did that is not unable to restore and gather them for repayment for their deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 424} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 425}
He then notes that God went on to count His favours to these people and the Jewish rabbis, and began with their father Adam, the honour shown him, and what befell him and his enemy Iblis. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 425}
