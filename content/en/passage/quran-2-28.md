## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari notes the interpreters differ on "you were dead and He gave you life, then He causes you to die, then gives you life". He reports from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that it means you were nothing, so He created you, then makes you die, then gives you life on the Day of Rising; from Ibn Mas'ud that the verse in surat Ghafir about two deaths and two lives is like it; and similar from Mujahid, Abu Malik and Abu al-Aliya. From al-Dahhak on Ibn Abbas: you were dust before He created you, which was a death, then He gave you life, then He makes you die and you return to the graves, a second death, then raises you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 418} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 419}
Abu Salih said the meaning is that He gives you life in the grave, then makes you die. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 419}
Qatada said they were dead in their fathers' loins, so God gave them life and created them, then He causes the death that cannot be avoided, then gives life for the resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 419} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 420}
He reports Ibn Zayd that God brought them from Adam's back when He took the covenant, then made them die, then created them in wombs, then made them die, then gives them life on the Day of Rising. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 421}
Al-Tabari says each view has a basis in interpretation. Those who say "you were nothing" follow the Arabs' calling an obscure, forgotten thing "dead" and a well-known one "alive", citing a line by Abu Nukhayla, so the sense is that they were obscure and unmentioned and He gave them life by creating them as people who are remembered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 421}
