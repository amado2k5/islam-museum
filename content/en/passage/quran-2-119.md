## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "We have sent you with the truth as a bearer of good news and a warner" al-Tabari says: We sent you, Muhammad, with Islam, besides which I accept no religion from anyone, and it is the truth, to give good news to whoever follows you of victory in this world and the win of reward in the Hereafter and lasting bliss, and to warn whoever disobeys you and rejects what you called him to of disgrace in this world, abasement and a humiliating punishment in the Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 557} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 558}

On "and you will not be asked about the companions of Hell" he says most reciters read it with damma on the ta and raised lam as a report, meaning you conveyed what you were sent with, your part being to convey, and you are not held to account for whoever disbelieved; some of the people of Medina read "wa-la tas'al" with a jussive, a prohibition, meaning We sent you to convey and not to ask about the companions of Hell. For the sense of this reading he gives a report from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi that the Prophet said "if only I knew what became of my parents", and the verse was revealed (also by al-Thawri's route, and from Dawud ibn Abi Asim); the editor says these are mursal reports that are no proof, that the first two chains include Musa ibn Ubayda who is very weak, and cites what Ibn Kathir quoted from al-Qurtubi on the matter and Ibn Kathir's remark, and he thinks going into such matters is unprofitable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 558} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 559}

Al-Tabari holds the better reading is the raised form as a report, because God told the stories of peoples of the Jews and Christians and their error, then said to His Prophet "We sent you with the truth as a bearer of good news and warner", so convey My message, for you bear no consequence for the deeds of one who disbelieves in you after you convey it, nor are you asked what he did after; and there was no mention of the Prophet asking his Lord about the companions of Hell to which the words could be directed, so words are taken in their plain sense unless clear proof comes. He says no report stands as proof that the Prophet was forbidden to ask, and that if one thinks the report from Muhammad ibn Ka'b sound, the impossibility of doubt in the Prophet that the polytheists are of the people of Hell and that his parents were among them weighs against it; besides, beginning the clause with "wa" rather than "fa" shows more clearly it is a report, and the readings of Ubayy ("wa-ma tus'al") and Ibn Mas'ud ("wa-lan tus'al") also attest the raised report form. In a note the editor cites Ibn Kathir's remark that al-Tabari's course has room for doubt, since this might have been while he sought forgiveness for his parents before learning their state, after which he disowned them; the editor replies that al-Tabari meant that the report from Muhammad ibn Ka'b is not sound and that, if sound, the verse was not revealed for this, and that the context of the verses concerns the Jews and Christians. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 559} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 560} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 561}

He records that some Basran grammarians took "wa-la tus'al" as a circumstantial clause, as if meaning We sent you as bearer of good news and warner, not held to account for the companions of Hell, and allowed on that basis the reading "wa-la tas'alu" with fatha on the ta and damma on the lam; al-Tabari says he has stated what is right in his view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 561}
