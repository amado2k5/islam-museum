**Commentary on verse 34 (pp. 216-217)**

Al-Tabari says: believers who affirmed God and His messenger and acknowledged the oneness of their Lord, many of the scholars and readers of the Children of Israel among Jews and Christians "eat the wealth of the people falsely", taking bribes in their judgments, distorting God's book and writing books with their own hands and then saying "this is from God", taking a small price for it from their lowly; "and bar from the way of God" means they prevent whoever wants to enter Islam from entering it by forbidding him. Report 16648, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the rabbis are of the Jews, the monks of the Christians, and God's way is Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 216}

He goes on: and with them eat it too those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in God's way; so give the many of the rabbis and monks who eat people's wealth falsely, and those who hoard gold and silver without spending it in God's way, the news of a painful punishment on the day of resurrection, a punishment from God that hurts. The scholars differed on the sense of "hoarding". Some said it is any property on which zakat is due and the zakat is not paid, "not spending it in God's way" meaning not paying its zakat. Report 16649, from Ibn Umar, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Wahhab, Ayyub and Nafi': any property whose zakat you have paid is not a hoard even if buried, and any property whose zakat has not been paid is the hoard God mentioned in the Qur'an, with which its owner is branded even if it is not buried. The editor notes that al-Tabari gives Ibn Umar's report by several routes in differing wording, as Ibn Umar's own saying, which is the right form, that its chain is sound, and that Malik gives it in the same sense in the Muwatta'. Report 16650, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd, Sa'id ibn Maslama, Isma'il ibn Umayya and Nafi': any property whose zakat has been paid is not a hoard even if buried, and any property whose zakat has not been paid, even if not; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 217}

We stop here at the foot of p. 217, in the middle of the commentary on verse 34 (within report 16650); the next batch begins at p. 218. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 217}
