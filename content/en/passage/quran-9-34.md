## Summary
Verse 34 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 34 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 34 (pp. 216-227)**

Al-Tabari says: believers who affirmed God and His messenger and acknowledged the oneness of their Lord, many of the scholars and readers of the Children of Israel among Jews and Christians "eat the wealth of the people falsely", taking bribes in their judgments, distorting God's book and writing books with their own hands and then saying "this is from God", taking a small price for it from their lowly; "and bar from the way of God" means they prevent whoever wants to enter Islam from entering it by forbidding him. Report 16648, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the rabbis are of the Jews, the monks of the Christians, and God's way is Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 216}

He goes on: and with them eat it too those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in God's way; so give the many of the rabbis and monks who eat people's wealth falsely, and those who hoard gold and silver without spending it in God's way, the news of a painful punishment on the day of resurrection, a punishment from God that hurts. The scholars differed on the sense of "hoarding". Some said it is any property on which zakat is due and the zakat is not paid, "not spending it in God's way" meaning not paying its zakat. Report 16649, from Ibn Umar, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Wahhab, Ayyub and Nafi': any property whose zakat you have paid is not a hoard even if buried, and any property whose zakat has not been paid is the hoard God mentioned in the Qur'an, with which its owner is branded even if it is not buried. The editor notes that al-Tabari gives Ibn Umar's report by several routes in differing wording, as Ibn Umar's own saying, which is the right form, that its chain is sound, and that Malik gives it in the same sense in the Muwatta'. Report 16650, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd, Sa'id ibn Maslama, Isma'il ibn Umayya and Nafi': any property whose zakat has been paid is not a hoard even if buried, and any property whose zakat has not been paid, even if it is not buried, is a hoard. The editor notes that al-Hasan ibn al-Junayd al-Balkhi was al-Tabari's teacher and that Sa'id ibn Maslama ibn Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik was weak in hadith, so the chain is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 218}

Report 16651, from Ibn Umar, through Abu al-Sa'ib, Ibn Fudayl, Yahya ibn Sa'id and Nafi': any property whose zakat has been paid is not a hoard even if buried in the ground, and any property whose zakat has not been paid is a hoard with which its owner is branded even if it is on the face of the earth; the editor says al-Bayhaqi gives it and calls it the sound form as Ibn Umar's own saying, while Suwayd ibn Abd al-Aziz, who is not strong, raised it to the Messenger. Report 16652, from Ibn Umar, through Ibn Waki, his father, Jarir, al-A'mash and Atiyya: what zakat has been paid on is not a hoard; report 16653: even if under seven earths, and what has not had its zakat paid is a hoard even if in the open; report 16654, from Ikrima: what zakat has been paid on is not a hoard. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 218}

Report 16655, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: those who hoard gold and silver are the people of the qibla, and a hoard is what has not had its zakat paid even if on the surface of the earth and however little, whereas a great deal on which zakat has been paid is not a hoard. Report 16656, from Amir al-Sha'bi, through Ibn Waki, his father, Isra'il and Jabir: asked whether wealth on a shelf between heaven and earth whose zakat is unpaid is a hoard, he said it is branded with on the day of resurrection. Others said any property above four thousand dirhams is a hoard whether or not zakat was paid on it. Report 16657, from Ali, through Ibn Waki, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, Abu Husayn, Abu al-Duha and Ja'da ibn Hubayra: four thousand dirhams or less is spending, and more than that is a hoard; reports 16658 and 16659 are the same. The editor says Ja'da ibn Hubayra al-Makhzumi was a Successor born in the Prophet's lifetime, son of Umm Hani', daughter of Abu Talib. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 219}

Others said a hoard is whatever is left over from a person's need. Report 16660, from Abu Mujib, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Ubaydallah ibn Mu'adh, his father, Shu'ba and Ibn Abd al-Wahid: the chape of Abu Hurayra's sword was of silver and Abu Dharr forbade him it, saying the Messenger had said that whoever leaves yellow or white metal is branded with it. The editor says Ibn Abd al-Wahid and the one above him are unknown, that Ahmad gives it and that al-Hafiz mentions it, and explains the parts of a sword. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 220}

Report 16661, from Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd, through Ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal, Sufyan, Mansur, al-A'mash and Amr ibn Murra: when the verse came down the Prophet said three times "woe to gold, woe to silver", which was hard on the Messenger's Companions, who asked what wealth they should keep; Umar said he would find out, and told the Messenger his Companions found it hard and asked what wealth they should keep, and he said a remembering tongue, a grateful heart and a wife who helps one of you in his religion. Report 16662, from Thawban, is the same; report 16663, from Salim ibn Abi al-Ja'd, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri, Mansur and Amr ibn Murra: when the verse came down the Emigrants asked what wealth to keep, and Umar said he would ask the Prophet; I caught up with him on a camel and said the Emigrants asked what wealth to keep, and he said a remembering tongue, a grateful heart and a believing wife who helps one of you in his religion. The editor notes that Salim did not meet Umar and did not hear from Thawban, so the report is weak by a break in the chain, that Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi give it, the latter calling it good, and that al-Bukhari said Salim did not hear from Thawban. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 221}

Report 16664, from Abu Umama, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar, Qatada and Shahr ibn Hawshab: a man of the Suffa died and a dinar was found in his waistcloth, and the Messenger said "a brand"; another died and two dinars were found in his waistcloth, and he said "two brands"; report 16665 is the same. The editor says the chain is sound and Ahmad gives it by several routes. Report 16666, from Thawban, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir, Mansur and Salim: we were on a journey going along with the Messenger, and the Emigrants said they wished they knew which wealth was best to keep, since what had come down about gold and silver had come down; Umar said that if they wished he would ask the Messenger about it, and they said yes; he went off and I followed, hurrying my camel, and Umar said: Messenger of God, when God sent down about gold and silver what He sent down, the Emigrants said they wished to know which wealth is best to keep; he said yes, let one of you take a remembering tongue, a grateful heart and a wife who helps him in his faith. The editor refers to report 16662. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 223}

Al-Tabari then says that the soundest view is that reported from Ibn Umar: any property whose zakat has been paid is not a hoard that its owner is forbidden to hoard, however great, and any property whose zakat has not been paid, however little, leaves its owner liable to punishment and deserving of God's threat unless God favours him with pardon, if it is of the kind on which zakat is due. His reason is that God, by His messenger, made a quarter-tenth due on five ounces of silver and the same on twenty mithqals of gold; so it is known that a great sum, even thousands upon thousands, if it were among the hoards whose owners God threatened with punishment even when its zakat was paid, would not carry the zakat of a quarter-tenth, for what is obligatory to take out entirely and forbidden to hold has as its zakat the removal of all of it to its owners and not a quarter-tenth, like usurped property which the usurper may not keep and must return to its owner. Had what exceeds four thousand dirhams, or what is left over from the owner's unavoidable need, made its owner deserving of God's threat by keeping it even after paying their shares to those entitled, the owner's duty would not be a quarter-tenth but to part with all of it to its owners, as the usurper must return to the owner what he took. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 224}

Moreover, in report 16667, from Abu Hurayra, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar and Suhayl ibn Abi Salih, the Messenger said there is no man who does not pay the zakat of his wealth but on the day of resurrection it is made into plates of fire with which his forehead, brow and back are branded on a day whose length is fifty thousand years until judgment is made among the people, and then he sees his way; if it was camels, he is laid on a wide level plain and they trample him with their feet and bite him with their mouths, the first returning on the last, until judgment is made, and then he sees his way; and if it was sheep, the same, except that they gore him with their horns and trample him with their hooves. The editor says the report is sound, given by Muslim and Ahmad, notes differences in the wording and the manuscript reading, and explains the words for laying down and the plain. In like reports there is clear indication that the threat is on property whose prescribed dues have not been paid to those entitled, not on owning or hoarding it, and that the verse is specific, as Ibn Abbas said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 224}

This is in report 16668, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his grandfather: on "those who hoard gold and silver and do not spend it in God's way, give them news of a painful punishment" he said they are the People of the Book, and said it is specific and general. Al-Tabari explains this: it is specific for Muslims who did not pay the zakat of their wealth, and general for the People of the Book because they are unbelievers whose spending is not accepted. Report 16669, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha, shows it: they are those who do not pay the zakat of their wealth, any property whose zakat is not paid, on the surface of the earth or in its belly, is a hoard, and any property whose zakat is paid is not. Report 16670, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the hoard is what is hoarded away from obedience to God and His obligation; he said prayer and zakat were made obligatory together and not separated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 225}

Al-Tabari says he called it specific because "hoard" in the Arabs' speech is anything gathered one on another, in the belly of the earth or on its surface, citing a line of al-Mutanakhkhil al-Hudhali whose sense is that wheat is stored with him, piled one on another; the editor says the verses describe a hungry lost wanderer whose camels strayed, and explains their words. The Arabs also call a compact body "hoarded" for its parts being pressed together. Since that is the sense of the word and the words "those who gather gold and silver one on another and do not spend it in God's way" are general in the recitation, with no amount stated at which the threat falls, it is known that the specificity was reached only through the Messenger's stopping at it, namely the property whose due to God, the zakat, is unpaid, and no other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 226}

Some Companions used to say it is general for every hoard, though specific to the People of the Book, and that God meant them by it. Report 16671, from Zayd ibn Wahb, through Abu Husayn Abdullah ibn Ahmad ibn Yunus, Hushaym and Husayn: I passed al-Rabadha and met Abu Dharr, and asked him what had brought him to this country; he said he had been in Syria and read this verse about those who hoard gold and silver, and Mu'awiya said this verse is not about us, it is about the People of the Book, and he said it is about both us and them; words rose between them, he wrote to Uthman complaining of him, and Uthman wrote to him to come; he came, and when he reached Medina people crowded him as if they had never seen him before; he complained of this to Uthman, who told him to move aside nearby, and he said that by God he would not give up what he had been saying. Report 16672, from Zayd ibn Wahb, through Abu Kurayb, Abu al-Sa'ib, Ibn Waki, Ibn Idris and Husayn: we passed al-Rabadha, and then he related much the same from Abu Dharr. The editor gives the narrators and says al-Bukhari gives it in his Sahih and Ibn Sa'd in the Tabaqat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 227}
