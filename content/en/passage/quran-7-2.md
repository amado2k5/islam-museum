## Summary
Verse 2 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum

Then, on "a Book sent down to you", he says: God means this Qur'an, Muhammad, is a Book God sent down to you, with "Book" in the nominative in the sense "this is a Book". On "so let there be no constraint in your heart about it", God says to His Prophet: let your chest not be narrowed, Muhammad, from warning with it those you were sent to warn and conveying it to those you were commanded to convey it to, and have no doubt that it is from Me; be steadfast in carrying out God's command and following obedience in what He charged you with and laid on you of the burdens of prophethood, as the resolute among the messengers were steadfast, for God is with you. "Haraj" is narrowness in Arab speech, and he explained its meaning with evidence at [[passage/quran-6-125|6:125]], which spares repeating it; the note corrects the printed wording by the manuscript. He gives (14316) from Ibn Abbas that it means be in no doubt about it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 295}

He gives (14317) and (14318) from Mujahid that "haraj" is doubt; (14319) and (14320) from Qatada, doubt about it; (14321) from al-Suddi that "haraj" is doubt; and (14322) from Mujahid that it is doubt about the Qur'an. Al-Tabari says what the commentators reported is the meaning of what he said about "haraj", for doubt about it arises only from narrowness of the chest and lack of room to turn it toward its correct direction, and he chose to express it as narrowness because that is the predominant sense in Arab speech, as he explained before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 296}

Then, on "so that you may warn with it, and as a reminder to the believers", he says: God means, this is a Book We sent down to you, Muhammad, so that you warn with it those you are commanded to warn, "and a reminder to the believers", which is a case of what is delayed in the wording but advanced in sense, the meaning being a Book sent down to you to warn with and a reminder to the believers, so let there be no constraint in your heart about it. On that sense "a reminder" is accusative, meaning We sent you this Book to warn with and to remind the believers with. Had the sense been, this is a Book sent down to you, so let there be no constraint in your heart about warning with it and reminding believers, its soundness would not be rejected, and on that approach "a reminder" can be parsed two ways: accusative by following the position of "to warn with it", or nominative by coordination with "the Book", as if it said "Alif Lam Mim Sad, a Book sent down to you, and a reminder to the believers". The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 297}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
