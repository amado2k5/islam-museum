## Summary
Verse 149 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 149 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "say: to God belongs the conclusive argument", God tells His Prophet to say to those who speak falsehood against their Lord in their forbidding: if they are unable to produce an argument when you say "do you have knowledge?", and they surely cannot, being short of it because it is false and has no reality, then to God, who forbade you to set up partners with Him and to follow Satan's footsteps in your property of crops and cattle, belongs the conclusive argument against you, idolaters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 211}

"Conclusive" means that the argument reaches what He intends in establishing itself against whoever He argues it against among His creatures, and removes his excuse once it reaches him in the matter in which it is made an argument. "Had He willed, He would have guided you all" means that had your Lord willed, He would have enabled you all to unite on worshipping Him alone, disowning rivals and gods, holding forbidden what He forbade and lawful what He made lawful, leaving Satan's footsteps and other such obedience; but He did not will it, and made His creatures differ as He willed, some unbelievers and some believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 212}

He gives (14132) from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: no one who disobeyed God has an argument, but God has the conclusive argument over His servants; and on "had He willed, He would have guided you all" he said that He is not asked about what He does, while they are asked, as in [[passage/quran-21-23|21:23]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 212}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
