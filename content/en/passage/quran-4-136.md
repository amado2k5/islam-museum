**Commentary on verse 136: belief in God, His Messenger and the Book (pp. 312-314)**

Al-Tabari says the address is to those among the People of the Book who believe in the prophets and messengers before Muhammad: believe in God and in Muhammad, His Messenger sent to you and to all nations before you; believe in the Book sent down to him, which is the Qur'an, and in the Book sent before it, the Torah and the Gospel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 312}

Al-Tabari answers the question of how they can be told to believe when they are called believers: God did not call them believers, but described them as having believed, which is a particular description of assent. There were two kinds: the People of the Torah, who accepted it and rejected the Gospel, the Qur'an and Jesus and Muhammad; and the People of the Gospel, who accepted it and the Torah but rejected Muhammad and the Criterion. So God commanded them to believe in what they professed, and to accept Muhammad, since their own Book commanded that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 313}

The sense of 'whoever disbelieves in God, His angels, His books, His messengers and the Last Day' is, for al-Tabari: whoever disbelieves in Muhammad and denies his prophethood has gone far astray. The whole list is named because denying part is denying all, and no one's belief is valid except in what God commanded them to believe. The editor notes that a clause in the printed text is garbled and the manuscript reading is correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 313}

Al-Tabari says far astray means straying from the right path and turning aside from the road to ruin, because disbelief in these things is leaving the religion God prescribed for His servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 314}
