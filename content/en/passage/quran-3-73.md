## Summary
Verse 73 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 73 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 73: trust only those of your own faith.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: do not trust any except one of your own faith and was a Jew, and that this is God's report of what the group said to their fellow Jews in the previous verse. He says the preposition in the phrase about whoever held your faith is like the one in [[passage/quran-27-72|27:72]], where it means that something came close behind you. He says the interpreters spoke as he does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 511}

**Reports 7246-7248.** In report 7246 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) this is what some of them said to others; report 7247 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same. The printed edition then has a second report with the same number marked with a letter, which can be called 7247 repeated (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): trust only one who adhered to Judaism. In report 7248 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): trust only one who held to your faith, and whoever differs from it, do not trust. The editor says the printed text had a different wording from the manuscript through an added negative, that the right reading adds the conjunction as he has set it, that the verb should be construed with a preposition, and that both copies show a copyist's slip. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 512}

**The second half of verse 73: the views.** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the clauses saying that guidance belongs to God, that another might be granted the like of what they were granted, or that they might dispute with them before their Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 512}

**First view: the first clause is a parenthesis.** Some said the clause stating that guidance is God's is a parenthesis in the middle of the speech, a report from God that the clarity and the guidance are His, while the speech after it joins the first, a report of the Jews' talk among themselves. In their meaning: do not trust any but one of your own faith, and do not credit that anyone will be granted what you were granted, or that anyone will argue against you before your Lord; then God said to His prophet: say, grace is God's alone to grant as He chooses, and the guidance is God's. The editor says the printed text and the manuscript differ in the form of the phrase about the parenthesis and in the case of the word for report, and that he chose the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 512}

**Reports 7249 and 7250.** In report 7249 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) this was envy on the part of the Jews that prophethood might be in others, and a wish to be followed in their religion. Report 7250 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 513}

**Second view: no community is granted the like of what you were granted.** Others said the meaning is: say, Muhammad, guidance is God's and the clarity is God's, and the particle in the clause means a negation, that no one among the communities is granted the like of what you were granted; they cite a parallel sense in [[passage/quran-4-176|4:176]], where it means that you not go astray, and in [[passage/quran-26-200|26:200]] and [[passage/quran-26-201|26:201]], where it means that they not believe. They say that the like of what was granted is what you, Muhammad, and your community were given of Islam and guidance, and that the word translated "or" in the clause about disputing means "except", so that the sense is: unless they argue with you before your Lord about what He did to them. The editor refers to the fuller treatment of this reading in al-Farra's Maani al-Quran (1: 222-223). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 513}

**Report 7251 (al-Suddi).** Al-Tabari gives (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) that God told Muhammad: say that guidance is God's, that another be granted what you were granted, community of Muhammad, or they dispute, and the Jews will say God did such and such honour for us until He sent down manna and quails on us; the answer is that what I gave you is better, so say that grace is God's to grant as He chooses. On this reading the whole speech is a command from God to His prophet to say to the Jews, joined one part to the next with no parenthesis, the second "guidance" is an answer to the first, and the particle stands in the nominative as the predicate of "guidance". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 513} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 514} The editor says the printed text and the manuscript differ in the phrase about the whole speech, that the publisher, seeing it did not run, tried to mend it, and that the near-certain correction adds the word "command", which he thinks the copyist dropped because its writing resembles the word after it; he infers it from the opening of the next paragraph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 513} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 514}

**Third view: a command to the Jews not to envy.** Others said it is a command from God to His prophet to say to the Jews, and its sense is: say, Muhammad, guidance is God's, that anyone among people be granted the like of what you Jews were given of God's book and the like of your prophet, so do not envy the believers for being given the like of what you were given of My grace, for grace is in My hand. The editor says the printed text added a needless preposition. In report 7252 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) when God sent down a book like theirs and sent a prophet like theirs, they envied them; report 7253 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 514}

**Fourth view: the end of the command, and the dispute returns to what came before.** Others said the statement about guidance and about anyone being granted the like of what the Jews were given of God's book is the end of what God told His prophet to say to the Jews in this verse, and that the dispute clause returns to the clause forbidding trust in any but those of their own faith. In their meaning: do not trust any but one of your own faith, lest you abandon the right, for the one whose religion you followed and chose might argue against you with it before your Lord, being in the right, and since you find his description in your scripture; so the dispute clause would depend on an omitted answer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 514} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 515} In report 7254 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) the matter you are on, or that they dispute with you about it before your Lord: they said to one another, do not tell them what God made clear to you in His book, so that they cannot argue with you by it before your Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 515}

**Al-Tabari's choice.** Al-Tabari says the soundest of the views is that the clause stating that guidance is God's is a parenthesis and the rest runs in one course; its sense then is: do not trust any but one of your own faith, and do not credit that anyone will be granted what you were granted, meaning no one will be, or that they will dispute with you before your Lord. The text of the manuscript is then cut off at this point by dots marking a lacuna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 515}

**The editor's notes on p. 515.** He says the words he added between brackets are needed, that the printed text and the manuscript were both unsound here, and that he thinks the copyist, on reaching the clause in the preceding report, let his eye jump to its twin in al-Tabari's own speech, wrote the word for parenthesis after it, and dropped what lay between. He says the printed text had a different verb form for the one who followed the religion and the right one is from the manuscript, and that it had an extra preposition in the phrase on the like of what was granted, again corrected from the manuscript. He says the dots mark a lacuna of which he has no doubt; that the sentence in both the printed text and the manuscript is disordered, which led the first publisher to change it; that al-Tabari's account of the reading he chose is evidently missing here, in which the clause on guidance is restored to its place after the dispute clause; and that he thinks an unpointed word in the manuscript should be read as "envy of what He gave you". He adds that a reader who wished to make the speech run as one could read it: or that they dispute with you before your Lord, from envy of what He gave you, because you are nobler to God than they; but he does not accept this and judges that there is surely a lacuna. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 515}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at the lacuna in al-Tabari's choice on p. 515 and continue from p. 516.
