## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari recites the verse within verses 92 to 94 in a single segment. He explains the phrase "then followed a cause": then he traveled through roads and stations, and took paths "until he reached the distance between the two dams." Reciters differed in reading this; the majority of the reciters of Medina read it as "until he reached between the two dams" with a damma on the 'sin', as well as all other Qur'an with a damma on the 's'. Some Meccan reciters read it with a fatha on all of that. Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' used to open the letter 's' in this surah, and add a damma on the 'sin' in Ya-si', and say: 'Al-sadd' with a fatha means the barrier between you and something. Al-sadd with a damma means what is a membrane in the eye. As for the Kufis, their common recitation throughout the Qur'an with a fatha on the 's' is different from His saying: 'Until it reaches between the two dams,' they specifically included the 's' in that. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Ikrimah, Ibn Jurayj, and Ata.
