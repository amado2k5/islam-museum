## Summary
Surat al-Fatiha: surah 1 in the order of the Mushaf, Meccan, 7 verses, and revelation order 5 among the surahs. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Content
Its verses are recorded in the museum one by one under the records passage/quran-1-‹verse number›.

## In Tantawi's al-Tafsir al-Wasit
[[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Tantawi]] introduces his commentary on al-Fatiha in [[work/al-tafsir-al-wasit|al-Tafsir al-Wasit]] by saying that it is the only sura Islam commands its followers to recite in every prayer and every cycle of prayer, and that, short as it is, it sums up the aims of the religion: the oneness of God, worship, rulings, promise and warning. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.11} He then answers four questions:

- **When it was revealed:** the preferred view among careful scholars is that it came down in Mecca among the earliest revelations; others said it is Medinan, or that it came down twice, in Mecca when prayer was prescribed and in Medina when the qibla was changed. He quotes [[person/al-qurtubi|al-Qurtubi]], who prefers the first view because prayer was prescribed in Mecca and no prayer without al-Fatiha is known to have existed in Islam. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.11} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.12}
- **Its number of verses:** seven; he quotes [[person/ibn-kathir|Ibn Kathir]] that it is seven without disagreement and that those who said eight or six hold anomalous views; see [[concept/add-al-ay|counting the verses]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.12}
- **Its names:** al-Fatiha or Fatihat al-Kitab (the Opening of the Book), because recitation, the written mushaf and the prayers open with it; Umm al-Qur'an or Umm al-Kitab (the Mother of the Qur'an or of the Book), because it sums up the Qur'an's aims — he quotes [[person/al-tabari|Ibn Jarir]] that the Arabs call anything that gathers others a "mother"; al-Sab' al-Mathani (the seven oft-repeated), because it is repeated in prayer; al-Hamd (Praise); al-Kanz (the Treasure); al-Waqiya (the Protector); al-Shifa' (the Cure); al-Kafiya (the Sufficient); al-Asas (the Foundation); and al-Ruqya (the Charm). He notes that al-Qurtubi listed twelve names and al-Suyuti, in *al-Itqan*, twenty-five. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.12} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.13}
- **Its merit:** he cites the hadith of Abu Sa'id ibn al-Mu'alla in Sahih al-Bukhari that it is the greatest sura of the Qur'an and is "the seven oft-repeated and the great Qur'an"; the hadith of [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] in Sahih Muslim on the two lights given to no prophet before, al-Fatiha and the closing verses of al-Baqara; and the hadith of [[person/abu-hurayra|Abu Hurayra]] in Muslim that a prayer without the Mother of the Qur'an is "deficient", with the divine saying that divides the prayer between God and His servant. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.13} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.14}

He then discusses [[concept/taawwudh|seeking refuge]] before recitation, quoting al-Qurtubi that scholars agree the formula of refuge is not part of the Qur'an nor a verse of it, and then [[concept/al-basmala|the basmala]] and the disagreement over whether it is a verse of al-Fatiha. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.14} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.15} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.17}

## In al-Shawkani's Fath al-Qadir
In [[work/fath-al-qadir|Fath al-Qadir]], [[person/al-shawkani|al-Shawkani]] defines fatiha as, originally, whatever something is opened with; the sura is called "the Opening of the Book" because it is the first thing a scribe writes in the mushaf and the first a reciter recites, though it was not the first to be revealed, and it was known by this name in the Prophet's lifetime. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.12} He lists the reports on whether it is Meccan or Medinan; see [[question/where-was-al-fatiha-revealed|Where was Surat al-Fatiha revealed?]]. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.12} On its names he quotes al-Bukhari that it is called the Mother of the Book because its writing begins the mushaf and its recitation begins the prayer; Ibn Sirin, who disliked saying "Mother of the Book" and said "Opening of the Book" instead; and Ibn Kathir that the name "the seven oft-repeated" is sound because it is repeated in every cycle of prayer. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.12} From al-Kashshaf he gives the names "the Treasure", "the Complete", "Praise" and "the Prayer"; he adds that Sufyan ibn Uyayna called it "the Protector", that Abd Allah ibn Yahya ibn Abi Kathir called it "the Sufficient" because it suffices for others and no other suffices for it, and that al-Sha'bi called it "the Foundation of the Qur'an"; then he quotes al-Qurtubi that it has twelve names and Ibn Kathir that it has seven verses without disagreement. {cite:fath-al-qadir-dar-al-marifa p.12}
