**Commentary on verse 30: "his soul led him to kill his brother, and he killed him and became one of the losers", pp. 220-224**

Al-Tabari says "led him" means it yielded to him and helped him in it, a form from the root of obedience. The interpreters differed: some said it means his soul emboldened him to kill his brother, as reports 11742, 11743 and 11744, from Mujahid, say; others said it adorned it for him, as report 11745 from Qatada says. The editor corrects the chain of report 11742.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 220}

They then differed over how he killed him and why. One group said he found him asleep and crushed his head with a rock. Report 11746, from al-Suddi, from several Companions, says he sought him to kill him and the boy evaded him on the mountaintops; one day he came on him asleep as he grazed his flock on a mountain, lifted a rock, crushed his head and left him in the open.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 221}

Report 11747, from Ibn Jurayj, says the killer did not know how to kill him, so Iblis appeared to him as a bird, took a bird, cut off its head, then set it between two stones and crushed its head, teaching him killing. Report 11748, from Ibn Jurayj, says he killed him where he grazed the sheep; he did not know how, so he twisted his neck and held his head, and Iblis came down, took a creature or bird, laid its head on a stone and struck it with another while the killer watched, and he did the same to his brother. Report 11749, from Mujahid, is similar.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 222}

Report 11750, from Ibn 'Abbas, says that when the fire consumed the accepted offering, the other said to his brother: will you walk among people who know your offering was accepted and mine rejected? He said he would kill him, and his brother warned him of the Fire, but he did not desist, and his soul led him on.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 222}

Report 11751, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Uthman ibn Khuthaym, says that while throwing pebbles with Sa'id ibn Jubayr he was told from Ibn 'Abbas that a man was forbidden to marry his twin sister, and might marry another of his sisters; each birth gave a boy and a girl, one girl was beautiful and the other plain, and the plain one's brother asked to exchange sisters but was refused; they offered, the ram's owner's was accepted and the farmer's was not, and he killed him. That ram stayed kept with God until brought out as the ransom of Isaac, and was slaughtered on the rock at Thabir by the house of Samura al-Sawwaf, on your right as you throw the pebbles. Ibn Jurayj says others told the same story, and that Adam's children kept to this until four fathers had passed, when a man married his cousin and sister-marriage ended.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 223}

Al-Tabari's choice is that God reported that the killer killed his brother and we have no report settling how; it may have been as al-Suddi says or as Mujahid says, God knows which, but the killing is certain. "He became one of the losers" means he became of the party of those who sold their hereafter for their world and lost in their dealing.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 223}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
