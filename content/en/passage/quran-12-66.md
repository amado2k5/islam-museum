**Commentary on verse 66 (pp. 163-164)**

On "he said, I will never send him with you until you give me a pledge from God that you will bring him back to me, unless you are encompassed; and when they gave him their pledge he said, God is a guardian over what we say", al-Tabari says: Ya'qub said to his sons: I will not send your brother with you to the king of Egypt until you give a pledge from God, that is, give me a covenant, meaning an oath and agreement by which one is bound, that you will bring your brother to me, unless something encompasses all of you so that you are unable to bring him to me. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "mithaq" and "ihata". The interpreters said the same: reports 19480 and 19481, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, and through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa, both from Ibn Abi Najih: "their pledge" means their covenant. Report 19482, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa: "unless you are encompassed" means unless you all perish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 163}

Reports 19483 and 19484, from Mujahid, are the same. Report 19485, from Qatada, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Ma'mar: "unless you are encompassed" means unless you are overpowered so that you cannot do it. Report 19486, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: unless some matter befalls you that takes all of you away, and that would be your excuse with me. On "when they gave him their pledge", he says: when they gave him their covenants, Ya'qub said "God is a guardian over what we say", I and you, that is, He is witness over us that we keep what we all say. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "wakil". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 164}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
