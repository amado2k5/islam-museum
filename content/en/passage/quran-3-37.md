## Summary
Verse 37 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 37 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## Verse 37 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 344-348)

**Mary's acceptance and growth.** Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-37|3:37]]) says the sense is that God accepted Mary from her mother Hanna, and accepted her dedicating her to the church and to her service and the service of her Lord, with a fair acceptance. He explains that the word for acceptance is a verbal noun not matching the verb's form, and that if it did it would be the other form; the Arabs often bring verbal nouns on the root of the verbs even where the verb's pattern differs, as in saying 'so-and-so spoke speech', and the same for the verb for growth, which does not take the matching noun. The editor refers to the earlier explanation. Al-Tabari reports that Abu Amr ibn al-Ala said (6900, through Abu Ubayd, from al-Yazidi) that he never heard Arabs put a damma on the qaf of 'qabul', though analogy, like 'dukhul' and 'khuruj', would call for it, and that he knew no other word like it. As for her growth, he says it means her Lord made her grow in His nourishment and provision until she became a complete woman. Al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj (6901) says He accepted from her mother what she intended for the church and her reward in it, and that she grew in God's nourishment. The editor notes the printed text has a different preposition and that he prefers 'and her dedicating her', joined to 'accepted Mary'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 345}

**The two readings of the verb for guardianship.** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed: most reciters of the Hijaz, Medina and Basra read it with the letter lightened, meaning that Zechariah took her to himself, considering what God said in [[passage/quran-3-44|3:44]] about their casting their pens to see which of them would be Mary's guardian; most reciters of Kufa read it doubled, meaning that God made Zechariah her guardian. Al-Tabari chooses the doubled reading, that God joined her to Zechariah, because Zechariah too took her to himself by God's decree through the lot God brought out for him and the sign He showed his opponents, which made him more entitled than they, since he won the draw against those who contended. The editor explains 'qara'a' as winning the lot against others and 'shahha' as contending over something, neither wishing it to pass him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 345}

**The account of the lot.** Al-Tabari reports that it reached them that Zechariah and his opponents, when they contended over which of them should have Mary with him, drew lots with their arrows and threw them into the river Jordan. Some scholars said Zechariah's arrow stuck fast and stood while the water did not carry it, and the water carried the others' arrows away, and God made that a sign for Zechariah that he was the most entitled of the contenders. Others said that Zechariah's arrow rose up against the current while the arrows of the others went down with the flow and were lost, which was a sign from God that he was the most entitled of them. Al-Tabari adds that whichever it was, it was without doubt God's judgement for Zechariah against his opponents; so Zechariah took her to himself because God joined her to him by deciding for him against them when they contended. This account is given as it reached al-Tabari, with no museum verdict. The editor discusses the manuscript readings of 'irtazza' (which means to be fixed, as a knife fixes in a wall, where the first publisher wrote another word), of 'a sign', which the copyist wrote and overwrote so that the first publisher dropped it, and of 'rose', whose alif the publisher dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 346}

**His answer to the argument for the light reading.** Al-Tabari says the better of the two readings is the doubled one, and that the argument of those who read it lightened from the other verse about casting pens shows the weakness of their device: it is not impossible for a sensible person to say 'so-and-so made so-and-so a guarantor and so-and-so became guarantor'; likewise their casting pens to see which would take charge of Mary, by God's making him guarantor through the decree He decided between them when they cast pens. The editor says the printed text has 'choice' where the manuscript has the right reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 347}

**The readings of the name Zechariah.** Al-Tabari says Medinan reciters read it with the lengthened ending and Kufan reciters with the short; both are known dialects and widespread readings with no difference in meaning, so whichever the reciter uses is right. But in his view, when lengthened it is put in the accusative without tanwin, being a foreign name not declined, and, since he reads the verb doubled, it is accusative as the object. There is a third form that may not be recited because it contradicts the Muslims' codices, dropping the lengthening and the silent ya and treating it like a relative adjective, tanwin and all cases following the relative ya. The editor refers to al-Farra's remark on the name in Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 347}

**The sense of guardianship, and the line of poetry.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God joined her to Zechariah, citing a line of a poet of which the sense is that a man is, for the straying beasts, a taker-in, meaning one who gathers to himself what strays or is scattered of the herds; the line is also reported with a variant word meaning those that bolt and run from the herd, from the saying of 'the ostrich' for swift flight. One says to a man 'why do you take in every stray?' meaning gather it to yourself and seize it. The editor says he cannot recall the poet though he remembers the line, explains the herd-camel term, notes that in a report on Uthman he appointed Abu Ghadira over the stray camels, and refers to Tabaqat Fuhul al-Shu'ara (p. 490). Al-Tabari then says the commentators spoke much as he did. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 348}

**Zechariah's pen in the current.** Report 6902 (Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Aswad al-Tufawi, from Muhammad ibn Rabi'a, from al-Nadr ibn Arabi, from Ikrima) says they cast their pens and the current carried them all off except Zechariah's, which rose, and so he took her in. The editor notes that the word for 'rose' in both printed and manuscript texts makes no sense and refers back to p. 346; he explains the word for the current as the state of flowing, what modern writers call the stream. Report 6903 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says he joined her to himself and that they cast their staffs toward the current of the water, and Zechariah's staff overcame the current and won the draw; the editor thinks the word for 'faced' is doubtful and suggests a verb for 'rose above' or 'overpowered', citing Lisan al-Arab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 349}

**The report of al-Suddi.** Report 6904 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says her mother took her in her swaddling cloth to the sanctuary when she was born (others say when she reached it); those who wrote the Torah would cast lots over a person brought to them for upbringing, and Zechariah was the best of them that day, with Mary's maternal aunt as his wife. When they drew lots over her, Zechariah said he had the best claim as her aunt was with him; they refused and went to the river Jordan and cast the pens with which they wrote, to see whose would stand and so take her in. The pens flowed away, Zechariah's stood on its tip as if in mud, and he took the girl and kept her in his house, which is the sanctuary. The editor's notes: he judges that the word in the text meaning 'testing' was originally one meaning 'dedicating' (set free for service), relying on al-Bayhaqi's Sunan and al-Suyuti's al-Durr al-Manthur; says this report is in the Sunan through al-Suddi's chain, the one that recurs in the commentary, and that al-Tabari cut what follows it for length; holds that the 'sister of Mary' of some versions is an error, since it is established in history that Zechariah and Imran were married to two sisters, the mother of John and the mother of Mary, and Imran died while Mary's mother was pregnant (he refers to al-Tabari's History); explains that Zechariah meant the sister of Mary's mother; and defines the 'tip' as a projecting end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 349} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 350}

**Reports of Qatada and Mujahid.** Report 6905 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says 'joined her to himself'. Report 6906 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says he drew lots against them with his pen; 6907 is the same from al-Muthanna through Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. Report 6908 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdullah ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from Qatada) says Mary was the daughter of their master and imam, their rabbis contended for her and cast arrows over who should take her in, and Zechariah, husband of her sister, took her and she stayed with him; the editor says Qatada's words are abridged and mean the husband of the sister of her mother. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 350}

**Reports of Ikrima, Ibn Abbas, Ibn Ishaq and al-Hasan.** Report 6909 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza, from Ikrima and Abu Bakr, from Ikrima) says Mary's mother took her in her swaddling cloth to the sons of al-Kahin, son of Aaron the brother of Moses, who then served at Bayt al-Maqdis as the keepers serve at the Kaaba. She told them this was her vowed child whom she had dedicated, that a menstruating woman does not enter the church, and that she would not take her home. They said she was the daughter of their imam (Imran led them in prayer) and keeper of their offering. Zechariah asked that she be handed to him, her aunt being with him; they said their hearts would not allow it, so they cast lots with the pens with which they wrote the Torah, Zechariah won, and he took her in. The editor notes a blank in the manuscript after the word for 'keeper', finds the printed supplement sensible, and takes the clause in brackets to be the narrator's aside. Report 6910 (Ibn Abbas through Yala ibn Muslim and Said ibn Jubayr) says Zechariah kept her with him in his sanctuary, and Ibn Jurayj adds that 'kahin' in their language means the learned man; the editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. Report 6911 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says the guardianship came after her father and mother, the text recalling her orphanhood before telling her story and Zechariah's. Reports 6912 and 6913 (Said ibn Jubayr) say she was with him in his sanctuary, and 6914 (al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi and Abbad) says the people drew lots, Zechariah won, and took her in. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 351} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 352}

**The other view: guardianship without lots.** Al-Tabari reports that others said Zechariah took her in after Hanna bore her, without lots or dispute, because her mother died after her father while she was a small child and Zechariah's wife, her aunt al-Ishba daughter of Faqudh, was with him; it is also said that the name of the mother of John, aunt of Jesus, was Ashba. Report 6915 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Wahb ibn Sulayman, from Shu'ayb al-Jaba'i) gives the name of John's mother as Ashba. The editor comments that the printed edition wrote the name another way and that the manuscript and al-Tabari's History are right; that the People of the Book have it as Elizabeth, meaning 'God is her oath', apparently the old Hebrew name of Aaron's wife; and he gives biographical notes on Wahb ibn Sulayman al-Jundi al-Yamani and on Shu'ayb al-Jaba'i (Ibn Abi Hatim says he is Shu'ayb ibn al-Aswad, who narrates from books). They said he joined her to her aunt, mother of John, she stayed with them until grown and then was brought into the church for her mother's vow, and the casting of pens came long after, in a time of hardship, when Zechariah was too weak for her upkeep and they passed her from one to another, not out of desire or rivalry. Al-Tabari says he will give her story on this view when he reaches it. Report 6916 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq) is similar. Al-Tabari then says that on this interpretation the light form of the verb would be sound, were the interpretation sound, but the commentators lean to the first view, that the lots came before Zechariah's guardianship and he took her by his arrow winning over his rivals' (the editor glosses the word as 'the winner'); so his own preference is the doubled reading. This is al-Tabari's choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 352} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 353}

**The provision Zechariah found with her.** Al-Tabari turns to the next clause of the verse: whenever Zechariah went in to her in the sanctuary, after placing her there, he found provision from God for her nourishment, said to be winter fruit in summer and summer fruit in winter. Report 6917 (Abu Kurayb, from al-Hasan ibn Atiyya, from Sharik, from Ata, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says he found grapes in a basket out of season (the editor explains the basket as a large container for dates or grapes); 6918 is the same from Said through Ibn Humayd, Hakkam, Amr and Ata. Report 6919 (Ibrahim) says fruit out of season; 6920 (al-Dahhak, through Yaqub, Hushaym and Abu Ishaq al-Kufi) says summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer, and the editor says Abu Ishaq al-Kufi is Abdullah ibn Maysara, weak in hadith, and quotes Ibn Hibban that it is not lawful to argue from his reports. Further reports of al-Dahhak (6921-6923, through Ibn Waki, al-Muthanna and al-Qasim) and of Mujahid (6924-6926, grapes out of season; 6927 through Ibn Waki and al-Nadr ibn Arabi, summer and winter fruit) follow. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 354} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 355}

**More reports on the provision.** Report 6928 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says 'we were told' she was brought winter fruit in summer and summer fruit in winter, and 6929 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) says fruit out of season. Report 6930 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says Zechariah put seven doors between himself and her, and still found winter fruit in summer and summer fruit in winter. Report 6931 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says he kept her with him in a room, which is the sanctuary, and found summer fruit in winter and winter fruit in summer; the editor says the narrator's name 'Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman' is strange and he nearly asserts a scribal slip for Musa ibn Harun al-Hamdani, who recurs in al-Suddi's chain. Report 6932 is from al-Dahhak, and 6933 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Yala ibn Muslim, from Said ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says he found the fruits of Paradise. Report 6934 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from some scholars) is alike. Report 6935 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says it was provision from heaven, from God, not from people, and some said that had Zechariah known it was from her he would not have asked her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 355} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 356}

**Another view: a surplus beyond what was brought.** Al-Tabari says others held that Zechariah found with her a surplus of provision beyond what used to sustain her in those days. Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, says he took her after her mother's death and joined her to her aunt, mother of John, until she was grown and they brought her into the church for her mother's vow, and she grew and flourished; then a famine struck the Children of Israel until Zechariah was too weak to carry her, and he went out and told them he could no longer bear the daughter of Imran; they said they had suffered likewise, so they passed her back and forth until they cast pens and the arrow fell on a carpenter of the Children of Israel named Jurayj. Mary would tell him to think well of God, for God would provide for them; he provided at her place and brought her each day what sufficed from his earnings, and when it entered the church God increased and multiplied it, so Zechariah would see with her a surplus beyond what Jurayj brought and ask her where it came from, and she would answer that it was from God. The editor notes that the manuscript has a blank in the wording about their hardship and that the printed wording is good. These are reports of the Israelite type, given as transmitted; the museum gives no verdict on their details. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 356} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 357}

**The sanctuary in al-Tabari.** Al-Tabari begins his account of the word 'mihrab': it is the front of every assembly and place of prayer, the chief, noblest and most honoured of assemblies, and so too in mosques. He cites a line of Adi ibn Zayd describing women, likening them to ivory figures in sanctuaries or to ostrich eggs in a garden bright with blossom. The editor explains the line's words (eggs, garden, blossom), records the differences between the manuscript and the printed wording and says he leans to the familiar printed version. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 357}

**The plural of the sanctuary, and Zechariah's question.** Al-Tabari says the plural of 'mihrab' is 'mahārīb' and it may also be 'mahārib'; the editor says the language authorities did not state the second plural but it is an accepted analogy, and refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. Al-Tabari then turns to the rest of the verse: Zechariah asked Mary from what source came the provision he saw with her, and she answered that it was from God, meaning that God sent it to her and gave it. He asked because, as is reported, he would lock seven doors on her and leave, then return and find winter fruit in summer and summer fruit in winter, and marvel and ask her, and she would give that answer. Report 6937 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi), 6938 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from some scholars) are alike, and 6939 (Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) says he found fresh fruit with her when no one had fruit, so Zechariah would ask her. The editor refers to earlier discussion of the question-word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 359}

**Provision without reckoning.** Al-Tabari says the closing clause of the verse is God's report that He sends provision to whom He wills without count or number for which His servant is called to account, since sending it neither lessens His treasuries nor does giving add anything to His dominion, and nothing of what He provides is hidden from Him; only one who fears a loss to his dominion, or is ignorant of what he gives, keeps a reckoning. The editor notes that the manuscript has a blank in this sentence, that the printed edition dropped what preceded and joined the two parts, and that he supplied a word in the blank from the context and from the treatment of the sentence elsewhere, with a reference to earlier passages. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 359}
