**Commentary on verse 62 (pp. 329-330)**

Al-Tabari says to the believers: these hypocrites swear to you, believers, by God, to please you concerning what reached you of their hurting the Messenger of God, their mention of him with slander and fault-finding, and their secret siding with the unbelievers against you, by God and by false oaths, that they did not do that and that they are of your religion and with you against those who oppose you, seeking by that your approval. God, glorious is His praise, says "God and His Messenger are more deserving that they please Him", by repentance and return from what they said and spoke, "if they are believers", that is, if they affirm the oneness of God and acknowledge His promise and warning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 329}

Report 16906, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that a man of the hypocrites said "by God, these are our best and our nobles, and if what Muhammad says is true, they are worse than donkeys", and a Muslim heard it and said "by God, what Muhammad says is true, and you are worse than a donkey", and the man went to the Prophet of God with it, and he sent for the man and called him and said "what made you say what you said?", and the man began to call a curse on himself and to swear by God that he had not said it; the Muslim kept saying "O God, confirm the truthful and give the lie to the liar", and God sent down this verse. The editor explains that the verb for the man's calling a curse means he cursed himself or called fairly against himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 329}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
