## Summary
Verse 141 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 141 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 141, "it is He who raised gardens, trellised and untrellised", al-Tabari says this is God's notice of the favour He showed them, an alert to the place of His kindness, and a teaching of what He made lawful and forbidden and the dues He allotted from their property to those He gave a due in it. He says: your Lord, people, "raised", meaning brought into being and originated as a creation, "gardens", meaning orchards, "trellised", which is the vines people trained on trellises, "and untrellised", not raised or built up, which people neither plant nor raise but God raises, plants and makes grow. He gives (13955) Ibn Abbas: trellised means raised up; (13956) from him: the trellised is what people trellised and the untrellised is the fruits that come out in the wilds and mountains; (13957) al-Suddi: "gardens" are orchards and the trellised is what was trellised like the vine; and (13958) Ata' al-Khurasani from Ibn Abbas: what of vines is trellised and what is not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 155}

Then "and the palm and crops of varied taste, and the olive and pomegranate, alike and unlike; eat of their fruit when it fruits". Al-Tabari says: He raised the palm and crops of varied produce, "akl" meaning the fruit, that is, differing in what comes from them that is eaten of fruit and grain; "the olive and pomegranate, alike and unlike" in taste, some sweet, some sour, some mixed (the note says "mazz" is between sweet and sour). He gives (13959) Ibn Jurayj: alike in appearance and unlike in taste. "Eat of their fruit when it fruits" means eat of its fresh fruit while fresh; (13960) and (13961) from Muhammad ibn Ka'b and Musa ibn Ubayda: of its fresh dates and grapes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 157}

Then "and give its due on the day of its harvest". The commentators differed on its interpretation. Some said this is a command from God to give the obligatory charity from fruit and grain; he gives (13962) al-Hasan: the zakat; (13963) Anas ibn Malik: the obligatory zakat; (13964) and (13965) Ibn Abbas: the tenth and the half-tenth; and (13966) Ibn Abbas from his father: the zakat. The notes give biographies of the transmitters in these chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 158}

Al-Tabari continues the reports that "give its due on the day of its harvest" is the obligatory zakat: 13967, from Jabir ibn Zayd, the zakat; 13968 and 13970, from al-Hasan, it is the charity from grain and fruit; 13969, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, the obligatory charity; 13971, from Ibn Abbas, its obligatory zakat on the day it is measured or its measure is known; and 13972, another report attributed to Ibn Abbas, of which the editor says in a note that the chain "Ibn Abbas from his father" is problematic and he suspects the right reading is "from Ibn Tawus from his father". The notes also give biographies for the transmitters of report 13967. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 159}

Report 13972 says that when a man had sown and the day of harvest came, when he knew its measure and due, he gave one in ten and what people gleaned from its ears. He relates (13973) from Qatada that its due on the day of harvest is the obligatory charity, and it is mentioned that the Prophet of God laid down for what the sky or a running spring watered, or dew, or what was rain-fed, the full tenth, and if watered with a bucket and rope, a half-tenth; this applies to what is measured of the fruit, when the fruit reaches five awsaq, which is three hundred sa', then zakat is due on it, and they liked to give from the unmeasured fruit in proportion. The notes explain "ba'l" and "awsaq". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 160}

Further: 13974, from Qatada and Tawus, it is the zakat; 13975, from Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya, the day of its measuring, he gives the tenth or half-tenth; 13976, from him, the tenth and half-tenth; 13977, from Ibn Tawus from his father and from Qatada, the zakat; 13978 and 13979, from Ibn Abbas, the tenth and half-tenth; 13980, from al-Dahhak, the day it is measured, whether wheat, dates or raisins, its due being its zakat; 13981, from Ibn Zayd, eat of it, and when you reap it give its due, its due being its tithes; 13982 and 13983, from al-Hasan, the zakat when you measure it; and 13984, from Ibn Zayd ibn Aslam, the tithes, from his father and others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 161}

Others said that it is a right God made due on the property of owners, other than the obligatory charity. He relates (13985) from Ja'far ibn Muhammad from his father (in his book "from Ali ibn al-Husayn"): something besides the obligatory due; (13986) from Ata': a handful of the food; (13987) from Ata': of the palms, grapes and all grain; (13988) that Ibn Jurayj asked Ata' about fruits he had harvested, and he said they too are given from, from everything you harvest, palm, grape, grain, fruit, vegetables or cane, and that this is an obligation on people in all of it, with nothing set and known in it; and (13989) from Ata': he gives something easy to those present that day, and it is not the zakat. The note explains the manuscript reading "huder" for the present. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 162}

More: 13990, from Ata', it is not the zakat but feeding those present at that moment; 13991, from Hammad, they used to give fresh dates; 13992, from Mujahid, when the poor are present you throw them some of it, when you have cleaned it and begin measuring you scoop them a handful, when you know its measure you set aside its zakat, and when you begin cutting the palms you throw them some of the stalks; 13993, from him, apart from the obligation. The notes explain "hatha", "jidad al-nakhl" and "thafariq", and say Mujahid meant the little left on the cluster. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 163}

Report 13994, from Mujahid: something is thrown to the beggars from the ears at the harvest; when it is plastered it is thrown to them; when he carries it to heap it up it is thrown to them; when he threshes he feeds from it; when finished and he knows its measure he sets aside its zakat; and for palms, at the cutting he feeds from the fruit and stalks, at measuring from the dates, and when finished he sets aside its zakat. Report 13995 is similar: when the crop is reaped some is thrown from the ear, when the palm is cut some is thrown from the stalks, and when he measures it he pays zakat; 13996 and 13997, from him: at the reaping, the threshing and the picking he takes a handful for them, and when he measures it he sets aside its zakat (apart from the zakat); 13998, from him: something besides the zakat at the harvest and cutting; 13999, from Ibn Abi Najih: obligatory when it is picked. A note says the editor could not make out the meaning of "tuyyina" and found the report nowhere else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 164}

More reports that the due is besides the zakat: 14000, from Mujahid, when he reaps he feeds, and when he brings it to the threshing floor and threshes it he feeds from it; 14001, from Ibn Umar, the one who comes asking is fed, besides what is given as the tenth and half-tenth (the note says the "mu'tarr" is one who goes around to you seeking what you have); 14002, from Mujahid, a handful at reaping and a handful at the cutting; 14003, from Ibn Sirin, they gave to whoever came to them; 14004 to 14007, from Ibrahim, the "dighth" (the note: a handful of mixed grass or the like); and 14008, from Abu Ja'far and from Ibrahim, a handful is given. The note describes the confusion in this chain and the editor's correction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 165}

He relates (14009) from Yazid ibn al-Asamm that when the palms were cut a man would bring a bough from his palm and hang it at the side of the mosque, and the poor man would come and strike it with his staff and eat what fell from it; the Messenger of God came in with Hasan or Husayn, who took a date, and he took it out of his mouth, for he and his household did not eat charity; that is "give its due on the day of its harvest". Report 14010, from Maymun ibn Mihran and Yazid ibn al-Asamm: the people of Medina at the cutting brought a bough and put it in the mosque, and the beggar came and struck it with his staff so that some fell; and 14011 is similar. A note gives the editor's remarks on the chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 166}

More: 14012, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, the gleaning of the ears (the note: what the sickles miss and people pick up); 14013 and 14014, from Mujahid, they hung a bough in the mosque at the cutting and the weak ate of it, and he feeds something at the cutting; 14015, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the handful and what falls from the ears; 14016, from him, the fodder; 14017, from him, this was before the zakat, for the poor, a handful, and the bundle as feed for his animal; 14018, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b, little or much of it; and 14019, from Ibn Abi Najih, at reaping a handful is given, and at the cutting a handful is given, and they are left to follow the traces of the cutters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 167}

Others said this was something God commanded the believers before the fixed charity was made obligatory for them, and the known charity then abrogated it, so there is no obligation in property of whatever kind, crop or planting, except the charity God made obligatory in it. He gives (14020) and (14021) Ibn Abbas: the tenth and half-tenth abrogated it; (14022) Ibn al-Hanafiyya the same; (14023) Sa'id ibn Jubayr: this was before the zakat, and when the zakat came down it abrogated it, so they gave the handful; and (14024) Ibrahim: they did that until the tenth and half-tenth were laid down, and when they were laid down it was dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 168}

More: 14025 to 14027, from Ibrahim, it is abrogated, the tenth and half-tenth abrogated it; 14028, from al-Hasan, the zakat abrogated it; 14029, from al-Suddi, the zakat abrogated it; 14030, from Ibrahim, this surah is Meccan and the tenth and half-tenth abrogated it, and when asked on whose authority he said, the scholars'; and 14031 is similar. The note gives a biography of Shabbak al-Dabbi, the blind Kufan, of whom Ahmad said he was a reliable shaykh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 169}

He gives (14032) al-Suddi: whenever anyone passed them on the day of reaping or cutting they fed him from it, and God abrogated that for them with the zakat, the tenth and half-tenth applying to what the earth grew; (14033) al-Hasan: they used to give small gifts to their idolater relatives; and (14034) Atiyya: the tenth and half-tenth abrogated it; they used to give when they reaped and when they winnowed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 170}

Al-Tabari's choice: the soundest saying is that of those who said this was an obligation God laid on the believers in their food and fruit that their crops and plantings bring forth, and that God then abrogated it with the obligatory charity and the known assessment of the tenth and half-tenth. All are agreed, without dispute, that the charity on grain is taken only after threshing, cleaning and winnowing, and that the charity on dates is taken only after they dry on the tree (the note says the printed text altered the wording, and that to say a palm "ajazza" is that its fruit dried and it was time to cut). That being so, and since "give its due on the day of its harvest" indicates a command to give its due on the day of harvest, which is the day of cutting it, when the grain is still in the ear and the dates not yet fully dried, while the charity on grain is taken only after threshing, winnowing and measuring, and the charity on dates only after they are fully dry, this is the ground of his reasoning; the argument continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 170}

Al-Tabari completes his argument: what is taken as charity some time after the harvest is therefore different from what must be given to the poor on the day of harvest. If someone asks, why do you deny that this is a right God made due in property besides the obligatory charity, the answer is that it can only be either a binding obligation or a voluntary gift. If it is a binding obligation, its course must be that of the obligatory charities, whose neglect makes the owner sinful and opposed to his Lord's command, but the proof stands that God has no obligation in property after the zakat that is due as the zakat is due, other than the maintenance of those a person must support, which shows it is not so. If it is voluntary, the choice of giving it must lie with the owner of the crop and fruit, and the insistence of those who say it is obligatory shows it is not so. When the verse is excluded from meaning a recommendation and cannot be made to yield a binding obligation at this time, it is known to be abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 171}

What supports this is that God followed "give its due on the day of its harvest" with "and do not be wasteful; He does not love the wasteful". It is known that in God's ruling for His servants, since He made the charity obligatory on their property with a fixed measure, the one who collects it is their rulers and guardians; so what sense is there in forbidding the owner of the property to be wasteful in giving it, when the collector compels and takes only the right God made obligatory in it? If someone thinks it is a prohibition to the official who collects from guardians against overstepping into the owner's property, the end of the verse "do not be wasteful" is joined to its beginning, so if the one forbidden to waste is the collector, then the one commanded to give would have to be the one forbidden to waste, namely the ruler; and that is a saying outside the view of all the commentators and against the usual form of address, which is evidence enough of its error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 172}

If someone says, why do you deny that "give its due on the day of its harvest" means give its due on the day it is measured, not the day it is cut or severed, or picked, since you know who said this (14035, from al-Dahhak, the day of its measuring; 14036, from Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya, the day of its measuring, he gives the tenth and half-tenth)? The reply is that the day of measuring is not the day of harvest, and the meaning of those who say this must be one of two: either they turned "harvest" to the sense of "measure", which is not understood in Arab speech since "hasad" and "hasd" mean cutting and severing, not measuring; or they turned the interpretation to "give its due after the day of its harvest, when you have measured it", which is against the apparent sense of the revelation, as the command in its apparent sense is to give the due from it on the day of harvest, not after it. There is no difference between one who says it meant after the day of harvest and another who says before, since the apparent sense contradicts both. The note fixes the spelling "qasl" with qaf as cutting a plant while green. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 173}

Then "do not be wasteful; He does not love the wasteful". The commentators differed on the waste forbidden by this verse and on who is forbidden. Some said the one forbidden is the owner of the palms, crop and fruit, and the waste is going beyond the measure in giving to the point of ruining the owner. He gives (14037), (14038) and (14039) from Abu al-Aliya: they used to give something besides the zakat and then outdid one another in waste (the note: "tasarafu" means they excelled one another in waste), so God sent down "do not be wasteful"; and (14040) from Ibn Jurayj, that it came down about Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas, who cut his palms and said no one will come today but I will feed him, and he fed until evening and had no fruit left. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 174}

He relates (14041) from Ata', he forbids waste in everything, and recited "who neither waste nor stint" (25:67); (14042) from Iyas ibn Mu'awiya, when people in Kufa asked what waste is: whatever falls short of God's command is waste (the note discusses the wording, and says Lisan al-Arab reports from Iyas that waste is whatever falls short of God's right); and (14043) from al-Suddi: do not give away your wealth and become poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 175}

Others said the waste forbidden in this place is withholding the charity and the right God commanded the owner to give to those entitled in "give its due on the day of its harvest"; he gives (14044) Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: do not withhold the charity and so disobey (the note says Abu Bakr ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Sabra is discarded as a transmitter, and Ahmad said he forged hadith and lied), and (14045) Muhammad ibn Ka'b: waste is not giving where due (the note says Musa ibn Ubayda al-Rabadhi is weak). Others said the address is to the ruler, who is forbidden to take from the owner more than God laid on his property; he gives (14046) Ibn Zayd: He told the ruler, do not be wasteful, do not take without right, so this verse was between the ruler and the people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 175}

Al-Tabari's choice: God forbade by "do not be wasteful" all the senses of waste, singling out none. Since waste in Arab speech is missing the mark of right in giving, either by exceeding its limit in excess or by falling short of the required limit, it is known that one who scatters his wealth in rivalry, and one who gives people until his giving ruins him, is wasteful by passing God's limit to what is not his, and likewise one who falls short in giving what God obliged him to give, such as withholding from those entitled to shares of charity when it is due on it, or withholding from those whose maintenance God made his duty, his family and dependants; and likewise the ruler who takes from his subjects what God did not allow him to take. All these are wasteful in what they do. The editor notes that he put a phrase in brackets to complete a sentence that is not intelligible in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 176}

Al-Tabari says a verse may come down to the Messenger of God because of a particular matter while its ruling applies generally, and so do most verses of the Qur'an, and so it is with "do not be wasteful; He does not love the wasteful", though what precedes it is a command to give what is due to its people on the day of harvest; it forbids the wastefulness of giving in a way that ruins the giver. He cites in support of the sense of "israf" a line of Jarir, "they gave a hundred camels driven by eight; in their giving is neither reproach nor israf", where israf means a mistake in giving. The editor notes that at this point the ninth part of his manuscript ends, with the copyist's closing note saying the writing was finished in Jumada I of the year 715 of the Hijra (about 1315 CE) and that the tenth part begins with the commentary on "and of the cattle, load-bearers and bedding". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 177}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
