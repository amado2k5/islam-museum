## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and they said, none will enter Paradise unless he is a Jew or a Christian", al-Tabari says the sense is that the Jews said none will enter Paradise except a Jew, and the Christians said none except a Christian, the two parties being joined in the report because the meaning is clear to those addressed. On "hud" there are two views: that it is a plural of "ha'id" (the one who repents and returns to the truth), like "'ud" of "'a'id", or a verbal noun for the group, like "rajul sawm"; he mentions a view that it is "yahud" with its ya dropped, and that in Ubayy's reading it is "yahudiyyan aw nasraniyyan" (the editor disputes in a note his statement about the verbal noun). On "those are their wishes" he says it is a report of their saying that these are wishes they entertain of God without right, proof or demonstration, from Qatada and al-Rabi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 507} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 508}

On "say: bring your proof, if you are truthful" he says God ordered His Prophet to call these to a just matter between all the parties, which is to bring evidence for their claim; "burhan" is the exposition, the proof, the evidence, from Qatada, al-Suddi, Mujahid and al-Rabi'. He holds that the words are outwardly a call to bring proof but in sense a denial by God of their claim, since they cannot ever produce proof, as "yes, whoever submits his face to God while doing good" shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 509} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 510}
