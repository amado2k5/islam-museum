**Commentary on verse 205 (pp. 353-357)**

Al-Tabari says: "remember", you who listen in silence to the Qur'an when it is recited in prayer or a sermon, "your Lord in yourself", meaning take admonition from what is in the verses of the Qur'an, take lesson from it and recall your return to Him when you hear it; "humbly" meaning do that in submission to God and humility before Him; "and in fear" meaning in fear of God that He may punish you for a failing on your part in taking admonition and lesson from it and for heedlessness of the limits He set out in it; "and without raising the voice in speech" meaning and a calling on God with the tongue in secret, not aloud, so that your remembrance of God while you listen to the Qur'an be, if you call on Him, not aloud but in secret speech. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir rejected al-Tabari's view, and that of Abd al-Rahman ibn Zayd ibn Aslam before him, that the listener to the Qur'an is commanded to remember in this way, saying it is far-fetched and contrary to the silence commanded, and that the aim is to urge servants to much remembrance morning and evening so as not to be among the heedless; the editor holds this the right view. He refers to the earlier explanations of "humility", "fear" and "aloud". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 353}

Report 15619, from Ibn Zayd: he does not say it aloud; report 15620, from Mujahid: they were commanded to remember Him in their breasts, humbly and in fear; report 15621, from Ubayd ibn Umayr: God says, when My servant remembers Me in himself I remember him in Myself, when My servant remembers Me alone I remember him alone, and when he remembers Me in a company I remember him in a better and more honoured one; report 15622, from Ibn Jurayj: humility in supplication and meekness are commanded and raising the voice, calling out and shouting in prayer are disliked. The editor identifies Ibn al-Taymi as Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, Hayyan ibn Umayr al-Qaysi as trustworthy and of few hadiths, and Ubayd ibn Umayr ibn Qatada al-Jundai as the storyteller of the people of Mecca, a trustworthy Follower. As for "in the mornings and evenings", it means early mornings and late afternoons; "asal" is a plural, and the people of Arabic differed over it: some said it is the plural of "asil", as "aymaan" is of "yamin" and "asraar" of "sarir"; others said it is the plural of "usul", which is itself the plural of "asil"; others said it is the plural of "asl" and "asil", and one may take it as a plural of "asil" or as a singular, the Arabs saying "the asl has drawn near" as a singular. Al-Tabari chooses that it is the plural of "asil" and "usul", since both may be pluralised on the pattern "af'al", and "al-asal" is, it is said in Arab speech, the time between the afternoon and the sunset. The editor explains "sarar", plural of "asrar", as the bed of the truffle, the earth and husks upon it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 354} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 355}

As for "and be not among the heedless", he says: do not be of those who divert themselves when the Qur'an is recited from its admonitions and lessons and the wonders in it, but ponder it and understand it and make your heart feel it through remembrance of God, submission to Him and fear of God's power over you should you be heedless of it. Report 15623, from Ibn Zayd: "in the mornings and evenings" means early morning and evening; report 15624, from Mi'raf ibn Wasil that he heard Abu Wa'il say to his servant at sunset, have we entered the asil yet? The editor identifies Mi'raf ibn Wasil al-Sa'di as trustworthy, imam of the mosque of the Banu Amr ibn Sa'd for sixty years without ever forgetting in prayer, and Abu Wa'il Shaqiq ibn Salama al-Asadi as one who lived in the Prophet's time without seeing him and is an authority in Arabic. Report 15625, from Mujahid: "the morning" is the end of the dawn, the dawn prayer, and "the evenings" the end of the afternoon, the afternoon prayer, each having its time, the beginning of dawn and its end, like His words in Al Imran, [[passage/quran-3-41|3:41]]; and it is said "the evening" is the sun's inclining until it sets and "the dawn" is the first of daybreak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 355} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 356}

Report 15626, from Ibn Abbas that he was asked about a prayer… and he recited [[passage/quran-24-36|24:36]]; the editor explains that the report stands so in the manuscript and printed edition, that he almost certainly holds it an error and corruption with a gap, that he did not find the report with its chain, and found its text without chain in al-Durr al-Manthur about the forenoon prayer, not the dawn prayer, which is right, and that he has put the manuscript's words in brackets. Report 15627, from Qatada: God commanded remembrance of Him and forbade heedlessness; "in the mornings" is the dawn prayer and "the evenings" the evening time. The editor identifies Muhammad ibn Sharik al-Makki as trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 356} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 357}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
