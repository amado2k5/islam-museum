## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 15 to 18 as a single section. He interprets the phrase “So say, ‘Would you like to purify yourself?’” as meaning: “Say to him: Would you like to cleanse yourself from the defilement of disbelief and believe in your Lord?” Similarly, Ibn Zayd said, regarding the phrase “Would you like to purify yourself?”: “Would you like to submit?” He also said: “Purification throughout the Quran means Islam.” He recited God’s words, “And that is the reward of whoever purifies himself,” meaning whoever submits to Islam. He also recited, “And what will make you know? Perhaps he will purify himself,” meaning he will submit to Islam. And he recited, “And it is not upon you if he does not purify himself,” meaning if he does not submit to Islam. On the authority of al-Hakim ibn Aban, on the authority of Ikrimah, regarding Moses' words to Pharaoh: "Would you be willing to purify yourself?" meaning, would you be willing to say, "There is no god but God"? The reciters differed in their reading of the word "purify yourself." The majority of the reciters of Medina read it as "tazakka" with a shaddah on the zay, while the majority of the reciters of Kufa and Basra read it as "ila an tazakka" with a sukun on the zay. The passage also includes statements attributed to Mujahid, Qatadah, al-Hasan, Ibn Zayd, and Sufyan.
