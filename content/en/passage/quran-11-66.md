**Commentary on verse 66 (pp. 373-380)**

On [[passage/quran-11-66|11:66]] al-Tabari says: when Our punishment came to Thamud We saved Salih and those who believed with him 'by mercy from Us', by blessing and favour from God, 'and from the disgrace of that day', meaning We saved them from the abasement and shame of that day through that punishment; 'your Lord is the Strong' in His seizing, destroying whatever He seizes as He destroyed Thamud when He seized it, 'the Almighty', whom no overcomer overcomes and no subduer subdues, but who overcomes and subdues everything. Report 18289, from Qatada: God saved him by mercy from Him and saved him from the disgrace of that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 374}

Report 18290, from Amr b. Kharija, from the Messenger of God about Thamud: Thamud were the people of Salih, whom God gave long life in the world until one of them would build a house of clay that collapsed while he was still alive; seeing that, they took houses in the mountains, carving, boring and hollowing them out. They said, Salih, ask your Lord to bring forth a sign by which we will know you are God's messenger; Salih prayed and God brought out the she-camel, whose drinking day was one and theirs another, appointed; on her day they left her to the water and milked her enough to fill every vessel and skin, and on theirs they turned her from the water and she drank nothing, while they filled every vessel. God inspired Salih that his people would hamstring the she-camel; he told them, and they said we would never do it; he said, if you do not, a child born among you is about to; they said what is the sign of that child, by God we will kill any we find; he said, a fair, blue-eyed, reddish boy. In the city were two strong, protected elders, one with a son he thought above marriage and one with a daughter for whom he found no match; they married them, and the child was born. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 374}

There were in the city eight men who spread corruption and did no good; when Salih said a child born among you will hamstring her, they chose eight midwives from the village and put police with them, who went about the village, and when they found a woman in labour they looked at her child, turning over a boy to see what he was and turning away from a girl. When they found that child the women screamed, this is the one the Messenger of God Salih means; the police wished to seize him but his two grandfathers came between them and said, had Salih meant this one we would have killed him. He was the worst of children, growing in a day as others grow in a week, in a week as others in a month. The eight corrupters gathered, with the two elders among them, and said, put this boy in charge of us, for his rank and his grandfathers' standing, so they were nine. Salih did not sleep among them in the village; he stayed in a mosque called Salih's mosque, where he spent the night, and in the morning came and preached to them and reminded them, and at evening went out to his mosque and spent the night in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 375}

Hajjaj said, and Ibn Jurayj said: when Salih told them a boy would be born by whose hand their destruction would come, they said, what do you tell us to do? He said, I tell you to kill them, and they killed them all but one. When that child grew up they said, if we had not killed our children each of us would have one like this; this is Salih's doing, and they plotted to kill him, saying, let us go out as travellers with the people watching us openly, then return on such-and-such night of such a month and lie in wait at his place of prayer and kill him, so that people think only that we are travelling. They came and went under a rock to lie in wait for him, and God sent the rock down on them and crushed them, and they were crushed by morning. Some men who knew of it went and found them crushed, and returned; the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 375}

The rest of the report: they would shout in the village, servants of God, is Salih not content to have ordered them to kill their children until he killed them? and all the villagers agreed to kill the she-camel and held back from her only that son of the tenth. The report then returns to the Messenger of God's: they wished to plot against Salih, so they went to a tunnel on Salih's road and eight hid in it, saying when he comes out we shall kill him and go to his family and fall on them by night; God ordered the earth and it levelled over them. They gathered and went to the she-camel standing at her trough, and the wretched one said to one of them, go and hamstring her; he went, but it seemed too great and he turned from it; he sent another, who found it too great, and he sent none but that the matter seemed great to him, until they walked to her and he reached up and struck her hamstrings and she fell kicking. A man of them came to Salih and said, come to the she-camel, she has been hamstrung; he came, and they went out to meet him and excuse themselves, saying, prophet of God, so-and-so hamstrung her, we are not at fault; he said, see if you can catch her foal, for if you catch it perhaps God will lift the punishment from you; they went out to look for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 376}

When the foal saw its mother thrashing it went to a short mountain called al-Qara and climbed it, and they went to take it, but God inspired the mountain and it rose in the sky until birds could not reach it. Salih entered the village, and when the foal saw him it wept until its tears ran, then faced Salih and bellowed, then bellowed again and again, and Salih said to his people, for each bellow a day's term, 'enjoy yourselves in your home three days, that is a promise not belied'; the sign of the punishment is that on the first day your faces turn yellow, on the second red and on the third black. In the morning their faces were as if smeared with saffron perfume, small and great, men and women; at evening they all cried out, a day of the term has passed and the punishment is upon you; on the second morning their faces were red. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 376}

as if dyed with blood, and they shouted, clamoured and wept, knowing the sign of the punishment; at evening they all cried out, two days of the term have passed and the punishment is upon you. On the third morning their faces were black as if smeared with tar, and they all cried, the punishment is upon you, so shroud yourselves and embalm; their embalming was aloes and myrrh and their shrouds hides, and they threw themselves on the ground, turning their eyes, looking once to the sky and once to the earth, not knowing from where the punishment would come, from above in the sky or beneath their feet from the earth, in anguish and fear. On the fourth morning a cry came to them from the sky containing the sound of every thunderbolt and every thing on earth that has a sound, and their hearts were torn in their breasts, and in the morning they were prostrate in their homes. The editor says the narrators of this report are weak, that al-Hakim said Shahr b. Hawshab alone related it and it has no other chain, that al-Dhahabi said Abu Bakr is flimsy, and he judges it a weak and broken report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 377}

Report 18291, from Ibn Jurayj: I was told that when the cry seized them God destroyed all of them between the east and west but one man who was in God's sanctuary, whom God's sanctuary kept from God's punishment; he was asked who, Messenger of God, and said Abu Righal. When the Messenger of God came to the village of Thamud he said to his companions, none of you is to enter the village or drink of its water, and he showed them where the foal climbed in al-Qara; from Ibn Umar, that the Prophet said, do not enter on these punished people unless you are weeping, and if you are not weeping do not enter on them lest what struck them strike you; and from Jabir b. Abd Allah, that the Prophet at al-Hijr praised God and said, do not ask your messenger for signs; these were Salih's people, who asked their messenger for a sign, and he sent them the she-camel, which came down from this pass and went out by that pass and drank their water on her day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 378}

Report 18292, from Qatada: it was told us that the Prophet of God, passing the valley of Thamud on his way to Tabuk, ordered his companions to hurry on and not to halt in it nor drink its water, and told them it is a cursed valley; and it was told us that the well-off man of Salih's people used to give the poor man what to shroud himself in, and each dug a grave for himself and his family for the time Salih had promised them; and one who saw them told that they were on the roads, in courtyards and in houses, young men and old, whom God left as a lesson and a sign. Report 18293, from Abu al-Tufayl: when the Messenger of God made the Tabuk expedition he halted at al-Hijr and said, people, do not ask your prophet for signs; these were Salih's people who asked their prophet to send a sign, and God sent them the she-camel, which came in on them on her day from this pass and drank their water, while on their day they took provision from it and then milked her for milk; then she went out by that pass. They rebelled against their Lord's command and hamstrung her, so God promised them the punishment after three days, a promise not belied, and destroyed all of them in the east and west of the earth but one man in God's sanctuary, whom the sanctuary kept from God's punishment; they asked who is that man, Messenger of God, and he said Abu Righal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 380}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
