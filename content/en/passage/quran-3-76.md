## Summary
Verse 76 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 76 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 76: fulfilling the covenant and fearing God.** Al-Tabari says this is God's report of what belongs to the one who discharged his trust to the one who entrusted him, out of fear of God and awareness of Him. God said it is not as these liars against God among the Jews say, that they have no blame or sin in the property of the unlettered; rather, whoever fulfilled his covenant, which is the charge He gave them in the Torah to believe in Muhammad and what he brought. The editor says the printed text and the manuscript differ in one phrase, that his reading is the manuscript's and entirely right, and that the two words in the accusative are objects of purpose. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 525} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 526}

**The pronoun in the covenant.** Al-Tabari says the pronoun on the word for covenant refers to the name of God in the preceding clause. The meaning is: whoever fulfilled God's covenant made in His book, believed Muhammad and affirmed him and what he brought from God, including discharging a trust to the one who entrusted it and the rest of God's commands and prohibitions, and feared God, avoiding what He forbade of disbelief in Him and the other sins He made unlawful, out of watchfulness for His warning and fear of His punishment; for God loves those who fear Him and are wary of His punishment, who avoid what He forbade them and obey Him in what He ordered. He notes that it has been related from Ibn Abbas that he said it is the avoidance of associating others with God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 526}

**Report 7277.** In report 7277 (al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from Muawiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) the sense is that he guarded himself against associating others with God, and that God loves those who guard themselves against it. Al-Tabari says he has set out the interpreters' disagreement on this and the right view with its proofs earlier in his book, enough to spare repeating, and the editor refers to earlier places on the covenant and on fearing God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 526} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 527}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
