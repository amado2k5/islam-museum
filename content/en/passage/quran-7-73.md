## Summary
Verse 73 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 73 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on [[passage/quran-7-73|7:73]], al-Tabari says: Thamud is Thamud son of Ghathir son of Iram son of Sam son of Noah, brother of Jadis son of Ghathir; their dwellings were al-Hijr between the Hijaz and Syria, reaching to Wadi al-Qura and what lies around it, and the sense is: to the Banu Thamud, their brother Salih. The editor notes how the manuscripts differ on the spelling "Ghathir". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 524}

The name Thamud is left without nunation because it is a tribe's name, like Bakr and Tamim. Salih then told his people: worship God alone, who has no partner; you have no other god it is lawful to worship, and a proof has come to you for the truth of what I say. My proof is this she-camel, which God brought out of this rock as evidence of my prophethood; you know it is among the miracles that none but God can do. Salih cited the camel because they themselves had asked for it as a sign. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 525}

(14810) from Abu al-Tufayl: Thamud said to Salih, bring us a sign. He told them to go out to a rise of land; they went and it heaved like a woman in labour, then split and the she-camel came out from its middle. Salih said this was God's she-camel, with a day's drink for her and a day's for them; and when they hamstrung her he told them to enjoy their homes three days. Abd al-Aziz adds from another man that Salih gave the sign of the punishment: they would wake on the first day red, on the second yellow and on the third black. When it came, they embalmed themselves and made ready. The editor comments on Abd al-Aziz ibn Rufay and Abu al-Tufayl, explains "tahannut" (using burial perfumes), and notes that the number 14811 was left out of the numbering by oversight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 526}

(14812) from al-Suddi: God sent Salih to Thamud; they denied him, asked for a sign, and he brought the she-camel with her share of water and theirs on a known day. They all admitted her, but out of dissimulation. When she drank she passed between two mountains, whose marks of her remain to this day, and then she stood for them to milk, and the milk satisfied them. She had a foal with her. Salih told them a boy would be born in that month at whose hands they would perish. Nine of them had sons and killed them; the tenth refused, and his son grew quickly, blue-eyed and ruddy. The nine were angry at Salih and swore by God to attack him and his family by night and then tell his kinsman they had not witnessed their destruction, as in [[passage/quran-27-49|27:49]]. They entered a cave to kill him as he went to the mosque at night, but the cave fell on them and killed them, which is what [[passage/quran-27-48|27:48]] to [[passage/quran-27-51|27:51]] point to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 526}

The tenth man's son grew up and sat with men drinking; they wanted water to mix with their wine and found the camel had drunk it that day. They were angry and said that if they could take that water they could water their herds and fields. The boy offered to hamstring her and they agreed, and what they had hidden of their religion came out. He hid behind a rock on her path; they drove her towards him, and as she passed they called to him, and he took hold of her and hamstrung her so that she fell; [[passage/quran-54-29|54:29]] alludes to this. They defied their Lord's command and said, Salih, bring us what you promise. Some of them ran to Salih and told him the camel was hamstrung; he said, bring me the foal. They found it on a rise, and it climbed with them up into the sky and they could not reach it. Then the foal cried out (the report is not yet finished). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 527}

When the foal cried out to God, God revealed to Salih that he should tell them to enjoy their homes for three days, with the sign that their faces would be yellow on the first day, red on the second and black on the third, and the punishment on the fourth. When they saw the signs they shrouded themselves, embalmed themselves, smeared themselves with myrrh, put on hides, and dug burrows and entered them to wait for the cry, until the punishment came and they perished; [[passage/quran-27-51|27:51]] points to this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 528}

(14813) from Ibn Ishaq: when God destroyed Ad, Thamud settled the land after them, was made successor in it and spread, then defied God and worshipped others, so God sent them Salih as a messenger. They were Arabs, and he was among the middle of them in lineage and the best in standing. Their dwellings ran from al-Hijr to Qurh, the market of Wadi al-Qura, eighteen miles apart between the Hijaz and Syria. He called them as a young man until he grew grey and old, and only a few weak people followed him. When he pressed them and warned them often, they asked for a sign confirming what he said. He asked which sign they wanted. They said: come out with us to our festival, a day in the year when they went out with their idols; you will pray to your God and we to ours, and if you are answered we follow you, and if we are answered you follow us. He agreed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 528}

They went out with their idols and Salih with them, and they prayed to their idols that he would not be answered in anything. Then Junda ibn Amr, the chief of Thamud at the time, said: Salih, bring out for us from this rock, a lone rock on one side of al-Hijr called al-Katiba, a she-camel that is "mukhtarija", pregnant and thick-furred ("mukhtarija" means like the Bukht camels). Thamud said the same, and promised to believe if he did it. Salih took their pledges to believe in him if God did it, and they gave their covenants, and he prayed to his Lord. The editor explains "mukhtarija", "bukht" and "natuj" (pregnant). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 529}

Ibn Ishaq then reports from Yaqub ibn Utba that they watched the rise labour with the she-camel as a pregnant animal labours with her young, then shake and split open on a she-camel just as they had described, pregnant and thick-furred, what lay between her flanks known only to God. Junda and those of his clan with him believed, and the nobles of Thamud wanted to believe, but Dhu'ab ibn Amr, al-Hubab the keeper of their idols, and Rubab ibn Sam'ar, themselves nobles, turned them back from what Salih called them to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 529}

Junda had a cousin, Shihab ibn Khalifa, a noble and a man of standing, who wanted to become Muslim; the same group forbade him and he obeyed them. A Muslim of Thamud, Muhawwis ibn Anama, composed verses saying that a band of the house of Amr had called Shihab, the lord of Thamud, to the Prophet's religion and he had been about to answer; had he answered, Salih would have been mighty among us, but the misguided of the house of Hijr turned away after finding guidance. The editor gives the sources of the verses in Ibn Kathir and al-Thalabi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 530}

So the she-camel stayed in Thamud's land with her foal, grazing and drinking, and Salih told them this was God's she-camel, a sign, to be left to eat on God's earth. God revealed to him that the water was divided between them, one day for them and one for her. She came every other day and put her head into a well in al-Hijr called the Camel's Well, and did not raise it until she had drunk all the water in the valley, then raised her head and spread her legs and they milked as much as they liked, drinking and storing until they filled all their vessels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 530}

Then she left by a different gap from the one she came in by, because it was too narrow for her to go back. The next day was theirs and they drank as they pleased and stored for her day, so they lived in ease. In the heat she stood on the high ground of the valley and their herds fled from her down to the valley floor, and in winter she stood in the valley floor and their herds fled up to the high ground in cold and barrenness. That harmed their herds, as a trial and test; it weighed on them, they defied their Lord's command and resolved to hamstring her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 531}

Two women of Thamud were the fiercest in enmity and unbelief toward Salih: Unayza daughter of Ghanm, called Umm Ghanm, an old wealthy woman with beautiful daughters and herds, wife of Dhu'ab ibn Amr; and Sadouf daughter of al-Muhayya, a chief of Banu Ubayd, one of the most beautiful and richest of people. They schemed to have the camel hamstrung because it harmed their herds. The editor notes variant spellings of their names. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 531}

Sadouf was married to a cousin, Sintim ibn Harawa of Banu Hulayl, who became Muslim and a good one; she had entrusted him with her wealth, and he spent it on those who followed Salih until it thinned. When she found out she rebuked him, and he showed her his religion and called her to Islam, but she refused, took his sons and daughters and hid them among Banu Ubayd. He demanded them; she proposed arbitration before Banu Sanan or Banu Jundu, he proposed Banu Mirdas, who had been quick to accept Islam, and she refused any but those she had named. Banu Mirdas said: by God, you will hand over his children willingly or not, and seeing that she gave them to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 532}

Then Sadouf and Unayza set their plot against the camel. Sadouf asked al-Hubab, offering herself if he did it, and he refused; then her cousin Musda ibn Mihraj, to whom she offered herself, and he agreed. Unayza asked Qudar ibn Salif of Qurh, a short red-and-blue-eyed man said to have been born of another man than the one he was named for, but strong and protected among his people, and offered him any of her daughters he wished. Qudar and Musda stirred up the troublemakers of Thamud and seven followed, making nine, among them Huwayl ibn Mylagh, Qudar's maternal uncle, Du'ayr ibn Ghanm, Da'b ibn Mihraj, brother of Musda, and five whose names were not kept. The editor notes a gap in the manuscript here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 533}

They lay in wait as she returned from the water, Qudar hiding by one rock on her path and Musda by another. Musda shot her with an arrow in the calf muscle. Unayza came out and told her daughter, one of the most beautiful, to unveil herself to Qudar and urge him on; he charged the camel with his sword and struck her hock, and she fell and cried out once, warning her foal; then he stabbed her at the throat and killed her. The foal ran to a high mountain, called Sanw, and took refuge at a rock on its top. Salih came and saw the camel hamstrung and said: you have violated God's sanctity, so hear the news of His punishment. Four of the nine followed the foal and Musda shot it in the heart, and they dragged it down and threw its flesh in with its mother's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 534}

When Salih told them to expect the punishment they mocked: when, and what is the sign? They named the days Awwal (Sunday), Ahwan (Monday), Dubar (Tuesday), Jubar (Wednesday), Mu'nis (Thursday), al-Aruba (Friday) and Shiyar (Saturday), and had hamstrung the camel on Wednesday. Salih said: you will wake on Mu'nis, Thursday, with yellow faces, then on Friday with red faces, then on Saturday with black faces, and on the Sunday, Awwal, the punishment will come on you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 534}

The nine who had hamstrung her then said: let us kill Salih; if he is truthful we hasten him before us and if he lies we join him to his camel. They came by night to fall on him with his family, and the angels crushed them with stones. When they were late their fellows came to Salih's house, found them crushed, said Salih had killed them and meant to kill him, but his kin stood before him with weapons and said: you will never kill him, for he promised the punishment in three days; if he is truthful you add only God's wrath, and if he lies you can still do what you want. They left. These nine are those God mentions in [[passage/quran-27-48|27:48]] to [[passage/quran-27-52|27:52]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 535}

From that night their faces were yellow, so they were sure of the punishment and knew Salih had told them the truth, and they sought to kill him. He fled until he took refuge with a branch of Thamud, Banu Ghanm, staying with their chief Nufayl, called Abu Hudb, a polytheist, who hid him so that they could not find him. They turned on Salih's followers and tortured them to make them reveal him, and one of them, Midda ibn Haram, said: Prophet of God, they torture us to make us tell them where you are, shall we tell? He said yes, and Midda told them. They went to Abu Hudb and spoke to him, and he said Salih was with him and they had no way to him, so they turned away, and what God sent down on them of punishment kept them from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 535}

They began to tell one another what they saw in their faces: on Thursday their faces were yellow, on Friday red and on Saturday black. On the night of Sunday Salih left them with those who had become Muslim and went to Syria, settling at Ramla in Palestine. Midda ibn Haram stayed behind and lodged at Qurh with its chief Amr ibn Ghanm, who had eaten of the camel's meat but had not shared in killing her. Midda told him to leave the town, since Salih had said whoever stayed would perish and whoever left would be saved; Amr answered that he had not joined in hamstringing her and was not pleased with what was done. On the morning of Sunday the cry took them and none, small or great, remained, except a crippled girl called al-Zurayaa who was an unbeliever and fiercely hostile to Salih. God freed her legs after she had seen the whole punishment and she ran faster than anything seen, reached the people of Qurh and told them what she had witnessed, then asked for water, was given it, and died when she drank. The editor discusses the reading of her name in the printed edition and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 536}

(14814) from al-Hasan: when Thamud hamstrung the camel her foal went off and climbed a hill and said, Lord, where is my mother? then cried out, and the cry came down and silenced them; (14815) from him the like; (14816) from Qatada: Salih told them when they hamstrung the camel to enjoy three days, the sign of their destruction being yellow, then red, then black faces, and so it was; on the third day, sure of destruction, they shrouded and embalmed themselves, then the cry took them. Qatada also said the one who hamstrung her told them he would not kill her until all agreed, so they went in to each woman in her private place and asked, are you content? She said yes, and likewise the boy, until all were content, and then he hamstrung her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 537}

(14817) from Jabir ibn Abd Allah: when the Prophet passed al-Hijr he said, do not ask for signs, for the people of Salih asked for them. The camel came in by this gap and went out by that one; they defied their Lord's command and hamstrung her. She drank their water one day and they drank her milk the next. The cry took them, and God silenced everyone of them under the sky's dome except one man who was in God's sanctuary, Abu Rughal; when he left the sanctuary he was struck as his people were. The editor notes the standing of Abd Allah ibn Uthman ibn Khuthaym, that Ahmad related the report, that Ibn Kathir and Ibn Hajar cite it, and that its chain is good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 537}

(14818) from Ismail ibn Umayya: the Prophet passed the grave of Abu Rughal and said, this is the grave of Abu Rughal, a man of Thamud who was in God's sanctuary, which kept God's punishment from him; when he left he was struck as his people were and he was buried here with a branch of gold buried with him, and the people hurried with their swords and dug and took the branch out. (14819) from al-Zuhri that Abu Rughal was the father of Thaqif; (14820) from Jabir like the earlier report. The editor notes that 14818 is a mursal report, that Abu Dawud transmits a connected version from Abd Allah ibn Amr, and that Ibn Kathir fears the attribution to the Prophet is a slip because its narrator Bujayr ibn Abi Bujayr is known only through this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 538}

(14821) from Qatada: it used to be said that the red man of Thamud who hamstrung the camel was of illegitimate birth; (14822) from Abu Musa that he came to the land of Thamud and measured the camel's path out and found it sixty cubits; (14823) from Maamar and Ismail ibn Umayya like Jabir's report: the Prophet passed the grave of Abu Rughal, who was Abu Thaqif, with the gold branch. Al-Hasan said the camel had a day and they had a day, which harmed them. Another report printed with the same number 14823, from al-Zuhri: when the Prophet passed al-Hijr he said, do not enter the dwellings of those who wronged themselves unless weeping, lest what struck them strike you; then he said this is the valley of the nafar, covered his head and hurried until he crossed the valley. The editor takes "valley of the nafar" to mean the nine who agreed to kill Salih and traces the report to al-Bukhari and Muslim, where that phrase does not occur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 539}

As for "and do not touch her with harm", it means do not touch God's camel by hamstringing or slaughter, and "a painful punishment will seize you" means a pain-giving one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 540}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
