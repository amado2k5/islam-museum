## Summary
Verse 9 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 9 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Start of the commentary on verse 9: what the fear refers to (pp. 19-21, reports 8707-8715).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed. Some said: let those who attend a testator fear that they would command him to scatter his wealth by bequeathing it to those who do not inherit from him; rather let them command him to leave his wealth to his children, as they would be glad, were they the testators, if those attending urged them to keep their wealth for their children and not leave them dependent in their weakness and inability to manage and make a living. In the editor's note, both witnesses have a wrong form at the start of the sentence and he put the correct one, and he refers to the earlier commentary on fear, offspring and the weak (the volume and page numbers in those references are hard to read). Ibn Abbas (8707, through Ali ibn Dawud from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha): this is about a man who is dying and someone hears him make a bequest that harms his heirs; God commanded the one who heard to fear God and to guide him to what is right, and to look to the man's heirs as he would want done for his own if he feared they would be lost; Ibn Abbas (8708): it means the man whom death attends and who is told to give in charity from his wealth, free slaves and give in God's way, and they were forbidden to command him thus, that is, whoever attends a sick man at death should not command him to spend his wealth on freeing slaves or charity or God's way, but command him to state his wealth and what debts he owes and to bequeath to his kin who do not inherit a fifth or a quarter, saying to them: does not each of you dislike, if he died leaving small, weak children, to leave them without wealth to be dependents on others? So you should not command him to do what you would not accept for yourselves and your children, but say what is right; Qatada (8709, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh): whoever attends a dying man should command him to justice and kindness, forbid him injustice and wrong in his bequest, and fear for his family what he would fear for his own family had death come to him; Qatada (8710, through Abd al-Razzaq from Mamar): when you attend a dead man's bequest, command him what you would command yourself of what draws near to God, and fear in it what you would fear for your own weak ones if you left them after you, so fear God and say what is sound if he swerves (the editor says the printing added a possessive letter to one word and the manuscript is plainly right, and that the word for the weak means the young); al-Suddi (8711, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat): a man is dying and people attend the bequest, and it is not proper for them to say to him, bequeath all your wealth, send ahead for yourself, God will provide for your family, nor to let him bequeath all of it; as one of you fears for his family if he died leaving them small, weak and with nothing, so let him fear for the family of his Muslim brother and say the sound word to him (the editor says the wording in both witnesses does not work, so he preferred another form and allowed a second); Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8712, through Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Habib): I went with al-Hakam ibn Utayba and asked him, and he said: a man is dying and those present say "fear God, join them, give to them, be good to them"; if they were the ones being commanded to bequeath they would want to keep it for their children (the editor says al-Hakam ibn Utayba al-Kindi has an entry at report 3297, the printing having "Ibn Uyayna", a mistake, and the manuscript undotted); Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8713, through Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Habib ibn Abi Thabit): orphans are present and say "fear God, join them, give them", and if they were in his place they would want to preserve for their children; al-Dahhak (8714, through Yahya ibn Abi Talib from Yazid from Juwaybir): if one of you attends a dying man at his bequest, do not say "free slaves from your wealth, give in charity", so that he scatters his wealth and leaves his family dependents (the editor glosses the plural as the poor and needy), but tell him to write down what is his and what debts are on him, to set aside a fifth of his wealth for his kin, and to leave the rest to his heirs; Mujahid (8715, through Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) begins, and breaks off at the end of p. 21. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 19} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 20} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 21} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
