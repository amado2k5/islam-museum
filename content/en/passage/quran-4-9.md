## Summary
Verse 9 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 9 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Start of the commentary on verse 9: what the fear refers to (pp. 19-21, reports 8707-8715).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed. Some said: let those who attend a testator fear that they would command him to scatter his wealth by bequeathing it to those who do not inherit from him; rather let them command him to leave his wealth to his children, as they would be glad, were they the testators, if those attending urged them to keep their wealth for their children and not leave them dependent in their weakness and inability to manage and make a living. In the editor's note, both witnesses have a wrong form at the start of the sentence and he put the correct one, and he refers to the earlier commentary on fear, offspring and the weak (the volume and page numbers in those references are hard to read). Ibn Abbas (8707, through Ali ibn Dawud from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha): this is about a man who is dying and someone hears him make a bequest that harms his heirs; God commanded the one who heard to fear God and to guide him to what is right, and to look to the man's heirs as he would want done for his own if he feared they would be lost; Ibn Abbas (8708): it means the man whom death attends and who is told to give in charity from his wealth, free slaves and give in God's way, and they were forbidden to command him thus, that is, whoever attends a sick man at death should not command him to spend his wealth on freeing slaves or charity or God's way, but command him to state his wealth and what debts he owes and to bequeath to his kin who do not inherit a fifth or a quarter, saying to them: does not each of you dislike, if he died leaving small, weak children, to leave them without wealth to be dependents on others? So you should not command him to do what you would not accept for yourselves and your children, but say what is right; Qatada (8709, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh): whoever attends a dying man should command him to justice and kindness, forbid him injustice and wrong in his bequest, and fear for his family what he would fear for his own family had death come to him; Qatada (8710, through Abd al-Razzaq from Mamar): when you attend a dead man's bequest, command him what you would command yourself of what draws near to God, and fear in it what you would fear for your own weak ones if you left them after you, so fear God and say what is sound if he swerves (the editor says the printing added a possessive letter to one word and the manuscript is plainly right, and that the word for the weak means the young); al-Suddi (8711, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat): a man is dying and people attend the bequest, and it is not proper for them to say to him, bequeath all your wealth, send ahead for yourself, God will provide for your family, nor to let him bequeath all of it; as one of you fears for his family if he died leaving them small, weak and with nothing, so let him fear for the family of his Muslim brother and say the sound word to him (the editor says the wording in both witnesses does not work, so he preferred another form and allowed a second); Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8712, through Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Habib): I went with al-Hakam ibn Utayba and asked him, and he said: a man is dying and those present say "fear God, join them, give to them, be good to them"; if they were the ones being commanded to bequeath they would want to keep it for their children (the editor says al-Hakam ibn Utayba al-Kindi has an entry at report 3297, the printing having "Ibn Uyayna", a mistake, and the manuscript undotted); Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8713, through Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Habib ibn Abi Thabit): orphans are present and say "fear God, join them, give them", and if they were in his place they would want to preserve for their children; al-Dahhak (8714, through Yahya ibn Abi Talib from Yazid from Juwaybir): if one of you attends a dying man at his bequest, do not say "free slaves from your wealth, give in charity", so that he scatters his wealth and leaves his family dependents (the editor glosses the plural as the poor and needy), but tell him to write down what is his and what debts are on him, to set aside a fifth of his wealth for his kin, and to leave the rest to his heirs; Mujahid (8715, through Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) begins, and breaks off at the end of p. 21. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 19} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 20} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 21}

**End of report 8715 and the second view: forbidding bequests to relatives (pp. 22-23, reports 8715-8718).** Mujahid (8715) continues: this is one who scatters the wealth when it is divided, and those present say "you gave too little, add so-and-so"; so God says let those people fear, and let them say about them, with justice, what one would want said about one's own child, if he gave much: keep for your child. Others said: the sense is that those who attend the testator while he is bequeathing, who, if they left weak offspring behind, would fear for them, should fear to forbid him bequests to his relatives and to command him to hold on to his wealth for his children, although if they were the testator's relatives they would be glad of a bequest to them. Habib, reporting from Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8716, through Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan): I went with al-Hakam ibn Utayba, we came to Miqsam and asked him, and he said: what did Sa'id ibn Jubayr say? We said so and so. He said: but it is the man at death, and the one who attends says to him, "fear God and hold on to your wealth, no one has more right to your wealth than your children", whereas if the one making the bequest were their relative they would love him to bequeath to them; the same from Miqsam (8717, through Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri). In the editor's note, Miqsam is Miqsam ibn Bujra (entry at report 4806), the printing again has "al-Hakam from Uyayna" where the correct form is "Ibn Utayba", and he refers to his note on 8712. A villager (8718, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la from al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman from his father) claimed that the verse means the one present at the bequest should command the bequest to those entitled, just as, if his own offspring were in that position, he would love a bequest made to them, and that if he is himself the heir that does not stop him commanding what is due. The editor says the manuscript's wording at the end of the sentence is unintelligible and he did not find its correct form, so he left the printing's wording and fears something has dropped out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 23}

**Third view: a command to the guardians of orphans to treat them well (pp. 23-25, reports 8719-8720).** Others said: it is rather a command from God to the guardians of orphans to take charge of them with kindness to their persons and wealth, not to eat their wealth extravagantly and in haste before they grow up, and to be to them as they would want the guardians of their own small children to be after them, were they the ones who died. Ibn Abbas (8719, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, uncle, father and grandfather): it is a man who dies leaving small, weak children, fears poverty and loss for them, and fears afterwards that those who take them in will not treat them well; if he becomes the guardian of weak orphans like his own offspring, let him treat them well and not eat their wealth wastefully and hurriedly out of fear that they grow up. Others said the sense is that God will suffice them against whatever concerns their descendants after them: al-Saybani (8720, through Ibrahim ibn Atiyya ibn Rudayh from his uncle Muhammad ibn Rudayh from his father): we were at Constantinople in the days of Maslama ibn Abd al-Malik, and among us were Ibn Muhayriz, Ibn al-Daylami and Hani ibn Kulthum; we were discussing what will happen at the end of time and I was distressed by what I heard; I said to Ibn al-Daylami, Abu Bishr, I wish no child were ever born to me. He struck my shoulder and said: nephew, do not; there is no soul that God has written to come out of a man's loins but it will come out, whether he wishes or not. Shall I tell you of something which, if you reach it, God will save you from it, and if you leave your children after you God will guard them for your sake? I said yes. Then he recited this verse. In the editor's long note (pp. 24-25), he found no entry for Ibrahim ibn Atiyya ibn Rudayh or for Muhammad ibn Rudayh, though the latter is named in his father's entry in al-Tahdhib as having narrated from him; Rudayh ibn Atiyya al-Qurashi al-Sami, muezzin of Bayt al-Maqdis, narrated from al-Saybani and is reliable (al-Tahdhib; al-Bukhari's al-Kabir 1/2/306; Ibn Abi Hatim 2/1/518), the printing having "Duraij" in both places by mistake; al-Saybani is Yahya ibn Abi Amr al-Saybani, with a plain sin, from Sayban, a clan of Himyar, and cousin of al-Awza'i, the printing having "al-Shaybani" by mistake; Ibn Muhayriz is Abd Allah ibn Muhayriz al-Jumahi, who lived in Bayt al-Maqdis and narrated from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, Mu'awiya, Ubada ibn al-Samit and other Companions, and al-Awza'i never listed five of the predecessors without including him, a reliable Successor; Ibn al-Daylami is Abd Allah ibn Fayruz al-Daylami, Abu Bishr (also given as Abu Busr), who lived in Bayt al-Maqdis and narrated from a number of Companions, and from whom Yahya ibn Abi Amr al-Saybani narrated, a reliable Successor; Hani ibn Kulthum ibn Abd Allah ibn Shurayk al-Kinani, of Palestine, a devout man who narrated from Umar ibn al-Khattab, Mu'awiya and others, listed by Ibn Hibban among the reliable, of whom Ata al-Khurasani used to say, when Ibn Muhayriz and Hani and others were mentioned, that some of them strove harder than Hani but that he excelled them in good character; the note then adds a report about Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz and Palestine whose wording is hard to follow in the scan; and the printing has a wrong form in the narrator's remark, the manuscript being correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 23} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 24} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 25}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 25-26, report 8721).** Abu Jafar says the soundest interpretation is: let those fear who, if they left weak offspring behind, would fear want for them had they scattered their wealth in their lifetime or divided it by bequest to their kin and to orphans and the poor, and so kept their wealth for their children for fear of their want after them, given their weakness and helplessness; so let them command those whom they attend as he bequeaths, to his kin, or to orphans and the poor and others, to do so with justice, and let them fear God and say a sound word, which is to make him know what God allowed him of bequest and what He chose for those among believers in God, His Book and His sunna who make bequests (the editor says the wording differs between the printing and the manuscript on the word for those who bequeath and that the sense requires the form he gave). His argument is what he showed earlier, that the preceding verse means that when kin, orphans and poor are present at the division of wealth, bequests should be made to them; if so, this verse must be God's instruction to His servants in the matter of bequests, since it follows the preceding verse on the ruling of bequests, and its clearest sense is what he said, and joining its ruling to the ruling before it, given the similarity of their meanings, is better than turning its ruling to something it does not resemble. Those whose statements he cited at the start of the commentary said the same, and so did Ibn Zayd (8721, through Yunus from Ibn Wahb): he speaks a sound word, reminds this poor man and benefits him, and does not wrong this orphan, the heir of the one who bequeaths, nor change things to his harm, because he is small and cannot defend himself; so look to him as you look to your own children if they were small. The sound word is justice and right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 25} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 26} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 26}
