**Commentary on verse 19: the excuse cut off by the bearer of good news and warner (pp. 155-158)**

On 'People of the Book, Our messenger has come to you making things clear after a gap in the messengers, lest you say "no bearer of good news or warner came to us"', al-Tabari says the People of the Book here are the Jews who lived around the Prophet's place of emigration when the verse came down. When he called them, or some of them, to believe in him and in what he brought, they said God sent no prophet after Moses and sent down no book after the Torah. Report 11616, from Ibn Abbas, says Mu'adh ibn Jabal, Sa'd ibn Ubada and Uqba ibn Wahb told the Jews to fear God, since they knew he was God's messenger, having mentioned him and described him to them before his mission; Rafi' ibn Huraymila and Wahb ibn Yahuda replied that they had not said this and that God sent down no book after Moses and no bearer of good news or warner after him, so God sent down this verse. The editor corrects both names from Ibn Hisham's biography. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 155}

'Our messenger has come to you' means Muhammad, and 'making things clear' means making the truth known, marking out the signs of guidance and leading to God's approved religion. Report 11617, from Qatada, says he brought the Furqan by which God separated truth and falsehood, containing God's explanation, light and guidance and a safeguard for whoever holds to it. 'After a gap in the messengers' means a break in them; the word for gap here means interruption, from the saying that a matter 'slackened' when it grew quiet, so it means the quiet of messengers' coming. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 156}

The length of the gap is disputed, and the reports from Qatada differ. Ma'mar's report 11618 gives five hundred and sixty years between Jesus and Muhammad; Sa'id ibn Abi Aruba's report 11619 says it was told to them as six hundred years, or as God wills, and God knows best. Report 11620, from Ma'mar from his companions, says five hundred and forty years, and Ma'mar says Qatada said five hundred and sixty. Report 11621, from al-Dahhak, says four hundred and some thirty years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 156}

Al-Tabari explains that 'lest you say "no bearer of good news or warner came to us"' means 'that you not say' and 'so that you not say', like 'God makes clear to you that you not go astray' in al-Nisa. So the sense is: Our messenger has come to you making things clear after a gap in the messengers, so that you cannot say no bearer of good news or warner came; He is telling them He has cut off their excuse through His messenger and carried the proof to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 157}

The bearer of good news is the one who gives glad tidings of great reward in the hereafter to whoever obeys God, believes in Him and His messenger and acts on what came from Him; the warner warns whoever disobeys Him, denies His messenger and acts against His command and prohibition of painful punishment he cannot bear on the day of return. Then on 'now a bearer of good news and a warner has come to you, and God has power over all things': God tells these Jews that He has removed their excuse and argued against them through His messenger Muhammad, sent to make clear what was unclear to them of their religion so they could not say no messenger came to them. A messenger has come to give good news to whoever believes, acts as commanded and abstains from what is forbidden, and to warn whoever disobeys; He is able over all things, to punish the disobedient and reward the obedient, so fear His punishment for your disobedience and denial of His messenger and seek His reward for obeying and believing His bearer of good news and warner. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 158}
