**Commentary on verse 89 (pp. 243-244)**

On "he said, do you know what you did to Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant?", al-Tabari says: it is reported that when his brothers said to Joseph "O Aziz, hardship has touched us and our family, and we have brought poor merchandise, so give us full measure and be charitable to us; God rewards the charitable", tenderness overcame him and he disclosed to them what he had been hiding from them. Report 19789, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: I was told that when they spoke to him with these words his feelings overcame him and his tears flowed as he wept, and he disclosed to them what he had hidden and said "do you know what you did to Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant?" By mentioning his brother he did not mean what he himself had done to him in taking him, but the separation between him and his brother, when they did to Joseph what they did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 243}

Report 19790, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: "when they came in to him they said, O Aziz, hardship has touched us and our family", and the rest of the verse; he said: he took pity on them then and said to them "do you know what you did to Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant?" Al-Tabari says the sense of the speech is: do you remember what you did to Joseph and his brother when you separated them and did what you did while you were ignorant, meaning ignorant of the outcome of what you were doing to Joseph and what his affair and yours would come to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 244}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
