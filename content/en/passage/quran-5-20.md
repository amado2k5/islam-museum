**Commentary on verse 20: Moses reminds his people of God's blessing (p. 159)**

On 'and when Moses said to his people: my people, remember God's blessing upon you', al-Tabari says this too is God's teaching to His Prophet Muhammad about these Jews' old persistence in error, their distance from the truth, their poor choices for themselves, their sharp opposition to their prophets and their slowness to return to right guidance, despite the abundance of God's favours to them, to console him for the trouble he suffers dealing with them and the hardship he meets for God's sake. God says to him: do not grieve over what befell you from them, for turning away from God and distance from the truth are their habit and the habit of their forebears; take comfort in what your brother Moses met from them, and recall when Moses said to them: remember God's favours with you and His benefits before you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 159}

Report 11622, from Ibn Uyayna, says God's blessing means His favours with you and His days, and report 11623, from Ibn Abbas, says it means God's well-being. Al-Tabari chooses what he said because God did not single out any one blessing but spoke generally of blessings, so it covers well-being and others, well-being being one meaning of blessings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 159}

Then 'when He placed prophets among you and made you kings': Moses reminded his people of the Children of Israel of God's days with them and His favours before them, urging them to follow God's command to fight the giants. He said: remember God's blessing on you, that He favoured you by placing among you prophets who bring His revelation and tell you news of the unseen, which He gave to no one else in your time. It is said the prophets are the seventy Moses chose for the appointed meeting. 'Made you kings' is said to mean He made others serve you, or that Moses said it because no one then had servants from among the children of Adam but them. The editor corrects two wordings in the printed text and manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 160}

Report 11624, from Qatada, says they were the first to be given servants among the children of Adam, and so became kings. Others said anyone who has a house, a servant and a wife is a king, whoever he is. Report 11625 tells that a man asked Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As whether they were not among the poor emigrants; Abd Allah asked whether he had a wife to shelter with and a dwelling, and on his yes said he was among the rich; when the man said he had a servant, he said then he was among the kings. The editor notes that Muslim records the report in his Sahih. Report 11626, from Zayd ibn Aslam, who thinks the Prophet said it, has: whoever has a house and a servant is a king; the editor says Ibn Kathir calls it a strange mursal report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 161}

Report 11627 says al-Hasan al-Basri recited the verse and asked: what is kingship but a mount, a servant and a house? Those who hold this view say Moses spoke thus because they owned houses and servants and had wives. Reports 11628 and 11629, from al-Hakam, say that a man of the Children of Israel who had a house, a wife and a servant was counted a king, and Sufyan adds, or two of the three; report 11630 from Ibn Abbas says the house and the servant, report 11631 from him the wife, servant and house, and report 11632 from Mujahid that He gave them wives, servants and houses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 162}

Report 11633, from Ibn Abbas, says a man of the Children of Israel with a wife, servant and house was called a king, and the editor notes the weakness of one transmitter in the chain. Report 11634, from Qatada, says their kingdom was servants and that they were the first to own servants; report 11635, from Mujahid, repeats the earlier statement. Others said it means they own themselves, their families and their property, and report 11636, from al-Suddi, says a man of you owns himself, his family and his wealth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 163}

Then 'and gave you what He gave to no one else in the worlds', where the addressees are disputed. Some said the community of Muhammad, as report 11637 from Abu Malik and Sa'id ibn Jubayr has it. Others said the people of Moses, as report 11638 from Mujahid says, and report 11639, from Ibn Abbas, says they were among those living then; the editor notes that al-Hakim records it as sound and al-Dhahabi agreed. They then differed over what God gave them that He gave no one else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 164}

Some said it was manna and quails, the rock and the cloud; reports 11640 and 11641 from Mujahid and 11643 from Ibn Abbas say so, and the rock is the one Moses struck with his staff, from which twelve springs burst. Others said it was the house, the servant and the wife; report 11642, from Ibn Abbas, says a man who has a house, a servant and a wife, and the editor notes how weak a transmitter in this chain is. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 165}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that 'gave you what He gave to no one else in the worlds' belongs within 'remember God's blessing upon you' and is joined to it, with nothing in the wording turning it away from those first addressed, so it is more fitting that it addresses them. Whoever thinks it cannot address them, because the community of Muhammad received from God's honour through its prophet what no one else did, is mistaken: it is Moses speaking to his people then, meaning the worlds of his time, not of all time, and no one in that time received God's blessing and honour as his people did. The editor notes that the printed text's editor misread al-Tabari's Arabic and altered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 166}
