## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We saved you from Pharaoh's people" al-Tabari says it is joined to "remember My favour", as if saying: remember My favour and remember My kindness in saving you from Pharaoh's people. Pharaoh's people are those of his religion, his community and his followers; "al" is originally "ahl", with the ha turned into a hamza, and he explains its usage, holding that it is best used with well-known names. "Pharaoh" is a title borne by the kings of the Amalekites in Egypt, as Roman kings were called Caesar and Persian kings Chosroes; the Pharaoh of Moses is said to have been named al-Walid ibn Mus'ab ibn al-Rayyan, as reached Ibn Ishaq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 36} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 37} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 38}
He holds it proper to say "We saved you" to people who did not live through it, since those addressed are descendants of those who were saved, so he attributes what was done for their fathers to them, as well as their fathers' ingratitude, as one says "we did this to you, we killed you and took you captive" of one's own people, citing al-Akhtal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 38}

On "who afflicted you with evil torment" al-Tabari allows two readings: a fresh statement about what Pharaoh did to the Children of Israel (so the verb stands in the nominative), or a circumstantial clause describing Pharaoh's people. "Yasumunakum" means they inflicted it on you and made you taste it, and "evil torment" is what grieves them; he rejects the reading "the severest torment", since that would have been said "worst". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 39} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 40}
He explains that this torment is what God describes next, "slaying your sons and sparing your women". He relates from Ibn Ishaq that Pharaoh made the Israelites servants and bondsmen in his works, one class building, one ploughing, one sowing, and whoever had no craft paid a poll tax, and from al-Suddi that he set them to the filthiest work, killing their sons and sparing their women. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 40} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 41}
He notes that God attributes the deed to Pharaoh's people rather than to Pharaoh because they did it with their own hands, though by his power and order. From this he draws a legal opinion that is his own: whoever kills unjustly at another's command is the one to whom the act is attributed and is subject to retaliation, even if compelled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 41}
On how the killing was carried out he reports, with a chain stopping at Ibn Abbas, that Pharaoh and his courtiers discussed God's promise to Abraham of prophets and kings among his descendants and sent men with knives to kill every newborn boy. Seeing the elders die naturally while the young were killed, they resolved to kill in one year and spare in the next; Moses's mother conceived Aaron in a year of sparing and Moses in a year of killing. The editor says the chain to Ibn Abbas is sound but the content cannot be affirmed, perhaps being what Companions recounted of ancient history from the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 42}
In another report from Ibn Abbas through Ikrima, the soothsayers told Pharaoh a child would be born that year who would take his kingdom, so he set officers over pregnant women to kill boys and spare girls; Abu al-Aliya and al-Rabi' ibn Anas say Pharaoh ruled four hundred years and, warned of a boy who would bring his end, sent midwives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 43}

He reports from al-Suddi that Pharaoh dreamed of a fire coming from Bayt al-Maqdis that burned the Copts and spared the Children of Israel; his soothsayers told him a man would arise from the land the Israelites came from, bringing Egypt's ruin, so he ordered boys killed. When death swept through the Israelite elders, the Copts feared the work would fall to them, and he ordered killing one year and sparing the next. From Ibn Ishaq he reports that astrologers told Pharaoh a child of Israel whose time was near would take his kingdom, so he gathered midwives and ordered the males killed; from Mujahid comes a harrowing account of what pregnant Israelite women suffered, until Pharaoh was told he was destroying the people who were his servants and workers and so decreed killing in alternate years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 44} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 45} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 46}
On "sparing your women" al-Tabari's choice is that it means they let them live and did not kill them, and that "women" covers mothers and little girls, as one says "the men came" though boys are among them. He records that Ibn Jurayj read "yastahyun" as taking them into servitude, which he rejects, since "istihya'" is formed from "life" like "istibqa'" from "remaining", and that meaning is found in no language. Another view takes "your sons" to mean the grown men; he answers that God told Moses's mother to cast him into the river for fear, which she would not have needed had only men been killed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 46} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 47} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 48}
On "in that was a great trial from your Lord" he holds the sense to be that in being saved from what they were in lay a great favour from their Lord, and reports from Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi that "bala'" here means favour. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 48}
