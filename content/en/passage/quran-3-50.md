## Summary
Verse 50 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 50 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The accusative of the word for confirming.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: and that I have come to you with a sign from your Lord, and I have come confirming what came before me of the Torah; the word for confirming is therefore accusative as a circumstantial clause on 'I have come to you'. What shows it is accusative on that, and not coordinated with 'eminent', is the first person in 'what is before me'; had it been coordinated with 'eminent', the speech would have the third person. The editor refers to al-Farra (1:216) and to earlier pages (160-161). He says Jesus believed in the Torah and acknowledged it, and that it was from God, and all prophets likewise confirm whatever books and messengers came before them, even if some of the rulings of their laws differ because God made them differ in that; and Jesus, as the report has reached them, acted by the Torah and did not depart from any of its rulings except what God lightened for its people in the Gospel of what was made hard for them in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 438}

**The law and the lightening.** Report 7111 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Karim, from Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil) says he heard Wahb ibn Munabbih say that Jesus was on the law of Moses, kept the Sabbath and faced Bayt al-Maqdis, and told the Israelites he had not called them to the opposite of a letter of the Torah except to make lawful for them some of what was forbidden them and to lift from them the burdens; the editor says the burdens are heavy covenants such as their killing themselves, the cutting of skin touched by impurity and other severe rulings. Report 7112 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says what Jesus brought was gentler than what Moses brought, and in what Moses brought were forbidden to them the flesh of camels and the thin fat over the stomach, and some birds and fish; the editor explains the word for that fat. Report 7113 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says much the same, adding that fats were forbidden them and made lawful in Jesus' message, with some fish and some birds without a spur, and that Jesus came with lightening, so what he brought was gentler than Moses'; the editor explains that the spur is the thorn on a cockerel's foot and that cattle horns are called by the related word from which fortresses get their name. Report 7114 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says what was forbidden them was camel flesh and fats, and when Jesus was sent he made them lawful, being sent to the Jews, who differed and divided. Report 7115 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn) begins, and the chain breaks off at the end of p. 439 and continues on p. 440. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 438} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 439}

**The rest of report 7115.** The chain is completed at the head of p. 440 by the words 'Jafar ibn al-Zubayr', so as printed it reads: Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn (end of p. 439) / Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (start of p. 440); nothing is added to the printed text. The report glosses the phrase about what came before him of the Torah, then says that he tells them what he made lawful for them had been forbidden to them and that they had left it; he made it lawful as a lightening, so that they obtain its ease and are free of its consequence. The editor explains the consequence as the sin that follows one who commits the act, and refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira (2:231), noting that it belongs to the rest of the reports whose last was number 7085. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 440}

**The reports of al-Hasan and Mujahid.** Report 7116 (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan) says that things had been forbidden them and Jesus came to make lawful for them what had been forbidden, seeking their gratitude by it. Al-Tabari says the proof and lesson from their Lord is something by which they would know the truth of what he says. Report 7117 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) and report 7118 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) say that it means what Jesus made clear to them of all things and what his Lord gave him. Al-Tabari adds that 'from your Lord' means 'from beside your Lord'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 440}

**Al-Tabari on the call to fear God and obey.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: I have come to you with a proof by which you know my truthfulness with certainty, so fear God, children of Israel, in what He commanded and forbade in His book sent down to Moses, fulfil the covenant you made in it, and obey me in what I called you to, namely believing what my Lord and yours sent me with and the making lawful of some of what was forbidden you in your book; in his view that is the sound road and firm guidance with no crookedness, and the editor refers to earlier pages on the straight path. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 441}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
