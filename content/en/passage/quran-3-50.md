## Summary
Verse 50 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 50 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The accusative of the word for confirming.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: and that I have come to you with a sign from your Lord, and I have come confirming what came before me of the Torah; the word for confirming is therefore accusative as a circumstantial clause on 'I have come to you'. What shows it is accusative on that, and not coordinated with 'eminent', is the first person in 'what is before me'; had it been coordinated with 'eminent', the speech would have the third person. The editor refers to al-Farra (1:216) and to earlier pages (160-161). He says Jesus believed in the Torah and acknowledged it, and that it was from God, and all prophets likewise confirm whatever books and messengers came before them, even if some of the rulings of their laws differ because God made them differ in that; and Jesus, as the report has reached them, acted by the Torah and did not depart from any of its rulings except what God lightened for its people in the Gospel of what was made hard for them in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 438}

**The law and the lightening.** Report 7111 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd al-Karim, from Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil) says he heard Wahb ibn Munabbih say that Jesus was on the law of Moses, kept the Sabbath and faced Bayt al-Maqdis, and told the Israelites he had not called them to the opposite of a letter of the Torah except to make lawful for them some of what was forbidden them and to lift from them the burdens; the editor says the burdens are heavy covenants such as their killing themselves, the cutting of skin touched by impurity and other severe rulings. Report 7112 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says what Jesus brought was gentler than what Moses brought, and in what Moses brought were forbidden to them the flesh of camels and the thin fat over the stomach, and some birds and fish; the editor explains the word for that fat. Report 7113 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi) says much the same, adding that fats were forbidden them and made lawful in Jesus' message, with some fish and some birds without a spur, and that Jesus came with lightening, so what he brought was gentler than Moses'; the editor explains that the spur is the thorn on a cockerel's foot and that cattle horns are called by the related word from which fortresses get their name. Report 7114 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says what was forbidden them was camel flesh and fats, and when Jesus was sent he made them lawful, being sent to the Jews, who differed and divided. Report 7115 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn [Jafar ibn al-Zubayr, completing the chain on the next page]) begins and the text breaks off at the end of p. 439. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 438} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 439}
