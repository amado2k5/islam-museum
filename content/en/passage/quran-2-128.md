## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "our Lord, make us submissive to You and from our offspring a nation submissive to You" al-Tabari says it is God's report of Abraham and Ishmael that they raised the foundations while saying this, meaning: make us surrendered to Your command, humble in obeying You, associating no one with You, having explained earlier that "islam" is submission to God by obedience. On "and from our offspring a nation submissive to You" he holds they specified some of their offspring because God had earlier told Abraham that some of his descendants would not receive His covenant because of their wrongdoing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 73} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 74}

It was said that they meant the Arabs, from al-Suddi. Al-Tabari says the apparent sense of the Book is against this, since they prayed that people of obedience to Him be made of their descendants, and Abraham's children include the Arabs and others, so he sees no ground for singling out one group except arbitrary choice. "Umma" here is a group of people, as in "and of the people of Moses is a nation guiding by the truth". In a footnote the editor says Ibn Kathir thought the context concerned the Arabs because of "and send among them a messenger from them", and the editor answers that the prayer is that of Abraham and Ishmael together and each had descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 74} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 75}

On "and show us our rites" al-Tabari notes the readers' disagreement: some read "arina" in the sense of seeing with the eye, that is, make them appear to our eyes, the reading of most people of the Hijaz and Kufa, while some made the ra' silent with a hint of kasra. Those who read so differ on "manasik": some said they are the rites and landmarks of the pilgrimage, from Qatada: God showed them their rites, the circling of the House, the running between al-Safa and al-Marwa, the departure from 'Arafat and from Jam', and the stoning of the pillars, until God completed the religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 75} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 76}

From al-Suddi, when Abraham and Ishmael finished building the House God told him to call "and proclaim to the people the pilgrimage", so he called between the two hills of Mecca, People, God commands you to make the pilgrimage to His House; it settled in the heart of every believer and everything that heard him of mountain, tree or beast answered with the talbiya. He was then told to go to 'Arafat, and it was described to him; the report says Satan met him at the pass and he threw seven pebbles at him, saying the takbir with each, and again at the second and third pillars; he reached Dhu al-Majaz, then 'Arafat, where, recognising the description, he said "I have known", so it was called 'Arafat; he went on to Jam', called al-Muzdalifa, and threw at Satan where he first met him, then stayed at Mina until he finished the pilgrimage, and that is "and show us our rites". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 76} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 77}

Others among those who read this way said "manasik" are the places of slaughter, so the meaning is: show us how to offer our sacrifices to You so we slaughter them for You, from 'Ata' and Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 77}
