**Commentary on verse 96 (pp. 258-260)**

On "when the bearer of good news came, he cast it over his face, and he regained his sight; he said, did I not tell you that I know from God what you do not know?", al-Tabari says: when the herald came to Ya'qub from his son Joseph, the one bringing the good news of Joseph's message, a courier, as is related, whom Joseph had sent to him, and the courier and herald was, as is related, Judah ibn Ya'qub, Joseph's brother by his father. Report 19857, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: "al-bashir" is the courier. Reports 19858 and 19859, from al-Dahhak, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hushaym and Juwaybir, and al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Wasiti: the courier. Reports 19860 and 19861, from Mujahid, through Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih, and Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa: Judah ibn Ya'qub. The editor notes that the printed text has "barrada ilayhi" and the manuscript "abradahu", both correct, one saying "barada baridan" and "abradahu" for he sent him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 258}

Reports 19862 to 19865, from Mujahid: he is Judah ibn Ya'qub, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj; and al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and Sufyan. Sufyan said: Ibn Mas'ud used to read "and the herald came from between the hands of the caravan"; the editor says this is a reading not used for reciting because it differs from the codex and that it raises a difficulty over the placing of the fa'. Report 19866, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', al-Muharibi and Juwaybir: the courier, who is Judah ibn Ya'qub. Report 19867, from al-Suddi, through Amr and Asbat: Joseph said "go with this shirt of mine and cast it over my father's face, he will regain his sight, and bring me all your family"; Judah said: I went to Ya'qub with the shirt stained with blood and told him the wolf had eaten Joseph, and today I will go with the shirt and tell him he is alive, so as to gladden him as I grieved him. So he was the herald. Report 19868, from al-Dahhak, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Hushaym and Juwaybir: the courier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 259}

One of the Kufan grammarians said that "an" in "fa-lamma an ja'a al-bashir" and its omission are the same in meaning, and said that this is particular to "lamma" and "hatta", the Arabs sometimes adding it with them and sometimes dropping it, as in "wa-lamma an ja'at rusuluna" in Sura al-Ankabut and "wa-lamma ja'at rusuluna" in Sura Hud; he said it is a redundant particle with no place in these two verses, and one says "hatta kana kadha" or "hatta an kana". On "he cast it over his face", he says the herald cast Joseph's shirt over Ya'qub's face. Report 19869, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when the herald came he cast the shirt over his face. On "and he regained his sight", he says he returned and came back seeing with his eyes after having gone blind; "he said, did I not tell you that I know from God what you do not know?" means Ya'qub said to those of his sons then with him: did I not tell you, my sons, that I know from God that He will return Joseph and unite me with him, while you did not know what I knew, because Joseph's dream was true and God had decreed that I and you fall down in prostration to him, and I was sure of His decree. The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "irtadda". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 260}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
