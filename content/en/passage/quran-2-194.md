## Summary
Verse 194 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 194 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "the sacred month for the sacred month, and for sanctities there is retribution", al-Tabari says the sacred month is Dhu al-Qa'da, the month in which the Messenger of God made the 'umra of al-Hudaybiyya and the polytheists of Mecca barred him from the House in the sixth year of his hijra; in that year he made peace with them on condition that he would return the next year, enter Mecca and stay three days. In the next year, the seventh, he set out for 'umra with his companions in Dhu al-Qa'da, the people of Mecca vacated the town for him, he entered, completed his 'umra, stayed three days and returned to Medina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 575} God said to the Prophet and the Muslims: the sacred month in which God brought you to His sanctuary and House despite the dislike of the polytheists of Quraysh is for the sacred month in which the polytheists barred you the year before, so God gave you retribution against them by bringing you into the sanctuary in the sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 575} From 'Ikrima, from Ibn 'Abbas, on "and for sanctities there is retribution": they are the polytheists, who detained Muhammad (the report continues on the next page). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 575}

To complete the report of 'Ikrima from Ibn 'Abbas: "and sanctities are retribution", they are the polytheists who detained Muhammad in Dhu al-Qa'da from the House and boasted over him, so God brought him back in Dhu al-Qa'da, entered him into the Sacred House and gave him retribution against them. The editor vouches for Muhammad ibn 'Abd Allah ibn Bazi', al-Tabari's shaykh, judges Yusuf ibn Khalid al-Samti very weak and a liar, and corrects his name as printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 575} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 576} From Mujahid, Quraysh boasted of turning back the Messenger of God at al-Hudaybiyya, in ihram in Dhu al-Qa'da, from the Sacred Land, and God brought him into Mecca the next year in Dhu al-Qa'da, he completed his 'umra, and God gave him retribution for what was put between him and it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 576} From Qatada, the Prophet and his companions came to perform 'umra in Dhu al-Qa'da with the sacrificial animals until, at al-Hudaybiyya, the polytheists barred them; he made peace with them that he would return that year and come back the next, to be in Mecca three days, entering only with a rider's weapons and taking no one of the people of Mecca out with him; they sacrificed the animals at al-Hudaybiyya, shaved and trimmed, and the next year they came and performed 'umra in Dhu al-Qa'da and stayed three nights; the polytheists had boasted over him when they turned him back, and God gave him retribution against them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 576}

From Qatada and Miqsam, this was on the journey of al-Hudaybiyya: the polytheists barred the Prophet and his companions in the sacred month, and they agreed with them that they might perform 'umra the next year in this same month, so God gave them a sacred month in which to perform 'umra in place of the month in which they were barred; the editor explains the verb "qada" and the name 'umrat al-qadiyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 577} From al-Suddi, when the Messenger of God made the 'umra of al-Hudaybiyya in Dhu al-Qa'da of the sixth year the polytheists barred him, then made peace on condition that they vacate Mecca for him three days the next year; he came to them after the conquest of Khaybar in the seventh year and they vacated Mecca for him for three days, and in that 'umra he married Maymuna bint al-Harith al-Hilaliyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 577} From al-Dahhak and al-Rabi', the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 577} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 578} From Ibn 'Abbas, "and sanctities are retribution": they are the polytheists who had held Muhammad back in Dhu al-Qa'da from the House and boasted over him, and God brought him back in Dhu al-Qa'da, entered him into the Sacred House and gave him retribution. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 578} From Ibn Zayd, all this has been abrogated: God commanded him to fight the polytheists; he recited "fight the polytheists all together" and "fight those of the unbelievers who are near you" from al-Tawba, saying these were the Arabs, and when he had finished with them he recited "fight those who do not believe in God nor the Last Day" to "while they are humbled", saying these were the Romans, and the Messenger of God sent a force to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 578} From Ibn 'Abbas through 'Ikrima, "God commanded you retaliation, [and takes] aggression from you"; the editor says the bracketed words are as in the original, that he found the report nowhere, that there is an error or gap in the wording, and judges the meaning to be that God commanded retaliation and disliked aggression. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 578} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 579} From 'Ata', it was revealed about al-Hudaybiyya, when they were barred in the sacred month: an 'umra in a sacred month for an 'umra in a sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 579}

Al-Tabari says God named Dhu al-Qa'da "the sacred month" because the Arabs in the jahiliyya forbade fighting and killing in it and laid down their arms, and none killed another in it even if he met the killer of his father or son, and they called it Dhu al-Qa'da because they sat out raids in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 579} "The sanctities" (hurumat) is the plural of hurma, like zulumat of zulma, and it is plural because He meant the sacred month, the sacred land and the sanctity of ihram, so the meaning is: your entering the sanctuary in ihram in this sacred month of yours is retribution for what you were prevented from doing the previous year; he says qisas is requital by act, word or body, and here it is by act. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 579}

On "so whoever transgresses against you, transgress against him in the like of what he transgressed against you", al-Tabari notes the commentators differed over what it was revealed about. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 580} From Ibn 'Abbas, this and the like was revealed at Mecca when the Muslims were few and had no authority to subdue the polytheists, who assailed them with abuse and harm, so God commanded those of the Muslims who would requite to requite in kind, or be patient or pardon, which is better; when the Prophet emigrated to Medina and God strengthened his authority, He commanded the Muslims to bring their grievances to their authority and not to assault one another as in the jahiliyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 580} Others said the meaning is rather: whoever of the polytheists fights you, believers, fight them as they fight you, and said it was revealed at Medina after the 'umrat al-qadiyya; from Mujahid, "fight them in it as they fight you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 580}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the nearer of the two interpretations to what the apparent sense of the verse indicates is the one reported from Mujahid, because the verses before it are God's command to the believers to fight their enemy in a described manner, and "so whoever transgresses against you, transgress against him" is within the run of verses that command fighting, and God made fighting obligatory on the believers only after the hijra, so it is known that this saying is Medinan, not Meccan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 580} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 581} In his reading it means: whoever transgresses against you in the sanctuary and fights you, transgress against him by fighting like his transgression against you, since God made the sanctities retribution; whoever of the polytheists treats as lawful a sanctity in His sanctuary, treat as lawful against him the like of it there. He says this verse was abrogated by God's permission to His Prophet to fight the people of the sanctuary first in the sanctuary and by "fight the polytheists all together"; the editor then marks with dots a gap in the text, saying that al-Tabari's words are lost at this point. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 581} The editor puts in square brackets what he infers from the substance of al-Tabari's words about the two senses of i'tada, and what follows in the text: one, that i'tida' is from 'udwan, which is exceeding the limit wrongfully and unjustly, so that the meaning is requite him in the like of what he did to you, a matter of one word following another in form while the meanings differ, as in "they plotted and God plotted" and "they mock them; God mocks them"; the other, that it is from 'adw, rushing and leaping, as in "the lion rushed upon its prey", so that the meaning is whoever rushed upon you and leapt wrongfully, rush upon him as retribution and not as wrong, and the ta' is added so that ifta'ala stands for fa'ala, like iqtaraba for qaruba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 581} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 582}

On "and fear God and know that God is with the God-fearing", al-Tabari says: fear God, believers, in His sanctities and limits, that you not transgress in them and go beyond what He has made clear and bounded for you, and know that God loves the God-fearing, those who fear Him by performing His obligations and avoiding what He has forbidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 582}
