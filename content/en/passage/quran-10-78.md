**Commentary on verse 78 (pp. 157-159)**

On [[passage/quran-10-78|10:78]] al-Tabari says: Pharaoh and his chiefs said to Moses, 'have you come to turn us' away, to divert us, 'from what we found our fathers following' of religion before you came. One says 'so-and-so turned so-and-so's neck' when he twisted it, and he cites a line of Ru'ba in a rajaz in which the poet speaks of himself as a lion, twisting and pounding whatever he meets; 'tahzi'' is pounding and 'lafat' twisting. The editor says the printed text and manuscript have 'as Dhu al-Rumma said', plainly a mistake, so he put Ru'ba in brackets, as in Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, and judges the slip the copyist's, not al-Tabari's. Report 17765, from Qatada on 'to turn us': to twist us away from what we found our fathers following. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 157}

His words 'and that the two of you may have greatness in the land' mean grandeur, the 'fi'liya'' form from kibr, pride, and he cites a line of Ibn al-Riqa' about a lordship that is not vulgar and which neither tyranny nor arrogance touches; the editor says he did not find the line elsewhere and has pointed 'tijbar' as a verbal noun of the form tif'al because that keeps the metre. Reports 17766, 17767 and 17768, from Mujahid: kingship, authority in the land, and kingship in the land; report 17769, from al-Dahhak: obedience. Further reports from Mujahid follow, also explaining it as kingship, among them 17772 from Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid likewise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 158}

A report following this, from al-A'mash from Mujahid: authority in the land. Al-Tabari says all these sayings are close in meaning, since kingship is authority and obedience is kingship; yet the meaning of 'greatness' is what is established in Arab speech, which then becomes grandeur by way of kingship, authority and the like. His words 'and we are not believers in the two of you' mean: we do not, Moses and Aaron, acknowledge that you are two messengers sent to us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 159}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
