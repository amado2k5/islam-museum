## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 42 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 42: "listeners to falsehood, eaters of what is unlawful", pp. 318-324**

Al-Tabari says these Jews, whose description God gave the Prophet, are listeners for false and lying speech, such as one saying to another that Muhammad is a liar and no prophet, and another saying that the Torah's ruling for a married adulterer is lashing and blackening, and other falsehoods and slander, and they accept bribes and eat them over their lying against God and their invention about Him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 318}

Report 11942, from al-Hasan, says the verse means the judges, who listened to a lie and ate a bribe; report 11943, from Qatada, says this was in the Jewish judges before you, who listened to lies and took bribes; report 11944, from Mujahid, says "eaters of what is unlawful" is bribery in judging and they are Jews. Reports 11945 to 11949, from 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, say the unlawful gain is the bribe, and when he was asked about it in judging he said: that is unbelief.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 319}

Report 11950, from Masruq, says he asked 'Abdallah about unlawful gain and he said: a man seeks a need for another, who meets it, and the man sends him a gift and he takes it. Reports 11951 and 11952 say it is bribes and bribery in religion; report 11953, from 'Umar, says among unlawful gains are bribes and the prostitute's fee; reports 11954 and 11955, from Ibrahim and Qatada, say bribes; report 11956, from Abu Hurayra, says the fee of a prostitute is unlawful, as are stud fees, the cupper's earnings and the price of a dog. The editor explains the stud fee.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 320}

Report 11957, from al-Dahhak, says it is bribery in judging, and report 11958 has Masruq asking Ibn Mas'ud, who said bribes; he asked "in judging?" and he said that is unbelief. Report 11959, from al-Suddi, says bribes. Report 11960 has Masruq and 'Alqama asking Ibn Mas'ud about the bribe; he said it is unlawful gain, they said in judging, he said that is unbelief, and recited the verse of [[passage/quran-5-44|5:44]]. Report 11961, from Muslim ibn Subayh, says Masruq interceded for a man in a need and was given a slave girl as a gift, and he was very angry and said he had heard Ibn Mas'ud say whoever intercedes to return a right or lift a wrong and is given a gift and accepts has taken unlawful gain.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 321}

It adds that he was told they had thought it only taking for judging, and he said taking for judging is unbelief. Report 11962, from Ibn 'Abbas, says they took bribes in judging and ruled by falsehood. Report 11963 has Masruq asking Ibn Mas'ud whether unlawful gain is the bribe in judging, and he said no: whoever does not rule by what God sent down is an unbeliever, and whoever does not is a wrongdoer, and whoever does not is a sinner; but unlawful gain is when a man asks your help against a wrong and you help him, and he sends you a gift and you accept it. Report 11964, from 'Abdallah ibn Hubayra, says three things are unlawful gain: the prostitute's fee, bribery in judging, and what was given to soothsayers in the Age of Ignorance.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 322}

Report 11965, from 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, says of the cupper's earnings, the prostitute's fee, the price of a dog, taking a fee for a judgement, the soothsayer's reward, stud fees, bribery in judging, the price of wine and the price of carrion that they are unlawful gain. Report 11966, from Ibn Zayd, says bribery in judging. Report 11967, from 'Umar ibn Hamza ibn 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar, says the Prophet said any flesh grown on unlawful gain, the Fire is more fitting for it; he was asked what that is and said bribery in judging; the editor says it is a report with a gap in its chain. Report 11968, from Anas ibn Malik, has him tell a governor's son: when you go back to your father, tell him beware of bribes, for they are unlawful gain.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 323}

Report 11969, from Masruq, from 'Abdallah, says the bribe is unlawful gain, and when asked if also in judging he said no, and then recited the verses on ruling by what God sent down, [[passage/quran-5-44|5:44]], [[passage/quran-5-45|5:45]] and [[passage/quran-5-47|5:47]]. Al-Tabari explains that the root sense of the word is the greed of hunger; one says a man's stomach is "consumed" when he is a glutton always found hungry, and the bribe is called by it by likeness, as if the one who takes a bribe has the same greed for what he is given that the glutton has for food. One says he consumed it or ruined it, two reported usages, and he cites a line of al-Farazdaq about a harsh time that left of wealth only what is eaten away, meaning stripped bare by being eaten and spoiled, and [[passage/quran-20-61|20:61]] is of this; the Arabs tell a barber to cut the hair off, meaning clean it off. The editor explains the line and its variants.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 324}

We stop here at the foot of p. 324, in al-Tabari's explanation of the root sense of the word for unlawful gain; the rest of verse 42 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
