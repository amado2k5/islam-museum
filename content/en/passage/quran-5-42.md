## Summary
Verse 42 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 42 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 42: "listeners to falsehood, eaters of what is unlawful", pp. 318-324**

Al-Tabari says these Jews, whose description God gave the Prophet, are listeners for false and lying speech, such as one saying to another that Muhammad is a liar and no prophet, and another saying that the Torah's ruling for a married adulterer is lashing and blackening, and other falsehoods and slander, and they accept bribes and eat them over their lying against God and their invention about Him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 318}

Report 11942, from al-Hasan, says the verse means the judges, who listened to a lie and ate a bribe; report 11943, from Qatada, says this was in the Jewish judges before you, who listened to lies and took bribes; report 11944, from Mujahid, says "eaters of what is unlawful" is bribery in judging and they are Jews. Reports 11945 to 11949, from 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, say the unlawful gain is the bribe, and when he was asked about it in judging he said: that is unbelief.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 319}

Report 11950, from Masruq, says he asked 'Abdallah about unlawful gain and he said: a man seeks a need for another, who meets it, and the man sends him a gift and he takes it. Reports 11951 and 11952 say it is bribes and bribery in religion; report 11953, from 'Umar, says among unlawful gains are bribes and the prostitute's fee; reports 11954 and 11955, from Ibrahim and Qatada, say bribes; report 11956, from Abu Hurayra, says the fee of a prostitute is unlawful, as are stud fees, the cupper's earnings and the price of a dog. The editor explains the stud fee.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 320}

Report 11957, from al-Dahhak, says it is bribery in judging, and report 11958 has Masruq asking Ibn Mas'ud, who said bribes; he asked "in judging?" and he said that is unbelief. Report 11959, from al-Suddi, says bribes. Report 11960 has Masruq and 'Alqama asking Ibn Mas'ud about the bribe; he said it is unlawful gain, they said in judging, he said that is unbelief, and recited the verse of [[passage/quran-5-44|5:44]]. Report 11961, from Muslim ibn Subayh, says Masruq interceded for a man in a need and was given a slave girl as a gift, and he was very angry and said he had heard Ibn Mas'ud say whoever intercedes to return a right or lift a wrong and is given a gift and accepts has taken unlawful gain.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 321}

It adds that he was told they had thought it only taking for judging, and he said taking for judging is unbelief. Report 11962, from Ibn 'Abbas, says they took bribes in judging and ruled by falsehood. Report 11963 has Masruq asking Ibn Mas'ud whether unlawful gain is the bribe in judging, and he said no: whoever does not rule by what God sent down is an unbeliever, and whoever does not is a wrongdoer, and whoever does not is a sinner; but unlawful gain is when a man asks your help against a wrong and you help him, and he sends you a gift and you accept it. Report 11964, from 'Abdallah ibn Hubayra, says three things are unlawful gain: the prostitute's fee, bribery in judging, and what was given to soothsayers in the Age of Ignorance.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 322}

Report 11965, from 'Ali ibn Abi Talib, says of the cupper's earnings, the prostitute's fee, the price of a dog, taking a fee for a judgement, the soothsayer's reward, stud fees, bribery in judging, the price of wine and the price of carrion that they are unlawful gain. Report 11966, from Ibn Zayd, says bribery in judging. Report 11967, from 'Umar ibn Hamza ibn 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar, says the Prophet said any flesh grown on unlawful gain, the Fire is more fitting for it; he was asked what that is and said bribery in judging; the editor says it is a report with a gap in its chain. Report 11968, from Anas ibn Malik, has him tell a governor's son: when you go back to your father, tell him beware of bribes, for they are unlawful gain.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 323}

Report 11969, from Masruq, from 'Abdallah, says the bribe is unlawful gain, and when asked if also in judging he said no, and then recited the verses on ruling by what God sent down, [[passage/quran-5-44|5:44]], [[passage/quran-5-45|5:45]] and [[passage/quran-5-47|5:47]]. Al-Tabari explains that the root sense of the word is the greed of hunger; one says a man's stomach is "consumed" when he is a glutton always found hungry, and the bribe is called by it by likeness, as if the one who takes a bribe has the same greed for what he is given that the glutton has for food. One says he consumed it or ruined it, two reported usages, and he cites a line of al-Farazdaq about a harsh time that left of wealth only what is eaten away, meaning stripped bare by being eaten and spoiled, and [[passage/quran-20-61|20:61]] is of this; the Arabs tell a barber to cut the hair off, meaning clean it off. The editor explains the line and its variants.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 324}

**Commentary on verse 42, continued: "if they come to you, judge between them or turn away from them", pp. 325-331**

Al-Tabari says "if they come to you, judge between them or turn away" means: if these other people who had not yet come, the family of the woman who committed adultery, come to you seeking judgement, then judge between them if you wish by the truth God made the ruling for one who does as that woman did, or turn away and leave judging between them if you wish; the choice is yours. A group of interpreters said likewise.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 325}

Report 11970, from Mujahid, says a Jewish man of lowly lineage committed adultery and they stoned him, then a noble among them did and they blackened him and paraded him, then they sought the Prophet's ruling in the hope he would agree with them; he gave them stoning, they refused it, and he told them to bring their rabbis and monks, adjured them by God whether they found it in the Torah, and they hid it, except a one-eyed man, the youngest of them, who said: they have lied to you, Messenger of God, it is in the Torah. Report 11971, from Ibn Shihab, says the verse concerned stoning. Report 11972, from Ibn 'Abbas, says they came to him about a woman of theirs, asking about her penalty; he said: how do you find it written in your Torah? They said: the adulteress is ordered stoned, and he ordered her stoned, and God had said "if you turn away from them they cannot harm you at all, and if you judge, judge between them with justice; God loves the just".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 326}

Report 11973, from 'Abdallah ibn Kathir, says they used to apply penalties for adultery until a young noble among them committed it, and some said to others: his people will not let you stone him, so lash him and disgrace him; they lashed him, mounted him on a saddled donkey facing its tail, until another, lowly with no standing, committed it, and they said: stone him, then said: why did you not stone the one before? Do to this one as you did to him. When the Prophet came, they said: ask him, perhaps you will find a concession with him, and the verse was revealed, up to "God loves the just".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 326}

Others said the verse was revealed about a man killed among the Jews and killed by one of them. Report 11974, from Ibn 'Abbas, says the verses in al-Ma'ida, from "judge between them or turn away" to "the just", were revealed about the blood-money among Banu al-Nadir and Banu Qurayza: those slain of al-Nadir, who had standing, were paid in full blood-money, while Qurayza paid half; they took it to the Prophet for judgement, God sent this down about them, he held them to the right in it and made blood-money equal; God knows which it was. The editor says it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and in Ahmad in brief.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 327}

Report 11975, from Ibn 'Abbas, says al-Nadir were nobler than Qurayza, and if a man of Qurayza killed a man of al-Nadir he was killed for him, while if a man of al-Nadir killed a man of Qurayza he paid a hundred wasqs of dates; when the Prophet was sent, a man of al-Nadir killed a man of Qurayza, they said hand him over, the others said the Messenger of God is between us, and "if you judge, judge between them with justice" was revealed. Report 11976, from Ibn Zayd, says in Ibn Akhtab's ruling an al-Nadir man's blood-money was two and a Qurayza man's one, and God told His prophet what was in the Torah, a life for a life [[passage/quran-5-45|5:45]]; seeing that, Qurayza were not satisfied with Ibn Akhtab's ruling and said let us bring our case to Muhammad.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 327}

It adds that when a noble committed adultery with a lowly woman they stoned her and blackened the noble's face and mounted him on a camel facing its tail, and when a lowly man did so with a noble woman they stoned him and did the same to her; they came to the Prophet and he stoned her. He asked who was most learned in the Torah, they said such-and-such the one-eyed, he was sent for and the Prophet asked him by God and the Torah revealed to Moses at Mount Sinai what he found in it on the two adulterers; he evaded until he said: Abu al-Qasim, "the old man and old woman, when they commit adultery, stone them certainly"; the Prophet said: that is it, take them and stone them. 'Abdallah said he was among those who stoned them, and the man kept bending over her, shielding her from the stones with himself until he died. The editor traces 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar's report of the stoning of the Jewish man and woman in Muslim, al-Bukhari and Abu Dawud.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 328}

The interpreters then differed over this verse's ruling: whether it stands today, and whether judges have a choice in ruling and examining between the protected and covenanted when they bring their case, like what God gave His prophet in the verse, or whether it is abrogated. Some said it stands today and nothing has abrogated it, and judges in every age have the choice as God gave His messenger. Reports 11977, 11978 and 11979, from Ibrahim and al-Sha'bi, say if a polytheist's case is brought to you, you may judge between them by what God sent down or turn away, and if you judge, judge by the Muslims' ruling and no other. Reports 11980 and 11981, from 'Ata' and al-Sha'bi, say much the same, the last adding that he judges between them by Muslim ruling or leaves them with their own people judging them, except in theft or killing.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 329}

Reports 11982 to 11985, from 'Ata', 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb, Ibrahim, al-Sha'bi and Qatada, say judges have a choice: if they wish they judge between the People of the Book, and if they wish they turn away; and if they judge, they judge by God's book or the Muslims' ruling and no other. Others said the choice is abrogated and the judge must judge between the protected when they bring their case, and may not leave off examining; report 11986, from 'Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, says it was abrogated by the verse of [[passage/quran-5-49|5:49]].  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 330}

Reports 11987 and 11988, from 'Ikrima, say "and judge between them by what God sent down" abrogated it. Report 11989, from Mujahid, says nothing in al-Ma'ida was abrogated but two verses: the first was abrogated by [[passage/quran-5-49|5:49]], and the second, [[passage/quran-5-2|5:2]], by [[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]]. Report 11990, from Mujahid, is like the first. Report 11991, from Qatada, says "if they come to you" means the Jews, and God commanded His prophet to judge between them, allowing him to turn away if he wished; then He sent down the verse after it, [[passage/quran-5-48|5:48]], and God commanded His prophet to judge between them by what He sent down, after having allowed him to turn away.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 331}

We stop here at the foot of p. 331, in report 11991 from Qatada on the abrogation of the choice; the rest of verse 42 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
