**Commentary on verse 27 (pp. 73-77)**

Ibn Abi Layla used to say of "neither dust will cover their faces" that it is after they look upon their Lord, in reports 17643 and 17644 (through Hammad ibn Zayd and Thabit); report 17645, from Ibn Abbas: "qatar" is blackness of faces. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 73}

He then explains "Those who earned evil deeds, the repayment of an evil is its like, and humiliation covers them; they have no protector from God, as if their faces were covered with dark pieces of the night; those are the companions of the Fire, abiding in it". Those who did evil in the world, disobeying God and disbelieving in Him and His messenger, have as the repayment of the evil deed they did in the world its like in God's punishment in the next life, "and humiliation covers them" with disgrace and abasement by God's punishing them, "they have no protector from God" means they have no one to hold off God's punishment from them. Report 17646, from Ibn Abbas: disgrace and hardship cover them. The editor refers to the glosses on earning, evil deed, covering and humiliation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 73}

The grammarians differed over what governs the nominative "repayment". Some Kufans said it is nominative by an implied "for them", as though it said "and for them the repayment of an evil is its like", as in "fasting three days in the pilgrimage", meaning "he owes fasting"; or one may make it nominative through the preposition in "by its like"; the editor says this is al-Farra's statement and notes the connecting particle differs in the printed text and in al-Farra. Some Basrans said it is nominative as the subject with "by its like" as predicate, the meaning being "the repayment of an evil is its like", with the preposition added as in "enough for you is the saying of evil"; others rejected this, arguing that the preposition may be added to "enough" because the sense is "if you say evil, it is enough for you", and that it enters after "enough" when what follows is praised, as in "enough for you is Zayd", but not before it because Zayd is the one praised and so it is not the sense of a predicate. The editor points out where he corrected the printed text and manuscript and fears something has dropped out of the passage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 74}

Al-Tabari prefers that "repayment" is nominative through an implied "so for them is the repayment of an evil by its like", because in the previous verse God said "for those who do good is the best reward and more", describing what He prepared for His friends, and then followed it with what He prepared for His enemies, so it fits better to say "and for those who earned evil deeds is the repayment of an evil"; on this sense the preposition links to "repayment". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 75}

Then "as if their faces were covered with dark pieces of the night; those are the companions of the Fire, abiding in it": as if the faces of those who earned evil deeds were clothed with "pieces of the night", plural of "piece"; the editor refers to the gloss on "covering". Report 17647, from Qatada: darkness of the night. The readers differed on "pieces": most readers of the main cities read it with a fatha on the ta as the plural of "piece", in the sense that each person's face is covered with a piece of the blackness of the night and then put in the plural because "face" is plural. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 75}

Some later readers read it with a sukun, "qit'an", meaning blackness of the night and a remnant of it, as in "set out with your family in a part of the night", meaning in a remnant of it, and they defend this reading by the fact that in Ubayy's copies it reads "a dark part of the night covers their faces". Al-Tabari says the reading that, in his view, may not be opposed is that with fatha on the ta, because the authoritative readers of the main cities agree it is correct and others are irregular, and it is enough to show the other is wrong that its reader departs from what the readers of the lands of Islam hold. The editor refers to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 76}

He then raises an objection: if the right reading is as he says, why is "dark" in the masculine singular when it describes "pieces", the plural of a feminine? He says there are two reasons for the masculine, the first being that "pieces" is a state from "the night", and that "dark" describes "the night"; since "pieces" is indefinite and "the night" definite, it is put in the accusative as a state, so the sense is "as if their faces were covered with pieces of the dark night", and the article was then dropped. The editor notes that this grammatical term for the state is one al-Tabari has explained often. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 76}

The second reason follows a line of verse that the editor attributes to Abu Dhu'ayb, about the praise of a living man; the editor adds that the reading in the poet's collection does not contain the point of proof and that the other reading does. Al-Tabari says the first reason is the better of the two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 77}

He then explains "those are the companions of the Fire, abiding in it": those whose description has been given are the people of the Fire, who are its own people, and "abiding" means staying in it. The editor refers to the glosses on "companions of the Fire" and "abiding" in the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 77}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
