## Summary
Verse 145 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 145 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 145 (pp. 106-109)**

Al-Tabari says: and We wrote for Moses in his tablets. The definite article was put on "the tablets" in place of the possessive, as the poet al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani did in a line describing a people with a quality time has not wasted and minds that do not stray, and as in the words of sura al-Nazi'at that the Garden is "the abode", meaning his abode. The editor notes that the line has been treated with its source and explanation earlier and that this place should be recorded there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 106}

On "of everything" he says: of reminder and warning about God's greatness and mighty rule, "an admonition" for his people and for those commanded to act on what is written in the tablets, "and detailing of everything", that is, clarification of everything of God's command and prohibition. The interpreters said the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 106}

Report 15106, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr or Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "detailing of everything" is what they were commanded and forbidden. Report 15107, from Mujahid, similar, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl. Report 15108, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the lawful and the unlawful. Report 15109, from Mujahid: what they were commanded and forbidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 107}

Report 15110, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather, Atiyya saying that Ibn Abbas told him: when death distressed Moses he hurried and said, this is because of Adam; God had placed us in a house of abiding where we do not die, and Adam's slip sent us down here. God said to Moses, shall I send Adam to you so you can dispute with him? He said yes. When God sent him, Moses questioned him, and Adam said, Moses, you asked God to send me to you! Moses said, were it not for you we would not be here. Adam said, did God not give you of everything an admonition and detailing, and do you not know what is said at [[passage/quran-57-22|57:22]], that every misfortune is written before it happens? Moses said, yes. So Adam prevailed over him, that is, won the argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 108}

In notes to pp. 107-108 the editor explains that Atiyya is al-Awfi, grandfather of Muhammad ibn Sa'd, that the printed text drops the word "hurried", that the manuscript is undotted here and he reads it as meaning he hurried or stripped himself, and he draws on al-Bukhari's report of the angel of death being sent to Moses. He states that this report's chain is very weak, that the report of the dispute of Adam and Moses is in the two Sahihs and the other books of Sunan, and that Ibn Kathir has a good section on the death of Moses in al-Bidaya wa al-Nihaya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 108}

Report 15111, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil, on "We wrote for him in the tablets of everything an admonition and detailing": it was written for him: do not associate with Me anything of the people of heaven or earth, for all of that is My creation; do not swear falsely by My name, for whoever swears falsely by My name I will not purify; and honour your parents. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 108}

**On "so take them with strength"**

Al-Tabari says: We said to Moses, after writing for him in the tablets of everything an admonition and detailing, take the tablets with strength; the editor refers to the verse of sura al-Hadid. The interpreters differed on the meaning of "strength" here. Some said: earnestly. Report 15112, from Ibn Abbas, through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Ibn Uyayna, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: earnestly. Report 15113, from al-Suddi, through Musa, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat: earnestness and effort. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 109}

Others said it means: take them with obedience to God. Report 15114, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Sa'd and Abu Ja'far: with obedience. Al-Tabari says he has explained the meaning and the interpreters' difference in sura al-Baqara at "take what We gave you with strength", which spares repeating it here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 109}

**On "and command your people to take the best of it"**

Al-Tabari says: We said to Moses, command your people, the Children of Israel, to take the best of it, that is, to act on the best of what they find in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 109}

We stop here at the foot of p. 109, where al-Tabari begins the reports on "take the best of it" in verse 145; the next batch begins at p. 110. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 109}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
