## Summary
Verse 271 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 271 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-271|2:271]]), pp. 582 to 584. Al-Tabari explains showing charity as making it public and giving it to whoever receives it, and what a fine thing it is, and hiding it as concealing it and giving it to the poor in secret, so concealing it is better than making it public, and that is for voluntary charity; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 582} The editor corrects a sentence in manuscript and print alike and refers to earlier commentary on the sense of giving. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 582}

Report 6195, Qatada: each is accepted if the intention is true and secret charity is better, and they were told that charity puts out the offence as water puts out fire; 6196 the same from al-Rabi ibn Anas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 582} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 583} 6197, Ibn Abbas: God made secret voluntary charity better than public by seventy times, and for obligatory charity made public better than secret, said to be by twenty-five times, and so with all obligatory and voluntary acts; the editor notes the print altered a phrase and he keeps the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 583} 6198, Sufyan al-Thawri: it is other than zakat; the editor refers to the men of this chain in two earlier places and to report 6200 after it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 583}

Others said what is meant is making charity public to the People of the two Books, Jews and Christians, and that hiding it and giving it to their poor is better; as for what is given to poor Muslims of zakat and voluntary charity, hiding it is better than showing. 6199, Yazid ibn Abi Habib: the verse came down about charity to Jews and Christians; the editor gives the biography of the narrator Abd al-Rahman ibn Sharih, quoting Ahmad that he is reliable and noting he died in Alexandria in 167. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 583} 6200, from Ibn Lahi'a: Yazid ibn Abi Habib used to order zakat to be divided in secret, while Abd Allah ibn al-Mubarak said he liked it to be given openly, meaning zakat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 584}

Al-Tabari's choice and argument. Abu Jafar says God did not single out one kind over another in the verse, so it is general except for obligatory zakat, since all agree that for obligatory duties the merit lies in doing them openly, and zakat, though people differ over it, all agreeing it is obligatory, has the same rule as other duties, that the merit lies in paying it openly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 584} The editor notes the sentence came thus in manuscript and print so he added the bracketed words to make it run, thinks something dropped from the copyist's text, and refers to other copyist slips. This is al-Tabari's choice and his attribution of agreement, not a ruling by the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 584}

Al-Tabari then begins on the readers' differences over the reading of the atoning of offences: it is reported that Ibn Abbas read it with the feminine marker, meaning for those who so read that the charities atone for your offences, while others read it with the masculine marker, meaning that God atones for your offences through your charity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 584} We stop at the end of p. 584. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's choices are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.
