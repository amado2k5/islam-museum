## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and those who followed will say, if only we had another turn, we would disown them as they disowned us" al-Tabari says the followers of the men they took as equals, obeying them in disobedience to God, will say when they see the punishment "if only we had a return"; the "karra" is the return to the world, from "I charged back on the people", when one attacks again after withdrawing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 293}

Al-Tabari completes his explanation of "if only we had another turn": the "karra" is a return to the world; he cites Qatada and al-Rabi' that they said, if only we had a return to the world we would disown them as they disowned us, and says "we would disown" is in the subjunctive as the answer to a wish with fa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 293} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 294} On "thus will God show them their deeds as regrets for them" he says that as He showed them the punishment, so He will show them their evil deeds, by which they earned punishment, as regrets (hasarat, plural of hasra). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 295} He asks how their deeds can be regrets when they did no good whose loss they could regret, and gives two views. One: the deeds are those God made obligatory for them and they wasted, so they see the reward and dwellings they lost; from al-Suddi, Paradise is raised for them and they see their houses in it had they obeyed, then it is divided among the believers who inherit them; and from 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud in a long report, every soul looks at a house in Paradise and a house in the Fire, and that is the Day of Regret: the people of the Fire are told, "if only you had acted", and regret seizes them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 296} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 297} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 298} The other: the deeds are their evil deeds, which became regret for them on the Day of Resurrection, from al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 298} Al-Tabari prefers the second reading, saying that what al-Suddi said, though the verse could bear it, is a remote stretch with no report to carry proof, and the apparent sense of the text is not to be turned into a hidden one without indication. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 298} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 299} On "and they will not come out of the Fire" he says that though they regret and wish for a return, they will not come out and their regret will not save them, and that the verse refutes those who claim the punishment of the unbelievers in the Fire ends at a limit, since God closes the report with their not coming out, without exempting any time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 299}
