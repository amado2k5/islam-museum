## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "O people, eat of what is on earth, lawful and good, and do not follow the footsteps of Satan; he is a clear enemy to you" al-Tabari says: eat of the foods I made lawful through my Messenger, of what you forbid yourselves, such as the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and the like that I did not forbid, apart from what I did forbid, carrion, blood, pork and what is dedicated to other than God; and leave the footsteps of Satan, a clear enemy who showed his enmity by refusing to prostrate to your father and deceiving him until he was brought out of Paradise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 300} "Lawful" means free, "good" means pure, neither unclean nor forbidden, and the "footsteps" (khutuwat) are the plural of khutwa, the distance between a walker's feet; the prohibition of following them is a prohibition of his way and trace in what he calls to against obedience to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 300} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 301} He gives the interpreters' views on "footsteps of Satan": his work, from Ibn 'Abbas; his sins, from Mujahid, Qatada and al-Dahhak; obedience to him, from al-Suddi; and vows in disobedience, from Abu Mijlaz. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 301} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 302} Al-Tabari holds these are close in meaning, and that the true sense of the word is as he explained, then used for all his traces and ways. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 302}
