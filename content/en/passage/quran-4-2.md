## Summary
Verse 2 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 2 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of the commentary on verse 2 (p. 524).** Abu Ja'far al-Tabari says the address is to the guardians of orphans, to whom He says: hand over to the orphans what belongs to them, guardians, once they reach puberty and sound judgement is perceived in them, and there the sentence breaks off, continuing on p. 525. Two notes by the editor: the manuscript and printed edition omit the second word for orphans, which the context requires, so he supplies it in brackets, the copyist having been misled by the repetition; and the second refers to the explanation of the verb of giving in the language indexes and of the word for orphans earlier (3: 292).

**The prohibition on exchanging the forbidden for the lawful (p. 525, reports 8436-8438).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: do not exchange what is forbidden to you of the orphans' property for your own lawful property. Mujahid (8436), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: the lawful for the unlawful; also (8437) through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih; and (8438) through Sufyan ibn Waki' from his father from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih: the unlawful in place of the lawful. Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed on the manner of this exchange. In the editor's note the printed edition dropped the word "kana" from al-Tabari's sentence because its publisher objected to the Arabic, which the editor calls entirely correct, and he restored it from the manuscript.

**First view: taking the good and leaving the poor (pp. 525-526, reports 8439-8442).** Some said the guardians of orphans used to take the fine and choice part of the orphan's property and put the poor and mean in its place, and that was the exchange they were forbidden. Ibrahim (8439), through Abu Kurayb from Ibn Yaman from Sufyan from Mughira: do not give a counterfeit and take a good one. Al-Suddi, Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab (through Yahya ibn Sa'id) and Ma'mar from al-Zuhri (8440), through Abu Kurayb from Ibn Yaman from Sufyan: giving a lean animal and taking a fat one. Al-Dahhak (8441), by the same chain from Sufyan from a man: do not give the spoiled and take the good. Al-Suddi (8442), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: one of them would take the fat sheep from the orphan's flock and put a lean one in its place, saying a sheep for a sheep, and take the good dirham and cast down a counterfeit, saying a dirham for a dirham.

**Second view: do not hasten unlawful provision (p. 526, reports 8443-8444).** Others said the sense is: do not hasten unlawful provision and eat it before there reaches you what was decreed for you of the lawful. Mujahid (8443), through Abu Kurayb from Ibn Yaman from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih; and Abu Salih (8444), by the same chain from Sufyan from Isma'il, likewise.

**Third view: pre-Islamic inheritance (p. 526, report 8445).** Others held what Ibn Zayd (8445) said, through Yunus ibn 'Abd al-A'la from Ibn Wahb: the people of the Jahiliyya did not give inheritance to women or to the young, the eldest taking it; and he recited the passage in [[passage/quran-4-127|4:127]] about those who desire to marry orphan girls and about the weak among children, saying: so his share of the inheritance is good and what he took is bad. In the note the editor says both copies have a verb form which he corrected.

**Al-Tabari's choice and argument (pp. 526-528).** Abu Ja'far says the most fitting of these views is that of those who said: do not exchange the property of your orphans, guardians, taking the unlawful bad for you in place of the good lawful from your own property, [that is, do not take] the poor mean in place of it, taking their best and choice. His argument: in the speech of the Arabs, exchanging is taking one thing in place of another, which one gives to the one taken from or puts where he took; if that is the sense, then Ibn Zayd's saying, that the dead man's eldest son takes all his father's property without the younger ones, has no meaning, because the eldest, taking it, exchanged nothing for what he took. As for Mujahid and Abu Salih, if they did not mean something like what is related from Ibn Mas'ud, that a man is deprived of provision by the sin he commits, their view is as invalid as Ibn Zayd's, since one who hastens the unlawful and eats it, and then God gives him his lawful provision, exchanged nothing for anything; but if they meant that God forbade His servants to hasten the unlawful and eat it before the lawful comes, so that eating it becomes a cause of being deprived of the good, that is a known way and a reasonable doctrine that the interpretation allows, though the more fitting of the two views is what he has said, as it is the most apparent of its senses and because God mentioned it in the account of the orphans' property and its rulings, so that it belongs to the same kind as the ruling at the beginning and end of the verse rather than to another kind. This is al-Tabari's choice and the museum gives no verdict. The editor's notes: the bracketed words are his own supplement from al-Tabari's later interpretation, without which the sentence hardly stands; he refers to the earlier explanations of the words for the bad and the good (the numerals are unclear in the scan) and of exchanging and substituting (2: 112, 130, 494); the printed edition has a different form which he corrected from the manuscript; the printed edition dropped a particle in one sentence and the manuscript has a corrupt form from the copyist's haste, which he restored; the words "the two views" in the phrase "the more fitting of the two views" he supplied in brackets; and two other phrases, with a word for "more fitting", were disturbed in both copies by the copyist's slip and he mended them.

**The sense of mixing the properties (pp. 528-529, reports 8446-8447).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: do not mix the orphans' property with yours and eat it along with yours. Mujahid (8446), through Ibn Bashshar from Sufyan from Ibn Abi Najih: do not eat your property and theirs, mixing them and eating them together. Al-Hasan (8447), through al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Abu Zuhayr from Mubarak: when this verse came down about the orphans' property, they disliked mixing with them and the orphan's guardian began setting the orphan's property apart from his; they complained of it to the Prophet, and God sent down the passage at [[passage/quran-2-220|2:220]]; he said, so they mixed with them and kept their duty to God. In the editor's notes: a reference to the earlier explanation of eating property (3: 548-549) and of the particle usually meaning "to" being used for "with" (1: 299; 6: 443-444); and that al-Tabari did not narrate report 8447 in his commentary on the verse in al-Baqara (4: 349-350), which the editor takes as one of the proofs that he abridged this commentary.

**The meaning of hub (pp. 529-530, reports 8448-8455).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that eating your orphans' property is a grave sin, the pronoun referring back to the act understood from the eating. Hub is sin; one says "the man sinned" with several verbal nouns, and "he avoided such a thing" when he guards against sin in it; he cites a line of Umayya ibn al-Askar al-Laythi about two emigrants who embraced him that morning and erred and sinned, described and not reproduced; and from it the saying "we alighted at a hubba of the land", that is, a bad place. "Great" means mighty. In the editor's notes: "al-Askan" in both copies is a pure error for "al-Askar"; the line passed with its tracing at 2: 110, will recur at 13: 37 (Bulaq) and is in Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 13), where the places it recurs in the commentary were not recorded and should be; Majaz reads a word with the dotted letter, while he holds the undotted reading here the better, the other an old scribal slip, and al-Tabari's sense the better fit to the line; and a reference to the explanation of "great" (4: 300; 3: 166; 2: 15). The reports in the same sense: Mujahid (8448), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr and 'Amr ibn 'Ali from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: sin; Mujahid (8449), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl; Ibn 'Abbas (8450), through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih from Mu'awiya from 'Ali ibn Abi Talha: a grave sin; al-Suddi (8451), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat: hub is sin; Qatada (8452), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: sin; Qatada (8453), through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Sa'id: great wrongdoing; Ibn Zayd (8454), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: a great offence, and it is for the people of Islam; and al-Hasan (8455), through 'Amr ibn 'Ali from Yahya ibn Sa'id from Qurra ibn Khalid: by God, a mighty sin. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 530}
