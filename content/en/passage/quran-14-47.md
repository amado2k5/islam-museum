## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains: "So do not think that Allah breaks His promises to His messengers" as a prohibition for the Prophet against the suspicion of Allah breaking His promises by lying and denying what He brought. Rather, he said it to affirm it, strengthen His resolve, and indicate that His wrath has befallen those who denied Him, like what was revealed to those who followed their path among the nations before them. And "Indeed, Allah is Mighty, Possessor of Avengers": Mighty and does not object to anything He intends to punish Him and does not avoid by fleeing from it, and He takes revenge on those who disbelieve in His messengers, lie to them, and associate partners with Him. Al-Tabari then elaborates on the parsing of 'Mukhlaf wa 'Wa'da Rasulu': Ikhlaf applies to two different accusatives, so it is permissible to precede either of them. He reduced 'wa'adhu' by adding 'mukhlaf' to it and accusative 'rasulalu,' meaning 'mukhlaf rusluhu wa wa'd'. He cites verses by Al-Farazdaq and others to give precedence to one of the objects and add the active participle to it. He mentions that whoever reads 'mukhlaf wa wa'da rasuluhu' in the nominative case has clarified the authenticity of the next in Surah Al-An'am.
