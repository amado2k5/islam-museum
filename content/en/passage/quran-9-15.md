**Commentary on verse 15 (pp. 161-162)**

Al-Tabari says: and He takes away the resentment of the hearts of these believers of Khuza'a against the people who broke their oaths among the polytheists, and their grief and distress at what they felt against them for helping Bakr against them. Report 16546, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki, Amr ibn Muhammad al-Anqazi and Asbat: "and removes the rage of their hearts" when Banu Bakr killed them with Quraysh helping; report 16547 is similar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 161}

As for "and God turns in mercy to whom He wills", it is a new statement and so is in the indicative, while the three verbs before it are in the jussive as a consequence, as though He said fight them, for if you fight them God will punish them by your hands, disgrace them and give you victory over them; then He began anew, saying that God turns in mercy to whom He wills, because fighting does not make repentance from God due to them, but does make punishment, disgrace, the healing of the believers' breasts and the removal of their rage due. The sense is that God favours whom He wills of His unbelieving servants and brings them to repentance by granting them success; God is knowing of the secrets of His servants and of who is fit for repentance, so He turns to him, and who is not, so He forsakes him, and wise in moving His servants from unbelief to belief by granting success to whom He grants it, and from belief to unbelief by forsaking those He forsakes of obedience and affirmation of His oneness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 162}
