## Summary
Verse 143 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 143 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 143 (pp. 90-102)**

Al-Tabari says: when Moses came at the time We had set for him to meet Us, and his Lord spoke to him and conversed with him, Moses said to his Lord, show me, that I may look at You, and God answered that he would not see Him, but should look at the mountain. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "the appointed time". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 90}

The reason for Moses' asking his Lord to look at Him is given in report 15073, from al-Suddi, through Musa ibn Harun, Amr and Asbat: when his Lord spoke to him he wished to look at Him and asked, and was told to look at the mountain. The mountain was ringed with angels, a fire was set around the angels, and angels around the fire, and then his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 90}

Report 15074, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far from his father, on [[passage/quran-19-52|19:52]]: someone who met the Companions told me that the Lord drew him so near that he heard the scratching of the pen, and at that moment, in longing for Him, he said what he said. The editor glosses "scratching" as a sustained, sharp sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 91}

Report 15075, from Abu Bakr al-Hudhali, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: when Moses remained after the thirty nights and heard God's speech, he longed to look at Him; he was told that no human could bear to look at Him in this world and that whoever looked at Him would die. Moses answered that he had heard His speech and longed to look, and that looking and then dying was dearer to him than living without seeing; so he was told to look at the mountain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 91}

Report 15076, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali, on "show me": give me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 91}

Report 15077, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Moses made Aaron his successor over the Children of Israel and charged him, and set out in haste to meet his Lord out of longing, while Aaron stayed with the Israelites, and the Samaritan was with him, leading them along Moses' track. When God spoke to Moses he hoped to see Him. Ibn Ishaq says that this is what has reached us in God's Book, and that the People of the Book claim there was an explanation, a story, further matters and an exchange that have not come to us in God's Book, and God knows best. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 91}

Ibn Ishaq then relays, from a scholar of the early Scripture reports, what they find in their own account of Moses' request: that he purified himself and his clothes and fasted for the meeting, and when he came to Mount Sinai and God drew near to him in the cloud and spoke to him, he glorified, praised, magnified and sanctified Him with entreaty and sorrowful weeping. He then praised God's greatness, His oneness and might, His throne and its veils and the distance between Him and His angels, and said that when God wills a matter among His hosts He sends the spirit, which enters whomever He wills of His prophets, and they convey it to those He wishes. The editor adds a note on correcting the reading "spirit" and refers to sura Ghafir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 92}

In his prayer Moses recounts God's favours to him: He made him great among the nations and before the angels, let him hear His voice and gave him His wisdom; at his prayer against Pharaoh he struck the sea with his staff and it parted; he asked for water and struck the rock; he asked food for his people and was given manna from the east and quail from the west; he complained of the heat and was shaded by the cloud. Then he asks to see His face. This is a report Ibn Ishaq takes from the People of the Book, and the editor remarks in a note on the disorder of some of its wording and on points where the printed text and manuscript differ. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 93}

His Lord answers that no one sees Him and lives, and that neither the heavens nor the earth could bear it. Moses says that to see Him and die is dearer to him than not to see Him and live, and repeats his prayer that God complete His favour through this request. He is told to make two tablets and go to the great rock at the head of the mountain and sit on it. God then commands the angels of the lowest heaven to ring the mountain and pass before Moses, glorifying with voices like thunder, and Moses is afraid and regrets his request. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 94}

The angels of the second, third, fourth and fifth heavens follow in turn, each rank greater than the last in form and voice, and each time Moses grows more fearful and weeps, while the chief of the angels tells him to be patient because what he has seen is little of much. God then commands the angels of the sixth heaven to descend on the servant who asked to see Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 95}

The rest of the account Ibn Ishaq relays from the People of the Book: the angels of the sixth heaven descend, each holding a fire like a tall palm tree, brighter than the sun, with garments like a flame, and when they glorify and sanctify, the angels of all the heavens answer with glorification of the mighty Lord who never dies; each angel has four faces. Moses raised his voice in glorification with them, weeping, and asked his Lord to remember him and not forget him, saying he did not know whether he would escape what he was in. God then had His throne made and said, pass by My servant that he may see Me, for it is little of the much he has seen. The mountain split from the Lord's greatness, the light of the throne covered Moses' mountain, the angels raised their voices together, the mountain and every tree on it trembled, and Moses fell on his face senseless. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 96}

It is then related that God sent life by His mercy, that the spirit covered him, and that the rock he was on was turned into something like a dome so that Moses would not be burned; the spirit raised him as a mother raises her unborn child when it is thrown down. Moses rose glorifying God and saying that he believed in Him and affirmed that no one sees Him and lives, and that whoever looks at His angels has his heart wrenched, then declared his repentance and praise to God who has no partner. In notes the editor remarks on the omission of "the spirit" in the printed text and on a word he is unsure is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 96}

**On "when his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain, He made it crumble and Moses fell senseless"**

Al-Tabari says: when the Lord looked down on the mountain, God made it dukk, level with the ground, and Moses fell down "sa'iq", that is, in a swoon; the editor refers to the earlier explanation of "the swoon". He adds that the interpreters said as he does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 97}

Report 15078, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Husayn ibn Muhammad al-Anqazi, Asbat, al-Suddi and Ikrima: only as much as the little finger of Him appeared; "made it crumble" means to dust, and "senseless" means in a swoon. Report 15079, from Ibn Abbas, is similar, adding that Moses stayed senseless as long as God willed. Report 15080, from Ibn Zayd: in a swoon. Report 15081, from Qatada: part of it fell upon part, and "senseless" means dead. Report 15082, from Ibn Jurayj: dead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 97}

Report 15083, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: part of it crushed part. Report 15084, from Sufyan: the mountain sank into the earth until it fell into the sea and goes along with it. Report 15085, from Abu Bakr al-Hudhali: it was uprooted and went under the earth, and will not appear until the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 98}

Report 15086, from Anas from the Prophet, through Ahmad ibn Sahl al-Wasiti, Qurra ibn Isa, al-A'mash and an unnamed man: when his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain, he pointed with his finger and made it level, and Abu Isma'il showed us with his forefinger. The editor says he found no biography for Ahmad ibn Sahl or Qurra ibn Isa, and that Ibn Kathir quoted the report and said only that its chain has an unnamed man. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 98}

Report 15087, from Anas: the Prophet recited this verse and indicated with his finger, putting his thumb on the upper joint of the little finger, and the mountain sank. The editor explains that Hammad is Ibn Salama and Thabit is the reliable al-Bunani, and that the chain's men are trustworthy. Report 15088, from Anas ibn Malik, is similar, through Hudba ibn Khalid, Hammad ibn Salama and Thabit: he put the thumb near the tip of the little finger and the mountain sank; Humayd asked Thabit, do you say this? and Thabit struck Humayd's chest, saying that the Messenger of God said it and Anas said it, so should he conceal it? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 99}

In a long note the editor records that al-Tirmidhi gave the report in the explanation of the verse and called it sound and rare, that Ahmad in his Musnad and al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak relayed it through Hammad ibn Salama, that al-Hakim called it sound on Muslim's condition and al-Dhahabi agreed, and that Ibn Kathir quoted it noting a flaw in the chain; the editor points out that Ibn Kathir has a "Layth" in the chain who is in neither the manuscript nor the printed text. The Humayd named is Humayd al-Tawil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 99}

Report 15089, from al-Rabi', through al-Muthanna, Ishaq and Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far from his father: when the covering was lifted and the mountain saw the light, it became like one of the mounds. Report 15090, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Sa'd: Moses looked at the mountain, which could not hold itself, and it began to crumble from its base, and when Moses saw what the mountain did he fell senseless. The editor comments on the printed and manuscript readings of "from its base". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 100}

**The readings of "dukk"**

The readers differed over the word. Most readers of Medina and Basra read it with the nunation, meaning that God crushed the mountain, i.e. broke it to bits, taking as support His words in sura al-Fajr and sura al-Haqqa; one of them cited a line of verse by Humayd al-Arqat describing an army that flattens the mountains' corners with the roar of its thunder while its brave horsemen swagger with thin white swords. The editor explains the line's words in a note and says he has not found it elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 100}

Most readers of Kufa read "dakka'", with the long ending and no nunation, like "hamra'" and "sawda'", among them Ikrima. Report 15091, from Ikrima, through Ahmad ibn Yusuf, al-Qasim ibn Sallam, Abbad ibn Abbad and Yazid ibn Hazim: "a mound among the mounds", adding that when God looked at the mountain it became a plain of dust. The editor gives biographies of Abbad ibn Abbad and Yazid ibn Hazim and explains that "daka'" is a low rise of clay. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 101}

The grammarians differed on what it means when read so. Some of the grammarians of Basra said that the Arabs call a she-camel "daka'" when it has no hump, and that since "mountain" is masculine the word can fit only if "like" is dropped, "He made it like a hump-less one", as in "ask the town". Some of the grammarians of Kufa said that it means He made the mountain a levelled ground, then "ground" was dropped and "daka'" put in its place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 101}

Al-Tabari then chooses the reading "dakka'" because the report from the Messenger of God shows it right: it says "the mountain sank", not "it was broken up" or "turned to dust", and when it sank and was gone the face of the ground appeared, like a camel whose hump is gone. When one part crushes another, by contrast, it breaks and crumbles and does not sink. "Daka'" stands in for "ground", hence the feminine. The meaning is therefore that when his Lord manifested Himself to the mountain it sank, and He made its place a level ground. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "swoon" and notes the printed and manuscript variants of "made feminine". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 102}

**On "when he recovered he said, glory to You, I turn to You in repentance, and I am the first of the believers"**

Al-Tabari says: when Moses' mind returned to him from his swoon, which is the recovery from the stupor into which he fell, he said "glory to You", as exaltation of You, Lord, and clearing You of being seen by anyone in this world who would then live on; "I turn to You" from my asking You for the sight; "and I am the first of the believers", in You among my people, that no one sees You in this world without perishing. A number of the interpreters said the same. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "glory". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 102}

We stop here at the foot of p. 102, before report 15092 from Ibn Waki', in the commentary on the words "when he recovered he said, glory to You" of verse 143; the next batch begins at p. 103. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 102}
