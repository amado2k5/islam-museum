## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 41 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 41 (pp. 545-552)**

Al-Tabari says on "know that whatever you take as spoils" [[passage/quran-8-41|8:41]]: this is God's teaching to the believers how to divide their spoils when they take them, saying: know, believers, that what you take of spoils. The people of knowledge differed over the meaning of "spoils" and "fay'". Some said they have two meanings, each different from the other; report 16087, from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib: when the Muslims prevail over the polytheists and over their land and take them by force, what they take of wealth they prevailed over is spoils, while the land, in our countryside here, is fay'; and he cited [[passage/quran-59-7|59:7]]. Others said spoils are what is taken by force and fay' is what comes by treaty; report 16088, from Sufyan al-Thawri: spoils are what the Muslims got by force in fighting, in which is the fifth and four fifths of it belong to whoever attended, while fay' is what they were reconciled on without fighting, in which there is no fifth, and it belongs to those God named. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 545} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 546}

Others said spoils and fay' have one meaning, and said this verse in al-Anfal abrogates the verse of al-Hashr [[passage/quran-59-7|59:7]]; report 16089, from Qatada: fay' was for these, then that was abrogated in the sura of al-Anfal by His words "know that whatever you take as spoils, a fifth is God's and the Messenger's...", so this abrogated what was before it, and the fifth was made for those who had the fay' in al-Hashr and the rest for those who fought for it. The editor says the printed edition and manuscript wrote "in the sura of al-Hashr" and that the right reading is what he gave, the point being that it abrogated what is at the start of al-Anfal, and that the report will come at length in the commentary on al-Hashr. Al-Tabari has explained earlier "spoils" as wealth reached from the wealth of those to whom God gave their religion's people the wealth, by overcoming and subduing in fighting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 546}

As for fay', it is what God returned to the Muslims of the wealth of the polytheists, which is what He gave back to them by treaty without any horse or camel being driven at it. What their swords, spears and other weapons gave back to them may also be called fay', for fay' is a verbal noun from one's saying, a thing returned, returning, when it comes back, and God returned it when He gave it back. But what God's ruling on fay' was turned to is His ruling in the sura of al-Hashr, and that is only the kind I described, not what horses and camels were driven at, for reasons al-Tabari has explained in his book on the subtleties of the rulings of the laws of religion and will explain also in the commentary on al-Hashr when he comes to it. As for the saying of those who said the verse in al-Anfal abrogates the verse in al-Hashr, it has no sense, since nothing in one of the two verses negates the ruling of the other, and he has explained the meaning of abrogation, the negation of a ruling established by a contrary ruling, in more than one place. The editor prefers the manuscript's reading in a sentence the printed edition spoiled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 547}

As for "of anything", what is meant is everything to which the name "thing" applies of what God gave the believers of the wealth of those polytheists whose wealth they overcame, of what falls to be divided, down to the thread and the needle; reports 16090, 16091 and 16092, from Mujahid: the needle is of the "thing". The editor explains that "khayt" is the needle and what is sewn with it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 547} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 548}

Then he says on "a fifth of it is for God and the Messenger, the relatives, the orphans, the poor and the traveller": the interpreters differed in its interpretation. Some said the words "a fifth of it is for God" are an opening of speech, since the world and the hereafter and all in them are God's, and the sense is only that the fifth is the Messenger's. Reports 16093 and 16094, from Qays ibn Muslim: I asked al-Hasan about it and he said this is an opening of speech, the world and the hereafter are God's, the editor showing that al-Hasan here is al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn al-Hanafiyya and not al-Hasan al-Basri; report 16095, from Ibn Abbas: when the Prophet sent out a raiding party and they took spoils, he took the fifth of the spoils and divided that fifth into five, then recited the verse, and said the words "a fifth is God's" are an opening of speech, what is in the heavens and earth is God's, so God made God's share and the Messenger's share one, the editor weakening Nahshal ibn Sa'id in its chain; reports 16096 and 16097, from Ibrahim: everything is God's, and a fifth is for God and His Messenger and what is besides is divided into four shares; report 16098, from Qatada: spoils were divided into five fifths, four fifths for those who fought for it, and the remaining fifth divided into five fifths, one fifth for God and the Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 548} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 549} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 550}

Report 16099, from al-Hasan: Abu Bakr made a bequest of a fifth of his wealth and said, shall I not be content with of my wealth what God was content with for Himself; report 16100, from Ata: God's fifth and His Messenger's fifth are one, the Prophet would carry from it and put into it what he wished, the editor pointing to Abu Ubayd's Kitab al-Amwal; report 16101, from Ibrahim: everything is God's, the fifth is for the Messenger, the relatives, the orphans, the poor and the traveller. Others said its sense is: a fifth of it is for the House of God and for the Messenger; report 16102, from Abu al-Aliya: spoils would be brought to the Messenger of God, who divided them into five, four fifths for whoever attended, then he took the fifth and struck his hand into it and took what his hand grasped and made it for the Ka'ba, which was God's share, then divided what remained into five shares, a share for the Messenger, a share for the relatives, a share for the orphans, a share for the poor and a share for the traveller; report 16103, from him, is like it and adds that he would say, do not set a portion for God, for the world and the hereafter are God's. Others said what is named for the Messenger of God in it is meant for his kin, with nothing in it for God or His Messenger; report 16104, from Ibn Abbas: spoils were divided into five fifths, four for those who fought for them, and one fifth divided into four, a quarter for God, the Messenger and the relatives, meaning the Prophet's kin, so what was for God and the Messenger was for the Prophet's kin, and the Prophet took nothing of the fifth for himself, the second quarter for the orphans, the third for the poor, the fourth for the traveller. The editor notes Abu Ubayd gave both reports in Kitab al-Amwal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 550} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 551}

Abu Ja'far said: the best of the views is that of those who said the words "a fifth of it is for God" are an opening of speech, because the authority is agreed that the fifth is not to be divided into six shares, and had God a share in it, as Abu al-Aliya said, the fifth of the spoils would have to be divided into six shares; the people of knowledge differ only about its division into five or fewer, and as for more than that we know of no one who said it but the report from Abu al-Aliya, and in the agreement of those I mentioned is the clear proof that what he chose is sound. As for those who said the Messenger's share was for the relatives, they have established a share for the Messenger even though he spent it on his kin, so the division did not leave being into five shares. Then report 16105, from Qatada: when God's Prophet took spoils, they were made into fifths, a fifth for God and His Messenger and the Muslims divided the rest; and the fifth made for God and His Messenger was for His Messenger, the relatives, the orphans, the poor and the traveller, so this fifth was five fifths. The editor corrects the manuscript's reading in this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 552}

We stop here at the foot of p. 552, after report 16105 in the commentary on 8:41; the next batch begins at p. 553. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 552}
