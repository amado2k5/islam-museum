**Commentary on verse 16: the Book's guidance to the paths of peace (pp. 144-145)**

On 'God guides by it whoever follows His good pleasure to the paths of peace', al-Tabari says God guides by this clear book, the pronoun in 'by it' referring to the book, and 'whoever follows His good pleasure' means whoever follows God's approval. The meaning of God's approval is disputed. Some said it is acceptance, praise and commendation: He accepts faith, purifies it, commends the believer for believing and describes faith as light, guidance and a criterion. Others said approval is a plain concept, the opposite of displeasure and one of His attributes in the sense reason understands, not praise, since praise is speech and one praises only what one has approved; approval is one thing and commendation another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 144}

'Paths of peace' means the ways of al-Salam, who is God. Report 11612, from al-Suddi, says the path of God, which He laid down for His servants, invited them to and sent His messengers with, is Islam, without which no deed is accepted from anyone, neither Judaism nor Christianity nor Zoroastrianism. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 145}

Then 'and brings them out of the darkness into the light by His leave': God guides by this clear book whoever follows His approval to the paths of peace and the laws of His religion, and brings them out of the darkness of disbelief and idolatry into the light and radiance of Islam by His leave; His leave here means making faith dear to them by lifting the stamp of disbelief and the seal of idolatry from their hearts, and granting them success in seeing the paths of peace. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 145}

Then 'and guides them to a straight path': He directs and steers them to a straight road, which is God's upright religion, with no crookedness in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 146}

We stop here at the foot of p. 146, at the end of the commentary on verse 16; verse 17 begins on the same page.
