**Commentary on verse 189 (pp. 303-308)**

Al-Tabari says: "it is He who created you from one soul", by the one soul meaning Adam; report 15497, from Mujahid: Adam; report 15498, from Qatada: from Adam. By "and made from it its mate" he means He made from the one soul, who is Adam, his mate Eve; report 15499, from Qatada: Eve, who was made from one of his ribs, "that he might dwell with her". By "that he might dwell with her" he means that he might take refuge with her to meet his need and pleasure. By "when he covered her" he means when he lay with her to meet his need and met his need of her, "she bore a light burden", with something omitted from the sentence, its mention left out because what appears makes it unnecessary: the sense is "when he covered her and met his need of her, she bore". The lightness of the burden is the seed Eve bore in her womb from Adam, as a woman's bearing a man's seed is light for her. As for "she passed with it", it means she went on with the seed, standing and sitting with it, and carried to term; report 15500, from Ayyub: I asked al-Hasan about this and he said, if you were an Arab you would know what it is, it is simply "she went on with it". The editor identifies Abu Umayr as al-Harith ibn Umayr al-Basri, trustworthy but spoken of, and Ayyub as al-Sakhtiyani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 303} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 304} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 305}

Report 15501, from Qatada: "she passed with it" means her pregnancy became evident; report 15502, from Mujahid: her pregnancy went on; report 15503, from al-Suddi: "she bore a light burden" is the seed, and "she passed with it" means she went on with it. Others said it means she doubted it, and report 15504, from Ibn Abbas, says she doubted whether she had conceived or not. By "when she grew heavy" he means when what was in her womb, which had been light, became heavy and her delivery drew near; one says "so-and-so grew heavy" when she became heavy with her burden, as one says "so-and-so fruited" when he has fruit; report 15505, from al-Suddi: the child grew in her womb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 305}

Then al-Tabari says: "they called on God their Lord" means Adam and Eve called their Lord and said, "our Lord, if You give us a sound one, we shall be among the thankful". The interpreters differed over the meaning of the "soundness" Adam and Eve swore that if He gave them a sound one in Eve's pregnancy they would be among the thankful. Some said that it is that the child be a boy, and report 15506, from al-Hasan, says a boy. Others said rather that the newborn be a sound human like them and not a beast; report 15507, from Abu al-Bakhtari: they feared it would be something less than a human; report 15508, from him: they feared it would not be human; report 15509, from Abu Salih: when Adam's wife conceived and grew heavy they feared it would be a beast, so they called on their Lord; report 15510, from Ibn Abbas: they feared it would be a beast. The editor identifies Zayd ibn Jubayr al-Jushami as trustworthy and narrated from by the compilers of the group. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 306}

Report 15511, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when Adam and Eve came down, desire was cast into him and he lay with her; no sooner had he lain with her than she conceived, and no sooner had she conceived than the child stirred in her womb. She said: what is this? Iblis came to her and said you have conceived and will give birth. She said: what shall I bear? He said: do you see anything on the earth but a she-camel or a cow or a ewe or a goat, or some such, and it will come out of your nose or your ears or your eyes. She said: by God, nothing of me is not too narrow for that. He said: obey me and name him Abd al-Harith, which was his name among the angels, al-Harith, and you will bear one like you both. She told Adam, who said, he is our companion, whom you know. The child died; then she conceived another and he came and said obey me and name him Abd al-Harith or you will bear a she-camel or cow or ewe or goat, or I shall kill him, for I killed the first. She told Adam and he seemed not to dislike it, and she named him Abd al-Harith; that is "if You give us a sound one", meaning one like us, and "when He gave them a sound one", one like them. The editor says the words in brackets are from al-Durr al-Manthur and the manuscript is disordered here, and at the end of the note he says these are baseless reports, as he pointed out repeatedly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 307}

Report 15512, from al-Suddi: "when she grew heavy", the child grew in her womb, and Iblis came to her, frightened her and said to her… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 307}

Report 15512 from al-Suddi is completed: how do you know what is in your belly? Perhaps it is a dog or a pig or a donkey, and how do you know from where it will come out? From your rear so that it kills you, or from your front, or your belly will split and kill you? That was when they called on God their Lord, "if You give us a sound one", meaning like us, "we shall be among the thankful". Then al-Tabari says: the right view is that God reported of Adam and Eve that they called on God their Lord over Eve's pregnancy and swore that if He gave them what was in Eve's womb sound, they would be among the thankful to God. "Soundness" may cover many senses: soundness in the evenness of the make, soundness in religion, soundness in reason and judgement; and since no report from the Messenger makes it binding that it is one sense and not another, and no indication in reason, it must be taken as general as God made it general, so that they said "if You give us a sound one" in all the senses of soundness. As for "we shall be among the thankful", it means among those who thank You for the child You gave sound. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "soundness". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 307} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 308}
