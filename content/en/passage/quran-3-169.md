## Summary
Verse 169 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 169 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 169: the martyrs alive with their Lord (8204-8205, pp. 384-385).** Abu Ja'far: the sense is "do not suppose": Ibn Ishaq (8204), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama, "do not suppose" (the report is in the Sira, 3: 126, a continuation of reports ending with 8199). Those killed in God's way are those killed at Uhud among the companions of the Messenger of God; do not suppose them, Muhammad, dead, feeling nothing, finding no pleasure and no enjoyment: they are alive with God, enjoying His provision, glad and delighted at what He gave them of His honour, bounty, abundant reward and gift (p. 384). Ibn 'Abbas (8205), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, and also through Yunus ibn 'Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Wahb, from Isma'il ibn 'Ayyash, from Ibn Ishaq, from Isma'il ibn Umayya, from Abu al-Zubayr al-Makki: the Messenger of God said: when your brothers were struck at Uhud, God put their spirits in the crops of green birds that come to the rivers of Paradise, eat of its fruits and shelter in lanterns of gold in the shade of the Throne; when they found how good their drink and food and their resting-place were, they said: if only our brothers knew what God did for us, so that they would not turn from jihad nor shrink from war! God said: I will convey it to them for you; and God sent down these verses on His messenger (p. 385). The editor's note explains that "nakala 'an 'aduwwihi" means he was fearful, drew back and turned away from him in dread. In tracing the hadith he says that Abu al-Zubayr is Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Tadrus al-Makki, a reliable follower, of whom it is said that he did not hear from Ibn 'Abbas, and he cites Ibn Abi Hatim's Marasil (p. 71) for Ibn 'Uyayna's report that people say al-Makki did not hear from Ibn 'Abbas and for his father's saying that he saw Ibn 'Abbas; that Ahmad related it in the Musnad (2388) through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim ibn Sa'd, from his father, from Ibn Ishaq, with this chain, and then (2389) similarly through 'Uthman ibn Abi Shayba, from 'Abdallah ibn Idris, from Ibn Ishaq, adding Sa'id ibn Jubayr between Abu al-Zubayr and Ibn 'Abbas; that Abu Dawud (Sunan, 2520) and al-Hakim (Mustadrak, 2: 297-298) relate it likewise through 'Uthman ibn Abi Shayba, al-Hakim calling it sound on Muslim's condition though the two did not extract it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; that Ibn Kathir (2: 290-291) mentions it from the first Musnad narration, refers to al-Tabari's, then to the addition of Sa'id ibn Jubayr in Abu Dawud and al-Hakim, and says this is better established, as Sufyan al-Thawri related it from Salim al-Aflas, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn 'Abbas; and that al-Suyuti (2: 95) mentions it and adds Hannad, 'Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Bayhaqi in the Dala'il. He adds that the phrase for "the goodness of their resting-place" is in the Musnad with a different word for "return", that he found in the Riyadh manuscript of the Musnad another copy with the word in the margin, which is sounder and agrees with what Ibn Kathir quoted from the Musnad and with Abu Dawud and al-Hakim, and agrees with the wording of the Qur'an in [[passage/quran-25-24|25:24]]; and he refers to later hadith of Ibn Mas'ud (8206-8208 and 8218-8219) and Ibn 'Abbas (8209-8213), with numbers garbled in the scan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 384} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 385}
