## Summary
Verse 169 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 169 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 169: the martyrs alive with their Lord (8204-8205, pp. 384-385).** Abu Ja'far: the sense is "do not suppose": Ibn Ishaq (8204), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama, "do not suppose" (the report is in the Sira, 3: 126, a continuation of reports ending with 8199). Those killed in God's way are those killed at Uhud among the companions of the Messenger of God; do not suppose them, Muhammad, dead, feeling nothing, finding no pleasure and no enjoyment: they are alive with God, enjoying His provision, glad and delighted at what He gave them of His honour, bounty, abundant reward and gift (p. 384). Ibn 'Abbas (8205), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, and also through Yunus ibn 'Abd al-A'la, from Ibn Wahb, from Isma'il ibn 'Ayyash, from Ibn Ishaq, from Isma'il ibn Umayya, from Abu al-Zubayr al-Makki: the Messenger of God said: when your brothers were struck at Uhud, God put their spirits in the crops of green birds that come to the rivers of Paradise, eat of its fruits and shelter in lanterns of gold in the shade of the Throne; when they found how good their drink and food and their resting-place were, they said: if only our brothers knew what God did for us, so that they would not turn from jihad nor shrink from war! God said: I will convey it to them for you; and God sent down these verses on His messenger (p. 385). The editor's note explains that "nakala 'an 'aduwwihi" means he was fearful, drew back and turned away from him in dread. In tracing the hadith he says that Abu al-Zubayr is Muhammad ibn Muslim ibn Tadrus al-Makki, a reliable follower, of whom it is said that he did not hear from Ibn 'Abbas, and he cites Ibn Abi Hatim's Marasil (p. 71) for Ibn 'Uyayna's report that people say al-Makki did not hear from Ibn 'Abbas and for his father's saying that he saw Ibn 'Abbas; that Ahmad related it in the Musnad (2388) through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim ibn Sa'd, from his father, from Ibn Ishaq, with this chain, and then (2389) similarly through 'Uthman ibn Abi Shayba, from 'Abdallah ibn Idris, from Ibn Ishaq, adding Sa'id ibn Jubayr between Abu al-Zubayr and Ibn 'Abbas; that Abu Dawud (Sunan, 2520) and al-Hakim (Mustadrak, 2: 297-298) relate it likewise through 'Uthman ibn Abi Shayba, al-Hakim calling it sound on Muslim's condition though the two did not extract it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; that Ibn Kathir (2: 290-291) mentions it from the first Musnad narration, refers to al-Tabari's, then to the addition of Sa'id ibn Jubayr in Abu Dawud and al-Hakim, and says this is better established, as Sufyan al-Thawri related it from Salim al-Aflas, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn 'Abbas; and that al-Suyuti (2: 95) mentions it and adds Hannad, 'Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Bayhaqi in the Dala'il. He adds that the phrase for "the goodness of their resting-place" is in the Musnad with a different word for "return", that he found in the Riyadh manuscript of the Musnad another copy with the word in the margin, which is sounder and agrees with what Ibn Kathir quoted from the Musnad and with Abu Dawud and al-Hakim, and agrees with the wording of the Qur'an in [[passage/quran-25-24|25:24]]; and he refers to later hadith of Ibn Mas'ud (8206-8208 and 8218-8219) and Ibn 'Abbas (8209-8213), with numbers garbled in the scan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 384} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 385}

**Ibn Mas'ud's report on the spirits of the martyrs (8206-8208, pp. 386-387).** Ibn Mas'ud (8206), through Ibn Humayd, from Jarir ibn 'Abd al-Hamid, and also from Salama, both from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from al-A'mash, from Abu al-Duha, from Masruq ibn al-Ajda': we asked 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud about these verses, and he said: we asked about them and were told that when your brothers were struck at Uhud God put their spirits in the crops of green birds that come to the rivers of Paradise, eat of its fruit and shelter in gold lanterns in the shade of the Throne; God looks at them and says: servants of Mine, what do you wish that I may add? They say: our Lord, nothing above what You have given us, Paradise we eat of wherever we wish; three times, and in the last they say they would love that their spirits be returned to their bodies and then they be returned to the world and fight in it until they are killed again. The notes on p. 386 say that "nothing above what You gave us" means nothing is above that; cite Abu Dharr al-Khushani that the word "Paradise" is transmitted in the genitive as apposition, in the nominative as predicate of an implied subject, and could be accusative with the sense of "You gave us"; that the printed text and the manuscript both have "we choose", a corruption of the Sira's "we love", which the editor kept, while Muslim's version has "we want", the two being the same; that Abu al-Duha is Muslim ibn Subayh al-Hamdani, discussed repeatedly, last at 7217; and that al-A'mash had two teachers for the hadith and heard it from both from Masruq, the editor referring the tracing to the last. In 8207, through al-Hasan ibn Abi Yahya al-Maqdisi, from Wahb ibn Jarir, from Shu'ba, from al-A'mash, from Abu al-Duha, from Masruq: the like, adding in God's words that He had decreed that they would not return. The editor says he has not managed to identify al-Hasan ibn Abi Yahya al-Maqdisi, al-Tabari's teacher, who appeared before at 7216; that the printed text has "al-Hasan ibn Yahya al-'Abdi" and the manuscript's reading is right; and that this repeats the previous report by this route. In 8208, through Ibn al-Muthanna, from Ibn Abi 'Adi, from Shu'ba, from Sulayman (al-A'mash), from 'Abdallah ibn Murra, from Masruq: we asked 'Abdallah about the spirits of the martyrs, and but for 'Abdallah nobody would have told us; he said: the spirits of the martyrs are with God in the crops of green birds in lanterns under the Throne, roaming Paradise where they wish and returning to their lanterns, and their Lord looks at them and says, what do you want? and they say, we want to return to the world and be killed again. The editor says that 'Abdallah ibn Murra al-Hamdani al-Kharifi was a reliable follower whom the six collections used, with entries in the Tahdhib, Ibn Sa'd (6: 203) and Ibn Abi Hatim; that Muslim (2: 98) relates the hadith with chains and a wording longer than this, al-Tirmidhi (4: 84-85) too, Ibn Kathir (2: 289) quoting it from Sahih Muslim, and al-Suyuti (2: 96) attributing it also to 'Abd al-Razzaq's Musannaf, al-Firyabi, Sa'id ibn Mansur, Hannad, 'Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tabarani and al-Bayhaqi in the Dala'il; that he has not found it in the Musnad as far as he has verified, unless it lies in a musnad of another Companion not yet reached by his edition; and that it will come a fourth time (8218) and a fifth (8219) from 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud's son Abu 'Ubayda (given in a note on p. 387 as Abu 'Ubayd). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 386} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 387}

**Ibn 'Abbas's reports: the martyrs on Bariq (8209-8213, pp. 387-388).** Ibn 'Abbas (8209), through Abu Kurayb, from 'Abd al-Rahim ibn Sulayman and 'Abda ibn Sulayman, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq, from al-Harith ibn Fudayl, from Mahmud ibn Labid: the Messenger of God said: the martyrs are on Bariq, a river at the gate of Paradise, in a green dome; and 'Abda said: in a green meadow; their provision comes out to them from Paradise morning and evening. The editor notes that this report came earlier with this chain at 2323 (as given in the scan) and was discussed at length there, and that four chains follow it (8210-8213) repeating it: Abu Kurayb from Yunus ibn Bukayr (8210) with the like, saying "in a green dome"; Ibn Waki' from Ibn Idris (8211); Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from al-Harith ibn al-Fudayl al-Ansari from Mahmud ibn Labid al-Ansari (8212); and Yunus from Ibn Wahb from Isma'il ibn 'Ayyash from Ibn Ishaq (8213) to the same effect. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 387} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 388}

**Jabir's report of the reviving of his father (8214, pp. 388-389).** Jabir ibn 'Abdallah (8214), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from some of his companions, from 'Abdallah ibn Muhammad ibn 'Aqil ibn Abi Talib: the Messenger of God said to me: shall I give you good news, Jabir? I said, yes. He said: your father, when he was struck at Uhud, God revived him and said to him: what do you love that I should do for you, 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr? He said: Lord, I love that You return me to the world and I fight for You and am killed again. The editor says that Ibn Ishaq related this hadith leaving his teacher unnamed, which weakens the chain; that it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 127); that its sense came from Jabir by another sound chain in Ahmad's Musnad (14938), through 'Ali ibn al-Madini, from Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna, from Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn Rabi'a al-Sulami, from 'Abdallah ibn Muhammad ibn 'Aqil, from Jabir, in which God says He has decreed that they do not return to it; that Muhammad ibn 'Ali ibn Rabi'a al-Sulami is trustworthy (Ibn Ma'in and others), with entries in Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Bukhari's Kabir and Ibn Sa'd (6: 257), none naming a fault in him; that Ibn Kathir (2: 289) cites it from the Musnad and says Ahmad alone related it by this route, pointing to al-Tirmidhi (4: 84), who related its sense at greater length from another route and called it good and unusual from this route; that al-Suyuti (2: 95) gives the long version and attributes it also to Ibn Majah, Ibn Abi 'Asim in al-Sunna, Ibn Khuzayma, al-Tabarani, al-Hakim, who graded it sound, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Bayhaqi in the Dala'il, and the editor refers to the Mustadrak (3: 203-204); and he identifies Jabir's father as 'Abdallah ibn 'Amr ibn Haram al-Ansari al-Khazraji al-Salami, an eminent Companion, one of the people of al-'Aqaba, present at Badr and one of the leaders (naqibs), who was martyred at Uhud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 388} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 389}

**Qatada, al-Rabi' and Ibn Jurayj (8215-8217, pp. 389-390).** Qatada (8215), through Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that some men among the companions of the Messenger said, if only we knew what our brothers killed at Uhud did, and God sent down the two verses about that; and we were told that the spirits of the martyrs make each other's acquaintance in white birds that eat of the fruits of Paradise, and that their dwellings are the lote-tree (the report came at length as 2319 in the scan). Al-Rabi' (8216), through 'Ammar, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father: the like, except that he said they make acquaintance in green and white birds, adding: it was mentioned to us from some of them that they are those killed at Badr and Uhud. Ibn Jurayj (8217), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Muhammad ibn Qays ibn Makhrama: they said, Lord, have we no messenger to tell the Prophet about us what You gave us? and God said, I am your messenger, and ordered Jibril to bring these two verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 389} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 390}

**Further chains from Ibn Mas'ud (8218-8219, pp. 390-391).** Ibn Mas'ud (8218), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from 'Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from al-A'mash, from 'Abdallah ibn Murra, from Masruq: the spirits of the martyrs are with God like green birds with lanterns hung from the Throne, roaming Paradise where they wish; your Lord looked at them and said, do you wish for anything I could add? They said, Lord, do we not roam Paradise wherever we like? Then He looked at them a third time and said, do you wish for anything? They said, return our spirits to our bodies so that we fight in Your way once more; and He was silent to them. The editor says 'Abd al-Razzaq relates it in the Musannaf (3: 115, photographed manuscript) with this chain and wording, but the Musannaf copy lacks "green" for the birds, and the phrase "then He looked a third time" is likewise so in the Musannaf with the second look omitted, so this is not a copyists' omission but an abridgment in the report, and that this is the fourth chain for Ibn Mas'ud's hadith. In 8219, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn 'Uyayna, from 'Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib, from Abu 'Ubayda, from 'Abdallah: they said the third time, when He said do you wish for anything that I may add, "convey our greeting to our Prophet and tell him that we are pleased and that He is pleased with us". The editor says that Abu 'Ubayda ibn 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud is a reliable follower, that his name is most probably his byname (it was also said to be 'Amir), and that his report from his father is broken because his father died when he was young, Abu Hatim and others holding with certainty that he did not hear from him (al-Marasil, pp. 91-92), citing al-Tirmidhi's sound chain through 'Amr ibn Murra, who asked Abu 'Ubayda if he remembered anything of 'Abdallah and was told no; that al-Tirmidhi relates the hadith by this route from Ibn Abi 'Umar, from Sufyan ibn 'Uyayna, making it follow al-A'mash's report from 'Abdallah ibn Murra and calling it good; and that the closing words are in the passive, meaning that God was pleased with us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 390} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 391}

**Ibn Ishaq, al-Dahhak, al-Suddi and al-Hasan (8220-8223, pp. 391-392).** Ibn Ishaq (8220), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: God said to His prophet, to make the believers desire the reward of Paradise and to make killing light for them, in effect that He had revived them, so that they are with Him provided for in the ease of Paradise and its bounty, rejoicing in what God gave them of His reward for their jihad for Him (the report is in the Sira, 3: 126, completing reports ending with 8204). Al-Dahhak (8221), through al-Husayn, from Abu Mu'adh, from 'Ubayd ibn Sulayman: the Muslims used to ask their Lord to show them a day like Badr in which they might do well and be granted martyrdom, Paradise and life in provision; they met the idolaters at Uhud, and God took martyrs from among them, who are those He mentions in the verse. Al-Suddi (8222), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat: he mentioned the martyrs through the end of the two verses and claimed that the spirits of the martyrs are in the crops of green birds in gold lanterns hung from the Throne, grazing morning and evening in Paradise and spending the night in the lanterns; when they roam, a caller calls, what do you want, what do you wish? and they say, we are in what our souls desired; their Lord asks again and they answer so; a third time they say the same but that they would love their spirits to be returned to their bodies, having seen the excellence of the reward. The editor notes that "claimed" does not mean a false statement but simply a statement, true at times and false at others, citing a line of Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt (described, not quoted), and that the printed text has a different wording where the manuscript's was kept. Al-Hasan (8223), through Ibn Humayd, from 'Abbad, from Ibrahim ibn Ma'mar (as read in the scan): the son of Adam kept seeking praise (the note: he seeks praise by his deed) until he became alive and never dies; then he recited the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 391} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 392}

**The report of Bi'r Ma'una (8224, pp. 392-394).** Anas ibn Malik (8224), through Muhammad ibn Marzuq, from 'Umar ibn Yunus, from 'Ikrima (whose name fell out of the chain in the manuscript and printed text and was restored by the editor from the Tarikh and Ibn Kathir's tafsir), from Ishaq ibn Abi Talha, about the companions whom the Prophet sent to the people of Bi'r Ma'una, "I do not know whether forty or seventy", the water being held by 'Amir ibn al-Tufayl al-Ja'fari: the party went out until they came to a cave overlooking the water and hid in it, and then some said to the others, which of you will carry the Messenger's message to the people of this water? A man, "I think Ibn Milhan al-Ansari", said, I will. He went out to a clan of theirs, sat cross-legged before the houses and said: people of Bi'r Ma'una, I am the messenger of the Messenger of God to you; I testify that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, so believe in God and His messenger. A man came out to him from the side of the tent with a spear and thrust it into his flank until it came out the other side, and he said, God is greatest, I have won, by the Lord of the Ka'ba! They followed his trail until they reached his companions and 'Amir ibn al-Tufayl killed them all. Ishaq said: Anas ibn Malik told me that God sent down a Qur'an about them which was raised after we had read it for a time, and God sent down [[passage/quran-3-169|3:169]]. In the notes: the "side of the tent" is the lower part of the tent's cloth next to the ground; the manuscript's "they killed them" was corrected from the Tarikh and other references; and the text in al-Tabari's Tarikh is that God sent down about them a Qur'an telling their people that they had met their Lord and He was pleased with them and they with Him, which was then abrogated and raised after they had read it for a time (this describes the sense and does not quote it). On pp. 393-394 the editor says that Muhammad ibn Marzuq, al-Tabari's teacher, is Muhammad ibn Muhammad ibn Marzuq, named after his grandfather, with entries in the Tahdhib, the Tarikh Baghdad (3: 199-200) and Ibn Abi Hatim; that "'Umar ibn Yunus al-Yamami" appears in the sources as "'Amr ibn Yunus", also in al-Tabari's Tarikh and Ibn Kathir's tafsir, and the error may be al-Tabari's own since it is unlikely that copyists of three sources made the same one, and no narrator is known by that name; that 'Ikrima is 'Ikrima ibn 'Ammar al-Yamami (biography at 2185), not the freedman of Ibn 'Abbas; that Ishaq ibn Abi Talha is Ishaq ibn 'Abdallah ibn Abi Talha al-Ansari, a reliable follower and authority used by the six collections, his father Abu Talha being Zayd ibn Sahl, Anas ibn Malik's maternal half-brother; that al-Tabari also relates the report in the Tarikh (3: 36), Ibn Kathir quotes it (2: 288) and al-Hafiz refers to it in the Fath (7: 298); that "Abu Milhan" in the manuscript, printed text and Ibn Kathir's quotation is an old copyists' error for "Ibn Milhan", that is Haram ibn Milhan al-Ansari, Anas's maternal uncle, brother of Umm Sulaym bint Milhan; that the Bi'r Ma'una report is firm from Anas by several routes, in Ahmad's Musnad (13228 and 14119) through Hammam from Ishaq ibn 'Abdallah ibn Abi Talha, in al-Bukhari (7: 297-299) through Musa ibn Isma'il from Hammam, and in Ibn Sa'd; that part of its sense came abridged earlier in al-Tabari's tafsir from Qatada from Anas; that the story is detailed in Ibn Kathir's Tarikh (4: 71-74) and Ibn Hazm's Jawami' al-Sira (pp. 178-180); and that Ahmad relates part of this sense from a hadith of Ibn Mas'ud (3902). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 392} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 393} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 394}

Report 8225 opens at the top of p. 394 with its chain (Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir) and continues on p. 395. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 394}
