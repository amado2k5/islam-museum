**Commentary on verse 175 (pp. 252-261)**

Al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: recite to your people, Muhammad, the tidings of him to whom We gave Our signs, meaning his report and story. The signs of God that were given to him were, it is said, the Greatest Name of God, and it is also said prophethood. The interpreters differed over him: some said he was a man of the Children of Israel, and the editor refers to the account of Bal'am ibn Ba'ur in al-Tabari's History and to the earlier explanations of "recite" and "tidings". Reports 15381 to 15389, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud by way of Abu al-Duha from Masruq: he is Bal'am, his father's name being given in several spellings in the reports, and in one narration, that of Shu'ba, no father's name at all; one of them from Ibn Mas'ud says a man of the Children of Israel called Bal'am. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 252} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 254}

Report 15387, from Ibn Abbas: he is Bal'am ibn Ba'ir; report 15390, from Ibn Abbas by the route of Ali ibn Abi Talha: a man of the city of the giants called Bal'am; reports 15391 to 15393, from Mujahid: Bal'am ibn Ba'ur, of the Children of Israel; reports 15394 to 15398, from Ikrima and Mujahid: he is Bal'am; and report 15399, from Ibn Abbas by the route of Mujahid: Bal'am. In Ibn Abbas's report is the phrase "and Thaqif said, he is Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt"; the editor notes that the printed edition dropped it though it stands in the manuscript, that he does not know whether it is al-Tabari's words, Ibn Abbas's or some narrator's, and that it is likelier a narrator's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 254} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 255}

Others said Bal'am was from the people of Yemen, and report 15400, from Ibn Abbas, says he is a man called Bal'am of the people of Yemen. Others said he was a Canaanite, and report 15401, from Ibn Abbas by the route of Ali, says a man of the city of the giants called Bal'am. Others said he is Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt, and report 15402, from Abd Allah ibn Amr, says he is Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt. The editor identifies Sa'id ibn al-Sa'ib al-Thaqafi, Abu Hafs, as trustworthy, counted by some among the abdal and whose tears never dried; Ghutayf ibn Abi Sufyan as a trustworthy Follower; and Nafi ibn Asim ibn Urwa ibn Mas'ud al-Thaqafi as a trustworthy Follower. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 255}

Reports 15403 and 15404, from Nafi ibn Asim: Abd Allah ibn Amr said, he is your companion, Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt; the editor explains that he said so because Nafi is Thaqafi like Umayya. Report 15405, from Abd Allah ibn Amr on the words "but he clung to the earth and followed his desire": he is Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt; reports 15406 to 15408 are to the same effect, the last saying "he is Umayya". Report 15409, from Abd al-Malik ibn Umayr: they discussed this verse in the mosque of Damascus, some saying it was revealed about Bal'am ibn Bahura, others about the monk, and Abd Allah ibn Amr ibn al-As came out to them and they asked about whom it was revealed; he said: it was revealed about Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt al-Thaqafi. The editor identifies the monk as Abu Amir al-Rahib, Abd Amr ibn Sayfi ibn Malik ibn al-Nu'man, whom the Prophet called Abu Amir the sinner and whose story is well known in the biographies. Report 15410, from al-Kalbi: he is Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt, while Qatada was unsure, some saying Bal'am and some Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 256} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 257}

Then al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over the signs he was given. Some said they were the Greatest Name of God. Report 15411, from al-Suddi: when the forty years were completed, those mentioned in [[passage/quran-5-26|5:26]], God sent Joshua son of Nun as a prophet, who called the Children of Israel and told them he was a prophet and that God had commanded him to fight the giants; they pledged to him and believed him, and a man of the Children of Israel called Bal'am went off, a scholar who knew the Greatest Name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 257}

Report 15411 from al-Suddi is completed: [the man] taught the Greatest Name, kept hidden, and disbelieved, and he went to the giants and said do not fear the Children of Israel, for when you go out to fight them I will make a prayer against them and they will perish. He had of the world whatever he wished, except that he could not go to women because of their size, so he would lie with a she-donkey of his; and he is the one of whom God says "he stripped himself of them", meaning he was given sight. The editor says the printed edition had a different reading of the phrase on size, that he took it to mean the great size of the women of the giants, that the "atan" is the female donkey, and that the printed edition and the History differ on the word glossed as "given sight" and he keeps the manuscript; al-Tabari gave it in his History and it will come in full as report 15423. Report 15412, from Ibn Abbas: he is a man called Bal'am who knew the Greatest Name of God; report 15413, from Ibn Zayd: he asked God for nothing but He gave it to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 258}
Others said the signs he was given were a book of God's books, and report 15414, from Ibn Abbas, says there was in the Children of Israel Bal'am ibn Ba'ur, who was given a book, the editor referring to its long form as report 15432. Others said he was given prophethood, and report 15415, from Mujahid, says he was a prophet among the Children of Israel, meaning Bal'am, given prophethood, whom his people bribed to keep silent, which he did, leaving them as they were; report 15416, from Sayyar, says he was a man called Bal'am, given prophethood and answered in prayer, the editor referring to its full form as report 15420. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 258} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 259}
Al-Tabari then chooses: the right view is that God commanded His prophet to recite to his people the story of a man to whom He gave His proofs and evidences, which are the "signs", and he has shown earlier that signs means proofs and marks. It may be that the one to whom God gave this was Bal'am, or Umayya; likewise if the signs are a proof in the sense of one of God's books sent down to one of His prophets, which the man mentioned learnt, it may be Bal'am or Umayya, since Umayya is said to have read some of the books of the People of the Book. But if they mean a book sent down to the one whose story the Prophet was told to recite, or the Greatest Name, or prophethood, it cannot be Umayya, because the community does not differ that he was given none of that; there is no report saying which was meant and which of the two men is intended that would settle it, and no indication in reason. So the right course is to say of it what God said and to affirm the apparent sense of the revelation as it came. The editor explains the construction of the sentence and that the manuscript and printed edition had a wrong word in it, which he corrects. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 259} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 260}
As for "he stripped himself of them", it means he went out of the signs God had given him and disowned them, and the interpreters said the like. Report 15417, from Ibn Abbas: when Moses camped against the giants with those with him, Bal'am's cousins and his people came to him and said Moses is a fierce man with many troops, and if he prevails over us he will destroy us, so ask God to turn Moses and those with him away from us. He said: if I ask God to turn Moses and those with him away, my world and my hereafter are lost; but they did not cease until he prayed against them, and God stripped him of what he had been in, which is the words "he stripped himself of them, and Satan followed him, and he was among the misled". Report 15418, from Ibn Abbas: God had given him His signs and he left them; report 15419: "he stripped himself of them", knowledge was taken from him. Al-Tabari says "Satan followed him" means he made him a follower of himself, going to his order in disobedience to God, and "he was among the misled" means among the lost, for his straying, his going against his Lord's command and his obeying Satan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 260} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 261}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
