## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And what is that in your right hand, O Moses": And what is this that is in your right hand, O Moses? The 'b' in the phrase "in your right" is from the connection to that, and the Arabs connect that and that as the one who connects the one who is connected to the one who is connected to it. From this is the saying of Yazid ibn Mufragh: 'Lentils have no authority over you, I have secured you, and this is a free one, as if he said: And the one you carry is free.' Perhaps he might say: And what is the reason for God informing Moses about what is in his hand? Was he not aware that what was in his hand was a staff? He was told: This is different from what I went to. Rather, the Almighty said this to him when He wanted to turn it into a snake that would satisfy, which is a piece of wood. He warned him about it and confirmed that it was a piece of wood to lean on and to graze on his sheep, to show him His power over whatever He willed, to magnify His authority, and to carry out His command in what He loved by turning it into a snake that scurried, if He intended to make it a sign for Moses along with all His signs to Pharaoh and his people.
