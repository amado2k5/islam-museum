## Summary
Verse 1 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 1 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Alif, Lam, Mim, Sad", al-Tabari says the commentators differ on the meaning. Some said it means "I am God, I detail": he gives (14310) from Ibn Abbas this, and (14311) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr the same. Others said it is the spelling out of the letters of God's name "al-Musawwir" (the Fashioner), which is (14312) from al-Suddi. Others said it is a name among God's names by which our Lord swears, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 293}

which is (14313) from Ibn Abbas, that it is an oath God swore and is one of God's names. Others said it is a name among the names of the Qur'an: (14314) and (14315) from Qatada. Others said they are disjointed letters of the alphabet; others that they belong to the abjad reckoning; others that they are letters containing many meanings through which God showed His creation what He intends by all that; and others that they are the letters of God's greatest name. Al-Tabari says he gave all of this earlier with the reports on it, each party's reasoning and what is right in his view, with evidence, which spares repeating it here, and the editor refers to the earlier place and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 294}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
