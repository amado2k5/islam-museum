## Summary
Verse 4 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 4 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-3-4|3:4]]), pp. 161 to 164. Al-Tabari says that the first clause (the close of the mention of the books begun in the previous verse) means that God sent the Torah down on Moses and the Gospel on Jesus before the scripture He sent to Muhammad, and made that a clarification for the people from God of what they disagreed about: God's oneness, belief in His messengers, your description, Muhammad, as His prophet and messenger, and other laws of the religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 162} In a footnote on p. 162 the editor explains that the print wrote a word with another sense and the manuscript's letters are unclear, and that he preferred the reading 'your description' because people disagreed about Muhammad's description when God described him as prophet and messenger, as the Najran Christian delegation also did, as is clear from their account in the Sira of Ibn Hisham; the print's reading is, in his view, unsuitable in context and meaning.

In 6559, Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said): they are two books God sent down, containing clarification from God and protection for whoever takes them, believes them and acts on what is in them. In 6560, Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq): the Torah on Moses and the Gospel on Jesus, as He sent books to those before him; the editor says it is the remainder of the earlier reports ending at 6556, and that the print wrote 'on those before the two of them', the right wording being from the manuscript and the Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 162}

Al-Tabari then explains the mention of the Furqan as the separation between truth and falsehood in what the factions and the people of the religions disagreed about regarding Jesus and others, recalling that he had shown earlier that al-furqan is the pattern fu'lan from 'God separated truth from falsehood', that is, divided them by helping the truth against falsehood, either by the conclusive argument or by overpowering and prevailing with strength. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 163} The editor puts the verb in the past tense rather than the present of the manuscript and print, refers to part 1 (pp. 98-99) and part 3 (p. 448), and says al-ayd means intensity and strength and that al-aydi in the print is an error. Al-Tabari adds that the interpreters said this, though some direct the meaning to the separation in the matter of Jesus and some to the separation in the rulings of the laws.

For the first group, 6561 gives Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr (through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq): the separation between truth and falsehood in what the factions disagreed on of the matter of Jesus and others; the editor says it is the remainder of the reports ending at 6560. For the second group, 6562 gives Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said): it is the Qur'an, which He sent to Muhammad and by which He separated truth from falsehood; in it He made lawful what is lawful, forbade what is forbidden, laid down its laws, set its limits, ordained its obligations, explained its explanation, commanded obedience to it and forbade disobedience. In 6563, al-Rabi' (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father): the Furqan is the Qur'an, which separated truth from falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 163}

Al-Tabari says, as his own choice, that what he gave from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr is sounder than what he gave from Qatada and al-Rabi', and that the meaning of the Furqan here is God's separation between His prophet Muhammad and those who disputed with him about Jesus and his other affairs, by the conclusive argument that cut off their excuse and the excuse of their like among those who disbelieve in God. His reason is that God's report of sending down the Qur'an already preceded, in the previous verse, His report of sending down the Torah and the Gospel, and there is no doubt that the scripture there is the Qur'an, so there is no cause to repeat it, repeating adding nothing beyond what the first mention gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 164}

He then begins the next clause: by those who disbelieved in God's signs al-Tabari means those who denied God's marks and proofs of His oneness and divinity and that Jesus is His servant, and who took the Messiah as god and lord or claimed him as God's son; for them there is severe punishment from God on the Day of Resurrection. The signs are God's marks, proofs and arguments; the editor refers to the language indexes under the roots for disbelief and for sign. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 164}

We stop at the end of p. 164, in the explanation of this clause. These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices and arguments are his, and the editor's notes and references are his; the Christian positions in these reports are attributed to those who held them as al-Tabari relates, with no museum verdict.
