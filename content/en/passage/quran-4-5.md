## Summary
Verse 5 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 5 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of the commentary on verse 5 (p. 560).** Abu Ja'far al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the foolish whom God forbade His servants to give their property to; some said they are women and children, and he begins to list the reports with report 8523, through Mahmud ibn Bashshar from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, which the page end cuts off and which continues on p. 561. In the editor's notes: both copies give the verse's context only to the word for "support", but Abu Ja'far's commentary covers the rest of the verse, as will come at p. 571, so the editor completed it; and he refers to the earlier explanation of the words for foolishness and the foolish (6: 129 and 90; 3: 293-295; 1: 57-60).

**Reports that "the foolish" are women and children (pp. 561-563, reports 8523-8538).** The end of report 8523 (through Muhammad ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi) closes with Israil from Abd al-Karim from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the orphans and the women. Al-Hasan al-Basri: do not give the young and the women (8524, through al-Muthanna from Amr ibn Awn from Hushaym from Yunus); the woman and the boy (8525, through Ibn Bashshar from Yazid ibn Zurayʿ from Yunus); the women and the young, and women are the most foolish of the foolish (8526, through Hushaym from Sharik from Abu Hamza); and in Mamar's report from him (8527) they are your foolish son and your foolish wife, after which the report mentions that the Prophet said: fear God concerning the two weak ones, the orphan and the woman. Al-Suddi: women and boys (8528, through al-Himmani from Humayd from Abd al-Rahman al-Ru'asi, who traces it back to Abd Allah); the child and the woman (8529, through Asbat). Al-Dahhak: a man's child and his wife, who is the most foolish (8530); the children and the women, "so that they would be lords over you" (8531, through Juwaybir, finishing at the top of p. 562); your children and your women (8532, through Sufyan from Salama ibn Nubayt); women and boys (8533, through al-Himmani). Mujahid: women and little ones (8534, through Sufyan from Humayd al-A'raj). Al-Hakam ibn Utayba: women and little ones (8535). Qatada (8536, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh): God commanded that this wealth be stored and well kept, and that neither the foolish woman nor the foolish boy own it. Abu Malik: women and boys (8537). Ibn Abbas (8538, through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih from Mu'awiya from Ali ibn Abi Talha): your wife and your sons, and the foolish are the little ones, women being the most foolish (finishing on p. 563). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 561} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 562} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 563}

**The editor's note on report 8535 (p. 562).** Shakir identifies Abu Nu'aym as al-Fadl ibn Dukayn (his entry is at reports 3035 and 2554) and Ibn Abi Ghaniyya as Abd al-Malik ibn Humayd ibn Abi Ghaniyya al-Khuza'i, who narrated from his father, Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i, Abu Ishaq al-Shaybani and al-Hakam ibn Utayba, and from whom narrated al-Thawri (his contemporary), Waki', Yahya ibn Abi Za'ida, Umara ibn Bishr, Abu Nu'aym and others; he calls him reliable. The earlier printing has "Ibn Abi Anbasa", while the manuscript scribe wrote the name badly so that it looks like "Ibn Abi Unayya", and the editor put in the form he identifies as correct. He adds that al-Hakam is Ibn Utayba al-Kindi, already met many times (report 3297). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 562}

**Reports limiting it to boys, to a man's children, or to women (pp. 563-565, reports 8539-8553).** The second view is that the foolish are the young only. Sa'id ibn Jubayr: they are the orphans (8539, through al-Muthanna from Suwayd ibn Nasr from Ibn al-Mubarak from Sharik from Salim; and 8540, through Ibn Waki' from his father); al-Hasan: do not make gifts to the young (8541). The third view is that the foolish ones of a man's own children are meant. Abu Malik: do not give your foolish child your wealth, which he would spoil, for it is your support after God (8542); Ibn Abbas (8543, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather): do not give power to the foolish among your children, and he used to say it was revealed about the foolish and the orphans have no part in it. The editor notes that both the earlier printing and the manuscript have an awkward grammatical form in the last clause and he fears it is a slip of the scribe. Abu Musa al-Ash'ari (8544, through Ibn al-Muthanna from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba from Firas from al-Sha'bi from Abu Burda): three pray to God and are not answered: a man with an ill-tempered wife who does not part from her, a man who gave his wealth to a foolish person, and a man owed a debt who did not call a witness. In the editor's note, al-Hakim recorded it in the Mustadrak (2: 203) as a saying traced to the Prophet through Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh al-Anbari from his father from Shu'ba, and said it is sound on the condition of the two sheikhs though they did not record it, because Shu'ba's companions stopped it at the Companion; al-Dhahabi says they did not record it because most narrated it from Shu'ba as a Companion's saying and Mu'adh alone raised it; the editor adds that Shu'ba's companions agreed on another report with this chain, "three receive their reward twice". Ibn Zayd (8545): do not give the foolish one among your children an animal or an orchard or anything that is for you a support out of your wealth. The fourth view is that the foolish are women only: the story a villager told of a man who gave his wealth to his wife and she put it where it did not belong (8546, through al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman from his father); Mujahid: the women (8547, through Sufyan from Humayd; 8548, through al-Thawri from Humayd from Qays); Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih (8549, 8550): men were forbidden to give women their wealth, for they are foolish whoever they are, wives, mothers or daughters; al-Hasan: the woman (8551); al-Dahhak: women are the most foolish (8552); and Muwarriq (8553): a woman of fine appearance and bearing passed by Abd Allah ibn Umar and he recited the verse to her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 563} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 564} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 565}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 565-566).** Abu Jafar says God made the address general and did not single out one foolish person over another, so it is not permissible for anyone to give a foolish person his wealth, whether a small child or a grown man, male or female. By the foolish person whose guardian may not give him his wealth he means the one who deserves interdiction through wasting his wealth, corrupting it, corrupting others and managing it badly. His argument is that the following verse commands the guardians of orphans to hand over their wealth once they reach marriageable age and sound judgment is found in them; "orphans" covers males and females, and the command to hand over is not limited to one sex; so those whose wealth is to be handed over differ from those whose wealth is to be withheld, and the foolish are those who deserve interdiction, while anyone else is not foolish, since interdiction is not due to one who has come of age and shown sound judgment. Against those who limited it to women, he says they put the language in the wrong place: the Arabs hardly ever form the plural of the pattern fa'il on fu'ala' except for males or males and females together, and for females alone they use fa'a'il and fa'ilat, as gharib in the feminine gives gharaib and gharibat, while ghuraba' is the plural of the masculine. The editor has a note approving this linguistic argument and criticising what he calls forced and daring interpretation against the Arabic language, which he says in his own time has become the rule on which holders of innovated opinions ride; that is the editor's view, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 565} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 566}

**The rest of the sentence: provision, clothing, kind speech, and the attribution to the guardians (pp. 566-568, reports 8554-8557).** They differed about what follows. Some said: do not give the foolish among women and boys, O you who are sound in judgment, your wealth which you own, so that you set them over it and they waste it; rather, provide for them out of it if their maintenance falls on you, clothe them and speak well to them. Al-Tabari notes that he has already given the reports of Abu Musa, Ibn Abbas, al-Hasan, Mujahid, Qatada and the villager for this, and he adds others: al-Suddi (8554), do not give your wife and child your wealth so that they come to manage you, but feed and clothe them from it; Ibn Abbas (8555), do not give power over your wealth to the foolish among your children, and he commanded him to provide for him and clothe him from it; Ibn Zayd (8556), do not give the foolish anything that is yours. Others said that the wealth meant is the foolish people's own, but is attributed to the guardians because they manage and administer it, among them Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8557, through al-Muthanna from Suwayd ibn Nasr from Ibn al-Mubarak from Sharik from Salim): it is the orphan's wealth with you, do not hand it to him but spend on him until he comes of age. In the editor's note (p. 568), the bracketed part of that report is an addition absent from both the printing and the manuscript, which he added from the commentary of al-Baghawi (printed in the margin of Ibn Kathir, 2: 349) as closer to al-Tabari's text; al-Baghawi attributes the saying to Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ikrima; al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 120) quotes from Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim a report from Sa'id that they are the orphans and that "your wealth" means their wealth; and the editor hopes nothing has fallen out of Abu Jafar's following words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 566} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 567} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 568}

**Al-Tabari joins the two readings; the word for "support" and the two recitations (pp. 568-569, reports 8558-8559).** Al-Tabari says the prohibition covers both the wealth of those forbidden to hand it over and the wealth of the foolish, because the word for "your wealth" is not limited to some of it, and the Arabs allow a speech to a group to be partly about them and partly about absent persons, as when one says to a man "you have eaten your wealth by falsehood", meaning you and your companions or your people; so the sense is: do not give, O people, your foolish ones your wealth, some of it yours and some of it theirs, so that they waste it, the foolish being included among those addressed. He then explains that three forms of the word for "support" mean one thing, and that the original is qiwam, in which the waw became ya because of the preceding kasra, as one says siyam for fasting; and one says so-and-so is the support of his household. In the editor's note, in the second example both the printing and the manuscript have a form written with a different first letter, which he says is probably wrong, and he put in what he judged correct. The reciters differed: some read it with a kasra on the qaf and a fatha on the ya with no alif, others with an alif; al-Tabari chooses the form with the alif because it is the known reading in the Muslim regions, though the other is not a mistake or corruption, his reason being that when readings differ in form and agree in meaning, the one dearest to him is the clearest and best known among the reciters of the regions. The editor notes that the earlier printing here writes "Muhammad said" (meaning Muhammad ibn Jarir, Abu Jafar) instead of "Abu Jafar said", the second time, and refers to p. 519, note 1. Among the reports on this word: Abu Malik (8558), that which is your support after God, which the editor notes is an abridgement of 8542; then al-Suddi (8559, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat), which begins "wealth is..." and breaks off at the end of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 568} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 569} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 569 in the commentary on verse 5 of sura 4, at report 8559, which is cut off at the end of the page, and continue from p. 570. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 569}
