## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We parted the sea for you" al-Tabari takes it as joined to "when We saved you", with "faraqna bikum" meaning that He divided the sea for them: they were twelve tribes, so the sea split into twelve paths and each tribe took one. He reports this from al-Suddi, who adds that Moses struck the sea and each part was like a great mountain. He records a view of some Basran grammarians that it means "We separated you from the water", and rejects it as against the apparent wording, since God says He parted the sea with them, not that He separated them from the sea. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 50}
On "and We saved you and drowned Pharaoh's people while you looked on" he relates from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad ibn al-Had, through Ibn Ishaq, that Pharaoh set out after Moses with seventy thousand dark horses besides the dappled ones; when Moses reached the sea with no way back and Pharaoh appeared behind, Moses's companions said they would be overtaken and he answered that his Lord was with him and would guide him. From Ibn Ishaq he reports that God told the sea to part when Moses struck it with his staff; the sea spent the night heaving in fear of God, awaiting His command, and then became a dry road by which Moses led the Children of Israel, with Pharaoh and his troops following. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 51} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 52}
In another report from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, Pharaoh stopped at the shore and his stallion hesitated; Gabriel came in front of him on a mare, the stallion followed her in, the army entered behind Pharaoh, and Michael drove them from the rear, until the sea closed over them once Gabriel was out and the last of them in; Pharaoh cried out on seeing God's power. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 52}
From Amr ibn Maymun he reports that Pharaoh ordered them not to pursue until the cock crowed, no cock crowed that night, and he gathered six hundred thousand Copts; Yusha ibn Nun rode his horse into the sea up to the depths and came back asking where his Lord had commanded Moses to go, doing this three times until God told Moses to strike the sea with his staff. Qatada is reported to say Moses had six hundred thousand with him and Pharaoh followed with a million and a hundred thousand horses; these figures stand as reported. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 53}

Through Sufyan ibn Uyayna he reports from Ibn Abbas that when the Israelites saw the sea ahead and Pharaoh behind they said they had been harmed before and after, and Moses answered that their Lord might destroy their enemy; the sea spent the night trembling, not knowing from which side Moses would strike; when it parted there were twelve paths, one per tribe, and when the tribes could not see one another and asked for their companions, Moses gestured with his staff at the walls and windows opened through which they could see one another. The report then returns to the mare, Gabriel, Pharaoh's entry and the sea closing over his people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 53} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 54} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 55}
From al-Suddi he gives a long report: God told Moses to lead the Israelites out by night; death struck the firstborn of the Copts, who were busy burying them until sunrise; those who went with Moses numbered six hundred and twenty thousand fighters besides the families, and Haman led Pharaoh's vanguard with one million seven hundred thousand horses; Aaron struck the sea and it refused, until Moses addressed it as "Abu Khalid" and struck it so that it parted; and when the tribes feared their companions had perished, Moses prayed and God made the walls into arches so they could see one another. In this report Pharaoh, seeing the sea open, says it parted for him so that he might overtake his enemies, and Gabriel comes down on a mare whose scent draws the horses after her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 55} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 56}
From Ibn Zayd he relates that Moses addressed the sea, which replied that it was a servant with no command but what God commands, whereupon God told it to part when Moses struck it with his staff. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 56} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 57}
Al-Tabari explains "while you looked on" as their seeing God part the sea for them and destroy Pharaoh's people where He saved them, and His great power in the sea's obedience; he says this reminds the addressees and warns them, in their denial of the Prophet Muhammad, against what befell Pharaoh and his people. He records the view of some Arabic grammarians that the looking here is knowing, not seeing, since the Israelites were too occupied to watch Pharaoh, and rejects it: it was beyond doubt sight with the eyes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 57} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 58}
