## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when We parted the sea for you" al-Tabari takes it as joined to "when We saved you", with "faraqna bikum" meaning that He divided the sea for them: they were twelve tribes, so the sea split into twelve paths and each tribe took one. He reports this from al-Suddi, who adds that Moses struck the sea and each part was like a great mountain. He records a view of some Basran grammarians that it means "We separated you from the water", and rejects it as against the apparent wording, since God says He parted the sea with them, not that He separated them from the sea. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 50}
On "and We saved you and drowned Pharaoh's people while you looked on" he relates from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad ibn al-Had, through Ibn Ishaq, that Pharaoh set out after Moses with seventy thousand dark horses besides the dappled ones; when Moses reached the sea with no way back and Pharaoh appeared behind, Moses's companions said they would be overtaken and he answered that his Lord was with him and would guide him. From Ibn Ishaq he reports that God told the sea to part when Moses struck it with his staff; the sea spent the night heaving in fear of God, awaiting His command, and then became a dry road by which Moses led the Children of Israel, with Pharaoh and his troops following. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 51} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 52}
In another report from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, Pharaoh stopped at the shore and his stallion hesitated; Gabriel came in front of him on a mare, the stallion followed her in, the army entered behind Pharaoh, and Michael drove them from the rear, until the sea closed over them once Gabriel was out and the last of them in; Pharaoh cried out on seeing God's power. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 52}
From Amr ibn Maymun he reports that Pharaoh ordered them not to pursue until the cock crowed, no cock crowed that night, and he gathered six hundred thousand Copts; Yusha ibn Nun rode his horse into the sea up to the depths and came back asking where his Lord had commanded Moses to go, doing this three times until God told Moses to strike the sea with his staff. Qatada is reported to say Moses had six hundred thousand with him and Pharaoh followed with a million and a hundred thousand horses; these figures stand as reported. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 53}
