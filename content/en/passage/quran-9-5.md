**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 133-137)**

Al-Tabari says "when the sacred months have passed" means when they have ended, gone by and left; it is said "we passed out of such a month" in the sense of leaving it, and from it comes the skinned sheep, one stripped of its hide. The sacred months are Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja and Muharram, but only the passing of Muharram is meant here, since the proclamation was made on the day of the greater pilgrimage, so it is clear they were not given all the sacred months; but because Muharram follows the two before it, the passing of "the sacred months" was said; the sense is: when the three sacred months have passed for those with no covenant, or those whose covenant was broken by backing enemies against the Messenger and his Companions, or whose covenant had no known term. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 134}

"Kill the polytheists wherever you find them" means wherever you meet them on earth, in the sanctuary or outside it, in the sacred months or outside them; "take them" means capture them; "besiege them" means bar them from moving about in the lands of Islam and from entering Mecca; "lie in wait for them at every ambush" means sit in wait to kill or capture them on every road and lookout, the word for ambush coming from watching for someone. "If they repent" means turn from polytheism with God and denial of Muhammad's prophethood to the oneness of God, sincere worship of Him alone and acknowledgement of Muhammad's prophethood; "establish prayer" means perform what God made obligatory of it with its limits; "pay the zakat" means the zakat God made due on their wealth to its recipients; "let them go their way" means let them move about your cities and enter the Sacred House; God is forgiving and merciful to the servant who repents and turns to obey Him after disobedience, covering his sin and not punishing him for his earlier sins after repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 135}

Al-Tabari recalls the disagreement over those who were given until the end of the sacred months. Report 16475, from Anas, through Abd al-A'la ibn Wasil al-Asadi, Ubaydallah ibn Musa, Abu Ja'far al-Razi and al-Rabi': the Messenger said that whoever leaves the world in sincerity to God alone and worship of Him without associating anything with Him leaves it with God pleased with him; Anas said this is the religion of God that the messengers brought and conveyed from their Lord, before the confusion of reports and the clash of desires, and its confirmation is in God's book among the last revealed; their repentance is putting away idols, worshipping their Lord, establishing prayer and paying zakat, and he then cited another verse, [[passage/quran-9-11|9:11]]. The editor gives the narrators, says Ibn Majah gives the report in his Sunan and al-Hakim in the Mustadrak, and that al-Dhahabi adds that the elevated part is only the opening of the report and the rest seems to be interpolated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 136}

Report 16476, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: let go the way of those whom God told you to let go, for people are three: a Muslim group who owe zakat, a polytheist who owes the poll tax, and a man of war who is safe in his trade among the Muslims if he pays a tenth of his goods. Report 16477, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the sacred months are the four counted, twenty of Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram, Safar, Rabi' al-Awwal and ten of Rabi' al-Akhir; those who held this view said they were called sacred because God forbade the believers in them to shed the polytheists' blood or interfere with them except in a good way. The editor notes a difference between manuscript and printed text in the word for interference. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 136}

Report 16478, from Mujahid and Amr ibn Shu'ayb, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Ibrahim ibn Abi Bakr: the sacred months are the four of "travel the land", called sacred because the polytheists were made safe in them so they could travel. Report 16479, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: a term of four months was set for them and every polytheist was disavowed; then, once those months had passed, the command came to kill, take, besiege and lie in wait for them, not leaving them to roam the land or go out to trade but tightening on them after that, and then to pardon those who repent, pray and pay zakat. Report 16480, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the sacred months are the four in which a term was set for the holders of the general covenant among the polytheists, whom he is then to kill wherever found. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira and notes that the phrase "the holders of the general covenant" is al-Tabari's own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 137}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
