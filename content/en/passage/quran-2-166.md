## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "when those who were followed disown those who followed, and they see the punishment" al-Tabari says the followed disown those who followed them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 287} He reports the interpreters' differences about who they are: some said the leaders and heads in polytheism who disown their weaker followers on the Day of Resurrection, from Qatada, al-Rabi' and 'Ata'; others said the devils who disown humans, from al-Suddi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 287} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 288} Al-Tabari holds that God reports that every one followed in polytheism and error disowns his followers when they see the punishment, without limiting it, and that the verse shows the "equals" of the previous verse are these followed ones; so he finds al-Suddi's reading sound, that the equals here are men obeyed in disobedience to God, and rejects the view that they are devils, since the context concerns those who took equals. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 288}

On "and the ties between them are cut" he gives the views on "ties": the bond and affection between them in this world, from Mujahid, Ibn 'Abbas and Qatada, with Qatada adding that they are the ties of regret and that on that day friends are enemies to one another except the God-fearing, and from al-Rabi' that they are regret; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 289} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 290} the stations, from Ibn 'Abbas and al-Rabi' ibn Anas; the kinships, from Ibn 'Abbas; and the deeds, from al-Suddi and Ibn Zayd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 291} He explains that a "tie" (sabab) is anything by which one reaches a need, such as a rope, a road, marriage or a means, and chooses that God reports that all the unbelievers' ties are cut, so no friendship, worship of equals, kinship or deeds avail them but become regrets, and that whoever limits it to some ties is to be asked for proof of his claim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 292} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 293}
