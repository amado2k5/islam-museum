## Summary
Verse 77 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 77 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 77: "People of the Book, do not go beyond the limits in your religion", p. 487**

Al-Tabari says: this is God addressing His prophet, telling him to say to the Christians who exaggerate about the Messiah: People of the Book, meaning the Gospel, do not overstep in your religion, that is, do not go to excess in what you say of the Messiah and pass from truth into falsehood by saying he is God or His son; say instead that he is God's servant and His word that He cast to Mary and a spirit from Him. "Do not follow the desires of a people who went astray before" means do not follow in his case the desires of the Jews, who erred before you, by saying as they did that he was of illegitimate birth, slandering his mother as they slandered her.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 487}

**Commentary on verse 77, continued: "They led many astray and strayed from the right way", p. 488**

Al-Tabari goes on that the Jews slandered the mother of the Messiah with an invention, though she was a truthful woman. "They led many astray" means these Jews led many people away from the way of truth and made them disbelieve in God and deny the Messiah; "strayed from the right way" means they left the straight path and took a road other than the way of truth. He takes this to mean their unbelief in God, their denial of His messengers Jesus and Muhammad, and their distance from faith.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 488}

Report 12296, from Mujahid, says those who "strayed from the right way" are the Jews, and report 12297, from al-Suddi, that they are those who went astray and led their followers astray from the just way.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 488}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
