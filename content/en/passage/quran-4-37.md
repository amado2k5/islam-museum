## Summary
Verse 37 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 37 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The grammar of the relative and the meaning of stinginess (pp. 350-351, report 9493, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God does not love the conceited and self-vaunting man who is stingy and urges others to stinginess. The editor says the relative may be in the nominative, picking up the pronoun in the word for the self-vaunting, or in the accusative as a qualifier (the printed edition has a meaningless word where the manuscript is right; he adds that Abu Hayyan in his commentary, 3:247, gave this reading without crediting al-Tabari). Al-Tabari says stinginess in Arab usage is a man's refusing his petitioner what he has and what is surplus to him (the editor notes that this explanation is seldom found in the lexica, and that the printed edition and the manuscript read a phrase he thinks should be otherwise). Al-Qasim (9493, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Tawus) from his father: stinginess is a man's being tight with what is in his hands, while greed is being greedy for what is in others' hands, wanting to have it by lawful and unlawful means and never content. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 351}

**The two readings (p. 351).** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed over the word for stinginess in the verse: most reciters of Kufa read it with an open first and second letter, and most reciters of Medina and some of Basra read it with a closed vowel on the first. He says they are two eloquent dialectal forms with one meaning and two known readings, so whichever the reciter reads he is right (his choice). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 351}

**The view that they are the Jews who concealed (pp. 351-353, reports 9494-9501).** It was also said that God meant the Jews who concealed Muhammad's name and description and did not make them clear to people, though they find him written in the Torah and the Gospel. Muhammad b. Abd al-A'la (9494, from al-Mu'tamir b. Sulayman, from his father) from al-Hadrami: they are the Jews, who were stingy with the knowledge they had and concealed it; Muhammad b. Amr (9495, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid: what lies between this verse and the close of the third verse ([[passage/quran-4-39|4:39]]) concerns the Jews; al-Muthanna (9496, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid, likewise; Bishr (9497, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: they are the People of the Book, stingy with God's due over them, who concealed Islam and Muhammad though they find him written (Qatada's wording as transmitted calls them enemies of God; this is his judgement as reported, not the museum's); Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9498, from Ahmad b. al-Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: the Jews, stingy with Muhammad's name and bidding one another conceal it; Muhammad b. **The sense on the two interpretations, and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 353-355).** On the first interpretation, al-Tabari says, God does not love the conceited and self-vaunting who are stingy in making clear what He commanded them to make clear to people of Muhammad's name, qualities and description that He sent down in His books to His prophets, though they know it, and who bid others who know as they do to conceal it, and conceal the knowledge God gave them of it. On the interpretation of Ibn Abbas and Ibn Zayd (stinginess with surplus wealth) the rest of the interpretation is the same. He says the best-founded view is that of those who said God described these people as stingy in telling those ignorant of Muhammad's affair that it is true, that he is a sent prophet, and the rest of the truth God had made clear in His books, and as bidding those in their position of knowledge to conceal it (his choice). His reason: God described them as bidding people to stinginess, and he has not heard of any nation bidding people to stinginess as religion or character; rather each sees it as ugly, blames the one who does it and praises generosity, counting it among noble acts, even if it itself practises stinginess; so he says their stinginess was with knowledge, not money, unless the sense is those stingy with money they would spend in God's ways and bidding Muslims to stop spending, in which case there is an intelligible reading according to the report he gave from Ibn Abbas. The two notes on p. 354 say the printed edition read two unpointed words in the manuscript with a different letter and, from that, altered other phrases and spoiled them, the right reading being the editor's from the manuscript; the note on p. 355 says the clause on practising stinginess is a parenthesis and corrects a further phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 355}

**The close of the verse (p. 355, notes 2-4).** Al-Tabari says God prepared for those who deny His favour to them, namely the knowledge of Muhammad's prophethood, and call him false after knowing, concealing his description from those they were told to make it clear to, a degrading punishment for whoever is tormented by remaining in it, held ready for him in the next life when he comes to his Lord and finds it for his earlier denial of God's duty on him. The notes refer back to earlier treatments of the words for preparing and degrading (the page and volume numbers print unclearly) and say the printed edition erred in one sentence, the manuscript being right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 355}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 355}
