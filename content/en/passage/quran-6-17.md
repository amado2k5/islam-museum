## Summary
Verse 17 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 17 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "if God touches you with harm none can remove it but he, and if he touches you with good, he is able to do all things" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: if God afflicts you with harm, that is hardship in your world, scarcity of livelihood and narrowness in it, none will lift that from you but God, who commanded you to be the first to submit to his command and prohibition and to yield to him among the people of your time, and not what those who hold others equal to him call you to worship of idols and images or anything else of his creation. "And if he touches you with good", ease of living, breadth of provision and abundance of wealth, so that you acknowledge he gave you this, "he is able to do all things": God, who gave you that, is able to benefit you and harm you and is able to do all he wills; nothing he wills is beyond him and nothing he seeks refuses him, unlike the abased, despised gods that cannot draw benefit to themselves or others or ward off harm from themselves or others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 287}

The editor refers for "touch", "harm" and "able" to earlier volumes 10 and 7 and the language indexes.

He says: how then can you worship what is so, or how can you not give pure worship and acknowledge him in whose hand are harm and benefit, reward and punishment, to whom belong perfect power and manifest might? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 288}

Verse 17 closes here at p. 288, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-18|6:18]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
