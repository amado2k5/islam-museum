**Commentary on verse 156 (pp. 152-158)**

**On "write for us ... we have turned to You"**

Al-Tabari, relating the prayer of His prophet Moses, says he said, "write for us", that is, make us among those for whom You have written good in this world, meaning righteous deeds, and in the Hereafter among those for whom You have written forgiveness of their sins. Report 15176, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "good" means forgiveness. "We have turned to You" means we have repented to You, and the interpreters said the same. The editor refers to the explanations of "good deed" and "turned". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 152}

Al-Tabari then gives many reports with this meaning. Reports 15177, 15178, 15179, 15180, 15181, 15182 and 15183, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas, that "hudna" means "we repented", through Ibn Waki', Jarir, Ibn Fudayl, Imran ibn Uyayna, Ata, Zayd ibn Hubab, Hammad ibn Salama, Jabir ibn Nuh, Abu Rawq, al-Dahhak, Abd Allah ibn Bakr, Hatim ibn Abi Saghira, Simak, al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj, Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, uncle and grandfather; among them is Abd Allah ibn Bakr ibn Habib al-Sahmi, who is reliable and one of Ahmad's teachers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 153}

Reports 15183 to 15198 give the same meaning from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Sufyan, Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Asbahani, Waki' ibn al-Jarrah, Habawayh and Ja'far, and from Ibrahim and Ibrahim al-Taymi, and from Qatada, Ma'mar, al-Suddi, Mujahid, Abu al-Aliya, al-Dahhak, Juwaybir, Ubayd ibn Sulayman and Jabir, all that the meaning is "we repented to You". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 154}

Report 15199, from Ali, through Sharik, Jabir and Abd Allah ibn Yahya: the Jews were so called because they said "hudna ilayka". Report 15200, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: it means we repented to You. Report 15201, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn al-Barqi, Amr and a man who asked him. Al-Tabari says he has explained its meaning with evidence earlier, which spares repeating. The editor says he found no biography of Abu Hujayr, who relates from al-Dahhak, except in Lisan al-Mizan, and that the name of the transmitter of 15199 is confused in the manuscript and printed text and has no mention in the books. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 155}

**On "He said, My punishment I inflict on whom I will"**

Al-Tabari says: God said to Moses, this with which I struck your people, the tremor, is My punishment, which I inflict on whom I will of My creation as I struck these of your people with it; "and My mercy encompasses everything" means My mercy extends to all My creatures. The editor refers to the explanations of "striking" and "encompassing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 156}

The interpreters differed on this. Some said it is general in form and particular in meaning, intended to say that His mercy encompasses the believers in Him among the community of Muhammad, citing what follows, "I shall write it for those who fear God". Report 15202, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salama al-Minqari, Hammad ibn Salama, Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he recited the verse and said God made it for this community. The editor gives a biography of Abu Salama al-Minqari, who is Musa ibn Isma'il al-Tabudhaki, a reliable imam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 156}

Report 15203, from Abu Bakr al-Hudhali, through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar and Sufyan: when "My mercy encompasses everything" was revealed, Iblis said, I am part of "everything"; so God took it from Iblis and said, "I shall write it for those who fear and give zakat and who believe in Our signs"; the Jews said, we fear and give zakat and believe in our Lord's signs, so God took it from the Jews and said what is at [[passage/quran-7-157|7:157]] about following the unlettered Messenger-Prophet; thus God took it from Iblis and the Jews and made it for this community. The editor notes that Abu Bakr al-Hudhali is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 157}

Report 15204, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj, is similar: Iblis said, I am of everything; the Jews said, we fear and give zakat too; so God revealed following the unlettered Messenger-Prophet, took it from Iblis and the Jews and made it for the community of Muhammad: I shall write it for those of your people who fear. Report 15205, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: Iblis said, I am of that "thing"; so God revealed "I shall write it for those who fear" God's disobedience "and who believe in Our signs"; the Jews and Christians wished for it, so God sent down a firm, clear condition and said following the unlettered Messenger-Prophet, who is your Prophet, who was unlettered and did not write. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 157}

Report 15206, from Ibn Abbas, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya, Khalid al-Hadhdha' and Unays Abu al-Uryan, on "write for us": he was not given it, so He said "My punishment I inflict on whom I will" up to "the unlettered Messenger-Prophet". Report 15207 is similar, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Ulayya and Abd al-A'la: Moses was not given it. The editor explains that Unays's name differs between the manuscript and the printed text and between the two reports, and that he could not decide which is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 158}

Report 15208, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: God had written in the tablets the mention of Muhammad and his community, and what He had stored for them with Him, what He made easy for them in their religion and what He made wide for them in what He made lawful; then He said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 158}

We stop here at the foot of p. 158, in the middle of report 15208 from Ibn Abbas on "My punishment I inflict on whom I will" in verse 156; the next batch begins at p. 159. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 158}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
