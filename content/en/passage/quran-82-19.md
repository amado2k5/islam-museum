## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 14 to 19 as a single unit. He interprets the phrase, “On that Day, no soul will have power over another, and the command will be entirely with God”: “The command, by God, will be with God on that Day, but on that Day, no one will dispute His authority.” The reciters differed in their reading of the phrase, “On that Day, no soul will have power over another.” The majority of reciters in the Hijaz and Kufa read it with the accusative case for “Day,” since the genitive construction was not pure. Some readers of Basra read it with the damma on “yawm” and raised it in response to the first day, and raising it is more eloquent in the speech of the Arabs, because the day is added to “yaf’al” and the Arabs, when they add the day to “taf’al” or “yaf’al” or “af’al” they raise it and they said: This is the day I do such and such, and when they add it to a past tense verb they put it in the accusative case, and from it is the saying of the poet: When I reproached old age for youth and said: Have you not yet awakened and old age is a deterrent? And in the passage, statements are transmitted from Ibn Abbas and Qatadah.
