## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 48-49 as a single unit. He interprets the words, “So be patient for the judgment of your Lord, and do not be like the companion of the fish when he called out while he was distressed”: “So be patient, O Muhammad, for your Lord’s decree and judgment concerning you and these polytheists regarding what you have brought them of this Quran and this religion. Proceed with what your Lord has commanded you, and do not let their denial of you and their harm to you deter you from conveying what you have been commanded to convey. And His words, ‘and do not be like the companion of the fish,’ who was imprisoned in its belly—that is, Jonah, son of Matta—so that your Lord will punish you for failing to convey this message, just as He punished him by imprisoning him in its belly.” “When he called out while he was distressed” means: when he called out while he was grieved, overwhelmed by sorrow and suppressing it. As Mu’awiyah narrated to me, on the authority of Ali, on the authority of Ibn Abbas, regarding the verse, “When he called out while he was distressed,” he said: grieved. And on the authority of Ibn Abi Najih, on the authority of Mujahid, regarding the word “distressed,” he said: grieved. And Qatadah used to say regarding the verse, “And do not be like the companion of the fish,” that it means: do not be like him in haste and anger. The passage also includes statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Qatadah.
