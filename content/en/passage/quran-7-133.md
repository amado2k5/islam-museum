## Summary
Verse 133 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 133 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 133 (p. 49 onward)**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the meaning of "the flood". Some said it is water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 49}

Report 14989, from Ibn Abbas through Ibn Waki', Habawayh Abu Yazid, Ya'qub al-Qummi, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when Moses came with the signs, the first of the signs was the flood, and God sent the sky upon them. The editor identifies Habawayh Abu Yazid as Ishaq ibn Isma'il al-Razi, and says the printed text read the name rightly but misread the manuscript and altered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 50}

Report 14990, from Abu Malik through Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, Ibn Yaman, Sufyan and Isma'il: the flood is water. Report 14991, from al-Dahhak through Ibn Waki', al-Muharibi and Juwaybir: water. Report 14992, from Ibn Abbas through Jabir ibn Nuh, Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak: the flood is drowning. Report 14993, from Mujahid through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the flood is water, and plague in every case; the editor thinks the sentence should read "and plague is death in every case". Report 14994, from Mujahid through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: the flood is death in every case. Report 14995, from Ibn Abbas through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: the flood is water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 50}

Others said it is death; their reports begin with report 14996, from Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i and Yahya ibn Yaman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 50}

The rest of report 14996, from Aisha, through Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, Yahya ibn Yaman, al-Minhal ibn Khalifa, al-Hajjaj and al-Hakam ibn Mina': she said, the Messenger of God said, the flood is death. The editor notes that al-Minhal ibn Khalifa al-Ijli (Abu Qudama) is spoken against: Ibn Ma'in, al-Nasa'i and al-Hakim held him weak, and al-Bukhari said "sound, but there is something in him"; that al-Hajjaj is Ibn Arta'a and al-Hakam ibn Mina' al-Ansari is a reliable successor; that Ibn Kathir gave the report in his commentary and called it "strange" (adding that Ibn Mardawayh gave it from Yahya ibn Yaman); that he himself judges it weak because of al-Minhal; and that al-Suyuti gave it in the "Durr al-Manthur", also ascribing it to Ibn Abi Hatim and Abu al-Shaykh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 51}

Report 14997, from Ibn Jurayj, through Abbas ibn Muhammad (al-Duri) and Hajjaj: I asked Ata' what the flood was; he said, death. Report 14998, from Mujahid through Ibn Waki', Abd Allah ibn Raja', Ibn Jurayj and Ata': the flood is death. Report 14999, from Abd Allah ibn Kathir: "We sent upon them the flood" means death; and from Ibn Jurayj, from Ata': death; and Mujahid said, death in every case. Report 15000, from Aisha from the Prophet, "the flood is death", through Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Yaman, al-Minhal ibn Khalifa, al-Hajjaj and an unnamed man; the editor says it is another chain for report 14996, in which the transmitter from Aisha is left unnamed and is given there as al-Hakam ibn Mina', and that the report's sourcing and weakness have been noted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 51}

Others said: rather it was a command of God that went round them. Report 15001, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Jarir and Qabus ibn Abi Zabyan from his father: "We sent upon them the flood" means the command of God, the flood; then he recited the verse about a visitor from your Lord going round the garden while they slept ([[passage/quran-68-19|68:19]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 52}

Al-Tabari records that a Basran authority on Arab speech (Abu Ubayda, in "Majaz al-Qur'an") claimed that the flood is, of torrents, the heavy and violent kinds, and, of death, the sweeping, swift kind; another said it is abundant rain and wind. A Kufan grammarian said "tufan" is a verbal noun like "ruhjan" and "nuqsan" and has no plural; a Basran grammarian (al-Akhfash) said it is a plural whose singular by analogy would be "tufana". In footnotes the editor explains the two words for torrent, and notes that the manuscript has "successive" where Abu Ubayda has "sweeping", which he keeps. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 52}

Al-Tabari's own choice is what Ibn Abbas said as Abu Zabyan relates: that it was a command of God that went round them, and that the word is a verbal noun from "God's command went round them, circling, tufan", as one says "this thing decreased, nuqsan". That being so, what went round them might have been violent rain, or sweeping death. As evidence that violent rain is called "flood", he cites verses by Husayl ibn Urfuta al-Asadi, by al-Ra'i and by Abu al-Najm describing violent rain and wind; the editor corrects the first poet's name in the footnotes and gives the sources and word meanings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 53}

As to "the lice" (qummal), the interpreters differed on its meaning. Some said it is the weevil that comes out of wheat: report 15002, from Ibn Abbas through Ibn Waki', Jarir, Ya'qub al-Qummi, Ja'far and Sa'id ibn Jubayr; report 15003, from Sa'id in similar words, through Ibn Humayd. Others said it is the locust nymph, the small wingless locust: report 15004, from Ibn Abbas through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali ibn Abi Talha; report 15005, from al-Suddi; report 15006, from Qatada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 54}

Report 15007, from Mujahid: the nymph. Report 15008, from Qatada through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the nymph, the young of locusts. Report 15009, from Ibn Abbas through Ibn Waki', Jabir ibn Nuh, Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak. Report 15010, from Ikrima: the daughters of the locust. Report 15011, from Ibn Abbas through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: the nymph. Others said the lice are fleas: report 15012, from Ibn Zayd through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: some people claimed the lice are fleas. Others said they are small black creatures: report 15013, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Hasan, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Abu Bakr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 55}

A Basran authority (Abu Ubayda) claimed that among the Arabs "qummal" is the "himnan", a kind of tick (singular "himnana"), larger than the "qamqama"; and that "qummal" is a plural whose singular is "qumla", a creature like a louse that camels eat, as he had heard, which is what al-A'sha meant in a line describing a people whose sons work at lice and chains. In footnotes the editor gives the setting of al-A'sha's poem addressed to Khosrow when he wanted hostages from Banu Dubay'a and its sense, glosses "strong" and "closed", and notes that the printed text wrongly has "al-A'mash". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 56}

Al-Farra' said: I have heard nothing about it; if it is a plural its singular is "qamil", like "sajid" and "raki'", and if it is a name with a collective sense its unit is "qumla". The editor says he did not find this in al-Farra's "Ma'ani al-Qur'an" at this place, where he says only that the lice are the wingless locust nymph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 57}

Then al-Tabari presents the effects that came to Pharaoh's people from these signs and the reason God sent them. Report 15014, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub al-Qummi and Ja'far ibn al-Mughira: when Moses came to Pharaoh he said, send the Children of Israel with me; he refused, so God sent the flood, which is rain, pouring some on them; they feared it was punishment and said to Moses, ask your Lord to lift the rain from us and we shall believe you and send the Children of Israel with you; he prayed and they did not believe or send them. That year God made grow for them what He had not grown before of crops, fruit and pasture, and they said, this is what we wished for. God then sent locusts upon the pasture, and when they saw their effect they knew the crops would not be spared, and said, Moses, ask your Lord to lift the locusts and we shall believe in you and send the Children of Israel with you; he prayed and it was lifted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 57}

The rest of report 15014, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: God lifted the locusts from them, but they did not believe or send the Children of Israel with him. They threshed the grain and stored it in their houses, saying, we have made it safe. So God sent the lice, which are the weevil that comes out of it; a man would take ten sacks to the mill and bring back not even three measures. They said, Moses, ask your Lord to lift the lice and we shall believe you and send the Children of Israel with you; he prayed and it was lifted, and they refused. While Moses sat with Pharaoh he heard a frog croak and said to Pharaoh, what you and your people will meet from this! Pharaoh said, what harm can this do! By evening a man sat up to his chin in frogs, and when he wished to speak a frog leapt into his mouth. They said to Moses, ask your Lord to lift these frogs and we shall believe you and send the Children of Israel with you; it was lifted and they did not believe. Then God sent the blood: what they drew from rivers and wells or held in their vessels they found to be fresh blood. They complained to Pharaoh that they had nothing to drink; he said he has bewitched you; they said, from where could we be bewitched when we find in our vessels no water but only fresh blood? They went to Moses and asked him to lift the blood and they would believe and send the Children of Israel; he prayed, it was lifted, and they did not believe or send them. The editor glosses "threshed", "stored" and "fresh blood", and notes a bracketed phrase absent from the manuscript where the printed text is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 58}

Report 15015, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Habawayh Abu Yazid, Ya'qub al-Qummi and Ja'far: when they feared drowning Pharaoh said, Moses, ask your Lord to lift this rain and we shall believe you; then he gave something like Ibn Humayd's report from Ya'qub. The editor says Habawayh Abu Yazid is Ishaq ibn Isma'il al-Razi, and the printed text misspelled the name where the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 58}

Report 15016, from al-Suddi, through Musa ibn Harun, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat: God sent the flood, which is rain, upon them and drowned everything of theirs; they said, ask your Lord to lift it and we shall believe and send the Children of Israel with you; God lifted it and their crops grew by it, and they said, we are not glad it did not rain on us! So God sent locusts that ate their crops, and they asked Moses to pray, saying they would believe; he prayed, it was lifted, though some crops remained, and they said, why believe when what remains is enough for us? So God sent the "duba", the lice, which licked up all the land; they got between a man's clothes and skin and bit him, and filled the food, so that a man who built a column of plaster and made it slippery so that nothing could climb it, and put his food on top, found it full of lice when he climbed up to eat; no calamity was harder on them than the lice, which is the "rijz" that God says in the Qur'an fell on them. They asked Moses to pray and said they would believe; when it was lifted they refused. God then sent the blood: an Israelite and a Copt would draw from the same water and the Copt's would come out blood while the Israelite's was water. When this grew hard they asked Moses to lift it and believe, and it was lifted, and they refused to believe, which is what God says: "when We removed the punishment from them, they broke their word" ([[passage/quran-43-50|43:50]]). The editor glosses "licked" and "made slippery" and says this report is part of a long one in al-Tabari's History (vol. 1, pp. 211-212). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 59}

Report 15017, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: God sent water until they stood in it, then lifted it and they did not believe, and their land was richer than ever; He sent locusts that ate it all but a little, and they did not believe; He sent the lice, which are the young locusts, that ate what remained of their crops, and they did not believe; He sent frogs that entered their houses and fell in their vessels and beds, and they did not believe; then He sent blood, so that whenever one wished to drink the water turned to blood; God said "distinct signs". The editor notes that where the manuscript says "they did not benefit", he kept the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 60}

Report 15018, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid ibn Zuray' and Sa'id: God sent water on them until they stood in it; they called Moses, he prayed and it was lifted, then they returned to their evil; their land then grew; God sent locusts that ate most of their crops and fruit; they called Moses, he prayed and it was lifted, and they returned; then the lice, the nymph that you have seen, ate what the locusts had left; the same; then the frogs that filled their houses and courtyards; the same; then the blood, so that they drew only red blood from their water, and it is said that Pharaoh set a Copt and an Israelite at one vessel, so that on the Israelite's side it was water and on the Copt's blood; Moses prayed and it was lifted, in nine signs: the years, the shortage of fruits, and he showed them Moses's hand and staff. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 60}

Report 15019, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: God sent the flood, which is rain, until they feared destruction, and they came to Moses to ask that the rain be lifted, saying they would believe and send the Children of Israel; it was lifted, God made their crops grow, and they said, we are not glad we were not rained on at the cost of our religion, so we shall not believe. He sent locusts quick to ruin their fruit and crops; they asked again, it was lifted, though remnants of their crops and livelihood remained, and they said what is left suffices us. He sent the lice, the nymph, which followed what the locusts had left; they despaired and felt destruction, asked, and it was lifted, and they said they would not believe. He sent frogs that filled their houses with a harm unlike before, leaping into their pots, spoiling their food and putting out their fires; they asked, it was lifted, and they refused. He sent blood: they could eat and drink only blood; they asked Moses to lift it, he did, and they said they would not believe. The editor notes that the manuscript omits two bracketed phrases and that supplying them completes the sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 61}

The report ends: these were distinct signs, one after another, so that God would have the argument against them, and God seized them for their sins and drowned them in the sea. Report 15020, from Ibn Abbas, through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: the signs sent on Pharaoh's people were locusts, lice, frogs and blood, distinct signs; an Israelite riding with an Egyptian in a boat would draw water while the Egyptian drew blood; an Egyptian would lie on one side and lice and frogs would crowd him so he could not turn to the other; they remained so until God inspired Moses to travel by night with His servants, because they would be followed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 62}

Report 15021, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: when Moses came to Pharaoh with the message he refused to believe or to send the Children of Israel; God sent the flood, which is water, raining on them until they almost perished and everything was cut off from them, and they asked Moses to pray, promising to believe and send the Children of Israel; it was lifted, their crops grew and everything of theirs lived, and they said, we are glad of this rain, it was good for us, so we shall not send them. He sent locusts that ate most of their crops; they asked, it was lifted, though some was left, and they said what is left suffices. He sent the lice, the wingless locust nymph, which followed what remained of crops and trees and was harder on them than the locusts, and they found no way against it; they asked again, it was lifted, and they broke their word. He sent frogs that filled the houses so that no food or drink lacked frogs; they asked, promised, it was lifted, and they did not act; and God sent down "when We removed the plague from them for a term which they were to reach, they broke it" ([[passage/quran-7-135|7:135]]) up to "and were heedless of them" ([[passage/quran-7-136|7:136]]). The editor notes he kept "I shall not send" from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 62}

Report 15022, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Humayd, Abu Tumayla, al-Hasan ibn Waqid, Zayd and Ikrima: the frogs were of the wild; when God sent them against the people of Pharaoh they heard and obeyed, throwing themselves into boiling pots and blazing ovens, and God rewarded their good obedience with the coolness of water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 63}

Report 15023, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Pharaoh went back defeated and fettered when the sorcerers believed, but would only stay in unbelief and persist in evil; so God sent signs one on another and seized him with years, then sent the flood, locusts, lice, frogs and blood, distinct signs. The flood, which is water, overflowed the land and then stood so that they could not plough or work until they were worn with hunger; they asked Moses to pray, saying they would believe and send the Children of Israel; he prayed and it was lifted, and they fulfilled nothing of what they said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 63}

As it reached Ibn Ishaq, God then sent locusts that ate the trees, even the iron nails of the doors, until their houses fell; they said as before, it was lifted, and they did not keep their word. Then the lice: he was told that Moses was ordered to walk to a dune and strike it with his staff; he went to a great loose sand dune and struck it, and lice poured over them until they overcame houses and food and kept them from sleep and rest; they said as before, it was lifted, and they did not keep their word. Then the frogs filled houses, food and vessels, so that whoever uncovered a garment, food or vessel found frogs on it; the same. Then the blood: the waters of Pharaoh's people turned to blood, and from no well or river or vessel did they draw but it became fresh blood. The editor glosses "loose dune" and "poured", and says al-Tabari gave this in his History (vol. 1, p. 215). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 64}

Report 15024, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq: he was told that a woman of Pharaoh's people would come to an Israelite woman when thirst had worn them and say, give me water; she would draw from her jar or pour from her skin, and it would turn to blood in the vessel; she would even say, put it in your mouth and spit it into mine, and she would take water into her mouth, but when she spat it into the other's mouth it became blood; they stayed in this for seven days. The editor notes al-Tabari gave this at length in his History (vol. 1, pp. 215-216). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 64}

Report 15025, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: the locusts ate their crops and plants, the frogs fell on their beds and food, and the blood was in their houses, clothes, water and food; and also from Mujahid, through Shibl and Abd Allah ibn Kathir: when the Nile ran blood, the Israelite drew sweet water and the Egyptian blood, and when they shared one vessel, what was on the Israelite's side was sweet water and on the Egyptian's blood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 65}

Report 15026, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Abu Bakr: when Moses had dealt with Pharaoh by the four signs, the staff, the hand, the shortage of fruits and the years, he said: Lord, this servant of Yours has exalted himself in the land, acted wrongly and oppressed, set himself over me and over You and been proud with his people; seize him with a punishment that is vengeance for him and his people, an admonition for my people and a sign for those after me among the nations that remain. So God sent the flood, which is water, with the houses of the Israelites and Copts intermingled; the Copts' houses filled with water until they stood in it to their collarbones, and whoever sat drowned, while not a drop entered the Israelites' houses. The Copts kept calling Moses, ask your Lord by the covenant He made with you, if you lift this plague from us we shall believe and send the Children of Israel; they made a covenant with Moses. The water took them on a Saturday and stayed seven days to the next Saturday; Moses prayed, the water was lifted, their land grew green, they lived a month in well-being, then denied and said, this water was only a blessing and a fertility for our land, we are not sorry it came. Someone told Ibn Abbas that he had asked Ibn Umar about the flood and he said, I do not know, whether it was death or water; Ibn Abbas said: does Ibn Umar not read sura al-Ankabut, where it mentions. The editor says the right word is "whoever sat drowned", not "whoever was held". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 65}

The rest of report 15026, from Ibn Abbas: God mentioned Noah's people and said "the flood seized them while they were wrongdoers" ([[passage/quran-29-14|29:14]]); "what if they had died, to whom would Moses have come with the four signs after the flood?" Moses said, Lord, Your servants have broken Your covenant and failed my promise; seize them with a punishment that is vengeance for them, an admonition for my people and a sign for the nations after them. So God sent locusts that left them not a leaf, tree, flower or fruit that they did not eat, until all greenery was gone, and they ate the wood, the doors and the roofs of the houses; the locusts were tried with hunger and never filled, though they did not enter the houses of the Israelites. They cried out to Moses, this time ask your Lord by the covenant He made with you, if you lift the plague from us we shall believe and send the Children of Israel; they gave him God's covenant and pledge, and God lifted the locusts after seven days, Saturday to Saturday; then they lived a month in well-being and returned to denial. The editor glosses "fruit" in the sense of the picked, and "cried out", and notes he kept the manuscript wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 66}

Moses prayed as before and God sent the lice. Abu Bakr said, I heard Sa'id ibn Jubayr and al-Hasan say: beside them was a red dune in a village of Egypt called Ayn Shams; Moses walked to it and struck it once with his staff and it became lice crawling to them, small black creatures, that took their hair, skin, eyelashes and eyebrows and clung to their skins like smallpox. They cried to Moses, we repent and will not return, so ask your Lord; the lice were lifted after seven days, Saturday to Saturday; they lived a month in well-being, then returned, saying: we have never had more reason to be sure he is a sorcerer than today, he has made sand into creatures! By Pharaoh's might we shall never believe him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 66}

Moses prayed as before and God sent the frogs: a man would lie down and the frogs would ride him in a heap, so that he could not turn to the other side; he would open his mouth for a morsel and the frog would reach his mouth first; no dough was kneaded without a frog spreading itself in it and no pot cooked without filling with frogs. They complained to Moses, saying this time we repent and shall not return; he took their covenant and pledge, prayed, and God lifted the frogs after seven days, Saturday to Saturday. They lived a month in well-being, then returned, saying, his sorcery is plain to you, making dust into creatures and bringing frogs away from water! They harmed Moses, who prayed as before; God tried them with blood, which ruined their livelihood: the Israelite and the Copt would come to the Nile and draw, and the Israelite would get water and the Copt blood; at the jar of water the Israelite would find water in his vessel and the Copt blood. The editor explains the word for "spread" and corrects the printed text, and glosses "jar". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 67}

Report 15027, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Sa'd al-Madani, on "We sent upon them the flood": death; "the locusts": they ate their goods, clothes and the nails of their doors; "the lice": the nymph, which God set upon them after the locusts; "the frogs": they fell into the foods in their houses and their drinks. The editor says "Ibn Sa'd" in the manuscript and printed text is an error for "Abu Sa'd". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 68}

Some said the "blood" God sent on them was nosebleed; report 15028, from Zayd ibn Aslam, through Ibn Humayd, Ahmad ibn Khalid, Yahya ibn Abi Bukayr and Zuhayr: as for the lice, they are lice; as for the blood, God set nosebleed upon them. The editor gives the transmitters' biographies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 68}

On "distinct signs" al-Tabari says the sense is marks and indications of the truth of Moses's prophethood and the reality of what he called them to, "distinct" because they were separated, one following another. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "sign" and "making distinct". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 68}

Report 15029, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya ibn Salih and Ali ibn Abi Talha: they were distinct signs, one after another, so that God would have the argument against them, then He seized them for their sins and drowned them in the sea. Report 15030, from Ibn Jurayj: one following another so that God has the argument against them and then takes vengeance; a sign stayed on them from Saturday to Saturday and was lifted for a month. Report 15031, from Ibn Ishaq through Ibn Humayd and Salama: sign after sign, one following another; the editor says it is a piece of report 15023 which al-Tabari took out and set here. Report 15032, from Mujahid through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Sa'd: "distinct signs" means known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 69}

On "but they were arrogant and were a criminal people" he says: those on whom God sent the signs and proofs mentioned grew proud against believing in God, holding Moses true and following him, exalted themselves against God and defied Him; "they were a criminal people" means they did what God dislikes of disobedience and wickedness, in defiance and rebellion. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "arrogance" and "crime". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 70}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
