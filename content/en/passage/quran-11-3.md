**Commentary on verse 3 (pp. 229-232)**

On [[passage/quran-11-3|11:3]] al-Tabari says: then its verses were set out so that you worship none but God and so that you ask your Lord's forgiveness. By 'ask your Lord's forgiveness' he means do, people, the deeds that please your Lord with you, so that He covers your grave sins which you committed by worshipping idols and images and joining gods and rivals with Him in worship. 'Then turn to Him in repentance' means then return to your Lord with pure worship of Him alone, apart from all else you worship besides Him, after you cast off rivals and renounce worshipping them. That is why it says 'then turn' and not 'and turn': repentance is returning to the work of obeying God, while asking forgiveness is from the polytheism they were persisting in; work for God is not work for Him until polytheism is given up, since the work of polytheism is only for Satan, and the polytheists thought they obeyed God in many of their deeds while persisting in their polytheism. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'asking forgiveness' and 'repentance'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 229}

His words 'He will give you good enjoyment for a fixed term' are God's to the polytheists He addressed in these verses: ask your Lord's forgiveness and turn to Him, for if you do so He will spread the world out for you, provide you with its adornment and defer your terms to the time when death is decreed for you. Report 17928, from Qatada: you are in that enjoyment, so hold to obedience to God and knowledge of His right, for God is a Giver of favour who loves the grateful, and the grateful are given more; 'a fixed term' means death, and reports 17929, 17930 and 17931, from Mujahid and Qatada, say death. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'enjoyment' and 'fixed term'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 230}

As for 'and give every possessor of merit his merit', it means He rewards everyone who showed merit toward another with his wealth, strength or kindness, reckoning it to God's account and seeking His face, with the fullest reward and favour in the next life. Reports 17932, 17933, 17934 and 17935, from Mujahid: what he reckoned to God's account of his wealth, or work of his hand or foot or a word, or all he volunteered of his affairs; report 17936, from Qatada: that is, in the next life. Report 17937 relates from Ibn Mas'ud: whoever does a wrong has one wrong written against him and whoever does a good deed has ten good deeds written for him; if he is punished in the world for the wrong, ten good deeds remain for him, and if not punished for it in the world, one of the ten is taken and nine remain; then he says, ruined is he whose ones outweigh his tens. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 231}

His words 'but if they turn away, I fear for you the punishment of a great day' mean: if they turn away from what I called them to, of pure worship of God and giving up the gods, and refuse to ask forgiveness and repent, and so turn their backs, then I fear for you, people, the punishment of a day great in its matter and terrible in its horror, the day when every soul is repaid for what it earned and they are not wronged. The speech moves from address to the absent and back to address because the Arabs, when they put a saying before the speech, address, then return to report about the absent, then return to address, as al-Tabari has shown in other places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 232}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
