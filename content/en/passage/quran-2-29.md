## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari reads the passage as follows: after counting His favours on the Jewish rabbis and the hypocrites and then telling the story of Adam, God singled out the People of the Book with the stories of Adam and others that they knew and the Arab polytheists did not, as an argument for His Prophet Muhammad against them, so they would know from what he told them that he is a sent messenger, since it was known that he never wrote, recited their books or sat with any of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 425} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 426}
On "He it is who created for you all that is on the earth" he says "huwa" refers to God, creating is bringing into being from nothing, and "ma" means "that which"; the earth and all in it is of benefit to people, in religion as evidence of their Lord's oneness and in this world as livelihood and a means to obedience. "How" here expresses wonder and rebuke rather than a question. He reports from Qatada that God subjected to them what is on the earth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 426} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 427}
On "then He rose over the heaven and formed seven heavens" he records disagreement over "istawa": some say it means He turned to it, citing a line of verse that al-Tabari considers misread; some say it did not involve moving but means His act, as one says the caliph moved from Iraq to Syria meaning his action moved; some say it means "it became level"; some that He aimed at it; and some that it means rising on high, which he reports from al-Rabi' ibn Anas as "He rose to the heaven". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 427} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 428}
He adds that those who take it as rising differ on who rose, some saying its Creator and others the smoke that God made a heaven for the earth. He then begins to show that "istiwa'" in Arabic has several senses, among them a man's reaching the height of his youth and strength, and the straightening of a matter that had been crooked. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 428}
