## Summary
Verse 106 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 106 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The omitted reply and the sentence's structure (p. 93).** Al-Tabari says the great punishment mentioned before falls on a day when some faces whiten and others darken. As for the question put to those with darkened faces, its sense is that they are told, in reproach, that they denied after affirming, so they are made to taste the penalty for that denial. He explains that the particle of division needs a reply introduced by the particle fa, and when the reply is dropped the fa drops with it, and the saying of "it is said" may be left out because what is mentioned shows it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 93} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 94}

**Who is addressed: first view, the people of our qibla (7601-7603, p. 94).** The interpreters differed over who is meant. Some said the people of our qibla among the Muslims. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7601): peoples disbelieved after their belief, and it was mentioned to them that God's prophet used to say that some who had accompanied him would come to him at the Basin and, when they were raised to him and he saw them, they would be pulled away from him; he would say, my companions, my companions, and be told that he did not know what they had introduced after him; and that those with whitened faces are the people of obedience to God and fidelity to His covenant. The editor says this report is mursal, that al-Bukhari relates it in his Sahih with other wording (Fath al-Bari 11:408 ff.) and Muslim (17:194), and he glosses "raised to me" as God showing them to him so he saw them from afar, and "pulled away" as snatched and drawn off. From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7602): this concerns those of the qibla who disbelieved when they fought one another. From Ibn Waki, his father, Hammad ibn Salama and al-Rabi ibn Subayh, Abu Mujalid, Abu Umama (7603): they are the Kharijites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 94}

**Second view: everyone who disbelieved after the first belief (7604, pp. 94-95).** Others said it means everyone who disbelieved in God after the belief he held when God drew the offspring from Adam's loins and made them witness against themselves; the editor refers to the covenant in [[passage/quran-7-172|7:172]]. From al-Muthanna, Ali ibn al-Haytham, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi, Abu al-Aliya, Ubayy ibn Ka'b (7604): on the Day of Resurrection they became two groups, and God spoke to those whose faces darkened, rebuking them; the belief meant is the one before the differing in Adam's time, when their covenant was taken, all acknowledged servanthood, He created them on Islam, and they were one community of Muslims; the others are those who stayed upright on that belief, kept religion and works sincere for Him, and God whitened their faces and admitted them to His good pleasure and garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 94} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 95}

**Third view: the hypocrites (7605, p. 95).** Others said those meant are the hypocrites. From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7605): they are the hypocrites, who gave the word of belief with their tongues and denied it by their hearts and deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 95}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 95-96).** Al-Tabari says the soundest of these views in his opinion is the one he related from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, that all disbelievers are meant, and that the belief for which they are reproached for turning away is the one they affirmed on the day of the covenant referred to in [[passage/quran-7-172|7:172]]. His argument is that God made the people of the Hereafter two groups, one with darkened faces and one with whitened, so all disbelievers belong to the first and all believers to the second; there is no ground for limiting the address to some disbelievers when the report covers them all, and since there is only that one state in which all of them had believed and then turned back, that state must be the one meant. The editor records in the note the manuscript's readings in two words where the printed text differs. This is al-Tabari's choice; the museum records all the views as they were transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 95} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 96}

**Al-Tabari's reading of the verse (p. 96).** So the verse's sense for him is: for them is a great punishment on a day when the faces of some whiten and of others darken; those with darkened faces are asked whether they denied God's oneness and His covenant and compact that they had made with Him, not to associate anything with Him and to devote worship to Him, after their affirming it, and told to taste the punishment for what they used to deny in the world of what God had taken their covenant to acknowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 96}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 96}
