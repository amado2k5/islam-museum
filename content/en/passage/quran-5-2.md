**Commentary on verse 2: do not violate God's symbols (pp. 462-464)**

Al-Tabari sets out the views on 'O believers, do not violate God's symbols'. Some say: do not violate God's sanctities or transgress His limits; report 10938, from Ata, who was asked about the symbols: God's sanctities, avoiding His prohibitions and following His obedience. Others say: do not violate God's sanctuary, that is the marks of God's sacred territory among the lands; report 10939, from al-Suddi: the symbols of God are His sanctuary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 462}

Others say: do not violate the rites of the pilgrimage and so waste them; report 10940, from Ibn Jurayj through Ibn Abbas: the rites of the pilgrimage. Report 10941, from Ibn Abbas through Ali ibn Abi Talha: the idolaters used to make the pilgrimage to the Sacred House, bring offerings, honour the sanctity of the places, and trade in their pilgrimage; the Muslims wanted to raid them, and God said: do not violate God's symbols. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 463}

Report 10942, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih: the symbols are al-Safa and al-Marwa, the offering animals and the camels; all this is among God's symbols; report 10943, from Mujahid through Shibl, the same. Others say: do not make lawful what God has forbidden you in the state of pilgrimage; report 10944, from Ibn Abbas: the symbols are what God forbade you to touch while you are in pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 464}

Al-Tabari says the shaʿa'ir are God's marks and landmarks that He made signs between truth and falsehood, by which His lawful and unlawful, His command and prohibition are known; the prohibition on making them lawful is general, and nothing may be excepted without proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 465}

On 'nor the sacred month', the sense is not to make lawful fighting your enemies among the idolaters in it. Report 10945, from Ibn Abbas: do not make lawful fighting in it. Report 10946, from Qatada: the idolater was then not barred from the House, so they were ordered not to fight in the sacred month nor near the House. Al-Tabari says the sacred month is Rajab of Mudar, though some say it is Dhu al-Qa'da; report 10947, from Ikrima: it is Dhu al-Qa'da. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 466}

On 'nor the offerings', they are animals a man offers to God's House, a camel, cow or sheep, seeking nearness to God; do not make them lawful and seize them from their people, nor stop them from reaching their place. Report 10948, from Ibn Abbas: an offering is an offering as long as it is not garlanded; once garlanded it is set apart. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 466}

On 'nor the garlands', some say they are the garlands of the offerings, meaning the unmarked and the garlanded offerings; report 10949, from Ibn Abbas: the garlands are the garlanded offerings, and when a man garlands his offering he enters the state of pilgrimage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 467}

Others say they are the garlands the idolaters wore on the way to the pilgrimage, of acacia bark, and on the way back of hair; report 10950, from Qatada: in the age of ignorance a man who set out for pilgrimage wore a garland of acacia and no one stopped him; when he returned he wore a garland of hair and no one stopped him. Report 10951, from Ata: they wore garlands of the bark of the sanctuary's trees and were safe when they left it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 468}

Reports 10952 to 10954, from Mujahid and al-Suddi: the bark on the necks of people and beasts is security for them; report 10955, from Ibn Zayd: a man would take bark from a tree in the sanctuary, wear it, and go wherever he wished, and be safe. Others say God forbade the believers to strip any tree of the sanctuary and wear it, as the idolaters did; reports 10956 and 10957, from Ata and al-Rabi' ibn Anas, say the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 469}

Al-Tabari prefers that 'nor the garlands' is joined to the first statement, so the sense is: do not make lawful the garlands, that is, the one garlanded, whether an offering or a person, rather than the garland itself; the garland is named for the garlanded, which the hearers understood. He cites a line of Hudhayfa ibn Anas, the Hudhali, about two men who killed two others who had worn such bark. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 470}

So the verse means: O believers, do not make lawful God's marks, nor the sacred month, nor the offerings, nor the one who garlands himself with the garlands of the sanctuary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 470}

On 'nor those making for the Sacred House seeking bounty from their Lord and His pleasure', it means: do not make lawful those intending for the Sacred House, who seek gains in their trade from God and His good pleasure; the Sacred House is God's house at Mecca. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 471}

On 'and good pleasure', it means that God is pleased with them for their rites. It is said the verse was revealed about a man of the Bakr tribe called al-Hutam. Report 10958, from al-Suddi: al-Hutam ibn Hind al-Bakri came to the Prophet alone, leaving his horses outside Medina; the Prophet invited him to Islam, and he asked for time to consult. When he left the Prophet said: he came with the face of an unbeliever and left with the back of a treacherous man; he drove off a flock of Medina's animals. The next year he came as a pilgrim with garlanded and offering animals, and the Prophet wished to send to him; the verse came down. Some Companions said: leave him to us, he is our companion; the Prophet said: he has garlanded, and they said: it is a thing we used to do in the Age of Ignorance; he refused, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 472}

Report 10959, from Ikrima through Ibn Jurayj: al-Hutam came to Medina with a caravan of food and sold it, then pledged allegiance to the Prophet and became Muslim; then he apostatised and set out with a caravan toward Mecca in Dhu al-Qa'da; a group of Emigrants and Helpers prepared to seize it, and the verse came down and they stopped. Report 10960, from Ibn Zayd: this was the day of the conquest, when men of the idolaters came to the House intending the minor pilgrimage; the Muslims said they would not leave them except by attacking them, and the Qur'an came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 474}

Report 10961, from Ibn Abbas: meaning whoever sets out for the pilgrimage. Report 10962, from al-Dahhak through Juwaybir: the pilgrim. Report 10963, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: those who intend the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 475}

The commentators then differ on what in the verse was abrogated, after agreeing that some of it was. Some say all of it was abrogated: report 10964, from Amir: nothing of al-Ma'ida was abrogated except this verse; report 10965, from Mujahid: it was abrogated by the verse of fighting; report 10966, from Qatada: it was abrogated because the idolater was not barred from the House and they were ordered not to fight in the sacred months or near the House, and the saying 'kill the idolaters wherever you find them' abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 475}

Report 10967, from al-Dahhak: it was abrogated by Bara'a. Report 10970, from al-Sha'bi: nothing of sura al-Ma'ida was abrogated except this verse. Report 10971, from Ibn Zayd: all of it is abrogated, abrogated by the command to fight the idolaters all together. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 476}

Others say only the phrase about the sacred month, the offerings, the garlands and those making for the Sacred House was abrogated. Report 10972, from Qatada: the phrase about those making for the Sacred House was abrogated by the verses of Bara'a, God saying: it was not for the idolaters to maintain God's mosques, and: the idolaters are unclean, so let them not come near the Sacred Mosque after this year; this was the year Abu Bakr made the pilgrimage and proclaimed the call. Report 10974, from al-Suddi: it was revealed about al-Hutam, then God abrogated it by the verse of fighting wherever you come upon them. Report 10975, from Ibn Abbas: believers and idolaters used to make the pilgrimage together, and God forbade believers to prevent anyone from making the pilgrimage, then abrogated this by the verses of Bara'a. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 477}

Others say nothing was abrogated except the garlands of acacia bark; report 10977, from Mujahid: the Companions of the Prophet said that all this was practice of the Age of Ignorance, which God forbade in Islam except the bark garlands, which were left. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 478}

Report 10978, from Mujahid through Ibn Abi Najih and Shibl: what God forbade every person is to frighten them. Al-Tabari judges the soundest view to be that God abrogated from this verse the phrase about the sacred month, the offerings, the garlands and those making for the Sacred House, since all agree God made fighting the idolaters lawful in the sacred months and other months, and an idolater who hung bark of the sanctuary's trees on his neck was not safe from death unless he had a prior covenant of protection or security. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 479}

Al-Tabari explains that 'nor those making for the Sacred House' may outwardly mean not making lawful the sanctity of those intending the House, of idolaters and Muslims alike; if idolaters are included, the words 'kill the idolaters wherever you find them' abrogate it, since one cannot be commanded to kill and not to kill at one time; most commentators hold this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 479}

On 'seeking bounty from their Lord and His good pleasure', al-Tabari says the bounty is the profit in trade and the good pleasure is God's approval of them, so that the punishment that befell other peoples in this world for their pilgrimage to His House is not to fall on them. Report 10979, from Qatada: they are the idolaters seeking God's bounty and good pleasure in what suits their worldly life; report 10980, from Qatada through Ibn Abi Arubah: that their livelihoods be set right in this world and that punishment not be hastened on them there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 480}

Report 10981, from Ibn Abbas: meaning that they seek God's pleasure by their pilgrimage; report 10982, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: trade in the pilgrimage, and good pleasure in the pilgrimage; report 10983, from Ibn Umar about a man who makes the pilgrimage and carries goods with him: there is no harm in it, and he recited this verse; report 10984, from Mujahid: they seek reward and trade. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 481}

On 'when you have come out of the state of pilgrimage, then hunt', al-Tabari says it means: when you come out of the state of pilgrimage, hunt the game that you were forbidden while you were in pilgrimage; there is no blame on you for it, since the reason it was forbidden has gone. Report 10985, from Mujahid: it is a concession; report 10986: five things in God's book are concessions and not obligations, and among them 'when you have come out, hunt'; whoever wills may do it, and whoever wills need not. Report 10989, from Mujahid: he did not regard eating from the offering of the enjoined pilgrimage as obligatory, and he explained this verse as he explained the verse of dispersing after prayer: whoever wills goes out, and whoever wills sits, as a concession from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 482}

On 'let not the hatred of a people move you', al-Tabari says it means: let not the hatred of a people move you. Report 10990, from Ibn Abbas: do not let the hatred of a people move you; report 10991, from Qatada: that is, do not let them move you. Grammarians differ: a Basran says the sense is 'do not make it right for you', as in 'undoubtedly for them is the fire'; a Kufan says 'do not move you', as one says: so-and-so moved me to do such a thing. They all cite a line on the killing of Abu Uyayna Husn ibn Hudhayfa on the day of Hajir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 483}

Al-Tabari holds that these views are close in meaning, since whoever moves a man to hate another has earned him hatred and made it right for him; the clearest explanation of the word is that of Ibn Abbas and Qatada: do not let the hatred of a people move you to transgress. Report 10992 gives the same. He discusses the readings: most readers read 'do not let them move you' with a fatha on the ya, while al-A'mash read it with a damma, from 'ajrama'; he prefers the common reading as widely transmitted and the other as an outlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 485}
