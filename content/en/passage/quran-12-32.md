**Commentary on verse 32 (pp. 85-86)**

On "she said: that is he about whom you blamed me" al-Tabari says the Aziz's wife told the women who had cut their hands that what had struck them, the loss of reason and understanding and the bewilderment that made them cut their hands, was what they had blamed her for in her love of him and the shaghaf of her heart, when they said she was in plain error. Then she admitted she had solicited him and that what they said of her was true. The editor explains "the going of understanding" as its departure, and "alihtunna" as being bewildered, from a root whose waw became a hamza, and says he took the sound wording from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 85}

On "he refused" she means refused what she asked of him. Report 19243, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Asbat: she says that after he had loosened the trousers he refused, I do not know what occurred to him. Report 19244, from Qatada: he held back; report 19245, from Ibn Abbas, through Ali ibn Dawud, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: he abstained. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on solicitation and on "'isma". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 86}

On "if he does not do what I command he will be imprisoned and be among the humbled" she says: if he does not yield to what I call him to, he will be jailed and be of those humbled and abased by prison, and I will humiliate him. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "prison" and "humbling". The pause on "layusjananna" is made on the nun because it is doubled, and the pause on "layakunan" is made with an alif because it is the light nun, like the nun of the case ending in nouns when one says "I saw a man" and pauses with an alif. The same is the pause on "la-nasfa'an" in Surat al-Alaq, and al-Tabari cites a verse of al-A'sha on pausing with an alif for the nun in a command to worship; the editor gives the divan's wording, slightly different, and thinks it the better of the two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 86}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
