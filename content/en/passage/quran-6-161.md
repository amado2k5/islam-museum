## Summary
Verse 161 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 161 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "say: my Lord has guided me to a straight path, an upright religion, the creed of Abraham, upright, who was not of the idolaters", God tells His Prophet to say to those who equate idols and images with their Lord: my Lord has directed me to the upright way, which is the religion of God He sent me with, the submitting hanifiyya, and enabled me to it; "upright religion" means straight, "the creed of Abraham" means Abraham's religion, "upright" means straight, and "who was not of the idolaters" means Abraham was not one who set up partners with God, for he was not among those who worship idols. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "guidance", "the straight path" and "creed". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 282}

The readers differ on "qiyaman": most readers of Medina and some Basrans read "qayyiman" with fatha on the qaf and a doubled ya, aligning it with "that is the upright religion" in [[passage/quran-9-36|9:36]] and elsewhere and with "the religion of the upright" in [[passage/quran-98-5|98:5]]; most Kufan readers read "qiyaman" with kasra on the qaf and an unmarked ya, saying "qayyim" and "qiyam" mean the same, two dialect forms meaning the upright religion. Al-Tabari says the sound view is that they are two well-known readings of the readers of the lands, agreeing in meaning, so whichever the reader reads he is right, though the fatha with doubled ya pleases him more because it is the more eloquent and better known form. "Religion" is accusative as a verbal noun from the sense of "my Lord guided me to a straight path", for the meaning is that my Lord guided me to an upright religion and I was guided to it, an upright religion; so it is accusative from the omitted "I was guided". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 282}

A Basran grammarian said it is accusative because when he said "my Lord has guided me to a straight path" he reported that he knew something, then said "an upright religion", as if he said, I know an upright religion, the creed of Abraham. As for the meaning of "hanif", al-Tabari explained it with evidence in its place in Surat al-Baqara, which spares repeating it here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 283}
