**Commentary on verse 90: "Wine, gambling, stone altars and divining arrows are an abomination", pp. 563-565**

Al-Tabari says: this is God's statement to those Companions who had forbidden themselves women, sleep and meat in imitation of priests and monks. He forbade them to make unlawful what He made lawful of good things, told them not to transgress His limits by making lawful what He forbade, and then told them what He forbade which, if they permitted it, would put them among the transgressors: the wine you drink, the gambling you play, the altars at which you sacrifice and the arrows by which you seek a decision are an abomination, sin and stench that God is angry at and dislikes for you, of the work of Satan, meaning his adorning and calling, not among the works your Lord invited you to or is pleased with for you. "Avoid it" means leave it and reject it, "that you may prosper" so that you succeed and attain success with your Lord by leaving it. He refers back to his earlier explanation of wine, gambling, arrows and altars.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 564}

Report 12510, from Ibn Abbas, says abomination means anger, and report 12511, from Ibn Zayd, that it means evil. The editor notes that a part of the manuscript's old division ends here, where the copyist closed it with a prayer and an invocation and began a new part; the museum describes this and does not quote it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 565}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
