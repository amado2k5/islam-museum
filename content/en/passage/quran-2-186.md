## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when My servants ask you about Me, I am near; I answer the call of the caller when he calls on Me", al-Tabari says the sense is: when My servants ask you, Muhammad, about Me, where I am, I am near them, hearing their supplication and answering the call of the caller among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 480} He then relates the differences over what the verse was revealed about. Some said it was revealed about a questioner who asked the Prophet "Muhammad, is our Lord near so we may whisper to Him, or far so we may call Him?"; this is by a chain through al-Salb ibn Hakim on his father on his grandfather, and the editor notes in a footnote that this chain is very weak because its narrators are unknown, and points out errors made by others in transmitting it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 480} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 481} From al-Hasan, the Prophet's companions asked him "where is our Lord?" and the verse came down; the editor notes that it is a mursal report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 481} Others said it was revealed in answer to people who asked the Prophet at what hour they should call on God, from 'Ata'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 482} From al-Suddi: there is no believing servant who calls on God without God answering him; if what he asks for is provision for him in this world, God gives it, and if it is not provision for him in this world, He stores it for him until the Day of Resurrection and wards off something disliked from him by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 482} Al-Tabari also relates a report that has reached a narrator from the Prophet that whoever is given supplication is not denied the answer, and the holders of this interpretation understand the verse as: when My servants ask you about Me, at what hour should they call on Me, I am near them at every time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 482} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 483} Others said it was revealed in answer to people who, when God said "call on Me and I will answer you", asked "to where do we call Him?", from Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 483} Others said it was revealed in answer to people who said "how shall we call?", from Qatada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 483}

On "let them respond to Me", he says the sense is: let them respond to Me with obedience; one says "istajabtu lahu" and "istajabtuhu" in the sense of "I answered him", and he cites a line of Ka'b ibn Sa'd al-Ghanawi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 483}
