## Summary
Verse 40 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 40 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**God does not diminish those who spend (pp. 359-360, reports 9502-9503, note 1).** Al-Tabari says God does not reduce any of His creatures who spent in His way of what He provided, in the reward of his spending in this world or its recompense on the day of resurrection, even by the weight of a dharra, as heavy as it would weigh, but repays and rewards him for it. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9502, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) from Qatada: he recited the verse ([[passage/quran-4-40|4:40]]) and said that to have his good deeds exceed his bad by a dharra's weight was dearer to him than the world and all in it; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9503, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: some people of knowledge used to say the same, dearer than owning the whole world. In note 1 the editor is sharp with the publisher of the printed edition, saying he did countless wrongs in distorting the text and handling it with no basis in understanding or trustworthiness, and, unable to read the manuscript, took what it has for nonsense and wrote other words (this is the editor's judgement). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 360}

**The dharra (pp. 360-361, report 9504, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari gives what was reported from Ibn Abbas on its sense: Ishaq b. Wahb al-Wasiti (9504, from Abu Asim, from Shabib b. Bishr, from Ikrima): the head of a red ant. Al-Tabari adds that Ishaq b. Wahb told him that Yazid b. Harun said: they claim this red dharra has no weight. The note on p. 360 identifies the chain: Ishaq b. Wahb b. Ziyad al-Allaf Abu Ya'qub al-Wasiti, from whom al-Bukhari, Ibn Maja, Abu Zur'a and Abu Hatim narrated, in al-Tahdhib; Abu Asim is al-Dahhak b. Makhlad; Shabib b. Bishr narrated from Anas and Ikrima and is described (as the editor gives it) as reliable but soft in hadith and prone to error. The note on p. 361 says the printed edition has 'the red worm', plainly wrong, and the manuscript a corrupt form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 361}

**Reports from the Prophet on rewarding the believer (p. 361, report 9505, note 2).** Al-Tabari says the sound reports from the Messenger agree with what he said. Ibn al-Muthanna and Ibn Bashshar (9505, from Abu Dawud, from Imran, from Qatada) from Anas: God does not wrong the believer a good deed, for which he is given provision in this world and repaid in the next, whereas the unbeliever is fed for it in this world and on the day of resurrection has no good deed. The note: Abu Dawud is al-Tayalisi and Imran is Ibn Dawar al-Qattan; the hadith is in al-Tayalisi's Musnad (2011) by this chain, in Ahmad's Musnad through Hammam from Qatada (12291, 12264, 14063), in Muslim (2:344-345) through Hammam and other paths, and cited by Ibn Kathir (2:450) and al-Suyuti (2:163). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 361}

**The hadith of intercession (pp. 361-362, reports 9506-9507, notes 1-2).** Musa b. Abd al-Rahman al-Masruqi (9506, from Ja'far b. Awn, from Hisham b. Sa'd, from Zayd b. Aslam) from Ata b. Yasar relates a long report (tied in its close, and in 9507, to Abu Sa'id al-Khudri from the Messenger): when the believers see they have been saved from the Fire they plead insistently for their brothers who prayed, fasted, made pilgrimage and fought with them and whom the Fire has seized; God tells them to bring out whomever they recognise by form, those forms being forbidden to the Fire, and they find men seized to mid-shin, knees or waist and bring out many; then they return and speak, and He tells them to bring out whoever has in his heart a qirat's weight of good, and they bring out many; this goes on until He says bring out whoever has in his heart a dharra's weight; Abu Sa'id used to say, when he related it, that if the hearers did not believe it they should read the verse (linked above), and then those still in the Fire say they left no good in it. Muhammad b. Abd Allah b. Abd al-Hakam (9507, from his father and Shu'ayb b. al-Layth, from al-Layth, from Khalid b. Yazid, from Ibn Abi Hilal, from Zayd b. Aslam, from Ata b. Yasar) from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri from the Messenger, to similar effect. Note 1: Ja'far b. Awn b. Amr b. Huraith al-Makhzumi al-Kufi is reliable and was used by the authors of the six books; the report is part of a long hadith on intercession given by the imams by many paths from Zayd b. Aslam, from Ata, from Abu Sa'id: al-Tayalisi (2179) via Kharija b. Mus'ab, Ahmad in the Musnad (3:16-17 and 3:94-95) via Abd al-Rahman b. Ishaq and Ma'mar, Muslim (1:66-67) via Hafs b. Maysara and then, without giving its wording, via Ja'far b. Awn from Hisham b. Sa'd, which is the path al-Tabari gives here. Note 2: al-Layth is Ibn Sa'd, Khalid b. Yazid is al-Jumahi al-Misri, Ibn Abi Hilal is Sa'id b. Abi Hilal al-Misri; the report repeats the one before; al-Bukhari (Fath al-Bari 13:358-361) has it through al-Layth b. Sa'd by this chain, and Ibn Kathir (2:449) gave a piece of it, crediting the two Sahihs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 362}

**Ibn Mas'ud on settling wrongs on the day of resurrection (pp. 362-364, reports 9508-9509, notes).** Others spoke as al-Muthanna relates (9508, from Muslim b. Ibrahim, from Sadaqa b. Abi Sahl, from Abu Amr) from Zadhan: I came to Ibn Mas'ud and he said that on the day of resurrection God will gather the first and the last and a caller from God will call: whoever seeks redress let him come to his right and take it; a man will rejoice that a right is established for him against his father, child or wife and take it though it is small, and the proof of that in God's Book is a verse of sura al-Mu'minun ([[passage/quran-23-101|23:101]]). He will be told to give them their rights and say: how, when the world has gone? God will tell His angels to look at his good deeds and give them from it; if a dharra's weight of good remains the angels say, and He knows better, that it remains after giving every holder of a right his right; and He says: multiply it for My servant and admit him to Paradise by the favour of My mercy, the proof of which is the verse under discussion, the large reward being Paradise. If his good deeds are exhausted and his bad remain, the angels say so, and that many claimants remain; God says add to it from their burdens and write him a writ to the Fire (Sadaqa said: or a writ to Jahannam, unsure which he said). Notes on p. 363: the printed edition misread a word, the right reading being 'the man' as in the references; the verb for being established is explained; a phrase is corrected from the manuscript; note 3 says Sadaqa b. Abi Sahl has biographies in al-Ta'jil (pp. 185-186), al-Bukhari's al-Kabir (2/2/298, no. 2891) and Ibn Abi Hatim (1/2/434-435, no. 1907), none mentioning any criticism, so the editor holds him reliable; the editor does not know which Abu Amr is meant, the kunya being common; Zadhan is al-Kindi al-Farra, a well-known reliable Tabi'i. Then al-Tabari gives 9509 (introduced as 'it was related to me from Muhammad b. Ubayd', from Harun b. Antara, from Abd Allah b. al-Sa'ib) from Zadhan from Ibn Mas'ud: the male and female servant will be taken by the hand on the day and a caller will call before the first and the last, this is so-and-so son of so-and-so, whoever has a right let him come to it; a woman will rejoice that a right is established for her against her father, son, brother or husband; Ibn Mas'ud then recited the verse of al-Mu'minun; God forgives of His own right what He wills and forgives nothing of people's rights; the servant is set up and told to bring the people their rights, says the world is gone, and is told to take from his good deeds and give each holder of a right according to his wrong; if he is a friend of God and a dharra's weight is left, it is doubled for him until he enters Paradise by it, and then he recited the verse to the hearers; if he is a wretched servant, the angel says his good deeds are exhausted and many claimants remain, and he says take from their bad deeds, add them to his, and write him a writ to the Fire. The note on p. 364: al-Tabari opened with an unspecified form, so the chain is lost; Ibn Kathir (2:449-450) took it from Ibn Abi Hatim through Abu Sa'id al-Ashajj, from Isa b. Yunus, from Harun b. Antara, removing the weakness at the start; Harun b. Antara's reliability was shown earlier, and Abd Allah b. al-Sa'ib al-Kindi al-Kufi is reliable and known, narrated from by al-A'mash and al-Thawri and used by Muslim, so the chain in Ibn Abi Hatim is sound; the report stops at Ibn Mas'ud, but the editor sees it as in effect attributed to the Prophet, because what Ibn Mas'ud says is not known by opinion and he was no one to relate from the People of the Book or accept Isra'iliyyat; Ibn Kathir cited it and said part of it has a witness in a sound hadith; al-Suyuti (2:193) adds Abd b. Humayd (these are the editor's judgements). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 364}

**Al-Tabari's reading on Ibn Mas'ud's account, and his choice (p. 365).** Al-Tabari says the sense of the verse on Abd Allah's account is that God does not wrong a servant owed a dharra's weight or more by another servant on the day of return, leaving it on the wrongdoer, but takes it from him for the wronged and takes from each wrongdoer for each wronged what he is liable for; and that, if a good deed is found, its reward and recompense are multiplied and he is given from Himself the large reward, which is Paradise on Abd Allah's account. The notes explain the word for what one is held liable for (a wrong or due one pursues a companion for), refer to the earlier treatment of the word for reward (page numbers unclear as printed), and say the verb for writing out a writ comes from the word for a document and is not recorded in the lexica, this being its witness. Al-Tabari says both interpretations, Ibn Mas'ud's and Qatada's, have an intelligible sense, and he chose the first because it agrees with the report from the Messenger and the outward sense of the verse points to it, since it comes in the context of the verse before, in which God urged spending in obedience to Him and condemned spending in obedience to Satan, and then joined to that what He promised those who spend in obedience to Him (the word is printed as 'hypocrites', and read here as 'those who spend' from the sense). This is al-Tabari's choice and argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 365}

**The two readings of the word for good deed (pp. 365-366, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed: most Iraqi reciters read it in the accusative, meaning if the weight of the dharra is a good deed He multiplies it, and most Medinese in the nominative, meaning if a good deed is found, as reported from Ibn Mas'ud. The note refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:269). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 366}

**The multiplying and who is meant (p. 366, report 9510, notes 2-4).** Al-Tabari says the verb comes with a long vowel and not with the doubling letter because, in the view of some grammarians, many-fold is meant; had double been meant the doubled form would be used; notes 2-3 say the reference is to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1:127), where the long form is many-fold and the doubled two-fold. Then the interpreters differed over those to whom this verse's promise was made. Some said all who believe in God and in Muhammad, and argued from al-Fadl b. al-Sabbah (9510, from Yazid b. Harun, from Mubarak b. Fudala, from Ali b. Zayd) from Abu Uthman al-Nahdi: I met Abu Hurayra and said it had reached me that he says a good deed is multiplied a million times; he said: what surprises you, by God I heard him, meaning the Prophet, say God multiplies a good deed two million times. Note 4: Ahmad has it in the Musnad (7932) from Yazid b. Harun by this chain, and the editor calls it sound; Ibn Kathir (2:451) gives it from the Musnad and from Ibn Abi Hatim by two chains; al-Suyuti (2:163) cites it. Others said the promise is for the emigrants only, not the desert dwellers and bedouin, and argued from a report (the report follows on the next page). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 366}

**The emigrants and the desert dwellers (p. 367, report 9511, notes 1-2).** Abu Nashit Muhammad b. Harun related from Yahya b. Abi Bukayr, from Fudayl b. Marzuq, from Atiyya al-Awfi, from Abdullah b. Umayr that the verse with the ten-fold promise ([[passage/quran-6-160|6:160]]) was revealed about the desert dwellers. A man asked whether it was for the emigrants too, and he answered that what is theirs is greater still, pointing to the promise of this verse, and added that when God calls a thing great it is great. In note 1 the editor calls the chain weak because of Atiyya al-Awfi (whose weakness he showed earlier, at 305), says al-Tabari's teacher Muhammad b. Harun b. Ibrahim al-Rabi'i is trustworthy and listed in al-Tahdhib, that Ibn Kathir (2:450) gives the report from Ibn Abi Hatim by way of Fudayl b. Marzuq with this chain and says nothing on it, and that al-Suyuti (2:162-163) cites it and adds Sa'id b. Mansur, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Tabarani. In note 2 he says the printed edition's form of the word for the emigrants differs from the manuscript and that he kept the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 367}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 367-368).** Abu Ja'far says the sounder of the two views is that the emigrants, not the desert dwellers, are meant, because it cannot be that God's reports, or his messenger's, contradict one another. If it is sound that a believer who brings a good deed has ten like it with God, and sound that some have their deed multiplied, and if the two reports he gave from the Prophet are both sound, then one must be general and the other explanatory, since the Prophet's reports confirm one another. So Abu Hurayra's report means that a good deed is multiplied two million fold for the emigrant believers and ten fold for the desert dwellers among them, as Ibn Umar reported from the Prophet; the ten-fold verse concerns the good deed of the believing desert dwellers, while the deed of their emigrants is multiplied and God gives them, from what is with Him, a recompense He calls great, and that recompense is paradise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 368}

**The recompense (p. 368, reports 9512-9514, notes 1-2).** Al-Muthanna related from Muslim b. Ibrahim, from Sadaqa b. Abi Sahl, from Abu Amr, from Zadhan, from Ibn Mas'ud (9512) that the meaning is paradise, which God gives; note 1 says it derives from the earlier report 9508. Al-Qasim related from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abbad b. Abi Salih, from Sa'id b. Jubayr (9513) that it is paradise; note 2 says Abbad is Abbad b. Abi Salih Dhakwan al-Samman, that is Abdullah b. Abi Salih, of whom al-Bukhari said in al-Saghir that his hadith is rejected, Ibn Ma'in said he is trustworthy, and al-Saji said he is trustworthy except that he related from his father what was not corroborated, and that he is listed in al-Tahdhib. Yunus related from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd (9514) that it is paradise too. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 368}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 368}
