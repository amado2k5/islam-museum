## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 64 to 66 in a single segment. He explains the phrase "Indeed, it has become a settled and a station": Indeed, Hell has become a settled and a station, meaning by 'stable' it means stability, and by 'maqam' it means residence, as if the meaning of the speech is: Hell has become a dwelling and a station. And if the mīm is attached to the maqam, it is from the iqāma, and if it is opened, it is from 'qammat'; it is said: the maqam, when the mīm is also opened, is the gathering. And from the maqam with a damma on the mim, meaning residence, is the saying of Salama ibn Jandal: Two days: a day of stations and clubs, and a day of marching to the enemies as a repentance. And from the maqam, which means gathering, is the saying of Abbas ibn Mirdas: 'So whatever is evil and you are lost, he does not see the maqamah,' meaning: the gathering. In the maqta, he quotes from al-Hasan and Ibn Zayd.
