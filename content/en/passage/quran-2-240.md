## Summary
Verse 240 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 240 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

He says the better of the two readings for him is the nominative, since the apparent sense of the Qur'an shows that the widow's staying a full year in her husband's house was a right of hers before the verse of four months and ten days and before the verse of inheritance came down, and because the reports of the Prophet agree, whether the husbands made a bequest for them or not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 252} His argument is that a testator bequeaths in his lifetime what he orders carried out after his death, and God gave the dead man's wife residence for a year after his death, so it is known to be a right due from his property without any bequest of his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 252} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} On the accusative reading the text would speak of those whom death is approaching, as in the verse on bequest elsewhere in the sura; and if it depended on a bequest it would not be her right when her husband made none, and his heirs could expel her before the year, yet God said without expulsion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} The sense of the clause for him is that God prescribed for their wives upon you a bequest from Him for them, that you do not expel them from their husbands' homes for a year, as in a verse in Surat al-Nisa' ([[passage/quran-4-12|4:12]]), the word prescribed being omitted because the speech indicates it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} Asked whether the accusative as a circumstantial is allowed, he says no, because it would be allowed only if the speech before it had something the bequest could issue from. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 254} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument on the reading and the meaning.

He then gives those who said that a year's residence was a right of the dead men's wives whether the husbands made a bequest or not, and that it was abrogated by the four months and ten days and by inheritance: Qatada, that the woman had residence and maintenance for a year from her husband's property so long as she did not leave, then this was abrogated in Surat al-Nisa', where she was given a known portion, an eighth if he had a child and a quarter if not, and a waiting period of four months and ten days (5572); al-Rabi', that this was before the verse of inheritance came down (5573). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 254}
