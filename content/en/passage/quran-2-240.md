## Summary
Verse 240 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 240 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

He says the better of the two readings for him is the nominative, since the apparent sense of the Qur'an shows that the widow's staying a full year in her husband's house was a right of hers before the verse of four months and ten days and before the verse of inheritance came down, and because the reports of the Prophet agree, whether the husbands made a bequest for them or not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 252} His argument is that a testator bequeaths in his lifetime what he orders carried out after his death, and God gave the dead man's wife residence for a year after his death, so it is known to be a right due from his property without any bequest of his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 252} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} On the accusative reading the text would speak of those whom death is approaching, as in the verse on bequest elsewhere in the sura; and if it depended on a bequest it would not be her right when her husband made none, and his heirs could expel her before the year, yet God said without expulsion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} The sense of the clause for him is that God prescribed for their wives upon you a bequest from Him for them, that you do not expel them from their husbands' homes for a year, as in a verse in Surat al-Nisa' ([[passage/quran-4-12|4:12]]), the word prescribed being omitted because the speech indicates it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} Asked whether the accusative as a circumstantial is allowed, he says no, because it would be allowed only if the speech before it had something the bequest could issue from. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 254} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument on the reading and the meaning.

He then gives those who said that a year's residence was a right of the dead men's wives whether the husbands made a bequest or not, and that it was abrogated by the four months and ten days and by inheritance: Qatada, that the woman had residence and maintenance for a year from her husband's property so long as she did not leave, then this was abrogated in Surat al-Nisa', where she was given a known portion, an eighth if he had a child and a quarter if not, and a waiting period of four months and ten days (5572); al-Rabi', that this was before the verse of inheritance came down (5573). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 254}

The commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-240|2:240]]) continues with abrogation reports. From Ibn 'Abbas: when a man died his widow observed a year in his house, maintained from his property, then the verse of four months and ten days came down as the waiting period of the widow, the pregnant woman's ending with delivery, and God set out the widow's inheritance of a quarter or an eighth in ([[passage/quran-4-12|4:12]]), leaving aside the bequest and maintenance (5574); from al-Dahhak, that the year was abrogated by the four months and ten days and the maintenance by the quarter and eighth (5575, 5576). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 255} From 'Ata', that the widow's inheritance from the dwelling was her residence for the year if she wished, then abrogated by inheritance; from Mujahid, that the year's residence was abrogated by inheritance; from Ibn Zayd, that the year's maintenance was abrogated by the quarter or eighth and the verse of the months is the abrogating one (5577, 5578). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 255} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 256} The editor notes in the margin that a word in the printed text was pointed wrongly and gives what he holds to be correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 256}

Al-Tabari then gives those who said this was by the husbands' bequest: Qatada, that before the fixed shares a man would bequeath to his wife and whomever he wished, then this was abrogated by the waiting period and by inheritance, and the bequest went to relatives who do not inherit (5579); al-Suddi, that maintenance and residence for a year came before the verse of shares came down (5580); and Qatada in another report (5581). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 256} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 257} Then those who said the provision was abrogated without specifying how: Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (5582, 5583); 'Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, that the inheritance verse abrogated it and made the period four months and ten days (5584); and Ibn 'Abbas in a sermon, that the verse on bequest for parents and kin ([[passage/quran-2-180|2:180]]) was abrogated, and so was this one (5585). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 257} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 258} The editor notes where the printed text departs from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 257} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 258}

Al-Tabari next gives those who said the verse's ruling stands and nothing in it was abrogated: Mujahid, that the verse of the months was an obligatory stay with her husband's family, then God gave her the rest of the year as a bequest, to stay if she wished or leave, the waiting period remaining obligatory (5586, 5587); 'Ata' from Ibn 'Abbas to similar effect, and 'Ata' himself, that inheritance abrogated residence so she observes the wait wherever she likes (5588). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 258} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 259}

Then al-Tabari makes his own choice and argument: that God gave the wives of dead men residence for a year in the home and maintenance from the estate to the year's end, and the heirs must not expel them, though if the women give up the right and leave the heirs bear no blame; then God abrogated the maintenance by the verse of inheritance, cancelled the residence of seven months and twenty nights, and returned them to four months and ten days, through the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 259} He then relates the report of Furay'a, sister of Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, whose husband went after a slave of his and was killed, and who asked the Prophet to move to her family; he told her to stay where she was until the prescribed term ended (5589). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 259} The editor's notes give the report's sources, correct her name, refer to Ibn Kathir and the Muwatta' of Malik, and judge the report sound; that judgement is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 259} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 260} This records what al-Tabari transmits and is not a legal ruling by the museum.

On the grammar, al-Tabari says the word for provision is in the accusative because the preceding clause carries the sense of providing for them, so it is a verbal noun from the meaning, not the form; that the negated expulsion word is accusative as a qualifier of the provision, as in saying this is standing without sitting; and that the view making it accusative through an expulsion verb is wrong, since that would make it part of a different sentence. The editor refers to al-Farra''s Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 260}

On the leaving clause, al-Tabari says the provision for the year is theirs while they stay in their husbands' homes, and their rights lapse if they leave of their own accord; there is no blame on the dead man's guardians for their leaving or giving up mourning, since staying the year and mourning was a permission and not a duty for them; if they leave there is no blame on them or the guardians for their adorning themselves, using perfume or marrying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 261} His reason is that were there blame on them, the guardians would be blamed for letting them leave when they could prevent it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 261} He explains the verse's ending: God is mighty in taking vengeance on whoever opposes His command and prohibition and exceeds His limits, men and women alike, and wise in what He decreed among His servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 261} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 262}
