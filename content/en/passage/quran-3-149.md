## Summary
Verse 149 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 149 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Forbidden to obey the deniers (pp. 276-277).** Al-Tabari: you who believed in God and His Messenger in His promise, warning, command and prohibition, if you obey those who denied the prophethood of your prophet Muhammad among the Jews and Christians in what they bid and forbid you, accepting their opinion and taking their counsel in what they claim to counsel you, they will carry you into apostasy after faith and disbelief in God, His signs and His Messenger after Islam, and you will turn back from your faith and religion as losers, perished, having lost yourselves, gone astray, and lost this world and the next. God here forbids the people of faith to obey the people of disbelief in their opinions and to take their counsel in matters of religion. The editor refers to earlier commentary on turning back on one's heels (p. 251, note 4) and on loss (references to earlier places in volumes 6 and 2 and elsewhere, garbled in the machine-read text). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 276} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 277}

**Reports (p. 277).** Ibn Ishaq (7998): that is, away from your religion, so that your world and the next are lost. The editor traces it to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 119), a continuation of 7997, whose wording there ("away from your enemy") makes no sense and is corrected from this place in al-Tabari. Ibn Jurayj (7999): do not take the Jews and Christians as counsellors about your religion and do not believe them in anything of your religion. Al-Suddi (8000): if you obey Abu Sufyan, he will turn you back to disbelief. The editor notes the printed text put the word in the plural and the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 277}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
