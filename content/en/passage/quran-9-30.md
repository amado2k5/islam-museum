## Summary
Verse 30 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 30 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 30 (pp. 201-208)**

The interpreters differed over who said that Uzayr is the son of God. Some said it was one man, Finhas. Report 16619, from Abdullah ibn Ubayd ibn Umayr, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: one man said it, whose name they say was Finhas, the one who said what is at [[passage/quran-3-181|3:181]], that God is poor and they are rich. Others said it was the saying of a group of them. Report 16620, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Yunus ibn Bukayr, Muhammad ibn Ishaq, Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad and Sa'id ibn Jubayr or Ikrima: Sallam ibn Mishkam, Nu'man ibn Awfa, Shas ibn Qays and Malik ibn al-Sayf came to the Messenger and asked how they could follow him when he had left their qibla and did not claim that Uzayr was the son of God, and God sent down the verse about their saying. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira and notes variations in the names there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 202}

Report 16621, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his grandfather: they said he was God's son because Uzayr was among the People of the Book, who had the Torah, and they acted on it as long as God willed, then neglected it and acted without right, the Ark being among them; when God saw that they had neglected the Torah and followed whims, He lifted the Ark from them, made them forget the Torah and erased it from their breasts, and sent a sickness on them so that their bowels loosened until they forgot the Torah and it was erased from their breasts, Uzayr being among them; they stayed as God willed after it was erased, and Uzayr, who had been one of their scholars, prayed to God and begged Him to return what had been erased from his breast of the Torah; and as he prayed, pleading, a light came down from God and entered his body, and what had gone from within him of the Torah returnedHe announced among his people, saying: people, God has given me the Torah and restored it to me; and he took to teaching them, and they stayed as God willed with him teaching them; then the Ark came down after it had been taken from them, and when they saw it they compared what was in it with what Uzayr had been teaching them and found it the same, and said by God, Uzayr was given this only because he is the son of God. The editor explains the phrase for "took to teaching" as meaning began, citing a line of Yazid ibn al-Tathriyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 203}

Report 16622, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the Jews said it because the Amalekites overcame them, killed them and took the Torah, and their scholars who remained passed away, they having buried the books of the Torah in the mountains; Uzayr was a boy who worshipped on the mountaintops and came down only on festival days, and the boy kept weeping and saying "Lord, You have left Israel without a scholar" until his eyelashes fell out. Once he came down for the festival, and on his return a woman appeared to him by one of those graves, weeping and crying "O my feeder, O my clother"; he said "woe to you, who fed you, clothed you, gave you drink and did you good before this man?", and she said God; he said "God is living and has not died"; she said "Uzayr, who taught the scholars before the Children of Israel?" and he said God; she said "then why do you weep for them?" and when he knew he had lost the argument he turned away, and she called him and said "Uzayr, tomorrow go to such and such a river, bathe in it, come out and pray two rak'as, and an old man will come to you; take what he gives you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 204}

In the morning Uzayr went to that river, bathed in it, came out and prayed two rak'as; the old man came and told him to open his mouth, and put into it something like a great red ember, three times; Uzayr went back the most knowing of people in the Torah and told the Children of Israel he had brought them the Torah, and they said Uzayr, you were never a liar; so he tied a pen to each of his fingers and wrote with all his fingers, writing the whole Torah. When the scholars returned and were told of Uzayr, they brought out their books of the Torah which they had buried in the mountains in jars, compared them with Uzayr's Torah and found them the same, and said God gave you this only because you are His son. The editor says this part of the report needs examination as to soundness and sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 204}

The reciters differed over the reading. Most of the reciters of Medina and some of Mecca and Kufa read it without the nunation on Uzayr, while some of Mecca and Kufa read it with nunation, saying it is a declinable name, though foreign, because of its lightness, and that it is not ascribed to God, so it is like the saying "Zayd son of Abdullah", the word "son" standing as the predicate. Those who omit the nunation do so because the letter after the "ibn" is quiescent along with the quiescent nunation, two quiescents meet, and the first is dropped to avoid the effort of vowelling it, citing a line of rajaz. Al-Tabari chooses as the better reading that with nunation on Uzayr, because the Arabs do not give nunation when "son" is an epithet of the name, but do when it is a predicate, as in "this is Zayd, son of Abdullah" when they mean to inform of Zayd that he is Abdullah's son; here "son" is the predicate of Uzayr, since those God reports as saying it were informing of Uzayr that he was so, though in saying it they were liars and inventors against God. The editor notes the manuscript sentence is badly confused and that the copyist dropped some of al-Tabari's words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 205}

"The Christians said the Messiah is the son of God; that is their saying with their mouths, imitating the saying of those who disbelieved before" means the saying of the Jews that Uzayr is the son of God; their saying resembles in lying against God and inventing against Him the lying of the Jews and their invention against God in ascribing to Uzayr that he is God's son, though it is not fitting that God have a child, rather to Him belongs what is in the heavens and the earth, all devout to Him, as at [[passage/quran-2-116|2:116]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 206}

Report 16623, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: they imitate means they liken; report 16624, from Qatada: the Christians imitated the Jews' saying before them; report 16625, from al-Suddi: the Christians imitate the Jews' saying about Uzayr; report 16626, from Ibn Jurayj: the Christians imitate the Jews' saying; report 16627, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: they said what the idol-worshippers said. It was also said that the sense is that they repeat in their saying the saying of the idol-worshippers who said al-Lat, al-Uzza and Manat the third, as at [[passage/quran-53-19|53:19]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 206}

The reciters differed over this too. Most of the reciters of the Hijaz and Iraq read the verb without the hamza, while Asim read it with the hamza, which is the dialect of Thaqif; the two are both dialects, one saying "I imitated him in such" when assisting him. Al-Tabari says the right reading is without the hamza, being the widespread reading among the reciters of the cities and the best Arabic. As for "may God fight them", it means, as reported from Ibn Abbas in report 16628, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali, "God curse them", every "killing" in the Qur'an being a curse. Ibn Jurayj said in report 16629, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj, that it refers to the Christians and is a phrase of the Arabs. Those who know the Arabs' speech say the sense is "God killed them"; the Arabs say a milder variant of the phrase, said to be gentler than "may God fight him", and they have a similar phrase for "may God make him wretched, how long he lasts". The editor explains it is a phrase the Arabs say without meaning killing, like "may your hands be dusted". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 207}

They said the sense of "may God fight them" is like "slain be the conjecturers" at [[passage/quran-51-10|51:10]] and "slain were the companions of the trench" at [[passage/quran-85-4|85:4]], having the sense of wonder. If what they say is so, it is a rare form of speech departing from the rule, since the "fa'ala" pattern rarely arises as a verb except between two parties, as in "I disputed with so-and-so" and "I fought him"; they claimed that the saying "may God keep you well" is of this kind, meaning "may God spare you", a prayer. And "how are they deluded" means in what way are they led off and made to swerve, and how are they barred from the truth, as has been explained earlier. {C(208)}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
