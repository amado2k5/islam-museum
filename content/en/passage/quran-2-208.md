## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "O you who believe, enter into al-silm entirely": the interpreters differed over the meaning of "silm". Some said Islam: Mujahid (4008, 4012), Qatada (4009), Ibn 'Abbas (4010), al-Suddi (4011), Ibn Zayd (4013) and al-Dahhak (4014). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 251} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 252} Others said "enter into obedience", from al-Rabi' (4015). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 252} The readers differed: most readers of the Hijaz read "salm" with fatha, most of Kufa with kasra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 252} Those who read it with fatha took it as peaceful dealing, that is, enter into settlement, ceasing war and paying the jizya; those who read it with kasra differed, some taking it as Islam and some as settlement, and for kasra with the sense of settlement he cites a verse of Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma, which the editor places in the story of the two mediators, al-Harith ibn 'Awf and Harim ibn Sinan, between 'Abs and Dhubyan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 253} Al-Tabari chooses as the soundest interpretation "enter into Islam entirely" and the reading with kasra, because for the Arabs the meaning of Islam and of lasting soundness is stronger in it than settlement; he cites a verse of Imru' al-Qays ibn 'Abis al-Kindi calling his kin to the silm when they apostatised, at the time Kinda apostatised with al-Ash'ath after the Prophet's death, and the editor identifies al-Ash'ath ibn Qays and what passed between him and Abu Bakr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254} Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala' read every other "silm" in the Qur'an with fatha except this one in al-Baqara, which he read with kasra, directing its meaning to Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254} Al-Tabari gives as his reason that the verse addresses believers: if it addresses believers in Muhammad who accept him, it would make no sense to tell them to enter into peace with the believers, because peace-making is commanded of those at war; if it addresses believers in the earlier prophets who deny Muhammad's prophethood, then Islam is meant, not settlement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254}
