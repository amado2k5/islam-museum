## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "O you who believe, enter into al-silm entirely": the interpreters differed over the meaning of "silm". Some said Islam: Mujahid (4008, 4012), Qatada (4009), Ibn 'Abbas (4010), al-Suddi (4011), Ibn Zayd (4013) and al-Dahhak (4014). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 251} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 252} Others said "enter into obedience", from al-Rabi' (4015). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 252} The readers differed: most readers of the Hijaz read "salm" with fatha, most of Kufa with kasra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 252} Those who read it with fatha took it as peaceful dealing, that is, enter into settlement, ceasing war and paying the jizya; those who read it with kasra differed, some taking it as Islam and some as settlement, and for kasra with the sense of settlement he cites a verse of Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma, which the editor places in the story of the two mediators, al-Harith ibn 'Awf and Harim ibn Sinan, between 'Abs and Dhubyan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 253} Al-Tabari chooses as the soundest interpretation "enter into Islam entirely" and the reading with kasra, because for the Arabs the meaning of Islam and of lasting soundness is stronger in it than settlement; he cites a verse of Imru' al-Qays ibn 'Abis al-Kindi calling his kin to the silm when they apostatised, at the time Kinda apostatised with al-Ash'ath after the Prophet's death, and the editor identifies al-Ash'ath ibn Qays and what passed between him and Abu Bakr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 253} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254} Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala' read every other "silm" in the Qur'an with fatha except this one in al-Baqara, which he read with kasra, directing its meaning to Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254} Al-Tabari gives as his reason that the verse addresses believers: if it addresses believers in Muhammad who accept him, it would make no sense to tell them to enter into peace with the believers, because peace-making is commanded of those at war; if it addresses believers in the earlier prophets who deny Muhammad's prophethood, then Islam is meant, not settlement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254}

Al-Tabari adds to his reasoning that God, in some circumstances, forbade His Prophet to call the disbelievers to settlement, saying "so do not weaken and call for peace when you are the upper hand" (47:35), and in others permitted accepting settlement when they called for it, in "and if they incline to peace, incline to it" (8:61); but calling them to settlement at the outset is not found in the Qur'an, so "enter into al-silm" cannot be directed to that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 254} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 255} He then asks which of the two groups was called to Islam entirely, and answers that this was disputed. Some said the believers in Muhammad and what he brought, and the point of calling them is to command them to act on all his laws, rulings and limits without neglecting some, so that "entirely" describes silm, enter into acting on all its meanings. Others said the believers in the prophets before Muhammad who deny Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 255} From 'Ikrima: it was revealed about Tha'laba, 'Abdallah ibn Salam, Ibn Yamin, Asad and Usayd the sons of Ka'b, Sa'ya ibn 'Amr and Qays ibn Zayd, all Jews, who said, Messenger of God, the Sabbath is a day we used to honour, so let us keep it, and the Torah is God's book, so let us stand with it at night; and the verse came down (4016); the editor notes that some of these names differ in the printed text and in al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 255} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 256} Al-Tabari says 'Ikrima stated plainly the sense he gave, that the verse calls believers to abandon everything not of the ruling of Islam and to act on all its laws, forbidding neglect of any of its limits. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 256} Others said the group called were the People of the Book, commanded to enter Islam: Ibn 'Abbas (4017) and al-Dahhak (4018). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 256} Al-Tabari concludes that, in his view, God commanded those who believe to enter into acting on all the laws of Islam; "those who believe" includes those who believe in Muhammad and those who believe in the earlier prophets and messengers, and God called both groups to act on the laws and limits of Islam and forbade neglecting any of them; so the verse is general for everyone the name of belief covers, and there is no reason to restrict it to some. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 256} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 257} Mujahid said the same: enter Islam entirely, enter all the deeds (4019). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 257}

On "kaffa": it means all, together, from Qatada (4020), al-Suddi (4021), al-Rabi' (4022), Mujahid (4023), Ibn 'Abbas (4024), Ibn Zayd (4025), who cited the verse of al-Tawba on fighting the polytheists all together (9:36), and al-Dahhak (4026). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 257} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 258}

On "and do not follow the footsteps of Satan; he is a clear enemy to you": al-Tabari means, act, believers, on all the laws of Islam, enter into belief in it in word and deed, and leave the ways and traces of Satan; Satan's way is whatever contradicts the ruling and laws of Islam, including keeping the Sabbath and the other practices of religious communities that differ from the way of Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 258}
