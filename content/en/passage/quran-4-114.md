## Summary
Verse 114 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 114 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 114: secret counsel, charity and reconciliation (pp. 201-202, note 2 on p. 201 and notes 1-4 on p. 202).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that there is no good in much of the secret counsel of people altogether, except that of one who commands charity or what is right; and what is right is everything God commanded or recommended of works of piety and goodness; or reconciliation between people, which is reconciliation between estranged or quarrelling parties by what God has allowed, so that they return to affection and unity of word as God permitted and commanded. Then he reports what God promised to whoever does that: whoever commands charity or what is right, or reconciles people, seeking God's pleasure, God will give a great reward, and the extent of what God has named great has no limit that anyone but He knows. The editor refers to earlier explanations of what is right (volume 7, p. 105 and volume 3, p. 371), of seeking (p. 170, note 3), of reward (p. 113, note 3) and of the word for known (volume 6, p. 518); and says the printed text omitted a letter in one sentence, where the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 202} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 202}

**The grammarians on the construction (pp. 202-204, notes 1-6 on p. 203).** Al-Tabari says the grammarians differed over the construction of the exception. A Basran grammarian said the sense is that there is no good in much of their counsel except in the counsel of one who commanded charity, as though "who" were joined to the pronoun in their counsel; al-Tabari says this is an error in the view of the grammarians, because the exception particle is not joined to the pronoun in such a place, since no negation reached it. A Kufan grammarian said "who" can be in the genitive or accusative. In the genitive the sense is no good in much of their counsel except in one who commanded charity, and the counsel is then the people conversing, as in 58:7 on the counsel of three, and 17:47 on when they were in counsel. In the accusative the counsel is taken as a verbal noun and the exception is a disconnected one, since "who" is other than the counsel, like the poet's line, attributed to al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani, which says nothing is in the dwelling except the tethering places the speaker can barely make out. It may also be in the nominative, as in a line of the poet Jiran al-Awd, about a land with no companion in it except gazelles and white antelope. Al-Tabari's choice, his own, is that "who" is in the genitive, by reference to the word for counsel, and counsel is the plural of those who confer, in the pattern of the words for drunk, wounded and ill, as that is its plainest sense; so the meaning is that there is no good in much of those who confer, Muhammad, from among people, except in one who commands charity, what is right or reconciliation between people, since in those there is good. The editor's notes: that the word he calls an act means a verbal noun; that the printed text has "from the opposite of counsel" where the manuscript is right; that the first poet is al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani, whose line with its full text and sources is given earlier (volume 1, pp. 183 and 523) and in al-Farra' (1:288); that the second poet is Jiran al-Awd al-Numayri, whose line is in his diwan (p. 52), Sibawayh (1:365 and 133), al-Farra', the Majalis of Tha'lab, the Khizana, al-Ayni and many works of grammar, and recurs later in the commentary; and, from p. 203 to p. 204, his account of the poem in the diwan, which describes a deserted tract left to a wolf, a lion and wild cattle and gazelles, with his explanations of the words for seeking food, the hearty eater (where he corrects the Khizana), the lion with a mane, the empty waste, the land to which animals resort, the dust-coloured and white gazelles, those entering their coverts and the proud-stepping ones. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 204} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 204}
