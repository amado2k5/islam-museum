## Summary
Verse 97 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 97 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "God has made the Ka'ba, the sacred House, a support for people, and the sacred month, the offering and the garlands": God made the Ka'ba, the sacred House, a support for people who have no support in a chief who restrains their strong from their weak, their wrongdoer from their doer of good, their oppressor from the oppressed, and likewise the sacred month, the offering and the garlands; by each of these He restrained some of them from others, since they had no other support, and He made them landmarks of their religion and benefits for their affairs. He notes that the Ka'ba, it is said, is called "Ka'ba" for its squareness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 89}

Report 12780, from Mujahid: the Ka'ba is so called because it is square. Report 12781, from Ikrima: it is called Ka'ba for its squareness. Al-Tabari explains that "qiyam" with ya is of the roots with waw, the waw turned to ya because of the kasra on the qaf, as the verbal noun of "I stood" is "qiyam" and of "I fasted" is "siyam", when the origin is "qiwam" and "siwam"; it has come in the original form with waw in the verse of the rajaz poet Humayd al-Arqat, who calls something the support of the world and the support of religion. God made the Ka'ba, the sacred month, the offering and the garlands a support to those of the Arabs who held these sacred and honoured them, in the position of the chief by whom the affairs of his followers stand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 90}

As for the Ka'ba, al-Tabari says it means the whole sanctuary, and that God called it "haram" because He forbade hunting its game, cutting its green plants or felling its trees. The words "and the sacred month, the offering and the garlands" mean that He also made the sacred month, the offering and the garlands a support for people, as He made the Ka'ba, the sacred House, a support for them. There is disagreement about the "people" for whom this was made a support: some said God made it in pre-Islamic times a support for all people, and some said the Arabs in particular were meant. Report 12782, from Mujahid: a support for people. Report 12783, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the soundness of their religion. Report 12784, from Mujahid: "a support for people" at a time when they hoped for no paradise and feared no fire, so God strengthened this by Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 91}

Reports 12785 and 12786, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a strengthening of their religion. Report 12787, from Ibn Abbas: its being a support is that whoever turns to it is safe. Report 12788, from Ibn Abbas: a support for their religion and landmarks of their pilgrimage. Report 12789, from al-Suddi: God made these four a support for people, the stay of their affairs. Al-Tabari judges that these statements, though their wording differs, come back to the point that the support of a thing is that by which it is set right, as the great king is the support of his subjects and those in his authority, since he manages their affairs, restrains their wrongdoers from their wronged, and wards off from them the harm of those who oppress and are hostile to them; so the Ka'ba, the sacred month, the offering and the garlands were the support of the Arabs' affairs by which they were set right in the pre-Islamic time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 92}

In Islam they are for its people the landmarks of their pilgrimage and rites, the direction of their prayer and their qibla, by facing which their obligation is completed; and a group of the interpreters spoke along these lines. Report 12790, from Qatada: barriers God left among people in the pre-Islamic time; a man might commit every offence and then take refuge in the sanctuary and would not be touched or approached; a man who met his father's killer in the sacred month would not molest him; a man heading for the House put on a garland of hair, which protected him and kept people from him, and on his return put on a garland of idhkhir grass or of acacia bark, which kept people from him until he reached his family. Report 12791, from Ibn Zayd: all peoples had kings who defended some from others, but the Arabs had no kings, so God gave them the sacred House as a support by which some are warded off from others, and the sacred month likewise and the garlands; a man might meet the killer of his brother or his cousin and not molest him; and all this has been abrogated. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 93}

Report 12792, from Ibn Abbas: "the garlands": in the pre-Islamic time some people put on bark of trees when they meant to make the pilgrimage, and were known by that. Al-Tabari says he has already given the explanation of "the sacred month", "the offering" and "the garlands". He then explains "that is so that you may know that God knows what is in the heavens and what is on the earth, and that God has knowledge of everything": "that" means His making the Ka'ba, the sacred House, a support for people, and the sacred month, the offering and the garlands. He says: I made that a support for you, people, so that you may know that He who brought about for the benefit of your worldly affairs what He brought about, by which you stand, knowing your benefits and harms, in the same way knows everything in the heavens and on the earth in which lies the good of your present and your later life, and so that you may know that He has knowledge of everything, that nothing of your affairs and deeds is hidden from Him, and He is counting them against you to repay the doer of good for his good and the doer of evil for his evil. The reports on verse 97 end here, on p. 94. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 94}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
