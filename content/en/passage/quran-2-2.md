## In al-Tabari's commentary: "that Book"
Al-Tabari rejects a view that takes "these letters are that Book" as a statement about the letters themselves, holding that it departs from what the Companions and Followers said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 221}
Al-Tabari reports that most exegetes read "that Book" as "this Book", citing [[person/mujahid-ibn-jabr|Mujahid]], [[person/ikrima-mawla-ibn-abbas|Ikrima]], [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] and [[person/ismail-ibn-abd-al-rahman-al-suddi|al-Suddi]]. The editor notes that the narrator in one of these chains is very weak and accused of fabricating reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 225}
He asks how "that" can mean "this", since "this" points to something present and "that" to something absent. His answer is that what has just been related is close to the listener and so counts as present; the speech about the letters has just ended, so the Book is pointed to with "that". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 226}
He gives a second reading, that the Book means the surahs revealed before al-Baqara at Mecca and Medina, since all of them belong to this one Book, and he prefers the first reading as the clearer sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 226} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 227}
He also reports a view, held by some, that the Book means the Torah and the Gospel, and says that on that reading "that" would simply refer to an absent thing without strain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 227} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 228}

## In al-Tabari's commentary: "no doubt in it"
Al-Tabari gives "no doubt in it" as the meaning of "la rayb fihi", reporting it from [[person/mujahid-ibn-jabr|Mujahid]], Ata, al-Suddi, [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]], [[person/abdullah-ibn-masud|Ibn Masud]], [[person/qatada-ibn-diama|Qatada]] and [[person/al-rabi-ibn-anas|al-Rabi' ibn Anas]], and from [[person/ibn-jurayj|Ibn Jurayj]] on Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 228} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 229}
He derives "rayb" from the verb meaning that a thing troubled or unsettled a person, and cites a line of verse by Sa'ida ibn Juayya that uses it in this sense. The pronoun in "fihi" refers to the Book: there is no doubt that this Book is from God, a guidance for the God-fearing. Ibn Kathir, quoting Ibn Abi Hatim, says that he knows of no disagreement on this point. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 229}
The discussion of "guidance for the God-fearing" is in the entry on [[concept/hidaya|hidaya]].
