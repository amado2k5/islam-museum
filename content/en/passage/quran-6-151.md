## Summary
Verse 151 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/wad|burying daughters alive (wa'd)]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 151 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you", God tells His Prophet to say to these idolaters, who claim God forbade them what they forbid of their crops and cattle: come, people, and I will recite what your Lord forbade, as truth and certainty, not as falsehood and guesswork such as your conjecture of lies about God, but as revelation God gave me and a sending down upon me: that you set up no partner with Him from among His creation, do not equate idols and images with Him and worship nothing else. "And kindness to parents" means "He enjoined kindness to parents"; the verb is dropped because the speech points to it and the hearer knows it, as al-Tabari showed earlier with evidence, and the editor refers to those places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 215}

As for "that" in "that you set up no partner", it is in the nominative, because the sense is: say, come, I will recite what your Lord forbade you, it is that you set up no partner with Him. Given that sense, "you set up partners" can be read two ways: as a jussive of prohibition, taking "not" as a prohibition, or in the subjunctive, taking the speech as a statement after "that not". If you wish, you may take "that" as accusative, in apposition to "what" and explaining it, and the two readings of the verb remain, with "that" in the nominative; the sense is then: come, I will recite what your Lord forbade you, I will recite that you set up no partner with Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 215}

If someone asks how the verb can be subjunctive after "that not", or the speech taken as a statement, when it is joined by "do not kill your children for poverty" and what follows in the jussive of prohibition, the answer is that this is allowed, as in [[passage/quran-6-14|6:14]], where "that I be" is made a statement and "that" a noun and a prohibition is then joined to it, and as in a line by a poet cited by al-Farra' in his Ma'ani al-Qur'an, where "that you not see" is a statement and prohibitions are joined to it. The editor notes that the phrase "and never be of the idolaters" is missing in both the printed text and the manuscript and that he restored it from al-Farra' because the sentence is spoiled without it; that he does not know the poet; and that al-Farra' lacks the third line and has a different one in its place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 216}

Then, on "do not kill your children for poverty", al-Tabari says God means: do not bury your children alive and so kill them out of fear of poverty on yourselves because of their expenses, for God is the provider of you and them, and their provision is not on you, so you need not fear for yourselves, over their lives, the inability to provide their food and sustenance. "Imlaq" is a verbal noun from "I ran out of provisions", said when one's provisions are gone, his wealth lost and he is destitute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 217}

He gives (14135) from Ibn Abbas that imlaq is poverty and that they killed their children for fear of poverty; (14136) from Qatada that it is fear of want; (14137) from al-Suddi that imlaq is poverty; (14138) from Ibn Jurayj that "for poverty" means their devils tell them to bury their children alive for fear of need; and (14139) from al-Dahhak that it means fear of poverty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 218}

Then, on "do not go near indecencies", he says: do not go near the forbidden things that are apparent, done openly among you, which you do not condemn, nor the hidden ones that you do in secret and do not flaunt; all of it is forbidden. It was said that the phrase "what is apparent of them and what is hidden" was used because they found some kinds of zina ugly and not others; he says this is not rejected, but the apparent sense of the revelation prohibits every indecency, apparent or hidden, and no report removes the excuse by showing that only part was meant; and the apparent sense of God's Book may not be turned to a hidden one without a proof that must be accepted. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "indecencies", "apparent" and "hidden". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 218}

He then lists the reports. Some make the verse specific: (14140) from al-Suddi that "what is apparent" is the prostitutes of the shops (the editor explains that such women set up a booth with a flag to advertise their state) and "what is hidden" is what is concealed; (14141) from al-Dahhak that people of the Jahiliyya did zina in secret and considered it lawful if secret, so God forbade it in secret and in public; and (14142) from Ibn Abbas, similarly, that they saw no harm in zina in private and disliked it in public, and God forbade it in both. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 219}

Some match al-Tabari's choice: (14143) from Qatada that it means their secret and their public, and (14144) the same by another route from Qatada. Others said "what is apparent" is marriage to mothers and fathers' wives and "what is hidden" is zina; (14145) from Mujahid that "apparent" is combining two sisters and a man's marrying his father's wife after him and "hidden" is zina; and others said "apparent" is wine and "hidden" is zina, which is (14146) from al-Dahhak, and the editor notes that he found no biography for some narrators of the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 220}

Then, on "do not kill the soul that God has forbidden", he says that the soul God forbade killing is that of a believer or one under covenant, and that "except by right" means what makes killing it lawful: that a person kills a soul and is killed in retaliation, or commits zina while married and is stoned, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 220}

or turns from his true religion and is killed; that is the right by which God permitted killing the soul He forbade believers to kill. "That" refers to these matters in which our Lord enjoined us not to do or leave them, matters He enjoined on us and on the disbelievers to act on together; and "that you may understand" means He enjoined it so that you may understand what your Lord enjoined. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "enjoined". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 221}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
