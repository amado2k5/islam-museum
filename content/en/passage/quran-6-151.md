## Summary
Verse 151 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/wad|burying daughters alive (wa'd)]]. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 151 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: come, I will recite what your Lord has forbidden you", God tells His Prophet to say to these idolaters, who claim God forbade them what they forbid of their crops and cattle: come, people, and I will recite what your Lord forbade, as truth and certainty, not as falsehood and guesswork such as your conjecture of lies about God, but as revelation God gave me and a sending down upon me: that you set up no partner with Him from among His creation, do not equate idols and images with Him and worship nothing else. "And kindness to parents" means "He enjoined kindness to parents"; the verb is dropped because the speech points to it and the hearer knows it, as al-Tabari showed earlier with evidence, and the editor refers to those places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 215}

As for "that" in "that you set up no partner", it is in the nominative, because the sense is: say, come, I will recite what your Lord forbade you, it is that you set up no partner with Him. Given that sense, "you set up partners" can be read two ways: as a jussive of prohibition, taking "not" as a prohibition, or in the subjunctive, taking the speech as a statement after "that not". If you wish, you may take "that" as accusative, in apposition to "what" and explaining it, and the two readings of the verb remain, with "that" in the nominative; the sense is then: come, I will recite what your Lord forbade you, I will recite that you set up no partner with Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 215}

If someone asks how the verb can be subjunctive after "that not", or the speech taken as a statement, when it is joined by "do not kill your children for poverty" and what follows in the jussive of prohibition, the answer is that this is allowed, as in [[passage/quran-6-14|6:14]], where "that I be" is made a statement and "that" a noun and a prohibition is then joined to it, and as in a line by a poet cited by al-Farra' in his Ma'ani al-Qur'an, where "that you not see" is a statement and prohibitions are joined to it. The editor notes that the phrase "and never be of the idolaters" is missing in both the printed text and the manuscript and that he restored it from al-Farra' because the sentence is spoiled without it; that he does not know the poet; and that al-Farra' lacks the third line and has a different one in its place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 216}
