## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "He prays for the one whose harm is closer to his benefit than his benefit, for wretched the master and the wretched of the tire": He calls upon the one who is turned upside down, because if he is afflicted by a trial of gods whose harm in the Hereafter is closer and quicker to him than its benefit. He mentioned that Ibn Mas'ud used to recite it: "He supplicates from his harm closer to his benefit than his benefit." The Arabs differed regarding the place of 'min'. Some grammarians of Basra say its position is in the accusative with 'yad'a', and they say its meaning: he prays to gods whose harm is closer to their benefit, and he says it is unusual because there is no mention in the text: 'praying for Zayd'. Some grammarians from Kufa used to say: the lam is from the conjunction 'ma' after 'min,' as if the meaning of the speech for him is: to call upon someone whose harm is closer to his benefit than his benefit. It was narrated from the Arabs that it was heard from them: 'To me, what is better than what others have,' meaning: 'I have what is better for someone else than him, and I have given you what is better than what is better than him,' meaning: 'What is better for someone else.' In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Ibn Zayd.
