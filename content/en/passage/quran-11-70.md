**Commentary on verse 70 (pp. 387-389)**

On [[passage/quran-11-70|11:70]] al-Tabari says: when Abraham saw their hands did not reach the calf he had brought them, the food he had set before them, he mistrusted them, for when he set his food before them, as is reported, they held back from eating it, being not of those who eat it; their holding back from his food, when they were his guests, was to Abraham strange, there was no acquaintance between them, and their matter startled him and he felt fear of them in himself. Qatada used to say, reports 18311 and 18312: when a guest came to the Arabs and did not eat of their food they thought he had not come with good and was thinking evil in himself, and then they would tell him what they had come for. Report 18313, from Jundab b. Sufyan: when Abraham's guests entered he brought the calf near them and they began to poke at it with arrows in their hands and their hands did not reach it, and he mistrusted them then. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 388}

One says 'nakartu the thing, ankaruhu' and 'ankartuhu, ankaruhu', of one sense; a line of al-A'sha joins both forms, and one of Abu Dhu'ayb about wild asses that heard the hunter, 'they mistrusted him and shied away'. His words 'and he felt in himself fear of them' mean he sensed fear of them in himself and kept it in; 'they said, do not fear' means the angels said, seeing Abraham's fear of them, do not fear us and be safe, for we are angels of your Lord, 'sent to the people of Lot'. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on 'fear'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 389}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
