**Commentary on verse 136 (pp. 74-75)**

Al-Tabari says: when they broke their covenants "We took vengeance on them", that is, We gained victory over them by bringing Our vengeance on them, which is His punishment, "and drowned them in the yamm", that is, the sea, as Dhu al-Rumma says in a line likening a fearsome desert and a dark night to the sea; the editor gives in a footnote what precedes the line, describing the desert, the meanings of its words, and calls the poem splendid. Also as al-Ajjaj says in a line describing the sea's waves, where the editor notes the printed text misread the manuscript and glosses "vast" and "the heart of the sea". The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "vengeance". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 74}

On "because they denied Our signs" he says: We did that to them for their denial of Our proofs and marks that We showed them; "and were heedless of them" means they were heedless of the vengeance We brought down on them before it fell, not knowing it was descending. The pronoun in "of them" refers back to the vengeance; if someone said it refers to the signs, taking the sense to be "and were turning away from them", making their turning away a heedlessness when they would not accept them, that would be a possible view. One says of heedlessness, "the man was heedless of such a thing", in several noun forms. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "sign" and "heedlessness". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 75}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
