**Commentary on verse 163 (pp. 179-184)**

Al-Tabari says: ask, Muhammad, these Jews, who are your neighbours, about the matter of "the town that was by the sea", meaning in the presence of the sea, near it on its shore. The people of interpretation differed about it. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "rijz". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 179}

Al-Tabari says the people of interpretation differed about this town. Some said it is Ayla, and they relate this from Ibn Abbas by several routes: one says it was a town between Madyan and al-Tur, another that it lay on the sea shore between Egypt and Medina; and there is the report of Ikrima, who entered on Ibn Abbas with the Qur'an in his lap while he wept, asked him why, and Ibn Abbas said: do you know the town that was by the sea? Ikrima answered: that is Ayla. The same is related from Abd Allah ibn Kathir, al-Suddi and Mujahid. The editor notes that the two teachers of al-Tabari in the last report were identified earlier and that the report is completed in a later one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 180} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 181}

Others said it means the coast of Madyan, and Qatada is reported as saying it was a town on the sea coast called Ayla. Others said it is "Maqna"; Ibn Zayd relates that it was a town between Madyan and "Aynuni", and the editor gives the spellings of that name and what Yaqut and al-Bakri say of it: a place in the Syrian region on the road the Egyptian pilgrims travelled, and he adds Ibn Sa'd's report of the Prophet's letter to Nu'aym ibn Aws, brother of Tamim al-Dari, granting him the villages of Hayri and Aynun. Others said it is Madyan, and Ibn Abbas is reported to have said it was a town between Ayla and al-Tur called Madyan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 181} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 182}

Al-Tabari's choice is that it is a town by the sea, and it may be Ayla, Madyan or Maqna, since each lies by the sea; there is no report from the Prophet that settles which, and knowledge of what passed and was not witnessed comes only through a report that gives certain knowledge, of which there is none here. The editor explains what the publisher altered in the printed text and that he returned to the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 182}

On "when they transgressed in the Sabbath", al-Tabari says the people of the town broke God's command regarding the Sabbath and went past it to what God had forbidden them; the Arabic verbs mean to overstep a command. Their transgression was that God had forbidden them the Sabbath and they fished in it. "When their fish came to them on their Sabbath day, openly" means visible on the water from every road and side, like the main roads, and Ibn Abbas is reported as saying visible on the water and from every place; the editor comments on the narrators of the chain and refers to a long report given earlier in the second volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 182} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 183}

"The day they did not keep the Sabbath" means all the other days, which they did not honour as they honoured the Sabbath, when the fish did not come. "Thus We tried them for their wickedness" means as described: the test of showing the fish on the surface on the day when hunting was forbidden and hiding them on the day it was permitted, trying them because of their wickedness, that is, their departure from obedience to God. He also gives the readers' differences over the verb "yasbitun": it was read with a fathah on the first letter, from "he kept the Sabbath", and it is related that al-Hasan al-Basri read it with a dammah, from "the people entered the Sabbath", as one says "we entered a Friday" when a Friday passes. The word "day" is in the accusative because of "they do not come". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 183} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 184}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
