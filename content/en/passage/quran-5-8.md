**Commentary on verse 8: standing for God in justice (pp. 95-96)**

On 'be steadfast for God, witnesses to justice', al-Tabari says: let it be among your traits to stand for God as witnesses to justice among your friends and your enemies; do not be unjust in your rulings and deeds, exceeding what God set for you against your enemies because of their enmity, nor falling short of what he set for you towards your friends because of their friendship, but in all cases stop at his limit. 'Let not hatred of a people lead you' means do not let enmity make you unjust in your judgement on them and conduct among them. He notes that he has given the reports on the similar verse in the Women chapter and on the opening of this chapter, and the differences in reading, earlier. It has been said that this verse came down when the Jews planned to kill the Messenger of God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 95}

Report 11556, from Abdallah ibn Kathir, says the verse came down about the Jews of Khaybar who wanted to kill the Prophet; Ibn Jurayj reports from him that the Messenger of God went to the Jews to ask their help with a blood-price and they planned to kill him, which is the sense of the verse. Al-Tabari then explains 'be just, that is nearer to piety': be just, believers, to everyone, friend or enemy, and hold them to the rulings I commanded, not deviating from them for anyone. 'It is nearer to piety' means that being just to them brings you nearer to being, in God's sight, people of piety, those who fear and guard against opposing any of his commands. Justice is described so because one who is just obeys God by his justice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 96}

Al-Tabari adds: one who is unjust is disobedient to God, and one who is disobedient is far from piety. 'It is nearer' stands for the act; the Arabs refer to acts with 'it' and 'that', as in two verses of the Cow chapter, and had 'it' been absent, 'nearer' would be in the accusative, as in 'desist, it is better for you'. As for 'fear God', it means: beware, believers, of being unjust to God's servants by overstepping his ruling and decree that he made clear to you, which would bring his punishment; God has knowledge of what you do in what he commanded and forbade, and counts it all so as to repay the doer of good with good and the doer of wrong with wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 97}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
