## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And it is forbidden for a town We destroyed that they will not return": The reciters differed in the reading of the phrase "and haram," so the majority of the reciters of Kufa read it as "and haram" with a kasra on the ha. The general reciters of Medina and Basra read this as "and haram" with a fatha on the ha and the alif. The correct view is that they are two well-known readings with a consistent meaning, not differing, because the sacred is the haram and the forbidden is the sanctuary, just as the permissible is halal and halal is permissible. Whichever the reader recites, he is correct. Ibn Abbas used to recite it: 'and it is forbidden' with the interpretation of: and 'azam' (resolved). From Abu al-Mu'alla, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas, he used to recite it: 'and he was forbidden to a village.' He said: So I asked Sa'id: What was forbidden? He said: He was resolved. And from Abu al-Mu'alla, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas, he used to recite it: 'and he was forbidden to a village.' I asked Abu al-Mu'alla: What is the sanctuary? He said: He resolved to it. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah, and Sa'id ibn Jubayr.
