## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 98 to 99 in a single passage. He explains His saying: "So go, for you have in life you may say, 'Do not be touched, and you have an appointment you will not break. And look to your God whom you have been devoted to, to burn him and then blow him into the sea as a blast.'" Moses said to the Samaritan: "So go, for in the days of your life you may say: 'No touch,' meaning neither yesterday nor yesterday..." He mentioned that Moses commanded the Children of Israel not to eat with him, not to mix with him, and not to pledge allegiance to him. Therefore, he said to him: You have in life the right to say 'no touch.' This remained in what was mentioned about his tribe, as in Qatadah, who said: By God, the Samaritan was great among the great Israelites, from a tribe called Samaritan, but the enemy of God was hypocritical after he crossed the sea with the Children of Israel. His saying: 'So go, for in life you have the right to say 'no touch.' Their remnants today say 'no touch.' In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas and Qatadah.
