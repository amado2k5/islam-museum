## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari takes God's words in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]) on divorced women to mean those divorced after their husbands have consummated the marriage with them, if they have menstrual cycles and times of purity, that they wait for themselves, refraining from marrying again, three quru'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499} The interpreters differed over the meaning of the word quru' that God meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499}

The interpreters differed over the meaning of the word quru' in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]). Some said it is menstruation. From Mujahid (4666); al-Rabi', she observes three menses (4667); Qatada, God set the waiting period of divorced women at three menses, and then from them were excepted the woman divorced before consummation, those past the age of menstruation, those who have not yet menstruated and the pregnant woman (4668); al-Dahhak, quru' are menses (4669, 4673); Ibn 'Abbas, three menses (4670); 'Amr ibn Dinar, the aqra' are menses, from the Companions of the Prophet (4671); 'Ikrima, the aqra' are menses and not purity, arguing that the wording of [[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]] refers to the waiting period and not to quru' ([[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]]) (4672); and al-Suddi (4674). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 501} The editor explains the words for menses and a single menstruation, and notes that the chain of 4669 has a name that is probably Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-A'la. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 500}

Al-Tabari reports that these held that her husband has more right to her until she bathes after the third menses. Ibrahim al-Nakha'i reported that the matter was taken to 'Umar, who said to Ibn Mas'ud, you must give a view; he said her husband has more right to her until she bathes from the third menses; 'Umar said, that is my view, you agree with what is in my mind, and ruled so (4675, 4676, 4677). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 501} From al-Hasan, a man divorced his wife and appointed a man of his family to handle it, who neglected it until she entered the third menses and brought her water to bathe; the husband said, I have taken you back, she said, no, by God, and they went to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, who made her swear by God that she had bathed when he called her; she said no, I had only brought my water to bathe, so he returned her to her husband and said, you have more right to her until she bathes from the third menses (4678, 4679); and from al-Hasan, from 'Umar likewise (4680). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 502} From Yunus ibn Jubayr, 'Umar divorced his wife and she wanted to bathe, and he said, my wife, by the Lord of the Ka'ba, and took her back (4681); Ibn Bashshar notes that Ibn Mahdi said Abu Hilal cannot bear this report, and the editor gives his biography and Ahmad's judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 502} From 'Alqama, a woman came to 'Umar and said her husband had divorced her once or twice and came when she had put down her water, locked her door and taken off her clothes; he asked 'Abdallah, who said I see her as his wife until prayer becomes lawful for her, and 'Umar said, I see that too (4682). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 502} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 503} From al-Aswad (4683, 4684) and Ibrahim (4685, 4686): when a man divorces his wife once or twice, he has more right to take her back, and they inherit from each other, until she bathes from the third menses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 503} From al-Hasan, a man divorced his wife and appointed a relative to handle it, who neglected it until she entered her place for bathing; they disagreed and went to al-Ash'ari, who asked her to swear, and she refused, so he returned her to him (4687). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 503} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} From al-Nakha'i, 'Umar consulted Ibn Mas'ud about a man who divorced his wife once or twice and she then had her third menses, and he said I see him as having more right to her until she bathes (4688), and from 'Ali likewise (4689). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr, when the blood stops there is no taking back (4690), and from Ibrahim, if he divorced her while she was pure she observes three menses besides the one she became pure from (4691). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} From 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb, 'Umar asked Abu Musa about it and he said, I ruled that her husband has more right to her until she bathes (4692); from 'Ali (4693); from Abu 'Ubayda ibn 'Abdallah, 'Uthman sent to his father, Ibn Mas'ud, who said I see him as having more right until she bathes from the third menses and prayer becomes lawful to her, and 'Uthman adopted that (4694); from Abu Qilaba and Qatada (4695); and from Ma'bad al-Juhani, when the divorced woman has washed from the third menses she is separate from him and free to marry (4696). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 505} The editor gives biographies of narrators including al-Nu'man ibn Rashid, Zayd ibn Rafi' and Ma'bad al-Juhani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 505}

To close the reports of those who said menstruation: Ibrahim reported from 'Umar that the husband may take her back until she bathes from the third menses, and fasting becomes lawful for her (4697); Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab reported from 'Ali that he has more right to her until she bathes from the third menses (4698, 4699). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 506} The editor gives the biography of Durust, the narrator from al-Zuhri, and corrects his name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 506}

Others said that the quru' by which God commanded divorced women to wait is purity (tuhr). This is reported from 'A'isha: the aqra' are the periods of purity (4700, 4701, 4706); and from 'Amra that 'A'isha used to say the qur' is purity and not the menstruation, and that when the divorced woman enters the third menses she is separate from her husband and free to marry (4702). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 506} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 507} From Abu Bakr ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Harith ibn Hisham, like the view of Zayd and 'A'isha (4703), and from Nafi' from Ibn 'Umar, like the view of Zayd (4704). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 507} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab and Sulayman ibn Yasar, Zayd ibn Thabit said: when the divorced woman enters the third menses she is separate from her husband and free to marry; Ma'mar added that al-Zuhri gave legal opinions by Zayd's view (4705). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 507} From Zayd: when she enters the third menses he has no right to take her back (4707, 4708); Ibn Abi 'Adi added from 'Ali that he has more right to her until she bathes (4708, 4709); and from Zayd, she has no inheritance (4710). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 507} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 508}

Among their reports is that al-Ahwas, a man of the notables of Syria, divorced his wife once or twice and died while she was in the third menses. The case went to Mu'awiya, who had no knowledge of it; he asked Fadala ibn 'Ubayd and the Companions of the Prophet who were there, and they had none either, so he sent a rider to Zayd ibn Thabit, who said she does not inherit from him, and had she died he would not have inherited from her; Ibn 'Umar held the same (4711, 4712, 4713). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 508} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 509} The editor gives the biography of al-Ahwas and the collections that record the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 508} From Ibn 'Umar: when she enters the third menses he has no right to take her back (4714), and she is separate (4715); from Ibn 'Umar and Zayd: she does not inherit from him nor he from her, she is free of him and he of her (4716). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 509} From Yahya ibn Sa'id, a report reaching him from Zayd (4717); from Salim ibn 'Abdallah, like Zayd's view (4718); and from Aban ibn 'Uthman (4719). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 509} From Zayd (4720, 4721, 4722), that when she sees blood in the third menses her waiting period has ended (4723); from 'Umar ibn Thabit al-Ansari from Zayd (4724); and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab that 'A'isha and Zayd both said the same (4725). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 510} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 511} In the notes the editor corrects narrators' names and a slip by a copyist in 4720, stating what the manuscript has. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 509} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 510}

Al-Tabari then turns to the meaning of the word in Arab usage. He says quru' is the plural of qur', which the Arabs also pluralise as aqra'; one says a woman "aqra'at" when she has become one who has menses and purity. The root sense of qur' in their speech is the time for the coming of something whose coming is customary at a known time, and likewise its going at a known time. So they say "a need of so-and-so has qara'at with me", meaning its fulfilment is near; a star "aqra'a" when the time of its setting, or rising, arrives; and the wind "aqra'at" when it blows at its time. He cites a line of poetry whose source the editor could not find, and a line by the Hudhali Malik ibn al-Harith about the wind. He concludes that some Arabs called the time of the coming of menses a qur', since it is blood whose appearance is customary at one time and hiding at another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 511}

To show that the Arabs call the time of menses a qur', al-Tabari cites the report that the Prophet told Fatima bint Abi Hubaysh to leave prayer during the days of her aqra', meaning the days when her menses comes on (4726); the editor gives the sources of the hadith in Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i and the comments of Ibn Kathir and Ibn Abi Hatim on its narrator al-Mundhir ibn al-Mughira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 512} He adds that other Arabs called the time of purity a qur', since it is the time when the menstrual blood goes away, and cites verses by al-A'sha Maymun ibn Qays praising Hawdha ibn 'Ali al-Hanafi, which say that he neglected his wives' periods of purity because of his campaigns, so using qur' for the time of purity; the editor explains the words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 512} Because of this shared sense, he says, the interpretation of the verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]) was difficult for the interpreters: some held that what the divorced woman is commanded to count is the qur' of menses, so she must wait three menses; others that it is the qur' of purity, so she must wait three periods of purity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 512} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 513}

Then he gives his own choice. Since God commanded the man who wishes to divorce his wife to divorce her only while she is pure and has not been touched, and forbade divorce during menses, the divorced woman whose marriage was consummated must count three quru', each lying between two periods of purity and differing from what she counted before; when they pass she is free to marry. The third qur' is for him the third purity, and her waiting period ends with its end and the coming of the menses that follows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 513} In the notes the editor explains that al-Tabari treats the word qur' as having a shared sense covering the time of purity and the time of menses, and corrects two readings in the manuscript and the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 513}

Al-Tabari answers anyone who thinks that, because both the time of purity and the time of menses are called qur', the waiting period should end with the second purity (the purity of divorce, the menses after it and the next purity all being quru'); he calls this ignorance. His rule is that whatever God sent down is to be taken as the apparent wording allows, unless God makes clear that He means a restriction, and the people of Islam agree that the three required quru' are separated by times of a different kind. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 514}

He also sees in the verse evidence against those who said that a woman from whom the husband took an oath of abstention becomes free to marry at the end of the four months if she has had three menses within them, because God made the waiting period obligatory after the husband resolves on divorce and it takes place; she was not divorced on the day of the oath, since all agree that the oath of abstention is not a divorce that requires a waiting period ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]; see [[passage/quran-2-227|2:227]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 514} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 515}

Al-Tabari then explains the word for divorced women: those whose way has been left free, not held by husbands nor engaged; it is the passive form from the man divorcing his wife. He notes that "she is talaq" derives from the woman being freed, and that some Arab groups say it of the woman as they say it of a ewe left without a shepherd who goes out alone to graze. The passive form used of a woman in labour comes from the pain of childbirth, a different root. Waiting (tarabbus) means holding back from marriage and restraining oneself from it, as he explained elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 515}

He then takes up the last part of the verse, what the verse says is created in their wombs ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]), on which the interpreters differed. Some said it is menstruation, and that divorced women may not hide it from their husbands in order to cancel the husbands' right to take them back. Ibn Shihab reported that it had reached them that it is pregnancy and that it is menses, and that they may not hide it so that the waiting period ends and the husband loses the right of return (4727). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 516} From Ibrahim al-Nakha'i: menses (4728, 4730), and the main thing meant is menses (4729, 4733); from 'Ikrima: menses, after which Khalid al-Hadhdha' said, blood (4731). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 516} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 517} Others said it is menses, but what is forbidden to her to conceal is that she tells her husband, when he wants to take her back before the third menses, that she has had the third menses, falsely, to cancel his right; this is reported from Ibrahim (4732). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 517} In the note on 'Ubayda ibn Mu'attib, the editor says he was blind, of poor memory and abandoned as a transmitter, quoting Ibn Hibban. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 517}

Others said that what the divorced woman is forbidden to hide from her husband is both pregnancy and menses ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]). From Ibn 'Umar: if she is menstruating she may not hide her menses, and if pregnant she may not hide her pregnancy (4734). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 518} From Mujahid: pregnancy and menses (4735, 4736), menses and the child (4737, 4738), and that a divorced woman may not say she is menstruating when she is not, nor say she is pregnant when she is not, nor deny pregnancy when she is pregnant (4739, 4740, 4741, 4742); in Layth's version he added that all this concerns the woman's dislike or love of her husband (4743). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 518} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 519} From al-Rabi' the same (4744); from Ibn Zayd, she must not hide menses or the child, nor hide it while he does not know when she is lawful, so as to keep him from taking her back and harm him (4745); and from al-Dahhak, menses and the child are what women were entrusted with (4746). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 519} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 520} The editor gives the biography of al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 519}

Others said that only pregnancy is meant, and then differed over why she is forbidden to hide it. Some said so that the husband's right to take her back before she gives birth is not cancelled. 'Ali ibn Rabah reported that 'Umar told a man to recite the verse, and then said that so-and-so was among those who hide what is in their wombs: she had been divorced while pregnant and hid it until she gave birth (4747). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 520} From Ibn 'Abbas: when a man divorces his wife once or twice while she is pregnant, he has more right to take her back until she gives birth, and that is what the verse means (4748). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 521} From 'Ikrima, it concerns the woman divorced once or twice who then hides her pregnancy to escape her husband; for the woman divorced three times he has no right to her until she marries another husband (4749). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 521} The editor gives biographies of Qabath ibn Razin and 'Ali ibn Rabah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 520}

Others said that in the Jahiliyya women hid it from their husbands for fear they would take them back, until they married others and the child's lineage was attached to the new husband; God forbade this. From Qatada: a divorced woman used to hide what was in her womb so as to carry the child off to someone other than its father (4750); God knew some of them hide it, and forbade it (4751); and she hid her pregnancy to ascribe it to another man (4752). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 521} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 522} The editor gives the biographies of Muhammad ibn Yahya al-Qutaʻi and 'Abd al-A'la ibn 'Abd al-A'la. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 522}

Others said that the cause was that a man who wanted to divorce his wife would ask whether she was pregnant, so as not to divorce her while she carried his child, because of the harm to him and his child in separation; so women were told to be truthful and forbidden to lie. From al-Suddi, he asks her and she hides it wanting to leave him, so he divorces her; when it is known she is returned to him as a punishment for hiding it, and her husband has more right to take her back, humbled (4753). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 522} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 523}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice. He says the best of these views for the meaning of the verse is the view that what a woman divorced once or twice is forbidden to hide from her husband, of what is in her womb, is both menses and pregnancy. For there is no disagreement that the waiting period ends with the birth of the child as it ends with blood seen after the third purity, for those who say the qur' is purity, or, for those who say it is menses, when it stops from the third menses and she purifies herself by bathing. He builds on this that God forbade the concealment only because it would cancel the husband's right after divorce until the waiting period ends, a right that is cancelled by giving birth for pregnant women and by the end of the three quru' for the others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 523}

Al-Tabari completes his argument. In his view women are forbidden to hide from their divorcing husbands each of menses and pregnancy just as they are forbidden the other, and there is no sense in the restriction of those who limited the verse to one of them, since both are among what is in the wombs and in each the husband's right is cancelled when it reaches its end; anyone who limits it to one is asked for proof of the claim, and the same argument then turns back on him, so that whatever he says of one he must say of the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 524} He rejects al-Suddi's view that the prohibition concerns hiding pregnancy when the husband wants to divorce, finding it contrary to the apparent wording: the prohibition comes after the description of women separating from their husbands by divorce and being told what waiting is required of them, so it belongs to what precedes it and not to something not mentioned before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 524} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 525} The editor corrects a reading in the printed text and explains its grammar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 524}

He then answers the question of why the verse ties this to belief in God and the Last Day, asking whether it would be lawful for them to hide it if they did not believe. He says the meaning is not that the prohibition is restricted to believing women. A divorced woman's hiding from her husband the menses and child in her womb during the waiting period, to harm him, is not the act or character of one who believes in God and the Last Day but of unbelieving women, so believers should not take on their character; and the duty lies on every woman bound by God's obligations who has menstrual periods and is divorced after consummation not to hide from her husband the menses and pregnancy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 525}

On the next part of the verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]), concerning their husbands, al-Tabari says the word ba'ula is the plural of ba'l, a woman's husband, and cites a line by Jarir, for which the editor gives the occasion, the exchange of satirical poems between Jarir and al-Farazdaq before al-Hajjaj. Ba'l can be pluralised ba'ula and bu'ul, as fahl takes fuhul and fuhula and dhakar takes dhukur and dhukura; the Arabs often add the ending to plurals of the pattern fu'ul, rarely to those of the pattern fi'al, though 'izam and 'izama are reported, from a rajaz verse whose poet the editor could not identify and which he traces to al-Jamhara and Lisan al-'Arab; and then hijara and hijar, mihara and mihar, dhikara and dhikar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 526} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 527}

He gives the meaning: the husbands of the divorced women, whom God required to wait three quru' and forbade to hide what is in their wombs, have more right and are better placed to take them back to themselves during their waiting, through the three quru' and the days of pregnancy, than the women have to refuse them that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 527} From Ibn 'Abbas: when a man divorces his wife once or twice while she is pregnant, he has more right to take her back until she gives birth (4754); from Ibrahim, during the waiting period (4755). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 527} From 'Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, a man who divorced his wife used to have more right to take her back even if he divorced her three times, and this was abrogated by the ruling in [[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]] (4756). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 527} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 528} From Mujahid, during their waiting period (4757, 4758, 4759); from Qatada, in the three menses, or three months, or if she is pregnant, so that if her husband divorced her once or twice he may take her back if he wishes while she is in her waiting period (4760), and that the woman used to hide her pregnancy to ascribe it to another man, so God forbade it, and he has more right to take them back during the waiting period (4761). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 528} From al-Rabi', during the waiting period as long as he has not divorced her three times (4762); from al-Suddi, more right to take her back, humbled, as a punishment for hiding the pregnancy (4763); from Ibn Zayd, as long as the waiting period has not ended (4764); and from al-Dahhak, as long as she is in the waiting period, if he wants to take her back (4765). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 529} The editor corrects a narrator's name in 4757 and refers to 4739. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 528}

Al-Tabari then puts a question: does the husband who has divorced once or twice after consummation have the right to take her back in her three quru' only if he intends by it to set right her affairs and his? He answers that between him and God it is not permitted if he intends harm by taking her back and not setting things right. In legal ruling, however, it is decided for him that he may take her back, as it is decided that his right is cancelled if she hid her pregnancy or menses until the waiting period ended, to harm him, although God forbade the hiding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 529}

Al-Tabari continues. So the man who takes back his wife, divorced once or twice after consummation, both of them free persons, is decided in his favour to have the right of return even if he intended harm by it, though he is sinful for the show in his act and is going ahead with something God has not permitted; God will repay him for it. Other people may not come between him and his wife whom he has taken back; and if after taking her back he tries to harm her without right, he is held to the rights that God made binding on husbands toward wives, until the harm he intended falls back on him and not on her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 529} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 530} The editor records the difference in the manuscript and the printed text over a word for "show" and explains the sentence structure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 530}

Al-Tabari sees in this part of ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]) clear evidence for the view that the man who swore abstention and then resolved on divorce and divorced his wife has the right to take her back in that divorce, and against the view that the passing of the four months is itself the resolve on divorce and an irrevocable divorce. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 530}

He then turns to the clause that women have rights like those over them, in what is right ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]), on which the interpreters differed. Some said: they have from their husbands good companionship and treatment in what is right, like the obedience they owe their husbands in what God made binding for the husband. From al-Dahhak, if they obey God and their husbands, he must treat her well, spare her harm and spend on her from his means (4766); from Ibn Zayd, they fear God regarding them as they must fear God regarding the husbands (4767). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 531} Others said: they have from their husbands the same adornment and willing compliance that they owe, and Ibn 'Abbas said he liked to adorn himself for his wife as he liked her to adorn herself for him (4768). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 531} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 532} The editor explains the words for adornment and compliance, cites verses of 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz on a person making himself what he is, and gives the biography of Bashir ibn Sulayman. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 531} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 532}

Al-Tabari gives his choice. The best view in his opinion is that divorced women, divorced once or twice after consummation, have a right over their husbands that they not take them back in the three quru' unless they intend to set right their affairs and the husbands', and not take them back to harm them, just as they owe their husbands not to hide what is in their wombs, child and menstrual blood, to harm them so as to slip away from them. For God forbade divorced women to hide it and made the husbands more entitled to take them back if they wish to set things right, thus forbidding each to harm the other and telling each what is theirs and what is on them; then He added that women have the like of what is on them, making clear that what each owes the other, not to harm them, equals what is owed to them. He holds this reading closest to the apparent sense of the wording; the clause may also include everything each owes the other, so that what al-Dahhak and Ibn 'Abbas said is included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 532} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 533} The editor corrects wordings in the printed text and cites the hadith of Fatima bint Qays for the sense of "slipping away". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 532}

On the clause that men have a degree over women ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]) they differed. Some said the degree is the excellence God gave men in inheritance, jihad and the like: Mujahid (4769, 4770); Qatada (4771). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 533} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 534} Others said it is command and obedience: Zayd ibn Aslam, authority (4772); Ibn Zayd, obedience, wives obey men and men do not obey them (4773); Muhammad ibn Sirin, he knew only that they have the like of what is on them if they recognise that degree (4774). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 534} Others said it is for him because of the dower he paid, and because if she accuses him of adultery she is punished by the fixed penalty while if he accuses her he swears the oath of imprecation (4775). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 534} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 535} Others said it is his giving of his bounty to her, his paying her right and his overlooking part or all of what is due to him from her; Ibn 'Abbas said he did not like to take all his right from her (4776). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 535} Others said it is that God gave him a beard and forbade it to her, from Hamid (4777). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 535} The editor gives biographies of Azhar ibn Sa'd and 'Ubayd ibn al-Sabbah and explains the verb for taking the whole. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 534} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 535}

Al-Tabari then chooses the view of Ibn 'Abbas: the degree mentioned here is the man's overlooking, for his wife, part of what is due from her, his tolerating it, and his paying in full what is due to her. He begins his reasoning from the fact that God mentioned the degree right after ruling that women have the like of what is on them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 535}
