## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari takes God's words in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]) on divorced women to mean those divorced after their husbands have consummated the marriage with them, if they have menstrual cycles and times of purity, that they wait for themselves, refraining from marrying again, three quru'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499} The interpreters differed over the meaning of the word quru' that God meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 499}

The interpreters differed over the meaning of the word quru' in the verse ([[passage/quran-2-228|2:228]]). Some said it is menstruation. From Mujahid (4666); al-Rabi', she observes three menses (4667); Qatada, God set the waiting period of divorced women at three menses, and then from them were excepted the woman divorced before consummation, those past the age of menstruation, those who have not yet menstruated and the pregnant woman (4668); al-Dahhak, quru' are menses (4669, 4673); Ibn 'Abbas, three menses (4670); 'Amr ibn Dinar, the aqra' are menses, from the Companions of the Prophet (4671); 'Ikrima, the aqra' are menses and not purity, arguing that God said "divorce them for their waiting period" and did not say for their quru' ([[passage/quran-65-1|65:1]]) (4672); and al-Suddi (4674). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 501} The editor explains the words for menses and a single menstruation, and notes that the chain of 4669 has a name that is probably Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-A'la. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 500}

Al-Tabari reports that these held that her husband has more right to her until she bathes after the third menses. Ibrahim al-Nakha'i reported that the matter was taken to 'Umar, who said to Ibn Mas'ud, you must give a view; he said her husband has more right to her until she bathes from the third menses; 'Umar said, that is my view, you agree with what is in my mind, and ruled so (4675, 4676, 4677). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 501} From al-Hasan, a man divorced his wife and appointed a man of his family to handle it, who neglected it until she entered the third menses and brought her water to bathe; the husband said, I have taken you back, she said, no, by God, and they went to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari, who made her swear by God that she had bathed when he called her; she said no, I had only brought my water to bathe, so he returned her to her husband and said, you have more right to her until she bathes from the third menses (4678, 4679); and from al-Hasan, from 'Umar likewise (4680). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 502} From Yunus ibn Jubayr, 'Umar divorced his wife and she wanted to bathe, and he said, my wife, by the Lord of the Ka'ba, and took her back (4681); Ibn Bashshar notes that Ibn Mahdi said Abu Hilal cannot bear this report, and the editor gives his biography and Ahmad's judgement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 502} From 'Alqama, a woman came to 'Umar and said her husband had divorced her once or twice and came when she had put down her water, locked her door and taken off her clothes; he asked 'Abdallah, who said I see her as his wife until prayer becomes lawful for her, and 'Umar said, I see that too (4682). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 502} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 503} From al-Aswad (4683, 4684) and Ibrahim (4685, 4686): when a man divorces his wife once or twice, he has more right to take her back, and they inherit from each other, until she bathes from the third menses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 503} From al-Hasan, a man divorced his wife and appointed a relative to handle it, who neglected it until she entered her place for bathing; they disagreed and went to al-Ash'ari, who asked her to swear, and she refused, so he returned her to him (4687). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 503} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} From al-Nakha'i, 'Umar consulted Ibn Mas'ud about a man who divorced his wife once or twice and she then had her third menses, and he said I see him as having more right to her until she bathes (4688), and from 'Ali likewise (4689). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr, when the blood stops there is no taking back (4690), and from Ibrahim, if he divorced her while she was pure she observes three menses besides the one she became pure from (4691). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} From 'Amr ibn Shu'ayb, 'Umar asked Abu Musa about it and he said, I ruled that her husband has more right to her until she bathes (4692); from 'Ali (4693); from Abu 'Ubayda ibn 'Abdallah, 'Uthman sent to his father, Ibn Mas'ud, who said I see him as having more right until she bathes from the third menses and prayer becomes lawful to her, and 'Uthman adopted that (4694); from Abu Qilaba and Qatada (4695); and from Ma'bad al-Juhani, when the divorced woman has washed from the third menses she is separate from him and free to marry (4696). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 505} The editor gives biographies of narrators including al-Nu'man ibn Rashid, Zayd ibn Rafi' and Ma'bad al-Juhani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 504} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 505}
