## Summary
Verse 7 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 7 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 398-408)**

Al-Tabari says: remember, people, "when God promised you one of the two parties", meaning one of the two groups, the group of Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and the caravan, and the group of the polytheists who rushed out of Mecca to protect their caravan, "that it would be yours", meaning that what they have is booty for you, "and you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours", meaning you wished that the party that had no thorn, that is no edge and no fighting in it, would be yours; you wished the caravan in which there was no fighting to be yours, rather than the host of Quraysh who came to protect their caravan, in meeting whom is fighting and war. The root of "shawka" is from "shawk", thorns. The editor explains "hadd" as sharpness, great might and injury, and refers to the earlier explanation of "party". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 398}

The interpreters said the like, and report 15719, from Urwa: Abu Sufyan came with the riders of Quraysh from Syria and took the coast road; when the Prophet heard of them he called his companions and told them of the wealth they carried and the fewness of their number; they went out wanting only Abu Sufyan and the caravan with him, thinking it only booty for them, not thinking there would be much fighting when they saw them, and that is what God sent down about, "and you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours". The editor identifies Ali ibn Nasr al-Jahdami and Abd al-Warith ibn Abd al-Samad al-Anbari as trustworthy and Aban ibn Yazid al-Attar as trustworthy, and says Abu Ja'far gave it at length in the History, being a letter from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr to Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. Report 15720, from Ibn Ishaq from al-Zuhri, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr and Yazid ibn Ruman from Urwa and others of our scholars from Ibn Abbas, each having told me part of this account, so that their reports joined in what I have given of the account of Badr: when the Messenger of God heard of Abu Sufyan coming from Syria he called the Muslims to them and said this is the caravan of Quraysh with their wealth in it, so go out to it, perhaps God will give it to you as a bonus; the people responded, some light and some heavy, because they did not think the Messenger of God would meet war; and Abu Sufyan, when he drew near the Hijaz, was feeling for news and asking the riders he met, fearing for… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 398} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 399}

…over people's wealth, until he got word from some of the riders that Muhammad had called his companions out against you and your caravan; he took care at that, hired Damdam ibn Amr al-Ghifari and sent him to Mecca, telling him to go to Quraysh to summon them to their wealth and tell them that Muhammad had set out against it with his companions; Damdam went out in haste to Mecca. The Messenger of God went out with his companions until he reached a valley called Dhafiran, went through it and, when he was in part of it, halted, and news reached him of Quraysh's march to protect their caravan; he consulted the people and told them about Quraysh. Abu Bakr stood and spoke well, then Umar stood and spoke well, then al-Miqdad ibn Amr stood and said: Messenger of God, go where God commanded you and we are with you; by God we shall not say as the Children of Israel said to Moses, [[passage/quran-5-24|5:24]], but go, you and your Lord, and fight, and we will fight with you both; by Him who sent you with the truth, if you march with us to Birk al-Ghimad we will fight beside you against any who stand in the way until you reach it. The Messenger of God spoke well to him and prayed good for him. Then he said, advise me, people… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 399} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 400}
…and he meant only the Ansar, since they were the greater number of the people, and when they pledged to him at al-Aqaba they had said, Messenger of God, we are free of your protection until you reach our abode, and once you reach us you are under our protection and we shall protect you as we protect our sons and women; so the Messenger of God feared that the Ansar might not hold it their duty to help him except against an enemy who came on him in Medina, and that they were not obliged to march with him against an enemy outside their land. When he said that, Sa'd ibn Mu'adh said to him: it is as though you mean us, Messenger of God? He said yes. He said: we have believed in you and trusted you and testified that what you brought is the truth, and we gave you on that our covenants and pledges to hear and obey, so go on, Messenger of God, as you wish; by Him who sent you with the truth, if you took us to this sea and plunged in, we would plunge with you, not one of us would stay behind, and we do not mind your meeting our enemy tomorrow with us; we are steadfast in war, true in meeting; perhaps God will show you of us what delights your eye, so march with us in God's blessing. The Messenger of God was pleased with Sa'd's words and they heartened him. Then he said, march in God's blessing and rejoice, for God has promised me one of the two parties; by God, it is as though I see now the places where the people will fall tomorrow. The editor explains "dhamam" as covenant, surety and inviolability, "dahamahu" as coming upon him unprepared, and "ista'rada al-bahr" as facing it without regard for its danger, and notes that the account is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and al-Tabari's History, divided, and that al-Hamdani says Birk al-Ghimad is at the far end of Yemen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 400} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 401}
Report 15721, from al-Suddi: Abu Sufyan came with a caravan from Syria carrying the trade of Quraysh, the perfume-laden caravan; the Messenger of God heard it was coming and called the people out, and some three hundred and ten-odd men went with him. He sent a scout of his from Juhayna, an ally of the Ansar, called Ibn Ariqt, who brought him news of the people. Abu Sufyan heard of Muhammad's going out and sent to the people of Mecca asking for help, sending a man of the Banu Ghifar called Damdam ibn Amr; the Prophet went out unaware of Quraysh's going out, and God informed him of it, and he feared that the Ansar might let him down and say we pledged to protect you if anyone came at you in our city. He turned to his companions and consulted them about pursuing the caravan; Abu Bakr said I have taken this road and know it, and the man left them in such and such a place; the Prophet was silent, then consulted them again and they kept advising him to take the caravan. When he had consulted much, Sa'd ibn Mu'adh spoke and said: Messenger of God, I see you consult your companions and they advise you and you consult them again, as if you do not accept what they advise and fear the Ansar will hold back from you; you are the Messenger of God, the Book was sent down on you, God has commanded you to fight and promised you help, and God does not break His promise; go on to what you are commanded, by Him who sent you with the truth not one of the Ansar will stay behind. Then al-Miqdad ibn al-Aswad al-Kindi stood and said: Messenger of God, we do not say to you as the Children of Israel said to Moses, [[passage/quran-5-24|5:24]], but we say, advance and fight, we are with you fighting; the Messenger of God rejoiced at that and said, my Lord has promised me the people and they have come out, so march to them; and they marched. The editor explains "latima" as perfume, and the caravan that carries perfume and the merchants' finest cloth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 402} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 403}
Report 15722, from Qatada: the two parties, one of them Abu Sufyan ibn Harb as he came with the caravan from Syria, and the other party Abu Jahl with a group of Quraysh; the Muslims disliked the thorn and the fighting and wished to meet the caravan, and God willed what He willed. Report 15723, from Ibn Abbas: the caravan of the people of Mecca came from Syria, the people of Medina heard and went out with the Messenger of God wanting the caravan; the people of Mecca heard and hurried toward it so that the Prophet and his companions would not take it; the caravan passed ahead of the Messenger of God, God having promised them one of the two parties, and they preferred to meet the caravan, with less thorn and a nearer prize; when the caravan got ahead and slipped past the Messenger of God, he marched with the Muslims toward the people, and the people disliked marching for the thorn in the people. Report 15724, from Ibn Abbas: they wanted the caravan; the Messenger of God entered Medina in Rabi al-Awwal and Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri raided the herds of Medina as far as al-Safra; the Prophet heard and rode after him but Kurz was ahead of him; the Prophet returned and stayed his year; then Abu Sufyan came from Syria with a caravan of Quraysh and when he was near Badr Gabriel came down to the Prophet and revealed the verse [[passage/quran-8-7|8:7]]; the Prophet marched out with all the Muslims, three hundred and thirteen men, of whom two hundred and seventy were Ansar and the rest Emigrants; Abu Sufyan heard the news and sent to all of Quraysh in Mecca, and Quraysh marched out in anger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 403} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 404}
In the notes the editor says "sarh" is the livestock sent to pasture and that al-Safra is a village above Yanbu rich in fields and palms, six stages from Medina; that a place-name in the manuscript and printed edition which he could not find is, he thinks, really "Idam", a valley in the Tihama mountains passing Medina in the land of Juhayna; and that it is known from the biographies that Abu Sufyan halted at a water where Majdi ibn Amr al-Juhani was, and when he sensed news of the Muslims turned his caravan toward the coast and left Badr on the left. Report 15725, from Ibn Jurayj: Gabriel had come down and told him of the march of Quraysh, who were coming for their caravan, and promised him either the caravan or Quraysh, and that was at Badr; they seized the water-carriers and asked them, and they told them; that is His words "and you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours", meaning the people of Mecca. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 404}
Report 15726, from Ibn Zayd: the Prophet went out to Badr, they wanting to intercept a caravan of Quraysh, and Satan came out in the form of Suraqa ibn Ju'shum to the people of Mecca and led them astray, saying Muhammad and his companions have set out against your caravan, and said no one of the people can overcome you today and I am your protector; they went out and called out that none of us should stay behind or we would pull down his house and make it lawful; and the Messenger of God and his companions seized at al-Rawha a scout of the people who told him of them, and the Messenger of God said, God has promised you the caravan or the people; the caravan was dearer to the people than the people, fighting being in the thorn and the caravan having no fighting; he said the thorn is fighting and the one without the thorn is the caravan. Report 15727, from Abu Ayyub: God sent down the verse, and when He promised us one of the two parties as ours our souls were at ease; the two parties were Abu Sufyan's caravan or Quraysh. The editor identifies Ya'qub ibn Muhammad al-Zuhri, Abd Allah ibn Wahb al-Misri the trustworthy, Ibn Lahi'a, Yazid ibn Abi Habib al-Misri, trustworthy, and Abu Imran Aslam ibn Yazid al-Tujibi, a trustworthy Follower, and says al-Haythami gave it at length, saying al-Tabarani gave it and its chain is good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 405}
Report 15728, from Abu Ayyub: they said the thorn is the people and the one without the thorn is the caravan, and when God promised us one of the two parties, either the caravan or the people, our souls were at ease; report 15729, from more than one: the thorn is Quraysh; the report from al-Dahhak: it is the caravan of Abu Sufyan, the companions of the Messenger of God wishing the caravan had been theirs and the fighting turned away from them; report 15730, from Ibn Ishaq: that is, the booty without the war. As for "that it would be yours", it is put in the accusative by the repetition of "promises", since "God promises you" has already acted on "one of the two parties"; so the sense of the speech is: He promises you that one of the two parties is yours, as He says in [[passage/quran-47-18|47:18]]; and He said "you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours", making "dhat" feminine because the party is meant, the sense being: you wished that the party that is without the thorn would be yours rather than the party with the thorn. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 406} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 407}
Then al-Tabari says on "God wills to establish the truth by His words and to cut off the last of the unbelievers": God wills to make Islam true and raise it by His words, meaning by His command to you, believers, to fight the unbelievers while you wanted booty and wealth; and "cut off the last of the unbelievers" means He wills to cut off the root of those who denied God's oneness. He has shown earlier that "dabir" is the last, and that cutting it off is coming upon all of them. Report 15731, from Ibn Zayd: that those whom He willed to cut off be killed, this is better for you than the caravan; report 15732, from Ibn Ishaq: that is, the blow struck at the leaders and chiefs of Quraysh on the day of Badr. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "true", "God's words" and "cutting off the last". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 408}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
