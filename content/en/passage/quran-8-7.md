**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 398-399)**

Al-Tabari says: remember, people, "when God promised you one of the two parties", meaning one of the two groups, the group of Abu Sufyan ibn Harb and the caravan, and the group of the polytheists who rushed out of Mecca to protect their caravan, "that it would be yours", meaning that what they have is booty for you, "and you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours", meaning you wished that the party that had no thorn, that is no edge and no fighting in it, would be yours; you wished the caravan in which there was no fighting to be yours, rather than the host of Quraysh who came to protect their caravan, in meeting whom is fighting and war. The root of "shawka" is from "shawk", thorns. The editor explains "hadd" as sharpness, great might and injury, and refers to the earlier explanation of "party". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 398}

The interpreters said the like, and report 15719, from Urwa: Abu Sufyan came with the riders of Quraysh from Syria and took the coast road; when the Prophet heard of them he called his companions and told them of the wealth they carried and the fewness of their number; they went out wanting only Abu Sufyan and the caravan with him, thinking it only booty for them, not thinking there would be much fighting when they saw them, and that is what God sent down about, "and you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours". The editor identifies Ali ibn Nasr al-Jahdami and Abd al-Warith ibn Abd al-Samad al-Anbari as trustworthy and Aban ibn Yazid al-Attar as trustworthy, and says Abu Ja'far gave it at length in the History, being a letter from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr to Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. Report 15720, from Ibn Ishaq from al-Zuhri, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr and Yazid ibn Ruman from Urwa and others of our scholars from Ibn Abbas, each having told me part of this account, so that their reports joined in what I have given of the account of Badr: when the Messenger of God heard of Abu Sufyan coming from Syria he called the Muslims to them and said this is the caravan of Quraysh with their wealth in it, so go out to it, perhaps God will give it to you as a bonus; the people responded, some light and some heavy, because they did not think the Messenger of God would meet war; and Abu Sufyan, when he drew near the Hijaz, was feeling for news and asking the riders he met, fearing for… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 398} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 399}
