## Summary
Verse 23 of [[passage/quran-14|Surat Ibrahim]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 23 of 52 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verses 23-25 (pp. 566-580)**

On "and those who believed and did righteous deeds will be admitted to gardens beneath which rivers flow, abiding therein by their Lord's leave; their greeting therein is peace. Have you not seen how God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree, its root firm and its branches in the sky, giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave? God strikes parables for people that they may remember", al-Tabari says, mighty be His mention: those who believed God and His Messenger will be admitted, acknowledging God's oneness and the mission of His messengers and that what they brought from God is true, "and did righteous deeds", he says, acted in obedience to God and stopped at God's command and prohibition, "gardens beneath which rivers flow", orchards beneath which rivers flow, "abiding therein", he says, remaining in them for ever, "by their Lord's leave", he says, they entered by God's command to them to enter, "their greeting therein is peace", and that, God willing, is as Ibn Jurayj relates in report 20657, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "their greeting therein is peace", he said, the angels greet them in Paradise. On "have you not seen how God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree" He says, mighty be His mention, to His Prophet Muhammad: have you not seen, Muhammad, with the eye of your heart {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 566}

so as to know how God coined a parable and made a likeness: "a good word", by the good meaning faith in Him, mighty be His praise, "like a good tree", good in fruit, the mention of "fruit" being left out, the hearers' knowledge sparing its mention once "tree" is mentioned. On "its root is firm and its branch in the sky" He says, mighty be His mention: the root of this tree is firm in the earth, "and its branch", its top, "is in the sky", he says, raised high toward the sky. On "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave" he says: it yields what is eaten of its fruit every season by its Lord's command; "and God strikes parables for people", he says, God coins parables for people and makes likenesses for them, "that they may remember", he says, that they may remember God's proof against them, learn from it and be admonished, and so turn from the disbelief they are in to faith. The interpreters differed over what is meant by the good word. Some said: the faith of the believer is meant. Report 20658, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "a good word", the testimony that there is no god but God, "like a good tree", the believer, "its root is firm", he says, "there is no god but God" is firm in the believer's heart, "and its branch is in the sky", he says, the believer's deed is raised up by it to the sky. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 567}

Report 20659, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far and his father: "a good word", he said, this is a parable of faith; faith is the good tree, its firm root, which does not vanish, is sincerity to God, and its branch is in the sky, its branch being fear of God. Report 20660, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "have you not seen how God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree", he said, like a palm tree. Ibn Jurayj said: others said the "good word", its root is firm, being rooted in the heart, "and its branch is in the sky", it ascends and is not veiled until it reaches God. Others said: rather the believer himself is meant. Report 20671, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "have you not seen how God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree", by the good tree he means the believer, and by the root firm in the earth and the branch in the sky that the believer works and speaks on earth, and his deed and word reach the sky while he is on the earth. Report 20672, from Atiyya al-Awfi, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad and Fudayl ibn Marzuq: "God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree", he said, that is the parable of the believer, from whom there keeps coming good speech and righteous deeds that ascend to Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 568}

Report 20673, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Abu Ja'far: "its root is firm in the earth", and he used to read it so; he said, that is the believer whose parable was struck, he said, sincerity to God alone and worship of Him without partner; he said "its root is firm", he said, the root of his deed is firm in the earth, "and its branch is in the sky", he said, his mention is in the sky. They differed over this "tree" made a parable of the good word. Some said: it is the palm. Reports 20674 to 20676, from Anas ibn Malik, through Ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Mu'awiya ibn Qurra; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Abu Qatan; and al-Hasan and Shababa: "like a good tree", he said, it is the palm; the editor notes this is a report of sound chain, a statement of Anas himself. Report 20677, from Shu'ayb ibn al-Hubhab, through Ya'qub, al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Ibn Ulayya: I went out with Abu al-Aliyah wanting to see Anas ibn Malik, and we came to him and he called for a cluster with ripe dates on it and said, eat of this tree of which God, mighty and exalted, said "God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree, its root firm {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 569}

and its branch in the sky". Report 20678, from Anas, through Khallad ibn Aslam, al-Nadr ibn Shumayl, Hammad ibn Salama and Shu'ayb ibn al-Hubhab: the Messenger of God was brought a basket of unripe dates and said "the likeness of a good word is like a good tree", and said, it is the palm. Report 20679, from Anas, through Sawwar ibn Abd Allah, his father, Hammad ibn Salama and Shu'ayb: the Messenger of God was brought a basket with unripe dates in it and said "the likeness of a good word is like a good tree", and said, it is the palm; Shu'ayb said, I told Abu al-Aliyah and he said, so they used to say. Report 20680, from Shu'ayb ibn al-Hubhab, through al-Muthanna, Hajjaj and Hammad: we were with Anas and a tray with ripe dates was brought, and he said, eat, Abu al-Aliyah, for this is from the tree God, mighty and exalted, mentioned in His Book, "God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree, its root firm". Report 20681, from Shu'ayb, through al-Muthanna, al-Hajjaj ibn al-Minhal and Mahdi ibn Maymun: Abu al-Aliyah used to come to me, and one day he came to my house after I had prayed the dawn prayer, and I went with him to Anas ibn Malik; a tray with ripe dates was brought, and Anas said to Abu al-Aliyah, eat, Abu al-Aliyah, for this is from the tree of which God said in His Book "have you not seen how God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree, firm its root"; he said, so Anas read it that day. In a note the editor sets out the routes of this report, raised to the Prophet and not raised, in al-Hakim and al-Tirmidhi, and notes that Hammad ibn Salama alone raised it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 570}

Report 20682, from Ibn Mas'ud, through Abu Kurayb, Talq, Sharik, al-Suddi and Murra: the like; the editor notes he fears a report containing Ibn Mas'ud's words has dropped out before it. Report 20683, from Masruq, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Abd al-Ghaffar ibn al-Qasim, Jami' ibn Abi Rashid and Murra ibn Sharahil al-Hamdani: "like a good tree", he said, the palm. Reports 20684 and 20685, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Harith, al-Hasan and Waraqa; al-Hasan, Shababa and Waraqa; and al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: "like a good tree", he said, like a palm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 571}

Report 20686, from Ibn Mas'ud, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il, al-Suddi and Murra: the like. Report 20687, from Ikrima, through al-Muthanna, Mu'alla ibn Asad, Khalid and Husayn: "like a good tree", he said, it is the palm, in which there is always some benefit. Report 20688, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra' and Juwaybir: "like a good tree", he said, God struck the parable of the believer as the palm, "giving its fruit every season". Report 20689, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "the likeness of a good word is like a good tree", we were told that it is the palm. Report 20690, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they claim it is the palm. Report 20691, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "giving its fruit every season", he said, it is the palm. Report 20692, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ubayd, al-A'mash, al-Minhal ibn Amr and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "and its branch is in the sky", he said, the palm. Report 20693, from Ikrima, through al-Hasan, Sa'id ibn Mansur, Khalid and al-Shaybani: "giving its fruit every season", he said, it is the palm. Report 20694, from Anas, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr, Ma'mar and Shu'ayb ibn al-Hubhab: "the good tree" is the palm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 572}

Others said: it is rather a tree in Paradise. Report 20695, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan, Abu Kudayna, Qabus ibn Abi Zibyan and his father: "God strikes a parable: a good word like a good tree, its root firm and its branch in the sky, giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, it is a tree in Paradise; the editor notes that Qabus is weak with a poor memory. Abu Ja'far says: the better of the two views is that of whoever said it is the "palm", for the soundness of the report from the Messenger of God in what Mujahid relates in report 20696, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and Ibn Abi Najih: I accompanied Ibn Umar to Medina and did not hear him relate from the Messenger of God anything but one hadith; he said, we were with the Prophet and palm-pith was brought, and he said, among trees is a tree whose likeness is that of the Muslim man; I wanted to say, it is the palm, but I was the youngest of the people, so I stayed silent; and the Messenger of God said, it is the palm; the editor notes that what is in brackets is an addition from the Musnad of Ahmad and that al-Bukhari and Muslim related the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 573}

Report 20697, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan, Yazid ibn Harun, Sulayman, Yusuf ibn Sarj and a man: the Messenger of God said, do you know what the good tree is? Ibn Umar said, I wanted to say it is the palm, but Umar's presence held me back; they said, God and His Messenger know best; and the Messenger of God said, it is the palm. Report 20698, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan, Yahya ibn Hammad, Abd al-Aziz and Abd Allah ibn Dinar: the Messenger of God said one day to his Companions, there is a tree among trees whose leaf does not fall, like the believer; the people fell to thinking of the trees of the desert, and it came to my heart that it was the palm, but I was shy, until the Messenger of God said, it is the palm; the editor notes that Ahmad related it in his Musnad and al-Bukhari and Muslim related it by several routes. Report 20699, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan, Asim ibn Ali, Abd al-Aziz ibn Muslim al-Qasmali and Abd Allah ibn Dinar: the Messenger of God said, there is a tree among trees whose leaf does not fall, and it is like {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 574}

the believer; tell me what it is; and he mentioned the like. Report 20700, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan, Ali, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Ubayd Allah and Nafi': the Messenger of God said, tell me of a tree like the Muslim man, giving its fruit every season, its leaves not falling; he said, it came to my mind that it was the palm, but I disliked to speak with Abu Bakr and Umar there, and when they did not speak the Messenger of God said, it is the palm; the editor notes that al-Bukhari and Muslim related it. Report 20701, from Ibn Umar, through al-Hasan, Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah, Isma'il, Ubayd Allah and Nafi': from the Prophet, similarly. The interpreters differed over the meaning of "hin", which God, mighty and exalted, mentioned in this place, when He said "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave". Some said its meaning is: it gives its fruit every morning and evening. Report 20702, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Abu Zibyan: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 575}

"hin", he said, may be morning and evening. Report 20703, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Ubayd, al-A'mash and Abu Zibyan: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, morning and evening. Reports 20704 to 20706, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya, Sufyan, al-A'mash and Abu Zibyan; Ibn al-Muthanna, Ibn Abi Adi, Shu'ba and Sulayman; and Abu Kurayb, Talq and Za'ida: the like. Reports 20707 and 20708, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan, Ali ibn al-Ja'd, Shu'ba, al-A'mash and Abu Zibyan; and Ahmad, Abu Ahmad and Sharik: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, early morning and evening. Report 20709, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, he remembers God every hour of the night and day. Report 20710, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan, Affan, Abu Kudayna, Qabus and his father: he said, morning and evening. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 576}

Report 20711, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Maghra' and Juwaybir: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, the believer obeys God by night and day and at every season. Report 20712, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far and his father: the ascent of his deed at the beginning of the day and its end. Report 20713, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Abu Ja'far: his deed ascends morning and evening. Report 20714, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, it brings out its fruit every season, and this is the parable of the believer who acts at every season, every hour of the day and every hour of the night, in winter and summer, in obedience to God. Others said the meaning is that it gives its fruit every six months, from its harvest to its bearing. Report 20715, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Yahya, Sufyan, Tariq ibn Abd al-Rahman and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "hin" is six months. Report 20716, from Ikrima, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Ayyub: I was asked about a man who swore not to do such and such until a "hin", and I said, there is a "hin" that is reached and a "hin" that is not reached; the unreached "hin" is His saying "you will know its news after a time" [[passage/quran-38-88|38:88]], and the "hin" that is reached is {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 577}

"giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave"; he said, that is from the time the palm is harvested until it comes into flower, which is six months. Report 20717, from Ikrima, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Sufyan and Ibn al-Asbahani: "hin" is six months. Report 20718, from Ikrima, through al-Hasan, Sa'id ibn Mansur, Khalid and al-Shaybani: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, it is the palm, and "hin" is six months. Report 20719, from Ikrima, through Abu Kurayb, Kathir ibn Hisham and Ja'far: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", he said, it is what lies between the palm's bearing and its being cut. Report 20720, from Ikrima, through al-Muthanna, Qabisa and Sufyan: "hin" is six months. Report 20721, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Qays, Tariq ibn Abd al-Rahman and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he was asked about a man who swore not to speak to his brother for a "hin", and said, a "hin" is six months; then he mentioned the palm, six months between its bearing and its harvest. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 578}

Report 20722, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Sufyan and Tariq: "giving its fruit every season", he said, six months. Report 20723, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave", and the "hin" is between seven and six, and it is eaten winter and summer. Report 20724, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: between six months and seven, meaning the "hin". Report 20725, from Ikrima, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan and Abd al-Rahman ibn al-Asbahani: the "hin" is six months. Others said the "hin" here is a year. Report 20726, from Ikrima, through Abu Kurayb, Waki' and Abu Makin: he vowed to cut off his servant's hand or imprison him for a "hin"; Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz asked me, and I said, his hand is not cut off, he is imprisoned for a year, and a "hin" is a year; then he recited "they will imprison him for a time" [[passage/quran-12-35|12:35]] and recited "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave"; and Abu Bakr al-Hudhali added from Ikrima that Ibn Abbas said, "hin" is of two kinds, a "hin" that is known and a "hin" that is not known; as for the "hin" that is not known, "you will know its news after a time" [[passage/quran-38-88|38:88]], and as for the "hin" that is known, it is His saying "giving its fruit every season by its Lord's leave". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 579}

Report 20727, from Shu'ba, through Ibn al-Muthanna and Muhammad ibn Ja'far: I asked Hammad and al-Hakam about a man who swore not to speak to a man for a "hin", and he said, a "hin" is a year. Report 20728, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Harith, al-Hasan and Waraqa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; and al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: "every hin", he said, every year; the editor notes that the manuscript has at this point a closing, as if it were the end of an earlier division from which the manuscript was copied, in which God is praised and a prayer of mercy is made for the Prophet, his family and Companions, and then the next part begins with the basmala. Report 20729, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "giving its fruit every season", he said, every year. Report 20730, from Ata' ibn al-Sa'ib, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sallam and a man of Thaqif: he asked Ibn Abbas and said, I swore that {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 580}

We stop here at the foot of p. 580, in the middle of the commentary on verses 23-25 (at the start of report 20730, on the meaning of "hin"); the next batch begins at p. 581 (marker 589). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 580}
