## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "And establish prayer and give zakat and bow with those who bow" al-Tabari relates that the rabbis of the Jews and the hypocrites used to tell people to establish prayer and give zakat without doing so themselves, so God told them to pray with the Muslims who affirmed Muhammad, give the zakat of their wealth with them, and submit to God and His messenger. He reports from Qatada that they are two binding obligations, so pay them to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 572} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 573}
He explains giving zakat as paying the obligatory charity. The root of zakat is growth and increase of wealth, as one says a crop "zaka" when what God brings out of it is abundant, and he cites verses. It was called zakat because God increases what remains with the owner of the wealth by his giving it out. He gives another possible reason, that it purifies what remains of a person's wealth and frees it of any wrong done to those entitled to a share, as in "a pure soul", meaning innocent and clean; he says this second sense is the one he prefers for the zakat of wealth, though the first is acceptable. Giving it means giving it to those entitled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 573} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 574}
Bowing, for him, is submission to God in obedience: one says "raka'a" for something when one submits to it, and he cites a line of verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 574} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 575}
Al-Tabari closes by saying that this is God's command to the rabbis and hypocrites of the Children of Israel to turn and repent, establish prayer, give zakat, join the Muslims in Islam and submit to Him in obedience, and a prohibition on concealing what they knew of Muhammad's prophethood, after the proofs had been made plain to them, warning given and His favours to them and their forebears recalled. This ends the first volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 575}
