## Summary
Verse 158 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 158 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 158: the return to God and the entering lam (pp. 339-340).** Abu Ja'far: if you die or are killed, believers, your return and gathering are to God and He will repay you for your deeds, so prefer what brings you near God, wins His pleasure and brings you near Paradise, namely fighting in His way and working in obedience to Him, over inclining to this world and its perishing goods, and over leaving obedience and fighting, which distances you from your Lord and brings you near the Fire. Ibn Ishaq (8118): whichever it is, the return is to God, so let this world's life not deceive you, and let fighting and what God urged on you in it be preferred by you to it (from Ibn Hisham 3: 123, the continuation of 8117). He explains that the lam entered this clause because it entered the conditional particle; had it been delayed to the verb it would have brought the heavy nun, as one says 'if you do well to me I shall surely do well to you' with a doubled nun; but since the lam was separated from the verb by the 'sifa' (the prepositional phrase), it entered the sifa and the verb kept free of the heavy nun, as in the same sentence with the object placed first and no doubled nun. The editor's footnote on p. 339 gives the variants of the printed text and the manuscript, explains 'sifa' as the preposition and refers to 1: 299 and the indexes of terms in earlier volumes (the note is cut at the foot of p. 339 and finished at the top of p. 340). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 339} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 340}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 340}
