## Summary
Verse 188 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 188 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 188 (p. 465): who is meant.** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed about those the verse addresses. It describes people who rejoice in what they have done and want to be praised for what did not come from them, and it threatens them with punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 465}

**First view: the hypocrites who stayed behind from campaigns (p. 465, reports 8335-8336).** Some said it means people of hypocrisy who stayed behind from the Prophet when he went on a campaign, and when he returned made excuses to him and wanted to be praised for what they had not done. Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (8335), through Muhammad ibn Sahl ibn 'Askar and Ibn 'Abd al-Rahim al-Barqi from Ibn Abi Maryam from Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn Abi Kathir from Zayd ibn Aslam from 'Ata' ibn Yasar: in the Prophet's time some of the hypocrites stayed behind when he went out to fight and were glad of their sitting at home, and when he came back from travel they excused themselves and wanted to be praised for what they had not done, so the verse came down about them. The editor's note says al-Bukhari reported it through his teacher Sa'id ibn Abi Maryam, as al-Tabari did (Fath al-Bari 8: 175), and that Ibn Kathir (2: 317) said Muslim reported it from Ibn Abi Maryam in similar words (the rest of the note is unclear in the scan). Ibn Zayd (8336), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: these are the hypocrites who tell the Prophet "if you went out, we would go with you", and when he goes out they stay behind and lie, glad of it, and think it a trick they have played. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 465}

**Second view: the Jewish rabbis (p. 466, reports 8337-8338).** Others said it means some rabbis of the Jews who rejoiced at leading people astray and at people ascribing knowledge to them. 'Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8337), through Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, client of Zayd ibn Thabit: it means Finhas, Ashya' and their like among the rabbis, who rejoiced at what they gained of this world through the error they made attractive to people, and wanted people to call them scholars when they were not people of knowledge, and to say they had done what they had not done. Ibn 'Abbas (8338), through Abu Kurayb from Yunus ibn Bukayr from Ibn Ishaq from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad from 'Ikrima, in similar words, except that he said they had not led people to guidance. The editor's first note says Ibn Hisham's Sira has a different wording from the printed text and manuscript, and that what he preferred is correct because the next report shows the point on which the two reports differ. His second note says both reports are in the Sira (2: 208), continuing report 8318, with an unbroken chain to Ibn 'Abbas. His third note says the printed text has "Ibn Kurayb", which is an error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 466}

**Third view: the Jews' agreement to deny the Prophet (pp. 466-468, reports 8339-8344).** Others said it means some Jews who rejoiced that their word was united in denying Muhammad and wanted to be praised as people of prayer and fasting. Al-Dahhak (8339), through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj from Abu Mu'adh from 'Ubayd ibn Sulayman: they rejoiced in their agreement to disbelieve in Muhammad and said God has united our word, none of us differing with another that Muhammad is not a prophet; they said we are God's children and beloved, people of prayer and fasting, and they lied, for they are people of disbelief, polytheism and fabrication. The editor's note says the bracketed sentence stood in the printed text and manuscript as two corrupt readings, and that he supplied it from a report al-Suyuti gives in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 109), which he attributes to 'Abd ibn Humayd and Ibn Jarir from al-Dahhak, and from the next report. Al-Dahhak (8340), through Yahya ibn Abi Talib from Yazid from Juwaybir: the Jews used to bid one another, and wrote to one another that Muhammad is not a prophet, so unite your word and hold to your religion and your book; they did so and rejoiced in it. The second note says the printed text and manuscript have a wording that does not hold together and the editor corrected it to the form in the text (the note is garbled in the scan). Al-Suddi (8341), through Muhammad from Ahmad from Asbat: they hid Muhammad's name, rejoiced in that and in their agreement on disbelief in him. Al-Suddi (8342), by the same chain: they hid his name and rejoiced when they agreed on it, and used to declare themselves pure, saying we are people of fasting, prayer and almsgiving and on the religion of Abraham, and wanted the Arabs to praise them for the purity they claimed, when they were not so. Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8343), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Abu al-Jahhaf from Muslim al-Batin: al-Hajjaj asked his companions about the verse, and Sa'id said they rejoiced in concealing Muhammad, and their love of praise is their saying that they are on Abraham's religion; the note refers to report 8332. Ibn 'Abbas (8344), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father from his uncle from his father from his grandfather: they are the People of the Book, to whom the Book was sent down and who ruled without truth, shifted words from their places and rejoiced in it and in their disbelief in Muhammad, while claiming to worship God, fast and pray. The note says the printed text has another wording and the manuscript's is correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 468}

**Fourth view: altering the Book and people's praise (p. 469, report 8345).** Others said the sense is their rejoicing in altering God's Book and wanting people to praise them for it. Mujahid (8345), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: they are the Jews, who rejoiced that people admired their altering of the Book and praised them for it, "and the Jews do not have that in their power". The editor's note understands this to mean the Jews have no power to escape God's punishment as the verse warns them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 469}

**Fifth view: rejoicing in what God gave Abraham's family (p. 469, reports 8346-8347).** Others said they rejoiced in what God gave the family of Abraham. Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8346), through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba from Abu al-Mu'alla: the Jews, who rejoice in what God gave Abraham. Sa'id (8347), through Ibn al-Muthanna from Wahb ibn Jarir from Shu'ba from Abu al-Mu'alla al-'Attar: they are the Jews, who rejoiced in what God gave Abraham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 469}

**Sixth view: concealing the answer to the Prophet's question (pp. 469-470, reports 8348-8349).** Others said it means some Jews whom the Prophet asked about something and who concealed it and rejoiced in their concealment. 'Alqama ibn Abi Waqqas (8348), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn Jurayj from Ibn Abi Mulayka: Marwan said to Rafi', go to Ibn 'Abbas and tell him: if every one of us who rejoices in what he did and loves to be praised for what he did not do is to be punished, God will punish us all. Ibn 'Abbas said: what have you to do with this? The Prophet called the Jews and asked them about something; they hid it from him and told him something else, showing him they had answered what he asked, and they rejoiced in their concealing it; then Ibn 'Abbas recited the verse of the covenant. Humayd ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf (8349), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj from 'Abd Allah ibn Abi Mulayka: Marwan ibn al-Hakam told his doorkeeper "Rafi'" the same; Ibn 'Abbas said it came down about the People of the Book, recited the covenant verse through to the later one, and said the Prophet asked them about something, they hid it and told him something else, went out having shown him they had informed him, sought his praise for that and rejoiced in concealing what he asked. The editor's note says both reports are in al-Bukhari's Book of Tafsir, the first through Ibrahim ibn Musa from Hisham from Ibn Jurayj and the second through Ibn Muqatil from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj, and also in al-Tirmidhi; Ibn Hajar treats them fully in Fath al-Bari (8: 175-176), on Rafi', whom the books of narrators do not mention, and on the disagreement over Ibn Jurayj's teacher's teacher, said at one time to be 'Alqama ibn Abi Waqqas and at another Humayd ibn 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf; he refers to al-Wahidi's Asbab al-Nuzul (pp. 101-102). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 470}

**Seventh view: the Jews of Khaybar (p. 471, reports 8350-8352).** Others said it means some Jews who showed hypocrisy to the Prophet out of love of praise, while God knew the opposite of what they showed. Qatada (8350), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that the Jews of Khaybar came to the Prophet of God claiming they were content with what he brought and were his followers, while holding to their error, and wanted him to praise them for what they had not done, so the verse came down. Qatada (8351), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar: the people of Khaybar came to the Prophet and his companions and said "we are on your opinion and way, and a support to you", and God belied them with the verse. The editor's first note says the printed text has "your bearing" and the manuscript an undotted word, which he judges is best read "your way", and that most narrators of the report omit the word; the way means the path. His second note says a support is a helper. Abu 'Ubayda (8352), through Ibn Bashshar from 'Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from al-A'mash from 'Amr ibn Murra: a man came to 'Abd Allah and said Ka'b greets you and says this verse was not sent down about you; he said tell him it came down while he was a Jew. The note refers to report 8325 and says Ka'b is Ka'b al-Ahbar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 471}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 471-472).** Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that of those who said the People of the Book are meant, those whose covenant God said He took to make Muhammad's matter clear to the people and not to conceal it, because the verse comes in the course of the report about them and resembles their story, and the interpreters agree that they are meant by what precedes. The meaning for him is then: do not think, Muhammad, that those who rejoice in what they did in hiding your matter and that you are a messenger with the truth (though they find you written in their books and the covenant was taken from them to acknowledge your prophethood and make your matter clear) are safe; in breaking the covenant they rejoice in disobeying God in this, and want people to praise them as people of obedience, worship, prayer, fasting and following the revelation He sent to His prophets, while they are empty of it, having belied His messenger and broken His covenant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 472}

**The meaning of escape from punishment (p. 472, report 8353).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: do not think them out of reach of the punishment God has prepared in this world for those He opposes, as he puts it, such as being swallowed by the earth, transformation, quaking and killing and the like, nor are they far from it. Ibn Zayd (8353), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: it means a place of deliverance from punishment. He adds that they also have a painful punishment in the hereafter along with what is hastened for them in this world. The editor's note refers to an earlier explanation of the verb from the same root (p. 452) and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 250), and says he fears the right wording of the last sentence is that the hereafter punishment is deferred alongside what is hastened in this world. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 472}
