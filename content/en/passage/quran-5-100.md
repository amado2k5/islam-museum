## Summary
Verse 100 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 100 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "say: the bad and the good are not equal, even if the abundance of the bad pleases you": God tells His Prophet: say, Muhammad, that the poor and the excellent, the sound and the corrupt, the obedient and the disobedient are not equal; "even if the abundance of the bad pleases you" means the disobedient and the obedient to God are not equal with God even if the disobedient are many and you wonder at their number, because the people of obedience to God are those who prosper and win God's reward on the day of resurrection, however few, and the people of disobedience are the losers and the frustrated, however many; so do not wonder at the number of those who disobey God while He gives them respite and does not hasten the punishment, for the good end belongs to the people who obey God and not to them. Report 12793, from al-Suddi: "the bad" are the polytheists and "the good" are the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 96}

Al-Tabari says that although this speech is in the form of address to the messenger of God, some of his followers are meant, as shown by the words "so fear God, you who have understanding, that you may prosper". He then explains that sentence: fear God by obeying what He commanded and forbade, and beware that Satan take hold of you through your wonder at the number of the bad, so that you become of them; "you who have understanding" means the people of intellect and judgment who grasp God's signs and know where His proofs fall; "that you may prosper" means fear God so that you succeed in seeking what is with Him. The reports on verse 100 end here, on p. 97. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 97}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
