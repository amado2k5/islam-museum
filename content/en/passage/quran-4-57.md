## Summary
Verse 57 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 57 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The promise to the believers (pp. 488-489, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is those who believed in God and His messenger Muhammad and accepted what was sent down to him as agreeing with what the Jews and the other nations had, and did good works, that is, performed the obligations God commanded and avoided the disobedience He forbade; God will in time bring them on the Day of Resurrection into orchards with streams running among them, remaining there without end or break, with companions free of filth, doubt, menstruation, excrement, urine, pregnancy, spittle and all that is found in the women of this world. He says he gave the reports on this earlier and need not repeat them. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the garden and of the verb "was" in the sense of "has always been". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 489}

**The dense shade (p. 489, report 9838, note 2).** Al-Tabari says God will bring them into thick shade, as in the description of extended shade in the sura of the Inevitable ([[passage/quran-56-30|56:30]]). Ibn Bashshar and Ibn al-Muthanna (9838, from Abd al-Rahman and Muhammad b. Ja'far, both from Shu'ba, from Abu al-Dahhak) report from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet, that in Paradise is a tree in whose shade a rider travels a hundred years without crossing it. The editor notes that Abd al-Rahman is Ibn Mahdi; that Abu al-Dahhak al-Basri is a Follower known only through this report, with only Shu'ba narrating from him; that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad by three routes, all through Shu'ba; that al-Mizzi says Ibn Majah gave it in the tafsir section; that Ibn Kathir took it from this place in al-Tabari; and that the base report is firmly established from Abu Hurayra by many routes in the Musnad and the two Sahihs and elsewhere, without the words "tree of eternity". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 489}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 489}
