## Summary
Verse 21 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 21 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Reaching one another (pp. 125-126, reports 8914-8919).** Abu Jafar explains the rebuke in this verse as: on what ground do you take from your wives what you gave as dowries, when you want to divorce them and replace them with others, and you have been alone together, touched and been close? He says that, though the sentence has the form of a question, its sense is censure, sternness and threat, as a man says to another "how can you do this, when I am not content with it?" He explains that the word for reaching to something means arriving at it by direct contact, and cites a line by an unknown poet that uses the same word of a worn waterskin whose stitching has decayed until the fault passes from outside to inside; here, he says, it means intercourse. In the notes on p. 125 the editor says the printed text has a different word where he kept the manuscript's, that the poet is unknown, that the manuscript and printed text left a blank in the line and he supplied the bracketed words by conjecture so the metre works, and that the word for stitching is the seam joined with two thongs in a skin. Abd al-Hamid ibn Bayan al-Qannad (8914, from Ishaq, Sufyan, Asim, Bakr ibn Abdallah) reports from Ibn Abbas that it is direct contact, but God is generous and speaks by allusion of what he wills; Ibn Bashshar (8915, from Abu Asim, Sufyan) and Ibn Humayd (8916, from Jarir, Asim) report from Ibn Abbas that it is intercourse; Muhammad ibn Amr (8917, from Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it is lying with women, and al-Muthanna (8918, from Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl) repeats it; and Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8919, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that it is intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 125} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 126}

**The firm covenant (pp. 127-129, reports 8920-8933).** Abu Jafar says the sense is what husbands bound themselves to for the women, a pledge and acknowledgement to keep them with kindness or release them with grace, and that, as he was told, in the marriage contracts of the early Muslims the groom was told that he owed it to God to keep with kindness or release with grace. Bishr ibn Muadh (8920, from Yazid, Said) reports from Qatada that the covenant God took for women from men is keeping them with kindness or releasing with grace, and that this used to be said at the contract. In the note on p. 127 the editor says the pronouns in the manuscript and printed text disagree, which is an error, so he fixed the pronoun; refers to the commentary on the word for covenant in earlier volumes; and says the printed text has "pledge" where the manuscript has "contract", which he kept. Abu Jafar then says the interpreters differed over this covenant in three views. **First view:** it is keeping with kindness or releasing with grace. This is in reports from al-Dahhak by Yaqub ibn Ibrahim (8921, from Hushaym, Juwaybir) and al-Muthanna (8922, from Amr ibn Awn); from Qatada by al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8923, from Abd al-Razzaq, Mamar); from al-Suddi by Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8924, from Asbat), that it is when a man marries a woman and her guardian says "we have married her to you by the trust of God, on condition that you keep her with kindness or release her with grace"; from Qatada by Amr ibn Ali (8925, from Abd al-Ala, Said), adding the oath-formula said to the groom at the contract; and from al-Hasan and Muhammad ibn Sirin by Amr ibn Ali (8926, from Abu Qutayba, Abu Bakr al-Hudhali). **Second view:** it is the word of marriage by which the woman became lawful. This is in reports from Mujahid by Muhammad ibn Amr (8927, from Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih) and al-Muthanna (8928); by Ibn Bashshar (8929, from Yahya ibn Said, Sufyan, Abu Hashim al-Makki), that it is the groom's saying "I have married"; by Ibn Humayd (8930, from Hakkam, Anbasa) from a man whose name the scan gives as something like "Buhma ibn Kab al-Qurazi" (possibly Muhammad ibn Kab al-Qurazi, which we do not assert), that it is the words "you have taken possession of the marriage"; by al-Muthanna (8931, from Abu Nuaym, Sufyan, Salim al-Aftas); by Yunus (8932, from Ibn Wahb) from Ibn Zayd, that the covenant is the marriage itself; and by Amr ibn Ali (8933, from Yahya ibn Said, Sufyan, Salim al-Aftas). In the note on p. 129 the editor says Abu Hashim al-Makki is Ismail ibn Kathir, a companion of Mujahid, called reliable with many reports by Ibn Sad, and listed in al-Tahdhib. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 127} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 128} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 129}

**Third view and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 129-130, reports 8934-8935).** The third view is that the Prophet's words are meant, that men took women by the trust of God and made them lawful by the word of God; Ibn Waki (8934, from his father, Israil) reports it from Jabir and Ikrima, and al-Muthanna (8935, from Ishaq, Ibn Abi Jafar, his father) from al-Rabi. The editor refers to reports 8905 and 8906. Abu Jafar then chooses that the covenant meant is what was taken for the woman against her husband at the contract, a pledge to keep her with kindness or release her with grace, which the man acknowledged, because God enjoined that on men concerning their wives; he adds that he has already explained the covenant and will not repeat it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 129} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 130}

**Is the ruling firm or abrogated? (pp. 130-132, reports 8936-8937).** Abu Jafar says people differed on the ruling of this verse. Some said it is firm and a man may not take anything of what he gave if he wants to divorce, unless the wife is the one who wants the divorce. Others said it is firm and he may never take anything, whoever wants the divorce; this is reported from Bakr ibn Abdallah al-Muzani, and Mujahid ibn Musa (8936, from Abd al-Samad, Uqba) says he asked Bakr whether a man may take anything from a woman who buys her release, and Bakr said no, citing the verse. In the note the editor says this report came earlier as 4877, that the narrator's name is given here as there in a form that is not the right one, and that Abu Jafar added there another chain from Bakr, one sign that he abridged this commentary. Others said it is abrogated by the verse on khul in the sura of the Cow, [[passage/quran-2-229|2:229]]; Yunus (8937, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that God gave a concession afterwards, so that verse abrogated this one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 130} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 131}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the ruling (pp. 131-132).** Abu Jafar chooses that the verse is firm and not abrogated, and that a man may not take anything of what he gave if he wants to divorce her without disobedience or suspicion on her part, because an abrogating ruling is one that denies its opposite, as he explained in his other books, and nothing here denies the ruling of the verse on khul. What God forbade the man is taking what he gave when he is the one who wants the divorce; what he permitted is when she wants it and he dislikes it. One verse cannot be judged abrogating and the other abrogated except by a proof that must be accepted. As for Bakr al-Muzani's view that the husband of a woman who buys her release may not take what he gave when she asks to part and he dislikes it, Abu Jafar finds it wrong because of the sound report that the Prophet told Thabit ibn Qays ibn Shammas to take what he had given his wife and part from her when she asked to leave him and the disobedience was hers. The editor refers to Abu Jafar's reply to Bakr earlier, where he called it a view with no meaning, to reports 4807-4811 on Thabit, and to his discussion of abrogation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 131} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 132}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 132}
