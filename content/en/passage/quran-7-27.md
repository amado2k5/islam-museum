## Summary
Verse 27 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 27 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Children of Adam, let not Satan tempt you as he drove your parents out of Paradise, stripping them of their garment to show them their nakedness", God says: children of Adam, let not Satan deceive you into exposing your private parts to people by your obeying him when he tests you, as he did with your parents Adam and Eve when he tested them and they obeyed him and disobeyed their Lord, so that through the plot and deceit he arranged for them he got them driven out of Paradise and had the clothing He had clothed them with taken from them, to show them their nakedness by uncovering it and displaying it to their eyes after it had been covered. Al-Tabari says he explained earlier that "fitna" means testing and trial. The commentators differ over the nature of the "garment" God said He took from our parents. Some said it was nails: he gives (14451) from Ikrima that each animal has its own covering and man's is the nail, and repentance reached Adam at his nail, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 373}

(14452) from Ibn Abbas that his nails were left on him as adornment and benefit; (14453) from him that their garment was the nail, and when they fell into the error it was taken from them and the nails were left as a reminder and an adornment; and (14454) from Ikrima that his garment was the nail, so his repentance reached his nails. Others said their garment was light: he gives (14455) and (14456) from Wahb ibn Munabbih that the garment of Adam and Eve was a light on their private parts, so neither saw the other's nakedness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 374}

Others said that by "stripping them of their garment" God meant stripping them of God-fearing, and he gives (14457), (14458) and (14459) from Mujahid that the garment is piety; the note says Mutallib ibn Ziyad was called very weak by Ibn Sa'd, trusted by others, and Abu Hatim said his hadith is written but not relied upon. Al-Tabari says the right view to him is that God warned His servants not to let Satan tempt them as he tempted their parents Adam and Eve, and not to let him strip them of God's clothing which He sent down to them, as He stripped their parents of their garment. "Clothing" unqualified, without being annexed to anything, in people's usage, is what the wearer dons of kinds of covering or with which he covers his body or part of it. That being so, the right thing to say is that what God reported of Adam and Eve's garment which Satan stripped from them was part of what they covered their bodies and private parts with, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 375}

and it may be that it was a nail, or that it was a light, or something else, and we have no report establishing proof as to which, so there is no sounder saying than to say as God said, "stripping them of their garment". God ascribed to Iblis the driving out of Adam and Eve from Paradise and the taking from them of the clothing they had, though God was the doer of that to them as punishment for their disobedience of Him, since what came from them in it came from his making it easy for them by his plot and deceit; so it is ascribed to him at times in that sense and to God at times for His doing it to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 376}

Then, on "he sees you, he and his tribe, from where you do not see them; We have made the devils allies of those who do not believe", he says: God means that the devil sees you; the "hu" in "innahu" returns to the devil; and "his tribe" means his kind and the genus of which he is one, who are the jinn; he gives (14460) from Mujahid that "he sees you, he and his tribe" means the jinn and the devils. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 376}
