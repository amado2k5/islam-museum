## Summary
Verse 115 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 115 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The variant readings (7664, p. 131).** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed over the two verbs. Most Kufan reciters read both in the third person, referring back to the description of the people God described as commanding and forbidding. Most reciters of Medina and the Hijaz and some Kufans read both in the second person, meaning: whatever good you, believers, do, your Lord will not deny it. Some Basran reciters allowed both. Al-Tabari chose the third person for both, reporting about the upright community that reads God's signs, because what precedes this verse is a report about them, and joining it to the sense of what comes before is better than turning it away when nothing shows a shift from their description; Ibn Abbas read as he chose. From Ahmad ibn Yusuf al-Taghlibi, al-Qasim ibn Sallam, Hajjaj, Harun, Abu Amr ibn al-Ala, who said it reached him that Ibn Abbas read both in the third person (7664). The editor says al-Taghlibi's entry is at no. 5954 and that the earlier printed text dropped a word of the name which the publisher could not read. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 131}

**Denial of reward and recompense (7665, 7666, p. 132).** On the reading he chose, the sense is: whatever good this community does and whatever work it does with which God is pleased, God will not void the reward of that work or leave them without recompense, but will increase their reward and make their honour splendid. He explained the meaning of the word for denial earlier, its root being covering a thing, so the sense of the negation here is that what they did of good will not be covered over so that they are left without recompense, but they will be thanked and rewarded richly. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7665): it will not be lost to you; from Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7666): the same. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 132}

**God's knowledge of the God-fearing (p. 132).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God has knowledge of those who fear Him by obeying Him and avoiding disobedience, and preserves their good works to reward and repay them, as a glad tiding in the present world and an urging to hold to the good character He approved for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 132}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 132}
