## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "Then Adam received from his Lord words" al-Tabari takes "received" as taking and accepting, from the root of meeting, so that God conveyed to Adam words of repentance, which he received and took up in repentance, and God accepted his repentance. He reports from Ibn Zayd that the words are the verse "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves", pointing to surah al-A'raf. He notes a reading with "kalimat" in the nominative, which Arabic allows, but permits only the nominative for "Adam", because the readers and the scholars of the earlier and later generations agree that the receiving is Adam's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 541} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 542}
He then records the differences over what the words were. From Ibn Abbas: Adam said, my Lord, did You not create me with Your hand, breathe into me of Your spirit, settle me in Your Garden, and has not Your mercy preceded Your anger; if I repent and make amends will You return me to the Garden? and He said yes, and this is "Adam received words". Similar reports come from Qatada, Abu al-Aliya and al-Suddi, and from al-Hasan that the two said "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 542} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 543}
