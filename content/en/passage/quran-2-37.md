## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "Then Adam received from his Lord words" al-Tabari takes "received" as taking and accepting, from the root of meeting, so that God conveyed to Adam words of repentance, which he received and took up in repentance, and God accepted his repentance. He reports from Ibn Zayd that the words are the verse "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves", pointing to surah al-A'raf. He notes a reading with "kalimat" in the nominative, which Arabic allows, but permits only the nominative for "Adam", because the readers and the scholars of the earlier and later generations agree that the receiving is Adam's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 541} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 542}
He then records the differences over what the words were. From Ibn Abbas: Adam said, my Lord, did You not create me with Your hand, breathe into me of Your spirit, settle me in Your Garden, and has not Your mercy preceded Your anger; if I repent and make amends will You return me to the Garden? and He said yes, and this is "Adam received words". Similar reports come from Qatada, Abu al-Aliya and al-Suddi, and from al-Hasan that the two said "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 542} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 543}

Al-Tabari continues the reports on the words. From al-Suddi: Adam asked his Lord whether He had written this against him, was told yes, and asked to be returned to the Garden if he repented. From Ubayd ibn Umayr, through someone he does not name: Adam asked whether his error was something written for him before he was created or something he originated, was told that it was written, and said "as You wrote it for me, forgive it for me", and this was "words". From Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid ibn Mu'awiya: the words "There is no god but You, glory and praise to You, I ask Your forgiveness and turn to You; accept my repentance, You are the Accepting, the Merciful", the editor vouching for him but finding no entry for his narrator Humayd ibn Nabhan. From Mujahid two reports: that they were "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves" and that they were a prayer in the form "my Lord, I have wronged myself, so forgive me". From Qatada and Ibn Zayd that they were "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 543} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 544} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 545} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 546}
Al-Tabari's own choice is that these reports agree in meaning that God conveyed words to Adam, who received them, accepted them, acted on them and repented, acknowledging his fault and regretting it; and that what the Book indicates is that the words are "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy on us we shall be among the losers". He says the other reports are not rejected but have no proof that obliges us to attribute them to Adam. He sees the account as teaching those addressed by the Qur'an how to repent, and as showing that their escape from error is like their father Adam's escape from his fault. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 546} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 547}
On "and He accepted his repentance" he says it means He granted him repentance, repentance being returning to God and to obedience from what He dislikes of disobedience. On "He is the Accepting, the Merciful" he says God is the one who accepts the repentance of sinning servants who turn to Him, His acceptance being that He grants them that repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 547}

Al-Tabari adds that "the Merciful" is the one who bestows mercy along with accepting repentance, His mercy being the pardoning of a slip and forgoing the punishment of the offence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 548}
