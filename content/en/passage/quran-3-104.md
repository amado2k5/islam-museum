## Summary
Verse 104 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 104 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The community and the call to good (pp. 90-91).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: let there be among you, believers, a group calling people to good, that is to Islam and its laws which God ordained for His servants, commanding people to follow Muhammad and the religion he brought from God, and forbidding disbelief in God and denial of Muhammad and what he brought from God, by striving with hands and limbs until they yield to you in obedience. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "community", "good" and "the known good". This is al-Tabari's interpretation as he gives it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 90} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 91}

**The meaning of success (p. 91).** He says the successful are those who attain with God, remaining in His gardens and bounty, and that he explained the meaning of success elsewhere, which spares repeating it here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 91}

**A reading attributed to Uthman (7595, p. 91).** From Ahmad ibn Hazim, Abu Nu'aym, Isa ibn Umar the reciter, Abu Awn al-Thaqafi, who heard Subayh say he heard Uthman recite the verse with an extra clause at its end about seeking God's help against what befell them. The editor says Isa ibn Umar al-Asadi, known as al-Hamdani, the blind reciter, is a reliable Kufan; that Abu Awn al-Thaqafi is Muhammad ibn Ubaydallah ibn Sa'id al-A'war, a reliable Kufan Successor; that Subayh he found only in Ibn Abi Hatim's al-Jarh wa-l-Ta'dil, with no criticism or praise, with a different word at the start of the verse from al-Tabari's, and that al-Bukhari's Tarikh has Subayh ibn Abdullah al-Absi, but he cannot say they are the same man; he refers to al-Durr al-Manthur. This is a report about a reading as transmitted, with no museum verdict. Report 7596, from Ahmad ibn Hazim via Abu Nu'aym, opens at the foot of the page and is completed on the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 91}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 91}

**A reading of Ibn al-Zubayr (7596, pp. 91-92).** Al-Tabari completes the report from Ahmad ibn Hazim, Abu Nu'aym, Ibn Uyayna, Amr ibn Dinar, who heard Ibn al-Zubayr recite the verse, and the report repeats the reading of Uthman given before. This is a note on a reading as transmitted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 91} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 92}

**Al-Dahhak (7597, p. 92).** From Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid, Juwaybir, al-Dahhak: those meant in the verse are the special Companions of God's messenger and the special transmitters. The editor notes that Ibn Kathir has the report in near wording and explains al-Dahhak's meaning as the fighters and the scholars. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 92}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 92}
