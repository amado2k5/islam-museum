## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and they said: be Jews or Christians and you will be guided" al-Tabari says the Jews said to Muhammad and his believing companions, be Jews and you will be guided, and the Christians said to them, be Christians and you will be guided, meaning by "guided" that you reach the path of truth. He relates from Ibn Abbas that 'Abd Allah ibn Suriya the one-eyed said to the Prophet, guidance is only what we are on, so follow us and be guided, and the Christians said the same, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 102}

Al-Tabari says God argued for His Prophet with the most eloquent, concise and complete argument: say to those among the Jews and Christians who tell you and your companions "be Jews or Christians and you will be guided", rather come, let us follow the religion of Abraham, which all of us agree to testify is God's religion that He approved and commanded, for his religion was the upright submitting one, and let us leave the other religions in which we differ, some of us denying them and some affirming. He gives three ways of taking the accusative of "the religion of Abraham": joined to the sense of following Judaism and Christianity, governed by an implied verb "we follow", or in the sense of "rather be people of the religion of Abraham". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 102}

Al-Tabari continues the third way of taking "the religion of Abraham" in the accusative: that it means "rather be people of the religion of Abraham", with "people" or "companions" dropped and "religion" put in their place, citing the verse "I took the cry of my mount for a kid", meaning the cry of a kid; it can also be accusative as an exhortation to follow the religion of Abraham, and some readers read it in the nominative, so that it means "rather guidance is the religion of Abraham". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 102} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 103}

On "say: rather the religion of Abraham, upright, and he was not of the polytheists" he says "milla" is religion and "hanif" the straight in anything, so the sense is: say, Muhammad, rather we follow the religion of Abraham, upright, with "hanif" a circumstantial of "Abraham". The interpreters differ on it. Some said the "hanif" is the pilgrim, and the religion of Abraham was named "hanifiyya" because he was the first leader whose following in the rites of pilgrimage was laid on the servants of his time and after, from al-Hasan, 'Atiyya, Mujahid, al-Dahhak and Ibn Abbas; and from 'Abd Allah ibn al-Qasim, the Mudar people made pilgrimage to the House in pre-Islamic times and were called "hunafa'", and "upright for God, not associating anything with Him" was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 104} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 105} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 106}

Others said "hanif" is the follower, from Mujahid; others that the religion of Abraham was called hanifiyya because he was the first leader to establish circumcision for the servants, so whoever is circumcised as Abraham was is a hanif; others that the "hanif" is the one who devotes his religion to God alone, from al-Suddi; and others that hanifiyya is Islam and everyone who follows Abraham in his religion and stays upright in it is a hanif. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 106} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 107}

Al-Tabari holds that "hanaf" is to stay upright in the religion of Abraham and follow him in his creed, and his argument is that had hanifiyya been pilgrimage, those polytheists who made pilgrimage in pre-Islamic times would have been hunafa', yet God denied that with "but he was upright, submitting, and he was not of the polytheists"; and likewise circumcision, for were hanifiyya circumcision the Jews would have been hunafa', yet God took them out of that with "Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 107}
