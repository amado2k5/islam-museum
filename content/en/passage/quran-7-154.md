**Commentary on verse 154 (pp. 137-139)**

Al-Tabari means by "when the anger subsided in Moses" that it left him and calmed. The editor says the publisher of the first printed edition tried to supply the wording and was wrong, and that the manuscript omitted the explanation of the phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 137}

Likewise anything that desists from something is said to be "silent" about it, and one who stops speaking is called silent because he desists from it. Yunus al-Jarmi is reported to have said that one says "grief fell silent in him", and for this there is a line of rajaz verse by Abu al-Najm in which a viper is about to glide away and the lark stops its cry. The editor explains that the gloss the first publisher put in is al-Zajjaj's, that he preferred to put the explanation from Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an because what follows is Abu Ubayda's statement, and that he did not find the lines elsewhere and the printed text's reading of them is wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 138}

"He took the tablets" means he took them after he had thrown them down, though some had gone; "and in what was copied from them is guidance and mercy" means in what was transcribed, that is written, in them, "guidance" being the clarification of truth, and mercy "for those who fear their Lord", those who fear God and dread His punishment for sins against Him. The editor refers to the explanations of "copy" and "guidance", and says the printed text replaced "that is, written in them" with another phrase because it did not read the manuscript, where the copyist wrote the word small at the end of a line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 138}

The scholars of Arabic differed on why the lam is entered in "for their Lord they fear", although the Arabs find it ugly to say "I feared for you" meaning "I feared you" or "I honoured for you". Some said it is like the words in sura Yusuf, "if you can interpret dreams", where the verb is joined by lam. Some said it means "for the sake of their Lord they fear". Some said it came after annexation, so "those who are fearful of their Lord" and "fearers of their Lord", and then the lam was put in for that sense and not by imposition. Some said it was done because the noun came before the verb, making the lam good. Others said the like occurs with the noun put after, in sura al-Naml at "has followed close behind you", and it is related of Isa ibn Umar that he heard al-Farazdaq say "I lost for him a hundred dirhams", meaning "I lost him a hundred dirhams", and said usage is broad. The editor explains that the printed text reads "suspension" in place of "imposition" and that he kept the manuscript, and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 139}
