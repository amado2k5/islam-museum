## Summary
Verse 185 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 185 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari says that "shahr" (month) is, it is said, from "ishtihar", being made public: one says "so-and-so unsheathed (shahara) his sword" when he drew it from its scabbard, and "the month appeared" when its new moon rose. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 444} Some people with knowledge of Arabic claimed that "Ramadan" was so called for the intensity of the heat in it, when the young camels' feet would burn on the sand; Mujahid disliked saying "Ramadan" alone, saying perhaps it is one of the names of God, and so it is said "the month of Ramadan" as in the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 444} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 445} He explains that "month" is in the nominative as the predicate of "a limited number of days", or in the sense "that is the month of Ramadan" or "the month of Ramadan has been prescribed for you", and gives the grammatical ways of the accusative for those who read it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 445}

On "in which the Qur'an was sent down", al-Tabari reports that it came down on the Night of Qadr from the Preserved Tablet to the lowest heaven and was then sent to Muhammad as God willed. He relates from Ibn 'Abbas that the Qur'an was sent down all at once from the Remembrance on the twenty-fourth night of Ramadan and placed in the House of Might, and from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that it came down at once on the Night of Qadr in Ramadan and was placed in the lowest heaven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 445} He relates a report from Wathila ibn al-Asqa' from the Prophet that the scrolls of Abraham came down on the first night of Ramadan, the Torah after six nights had passed, the Gospel after thirteen, and the Qur'an on the twenty-fourth; the editor notes in a footnote that its chain is sound and traces it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 446} From al-Suddi on Ibn 'Abbas, the blessed night is the Night of Qadr and the Qur'an came down all at once to the Frequented House in the lowest heaven, and then came down on Muhammad in pieces, in commands and prohibitions and wars. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 446} The same is reported from 'Ikrima on Ibn 'Abbas, with an addition that from its first to its last was twenty years, and from al-Sha'bi "it has reached us that it came down all at once to the lowest heaven". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 446} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 447} From Ibn Jurayj on Ibn 'Abbas, the Qur'an was sent down all at once to Gabriel on the Night of Qadr, and he sent down only what he was commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 447}

In a report from Miqsam on Ibn 'Abbas, a man told him doubt had entered his heart over "the month of Ramadan in which the Qur'an was sent down" and over its being sent down, in two other places, on a blessed night and on the Night of Qadr, when it was also sent down in Shawwal, Dhu al-Qa'da and other months; he replied that it was sent down in Ramadan on the Night of Qadr, the blessed night, all at once, and then sent down in stages through the months and days. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 448} On "guidance for the people", al-Tabari says it is direction for the people to the path of truth and the straight way; "clear proofs of guidance" are clear explanations pointing to God's limits, obligations and what is lawful and forbidden; and "the Criterion" is the separation of truth from falsehood; from al-Suddi, "clear proofs of the lawful and forbidden". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 448}

On "whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast it", he reports the commentators' differing views of what "witnessing the month" means. Some said it is the residence of one who is settled at home: whoever is a resident when Ramadan begins must fast the whole month, even if he later travels. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 449} This is related from Ibn 'Abbas in al-Dahhak's report ("its new moon appearing while he is at home"); in another report from him, whoever witnesses it as a resident must fast whether he stays or travels, and whoever witnesses it while travelling may fast or break the fast as he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 449} From 'Ubayda al-Salmani: if you witnessed its start, fast its end; from al-Suddi the same; from 'Ali (as Hammad supposes), that whoever reaches Ramadan as a resident is bound to fast; and from Ibrahim: if Ramadan reaches you do not travel in it, and if you fasted a day or two and then travelled, do not break the fast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 449} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 450} In a report from Abu al-Bakhtari on 'Ubayda, whoever fasted part of it in the town should fast the rest when he goes out, and Ibn 'Abbas used to say he may fast or break the fast as he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 450} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 451} From Umm Dharra, 'A'isha told her to tell her brother, who was bidding farewell to travel, to stay in Ramadan, saying that if Ramadan caught her on the road she would stay for it; and from Ibrahim ibn Talha, she told him, when he wanted to make the 'umra, "sit until you have broken the fast, then go". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 451} The editor in footnotes gives the circumstances of some narrators and traces the reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 450} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 451}

Others said the sense is: whoever witnesses the month, let him fast what he witnessed of it. From Abu Maysara, he set out in Ramadan and, on reaching the bridge, asked for water, drank and broke the fast; in another report he drank a handful from the Euphrates and broke the fast; and in Murthid's report he went out with Abu Maysara and he broke the fast at the bridge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 452} The editor in a footnote disputes al-Tabari's remark that Hannad erred in the narrator's name and judges otherwise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 452}

More reports on the traveller breaking the fast: from al-Hasan ibn Sa'd on his father, who was with 'Ali ibn Abi Talib on his land; 'Ali fasted and told the narrator, who was on foot while 'Ali rode, to break the fast; from al-Sha'bi, that he travelled in Ramadan and broke the fast at the bridge gate; from Sufyan, "I prefer that you complete it"; from al-Hakam and Hammad, who told Shu'ba to go out; from Ibrahim, "when it is the last ten, I prefer that he stay"; and from al-Hasan and Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, on one whom Ramadan reached as a resident and who then travelled: if he wishes he breaks the fast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 453}

Others said "whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast it" means whoever witnesses it of sound mind, adult and charged with the duty. Al-Tabari names Abu Hanifa and his companions among those who said this: they held that whoever Ramadan reaches healthy, sane and adult owes its fast; if he goes mad after it begins and recovers after it has ended, he must make up the days he lost his mind in, and likewise if it reaches him while mad and he recovers a day or more before it ends, he makes up the month except the day he fasted after recovering; and if he does not recover until the whole month has passed, he owes no making up, since he did not witness it as one charged with the fast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 454} Al-Tabari rejects this as an interpretation that has no sense: if madness removed the duty because of the loss of reason for the whole month, then that would have to be the way for anyone who lost his reason for the whole month, and he states that all agree that one who loses his reason for the whole month through fainting or delirium and recovers after it ends must make up the whole month; he concludes that the verse's meaning is not what the holders of this view said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 454}

Al-Tabari also holds that the view that whoever witnesses the start of the month as a resident must fast all of it is yet more invalid, because of the accumulated reports that the Prophet left Medina in Ramadan in the year of the conquest after fasting part of it, broke the fast and told his companions to break it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 454} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 455} He relates from Ibn 'Abbas that the Prophet travelled from Medina to Mecca in Ramadan and, reaching 'Usfan, called for a vessel, put it on his hand for the people to see, and drank; and from Ibn 'Abbas that he set out when ten nights of Ramadan had passed, fasted with the people fasting with him, until he reached al-Kadid and broke the fast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 455} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 456} From Abu Sa'id al-Khudri: "we went out with the Prophet when eighteen nights had passed, and among us were those fasting and those breaking the fast, and the one breaking did not find fault with the one fasting nor the one fasting with the one breaking". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 456} The editor traces these reports in the footnotes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 455} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 456} Al-Tabari concludes that the first two interpretations are invalid and that the sound one is the third: whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast all of it that he witnesses as a resident, and whoever is ill or travelling, a number of other days. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 456}

On "whoever is ill or on a journey, a number of other days", he says: whoever is ill or travelling in the month and breaks the fast owes the number of days he broke from days other than those of Ramadan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 457} He then reports the scholars' differences on the illness for which God allowed breaking the fast. Some said it is the illness in which the sufferer cannot stand to pray: from al-Hasan and Ibrahim, a sick person who cannot pray standing may break the fast, and from al-Hasan that he breaks it when the fast strains him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 457}

Completing al-Hasan's report on what permits breaking the fast: he said, when he cannot pray the obligatory prayers as commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 458} Others said it is any illness in which the sufferer's fasting would most likely worsen his illness by an unbearable increase; this is the view of Muhammad ibn Idris al-Shafi'i, reported from him by al-Rabi', and the editor quotes al-Shafi'i in al-Umm in a footnote: "if the sick man's illness clearly worsens he breaks the fast, but if the worsening is bearable he does not". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 458} Others said it is any illness that is called an illness, and it is reported that Tarif ibn Shihab entered on Muhammad ibn Sirin in Ramadan while he was eating and did not ask him; when he finished he said his finger had been hurting; the editor notes in a footnote that Tarif is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 458}

Al-Tabari chooses that the illness for which God permitted breaking the fast in Ramadan is that in which fasting strains the sufferer unbearably, so he may break the fast and make up a number of other days; for if he reached that point and were not permitted to break the fast, he would be burdened with hardship and denied ease, contrary to what God said He wants for His creatures, whereas one whom fasting does not strain is like the healthy person able to fast and must perform his duty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 459} He explains "a number of other days" as a counted number of days besides these ones, "ukhar" as the plural of "ukhra" like "kubar" and "qurab", and answers the question why "ukhar" is feminine though describing "days" by saying that the plural of days takes feminine adjectives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 459}

He then poses the question: if someone ill or travelling fasts the month although he may break the fast, does that suffice in place of fasting a number of other days, or does the duty of other days remain on him, and may the ill or traveller fast Ramadan at all, or is it forbidden to him until the one settles and the other recovers? He says the people of knowledge differed on all of this and that he will give their differences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 460} Some said breaking the fast in illness is an obligatory resolve ('azima) from God, not a concession. Ibn 'Abbas is reported as saying that breaking the fast on a journey is an 'azima; Ibn 'Umar, asked about fasting on a journey, said "do you not think that if you gave a man a gift and he returned it you would be angry? It is a gift from God that He has given you"; Abu Ja'far reports that his father did not fast on journeys and forbade it; and al-Dahhak disliked fasting on a journey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 460} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 461} The editor notes in a footnote that this opinion of Ibn 'Umar and others concerns one who refuses God's concession, and quotes Ibn Kathir on this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 460}

The holders of this view said that whoever fasted on a journey must make it up when he settles. It is reported that 'Umar told a man who had fasted on a journey to repeat it; from al-Muharrar ibn Abi Hurayra that his father told him "when you settle you will make it up"; and that 'Urwa told a man who had fasted on a journey to make it up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 461} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 462} The editor notes that some of the chains are weak because a narrator is unnamed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 461} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 462}

In a report from Kulthum, some people came to 'Umar ibn al-Khattab having fasted Ramadan on a journey, and he said to them "it is as if you were fasting", they said "we fasted", he said "and you were able to?", they said "yes", and he said "then make it up". The editor notes in a footnote that its chain is weak because it is broken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 462} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 463}

The reason of the holders of this view is that God, in "whoever of you witnesses the month, let him fast it", made the fast of Ramadan obligatory on whoever witnesses it as a resident and not a traveller, and put on the ill or travelling the fast of a number of other days; so as the resident may not break the days of Ramadan and fast other days in their place, the traveller may not fast them either, since what God made obligatory for him is a number of other days. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 463} They also relied on a report from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf from the Prophet, "the one fasting on a journey is like the one not fasting at home", which al-Tabari gives by two chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 463} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 464} The editor notes in footnotes the weakness of some narrators and the disagreement of the hadith scholars over whether it goes back to the Prophet or stops at 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, says that those he cites held it to be a statement of Ibn 'Awf, and notes that al-Tabari says on p. 474 that this report and its like have flimsy chains and may not be used as proof in religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 463} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 464}

Others said the permission to break the fast on a journey is a concession from God to His servants and the duty is the fast: whoever fasts has performed his duty, whoever breaks the fast does so by God's concession, and if he fasts on a journey he owes no making up when he settles. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 464} It is reported that 'Urwa and Salim were with 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz and discussed fasting on a journey; Salim said Ibn 'Umar did not fast on journeys, 'Urwa said 'A'isha fasted, and 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz said "O God, forgive: when it is easy, fast, and when it is hard, break the fast". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 465} It is reported that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab set out on a journey during the last nights of Ramadan and said "the month has thinned, so let us fast", and he fasted and the people fasted with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 465} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 466} From Khaythama, he asked Anas ibn Malik about fasting on a journey and Anas said he had told his servant to fast and he refused; he asked "where then is 'whoever is ill or on a journey, a number of other days'?" and Anas said it came down when "we used to set out hungry and halt without having eaten our fill, and today we set out full and halt full". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 466} From Anas also: whoever breaks the fast does so by God's concession, and whoever fasts, the fast is better; and from Muhammad ibn 'Uthman ibn Abi al-'As, breaking the fast on a journey is a concession and fasting is better. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 466} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 467} From Abu Qirsafa (who is Wathila ibn al-Asqa' according to the editor): "if you fasted on a journey you would not make it up". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 467}

More reports that breaking the fast on a journey is a concession: from 'Ata' and Salim ibn 'Abd Allah, "if you fast it suffices, and if you break the fast it is a concession"; from 'Ata', "whoever fasts has performed a right, and whoever breaks the fast has taken a concession"; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, "breaking the fast on a journey is a concession and fasting is better"; from 'Ata', "it is teaching, not an obligatory resolve"; and from al-Hasan, "if he wishes he fasts and if he wishes he breaks the fast". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 468} Al-'Awwam ibn Hawshab asked Mujahid about fasting on a journey and he said the Prophet used to fast and to break the fast on journeys, that it is a concession, and that fasting Ramadan is dearer to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 468} From Mujahid: "by God, both are lawful, fasting and breaking the fast, and God intended the breaking of the fast only as ease for His servants"; and from al-Ash'ath ibn Sulaym, he accompanied his father, al-Aswad ibn Yazid, 'Amr ibn Maymun and Abu Wa'il to Mecca and they used to fast Ramadan and other months on journeys. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 469} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, to whom a questioner said "we travel in Ramadan in winter, and fasting is easier for me than making it up in the heat", he replied, "God said 'God wants ease for you', so do what is easier for you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 469}

Al-Tabari chooses this view because, he says, all agree that if a sick person who may break the fast fasts Ramadan, his fast suffices and he owes no making up when he recovers; so it is known that the traveller's ruling is the same, since what is granted the traveller of breaking the fast and making up is the same as what is granted the sick. In the verse "God wants ease for you and does not want hardship for you" he finds sufficient proof, since there is no greater hardship than obliging one who fasted on his journey to make up a number of days, when he already took on his duty in the heavier of the two conditions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 470} He says the fast written for every believer is Ramadan, whether travelling or resident, and that "a number of other days" means that whoever breaks the fast by God's concession owes a number of other days in place of those broken; and he cites the accumulated reports from the Prophet that, asked about fasting on a journey, he said "if you wish, fast, and if you wish, break the fast". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 470} He relates this from 'A'isha: Hamza (al-Aslami, as the editor identifies him) asked the Prophet about fasting on a journey, he being one who fasted continuously, and he said "if you wish, fast, and if you wish, break the fast"; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 470} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 471} and from Abu Marawih on Hamza al-Aslami, who said "Messenger of God, I fast continuously, shall I fast on a journey?" and he said "it is only a concession from God to His servants; whoever takes it does well, and whoever leaves it, there is no blame on him"; and that Hamza, 'Urwa and Abu Marawih used to fast all the year, on journeys and at home. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 472} Al-Tabari holds that this and similar reports are evidence for his view that breaking the fast is a concession and not an obligatory resolve. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 472} The editor traces the reports and notes the differences among narrators over whether some chains are connected or broken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 471} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 472}

Al-Tabari raises an imagined objection: the reports you cite are many, but there are also many reports of the saying "fasting on a journey is no part of righteousness". He answers that this holds when the fast is in a condition like that in which the report says the Prophet said it to the man he said it to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 473} He relates from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah that the Prophet saw on a journey a man being shaded, with a crowd around him, and asked who it was; they said he was fasting, and he said "fasting on a journey is no part of righteousness", and in another wording "it is no part of righteousness that you fast on a journey". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 473} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 474} The editor notes in footnotes that al-Tabari himself doubted the chain of the first report (thinking Shu'ba dropped out between Ibn Idris and Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Rahman) and traces the report in the two Sahih collections and elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 473} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 474} Al-Tabari says that whoever is brought by fasting to the state of that man, his fast is no part of righteousness, because God forbade anyone to expose himself to what will destroy him when he has a way to save himself, and righteousness is sought through what God commended and not what He forbade. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 474} As for the reports from the Prophet, "the one fasting on a journey is like the one not fasting at home", he says they may have been said, if said at all, to one brought by fasting to the state of this man, and holds it impermissible to ascribe them to the Prophet because the reports that came with this are flimsy in their chains and may not be used as proof in religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 474} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 475}

Asked how "or on a journey" can be joined to "ill", a noun, when "on" is a particle, he answers that the joining is allowed since "on" has the sense of a verb, and the meaning is "or travelling", comparing the joining of "sitting or standing" to "on his side" elsewhere in the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 475}

On "God wants ease for you and does not want hardship for you", he says that by allowing them in illness and journey to break the fast and make up a number of other days, God wants lightening and easing for them, knowing the hardship it would be for them, and does not want hardship for them by charging them with the month's fast in these conditions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 475} From Ibn 'Abbas, ease is breaking the fast on a journey and hardship is fasting on a journey; asked about fasting on a journey he also said "ease and hardship, so take God's ease"; from Mujahid, it is breaking the fast on a journey, and making it a number of other days; from Qatada, "so want for yourselves what God wants for you"; from Ibn 'Abbas, "do not fault whoever fasts nor whoever breaks the fast"; and from al-Dahhak, ease is breaking the fast on a journey and hardship is fasting on a journey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 475} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 476} The editor in footnotes notes the weakness of the narrator of one of the reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 476}

On "and that you complete the number", he says the number of what you broke, of other days that God made obligatory for you to make up after you recover from illness or settle from a journey; from al-Dahhak, the number of what the sick and the traveller broke; and from Ibn Zayd, completing the number is fasting what he broke of Ramadan on a journey or in illness until he finishes it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 476} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 477} He then reports the Arabic scholars' differing views on the "wa" in "wa li-tukmilu": some said it is a conjunction with what precedes, as if it said "and He wants that you complete the number and magnify God"; and a Kufan grammarian said the "lam" is the "lam of purpose", which would be right if dropped, and that the Arabs bring it in with an implied verb after it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 477}
