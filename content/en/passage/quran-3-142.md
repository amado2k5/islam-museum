## Summary
Verse 142 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 142 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Opening of the commentary (p. 246).** Abu Ja'far says God addresses the Companions of Muhammad: did you suppose you would enter paradise and gain your Lord's honour and the nobility of ranks with Him before it is made clear to His believing servants which of you strives in His cause as He commanded. He says he has explained earlier, with proofs, what God's knowing means in such places, and the editor refers to that explanation (vol. 3: 158-162). He adds that the patient are those steadfast at the moment of hardship in what befalls them for God's sake of wound, pain and harm, and then begins the reports, which continue on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 246}

**Ibn Ishaq's report (p. 247).** Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq (7929): the sense is that they supposed they would receive God's reward and honour without His testing them with hardship and trying them with what they dislike, until He knows the truth of that in them through faith in Him and patience in what befell them in His cause. The editor says the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 117), continuing 7928, and that the printed text and manuscript had a corrupt wording at its end, which he restored from Ibn Hisham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 247}

**The grammar of the last verb (p. 247).** Abu Ja'far says the final verb is in the accusative by "sarf" (diversion): two verbs joined by a conjunction where the beginning of the sentence holds a negation, a question or a prohibition that cannot be repeated with the second verb, so the verb after the conjunction is put in the accusative as diverted from the sense of the first. He illustrates with the Arab saying that nothing would fit me while it is too narrow for you, since the negative cannot be repeated with the second verb. The reciters read it in the accusative. It is reported that al-Hasan read it with a kasra on the mim, intending the jussive, joined to the verb earlier in the verse. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "sarf" and to al-Farra' (Ma'ani al-Qur'an 1: 235-236). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 247}
