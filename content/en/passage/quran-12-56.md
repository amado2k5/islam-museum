**Commentary on verse 56 (pp. 151-152)**

On "and thus We established Joseph in the land, to settle in it wherever he pleased; We bestow Our mercy on whom We will, and We do not waste the reward of those who do good", al-Tabari says: thus We made a place for Joseph in the land of Egypt, "to settle in it wherever he pleased", that is, to take a dwelling in Egypt wherever he wished, after the prison and the straitness; "We bestow Our mercy on whom We will" among Our creatures, as We bestowed it on Joseph and established him in the land after servitude, captivity and the casting into the well; "and We do not waste the reward of those who do good", that is, We do not void the recompense of one who does good, obeys his Lord, does what he is commanded and refrains from what he is forbidden, as We did not void the recompense of Joseph's work when he did good and obeyed God. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "tamkin", "tabawwa'a", "ajr" and "ihsan". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 151}

Report 19459, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when Joseph said to the king "put me in charge of the storehouses of the land" the king said, I have done it; and, as they report, he gave him Itfir's post and removed Itfir from what he held. The narrator then says: I was told, and God knows best, that Itfir died in those nights and that the king, al-Rayyan ibn al-Walid, married Joseph to Itfir's wife Ra'il; when she came in to him he said to her, is this not better than what you wanted? They claim she said: O truthful one, do not blame me; I was a woman as you see, of beauty and loveliness, living at ease in wealth and the world, and my husband did not go to women, while you were as God made you in your beauty and bearing, so my self overcame me as you saw. They claim he found her a virgin and that she bore him two sons, Ephraim son of Joseph and Misha son of Joseph. This account is passed on in the form "they mention" and "they claim", and its narrator closes it with "God knows best". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 151}

Report 19460, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: the king put him in charge of Egypt and he was master of its affairs, handling sale, trade and all its business. Report 19461, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: We gave him dominion over what is in it wherever he wished of that world, to do in it as he wished, and it was entrusted to him; had he wished to put Pharaoh under his hand or above him, he would have done it. Report 19462, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Amr, Hushaym and Abu Ishaq al-Kufi: the king who was with Joseph became Muslim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 152}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
