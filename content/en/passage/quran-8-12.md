## Summary
Verse 12 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 12 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 12 (pp. 428-430)**

As for "when your Lord revealed to the angels, I am with you", to help you, "so make firm those who believe", he says: strengthen their resolve and make their intentions sound in fighting their enemy of the polytheists. It is said that the angels' making the believers firm was their presence in their battle with them; it is said it was their helping them by fighting their enemies; and it is said it was that the angel would come to a man among the companions of the Prophet and say, I heard these people, meaning the polytheists, say, by God, if they charge us we shall break; and the Muslims would tell one another this and their souls grew strong, and they said that was God's revelation to His angels. Ibn Ishaq said, in report 15783: "make firm those who believe" means, back up those who believe. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "with" and "making firm" and explains "inkishaf" as being routed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 428}

Then al-Tabari says on "I shall cast terror into the hearts of those who disbelieve; so strike above the necks and strike every finger of them": I shall terrify the hearts of those who disbelieved in Me, believers, because of you, and fill them with fear until they flee from you; "so strike above the necks". The interpreters differed over "above the necks". Some said it means strike the necks; report 15784, from Atiyya: strike the necks; report 15785, from al-Qasim: the Messenger of God said, I was not sent to punish with God's punishment, I was sent to strike necks and bind fast the captives; report 15786, from al-Dahhak: strike the necks. Those who hold this view argued that the Arabs say "I saw the self of so-and-so" meaning "I saw him", so "strike above the necks" simply means strike the necks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 429}

Others said it means rather strike the heads; report 15787, from Ikrima: the heads. Those who hold this argued that what is above the necks is the heads, and said it is not permitted to say "above the necks" meaning the necks, for if that were allowed it would be allowed to say "below the necks" meaning the necks, which is against the sense of the address and an upsetting of the meaning of speech. Others said the sense is: strike on the necks, "on" and "above" being near in meaning so that one may be put in place of the other. Al-Tabari chooses: the right thing to say is that God commanded the believers, teaching them how to kill the polytheists and strike them with the sword, to strike above their necks and the hands and feet; "above the necks" may mean the heads and may mean above the skin of the necks, so that it means on the necks; and since it admits that, the saying of those who said its sense is the necks is sound; and since the matter admits what we mentioned we may not direct it to some of its senses to the exclusion of others except by a proof we must submit to, and no proof indicates its restriction; so it must be said that God commanded striking the heads of the polytheists and their necks, hands and feet, by the companions of His prophet who were present with him at Badr. The editor refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 430}

We stop here at the foot of p. 430, in the commentary on 8:12 after al-Tabari's choice on "above the necks"; the next batch begins at p. 431. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 430}
