## Summary
Verse 26 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 26 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 26 (pp. 476-479)**

Al-Tabari says on "remember when you were few, oppressed in the land, afraid people might snatch you away, and He gave you refuge, strengthened you with His help and provided you with good things, that you might give thanks" [[passage/quran-8-26|8:26]]: this is a reminder from God to the Messenger's Companions, saying to them: obey God and the Messenger when he calls you to what gives you life and do not go against his command even if he orders you something hard on you, for God makes it easy for you by your obedience to Him and hastens for you from it what you love, as He did for you when you believed in Him and followed him while you were few, the unbelievers holding you weak, tempting you away from your religion and reaching you with what you hate in your persons and honour, afraid they would snatch you away and kill you all. "He gave you refuge" means He made for you a shelter to go to from them; "strengthened you with His help" means He strengthened you against them until you killed those you killed at Badr; "provided you with good things" means He fed you their spoils, lawful and good; "that you might give thanks" means that you thank Him for what He provided and favoured you with of that and His other blessings. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "snatching", "shelter", "strengthening", "provision" and "good things". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 476} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 477}

Then he says the interpreters differed over the "people" meant by "afraid people might snatch you away". Some said the unbelievers of Quraysh; report 15914, from Ikrima: he means at Mecca, with the Prophet and those of Quraysh, their allies and freedmen who followed him before the migration; report 15915, from al-Kalbi or Qatada: it came down on the day of Badr when they were afraid people would snatch them away and God gave them refuge and strengthened them with His help; report 15916, from Qatada, is like it. Others said it rather means others than Quraysh; report 15917, from Wahb ibn Munabbih: Persia; report 15918 from him: the people then were Persia and Rome; report 15919, from Qatada: this clan of the Arabs were the lowest of people in abasement, the most wretched in living, the hungriest in belly, the barest in skin and the clearest in error, on the top of a rock between the two lions, Persia and Rome, with nothing in their land then to be envied, whoever lived among them lived wretched and whoever died fell into the fire, eaten and not eating, until God brought Islam, and by it gave them standing in the land, wide provision, and made them kings over the necks of people, so give thanks to God for His blessings. The editor says the bracketed sentence is needed because the report shows that Persia and Rome are those meant, and he supplied it from al-Tabari's earlier report no. 7591, from which the addition and correction are taken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 477} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 478} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 479}

Abu Ja'far said: the better of the two views, to my mind, is that of those who said the polytheists of Quraysh are meant, because the Muslims did not fear for themselves from others before the migration, since the nearest unbelievers to them were the hardest on them then, with their many numbers and the few Muslims. As for "He gave you refuge", it means He gave you refuge in Medina, and likewise "strengthened you with His help" through the Ansar. The interpreters spoke as we have; report 15920, from al-Suddi: "He gave you refuge" with the Ansar in Medina, "strengthened you with His help" these Companions of Muhammad, whom He strengthened with His help at Badr; report 15921, from Ikrima: he means Medina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 479}

We stop here at the foot of p. 479, with the commentary on 8:26 complete; the next batch begins at p. 480 with the commentary on 8:27. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 479}
