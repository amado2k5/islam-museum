## Summary
Verse 77 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 77 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 77: selling the covenant and oaths for a small price.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: those who exchange their abandoning of the covenant God made with them and the charge He gave them in the books He sent down to His prophets, to follow Muhammad, believe him and acknowledge him and what he brought from God, and their false oaths by which they make lawful what God forbade them of people's property entrusted to them, for a mean price and substitute of the goods and rubbish of the world. He says those who do this have no share in the good things of the Hereafter and no portion of the bliss of Paradise and what God prepared there for its people. The editor says the construction of the sentence requires linking the verb with the price, and that the manuscript and printed text have a different word from the one he set. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 527}

**The sense of the threat.** Al-Tabari says he explained the interpreters' disagreement on the sense of "share" earlier and showed the right view. He then explains the next clause: God will not address them with what pleases them, and will not turn to them with good, in aversion, as one says to another "look at me as God looks at you", meaning be kind to me as God is kind to you with good and mercy, and as one says to a man "may God not hear your prayer", meaning may He not answer you, though nothing is hidden from God. He cites a verse of Shumayr ibn al-Harith al-Dabbi, a pre-Islamic poet, who says he called on God until he feared God was not hearing what he said. He explains that God not purifying them means He does not cleanse them of the stain of their sins and disbelief, and that they have a punishment that causes pain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 528}

**The editor on the verse of Shumayr.** He traces it to the Nawadir of Abu Zayd (p. 124), al-Khizana (2: 363) and Lisan al-Arab, and says that a line follows in which the poet asks to be carried on a horse because he is weak in walking, and that hearing what he says means answering, as in our phrase that God hears whoever praises Him. He says the poet is called Shumayr or Sumayr with the unpointed letter, in the diminutive. He refers to earlier explanations of purification and painfulness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 528}

**On whom the verse was sent down: the rabbis.** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the occasion and on whom the verse refers to. Some said it was sent down about rabbis among the Jews. In report 7278 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ikrima) it came down about Abu Rafi, Kinana ibn Abi al-Huqayq, Kab ibn al-Ashraf and Huyayy ibn Akhtab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 528} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 529}

**Second view: al-Ashath ibn Qays.** Others said it came down about al-Ashath ibn Qays and an opponent of his. In report 7279 (Abu al-Saib Salm ibn Junada, from Abu Muawiya, from al-Amash, from Abu Wail, from Abdallah) the Prophet said: whoever swears an oath in which he is lying in order to take the property of a Muslim man will meet God while He is angry with him. Al-Ashath ibn Qays said: by God this was about me; there was land between me and a Jewish man and he denied me, so I brought him to the Prophet, who asked me: have you proof? I said no, and he told the Jew to swear. I said: Messenger of God, he will swear and my property will go. And God sent down this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 529}

**The editor on report 7279.** He says Abu Wail is Shaqiq ibn Salama al-Asadi, and that the report is in truth two reports: the first part from Abdallah ibn Masud, the last part on the occasion of the verse from al-Ashath ibn Qays. He says al-Ashath ibn Qays ibn Madikarib al-Kindi is a well-known Companion, that Ahmad related it in the Musnad from Abu Muawiya from al-Amash with this chain, and then with the same chain in the Musnad of al-Ashath ibn Qays; that al-Bukhari relates it (Fath al-Bari 5: 53, 206) through Abu Muawiya, and Muslim (1: 49-50) through Abu Muawiya and Waki, both from al-Amash; that Ahmad related it abridged from Ibn Masud alone by other routes, and abridged and at length in the Musnad of al-Ashath by three further routes; and al-Bukhari in other places too, among them 11: 485-490, where the Hafiz explains it fully. He cites Ibn Kathir (2: 172-173) and al-Suyuti (2: 44), the latter adding ascription to Abd al-Razzaq, Said ibn Mansur, Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasai, Ibn Majah, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi in al-Shuab. He adds that it will also come as report 7282, from Mansur, from Shaqiq who is Abu Wail, in similar form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 529} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 530}

**Report 7280 (Adi ibn Amira).** Al-Tabari gives (Mujahid ibn Musa, from Yazid ibn Harun, from Jarir ibn Hazim, from Adi ibn Adi, from Raja ibn Haywa and al-Urs, who both told him, from his father Adi ibn Amira) that there was a dispute between Imru al-Qays and a man from Hadramawt, and they came to the Prophet, who said to the Hadrami: your proof, or else his oath. The man said: Messenger of God, if he swears he takes my land. The Prophet said: whoever swears a false oath to take his brother's right will meet God while He is angry with him. Imru al-Qays asked: what has he who leaves it, knowing it is a right? He said: Paradise. He said: then I call you to witness that I have left it. Jarir said he was with Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani when they heard this report from Adi, and Ayyub said Adi mentioned in the report of al-Urs ibn Amira that this verse was sent down, and Jarir said he had not memorised that from Adi that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 530}

**The editor's notes on report 7280.** He says Adi ibn Adi ibn Amira al-Kindi is a trusted Follower, whom al-Bukhari in al-Tarikh al-Kabir called the master of the people of the Jazira; he reports from his father, but here through his uncle al-Urs ibn Amira and Raja ibn Haywa. Raja ibn Haywa is a trusted Follower of much knowledge and many reports, of the clan of Imru al-Qays ibn Abis al-Kindi, the man of this incident. Al-Urs ibn Amira al-Kindi is a Companion whose companionship al-Bukhari affirmed, brother of Adi ibn Amira and uncle of Adi ibn Adi. Adi ibn Amira ibn Farwa al-Kindi is a well-known Companion with the kunya Abu Zurara and reports in Sahih Muslim, as al-Hafiz says in al-Isaba. He gives the vocalisation of Amira with a fatha on the first letter and kasra on the second, and says the rendering of Ibn Sad with a damma is an outright error, since Umayra with a damma is a woman's name; and that the manuscript here has Adi ibn Umayr and al-Urs ibn Umayr without the final letter, which is a mistake. He traces the report to Ahmad's Musnad (4: 191-192) from Yahya ibn Said from Jarir ibn Hazim with this chain, and then from Yazid ibn Harun, noting that the printed Musnad has dropped Ahmad's words "Yazid told us", a plain error; to Ibn Kathir (2: 172), who says al-Nasai related it from Adi ibn Adi, meaning in the larger Sunan; to al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawaid (4: 178), who says Ahmad and al-Tabarani in al-Kabir related it and their men are trustworthy; and to al-Suyuti (2: 44), who adds ascription to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir, al-Bayhaqi in al-Shuab and Ibn Asakir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 530} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 531}

**Report 7281 (begun).** Al-Tabari gives (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) that others said al-Ashath ibn Qays and a man brought a dispute to the Prophet over land that was in al-Ashath's hand and belonged to that man, who had taken it by force of his standing in the Jahiliyya. The Prophet said: bring your proof. The man said no one will testify for me against al-Ashath. He said: then his oath. Al-Ashath stood to swear and God sent down this verse; al-Ashath drew back and said: I call God and you to witness that my opponent is truthful. The land was returned to the man and al-Ashath added a large amount from his own land lest any of the man's right stay with him; it belongs to that man's descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 531}

**The editor's grading note on report 7281 (begun).** He says this report is disconnected, since Ibn Jurayj does not name who told it to him, and so its chain is weak; that Ibn Jurayj's words "others said" are in both the manuscript and the printed text and al-Suyuti does not mention them, perhaps abridging; that the sense is that Ibn Jurayj was speaking of the occasion of the verse, apparently reported something before this and then said "others said", and perhaps mentioned the previous report 7279 or the coming 7282 or the like, then brought this disconnected report; that it is weak in chain for being disconnected and weak too because it contradicts those two sound reports; and the note continues on p. 532. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 531}

**The rest of the editor's note on report 7281.** He says the report has the dispute between al-Ashath and a Jewish man, with the Jew as the defendant on whom the oath lay, and al-Ashath saying "then he will swear", so it is weak in chain and weak in context. He says al-Suyuti cited it (2: 44) and ascribed it to no one but al-Tabari. He says the wording in which al-Ashath stood to swear is the firm one in the printed text and is right, God willing, while the manuscript has a wording meaning the oath had been sworn, which is a mistake shown by the later statement that he drew back, since drawing back happens when the oath is offered or he is about to swear, not after swearing. He notes a wording in al-Durr al-Manthur which he thinks is a corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 532}

**Report 7282 (Ibn Masud and al-Ashath).** Al-Tabari gives (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Shaqiq, from Abdallah) that whoever swears an oath in which he is lying in order to claim property will meet God while He is angry with him, and then God sent down the confirmation of that. Then al-Ashath ibn Qays came out to them and asked what Abu Abd al-Rahman had told them; they told him what he said and he said: he is right, it came down about me; I had a dispute with a man over a well and we took it to the Prophet, who said: your two witnesses or his oath. I said: then he will swear and not care. And the Prophet said what is given above, and God then sent down its confirmation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 532}

**The editor on report 7282.** He says Jarir is the son of Abd al-Hamid al-Dabbi, Mansur the son of al-Mutamir, and Shaqiq is Abu Wail, and that the report is the earlier 7279 in similar form, that one through al-Amash and this one through Mansur. He lists among the routes of al-Bukhari for it Fath al-Bari (5: 207) from Uthman ibn Abi Shayba from Jarir by this chain; Muslim (1: 50) from Ishaq ibn Ibrahim, who is Ibn Rahawayh, from Jarir without giving its wording; Ahmad in the Musnad (5: 211) from Ziyad al-Bakkai from Mansur; al-Bukhari (11: 473) through Shuba from Sulayman, who is al-Amash, and Mansur, both from Abu Wail; and also (13: 106) through Sufyan al-Thawri from Mansur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 532}

**Reports 7283-7285.** Al-Tabari says others spoke of an occasion other than that. In report 7283 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Abd al-Wahhab, from Dawud ibn Abi Hind, from Amir) a man displayed his goods at the start of the day, and when it was near its end another man came and bargained with him, and he swore that he had refused so much for it at the start of the day and would not have sold it for that but for the evening, and the verse came down. Report 7284 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Abd al-Ala, from Dawud, from a man, from Mujahid) is similar. In report 7285 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) God put them in the position of sorcerers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 533}

**Reports 7286 and 7287 (Imran ibn Husayn).** In report 7286 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) Imran ibn Husayn used to say: whoever swears a perjured oath to take his brother's property, let him take his seat in the Fire. Someone asked: is this something you heard from the Messenger of God? He said: you find it in the Quran, and recited this verse. In report 7287 (Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Masruqi, from Husayn ibn Ali, from Zaida, from Hisham, Muhammad said, from Imran ibn Husayn): whoever swears an oath of compulsion, let him take his seat in the Fire on his face; then he recited the whole verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 533}

**The editor on reports 7286 and 7287.** On 7286 he says the chain is disconnected, because Qatada ibn Diama did not meet Imran ibn Husayn: Imran died in the year 52 and Qatada was born in the year 61, as the printed text gives them. The report comes next by another connected chain. On 7287 he says he gave the biographies of Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Masruqi and Husayn ibn Ali earlier, that Zaida is the trusted son of Qudama, Hisham the son of Hassan and Muhammad the son of Sirin, and that the manuscript and printed text read "Muhammad ibn Imran ibn Husayn", a plain error in which "from" was altered to "son of". He says the report seems here to stop at the Companion, but is in truth raised in ruling, since the warning in it is not something known by opinion or analogy or deduced from the Quran, and its raising is stated explicitly. He traces it to the Musnad of Ahmad (4: 436, 441) from Yazid ibn Harun from Hisham from Muhammad from Imran from the Prophet in the words of whoever swears a false oath of compulsion deliberately, without the verse cited; to Abu Dawud (3242) from Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah al-Bazzaz from Yazid; to al-Hakim in the Mustadrak (4: 294), who calls it sound on the condition of the two sheikhs though they did not record it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; to al-Mundhiri in al-Targhib (3: 47); and to al-Suyuti (2: 46), who has it like al-Tabari's, stopping at the Companion in wording with the verse cited, ascribed to Abd al-Razzaq, Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Dawud, Ibn Jarir and al-Hakim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 533} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 534}

**The oath of compulsion.** The editor quotes Qadi Iyad in al-Mashariq (2: 38) that the term comes from confinement and constraint, meaning that it is imposed and enforced, and al-Khattabi in Maalim al-Sunan that it is the oath binding on the one who swears by the judge's ruling, so that he is held for it; the root means holding, as in the saying "so-and-so was killed in holding", meaning held for the killing and constrained to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 534}

**Reports 7288 and 7289.** In report 7288 (Ibn Humayd, from Ibn al-Mubarak, from Mamar, from al-Zuhri, from Said ibn al-Musayyab) the perjured oath is among the major sins, and he then recited this verse. In report 7289 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) Abdallah ibn Masud used to say: we used to think, while with the Messenger of God, that among the sins that are not forgiven is the oath of compulsion when its swearer perjures himself in it. The editor says the chain is disconnected because Qatada did not meet Ibn Masud and was born about 29 years after his death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 534}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
