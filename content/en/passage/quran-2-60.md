## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when Moses asked for water for his people" al-Tabari takes the sense as: Moses asked Us to give his people water, with an omission the wording points to, and likewise "each tribe knew its drinking-place" means each tribe of them. Moses's people are the Children of Israel, and he asked for water for them in the wilderness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 119} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 120}
He reports from Qatada that they complained of thirst to their prophet in the wilderness and were told to take a stone from Tur for Moses to strike with his staff, which they carried with them; from Ibn Abbas that it was a square stone from which twelve springs flowed, three on each side, one for each tribe, and that they never moved on without finding it with them; from Mujahid, Ikrima and al-Suddi that this was in the wilderness; and from Ibn Zayd that it was a stone like a sheep's head that they put in the side of a sack when they moved on and Moses struck with the staff when they camped, and the springs burst out. Ibn Abbas also says the "asbat" were the sons of Jacob, twelve men, each of whom fathered a tribe. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 120} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 121}
