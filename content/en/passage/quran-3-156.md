## Summary
Verse 156 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 156 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 156: the warning against resembling the deniers (pp. 330-331).** Abu Ja'far: God addresses those who believed God and His messenger and accepted what Muhammad brought from Him: do not be like those who disbelieved in God and His messenger and denied Muhammad's prophethood, and said to their brothers among the unbelievers, when they travelled in the land for trade or went out from their lands to fight and perished on the journey or were killed in their fighting: had they stayed with us they would not have died or been killed. This reports what those unbelievers say of one of their number who went to war and was killed, or died on a journey he made in obedience to God or for trade. They say it so that God makes their saying a grief and sorrow in their hearts, and they do not know that this is in God's hand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 330} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 331}

**Who are they? (8107-8110, pp. 331-332).** It is said that those whom God forbade the believers to resemble in poor certainty in God are 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and his companions. Al-Suddi (8107): these are the hypocrites, the companions of 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy. Mujahid (8108-8109): it is the saying of the hypocrite 'Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. Others said they are all the hypocrites. Ibn Ishaq (8110): do not be like the hypocrites who forbid their brothers to fight in God's way and to travel in obedience to God and His messenger, and say, when they die or are killed: had they obeyed us they would not have died or been killed (from Ibn Hisham 3: 122-123, the continuation of the reports ending in 8096). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 331} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 332}

**'Striking the earth' and 'ghuzza' (8111-8112, p. 332).** The meaning of travelling in the land was disputed. Some said it is journeying for trade and travelling for a livelihood (al-Suddi, 8111). Others said it is travelling in obedience to God and His messenger (Ibn Ishaq, 8112, the continuation of 8110 in Ibn Hisham). The root sense is going far in it by travel (the editor refers to 5: 593 and Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an 1: 106). 'Ghuzza' is the plural of 'ghazin' on the pattern 'fu''al', as 'shuhhad' is of 'shahid' and 'qawwal' of 'qa'il'; a line of Ru'ba is recited for the plural of 'qa'il', in which he describes restraining himself from error after the madness of youth, with a proverbial phrase said as a rebuke. At p. 333 the editor traces the line in Ru'ba's Diwan (p. 166), Abu 'Ubayda's Majaz, Ibn Qutayba's Mushkil al-Qur'an (p. 438), Jamharat al-Amthal (p. 23), al-Maydani's proverbs (1: 38), al-Khizana (3: 90) and the Lisan, explains 'tahnahtu' as to rebuke and be rebuked and 'al-awb' as return, and lists the differing explanations of the proverbial phrase: Abu 'Ubayda ('if not this, then not that'), Ibn Qutayba ('if this does not happen, nothing else will', the letter being an exchange for another according to the grammarians), and Abu Hilal (a proverb for one who seeks something and, refused it, seeks another; al-Asma'i said he did not know its origin; a story is told of a soothsayer to whom two men brought a dispute and tested him; and the Arabs said it when a man saw his chance of revenge). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 332} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 333}

**The relative 'alladhina' and a past verb with future sense (pp. 333-335).** Abu Ja'far: the past verb is joined with a particle that normally takes only the future because the Arabs treat the relative 'alladhina' like conditional 'man' and 'ma', their meanings being close, since all are unspecified and not fixed like 'Amr' and 'Zayd'; just as it is sound, eloquent speech to say 'I honour whoever honoured you' and 'I honour every man who honoured you', the wording coming out past while the sense is future, so 'alladhina' is treated here. For 'ma' he cites a line by al-Tirimmah ibn Hakim (the editor refers to 2: 351 note 5) in which 'what was tomorrow' is meant as 'what will be'. Were the relative specified it would not be allowed, so it is not said after a specific demonstrative what can be said without it. He cites [[passage/quran-22-25|22:25]], where a present verb is joined to a past one, [[passage/quran-19-60|19:60]] (the reference is garbled in the scan and read from context) and [[passage/quran-5-34|5:34]], whose sense is future; similar cases in the Qur'an and in speech are many and the reason is the same. The editor explains the terms 'muwaqqat' (a defined noun) and 'majhul' (an indefinite), notes corrections to the printed edition and the manuscript, al-Farra's view at the same place (1: 243-244), and that al-Tirimmah is the poet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 333} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 334} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 335}

**Sorrow in their hearts (8113-8115, p. 335).** Abu Ja'far: the regret means grief in their hearts (the editor refers to 3: 295-299). Mujahid (8113-8114): their saying grieves them and does not benefit them at all. Ibn Ishaq (8115): that is for their lack of certainty in their Lord (from Ibn Hisham 3: 123, the continuation of 8110 and 8112). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 335}

**End of verse 156 (report 8116, p. 336).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is that God is the one who hastens death for whom He wills, from where He wills, and brings death to whom He wills whenever He wills, and no other of His creatures. He reads in it an encouragement from God to His believing servants to fight His enemy, to be patient in fighting him and to take fear of him out of their chests even if their number is small and the number of their enemies is large, a notice that giving death and life is in His hand and that no one dies or is killed except after the end of the term written for him, and a prohibition, since that is so, against grieving at the death or killing of any of them in fighting the polytheists. He adds that the closing clause of the verse means that God sees the good and evil they do, so they should be mindful of Him, and He counts it all so that every doer is repaid for his deed according to what he deserves. Ibn Ishaq (8116): He hastens what He wills of their terms and delays what He wills, by His power (from Ibn Hisham 3: 123, the continuation of the reports ending at 8115). In a footnote the editor suspects the copyist dropped part of the explanation of the verse, which would have run to the effect that God is the one who delays the term of whom He wills and He is the one who hastens. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 336}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 336}
