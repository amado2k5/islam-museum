## Summary
Verse 276 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 276 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-276|2:276]]), pp. 15 to 16. Al-Tabari explains the blotting out of usury as God diminishing it and removing it; 6251, Ibn Abbas: blotting out is diminishing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 15} He says this is like the report from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud from the Prophet, 6252, that usury, however much, ends in little; the editor notes that al-Hakim relates it in the Mustadrak, that Ibn Kathir gives it from the Musnad and Ibn Majah and quotes al-Tabari's wording, and refers to al-Durr al-manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 15} As for making charity increase, it means He doubles its reward and nurtures it and makes it grow for its giver; the editor corrects a word in manuscript and print and refers to the nearby explanation of usury and increase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 15}

Al-Tabari raises a question: how does God increase charities? He answers that it is by multiplying the reward for the giver, as in the verse on seven ears of grain at ([[passage/quran-2-261|2:261]]) and the verse on the good loan at ([[passage/quran-2-245|2:245]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 16} In 6253, from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet: God accepts charity and takes it in His right hand and nurtures it for one of you as one of you raises his foal, until a morsel becomes like Uhud, and its confirmation is in God's Book, and he cited the verse at ([[passage/quran-9-104|9:104]]) and this clause of ([[passage/quran-2-276|2:276]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 16} The editor gives the biography of Abbad ibn Mansur al-Naji al-Basri the judge, holding him reliable and those who spoke against him to have done so without proof, and of al-Qasim ibn Muhammad ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq as a reliable Follower and jurist; he says the report will come in the commentary on al-Tawba, that Ahmad relates it in the Musnad, and that there is an old error in the Musnad in the recitation of the first verse, which is in fact ([[passage/quran-9-104|9:104]]) and not ([[passage/quran-42-25|42:25]]), likewise in al-Tirmidhi and in Ibn Kathir's quotation, and he thinks the error older than the copyists, perhaps from Waki' or Abbad ibn Mansur. These sources and gradings are the editor's alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 16} We stop at 6253. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-276|2:276]]), pp. 17 to 19. The editor concludes his notes on report 6253 on the error in reciting the verse in the Musnad, al-Tirmidhi, al-Mundhiri's Targhib and Ibn Kathir; he thinks al-Tabari heard it from Abu Kurayb from Waki' as in al-Tirmidhi's version, and would not permit himself to give the verse wrongly, so he gave it correctly; he says al-Tirmidhi called it good and sound and that something like it is reported from A'isha, that Ibn Kathir gives it from Ibn Abi Hatim, that al-Suyuti adds ascriptions to al-Shafi'i, Ibn Abi Shayba, Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn Khuzayma, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Daraqutni's Sifat, and that Ahmad relates it by another route in short without the two verses; all this is the editor's documentation and grading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 17}

6254, from Abu Hurayra, the narrator saying I think he raised it: God accepts charity and accepts only what is good. The editor gives the biography of Sulayman ibn Umar ibn Khalid al-Aqta', says it is an abridgement of the preceding report and that doubt over its being raised does no harm, since it is sound as raised by the earlier chain and others, and refers to places in al-Tawba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 17}

6255, from A'isha from the Prophet: God accepts charity and accepts only what is good of it and nurtures it for its giver as one of you raises his foal or weanling, until a morsel becomes like Uhud, and its confirmation is in God's Book, which is this clause of ([[passage/quran-2-276|2:276]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 18} The editor goes on at length: he gives biographies of Muhammad ibn Umar al-Muqaddami and Rayhan ibn Sa'id al-Naji, citing Ibn Ma'in and that some spoke against him while al-Bukhari lists him without criticism, documents the report in Ahmad's Musnad by a sound chain in short form and in Ibn Hibban, al-Bazzar, al-Haythami's Majma' al-zawa'id and al-Suyuti, and notes that al-Dahhak ibn Uthman's report from Abu Hurayra is interrupted; these sources and gradings are the editor's alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 18}

6256, from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet: when a servant gives charity from the good, God accepts it from him and takes it in His right hand and nurtures it as one of you raises his foal or weanling, and a man gives a morsel in charity and it grows in God's hand until it is like Uhud, so give charity. These reports are transmitted as they came, without any interpretation by the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 19} The editor judges the narrator Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik most likely to be Ibn Zanjuwayh of Baghdad, reliable, whom al-Nasa'i and others declared reliable; notes that Ibn Kathir names him in a form he does not know; that Ahmad and Ibn Khuzayma relate it; that Ibn Kathir called it a strange route sound in chain with a surprising wording where the preserved one is the earlier; and the editor replies that he sees nothing surprising in its wording or strange in its chain, that it is sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs, that Muhammad ibn Thawr followed Abd al-Razzaq in it, and that it will come in al-Tawba; these judgements are the editor's alone, not al-Tabari's or the museum's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 19} We stop at 6256; report 6257 begins at the foot of p. 19 and runs on. The sayings are transmitted as they came, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
