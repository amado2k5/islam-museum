## Summary
Verse 30 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when your Lord said" al-Tabari records that a Basran reputed to know the Arabs' languages claimed "idh" is a redundant particle so the sense is "and your Lord said", citing two lines of verse. He rejects it: "idh" is a particle pointing to an unspecified time, and it is not permissible to void a particle that signals a meaning in the sentence, and he shows that dropping it would spoil the sense of both lines cited. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 439} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 441}
He asks what governs "idh" when nothing before it can be joined to it, and answers that God was addressing those He rebuked with "how can you disbelieve", reminding them of His favours to them and their forebears, so the sense is "remember My favour when I created you, and remember what I did with your father Adam when I said to the angels I am placing a successor on the earth", so "idh" is joined to the place of "you were dead and He gave you life"; he cites parallels in Arabic usage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 442} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 444}

On "to the angels" al-Tabari says "mala'ika" is the plural of "malak", the Arabs dropping the hamza in the singular, "malak", and restoring it in the plural, and "ma'lak" is also said. He holds the root meaning is "message", citing lines by Adi ibn Zayd, Labid, al-Nabigha and others, so the angels are called by that name for the message, being God's messengers between Him and His prophets and those of His servants to whom they are sent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 444} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 447}
On "I am placing on the earth" he records that the interpreters differ; some take "ja'il" as meaning "fa'il", doer, and he reports from al-Hasan and Qatada that God told His angels "I am placing a successor on the earth", meaning "I am doing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 447}

Al-Tabari says the sound reading of "I am placing on the earth a successor" is "one who appoints a successor on the earth and makes successors in it", which suits the view of al-Hasan and Qatada; he notes that some said "ja'il" means "creator", reported from Abu Ruq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 447} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 448}
He records a view that the earth mentioned is Mecca, with a report through Ibn Sabit that the Prophet said the earth was spread out from Mecca and the angels first circled the House; he gives it for those who held this view, and the edition's editor notes weakness in its chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 448}
He explains that "khalifa" is from "khalafa", to take another's place after him, citing the verse in surat Yunus about making them successors on the earth after others, which is also why the supreme ruler is called caliph. He reports Ibn Ishaq's reading as one who dwells in and builds up the earth, and holds that its meaning is what he described, even if God only told His angels of a successor who would dwell in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 449} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 450}
He then asks what was on the earth before the children of Adam and records the interpreters' differing answers. From Ibn Abbas, the jinn were the first to live on the earth, spread corruption and shed blood, so God sent Iblis with a host of angels who killed them, and then created Adam and settled him there; from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, God created the angels on Wednesday, the jinn on Thursday and Adam on Friday, some jinn disbelieved and the angels came down and fought them. Others said the meaning is successors, generation after generation of Adam's descendants, a view related from al-Hasan al-Basri; Ibn Sabit is reported that the angels meant the children of Adam; and Ibn Zayd that God then had no creation but the angels and the earth had none. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 450} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 451}

Al-Tabari reports through al-Suddi from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that God told the angels "I am placing a successor on the earth", they asked what that successor would be, and He said he would have offspring who would spread corruption on the earth, envy one another and kill one another. On this report the sense is a successor from Him who stands in for Him in judging among His creatures, namely Adam and whoever takes his place, while corruption and bloodshed come from his offspring, not from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 451} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 452}
He compares this with what is told of al-Hasan: it agrees in assigning corruption to someone other than the successor, and differs in that for al-Hasan the succession is one generation after another. He gives the argument of the supporters of al-Hasan's view, that the angels were speaking of the very successor, and answers that the angels did not assign corruption to the successor but said "will You place in it one who will spread corruption", and it is not implausible that their Lord had told them that his successor would have offspring among whom corruption would arise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 452} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 453}
On "will You place in it one who will spread corruption and shed blood" he poses the question of how the angels could say this when Adam and his offspring were not yet created: did they know the unseen, or speak in conjecture, saying what they did not know? He says the scholars gave several answers and he will give the strongest, then the best supported. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 454} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 455}
He reports from Ibn Abbas that Iblis was from a clan of angels called the Hinn, created from the fire of the samum, and a keeper of Paradise; the first to live on the earth were the jinn, created from smokeless fire, who spread corruption and shed blood, so God sent Iblis with a host of angels who killed them; Iblis then became vain, God saw this in his heart though the angels with him did not, and He told those angels "I am placing a successor on the earth". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 455}

Al-Tabari goes on with Ibn Abbas's account of Iblis: the angels said "will You place in it one who will spread corruption", since God had sent them against the jinn for that very thing, and He said "I know what you do not know", meaning He had seen in Iblis's heart a pride and conceit they had not. God then made Adam with His own hand from sticky clay, and he lay as a body for forty nights, Iblis kicking it so it rang, and saying he was made for some purpose. When God breathed His spirit into him, flesh and blood formed; when the breath was complete he sneezed and said "praise be to God, Lord of the worlds", inspired by God, and God said "may God have mercy on you, Adam". God then told the angels who were with Iblis to bow; they all did except Iblis, who refused and was proud, saying he was better, so God made him despair ("iblas") of all good and made him a stoned devil as a penalty for his disobedience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 455} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 456}
He then taught Adam all the names and showed them to those angels, asking them to tell Him the names, and they said "glory to You, we turn to You, we have no knowledge except what You taught us"; when Adam told them the names, God said "did I not say to you I know the unseen of the heavens and earth", meaning what Iblis hid of pride and conceit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 456} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 457}
Al-Tabari comments that this report shows the address was to a particular group of angels, Iblis's clan who fought alongside him against the earth's jinn, whom God singled out as a test, so they would see the limits of their knowledge, and that their words were a slip and a guess at the unseen, which God made plain to them until they repented. The edition's editor notes that al-Tabari does not give such reports to prove them but to clarify the meaning of the words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 457} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 458}
He then gives a different report from Ibn Abbas: Iblis was set over the lowest heaven and belonged to a clan of angels called the jinn since they were keepers of Paradise, and pride arose in his chest. Gabriel, then Michael, then the Angel of Death were sent to take clay from the earth; it sought refuge in God from them, and the Angel of Death took from the face of the earth red, white and black earth, which is why Adam's children differ. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 458} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 459}
