## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "and when your Lord said" al-Tabari records that a Basran reputed to know the Arabs' languages claimed "idh" is a redundant particle so the sense is "and your Lord said", citing two lines of verse. He rejects it: "idh" is a particle pointing to an unspecified time, and it is not permissible to void a particle that signals a meaning in the sentence, and he shows that dropping it would spoil the sense of both lines cited. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 439} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 441}
He asks what governs "idh" when nothing before it can be joined to it, and answers that God was addressing those He rebuked with "how can you disbelieve", reminding them of His favours to them and their forebears, so the sense is "remember My favour when I created you, and remember what I did with your father Adam when I said to the angels I am placing a successor on the earth", so "idh" is joined to the place of "you were dead and He gave you life"; he cites parallels in Arabic usage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 442} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 444}

On "to the angels" al-Tabari says "mala'ika" is the plural of "malak", the Arabs dropping the hamza in the singular, "malak", and restoring it in the plural, and "ma'lak" is also said. He holds the root meaning is "message", citing lines by Adi ibn Zayd, Labid, al-Nabigha and others, so the angels are called by that name for the message, being God's messengers between Him and His prophets and those of His servants to whom they are sent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 444} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 447}
On "I am placing on the earth" he records that the interpreters differ; some take "ja'il" as meaning "fa'il", doer, and he reports from al-Hasan and Qatada that God told His angels "I am placing a successor on the earth", meaning "I am doing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 447}
