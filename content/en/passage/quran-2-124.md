## In al-Tabari's commentary

The third volume of al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir edition) opens with "And when his Lord tested Abraham with words, and he fulfilled them". Al-Tabari says the "test" is a trial, and that God's testing of Abraham was by obligations laid on him and which he was charged to carry out; he then sets out the disagreement of the interpreters about the "words", views he attributes to their narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 7}

Some said they are the laws of Islam, thirty "shares", from Ibn Abbas through reports from 'Ikrima: no one was tested with this religion and established all of it except Abraham, so God wrote him a quittance, ten in the sura of repentance, ten in al-Ahzab and ten in al-Mu'minun and Sa'ala sa'il. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 7} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 8}

Others said they are ten practices from the customs of Islam, from Ibn Abbas in a report through Ibn Tawus from his father: He tested him with purification, five in the head (trimming the moustache, rinsing the mouth, sniffing water, the tooth-stick and parting the hair) and five in the body (paring the nails, shaving the pubic hair, circumcision, plucking the armpit and washing away the traces of excrement and urine with water); Qatada and Abu al-Jald give similar lists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 10}

Some said they are ten qualities, some concerning purification of the body and some the rites of pilgrimage, from Ibn Abbas: six in the person and four in the sacred sites, namely circumambulation, the running between al-Safa and al-Marwa, the stoning of the pillars and the tawaf al-ifada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 10}

Others said the "words" are His saying "I am making you a leader for the people" together with the verses of the rites, from Abu Salih. From Mujahid comes a detailed exchange: God said to Abraham, I will test you with a matter; he said, you will make me a leader of the people; He said yes; he said, and of my offspring; He said, My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers; he asked that the House be a resort and a place of safety, that they be made Muslims to Him, be shown their rites, and that its people be provided with fruits; to each He said yes. Al-Tabari notes Ibn Jurayj saying Mujahid and 'Ikrima agreed on this view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 11}

Al-Tabari goes on with the views on the "words". From al-Rabi' they are "I am making you a leader for the people", "and when We made the House a resort for the people", "take the station of Abraham as a place of prayer", "and We made a covenant with Abraham and Ishmael" and "when Abraham raised the foundations of the House"; from Ibn Abbas in another report they include the verses on the rites, the station, the provision for the House's dwellers and Muhammad among their descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 12}

Others said they are the rites of pilgrimage alone, reported from Ibn Abbas by several routes (through Qatada, Ma'mar and al-Tamimi); the editor notes in a footnote that some of these chains are weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 12} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 13}

Others said they are matters among which is circumcision, from al-Sha'bi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 13} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 14}

Others said they are six trials, the star, the moon, the sun, the fire, the emigration and circumcision, with which he was tried and bore them patiently, from al-Hasan al-Basri; in a report from Qatada on al-Hasan, the offering of his son is mentioned in the same way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 14}

From al-Suddi the "words" are the supplications in this sura, from "our Lord, accept from us" to "and teach them the Book and the wisdom". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 14} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 15}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice. In his words, God told His servants that He tested His friend Abraham with words He revealed to him and commanded him to act by, and he fulfilled them; those words may be all of what the interpreters named or some of it, because, as far as has reached him, Abraham was tried with all of it. So he holds it is not permissible to say that God meant one of these in particular and not the others, unless there is a proof that must be accepted, a report from the Messenger or a consensus, and no sound report on this has come. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 15}

He adds that two reports have come from the Prophet on a closely related meaning which, if established, would be the correct view: one, that God called Abraham "he who fulfilled" because he said every morning and evening "glory be to God when you reach evening and when you reach morning"; the other, that "fulfilled" means he completed the work of his day, four rak'as in the daytime. In a footnote the editor quotes the critics on their chains, including the weakness of Rishdin ibn Sa'd and Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr, and Ibn Kathir's remark that the two reports may be related only with a note of their weakness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 15} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 16}

Al-Tabari adds that had the chain of Sahl ibn Mu'adh's report been sound it would have been clear that the "words" were what Abraham said each morning and evening, "so glory be to God when you reach evening", and had the transmitters of Abu Umama's report been trustworthy it would be known that they were four rak'as of prayer each day; but both reports have something wrong in their chains. He holds that the right view on the "words" is what he set out above, and that to say the view of Mujahid, Abu Salih and al-Rabi' ibn Anas (that "I am making you a leader" and the verses on the rites explain the "words") is the better would also be a tenable position. In a footnote the editor says Ibn Kathir copied this passage and commented on it, and that the editor reads al-Tabari's statement as clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 17}

On "and he fulfilled them" he says Abraham's fulfilling them is his completing them by doing for God what He obliged on him in them, which is the "fulfilment" in "and Abraham who fulfilled"; Ibn Abbas says "he performed them" and Qatada and al-Rabi' say "he acted by them and completed them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 17} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 18}

On "I am making you a leader for the people" he says: I will make you one whom people follow and take as a model, who goes before those after him who believe in God and His messengers, who follow his guidance and the practice he performs by God's command and revelation, from al-Rabi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 18}

On "he said: and of my offspring" he says that when God raised Abraham's standing he asked his Lord to make leaders from his descendants to be followed as he had been made a leader, from al-Rabi'. Al-Tabari then mentions that some people claimed this was a request that his descendants be on his religion, as in "keep me and my sons away from worshipping idols", and that God then told him that some of his descendants would be wrongdoers opposed to his religion; he says the apparent sense of the revelation indicates otherwise, since Abraham's words follow God's "I am making you a leader", so what he asked for his offspring is the like of what he was given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 19}

On "My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers" he says this is God's report that a wrongdoer is not to be a leader whom people of good follow, and a reply to what might be supposed from his request: God said He would do it except for those of his descendants who were wrongdoers, since leadership belongs to those who are close to Him and obey Him. He then sets out the interpreters' disagreement on the "covenant": some said it is prophethood, from al-Suddi, so that wrongdoers and idolaters do not receive prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 20}

Others said it is the covenant of leadership, so that none of Abraham's descendants who is a wrongdoer will be made a leader of God's servants to be followed, from Mujahid by several routes and from 'Ikrima: "a wrongdoer will not be a leader". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 20} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 21}

Al-Tabari continues the views on the "covenant" in "My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers". From Ibn Jurayj, Mujahid said "there will be no wrongdoing leader", and 'Ata' said God refused to make a wrongdoer among his descendants a leader, and that His covenant is His command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 21} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 22}

Others said the sense is that no covenant binds you to a wrongdoer to obey him in his wrongdoing, from Ibn Abbas: "wrongdoers have no covenant, and if you made a covenant with one, break it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 22}

Others said the "covenant" is safety, so that the meaning is that God's safety does not reach wrongdoers among His servants, that is, He does not give them safety from His punishment in the Hereafter. From Qatada: that is with God on the day of resurrection; in this world they did receive God's covenant, inherited with the Muslims through it, fought alongside them and married among them, but when the day of resurrection comes God confines His covenant and honour to those close to Him; Ibrahim al-Nakha'i has similar words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 23}

Others said the "covenant" is God's religion, from al-Rabi': His religion does not reach the wrongdoers, citing "and among their offspring are the good-doer and the one plainly wronging himself", meaning not all of Abraham's descendants are on the truth; from al-Dahhak: My covenant does not reach one who disobeys Me, and I give it only to a friend who obeys Me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 23}

Al-Tabari says that although these words look like a report that God's covenant, which is prophethood and leadership for people of good, does not reach any of Abraham's descendants who is a wrongdoer and transgressor, they are a notice from God to Abraham that some of his offspring would associate partners with Him, depart from the right path and wrong themselves and His servants; from Mujahid: there will be wrongdoers among your descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 23} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 24}

On the accusative "al-zalimin" he says it is because the covenant is what does not reach the wrongdoers, and he notes it was reported in Ibn Mas'ud's reading with the nominative, "al-zalimun", meaning that the wrongdoers are those who do not receive God's covenant; both are allowed, since whatever reaches a person, the person has reached. He refers to the earlier explanation of "wrongdoing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 24}
