## In al-Tabari's commentary

The third volume of al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir edition) opens with "And when his Lord tested Abraham with words, and he fulfilled them". Al-Tabari says the "test" is a trial, and that God's testing of Abraham was by obligations laid on him and which he was charged to carry out; he then sets out the disagreement of the interpreters about the "words", views he attributes to their narrators. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 7}

Some said they are the laws of Islam, thirty "shares", from Ibn Abbas through reports from 'Ikrima: no one was tested with this religion and established all of it except Abraham, so God wrote him a quittance, ten in the sura of repentance, ten in al-Ahzab and ten in al-Mu'minun and Sa'ala sa'il. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 7} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 8}

Others said they are ten practices from the customs of Islam, from Ibn Abbas in a report through Ibn Tawus from his father: He tested him with purification, five in the head (trimming the moustache, rinsing the mouth, sniffing water, the tooth-stick and parting the hair) and five in the body (paring the nails, shaving the pubic hair, circumcision, plucking the armpit and washing away the traces of excrement and urine with water); Qatada and Abu al-Jald give similar lists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 10}

Some said they are ten qualities, some concerning purification of the body and some the rites of pilgrimage, from Ibn Abbas: six in the person and four in the sacred sites, namely circumambulation, the running between al-Safa and al-Marwa, the stoning of the pillars and the tawaf al-ifada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 10}

Others said the "words" are His saying "I am making you a leader for the people" together with the verses of the rites, from Abu Salih. From Mujahid comes a detailed exchange: God said to Abraham, I will test you with a matter; he said, you will make me a leader of the people; He said yes; he said, and of my offspring; He said, My covenant does not reach the wrongdoers; he asked that the House be a resort and a place of safety, that they be made Muslims to Him, be shown their rites, and that its people be provided with fruits; to each He said yes. Al-Tabari notes Ibn Jurayj saying Mujahid and 'Ikrima agreed on this view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 11}

Al-Tabari goes on with the views on the "words". From al-Rabi' they are "I am making you a leader for the people", "and when We made the House a resort for the people", "take the station of Abraham as a place of prayer", "and We made a covenant with Abraham and Ishmael" and "when Abraham raised the foundations of the House"; from Ibn Abbas in another report they include the verses on the rites, the station, the provision for the House's dwellers and Muhammad among their descendants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 12}

Others said they are the rites of pilgrimage alone, reported from Ibn Abbas by several routes (through Qatada, Ma'mar and al-Tamimi); the editor notes in a footnote that some of these chains are weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 12} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 13}

Others said they are matters among which is circumcision, from al-Sha'bi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 13} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 14}

Others said they are six trials, the star, the moon, the sun, the fire, the emigration and circumcision, with which he was tried and bore them patiently, from al-Hasan al-Basri; in a report from Qatada on al-Hasan, the offering of his son is mentioned in the same way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 14}

From al-Suddi the "words" are the supplications in this sura, from "our Lord, accept from us" to "and teach them the Book and the wisdom". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 14} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 15}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice. In his words, God told His servants that He tested His friend Abraham with words He revealed to him and commanded him to act by, and he fulfilled them; those words may be all of what the interpreters named or some of it, because, as far as has reached him, Abraham was tried with all of it. So he holds it is not permissible to say that God meant one of these in particular and not the others, unless there is a proof that must be accepted, a report from the Messenger or a consensus, and no sound report on this has come. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 15}

He adds that two reports have come from the Prophet on a closely related meaning which, if established, would be the correct view: one, that God called Abraham "he who fulfilled" because he said every morning and evening "glory be to God when you reach evening and when you reach morning"; the other, that "fulfilled" means he completed the work of his day, four rak'as in the daytime. In a footnote the editor quotes the critics on their chains, including the weakness of Rishdin ibn Sa'd and Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr, and Ibn Kathir's remark that the two reports may be related only with a note of their weakness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 15} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 16}
