## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "And if it was not when you entered your Paradise, you would say, 'What Allah wills? There is no power except with Allah. If you are angry, I am less than you in wealth and children.'" He says: 'And now, when you entered your garden, you were amazed at what you saw of it.' You said, 'What Allah wills was,' and in the words omitted, he was self-sufficient with the indication of what appeared to him, which is the answer to the reward, and that was it. If the speech is directed to this meaning we mentioned, 'ma' is a accusative accusative of the occurrence of Allah's action upon him, and he wills, and it is permissible to propose the answer, because the meaning of the statement is known, as it is said: 'If you can seek a tunnel in the earth,' and leave the answer, as its meaning is understood. Some Arabic scholars used to say 'ma' in the nominative case with an implicit 'hu' (meaning), as if it was said: I said: 'I said what Allah willed' 'There is no strength except with Allah,' meaning: 'There is no strength for what we strive to obey except through Him.'
