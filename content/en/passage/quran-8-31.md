**Commentary on verse 31 (pp. 502-504)**

Al-Tabari says on "when Our verses are recited to them they say we have heard, if we wished we could say the like of this; this is nothing but tales of the ancients" [[passage/quran-8-31|8:31]]: when the verses of God's book, clear to those whose breast God has opened to understand it, are recited to these unbelievers, they say, out of ignorance and stubbornness against the truth, while knowing they are lying: if we wished we could say the like of this that is recited to us, this Qur'an recited to them is nothing but tales of the ancients. "Tales" is the plural of "asturs", a plural of a plural, for the singular of "asturs" is "satr", whose plural is "asturs" and "suturs", and the plural of "asturs" is "asatir" and "asatir"; and some of the people of Arabic said the singular of "asatir" is "ustura". What the polytheists meant is that this Qur'an is only what the ancients wrote down of the news of nations, as if they ascribed it to being taken from the sons of Adam and not revealed by God to him. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "recitation" and "tales". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 502} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 503}

The interpreters spoke likewise; report 15977, from Ibn Jurayj: al-Nadr ibn al-Harith used to travel as a trader to Persia and would pass by the Ibad as they read the Gospel, bowed and prostrated, then came to Mecca and found Muhammad, to whom revelation had come, bowing and prostrating, and al-Nadr said what he said because of what he had heard from the Ibad, and the verse came down; our Lord related what they said at Mecca and their other saying in [[passage/quran-8-32|8:32]]. The editor explains "the Ibad" as people from various tribes who joined together in Christianity before Islam and settled in al-Hira, among them the poet Adi ibn Zayd. Report 15978, from al-Suddi: al-Nadr ibn al-Harith ibn Alqama, brother of Banu Abd al-Dar, used to go to al-Hira and hear the rhymed speech of its people and their talk, and when he came to Mecca and heard the Prophet's words and the Qur'an he said what he said, meaning the rhymed sayings of the people of al-Hira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 503} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 504}

Reports 15979 and 15980, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: at Badr the Prophet had Uqba ibn Abi Mu'ayt, Tu'ayma ibn Adi and al-Nadr ibn al-Harith put to death as captives; al-Miqdad had captured al-Nadr, and when the order to kill him came al-Miqdad said, my captive, Messenger of God; he said, he used to say what he said about God's book; al-Miqdad repeated it, and the Prophet prayed that God enrich al-Miqdad from His bounty, and al-Miqdad said, that is what I wanted; and about him this verse came down. Report 15980 has "al-Mut'im ibn Adi" in place of "Tu'ayma", and the editor says Ibn Kathir called this wrong, because al-Mut'im was not alive at Badr and had given the Prophet protection on his return from al-Ta'if. He identifies Abu Bishr Ja'far ibn Abi Wahshiyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 504}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
