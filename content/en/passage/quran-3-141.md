## Summary
Verse 141 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 141 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of purifying (pp. 244-245).** Abu Ja'far says it means God testing those who believed in Him and His Messenger, trying them with the polytheists' turn over them, until the sincere believer of sound faith is distinct from the hypocrite. The editor notes that the printed text added a word to the chain of the first report which is not in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 244}
- Mujahid, by two chains (7918, 7919): to test. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 244}
- Al-Hasan (7920): God purifies the believer until he affirms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 245}
- Al-Suddi (7921) and Ibn Abbas (7922): He tries the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 245}
- Qatada (7923): it was a purifying for the believers and an effacing for the unbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 245}
- Ibn Ishaq (7924): He tests the believers until He cleanses them by the trial that came on them, and sees how steadfast and certain they are; Ibn Hisham (3: 117), continuing 7917. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 245}

**The meaning of effacing (pp. 245-246).** Abu Ja'far says it means diminishing and destroying them; one says a man "effaced" food when he diminished or finished it, and the last days of the lunar month are called by the related name for their waning. The editor refers to the earlier explanation and notes the vowelling of the noun with damma or kasra on its first letter.
- Ibn Zayd through Ibn Wahb and Yunus (7925): He effaces those who are effaced in this world, and the rest of those effaced are in the fire in the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 245}
- Ibn Abbas (7926): He diminishes them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 246}
- Al-Hasan (7927): He effaces the unbeliever until he denies it (the end of the word is unclear in the scan, so this sense is probable, not certain). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 246}
- Ibn Ishaq (7928): He voids what the hypocrites say with their tongues that is not in their hearts, until the disbelief they conceal comes out; Ibn Hisham (3: 117), continuing 7924. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 246}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
