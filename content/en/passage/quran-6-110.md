## Summary
Verse 110 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 110 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "and We turn their hearts and their sight, as they did not believe in it the first time, and We leave them in their transgression wandering blindly", al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said it means that if We brought them a sign as they asked, they would not believe, as they did not believe in what was before it the first time, because God stood between them and it. Report 13751, from Ibn Abbas: when the polytheists denied what God sent down, their hearts did not settle on anything and were turned back from every matter; 13752, from Ibn Zayd: We prevent them from that as We did to them the first time; 13753, from Mujahid: We come between them and belief, so that even if every sign came to them they would not believe, as We came between them and belief the first time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 44}

Others said it means: We turn their hearts and sight, so that if they were sent back from the hereafter to the world, they would not believe, as We did to them so that they did not believe in the world; this is like "even if they were sent back they would return to what they were forbidden" (6:28). Report 13754, from Ibn Abbas: God told what the servants will say before they say it and what they will do before they do it, citing the verses of al-Zumar (39:56-58) in which the soul laments and says if only God had guided it and if it had another turn; and God reported that if they were returned to the world they would not stay on guidance, and said "We turn their hearts and sight as they did not believe in it the first time", that is, if they were returned to the world they would be kept from guidance as We kept them from it the first time, while in the world. The footnote explains the words the editor added from the manuscript so that the sentence is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 44}

Al-Tabari gives his choice: the best interpretation in my view is that God reports of these who swore by God their strongest oaths that if a sign came to them they would believe in it, that He turns their hearts and sight and directs them as He wills, and that this is in His hand, setting them straight when He wills and letting them swerve when He wills; and that "as they did not believe in it the first time" is a sign of a part of the sentence left unsaid, and "as" likens what follows it to something before. The meaning must then be: We turn their hearts, making them swerve from belief, and their sight from seeing the truth and knowing where the proof lies, even if the sign they asked for comes to them, so that they do not believe in God, His messenger and what he brought from God, as they did not believe in Our turning of them before its coming, once before. On that interpretation the pronoun in "in it" refers back to the turning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 45}

He explains "and We leave them in their transgression wandering blindly" thus: We leave these polytheists, who swore by God their strongest oaths that if a sign came to them they would believe in it when it came, in their rebellion against God and their overstepping of His limits, wavering, not guided to any truth and seeing nothing right, forsaking having overcome them and Satan having taken hold of them. The footnotes refer to the earlier commentary on "yadharu", "tughyan" and "'amah". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 46}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
