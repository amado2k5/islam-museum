## Summary
Verse 112 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 112 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "when the disciples said: Jesus son of Mary, can your Lord send down to us a table from heaven? He said: fear God if you are believers" al-Tabari says: remember, Jesus, my favour to you when I inspired the disciples to believe in me and my messenger, when they said this to Jesus; the second "when" is tied to "I inspired". The readers differed on "can your Lord": a group of Companions and Followers read "hal tastati'u rabbaka", with ta and the Lord in the accusative, meaning can you ask your Lord or call him; they said the disciples did not doubt that God could send this down, but asked Jesus whether he could. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 218}

Report 12993, from Aisha: the disciples did not doubt that God was able to send them a table, but said, Jesus, can you your Lord. Report 12994, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he read it so, "can you ask your Lord", and said, do you not see that they were believers? Most readers of Medina and Iraq read "hal yastati'u rabbuka" with ya and the Lord in the nominative, meaning that your Lord send it down to us, as a man says to his companion "can you rise with us for this?" knowing he can, but meaning will you rise with us; it may also be that the reader meant: will your Lord answer you and obey you in sending it down to us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 219}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, corrects "al-Tha'labi" to "al-Taghlibi" for Ahmad ibn Yusuf, identifies Jabir ibn Yazid ibn Rifa'a al-Ijli and Hassan ibn Mukhariq with al-Bukhari's remark on him, and keeps "Hassan" from the manuscript in place of the printed "Hayyan".

Al-Tabari prefers the reading "hal yastati'u rabbuka" with ya and the Lord raised, meaning will he answer you if you ask this and obey you in it, because, as he showed, "when the disciples said" is tied to "when I inspired", and God disliked what they said, thought it grave and told them to repent, to return to faith, to acknowledge God's power over all things and to trust his messenger. When they said it, Jesus said, thinking it grave, "fear God if you are believers"; in God's calling them to repent and to believe in him and his messenger at their words, and the prophet's finding their saying grave, there is sufficient proof for the reading with ya and the Lord raised, for there would be no cause for such outrage if they had said "can you ask your Lord to send down a table". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 220}

The editor explains the construction of "in God's calling them to repent ... sufficient proof", keeps the manuscript where the printed text was altered, and adds in brackets "he is mistaken" because the copyist dropped it by oversight and the thread of Abu Jafar's argument was thrown off, and the printed text was forced into alteration; he restored the wording.

If someone thinks that their saying it was thought grave because it was a request for a sign, he is mistaken: signs are asked of the prophets by those who deny them, so that the truth of the matter be settled for them, as the Quraysh asked our Prophet to turn the Safa into gold and to make rivers gush in the passes of Mecca, as the deniers among Salih's people asked for the she-camel, and the unbelievers among Shu'ayb's people that a piece of the sky fall. If those who asked Jesus asked in this way, then those who read it with ta and the Lord accusative have put them in a worse place than the one they thought to keep them from; or else they asked while certain that he was a sent prophet and that God was able. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 221}

And if they asked while so convinced, as one of them asks his prophet to ask his Lord to enrich him if he is poor, or to meet a need that arose, that is not asking for a sign but the request of a man in need; but God's report points the other way: when Jesus said to them "fear God if you are believers" they said they wished to eat of it, that their hearts be at ease and that they know he had told them the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 221}

The editor points to the confused places in the printed text, keeps the manuscript in "to whom he was sent", "if those who asked" and "which they thought to keep from", and says "if a need arose for him" in both manuscript and print is not Arabic, so he gave the Arabic form.

This reveals that they did not know that Jesus had told them the truth and that their hearts were not at rest about the truth of his prophethood. No statement is plainer than this that sickness and doubt about their religion and about trusting their messenger had mingled with their hearts, and that they asked as a test. The interpreters said much the same. Report 12995, from Ibn Abbas: Jesus said to the children of Israel, will you fast thirty days for God and then ask him, and he will give you what you ask, for the worker's pay is due from the one he worked for? They did so, then said, teacher of good, you told us the worker's pay is due from him he worked for, and bade us fast thirty days; we did, and we never worked thirty days for anyone without being fed when we finished; can your Lord send us a table from heaven? Jesus said: fear God if you are believers. The angels came flying with a table from heaven bearing seven fish and seven loaves and set it before them, and the last of the people ate of it as the first had. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 222}

Report 12996, from al-Suddi, on "can your Lord": they said, will your Lord obey you if you ask him? Then God sent down a table from heaven with every food but meat, and they ate of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 222}

The editor corrects "this told of their saying" to "from their saying", the first being a plain error that breaks the sense.

As for "ma'ida", it is the active form of "mada so-and-so the people, yamiduhum, maydan" when he fed them and provisioned them, as in a line of Ru'ba in a rajaz in which he praises himself and his people, describing how they send the heads of the pampered rivals to the commander of the faithful, the one asked for gifts; so the ma'ida is the feeder, the name given to the dining board because it feeds the eater from what is on it. "Ma'id" is one rocked at sea, from "mada yamidu". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 223}

The editor gives the line's place in Ru'ba's Diwan, in Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an and in Lisan al-Arab, notes that the right reading is "we send" with nun, that "the pampered" are those who luxuriate in the world's pleasures, that "andad" is the plural of "nidd", an opponent, and that the verse says they kill those who rebel against the commander of the faithful and send him their heads.

As for "he said: fear God if you are believers", it means Jesus said to the disciples who said that: watch God and fear him lest God send a punishment on you for this saying, for nothing he wills is beyond him, and your doubting God's power to send a table from heaven is unbelief in him; so fear that he send down his vengeance, "if you are believers", that is, if you believe me in what I threaten you with from God's punishment for your saying "can your Lord send down a table". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 223}

The editor keeps "watch God ... and fear him" from the manuscript.

We stop here at the foot of p. 223, at the end of al-Tabari's explanation of "fear God if you are believers", after which p. 224 begins on "they said: we wish to eat of it".

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
