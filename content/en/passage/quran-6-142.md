## Summary
Verse 142 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 142 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 142, "and of the cattle, load-bearers and bedding": al-Tabari says He raised of the cattle load-bearers and "farsh", together with the trellised and untrellised gardens He raised; "hamula" is what is loaded, of camels and others, and "farsh" the young camels that have not yet reached an age to be loaded. The commentators differed. Some said the load-bearers are the large and old camels that are loaded, and "farsh" their young which are not loaded because of their size; he gives (14047) from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud: the load-bearers are the large camels and "farsh" the small; (14048) from Ibn Abbas the same; and (14049) and (14050) from Mujahid: "hamula" is what of camels is loaded and "farsh" what is not. The note carries the copyist's closing of the ninth part and the opening of the tenth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 178}

More: 14051, from Mujahid, "farsh" are the young camels; 14052 to 14054, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, the load-bearers are the large and the "farsh" the small, and in 14054 Shu'ba notes that the narrator was Sufyan from Abu Ishaq; 14055, from al-Hasan, the load-bearers are of camels and cattle. Some said "hamula" is of camels and whatever is not load-bearing is "farsh", and report 14056 begins from al-Hasan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 179}

More: 14056, from al-Hasan, "hamula" is what is loaded and "farsh" its margins, meaning the young; 14057, from Ibn Abbas, "hamula" is what of camels is loaded and "farsh" the young camels, the weanling and whatever is smaller that is not loaded. It is also said that the load-bearers are of cattle and camels and the "farsh" the sheep and goats; others said "hamula" is what is loaded of camels, horses, mules and the like and "farsh" the small livestock. He gives (14058) from Ibn Abbas: load-bearers are camels, horses, mules, donkeys and everything loaded, and "farsh" are sheep and goats; (14059) from al-Rabi' ibn Anas: load-bearers are camels and cattle, "farsh" goats and sheep; and (14060) and (14061) from Qatada: load-bearers are camels and cattle, "farsh" small livestock. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 180}

He gives (14062) al-Suddi: load-bearers are camels and "farsh" the weanlings, calves and small livestock, and whatever is loaded is "hamula"; (14063) al-Dahhak: load-bearers camels, "farsh" sheep and goats; (14064) al-Hasan: "farsh" is the small livestock; and (14065) Ibn Zayd: load-bearers are what you ride and "farsh" what you eat and milk, a sheep that does not carry, whose flesh you eat and from whose wool you make coverlets and bedding. Al-Tabari's choice: "hamula" is whatever of the cattle is loaded, because that is a quality they have when they carry, not a name for them like camels, horses and mules; since they are called "hamula" for carrying, it must be that everything of the cattle that bears a load on its back is "hamula", a plural with no singular from its own word, like "rakuba" and "jazura". Likewise "farsh" is a description of what is small and low to the ground in body, and he supposes it was so called as a likeness of their evenness of teeth and lowness to the "farsh" of the earth, the level ground people walk on. As for "humula" with damma on the ha', it is loads, and also "humul". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 181}

Then "eat of what God provided you and do not follow the footsteps of Satan; he is a clear enemy to you". Al-Tabari says: eat of what God provided you, believers, for He made lawful to you the fruits of your crops and plantings and the flesh of your cattle, since the idolaters forbade some of it to themselves and assigned God a share of the crops and cattle He created and the like to Satan; "do not follow the footsteps of Satan" as those who slit the bahira and set the sa'iba free followed them, so forbidding yourselves of the good provision of God what they forbade, thereby obeying Satan and disobeying the Merciful. He gives (14066) Ibn Zayd: do not follow his obedience, it is sins for you and obedience to the foul one. "He is a clear enemy to you" means he seeks your ruin and to bar you from your Lord's path; he has made his enmity clear to you through his hostility toward your father until he drove him from paradise by his scheming and deceit, out of envy and aggression. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 182}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
