## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "And on the Day of Resurrection, you will see those who lied about Allah have their faces blackened. Is not in Hell a place for the arrogant?" He explains: "And on the Day of Resurrection you will see," O Muhammad, those "those who lied about Allah" from your people, claiming that He has a son, that He has a partner, and worshiped gods besides Him, "their faces are blackened." And the faces, even if they are nominative with a blackened form, have a nominative meaning, because with their predicate it is complete, you see. If His saying 'blackened' before faces is in the accusative, it is accusative. And if the blackened faces are accusative, it is in the accusative case, not in the Qur'an, if it is The 'al-maswada' is a 'sufwada' (the last word) that was permissible, as the poet said: 'Leave me, for your command will not be obeyed, and you have not accustomed me to my dream being lost.' So the accusative of 'al-halm' and 'muṣādā' is a repetition of 'ṣāṭātā'. The Arabs do the same for everything that requires a noun or predicate, such as 'zān' and its sisters.
