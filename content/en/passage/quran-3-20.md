## Verse 20 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 280-283)

Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-20|3:20]]) says the sense is: if the party of the Christians of Najran dispute with you, Muhammad, about Jesus, and argue with you falsely, say: I have submitted to God alone with my tongue, my heart and all my limbs. He singled out the face, he says, because the face is the most honoured of a person's limbs, holding his beauty and dignity, so when his face is humbled to something, the limbs below it in honour are humbled to it too. As for those who follow him, the sense is that they too submitted their faces to God with him, the relative being joined to the 'I' of the verb. In 6773, Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr (Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq): if they dispute with you with the falsehoods they bring, such as their saying 'we created, we did, we made, we commanded', these are false resemblances in which they know the truth, so say what you were told to say. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on disputing and on submitting the face, and says that Ibn Hisham gave it (2: 227) among the remaining reports that ended at 6770. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 280}

Al-Tabari then says of the next part that the sense is: say, Muhammad, to those given the Book, the Jews and Christians, and to the unlettered, the polytheist Arabs with no scripture, have you singled out oneness and devoted worship and divinity to the Lord of the worlds, to the exclusion of the rivals and partners you associate with Him in your worship of them and your acknowledging their lordship, while you know that there is no lord or god but Him? If they yield to singling out oneness for God and devoting worship to Him, they have reached the path of truth and travelled the road of right guidance. The editor notes that the word for 'partners' is a plural of the word for partner, like 'orphans' from 'orphan', and refers to the earlier commentary on the unlettered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 281}

Al-Tabari raises a question: how can this answer follow a question, and can one say to a man 'will you stand? If you stand I will honour you'? He answers that it is permitted when the speech is meant as a command though it takes the form of a question, as in al-Ma'ida ([[passage/quran-5-91|5:91]]), where the question means 'desist', and as in the disciples' words to Jesus ([[passage/quran-5-112|5:112]]), which are a request, as a man says 'will you hold back from us?' meaning 'hold back', or says to another 'where, where?' meaning 'stay, do not leave'. So the answer is allowed after a question as after a command, as in the reading of Abdullah ibn Mas'ud in al-Saff ([[passage/quran-61-10|61:10]]-[[passage/quran-61-11|61:11]]), where he interpreted the question by a command, while in our reading it is a statement: the jussive in ours depends on the question and in Abdullah's on the command, since that is the explanation. The editor says this is close to al-Farra (1: 202), and that in our copy of the text a statement stands where Abdullah's reading has the command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 281-282}

He then gives what some interpreters said to the same effect. In 6774, Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr (Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq): to those given the Book and the unlettered, who have no book. In 6775, Ibn Abbas (al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj): the unlettered are those who do not write. The editor says of the first that Ibn Hisham gave it (2: 227) among the remaining reports that ended at 6773. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 282}

At the close of the verse al-Tabari says: if they turn away, refusing what you call them to of Islam and of devoting oneness to the Lord of the worlds, you are only a messenger who conveys, with nothing upon you but to convey the message to those you were sent to and to do what He charged you with of obeying Him; and God knows which of His servants accept what He sent through you and obey by taking Islam, and which turn away from it and refuse what you were sent with, disobeying by refusing Islam. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on turning away and to p. 262. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 283}
