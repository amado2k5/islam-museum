**Commentary on verse 48 (pp. 283-286)**

Al-Tabari says: these hypocrites sought sedition among your Companions, Muhammad; they sought to turn them from their religion and were eager to bring them back to unbelief by discouraging them from it, as Abdullah ibn Ubayy did against you and your Companions at Uhud when he withdrew from you with those of his people who followed him; that was their seeking of what they had sought for the Messenger's Companions by way of sedition "before", meaning before this. "And they turned matters over for you" means they weighed against you and against nullifying the religion with which God sent you, by discouraging people from you, denying what you bring and rejecting it; "until the truth came" means until God's help came, "and God's command prevailed" means the religion God commanded and made obligatory on His creation, which is Islam, prevailed, "though they disliked it", the hypocrites disliking the prevailing of God's command and His help to you; and so now God will make you and His religion prevail over those who disbelieved among the Romans and others, though they dislike it. Report 16782, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "they turned matters over for you" so as to discourage your Companions from you and reject your affair, "until the truth came and God's command prevailed". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 284}

It is reported that this verse came down about a number of named persons. Report 16783, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq and Amr: of those who turned matters over were Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul, Abdullah ibn Nabtal, brother of Banu Amr ibn Awf, Rifa'a ibn Rafi' and Zayd ibn al-Tabut al-Qaynuqa'i. Abdullah ibn Ubayy's discouragement of his men from the Messenger on this campaign was as in report 16784, from Ibn Ishaq, from al-Zuhri, Yazid ibn Ruman, Abdullah ibn Abi Bakr, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada and others, each having related of the Tabuk campaign what reached him, some relating what others did not, their accounts combined in this one: the Messenger ordered his Companions to prepare to fight the Romans, at a time of hardship for the people, intense heat and drought in the land, when the fruit was ripe and shade was loved, so that people liked to stay with their fruit and shade and disliked leaving them in the state of the season. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 284}

The Messenger rarely set out on a campaign without disguising it and saying he meant something other than his target, except in the Tabuk campaign, which he made plain to the people because of the distance of the journey, the harshness of the season and the number of the enemy he was heading for, so that people would make the preparations for it; he ordered the people to fight and told them he meant the Romans, and they prepared despite their dislike of that direction, with the dread in which they held the Romans and fighting them. The Messenger pressed on with his journey, ordering the people to get ready and hurry, and urging the wealthy to spend and to provide mounts in God's way. When he left he pitched his camp at Thaniyyat al-Wada', and Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul pitched his camp apart, below it, opposite Dhubab, a mountain in the cemetery below Thaniyyat al-Wada', and it was, they claim, not the smaller of the two camps; when the Messenger moved on, Abdullah ibn Ubayy stayed behind among the hypocrites and doubters who stayed. Abdullah ibn Ubayy was a brother of Banu Awf ibn al-Khazraj, Abdullah ibn Nabtal a brother of Banu Amr ibn Awf and Rifa'a ibn Zayd ibn al-Tabut a brother of Qaynuqa', and they were among the great hypocrites and among those who plotted against Islam and its people; about them, Ibn Ishaq relates from al-Hasan al-Basri, "they sought sedition before" came down. The editor notes that this report is scattered through Ibn Hisham's Sira and complete in al-Tabari's History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 286}
