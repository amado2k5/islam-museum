## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 5 to 6 as one section. He explains His saying, “Indeed, the righteous will drink from a cup whose mixture is of camphor”: Those who are righteous by obeying their Lord in performing His obligations and avoiding His sins, will drink from a cup, which is any vessel that contains a drink, “whose mixture” means: the mixture of what is in it of drink is “camphor,” meaning: its pleasant smell is like camphor. It has been said that camphor is the name of a spring in Paradise. Those who hold this view interpret the word "spring" as referring back to camphor, clarifying its meaning. Those who consider camphor an attribute of the drink interpret it as an adverbial phrase, making "camphor" the predicate of the verb "was." A third interpretation allows for the accusative case of "spring," which is that it governs the verb "they drink from it," meaning that the righteous drink from a spring from which the servants of God drink, from a cup whose mixture is camphor. It is also permissible to interpret it as an expression of praise. However, most scholars of interpretation maintain that "camphor" is an attribute of the drink, as mentioned. The passage also includes statements attributed to Mujahid, Qatadah, and Sufyan.
