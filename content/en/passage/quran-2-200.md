## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "when you have completed your rites, remember God as you remember your fathers, or with greater remembrance": al-Tabari says it means when you have finished your hajj and slaughtered your sacrifices, remember God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 195} It is said "nasaka al-rajul yansuku nusukan" when he slaughters his sacrifice; "mansik" is a noun like "mashriq" and "maghrib"; and nusk in religion is drawing near to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 195} From Mujahid: "when you have completed your rites" means the pouring out of blood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 195} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 196}

As for "remember God as you remember your fathers or with greater remembrance", the interpreters differed on how they remembered their fathers. Some said that in the Jahiliyya after finishing their hajj they gathered and boasted of their fathers' deeds, and in Islam God commanded that their remembrance be in praise, thanks and exaltation of their Lord and no other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 196} From Anas: they remembered their fathers during the hajj, one saying my father used to feed people, another my father used to strike with the sword, another my father cut the forelocks of the sons of so-and-so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 196} From Mujahid: they used to say our fathers slaughtered camels and did such and such, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 196} From Abu Wa'il: the people of the Jahiliyya used to recount their fathers' deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 196}

From Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash from 'Asim from Abu Wa'il: when the people of the Jahiliyya finished the hajj they stood by the House and mentioned their fathers and their days, my father used to feed people, my father used to do such; from Mujahid: when they completed their rites they stood at the jamra and mentioned their fathers, their days in the Jahiliyya and their fathers' deeds; and from Mujahid again: the Arabs boasted among themselves of their fathers' deeds on the day of sacrifice when they finished, and were commanded to remember God in its place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 196} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 197} From Qatada: when the people of the Jahiliyya completed their rites at Mina they sat in circles recalling their fathers' deeds, their orator speaking and their narrator relating, and God commanded the Muslims to remember God as the people of the Jahiliyya remembered their fathers, or with greater remembrance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 197} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr and 'Ikrima: they recalled their fathers' deeds in the Jahiliyya when they stood at 'Arafa; from Mujahid: that was on the day of sacrifice, when they sacrificed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 198}

Others said the meaning is: remember God as sons and children remember their fathers. From 'Ata': it is the child's cry, "my father!"; from al-Dahhak, al-Rabi' and Ibn 'Abbas: as sons remember fathers; from 'Ata': like the child who clings to his father and mother. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 198} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 199} Others said they were told this because when they completed their rites and called on their Lord they mentioned only their fathers, so they were commanded to mention God as they mentioned their fathers; from al-Suddi: a man would stand and ask God, saying, God, my father had a great dish, a great tent, much wealth, so give me what You gave my father, not mentioning God but his fathers, and asking to be given in this world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 199}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the right view in his opinion is that God commanded His believing servants to remember Him in obedience, submission to His command and worship after completing their rites; it may be that this remembrance is the takbir commanded in the verse of the counted days in this surah (2:203), which He made obligatory on whoever finished his rites, imposing then a remembrance not required before, and urging them to keep it as sons keep to remembering fathers, with humility and entreaty like the child's to his father, or stronger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 200} He says the remembrance may be the takbir because there is no remembrance of God commanded after the completion of the rites which was not already required before, other than the takbir that God specified for the days of Mina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 200}

On "among the people is he who says, Our Lord, give us in this world, and he has no share in the hereafter": al-Tabari says it means when you have completed your rites, believers, remember God and turn to Him for the good He has of this world and the next, in entreaty and humility; make your deeds sincerely for His face, and do not be like those who bought this world's life at the price of the next, whose deeds were for this world and its ornament so they ask their Lord only for its enjoyment and have no share in God's reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 201} From Abu Wa'il: they said, give us sheep, give us camels; from Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash: the people of the Jahiliyya used to stand after completing their rites saying, God, provide us camels, God, provide us sheep, and God sent down this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 201}

From Anas: they used to circle the House naked and pray, saying God, give us rain, give us victory over our enemy, and return us righteous to the righteous. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 202} From Mujahid: they say "Our Lord, give us in this world" meaning victory and provision, and ask nothing for their hereafter; from Qatada: this is a servant who intended this world and worked for it, and he has a share in it; from al-Suddi: when the Arabs finished their rites and stayed at Mina, a man would not mention God but his father, asking to be given in this world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 202} From Ibn Zayd: there were three kinds in those places: the Messenger of God, the people of disbelief, and the people of hypocrisy; among the people is he who says "Our Lord, give us in this world", who performed hajj for this world and for asking, not wanting the hereafter or believing in it; among them is he who says "give us good in this world"; and the third kind is he whose speech about worldly life pleases you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 202} Al-Tabari says he has explained the meaning of "khalaq" elsewhere, that it is share. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 203}
