## Summary
Verse 234 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 234 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari opens the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-234|2:234]]) by saying it means those among men who die and leave wives, whose wives wait by themselves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 77} He asks where the predicate of "those who die" is, and answers that it is left out because the aim is to report what the waiting women owe of their waiting period when their husbands die, so the report turns from the dead first named to their wives, the meaning being plain; he compares examples from Arab speech and two lines of verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 77} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 78} The editor gives details of the poet and where the lines are found. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 77} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 78} Some grammarians said the predicate is omitted and the sense is that it is fitting for them to wait, and al-Tabari answers that he had already shown the error of explaining the nominative by its taking the place of "it is fitting". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 78} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 79} Another said that "those" here has the sense of a conditional, and al-Tabari says the two lines show clearly the opposite of what they said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 79}

Al-Tabari then explains the waiting as holding themselves back, keeping the waiting period from husbands, perfume, adornment and moving from the home they lived in during their husbands' lives, for four months and ten days, unless they are pregnant, in which case they wait until they give birth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 79} Some interpreters said the same: Ibn 'Abbas, this is the waiting period of the woman whose husband has died unless she is pregnant, whose period is giving birth (5071); Ibn Shihab, the same, adding that however long beyond the four months and ten days it is delayed only birth ends it (5072). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 79} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 80} Al-Tabari says he held this because of reports from the Messenger, and from Umm Salama, that a woman whose husband had died and whose eye hurt came to ask the Prophet about kohl; he said that in the Jahiliyya one of them would stay a year in her worst clothes and, when a dog passed, throw dung at it, and then he said, can it not be four months and ten days (5073). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 80} From Hafsa bint 'Umar, from the Prophet, it is not lawful for a woman who believes in God and the Last Day to mourn beyond three days except for a husband, for whom she mourns four months and ten days; Yahya al-Ansari said that mourning with them means not using perfume, wearing cloth dyed with wars or saffron, applying kohl or adorning herself (5074). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 81} The editor traces the two hadiths, identifies their narrators and names the collectors, among them al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Malik. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 80} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 81}

The reports on mourning in ([[passage/quran-2-234|2:234]]) continue. From Hafsa bint 'Umar, from the Prophet, it is not lawful for a woman who believes in God and the Last Day to mourn a dead person beyond three days except a husband (5075). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 82} From Zaynab bint Umm Salama, from Umm Salama or Umm Habiba, a woman came to the Prophet and said that her daughter's husband had died and she feared for her eye; he said one of you used to throw dung at the end of the year, and it is only four months and ten days now (5076, 5077, 5078); Yahya asked Humayd what throwing the dung meant, and he said a woman in the Jahiliyya, when her husband died, would go to the worst room of her house and stay there a year, and when the year passed she threw a piece of dung behind her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 82} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 83} In another narration it is from Umm Salama alone (5079), and in another from Umm Salama and Umm Habiba together, and Zaynab explained "the end of the year" as the woman choosing the worst room, sitting there until a year passed, then throwing dung behind her (5080). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 84} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 85} The editor treats the doubt between Umm Salama and Umm Habiba in the chains, judges it most likely that she related it from both, and traces the report to Muslim, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah and others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 82} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 83} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 84}

From 'A'isha, she would give the legal opinion that the widow mourns until her waiting period ends, wears no dyed or safflower-dyed cloth, uses no antimony or perfumed kohl even if her eye hurts, but may use aloe and what is not perfumed, wears no jewellery, and wears white rather than black (5081). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 85} From Ibn 'Umar, she is not to use kohl or perfume, spend the night away from her house, or wear dyed cloth, except an 'asb cloth (5082); from 'Ata' from Ibn 'Abbas, she is forbidden to adorn herself or use perfume (5083); and Ibn 'Umar the same, adding no combing, but seeing no harm in a striped cloak (5084). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 85} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 86}

Others said that the widow is commanded to wait only as regards husbands, not as regards perfume, adornment or sleeping away from the house, which she was not forbidden: from al-Hasan, who allowed adornment and saw no mourning (5085); from Ibn 'Abbas, God did not say she must spend the period in her house, so she may spend it where she likes (5086, 5087). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 86} Their argument is that God commanded the widow to wait only from marriage, so they limited the ruling, and the report of Asma' bint 'Umays, that when Ja'far was killed the Messenger said to her to mourn three days and then do as she pleased (5088, 5089). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 86} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 87} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 88} The editor traces the report, identifies its narrator, notes that al-Bayhaqi faulted it as mursal and that Ibn Hajar called its chain strong but answered that it is anomalous and against sound reports, and thinks the best answer is the one al-Tabari gives afterwards. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 87} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 88} Those who say this hold that the report shows there is no mourning for the widow, and that the waiting is from husbands only. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 88} At the foot of p. 88 al-Tabari begins to set out the argument of those who make mourning obligatory and forbid moving, and it continues on the next page.
