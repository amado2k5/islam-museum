## Summary
Verse 60 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 60 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Al-Tabari's explanation of the verse (p. 507, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the address is to the Prophet, to take note in his heart of those who profess to have accepted what was sent down to him of the Book and what was sent down before him of the scriptures, yet wish to bring their dispute to the taghut, meaning the one they exalt, take their word from and accept the rule of instead of God's; they were commanded by God to reject what this taghut brings, but left God's command and followed the command of Satan, and Satan wishes to turn them from the path of truth and guidance and drive them a long way from it. The editor refers to earlier explanations of taghut and of going astray (page numbers scrambled in the scan). Al-Tabari adds that it was said to have been sent down about a hypocrite who called a Jew, in a dispute between them, to one of the diviners to judge, while the Messenger was among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 507}

**The view: a diviner of Juhayna (pp. 508-509, reports 9891-9893).** Muhammad b. al-Muthanna (9891, from Abd al-Wahhab, from Dawud) reports from Amir al-Sha'bi that a Jew and a hypocrite were in dispute; the hypocrite wished to go to the Jews because he knew they took bribes, and the Jew wished to go to the Muslims because he knew they did not; they agreed on a diviner of Juhayna, and God sent down the passage about it, through to its close at [[passage/quran-4-65|4:65]]. Ibn al-Muthanna (9892, from Abd al-A'la, from Dawud) reports from Amir similarly, adding that those who profess to accept what was sent to him are the hypocrites and those who profess what was before him are the Jews, that the taghut is the diviner, and that each was commanded in his own book to reject the diviner. Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (9893, from Ibn Ulayya, from Dawud) reports from al-Sha'bi that a man who claimed to be Muslim and a Jew were in dispute, and the Jew said he would bring the case before the man's own religious people, or the Prophet, since he knew the Prophet took no bribes in judging; they differed and agreed on a diviner in Juhayna, and the verse came down, the claimant being the man of the Ansar and the other the Jew; al-Sha'bi recited the rest of the passage including what is at [[passage/quran-4-65|4:65]]. The editor notes that the manuscript has "the Jews" in one place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 509}

**The view: a Hadrami's account, and Bishr the Ansari (p. 509, reports 9894-9895, note 1).** Muhammad b. Abd al-A'la (9894, from al-Mu'tamir b. Sulayman, from his father) reports that a man of Hadramawt claimed that a Jew who had become Muslim was in a quarrel (the editor glosses mudara'a as quarrelling) with another Jew over a right; the other said, go to God's prophet; the convert knew he would be ruled against and refused, and they went to a diviner and brought the case before him. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9895, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that it was mentioned that the verse came down about two men, an Ansari called Bishr and a Jew, who took their quarrel to a diviner in Medina and left the Prophet; God censured this; the Jew had been calling him to the Prophet, knowing he would not wrong him, while the Ansari refused though claiming to be Muslim; God censured the claimant and the Jew, one of the People of the Book, through to the turning away that the passage describes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 509}

**The view: Qurayza, al-Nadir and Abu Burda (pp. 509-511, report 9896, notes).** Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9896, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that some Jews became Muslim and some of them were hypocrites. In the age of ignorance, if a man of al-Nadir was killed, Qurayza killed one of them in return, and if a man of Qurayza was killed, al-Nadir paid blood money of sixty wasq of dates. When some of them became Muslim, a man of al-Nadir killed a man of Qurayza and they went to the Prophet; the Nadiri said they used to give the blood money, so they would give it now; Qurayza said no, we are your brothers in descent and religion, our blood is like yours, you overpowered us in the age of ignorance and God has now brought Islam. God sent down words reproaching them for what they did, mentioning the life-for-life rule ([[passage/quran-5-45|5:45]]) and the Nadiri's statement and the judgement of the age of ignorance ([[passage/quran-5-50|5:50]]); the Nadiri was seized and killed for the man he killed. Then al-Nadir and Qurayza boasted, each saying it was nobler, and they entered Medina to Abu Burda the Aslami diviner. The hypocrite among them said, go to Abu Burda to decide between us; the Muslims said, rather the Prophet; the hypocrites refused and went to Abu Burda and asked him. He said, make the morsel large, meaning the stake; they said ten wasq for you; he said rather a hundred wasq as my blood money, since he feared that if he decided for al-Nadir, Qurayza would kill him, and the reverse; they refused to give more than ten and he refused to judge, and God sent down the verse, the taghut being Abu Burda, through to the passage's close. The editor notes that a wasq was a known measure of their time, about a camel's load; that the print has "Abu Barza al-Aslami", a plain error, and the manuscript's "Abu Burda" is right, Abu Barza being a distinguished Companion; that al-Tha'labi says in his tafsir that the Prophet invited Abu Burda al-Aslami to Islam and he refused until his sons spoke to him, and then accepted; that Ibn Hajar in al-Isaba cites al-Tabarani, with a good chain, from Ibn Abbas that Abu Burda al-Aslami was a diviner who judged between Jews, with the story of the verse's descent; that al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawa'id and Ibn Kathir in his tafsir also write "Abu Barza", an error; that nafara is to judge one disputant the winner over the other, and munafara is when two men boast against each other and make a man their arbiter; and, on p. 511, that the print repeats "Abu Barza" a third and fourth time, that khatar is the stake set between bettors and here the fee each disputant gives the arbiter, called a morsel figuratively, which language books do not record and which he did not find in accounts of munafara, so the report shows that arbiters in a munafara were given a fee after hearing and judging. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 511}

**The view: the taghut is Ka'b b. al-Ashraf (p. 511, reports 9897-9898).** Others said the taghut here is Ka'b b. al-Ashraf. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9897, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) reports from Ibn Abbas that the taghut was a Jew called Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, and when they were called to what God sent down and to the Messenger for judgement they said they would rather go to Ka'b. The next report is printed as 9798, which the sequence corrects to 9898; Muhammad b. Amr (from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that a hypocrite and a man disputed, and it continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 511}

**The end of report 9898 and reports 9899-9902 (pp. 512-513, note 1).** The rest of Mujahid's report (9898): the hypocrite said, come with us to Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, and the Jew said, come with us to the Prophet; and the verse and the one after it came down about them also. The editor says he could not make out the sense of the manuscript's wording here and that the print's is nearer the mark. Al-Muthanna (9899, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid the same, except that the Jew said, come with us to Muhammad; al-Muthanna (9900, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father) from al-Rabi b. Anas that two of the Prophet's Companions had a dispute, one a believer and one a hypocrite; the believer called the other to the Prophet and the hypocrite called him to Ka'b b. al-Ashraf, and the next verse came down ([[passage/quran-4-61|4:61]]). Al-Qasim (9901, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Mujahid that a believer and a Jew disputed; the Jew called to Ka'b and the believer to the Prophet. Ibn Jurayj says that what was sent down to him means the Qur'an and what was before him the Torah, that it happens between a Muslim and a hypocrite over a right, the Muslim calling the other to the Prophet and the hypocrite refusing and calling to the taghut, and that Mujahid said the taghut is Ka'b b. al-Ashraf. Al-Tabari gives (9902, from something told to him from al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) that al-Dahhak said it is Ka'b b. al-Ashraf. Al-Tabari says he explained the meaning of taghut elsewhere and does not repeat it; the editor refers to p. 507 note 1. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 513}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 513}
