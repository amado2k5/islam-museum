## Summary
Verse 155 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 155 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says the sense is: then because of these People of the Book's breaking of their covenant, that is, the pledges they made to God to act on what is in the Torah; and their disbelief in God's signs, that is, their denial of His proofs by which He argued against them for the truth of His prophets and messengers; and their killing of the prophets without right after the proof had been set against them, with no sin deserving death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 363}

Their saying 'our hearts are wrapped' means they say there is a covering and wrapping over them against what you call us to, so we do not understand what you say; al-Tabari has explained the sense of 'wrapped' and the reports on it earlier. 'God sealed them for their disbelief' means that they lied in saying our hearts are wrapped; they are not wrapped, nor have they coverings, but God set a seal upon them for their disbelief in Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 363}

Al-Tabari says that 'they believe only a little' means that those whose condition God has described, because He has sealed their hearts, will believe only a little, since they did not affirm what God commanded them; they affirmed some prophets and some books and denied others, while the books and the messengers confirm one another. Report 10774, from Qatada: then for their breaking of the covenant We cursed them; their saying our hearts are wrapped, that is, we do not understand, but God sealed them for their disbelief and cursed them when they did that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 364}

The commentators differ on whether 'then for their breaking' is joined to what comes before or detached from it. Some say it is detached, meaning: for their breaking of the covenant, their disbelief in God's signs, their killing of the prophets, and their saying our hearts are wrapped, God sealed them for their disbelief and cursed them. Report 10775, from Qatada, says that when they abandoned God's command, killed His messengers, disbelieved His signs and broke the covenant, God sealed their hearts for their disbelief and cursed them. Others join it to what precedes: the thunderbolt took them for their wrongdoing, and for their breaking of the covenant and their disbelief and killing of the prophets, the thunderbolt took them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 365}

Al-Tabari prefers that 'then for their breaking of the covenant' is detached, its sense being: for their breaking of the covenant and their disbelief in God's signs and their killing of prophets, and their saying, We cursed them and were angry with them; the cursing is left out because 'God has sealed their hearts for their disbelief' indicates it, for whoever has his heart sealed is cursed and has earned wrath. He argues that those struck by the thunderbolt were in Moses' time, while those who killed the prophets and slandered Mary lived long after him, so the thunderbolt cannot be their punishment for these. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 366}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
