## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "kill them wherever you find them, and expel them from where they expelled you", al-Tabari says: kill, believers, those of the polytheists who fight you wherever you reach their fighting-place and are able to kill them, which is the meaning of "wherever you find them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 564} He explains that thaqafa in a matter is skill and insight in it; it is said "he is thaqif laqif" of one who is expert at caution in fighting and sees where the killing-places are, while tathqif means straightening; so the meaning is kill them in whatever place you can and see their vital points. The editor says he found this verbal noun in no dictionary and vowelled it with a kasra on the tha'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 564} "And expel them from where they expelled you" refers to the emigrants who were expelled from their homes in Mecca; God told them to expel those who fight them, who had expelled them from their homes, from their dwellings as they expelled them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 565}

On "persecution (fitna) is worse than killing", al-Tabari says associating partners with God is worse than killing; the root of fitna is trial and testing, so the meaning is that the trial of a believer in his religion until he returns from it and becomes a polytheist after his Islam is harder on him and more harmful than being killed while holding to his religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 565} From Mujahid, a believer's reversion to idolatry is worse for him than killing. From Qatada, shirk is worse than killing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 565}

From Qatada and al-Rabi' on "persecution is worse than killing": shirk is worse than killing; from al-Dahhak and Mujahid, fitna is shirk; from Ibn Zayd, the fitna of unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 566}

On "and do not fight them at the Sacred Mosque until they fight you in it; but if they fight you, kill them; such is the recompense of the unbelievers", al-Tabari records a difference in reading. The majority of the readers of Medina and Mecca read "wa la tuqatiluhum ... hatta yuqatilukum", meaning do not begin fighting the polytheists at the Sacred Mosque until they begin it, and if they begin it there, kill them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 566} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 567} From Qatada, they were not fought there until fighting was begun, then this was abrogated by "and fight them until there is no persecution", and in another report from him God abrogated it by "when the sacred months have passed, kill the polytheists wherever you find them"; al-Rabi' has the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 567} Others said this verse is muhkam and not abrogated; from Mujahid, do not fight anyone in it, and whoever attacks you and fights you, fight him as he fights you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 567} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 568} Most readers of Kufa read "wa la taqtuluhum ... hatta yaqtulukum ... fa-in qatalukum", meaning do not begin killing them until they begin it; Hamza al-Zayyat asked al-A'mash how they could kill them if they had killed them, and he answered that when a man of the Arabs is killed they say "we were killed". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 568} The editor cites the entry for 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Hammad in the Tabaqat al-qurra' and judges "Abu Hammad" in the chain to be a copyists' error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 568}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: the sounder of the two readings is "wa la tuqatiluhum ... fa-in qatalukum", because God did not command His Prophet and companions, when the polytheists fought them, to submit to them until they killed one of them, so it is known that He allowed them to fight when the polytheists began, before and after they killed one of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 568} He holds that God abrogated this verse by "and fight them until there is no persecution", by "kill the polytheists wherever you find them" and similar verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 568} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 569} From Qatada, it was abrogated by "kill the polytheists wherever you find them". From Ibn Zayd, "until they begin it": this had been forbidden and then God made it lawful to him, and it stayed so until God commanded him to fight them afterwards. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 569}
