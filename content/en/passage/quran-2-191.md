## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "kill them wherever you find them, and expel them from where they expelled you", al-Tabari says: kill, believers, those of the polytheists who fight you wherever you reach their fighting-place and are able to kill them, which is the meaning of "wherever you find them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 564} He explains that thaqafa in a matter is skill and insight in it; it is said "he is thaqif laqif" of one who is expert at caution in fighting and sees where the killing-places are, while tathqif means straightening; so the meaning is kill them in whatever place you can and see their vital points. The editor says he found this verbal noun in no dictionary and vowelled it with a kasra on the tha'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 564} "And expel them from where they expelled you" refers to the emigrants who were expelled from their homes in Mecca; God told them to expel those who fight them, who had expelled them from their homes, from their dwellings as they expelled them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 565}

On "persecution (fitna) is worse than killing", al-Tabari says associating partners with God is worse than killing; the root of fitna is trial and testing, so the meaning is that the trial of a believer in his religion until he returns from it and becomes a polytheist after his Islam is harder on him and more harmful than being killed while holding to his religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 565} From Mujahid, a believer's reversion to idolatry is worse for him than killing. From Qatada, shirk is worse than killing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 565}
