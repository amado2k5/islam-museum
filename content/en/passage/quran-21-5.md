## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "Rather, they said, 'Foolish dreams, but I invented him.' Rather, he is a poet. So let him bring us a sign as the ancients were sent": they did not believe in the wisdom of this Qur'an, nor did they acknowledge that it was a revelation revealed by Allah to Muhammad. Some said: It is a dream he saw in a dream. Some said: It is a fabrication and fabrication he fabricated and fabricated by himself. Some said: Rather, Muhammad is a poet, and this is poetry that he brought to you. "Let him come to us," he says: They said, 'Let Muhammad come to us if he is truthful in saying that God sent him as a messenger to us, and that this is what is recited to us by a revelation from God revealed to us,' with a "verse" that says: as proof and indication of the truth of what he says and claims, "as the ancients were sent," meaning: as the first messengers before him brought it, such as reviving the dead, healing the blind and lepers, and the she-camel of Salih, and similar miracles that only God can perform and only the prophets and messengers can bring. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Jurayj.
