## Summary
Verse 16 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 16 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Whom the pronoun refers to, and the meaning of the dual (p. 81).** Abu Jafar says the dual means the man and the woman who both commit the indecency, and the pronoun of the act refers to the indecency of the preceding verse; the sense is: the two among you who commit the indecency, so harm them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 81}

**First view: the two unmarried (pp. 81-82, reports 8812-8813).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed over whom it means. Some said it is the two unmarried who had not been married, and that they are other than the women meant by the preceding verse; the preceding verse, they said, concerns the previously married women, wives, and this one the unmarried two. Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8812, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that he mentioned the girls and young men who had not married, and Yunus (8813, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that they are the two unmarried. The editor notes that the printing has the nominative form for "the two unmarried", as if objecting to the manuscript's, and that the manuscript's form, which he kept, is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 81} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 82}

**Second view: two men; third view: a man and a woman (pp. 82-83, reports 8814-8818).** Others said it means two male fornicators. Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i (8814, from Yahya from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Mujahid that they are two men who do it, and the report adds a remark that they are not hinted at (as the page text has it); Muhammad ibn Amr (8815, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that they are the two fornicators. Others said it means a man and a woman, without singling out a virgin rather than a previously married. Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i (8816, from Yahya from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ata that it is the man and the woman, and Muhammad ibn Humayd (8817, from Yahya ibn Wadih from al-Husayn from Yazid al-Nahwi) reports from Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri that God mentioned women in the preceding verse up to the making of a path, then mentioned the man after the woman, and then joined the two in this verse, with the ruling of harm, then turning away from them if they repent and mend their ways, and that God receives repentance and is merciful to them. Al-Qasim (8818, from al-Husayn from Hajjaj from Ibn Jurayj) reports that Ata and Abd Allah ibn Kathir said this is for the man and the woman together. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 82} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 83}

**Al-Tabari's choice and argument (pp. 83-84).** Abu Jafar says the soundest view is that of those who said it means the two unmarried who fornicated, one being a man and the other a woman. For if it had been meant to give the ruling on male fornicators, as the preceding verse was meant to give the ruling on female ones, it would have been said in the masculine plural or the singular, just as the preceding verse used the feminine plural and not the dual. So the Arabs do when they mean to state a threat or a promise for an act: they bring the names of its doers in the plural or the singular, since the singular indicates its kind, and not in the dual; one says "those who do such, for them such" and "who does such, for him such", and not "the two who do such, for the two such", unless it is an act that occurs only between two different persons, like fornication, which occurs only by a fornicator man and a fornicator woman; then the dual is used to mean the one who acts and the one acted upon. As for using the dual of two persons in an act that each may do alone, or in which they are not partners, that is not known in their speech. So the error of the view that it means two men is evident, and the soundness of the view that it means a man and a woman (the editor notes that "the soundness" is joined to "the error" in the nominative). He then says they are plainly not the women whose ruling was explained in the preceding verse, since these are two and those are a group; and that confinement was for the previously married women as a punishment until they die, before a path was made for them, since it is heavier a punishment than the harm that is rebuke, reproach, insult and censure, just as the path made for them, stoning, is heavier than the path made for the unmarried, a hundred lashes and a year's banishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 83} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 84}

**What the harm was (pp. 84-86, reports 8819-8822).** Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed over the harm God made the punishment of the two doers before making a path for them. Some said it was harm by word and tongue, like censure and reproach for what they did: Bishr ibn Mu'adh (8819, from Yazid from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that the two were harmed by words together, and Muhammad ibn al-Husayn (8820, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the girl and the young man, when they fornicated, were chided and rebuked until they stopped. Others said it was harm by the tongue but as insult: Muhammad ibn Amr (8821, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that it was insult. Others said it was harm by tongue and hand: al-Muthanna (8822, from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that a man who fornicated was harmed by reproach and struck with sandals. Abu Jafar then says the soundest view is that God commanded the believers to harm the two fornicators mentioned when they did it and they were Muslims; that harm is applied to anything disagreeable that befalls a person, bad speech with the tongue or an act; that the verse does not say which of these the believers were commanded to do then, nor is there a report from the Messenger of God by one transmitter or a group such that it cuts off excuse; that the interpreters differ, and it may have been harm by tongue or by hand or by both; and that there is no benefit in religion or this world in knowing which, nor harm in not knowing, since God abrogated it from His firm text by the ruling He obliged on His servants concerning them and the women before them: as for what He obliged concerning them, it is what is in [[passage/quran-24-2|sura 24]], a flogging of a hundred for each of them; as for what He obliged concerning the women before them, it is stoning, which the Messenger of God decided concerning them. All the interpreters agree that God made for those who commit the indecency, male and female fornicators, a path through the hadd penalties He ruled on them. The editor's notes: the printing has a different preposition in "may befall every disagreeable thing" and the manuscript's is right, the verb meaning here "come", "be put" or "descend" in usage; elsewhere the printing has "statement that that was", an error, the manuscript being right; the printing has "harm by tongue and hand" and the manuscript "harm by both", of which he judged the latter right; and the expression "there is no benefit in knowing which it was" is wrongly written in both and he gave the right form, an expression that recurs. These views and reports are given as transmitted, the choices are al-Tabari's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 84} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 85} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 86}

**The view that the verse of flogging abrogated the harm (p. 86, reports 8823-8825).** Abu Jafar records that a group of interpreters said God abrogated the ruling of harm by the verse of flogging in sura 24. Muhammad ibn Amr (8823, from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that all of that was abrogated by the verse in sura 24 with the prescribed hadd; Abu Hisham (8824, from Yahya from Ibn Jurayj) reports the same from Mujahid; and Ibn Humayd (8825, from Abu Tumayla from al-Husayn ibn Waqid from Yazid al-Nahwi) reports from Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri that this was abrogated by the verse of flogging, and then begins to give that verse; the report is cut off at the end of the page. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 86 in the commentary on verse 16 of sura 4, in report 8825 on the abrogation of the harm by the verse of flogging, which is cut off at the end of the page, and continue from p. 87. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 86}
