## Summary
Verse 36 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 36 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 36 (pp. 234-242)**

Al-Tabari says: the number of the months of the year with God is twelve in God's book, in which He wrote all that is to be in His decree, "on the day He created the heavens and the earth, of which four are sacred", that is among these twelve months four sacred months which the pre-Islamic people revered, held sacred and forbade fighting in, so that if a man met his father's killer in them he would not molest him, namely Rajab of Mudar and three in a row, Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja and Muharram, the reports from the Messenger agreeing on this. Report 16684, from Ibn Umar, through Musa ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Masruqi, Zayd ibn Hubab, Musa ibn Ubayda al-Rabadhi and Sadaqa ibn Yasar: the Messenger preached in the farewell pilgrimage at Mina in the middle of the days of drying meat, saying: people, time has come round to the form it had on the day God created the heavens and the earth, and the number of months with God is twelve, of which four are sacred, the first being Rajab of Mudar between Jumada and Sha'ban, then Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja and Muharram. The editor says Musa ibn Ubayda is very weak and rejected in hadith, so the chain is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 235}

Report 16685, from Abu Hurayra, through Muhammad ibn Ma'mar, Rawh, Ash'ath and Ibn Sirin: time has come round to the form it had the day God created the heavens and the earth, and the number of months is twelve, of which four are sacred, three in a row and Rajab of Mudar between Jumada and Sha'ban; the editor says Ibn Kathir quotes it and al-Bazzar gives it. Report 16686, from Abu Bakra, through Ya'qub, Isma'il ibn Ibrahim, Ayyub and Ibn Sirin: the Prophet preached in the farewell pilgrimage saying that time has come round to the form it had on the day God created the heavens and the earth, the year being twelve months, four of them sacred, three in a row, Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja and Muharram, and Rajab of Mudar between Jumada and Sha'ban. The editor explains that the chain is broken because Ibn Sirin did not hear from Abu Bakra, that al-Bukhari, Muslim and Ahmad connected it, and that the report is sound and agreed on. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 235}

Report 16687, from a man in Bahrain, through Mujahid ibn Musa, Yazid and Sulayman al-Taymi: the Messenger said much the same in his sermon in the farewell pilgrimage; report 16688, from Ibn Abi Najih: the Prophet said three in a row, Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja and Muharram, and Rajab between Jumada and Sha'ban; report 16689, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was told that God's prophet said the same in his sermon on the day of Mina. This is the view of most interpreters. Report 16690, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the four sacred are Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram and Rajab, and God's book is what is with Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 236}

Report 16691, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: by it the matter of intercalation is known, what fell short of the year; report 16692, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: by it the matter of intercalation is recalled. As for "that is the right religion", it means that what He told you, that the number of months with God is twelve in God's book and four of them sacred, is the straight religion. Report 16693, from al-Suddi: the straight; report 16694, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the right matter. Al-Tabari says: know, people, that the number of months with God is twelve in God's book in which He wrote all that is to be, and that four of these twelve are sacred months; that is God's straight religion, not what the intercalator does in declaring lawful what he makes lawful of the months of the year and forbidding what he forbids. The editor discusses the form of the word for intercalation in the manuscript and refers to its later explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 237}

As for "so do not wrong yourselves in them", it means do not disobey God in them and do not treat as lawful in them what God forbade you, earning for yourselves God's anger and punishment which you cannot bear, as in report 16695, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: wronging is acting in disobedience to God and leaving obedience to Him. The interpreters then differed over what the pronoun in "in them" refers to. Some said it refers to the twelve months and means do not wrong yourselves in any month. Report 16696, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "do not wrong yourselves in them" means in all of them; then He singled out four months, made them sacred and magnified their sanctity, making sin in them greater and good deeds and reward greater; report 16697, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki, Suwayd ibn Amr, Hammad ibn Salama, Ali ibn Zayd and Yusuf ibn Mihran: in all the months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 238}

Others said the sense is do not wrong yourselves in the four sacred months, the pronoun referring to the four months. Report 16698, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: wrongdoing in the sacred months is a greater sin and burden than wrongdoing in the others, although wrongdoing is always grave, for God magnifies of His affair what He wills; He said that God chose some of His creation, choosing messengers from the angels and from people, choosing His remembrance from speech, the mosques from the earth, Ramadan and the sacred months from the months, Friday from the days and the Night of Power from the nights; so magnify what God magnified, for matters are magnified, among people of understanding and sense, by what God has magnified. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 239}

Others said the sense is do not wrong yourselves by making the forbidden of the four months lawful and their lawful forbidden. Report 16699, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: do not make their forbidden lawful nor their lawful forbidden, as the polytheists did, for the intercalation they practised was an increase in unbelief by which the unbelievers are led astray; the editor refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. Reports 16700 to 16702, from al-Hasan and al-Hasan ibn Muhammad ibn Ali, through Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Qays ibn Muslim, al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Ahmad ibn Ishaq and Abu Ahmad: wronging yourselves is not holding them sacred as their sanctity requires. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 239}

Al-Tabari then says the soundest view is that of those who said do not wrong yourselves in the four months by treating their forbidden as lawful, since God magnified them and magnified their sanctity. His reason is the word "in them", the pronoun being that for a count between three and ten: the Arabs say for a count between three and ten, "we did that three nights past" and "four days remaining", and for what is above ten up to twenty "thirteen nights past" and "fourteen gone", so that the pronoun's form for the months God forbade the believers to wrong themselves in is the form of the few-plural from three to ten, which clearly shows that the pronoun refers to the four months and not the twelve; had it referred to the twelve it would have been the other form, "in them" in the plural of the many. The editor refers to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 240}

If someone objects that it may refer to the twelve, although what he mentions is the well-known usage, since it is also known that the Arabs may use the feminine singular suffix without the nun for a count of three to ten, as a poet, Amr ibn Bulja' al-Tamimi, said of camels that stayed seven nights at Qarh unfed, using the singular suffix and not the plural for the seven, the answer is that though it is permissible, it is not the most eloquent and best-known usage, and directing God's speech to the most eloquent and best-known is better than directing it to the less usual. If he objects that then it must be allowed to wrong ourselves in the other months, the answer is that it is not so, for it is forbidden in all times, but God magnified the sanctity of these months and honoured them above the rest of the year's months, so He singled out the sin in them for magnification as He singled them out for honour, which is like His words at [[passage/quran-2-238|2:238]] "guard the prayers and the middle prayer": God has ordered us to guard all the obligatory prayers and has not allowed neglect of them by ordering us to guard the middle one, but He added to its dignity and emphasis on guarding it and severity in neglecting it, and so it is here. The editor gives the sources of the line and says Qarh is the market of Wadi al-Qura. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 241}

As for "and fight the polytheists all together as they fight you all together", He says fight the polytheists in God, believers, all together without disagreeing, united and not divided, as the polytheists fight you all together, gathered and not split. Report 16703, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: "all together" means everyone and your affair united; report 16704, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: together; report 16705, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: together. The word is in every case in one form, neither masculine nor plural, for though it has the form of an agent noun it has the sense of a verbal noun like the words for well-being and outcome, and the Arabs do not put the article on it. As for "know that God is with the pious", it means that if believers fight the polytheists all together, fear God and obey Him in what He commanded and forbade without going against His command, God will be with them against their enemy and His, and one with whom God is will not be overcome by anything, since God is with whoever fears Him and obeys Him in what he is charged with of His command and prohibition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 242}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
