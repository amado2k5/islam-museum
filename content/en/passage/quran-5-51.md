**Commentary on verse 51: "Believers, do not take the Jews and Christians as allies; they are allies of one another", pp. 395-401**

Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed about whom this verse concerns, although all believers are commanded by it. Some said Ubada ibn al-Samit and 'Abd Allah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul: Ubada disowned his pact with the Jews, while Ibn Ubayy clung to it after their enmity to God and His messenger had shown, and God told him that whoever takes them as allies is one of them. Report 12156, from 'Atiyya ibn Sa'd, says Ubada came to the Prophet and said he had many Jewish clients and he disowned their alliance before God and His messenger; Ibn Ubayy said he was a man who feared reversals and would not disown his clients. The Prophet told Ibn Ubayy that whatever he held back of Jewish alliance from Ubada was now his alone, and he said he accepted; the verse came down to the words "you see those in whose hearts is sickness".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 395}

Report 12157, from al-Zuhri, says that after the defeat at Badr the Muslims told their Jewish allies to believe before God struck them with a day like Badr, and Malik ibn Sayf answered that they were fooled by meeting a few Quraysh who knew nothing of war, and that had the Jews agreed to gather against them the Muslims could not have fought them. Ubada disowned the alliance and Ibn Ubayy said he could not do without it; the exchange with the Prophet repeated and the verses came down up to "God protects you from people". The editor says 'Uthman ibn 'Abd al-Rahman al-Zuhri in the chain is weak and his reports are discarded; a note explains the phrase about making a resolve firm.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 396}

Report 12158, from 'Ubada ibn al-Walid ibn 'Ubada ibn al-Samit, says that when the Banu Qaynuqa' fought the Prophet, Ibn Ubayy took their side and stood up for them, and 'Ubada, who had the same pact with them, went to the Prophet, threw off the alliance and disowned it before God and His messenger; the verses of al-Ma'ida came down about him and Ibn Ubayy.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 397}

Others said believers are meant who, when they suffered what they did at Uhud, thought of taking from the Jews a pact to protect them, and God forbade them and told them whoever does so is one of them. Report 12159, from al-Suddi, says that after Uhud a group were gripped by fear that the unbelievers would prevail; one man told his friend he would go to a certain Jew, take a guarantee and become a Jew with him, and the other said he would go to a Christian in Syria and become a Christian; God sent the verse forbidding them. The editor could not find the Jew's name in the books and fears it is garbled.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 397}

Others said Abu Lubaba ibn 'Abd al-Mundhir is meant, for telling Banu Qurayza, when they accepted Sa'd's ruling, that it meant slaughter. Report 12160, from 'Ikrima, says the Prophet sent Abu Lubaba of the Aws to Qurayza when they broke the pact, and when they agreed to come down he gestured to his throat: slaughter.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 398}

Al-Tabari says the right view is that God forbade all believers to take Jews and Christians as supporters and allies against those who believe in God and His messenger and others, and told that whoever takes them as helper, ally and friend instead of God, His messenger and the believers is one of them in banding against God, His messenger and the believers, and that God and His messenger are free of him. The verse may have come down about 'Ubada, Ibn Ubayy and their Jewish allies, or about Abu Lubaba, or about the two men al-Suddi mentions; but no report on any of the three is sound enough to be a proof.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 398}

So he judges that the plain wording should be taken as general, allowing what the interpreters said where nothing is known against it, though without doubt the verse came down about a hypocrite who allied himself with Jews or Christians for fear of the turns of time, as the next verse, "you see those in whose hearts is sickness hurrying toward them", shows. "They are allies of one another" means Jews support one another against the believers as one hand, and so do the Christians against those who differ from their religion; this tells the believers that whoever allies with them is their ally against believers who differ with their creed. So let the believers be allies of one another and at war with the Jew and the Christian as these are at war with them, for whoever allies with them has declared war on the people of faith and cut his ties with them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 399}

On "whoever among you takes them as allies is one of them" al-Tabari says that whoever allies with the Jews and Christians instead of the believers and helps them against the believers is of their religion, since nobody befriends another unless he is content with him and his religion, and one who is content with a religion has become the enemy of what opposes it and takes its ruling. For this reason scholars ruled that the Christians of Banu Taghlib have, in their slaughtered meat, marriage and other matters, the rulings of the Christians of the Children of Israel, because they ally with them, accept their creed and support them in it, though their lineages differ. He sees in this proof that everyone who professes a religion has the ruling of that religion's people, whether before Islam or after, except a Muslim who moves to another creed, who is not left in it but put to death for apostasy unless he returns before it; and he rejects the claim that the ruling of the two Books holds only for an Israelite or one who joined before the Qur'an came down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 400}

He gives reports 12161 to 12164 for this: Ibn 'Abbas was asked about the slaughtered meat of the Arab Christians and recited the verse (12161); it concerns slaughtered meat, whoever enters a people's religion is one of them (12162); eat the slaughter of Banu Taghlib and marry their women, since if they were of them only by alliance they would still be of them (12163); al-Hasan saw no harm in the slaughter of Arab Christians or marrying their women and recited the verse (12164). The notes identify al-Ru'asi, Humayd ibn 'Abd al-Rahman, as reliable, and correct the name Husayn ibn 'Ali al-Ju'fi.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 401}

We stop here at the foot of p. 401, at the start of report 12165 (Ibn al-Mubarak), after al-Hasan on the slaughtered meat of the Arab Christians; the rest of verse 51 follows in the next batch, from p. 402.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
