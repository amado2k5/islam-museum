**Commentary on verse 113 (pp. 24-26)**

On "the sorcerers came to Pharaoh, saying, we shall have a reward" he says: a recompense for our overcoming Moses before you, "if we are the victors". The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "reward". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 24}

Report 14931, from Ibn Abbas through al-Abbas, Yazid, al-Asbagh ibn Zayd, al-Qasim ibn Abi Ayyub and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: every learned sorcerer was gathered for Pharaoh, and when they came they asked, how does this sorcerer work? They were told: with snakes. They said: no people on earth know more than we about sorcery with snakes, ropes and staffs, so what will be our reward if we win? He said: you are my kin and my close ones, and I will do for you whatever you wish. The editor notes manuscript and printed variants in some words and glosses "close kin". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 24}

Report 14932, from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim ibn al-Haytham, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: Pharaoh said we shall not contend with Moses except with someone of his own people; so he prepared learned men of the Children of Israel and sent them to a village in Egypt called al-Farama, where the sorcerers were taught sorcery as boys are taught writing in a school; they taught them a great deal. Moses then set a time with Pharaoh; when it came, Pharaoh brought the sorcerers and their teacher and asked him what he had done. He said he had taught them a sorcery that the sorcery of the people of earth could not match, unless it were a matter from heaven, against which they have no power. When the sorcerers came they asked Pharaoh whether they would have a reward if they won; he said yes, and you will then be among those brought near. The editor notes that the manuscript writes the wording of the verse in this form rather than as recited. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 25}

Report 14933, from al-Suddi through Musa ibn Harun, Amr and Asbat: Pharaoh sent summoners into the cities and they gathered the sorcerers; "a reward" means a gift given to us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 25}

Report 14934, from Ibn Ishaq through Ibn Humayd and Salama: "out-number him with sorcerers, perhaps you will find among them one who can bring the like of what he brought". Moses and Aaron had left him after showing him the authority of God that they showed. Pharaoh sent through his kingdom and left no sorcerer in his realm without bringing him; it is reported, and God knows best, that he gathered fifteen thousand. When they were assembled he told them: a sorcerer has come to us whose like we have never seen; if you beat him I will honour you, prefer you and bring you nearer than the people of my kingdom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 25}

They said: and shall we have that if we beat him? He said: yes. The editor notes that this is part of a long report that al-Tabari gives with the same chain in his History, and that he restored from the History what the manuscript dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 26}

Report 14935, from Ikrima through Ibn Humayd, Yahya ibn Wadih, al-Husayn and Yazid: the sorcerers were seventy; al-Tabari says he thinks Ikrima said "thousand". Report 14936, from Ibn al-Mundhir: the sorcerers were eighty thousand. Report 14937, from Ka'b through Ibn Waki', Jarir, Abd al-Aziz ibn Rufay', Khaythama and Abu Sawda: Pharaoh's sorcerers were twelve thousand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 26}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
