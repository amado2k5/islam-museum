## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 8 to 9 in a single passage. He explains His saying, "Indeed, We have placed chains around their necks, which are up to their necks, so they are wheated": We have made the oaths of these disbelievers chained around their necks, so they do not spread any good things. This is in Abdullah's recitation as mentioned: "Indeed, We have placed chains on their right hands, so they are up to the shoulders." And His saying: 'to the wrists' means: their oaths are bound by shackles around their necks, so it is a metaphor for oaths, and they are not mentioned to the listeners in the meaning of the speech. If the shackles are around the necks, they are only the hands of the shackles gathered to them, so it is sufficient to mention that the shackles around the necks are part of the oaths, as the poet said: 'And I do not know if I turn a face and desire good, which is the better I seek or the evil that does not come to me, so it is a metaphor for evil. The mention of good alone is for the knowledge of the hearer, and in the passage he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Qatadah.
