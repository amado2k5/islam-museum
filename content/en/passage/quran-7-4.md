## Summary
Verse 4 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 4 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "how many a town We destroyed, and Our might came to it by night or while they were napping", God tells His Prophet: warn these worshippers of others than Me, who equate gods and idols with Me, of My wrath lest I send My punishment on them and destroy them as I destroyed the nations before them who took their path; many a town whose people disobeyed Me, denied My messengers and worshipped others did I destroy; "Our might came to it by night" means our punishment and vengeance came to them at night before morning, "or while they were napping" means by day at the time of the midday rest. "How many" is said because the intent is to report how many exemplary punishments befell the earlier nations for denying His messengers and opposing Him, and so the Arabs do when they want to report a large number, as al-Farazdaq did in a line using "how many" for his many paternal and maternal aunts. The notes refer to the earlier commentary on "how many", "town", "destroying", "might" and "night attack". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 299}

If someone asks, God only reported that He destroyed towns; what in His report of destroying towns shows He destroyed their people? The answer is that towns are not called towns, nor a town a town, unless it has dwellings for its people and inhabitants from among them, so destroying it is destroying those in it. Some Arabists held that the speech is a report of the town while its people are meant. Al-Tabari says what he said is closer to the truth, being in agreement with the apparent recited text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 300}

If someone asks, how can it say "We destroyed it and Our might came", when no town perishes except by the coming of God's might and the descent of His vengeance and wrath on it? If the might came after its destruction, what sense is there in it coming to people already destroyed and gone, unaware of what befalls them? The answer is that there are two accounts of this, both sound. One is that the sense is: how many a town We destroyed by Our forsaking it from following the clear proofs and guidance We sent to it, and its choosing to follow the command of its patrons who led it astray from obeying its Lord, then Our might came to it when it did that, by night or while napping; so God's destruction of it is His forsaking it from obeying Him, and the coming of His might is requital for their disobedience of their Lord by His forsaking them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 300}

The other is that the destruction is the might itself, so mention of destruction shows mention of the coming of the might and mention of the coming of the might shows mention of destruction; and then it is the same to the Arabs whether one begins with destruction and joins might to it, or begins with might and joins destruction to it, as in "you visited me and honoured me", since the visit is the honour. Some Arabists claimed that something is omitted which, but for it, the speech would not be sound, and that the sense is: how many a town We destroyed, the coming of Our might to it being before Our destroying it, which is al-Farra's view; al-Tabari says there is no proof of its soundness in the apparent text or in a report that must be accepted, and a view without such proof is evidently unsound. Another said the "fa" here has the sense of "wa", which is meaningless, since "fa" has a ruling among the Arabs that "wa" lacks, and turning it to its commoner sense is better. If it is asked, how is "or" used when you know its commoner role is to express doubt, and doubt cannot be in God's report? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 301}

The answer is that the sense is other than you suppose: how many a town We destroyed, some of which Our might came to by night and some while napping. Had "wa" been put in place of "aw" the speech would have been nearly impossible, and the commoner sense would have been that the town God destroyed was visited by His might both at night and at midday, which would be a report that the might destroyed what was already destroyed and annihilated what was already annihilated, a bad kind of speech. The right form is what the revelation brought, since it did not separate the towns the might reached by night from those it reached at midday; had it separated them, it would have used "wa". It also said "Our might came to it" as a report about the town, running the speech on from how the verse began; had it said "came to them" it would have been sound and eloquent, returning the speech to its sense, since the might was aimed at the inhabitants and not the buildings, though the buildings were also ruined as the inhabitants were, and in "or while they were napping" it returned to the specific report about the inhabitants. Had it said "or while it was napping", it would also have been sound, since the hearers understood what was meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 302}

If someone asks, is "or while they were napping" not a report of the time of day when God's might came to them? The answer is yes. If he asks, are times in such speech not given among the Arabs with the "wa" that denotes a time? The answer is that though that is so, they sometimes drop it in such a place, finding it heavy to put two conjunctions together, since "aw" is among their conjunctions and so is "wa", so they say "you met me destitute or I am travelling", meaning "or and I am travelling", dropping the "wa" while intending it in the speech. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 303}
