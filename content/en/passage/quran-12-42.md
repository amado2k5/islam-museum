**Commentary on verse 42 (pp. 109-115)**

On "and he said to the one he thought would be saved: mention me to your lord" al-Tabari says Joseph told the one he knew would be saved of his two companions who had asked him to interpret: mention me to your master and tell him of the wrong done me, that I am jailed without a crime. Report 19303, from Ibn Ishaq: he said, meaning to Nabu, tell the great king of my wrong and my imprisonment for nothing; he said, I will. Report 19304, from Mujahid: to the one saved of the prison's two companions, Joseph says, mention me to the king. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "thinking" and "lord". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 109}

Report 19305, from Mujahid, is similar; report 19306, from Asbat: with the king of the land; report 19307, from Qatada: meaning the king; report 19308, from Mujahid. Report 19309, from Ibrahim al-Taymi: when he was brought to the prison door a companion of his said, your need, state your need; he said, my need is that you mention me to your lord, other than the Lord, Joseph said. The editor says he kept the manuscript's wording against the printed edition. Qatada took "thinking" here to mean thinking as opposed to certainty; report 19310, from him: interpreting dreams is by conjecture, and God makes true what He wills and voids what He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 110}

Al-Tabari says that what Qatada said, that interpreting dreams is conjecture, holds for those who are not prophets; as for prophets, it is not allowed that they report something as certain to occur and it does not, or as not to occur and it does, while testifying to the truth of what they report, since if that were allowed in their reports no such report of theirs could be trusted, and their proof against those to whom they were sent would fall. So it is clear that Joseph did not testify firmly to what he told the youths, and then confirm it with "the matter is decreed", unless he was certain it would surely come to pass; hence he told the one saved to mention him to his lord. This shows, he says, the error of Qatada's view. On "but Satan made him forget the mention of his Lord" this is a report from God of a lapse that befell Joseph through Satan, by which he forgot to mention his Lord, by whom, had he sought help, release would have come quickly; but he slipped and so his stay in prison grew long. Report 19311, from Malik ibn Dinar, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Ja'far ibn Sulayman al-Dubai' and Bistam ibn Muslim: when Joseph said to the cupbearer, mention me to your lord, it was said, Joseph, have you taken a guardian other than Me? I will lengthen your jail. Joseph wept and said, Lord, the abundance of trial made my heart forget, so I said a word; woe to my brothers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 111}

Report 19312, from Ikrima, through al-Hasan, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Uyayna and Amr ibn Dinar: the Messenger of God said, had he, meaning Joseph, not said the word he said, he would not have stayed in prison as long as he did. Report 19313, from al-Hasan: God's Prophet said, God have mercy on Joseph, had it not been for his word he would not have stayed in prison so long, meaning his words "mention me to your lord"; then al-Hasan would weep and say, when something befalls us we run to people. Report 19314, from al-Hasan, is similar, through Ya'qub, Ibn Ulayya and Abu Raja'. Report 19315, from Ibn Abbas: had Joseph not said the word he said he would not have stayed so long in prison, that is, when he sought relief from other than God. Report 19316, from Qatada: it reached me that the Prophet said, had Joseph not sought help against his Lord, he would not have stayed so long. The editor notes that Ibrahim ibn Yazid al-Khuzi al-Qurashi, in the chain of 19315, is abandoned, of denounced hadith and accused of lying, so the report is very weak in its chain; al-Haythami gave it at length in Majma' al-Zawa'id, saying al-Tabarani recorded it and that Ibrahim ibn Yazid al-Qurashi al-Makki is abandoned; al-Tabari recorded it in his History; and the other reports before and after it are mursal, with no proof in any of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 112}

Report 19317, from Qatada: we were told God's Prophet used to say, had Joseph not interceded against his Lord, he would not have stayed so long in prison, but he was only punished for interceding against his Lord. Reports 19318 to 19321, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ishaq, Abd Allah, Waraqa, al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: he said to him, mention me to your lord, and he did not mention him until the king saw the dream; Satan made Joseph forget his Lord and ordered him to mention the king and seek relief from him, so he stayed several years in prison for his words "mention me to your lord", and in one version as a penalty for his words. Muhammad ibn Ishaq used to say that it was rather the cupbearer whom Satan made forget to mention Joseph's affair to their king; report 19322, from him: when he, meaning the one he thought saved, got out, he was restored to his place and his master was pleased with him, and Satan made him forget to tell the king what Joseph had asked him to, so Joseph stayed afterwards several years in prison, a penalty from God for this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 113}

The interpreters differed over the length of the "bid'" that Joseph stayed in prison. Some said seven years; reports 19323 and 19324, from Qatada: Joseph stayed in prison seven years, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abu Uthma, Sa'id, Bishr and Yazid. Report 19325, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, through al-Hasan, Abd al-Razzaq and Imran Abu al-Hudhayl al-San'ani: Job was struck by trial for seven years, Joseph was left in prison seven years, and Nebuchadnezzar was punished and turned into a beast for seven years. Report 19326, from Ibn Jurayj: they claimed "bid'" is seven years, as Joseph stayed. The editor notes that Muhammad Abu Uthma is Muhammad ibn Khalid ibn Uthma, that Imran Abu al-Hudhayl is trustworthy and heard Wahb ibn Munabbih, that al-Tabari recorded it in his History, and that "turned into a beast" means transformed into one of the beasts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 114}

Others said "bid'" is between three and nine; reports 19327 and 19328, from Qatada and Mujahid, through Ibn Bashshar, Sulayman ibn Dawud al-Tayalisi, Abu Hilal al-Rasibi, Waki', Yahya ibn Adam, Isra'il and Mansur. Others said rather it is less than ten; report 19329, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. Al-Farra held that "bid'" is used only with ten and with the twenties up to the nineties, and is a surplus of three to nine, and said that this is what he saw the Arabs do; they do not say "a bid' and a hundred" or "a bid' and a thousand". Al-Tabari says the right account is that "bid'" runs from three to nine up to ten and is never less than three, and so too for what exceeds the decade up to a hundred, while above a hundred there is no "bid'". The editor corrects the wording "I heard Qatada" in the chain of 19327 and gives what the Lisan reports from Ibn Barri on al-Farra's saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 115}

This closes the commentary on verse 42 at the foot of p. 115; al-Tabari begins verse 43 on p. 116. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 115}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
