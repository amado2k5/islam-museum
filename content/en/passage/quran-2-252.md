## Summary
Verse 252 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 252 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari comments on ([[passage/quran-2-252|2:252]]): the pointing word refers to the passages in which God recounted the affair of those who left their homes by the thousand in fear of death, and the affair of the council of the Children of Israel who asked their prophet to send them a king, Talut, and what follows up to the close of ([[passage/quran-2-251|2:251]]); the word for signs here means His proofs, marks and evidences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 377} On the meaning of the address, he says that these proofs the Prophet was told of (God's power to cause those fleeing death to die in a single hour and then revive them; the kingship given to Talut over the Israelites when he had been a water-carrier or tanner and not of the royal house; its removal for his disobedience and its passing to David for his obedience; and the victory of Talut's men over Goliath and his troops despite their small number) are proofs against those of the two scriptures, Torah and Gospel, who deny the favour, knowing these hidden reports; the Prophet was unlettered and had read no books, so he could not be claimed to have learned them from their scrolls. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 377} Al-Tabari holds that God recites them to him in true certainty, as it was, without addition or distortion, and that being one of the messengers means that he is faithful in obeying God and preferring His pleasure over his own whim, following the way of the messengers before him, unlike Talut, whom his whim changed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 378} This is al-Tabari's reading and choice; that Talut was a water-carrier or tanner is among the transmitted reports given earlier, on which the museum does not rule. In the notes the editor explains the demonstrative usage, gives cross-references and corrects a manuscript reading; these are his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 377}
