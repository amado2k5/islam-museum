**Commentary on verse 46 (pp. 340-352)**

On [[passage/quran-11-46|11:46]] al-Tabari says: God said, Noah, the one you drowned and destroyed, whom you mention as of your family, is not of your family. The interpreters differed on the sense of 'he is not of your family': some said it means he is not of your offspring but of another, and said it came from wrongdoing; the editor explains 'hinth' as sin and disobedience and 'children of hinth' as children of fornication. Report 18208, from al-Hasan: he was not his son; report 18209, from Abu Ja'far: his wife's son; report 18210 begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 340}

Report 18210, from al-Hasan: no, by God, he was not his son; report 18211, from Abu Ja'far: 'Noah called to his son' is in the dialect of Tayyi', he was not his son, he was his wife's son; report 18212, from al-Hasan: he was not his son, and he used to read it 'he did a deed not righteous'; report 18213, from Qatada: I was with al-Hasan and he said, 'Noah called his son', by God's life he was not his son. I said, Abu Sa'id, God says 'Noah called to his son' and you say he was not his son? He said, what of His words 'he is not of your family'? I said, he is not of your family whom I promised to save with you, and the People of the Book do not differ that he was his son; he said, the People of the Book lie. Report 18214, from Qatada: I heard al-Hasan read 'he is not of your family, his is an act not righteous' and say at that, by God he was not his son, then he recited 'the two betrayed them' in [[passage/quran-66-10|66:10]]; Sa'id said I mentioned that to Qatada, who said he should not have sworn. Report 18215, from Mujahid on 'do not ask Me what you have no knowledge of': it became clear to Noah that he was not his son. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 341}

Reports 18216, 18217 and 18218, from Mujahid: God made clear to Noah that he was not his son; Ibn Jurayj said he called him thinking he was his son, and he had been born on his bed; report 18219, from Abu Ja'far: had he been of his family he would have been saved; report 18220, from Ubayd b. Umayr: we think that the Messenger of God's ruling 'the child belongs to the bed' was because of Noah's son; report 18221, from al-Hasan: no, by God, he was not his son. Others said the sense is: he is not of your family whom I promised to save. Report 18222, from Ibn Abbas on 'Noah called to his son': he said he was his son; report 18223, from him: he was his son, no prophet's wife ever committed adultery; report 18224, from him: no prophet's wife ever did, and 'he is not of your family' means of those I promised to save with you. The editor notes that Thuwayr is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 342}

Report 18225, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas: he was his son but differed from him in deed and intention, and in some readings Ikrima read 'he did a deed not righteous'; report 18226, from Ikrima: he was his son but differed from him in intention and deed, hence he was told he is not of your family; report 18227, from Sulayman b. Qatta: I heard Ibn Abbas, beside the Ka'ba, asked about 'the two betrayed them'; he said it was not by fornication, but this one told people he was mad and that one pointed out guests, then he recited 'a deed not righteous'; Ibn Uyayna said Ammar al-Duhni asked Sa'id b. Jubayr about it and he said he was Noah's son, God does not lie, 'Noah called to his son', and some scholars said no prophet's wife ever sinned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 343}

Report 18228, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: God, who speaks truth, said he is his son, 'Noah called to his son'; report 18229, from Ibn Abbas: no prophet's wife ever committed adultery; report 18230, from Abu Bishr on 'he is not of your family': not of the people of your religion and not of those I promised to save; report 18231, from Sa'id b. Jubayr to one who asked if he was his son: yes, by God, God's prophet ordered him to ride with him in the ship and he disobeyed and said 'I shall take refuge on a mountain'; 'Noah, he is not of your family, his is a deed not righteous' for disobeying God's prophet; report 18232, from him, to one who asked: he glorified God at length and said, there is no god but God, God tells Muhammad 'Noah called to his son' and you say he is not his? but he differed from him in deed and whoever does not believe is not of him; report 18233, from Ikrima: I bear witness he is his son, God said 'Noah called to his son'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 344}

Report 18234, from Mujahid and Ikrima: he is his son; report 18235, from al-Dahhak, to one who asked him about Noah's son: do you not wonder at this fool, asking me about Noah's son when he is Noah's son as God said; report 18236, from al-Dahhak: he is not of the people of your guardianship nor of those I promised to save of your family; 'a deed not righteous' means his deed was in polytheism; report 18237, from him: by God he is his son of his own loins; report 18238, from him: not of the people of your religion nor of those I promised to save, and he was his son of his loins; report 18239, from Ibn Abbas: not of those we promised salvation; report 18240, from al-Dahhak, likewise; report 18241, from Maymun and Thabit b. al-Hajjaj: he is his son, born on his bed. The editor notes the weakness of some narrators in 18235. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 345}

Al-Tabari says the soundest of the two views is that of those who said the sense is: he is not of your family whom I promised to save, because he differed from your religion and disbelieved in Me, and he was his son, since God told His prophet Muhammad that he was his son, saying 'Noah called to his son', and it is not allowed that He report he was his son and it be otherwise. In 'he is not of your family' there is no evidence that he was not his son, since it bears the sense we gave and also the sense 'he is not of the people of your religion', with 'religion' dropped and so 'not of your family', as in [[passage/quran-12-82|12:82]]. As for 'his is a deed not righteous', the readers differed. Most readers of the cities read 'amalun ghayru salih', with 'amal' in the nominative with tanwin and 'ghayru' nominative; they differed on the sense: some said it means this question of yours to Me is an act not righteous. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 346}

Report 18242, from Ibrahim: this question of yours is an act not righteous; report 18243, from Qatada: that is, evil, 'so do not ask Me what you have no knowledge of'; report 18244, from Ibn Abbas: your asking about what you have no knowledge of; report 18245, from Mujahid: your asking Me is an act not righteous. Others said rather the meaning is: the one you mentioned as your son, and asked Me to save, is an act not righteous, that is, he is not of true descent, and said the pronoun 'inna-hu' goes back to the son; report 18246, from al-Hasan, that he read 'amalun ghayru salih' and said by God he was not his son. It is reported of a group of the early generations that they read it 'inna-hu 'amila ghayra salih'; the commentary on the verse continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 347}

...as a report of the past verb, with 'ghayra' in the accusative; among those from whom this reading is reported is Ibn Abbas. Report 18247, from Sulayman b. Qatta, that Ibn Abbas read 'amila ghayra salih', and they directed its meaning to report 18248, from Ikrima from Ibn Abbas: he differed from him in intention and deed. Al-Tabari says he knows of none of the readers of the cities who read it so except some later ones, who argued from a report from the Messenger of God that he read it so, a report with an unsound chain: a hadith related from Shahr b. Hawshab, who sometimes says 'from Umm Salama' and sometimes 'from Asma' bt. Yazid', and he does not know a sound hearing by Shahr from Umm Salama. The editor discusses this hadith in a long note, listing who relates it in the Musnads and Sunan, saying its turning point is Thabit al-Bunani from Shahr, that Shahr was trusted by those who trusted him and was a reader, that al-Tabari's rejection of it for lack of hearing from the Mother of the Believers rests on nothing, that the difficulty remains in the mixing of the two reports, and mentioning the hadith of Aisha agreeing with it on this reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 350}

Al-Tabari says the right reading for him is what the readers of the cities hold, the nominative 'amalun' with tanwin and nominative 'ghayru', meaning: your asking Me what you ask regarding your son, who differed from your religion and sided with the people of polytheism against Me, for rescue from destruction, after My answer to your prayer against the disbelievers has gone before without excepting any of them, is an act not righteous, for it is a request from you that I not do what I had already said I would do in answering your request; that is the unrighteous act. His words 'so do not ask Me what you have no knowledge of' are God's forbidding His prophet Noah to ask Him the causes of His acts whose knowledge He has withheld from him and from other mortals. He says to him: Noah, I have told you of your question about the cause of My destroying your son, so after this do not ask Me what I have withheld the knowledge of from you, of the causes of My acts, which you do not know; 'I admonish you not to be among the ignorant' in your asking Me that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 351}

Ibn Zayd used to say on 'I admonish you not to be among the ignorant' what report 18249 gives: lest ignorance reach in you the point that I would not fulfil a promise I made you, such that you ask Me what you have no knowledge of; 'and if You do not forgive me and have mercy on me, I shall be among the losers'. The readers differed on 'do not ask Me': most readers of the cities read 'fa-la tas'alni' with kasra on the nun and light, pointing by the kasra to the ya' standing for God's name, and some Meccans and Syrians read 'fa-la tas'alanna' with doubled nun and fatha, meaning do not ask, Noah, what you have no knowledge of. Al-Tabari says the right reading for him is the light nun with kasra, because that is the eloquent speech of the Arabs in use among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 352}
