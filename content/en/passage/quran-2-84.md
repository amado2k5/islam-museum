## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "when We took your covenant: you shall not shed your blood nor drive yourselves out of your homes", al-Tabari says it is the same in sense and grammar as the earlier verse, "safk" meaning pouring out. To the question how they could kill themselves, he replies that the meaning is that they were forbidden to kill one another, a man's killing another of them being a killing of himself as their community was one; he cites the saying of the Prophet that believers are like one body, which the editor notes is in Sahih Muslim from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, and he also allows that it means the killer is put to death in retaliation and so has killed himself. He reports the same from Qatada. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 299} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 300}

Al-Tabari reports from Qatada on "you shall not shed your blood" that it means do not kill one another unjustly, and "nor drive yourselves out of your homes" that you shed the blood of the people of your own faith. On "then you affirmed" he says you affirmed the covenant, reported from Abu al-'Aliya and al-Rabi'. On "and you bear witness", the commentators differ about who is addressed: some say the Jews around the Prophet's place of Emigration, reproached for neglecting the Torah's rulings, with Ibn Abbas reporting it as witnessing that this was a right of the covenant; others say it reports their forebears in the form of an address, and Abu al-'Aliya says it means "you are witnesses". In his own choice al-Tabari holds it reports their forebears and includes the addressees who lived in the Prophet's time, since God did not single out some of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 300} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 301} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 302} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 303}
