## Summary
Verse 104 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 104 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Beginning of the commentary: the prohibition on weakness in seeking the people (pp. 170-171, notes 2-3 on p. 170 and notes 1-3 on p. 171).** The commentary on verse 104 begins on p. 170. Al-Tabari says the prohibition means do not weaken, from the saying "so-and-so weakened in this matter"; that seeking the people is looking for them and pursuing them, and that the people are the enemies of God and of the believers among the polytheists; that if the believers feel pain from the wounds they receive from them in this world, the polytheists feel the same from the believers' wounds and harm, and the believers hope from God a reward for what befalls them which the polytheists do not hope for what befalls them. So the believers, certain of God's reward in what the polytheists deny, are more worthy to be patient in fighting them than the polytheists are in fighting the believers, and to strive in seeking them to fight over what the polytheists weaken in and do not strive, so how much more over what they strive in without weakening. He says the interpreters said much the same. The editor's notes: references to his explanations of the word for weakness in volume 7 and of seeking earlier, and that the verbal noun he gives is not stated in most dictionaries; that "he felt pain" has several correct forms; that the printed text has "if you were certain", an error, and the clause between the dashes is parenthetical between subject and predicate; and that the printed text and manuscript are confused at the end of the passage because the manuscript is undotted, so he gave his reading, which makes the sense that they fight for a matter they pursue with an effort that has no weakness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 171}

**Reports 10400-10405 (pp. 171-172, notes 1-4 on p. 172).** Report 10400 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): do not weaken in seeking the people, for if you feel pain they feel pain as you do, and you hope from God a reward and recompense they do not hope for. Report 10401 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) is to the same effect. Report 10402 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid): do not weaken. Report 10403 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd Allah ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi'): do not weaken. Report 10404 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd): do not weaken in seeking them; if you suffer from the fighting they suffer as you do, and this is before wounds struck them, that is, if you dislike the fighting and feel its pain they feel pain like you, so do not weaken in seeking them at the place of fighting. Report 10405 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas): feeling pain means suffering. The editor notes that report 10403 is incomplete in the manuscript, because the page ended on a word of the verse and the copyist turned it and wrote the rest of the next report without its chain, and that he left the printed text, which is undoubtedly right; that the printed text adds the word "said" in 10404 and he kept the manuscript; that in 10401 the printed text adds a preposition where the manuscript is right; and that the reading of the word before "fighting" in 10404 differs between printed text and manuscript and he gave his reading, though the digital text of his note is damaged at this point. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 172}

**Reports 10406-10408: Uhud and the words of Abu Sufyan (pp. 172-173, notes 1-6 on p. 173).** Report 10406 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) says the word for feeling pain means that they suffer from what befalls them at the enemy's hands, while the enemy suffer the same, and the believers hope for a reward for what befalls them that the enemy do not hope for. Report 10407 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Hafs ibn Umar, from al-Hakam ibn Aban, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) tells of Uhud: when the fighting was over and the Muslims had suffered what they suffered, the Prophet went up the mountain, and Abu Sufyan came and called to him to come out, saying that war goes by turns, a day for them and a day for the Muslims. The Prophet told his companions to answer, and they said the two sides are not alike, since their dead are in Paradise and the enemy's dead in the Fire. Abu Sufyan then boasted of al-Uzza, and the Prophet told them to answer that God is their patron and the enemy have none; Abu Sufyan called on Hubal to be exalted, and the Prophet told them to answer that God is higher and more majestic. Abu Sufyan said their appointment was at Badr al-Sughra, and the Muslims slept with their wounds upon them. Ikrima added that the verse on the wounds of 3:140 was revealed about that day, and that this verse was revealed about the same people. Report 10408 (Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) says only that they suffer pain as the believers do. The editor's notes: that the printed text read a phrase about wounds that the undotted manuscript does not bear, and that the saying "war is turns" means once for one side and once for the other; that the printed text dropped the second "not alike" because the copyist had written and struck out something, and he restored it; that al-Uzza was an idol of Quraysh and Banu Kinana; that Hubal was an idol in the Kaaba, that he refers to an earlier discussion of its exaltation and criticises a mispointing of the verb; that the word for wounds is the plural of a word for a wound; and that this report is the same as an earlier report, number 7098, where the text is correct, and he corrected the faulty places from it and from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 173}

**Whether "hope" here means "fear" (pp. 174-175, notes 1-4 on p. 174).** Al-Tabari says it has been reported that some interpreted the clause about hoping from God what they do not hope as fearing from God what they do not fear, comparing 45:14, where those who do not hope for the days of God are understood as those who do not fear them. He answers that turning the word for hope to the sense of fear is not known in Arab speech except where it follows an earlier denial, as in 71:13, understood as not fearing a majesty for God; and he adduces two lines of poetry. One is by a reciter of rajaz verse, whose line has a speaker say that he does not fear when he meets the one who drives off the herd, whether seven or one. The other is by Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali, who describes a man collecting honey who is not afraid of the bees' sting once they sting him. Al-Tabari adds that the word is, as it reached him, a Hijazi usage, meaning "I do not mind" and "I take no heed". The editor's notes: the printed text has "we have mentioned of some of them", an error, and the manuscript's "it has been mentioned" is right; that the printed text called the first poet the "Hudhali poet" by carrying over the ascription of the Abu Dhu'ayb line, that he does not know who the reciter is and fears it may be Abu Muhammad al-Faq'asi; the references to al-Farra' in his commentary on the Qur'an (1:286), Ibn al-Anbari's book of opposite meanings (p. 9) and the dictionary Lisan al-Arab; the line is in the poet's diwan (p. 143) and is cited again in later places in the commentary, with a variant in the verb for stinging; and his explanation of the poem, which describes a honey-gatherer let down by ropes to the hives, trusted, skilled and the son of a skilled man, who goes in among the bees and does not fear their stinging, and of the words for the bees "returning" to their hive and "making honey". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 175} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 175}

**The closing clause of verse 104: knowing and wise (p. 175, notes 2-4).** Al-Tabari gives the sense that God has ever been knowing of the interests of His creatures and wise in His ordering and decree. Among His knowledge, believers, of what is good for you when the time of prayer comes and the duty of God is upon you while you face your enemy, is what brings you to perform God's obligation and keeps you safe from the enemy; and among His wisdom is that He sends you the means that strengthen you and weaken your enemy's scheme. The editor's notes refer to the language indexes for the explanations of the words "was", "knowing" and "wise", and say that the printed text has a form of "those who face you" that is wrong and adds a letter in the last sentence which the manuscript, the right text, does not have; his first note on this page is damaged in the digital text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 175} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 175}
