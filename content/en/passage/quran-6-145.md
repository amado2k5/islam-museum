## Summary
Verse 145 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 145 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 145, "say: I do not find in what was revealed to me": al-Tabari says God commands His prophet to say to those who gave God a share of the tillage and livestock He created and gave their partners the like, who said that some are fenced off and only whom they pleased may eat them, who forbade the backs of some livestock and left God's name off others, and who forbade part of what is in the bellies of their livestock to their females and wives while allowing it to their males: did a messenger come to you from God forbidding that, so tell us of it, or did He charge you with the prohibition face to face, so that you heard it from Him? You are liars if you claim it, and you cannot claim it, since people would know you lie. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 190}

"I find nothing forbidden to an eater in what was revealed to me of His book and verses" of what you say He forbade of these livestock whose prohibition you describe, "except carrion", that died without lawful slaughter; "or poured blood", the flowing kind; or the flesh of swine, which is foul; "or wickedness", meaning an animal slaughtered by an idol-worshipper for his idol and gods with its name mentioned. That slaughter is wickedness God forbade, forbidding believers to eat what is slaughtered so, because it is carrion. The editor's notes refer back to the earlier commentary on revelation and on eating. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 190}

Al-Tabari takes this as a notice to the idolaters who disputed with the Prophet and his companions about carrion, that what they argued over is the forbidden thing God forbade, that what they claimed God forbade is lawful, which He allowed, and that they are liars in ascribing its prohibition to God. He gives (14079) from Tawus that the people of the Jahiliyya forbade some things and allowed others, so God said: of what you forbid and allow I find only this; and (14080) in similar words, adding that some of the things they forbade are forbidden now. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 191}

He gives (14081) from Ibn Jurayj, from Tawus, that "an eater who eats it" means what is eaten, and that when asked whether this meant in the Jahiliyya he said yes; and from Mujahid that the sense is what they ate in the Jahiliyya: I find nothing of that forbidden to an eater except carrion or poured blood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 192}

He then explains "poured blood" as flowing, spilled blood; one says "I poured his blood" when one has spilled it. He supports the usage with a line of Tarafa ibn al-'Abd, about blood poured over the stone idols, and another of 'Abid ibn al-Abras, about women pouring tears after wailing. The editor's note says Tarafa's line is from three lines in the Diwan of the Six Jahili Poets with which he excused himself to 'Amr ibn Hind when word came that he had satirized him and the king threatened him; that 'Abid's line is in his own diwan, where the printed edition and the manuscript both corrupted a word and the diwan's reading is right; and that it comes from his poem rebuking his wife, who turned away from him when he aged and grayed, so he reminded her of his past in play, youth and war, including his spear thrust at a noble horseman; when women visit that wounded man they cry out in grief, then pour their tears because they have given up on his recovery. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 192}

The note on 'Abid's line is completed: "they poured" means they spilled and let tears run. Al-Tabari holds that God's naming only the "poured" blood when informing His servants of its prohibition is clear evidence that blood which is not poured is lawful and not impure. He gives (14082) from 'Ikrima: were it not for this verse, the Muslims would have hunted blood in the veins as the Jews did; and (14083) and (14084) in similar words. The editor's note sets out the grammatical run of the sentence on this condition. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 193}

He gives (14085) from Abu Mijlaz, on a cooking pot with redness on top from blood: God forbade only the poured blood; (14086) from him when the narrator asked about the blood that smears the place of slaughter on the head and about a pot in which redness is seen, and he said God forbade only the poured blood; and (14087) from Qatada: blood was forbidden so long as it was poured, while meat with blood mixed in is no harm. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 193}

He gives (14088) from Ibn Abbas that "poured blood" means spilled; (14089) from 'Ikrima: were it not for this verse, the Muslims would have hunted the veins of meat as the Jews did; (14090) from A'isha that she saw no harm in the flesh of predators, nor in the redness and blood on a pot, and she recited this verse; and (14091) from her by another chain that, recalling the verse, she said even a pot shows yellowness in its water. The editor's note says Ibn Kathir cites report 14090 and calls it sound but singular, that report 14091 is cut short, and that he mended its wording from the manuscript. Al-Tabari adds that he has already explained, earlier in the book, that "rijs" means filth, stench and whatever God is disobeyed by, so he does not repeat it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 194}

The same holds for "fisq" (wickedness) and for "that over which a name other than God's was invoked": both were covered earlier with sufficient evidence, and the editor points to the earlier places. He then reports the disagreement of the reciters on "unless it be carrion". Some in Medina, Kufa and Basra read "yakuna" with a ya and "mayta" with a light ya in the accusative, taking a veiled pronoun in "yakuna" with "mayta" as its predicate, and the verb is masculine because the pronoun is. Some in Mecca and Kufa read "takuna" with a ta and "mayta" light and accusative, with the same sense, feminising the verb for the feminine "carrion", as one says "she is standing, your slave girl" and "he is standing, your slave girl", making the pronoun masculine at one time and feminine at another according to the noun after it. Some in Medina read "takuna mayyatun" with a ta and a doubled ya and the noun in the nominative, so "carrion" is the subject of "takuna" and the verb is feminine for it; the verb stands complete with its subject alone because this is an exception, and the Arabs content themselves in an exception with nouns rather than verbs, as when they say "the people stood up unless it be your brother", with either case ending. The editor's note explains that "the verb" here means the predicate of the subject in the old Kufan usage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 195}

He goes on that the Arabs say "the people stood up unless your brother", in either case, so the noun after the particle of exception does not seek a verb. The editor refers to al-Farra''s Ma'ani al-Qur'an, and explains that he mended a garbled sentence, in both the printed text and the manuscript, taken from al-Farra' himself. Then he gives his choice: the right reading in his view is "yakuna" with a ya and "mayta" with a light ya in the accusative, because the pronoun in "yakuna" returns to a masculine, so the sense is: I find nothing forbidden to an eater unless that be carrion or poured blood. The nominative is no error in Arabic, but it is not the right reading in this verse, because God says "or poured blood" and none differ on the accusative of "blood", which is also how it stands in the Muslims' copies, joined to "carrion". Were "carrion" nominative, "blood" and "or wickedness" would be nominative too; since they are accusative, they are joined to it in the accusative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 196}

He takes up "but whoever is compelled, neither desiring nor exceeding, your Lord is forgiving, merciful", saying he has already given the disagreement of the commentators and his own choice on it in al-Baqara, so he need not repeat it. Its sense for him: whoever is compelled to eat what God forbade of carrion, poured blood, swine's flesh and what was dedicated to other than God, not desiring it in the eating for pleasure when there is no need of hunger, and not exceeding, by going beyond the limit God set and allowed him, so that he eats what repels the fear of perishing and no more, has no blame. "God is forgiving" for what he did, veiling it by leaving the punishment, though He could punish; "merciful" in permitting that eating at his need, though He could forbid and withhold it. The editor refers to the commentary on this verse in al-Baqara. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 197}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
