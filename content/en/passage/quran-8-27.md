## Summary
Verse 27 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 27 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 27 (pp. 480-486)**

Al-Tabari says on "you who believe, do not betray God and the Messenger, nor betray your trusts while you know" [[passage/quran-8-27|8:27]]: he says to the believers among his Companions, you who have believed God and His Messenger, do not betray God; their betrayal of God and the Messenger was by whoever of them showed the Messenger and the believers belief outwardly and sincere counsel while hiding unbelief and deceit inwardly, pointing the polytheists to their weak points and telling them what was hidden from them of their news. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "betrayal". The interpreters differed over whom it came down about and the occasion. Some said it came down about a hypocrite who wrote to Abu Sufyan telling him a secret of the Muslims; report 15922, from Jabir ibn Abd Allah: Abu Sufyan left Mecca and Gabriel came to the Prophet and told him where he was, and the Prophet told his companions to go out to him and keep it secret, and a man of the hypocrites wrote to Abu Sufyan that Muhammad was coming for you, so take care, and the verse came down. The editor dwells on Muhammad al-Muharram in this chain, lists the differing entries for him between Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Ubayd ibn Umayr and Muhammad ibn Umar al-Muharram, inclines to their being one man, says the report is very weak because he is abandoned in hadith, and notes that Ibn Kathir gave it and said it is very strange and its chain and wording are open to question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 480} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 481}

Others said it rather came down about Abu Lubaba, in what happened with him and Banu Qurayza; report 15923, from al-Zuhri: the Messenger of God sent him to them and he gestured to his throat that it was slaughter; then Abu Lubaba said: no, by God, I will taste neither food nor drink until I die or God accepts my repentance. He stayed seven days tasting neither food nor drink until he fell down fainting, then God accepted his repentance; he was told, Abu Lubaba, you have been forgiven; he said: by God, I will not untie myself until the Messenger of God himself unties me, and he came and untied him with his hand. Then Abu Lubaba said, part of my repentance is that I leave the home of my people where I committed the sin and strip myself of my property; he said, a third of it given in charity will suffice you. Report 15924, from Abd Allah ibn Abi Qatada: it came down about Abu Lubaba. The editor gives the story of Abu Lubaba ibn Abd al-Mundhir in Ibn Hisham's Sira, saying he tied himself to a pillar of the mosque, and notes that al-Wahidi gave it in Asbab al-Nuzul and that Malik gave part of it in the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 481} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 482}

Others said it came down rather about the matter of Uthman; report 15925, from al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba: this verse came down about the killing of Uthman. The editor weakens Yunus ibn al-Harith al-Ta'ifi in the chain, corrects the name of Abu Awn al-Thaqafi, judges the chain broken because al-Mughira died before the narrator could have met him, and explains "came down about the killing of Uthman" as meaning its ruling takes in what befell him, since for him the killing was a betrayal of God and the Messenger and of a trust, the killers having broken the pledge of allegiance on their necks; this is the editor's comment, and the museum gives no ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 482} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 483}

Abu Ja'far said: the best of the views is to say that God forbade the believers to betray Him, to betray His Messenger and to betray His trust; it may be that it came down about Abu Lubaba and may be about someone else, and we have no report which we must accept as sound about which it was, so the verse's meaning and interpretation are what we gave before. The interpreters spoke likewise; report 15926, from Ibn Zayd: He forbade you to betray God and the Messenger as the hypocrites did; report 15927, from al-Suddi: they would hear a saying from the Prophet and spread it until it reached the polytheists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 483}

Then they differed on "nor betray your trusts while you know". Some said: do not betray God and the Messenger, for that is a betrayal of your trust and its ruin; report 15928, from al-Suddi: when they betray God and the Messenger they have betrayed their trusts; report 15929, from Ibn Ishaq: do not show God of the truth what pleases Him in you then go against Him in secret to something else, for that is the ruin of your trusts and a betrayal of yourselves. Abu Ja'far said: on this interpretation "and betray" is in the accusative by way of diversion, as in a verse ascribed to al-Mutawakkil al-Laythi and to others, whose sense is that it is a great shame to forbid a trait and then do its like; the editor explains "diversion" and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani. Others said its sense is: do not betray God and the Messenger, and do not betray your trusts while you know; report 15930, from Ibn Abbas: do not betray them, meaning do not diminish them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 483} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 484} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 485}

Then the interpreters differed on the meaning of the trust God mentioned. Some said it is what God entrusted to servants of His obligations; report 15931, from Ibn Abbas: the trust is the works God entrusted to servants, meaning the obligation, and do not betray it meaning do not diminish it. At this point on p. 485 the scan is spoiled for the rest of the reports and notes, so we keep to what can be made out. Others said the trusts here are religion; report 15933, from Ibn Zayd: they are the hypocrites, who know they are unbelievers showing belief, and he recited [[passage/quran-4-142|4:142]], saying these hypocrites God and His Messenger trusted with His religion, and they betrayed, showing belief and hiding unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 485} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 486}

Abu Ja'far said: so the sense of the words is: you who believe, do not diminish for God His rights on you of His obligations, nor for His Messenger the obedience due to him from you, but obey them in what they commanded and forbade you and do not diminish them, "nor betray your trusts" and diminish your religion and the works that are due and binding on you, "while you know" they are binding on you, obligatory by the proofs God has established against you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 486}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
