## Summary
Verse 151 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 151 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Dread in the hearts of the deniers (p. 279).** Al-Tabari: God will cast, believers, into the hearts of those who disbelieved in their Lord and denied the prophethood of Muhammad, those who fought you at Uhud, dread, meaning fright and panic, because of their associating others with God, their worship of idols and their obedience to Satan, for which He gave them no proof; the proof is what He said He did not send down for their disbelief and idolatry. He reads this as a promise from God to the Companions of His Messenger of victory and ascendancy so long as they stayed true to His covenant and held to obedience to Him. Then He tells what He will do to their enemies after they return to Him: their destination on the Day of Resurrection is the Fire, and the dwelling of the wrongdoers is evil, those who wronged themselves by earning what made them deserve God's punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 279}

**Ibn Ishaq (8002).** God says: I will cast into the hearts of the deniers the dread by which I helped you against them, for their associating with Me what I gave them no proof for; so do not think they have an end of victory or ascendancy over you as long as you hold fast and follow My command, and know that the misfortune that struck you from them came through sins you sent ahead for yourselves, in which you went against My command and disobeyed the Prophet of God. The editor traces it to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 120), a continuation of 8001. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 279} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 280}

**Al-Suddi (8003).** When Abu Sufyan and the idolaters left Uhud for Mecca and had gone part of the way, they regretted it and said: you did badly, you killed them until only the fugitive was left and then you left them; go back and finish them. God cast dread into their hearts and they turned to flight. They met a Bedouin, offered him a payment, and told him to tell Muhammad, if he met him, of what they had gathered. God informed His Messenger, who went after them as far as Hamra' al-Asad, and the verse came down about this, mentioning Abu Sufyan when he wanted to return and the dread cast into his heart. In footnotes the editor says "the fugitive" is the printed reading and that of al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 82) while the manuscript's word is garbled and unreadable, referring to report 8237; and that a part of the old division from which his manuscript was copied ends at this point, with a closing blessing on the Prophet and his family and the opening invocation of the next part. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 280}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
