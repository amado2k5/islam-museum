**Commentary on verse 62 (pp. 118-123)**

On [[passage/quran-10-62|10:62]] he says: the helpers of God have no fear in the next life of God's punishment, for God is pleased with them and has made them safe from it, and they do not grieve for what they lost of the world. "Friends" is the plural of the word for helper. The editor notes that here there follows a detail on the meaning of "friends of God" for which there is no earlier parallel in al-Tabari's commentary. The commentary continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 118}

The interpreters differed over who deserves this name. Some said they are people whose sight recalls God, because of the marks of goodness and humility on them. Reports 17703, from Ibn Abbas, 17704, from Sa'id b. Jubayr from the Prophet, 17705, from Abu al-Duha, and 17706, from al-Ala' b. al-Musayyab on his father: those whom one sees and remembers God. On 17704 the editor says Ash'ath b. Ishaq is reliable and the report is mursal. Report 17707, from Abu al-Duha: some people are keys, who when seen bring God to mind; the editor explains "keys" as a figure, meaning they open the door of good to people, and the greatest good is remembering God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 119}

Reports 17708, 17710 and 17711, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, say the Prophet was asked about the friends of God and said they are those who, when seen, bring God to mind; report 17709 is from Abdullah b. Mas'ud and report 17712 from Abdullah b. Abi al-Hudhayl: when a friend of God is seen, God is remembered. On 17708 the editor notes that Sahl Abu al-Asad al-Qarari is reliable in some men's view and unknown in others', and that the report is mursal from Sa'id b. Jubayr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 120}

Others went by report 17713, from Abu Hurayra: among God's servants are servants whom prophets and martyrs envy; asked who they are, so that people might love them, the Prophet said they are people who loved one another for God without wealth or kinship, their faces light upon pulpits of light, who do not fear when people fear nor grieve when people grieve; and he recited the verse. Report 17714, from Umar b. al-Khattab, is similar: people who loved each other for God by the spirit of God, with no kinship between them and no wealth they exchanged. The editor says the chain of 17713 is sound, that Ibn Kathir copied the report and al-Suyuti cited it, and that the chain of 17714 is good but broken because Abu Zur'a related from Umar only by mursal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 121}

Report 17715, from Abu Malik al-Ash'ari, from the Prophet: there will come from the mixed throngs of people and strangers among the tribes a people between whom no near kinship joined them, who loved each other for God and were sincere for Him; on the Day of Resurrection God sets for them pulpits of light and seats them on them; people are afraid and they are not, and they are God's friends who have no fear and do not grieve. The editor explains the phrases "mixed throngs" and "strangers among the tribes", corrects names in the chain, and says the chain is sound and the report is in Ahmad's Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 122}

Al-Tabari says the correct view is that a friend of God is whoever has the attribute God gave him, the one who believed and was God-fearing, as He says "those who believed and were mindful of God". Ibn Zayd said likewise, report 17716: who are they, Lord? He said, those who believed and were mindful; and my father said, belief will not be accepted without mindfulness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 123}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
