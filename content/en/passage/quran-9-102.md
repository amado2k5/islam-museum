**Commentary on verse 102 (pp. 446-453)**

Al-Tabari says: among the people of Medina are hypocrites who persisted in hypocrisy, and among them are "others who confessed their sins", meaning acknowledged their sins; "they mixed a righteous deed", by which the Almighty means the righteous deed they mixed with the evil one, their confession of their sins and repentance from them, the "other evil one" being their staying behind from the Messenger of God when he went out to campaign and their leaving striving with the Muslims. If someone says: how is it said "they mixed a righteous deed and another evil one", when the speech is "they mixed a righteous deed with another evil one"? The answer is that the people of Arabic differed on this. One of the Basran grammarians said it was so said and that in Arabic the sense may be "with another", as you say "the water and the log were level", meaning with the log, and "I mixed the water and the milk"; another denied that it was like their saying "the water and the log were level", reasoning that the verb of mixing acts on both the first and the second and each may be put before the other, whereas putting the log before the water is not allowed in "the water and the log were level", which for him shows that mixing differs. The editor notes that the copyist dropped something of Abu Ja'far's words here and he left the passage as it stood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 446}

Al-Tabari's view of the correct answer is that it has the sense of their saying "I mixed the water and the milk", meaning I mixed it with the milk; "perhaps God will turn to them" means it may be that God will turn to them, and "perhaps" from God is binding, its sense being that God will turn to them, but in the speech of the Arabs it is as I described; "God is forgiving, merciful" means God is possessed of clemency and pardon for whoever repents of his sins and covers them for him, merciful to him in not punishing him for them. The editor refers to earlier explanations of "perhaps", forgiveness and mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 447}

The interpreters differed over whom this verse refers to and the occasion on which it was sent down. Some said it came down about ten men who stayed behind from the Messenger of God in the Tabuk campaign, among them Abu Lubaba, of whom seven tied themselves to the pillars where the Prophet would first pass, as repentance for their sin. Report 17136, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "others who confessed their sins, mixing a righteous deed with another evil one", he said, they were ten men who stayed behind from the Prophet in the Tabuk campaign, and when the Prophet's return drew near seven of them tied themselves to the pillars of the mosque, the Prophet's way in returning being through the mosque past them, and when he saw them he said "who are these who have tied themselves to the pillars?", and they said "this is Abu Lubaba and companions of his who stayed behind from you, Messenger of God, and swore that no one would free them until you free them and excuse them", and the Prophet said "and I swear by God that I shall not free them or excuse them until God is the one who frees them, they preferred ease to me and stayed behind from campaigning with the Muslims", and when that reached them they said "and by God we shall not free ourselves until God is the one who frees us", and God, blessed and exalted, sent down "others who confessed their sins", "perhaps" from God being binding, and when it came down the Prophet sent for them and freed them and excused them. The editor notes a gap in the wording which he completed from al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 448}

Others said rather that they were six, one of them Abu Lubaba. Report 17137, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: the Messenger of God campaigned at Tabuk, and Abu Lubaba and five with him stayed behind from the Prophet; then Abu Lubaba and two men with him reflected and regretted and were sure of ruin, and said "we are in the shelter and calm with the women while the Messenger of God and the believers are with him in striving; by God, we shall tie ourselves to the pillars and not free ourselves until the Messenger of God himself frees us and excuses us", and Abu Lubaba went and tied himself and two men with him to the pillars of the mosque, and three men remained who did not tie themselves. The Messenger of God returned from his campaign, and his way was through the mosque, and he passed them and said "who are these who tie themselves to the pillars?", and they said "this is Abu Lubaba and companions of his who stayed behind from the Messenger of God, and they covenanted with God not to free themselves until you free them and are pleased with them, and they have confessed their sins", and the Messenger of God said "by God, I shall not free them until I am ordered to free them, nor excuse them until God excuses them, for they stayed behind from me and preferred themselves over campaigning and striving with the Muslims", and God by His mercy sent down "others who confessed their sins", "perhaps" from God being binding, and when the verse came down the Messenger of God freed them, excused them and overlooked their fault. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 449}

Others said that those who tied themselves to the pillars were eight. Report 17138, from Zayd ibn Aslam, through Ibn Humayd and Ya'qub: they are the eight who tied themselves to the pillars, among them Kardam, Mirdas and Abu Lubaba. Report 17139, from Sa'id, through Ibn Waki', Jarir, Ya'qub and Ja'far: those who tied themselves to the pillars were Hilal, Abu Lubaba, Kardam, Mirdas and Abu Qays; the editor notes these are five, the full eight not being given. Others said they were seven. Report 17140, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: His words "others who confessed their sins, mixing a righteous deed with another evil one, perhaps God will turn to them", it was mentioned to us that they were seven men who stayed behind from the Tabuk campaign; as for four, they mixed a righteous deed with an evil one: al-Jadd ibn Qays, Abu Lubaba, Haram and Aws, all of them Helpers, and they are those about whom "take from their wealth a charity to purify them" was said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 450}

Report 17141, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they are a band of those who stayed behind from Tabuk, among them Abu Lubaba and among them al-Jadd ibn Qays, who were forgiven; Qatada said they are not the three; report 17142, from Qatada, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Abu Sufyan and Ma'mar: they are seven, among them Abu Lubaba, who stayed behind from the Tabuk campaign, and they are not the three. Report 17143, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it came down about Abu Lubaba and his companions, who stayed behind from the Prophet of God in the Tabuk campaign; when the Messenger of God turned back from his campaign and was near Medina they regretted their staying behind from the Messenger of God and said "we are in the shade with food and women while the Prophet of God is in the struggle and hardship; by God we shall tie ourselves to the pillars and not free ourselves until the Prophet of God himself frees us and excuses us", and they tied themselves up, and three remained who did not tie themselves to the pillars; the Messenger of God came back from his campaign and passed through the mosque, which was his way, and saw them and asked about them, and was told "Abu Lubaba and his companions, who stayed behind from you, Prophet of God, and have done to themselves what you see, and covenanted with God not to free themselves until you are the one who frees them", and the Prophet of God said "I shall not free them until I am ordered to free them, nor excuse them until God excuses them; they preferred themselves to the campaign of the Muslims", and God sent down "others who confessed their sins" to "perhaps God will turn to them", "perhaps" from God being binding, and the Prophet of God freed them and excused them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 450}

Others said rather that this verse refers to Abu Lubaba in particular, and that the sin he confessed and was forgiven for was what had been his affair with Banu Qurayza. Report 17144, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr, Warqa' and Ibn Abi Najih: "others who confessed their sins", he said, it came down about Abu Lubaba, who said to Banu Qurayza what he said. Report 17145, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa: Abu Lubaba, when he said to Qurayza what he said, pointed to his throat, that Muhammad would slaughter them if they submitted to God's judgment; report 17146, from him, is similar through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl, except that he said "if they submitted to his judgment". Report 17147, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Jarir and Layth: Abu Lubaba tied himself to a pillar and said "I shall not free myself until God and His Messenger free me", and the Prophet freed him, and about him this verse came down, "others who confessed their sins, mixing a righteous deed"; report 17148, from him, through al-Muharibi and Layth: it came down about Abu Lubaba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 451}

Others said rather that it came down about Abu Lubaba because of his staying behind from Tabuk. Report 17149, from al-Zuhri, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Abu Lubaba was one of those who stayed behind from the Prophet in the Tabuk campaign, and tied himself to a pillar, saying "by God, I shall not untie myself from it, nor taste food or drink, until I die or God turns to me"; he stayed seven days tasting neither food nor drink until he fell down fainting; then God turned to him, and he was told "you have been forgiven, Abu Lubaba", and he said "by God, I shall not untie myself until the Messenger of God unties me"; the Prophet came and untied him with his own hand, and then Abu Lubaba said "Messenger of God, part of my repentance is that I leave my people's house in which I committed the sin and strip myself of all my wealth in charity to God and His Messenger", and he said "a third suffices you, Abu Lubaba". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 452}

Some said the Bedouin are meant by this verse. Report 17150, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: they are of the Bedouin. Report 17151, from Abu Uthman, through Ibn Waki', Yazid ibn Harun and Hajjaj ibn Abi Zaynab: there is no verse in the Qur'an more hopeful to me for this community than His words "others who confessed their sins" up to "God is forgiving, merciful". The editor notes that Hajjaj ibn Abi Zaynab is weak and that al-Suyuti cites the report in al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 452}

Al-Tabari holds that the best of these views is that of those who said this verse came down about those who confessed the error of what they did in staying behind from the Messenger of God and leaving striving with him and going out to fight the Romans when he set out for Tabuk, and that those about whom it came down were a group, Abu Lubaba one of them. He says that view is better because God, glorious is His praise, said "others who confessed their sins", telling of the confession of a group of their sins, and the one who confessed his sin and tied himself to the pillar at the siege of Qurayza was Abu Lubaba alone; so since that is so and God described by confession of sins a group, it is known that the group He so described is not the one man, and it is clear that this description, being only of a group, and there being no group that did that, according to what the historians and reporters transmitted and the interpreters agreed, except a group of those who stayed behind from the Tabuk campaign, what we said is right; and we said Abu Lubaba was among them by the agreement of the authority of the interpreters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 453}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
