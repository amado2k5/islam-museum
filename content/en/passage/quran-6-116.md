## Summary
Verse 116 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 116 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "if you obey most of those on earth they will lead you astray from the way of God; they follow nothing but conjecture and they only guess", al-Tabari says: God says to His prophet Muhammad, do not obey, Muhammad, these who set up rivals to God in what they call you to, of eating what they slaughtered for their gods and dedicated to other than their Lord, and their like among the deviant and astray; for if you obey most of those on earth they will lead you astray from God's religion and the highway of truth and rightness, and turn you from it. He said "most of those on earth" of the sons of Adam to His prophet because they were then unbelievers and astray, and He told him not to obey them in what they called him to, for if he obeyed them he would go astray as they did and be like them, since they do not call him to guidance, having missed it. Then He reported how they stand in themselves, saying "they follow nothing but conjecture", reporting that in their own view they hold a supposition and reckoning on the soundness of a resolve they have made, although in truth it is an error (the editor writes in a footnote that he is unsure of the correctness of this wording). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 64}

"And they only guess" means: they are only conjecturing, supposing and making estimates, not with certain knowledge (the editor refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an). One says "kharasa, yakhrusu, kharsan and khurusan", meaning he lied; "takharrasa" with conjecture or with a lie; "I estimated (kharastu) the date palms"; and "your camels were kharisat", struck by cold and hunger. The footnote says the editor did not find "khurus" as a verbal noun in the lexica he had to hand, though Abu Hayyan mentions it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 65}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
