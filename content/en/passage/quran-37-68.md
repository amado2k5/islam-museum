## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 68 to 71 in a single segment. He explains the phrase "Then they will have over it a sour of a spark," meaning: "Then they will have a sour of a sour from the sparks of a spark," and these polytheists have a stingy tree of Zaqqum on what they eat from this tree, which is a confusion of the Arabs' saying: so-and-so grayed his food, so it is stained with sour and gray (from hives). Al-Haim is the hot water, which is what heats and its heat ends, and its origin is a direct verb drawn to fa'il. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Qatadah, Al-Suddi, and Ibn Zayd.
