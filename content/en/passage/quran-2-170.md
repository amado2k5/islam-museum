## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when it is said to them, follow what God has sent down, they say, no, we follow what we found our fathers on" al-Tabari gives two readings: the pronoun returns to "whoever" in "among the people are those who take equals besides God", or to "the people" in "O people, eat of what is on earth", being a turn from address to report about those absent, as in the verse of Yunus. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 304} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 305} He says the more likely is that it returns to "the people", since it follows their address, with other verses between; he mentions that it was revealed about some Jews who said this when called to Islam, and relates from Ibn 'Abbas that the Messenger of God called the Jews to Islam and Rafi' ibn Kharija and Malik ibn 'Awf said, no, we follow what we found our fathers on, for they were more knowing and better than us, and this was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 305} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 306} In a footnote the editor remarks that al-Tabari's printed wording may mean he held it was revealed about the pre-Islamic Arabs who forbade things to themselves, and that he doubts the Jews account. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 305} "Follow what God has sent down" means act on what He sent down in His book, treating its lawful as lawful and its forbidden as forbidden and taking it as a guide; "alfayna" means "we found". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 306}
