## Summary
Verse 84 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/death-of-abdullah-ibn-ubayy|Death of Abdullah ibn Ubayy]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 84 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 84 (pp. 405-410)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet Muhammad: do not pray, Muhammad, over anyone of these hypocrites who stayed behind from going out with you who dies, ever, "nor stand at his grave", meaning do not take charge of his burial and interment, from the saying that so-and-so "stood in" for so-and-so's affair when he saw to it for him; "they disbelieved in God and His Messenger" means they denied the oneness of God and the message of His Messenger, "and died as transgressors", meaning died outside Islam, departed from God's command and prohibition. The editor explains the word for interment as one of the terms of early jurists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 405}

It is mentioned that this verse came down when the Prophet prayed over Abdallah ibn Ubayy. Report 17050, from Ibn Umar, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Sufyan ibn Waki', Suwar ibn Abdallah, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Ubaydallah and Nafi': the son of Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul came to the Messenger of God when his father died and said "give me your shirt that I may shroud him in it, and pray over him and ask forgiveness for him"; he gave him his shirt and said "when you have finished, let me know"; and when he wanted to pray over him, Umar pulled him back and said "has God not forbidden you to pray over the hypocrites?", and he said "rather He gave me a choice, and said 'ask forgiveness for them or do not ask forgiveness for them'"; he said, and he prayed over him; he said, and God, blessed and exalted, sent down "never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave", and he gave up praying over them. The editor notes that the report is related by al-Bukhari in his Sahih by several routes and by Muslim, and that Ibn Kathir cites it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 406}

Report 17051, from Ibn Umar, through Ibn Waki', Abu Usama and Ubaydallah: when Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul died, his son Abdallah came to the Prophet and asked him to give him his shirt to shroud his father in, and he gave it; then he asked him to pray over him, and Umar ibn al-Khattab stood and took hold of the Prophet's garment and said "Ibn Salul! Will you pray over him when God has forbidden you to pray over him?", and the Prophet said "my Lord only gave me a choice, and said 'ask forgiveness for them or do not ask forgiveness for them; if you ask forgiveness for them seventy times God will never forgive them', and I shall add to seventy"; he said "he is a hypocrite!", and the Messenger of God prayed over him, and God sent down "never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave". Report 17052, from Jabir ibn Abdallah, through Suwar ibn Abdallah al-Anbari, Yahya ibn Sa'id, Mujalid and Amir: the chief of the hypocrites died in Medina, and left a request that the Prophet pray over him and that he be shrouded in his shirt, and he shrouded him in his shirt, prayed over him and stood at his grave, and God, blessed and exalted, sent down "never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave". The editor notes that Ibn Kathir cites Jabir's report from al-Bazzar's Musnad and says its chain is acceptable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 407}

Report 17053, from Anas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Salama and Yazid al-Raqashi: the Messenger of God wished to pray over Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul, and Gabriel took hold of his garment and said "never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave". The editor says Yazid al-Raqashi is weak, indeed abandoned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 407}

Report 17054, from Jabir, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Uyayna and Amr: the Prophet came to Abdallah ibn Ubayy after he had been placed in his grave, and had him taken out and laid on his knees and clothed him in his shirt and spat on him some of his saliva, and God knows best. The editor notes that the report is related by al-Bukhari and Muslim and that "God knows best" refers to knowledge of His decree. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 408}

Report 17055, from Umar ibn al-Khattab, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, al-Zuhri, Ubaydallah ibn Abdallah ibn Utba and Ibn Abbas: when Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul died the Messenger of God was called to pray over him and went to him, and when he stood over him to pray, I turned and stood in front of him and said "Messenger of God, will you pray over the enemy of God Abdallah ibn Ubayy, who said on such a day such and such?", counting his days, and the Messenger of God was smiling, until when I had said much he said "put that behind me, Umar; I was given a choice and chose, and it was said to me 'ask forgiveness for them or do not; if you ask forgiveness for them seventy times God will never forgive them'; had I known that if I added to seventy he would be forgiven, I would have added". He said, then he prayed over him and walked with him and stood at his grave until he was done. Umar said, I marvelled at myself and my boldness toward the Messenger of God, God and His Messenger know best; and by God it was not long before these two verses came down, "never pray over any of them who dies", and after that the Messenger of God did not pray over a hypocrite or stand at his grave until God took him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 408}

Report 17056, from Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, through Ibn Humayd, Salama and Ibn Ishaq: when Abdallah ibn Ubayy died his son Abdallah ibn Abdallah came to the Messenger of God and asked him for his shirt, and he gave it, and he shrouded his father in it. Report 17057, from Umar ibn al-Khattab, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, al-Layth, Aqil and Ibn Shihab: when Abdallah ibn Ubayy died, and he gave the like of Ibn Humayd's report from Salama. The editor notes the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira and that al-Bukhari relates it through al-Layth from Aqil from al-Zuhri, and explains "put that behind me" as send your opinion away from me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 409}

Report 17058, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave", he said, Abdallah ibn Ubayy sent for the Messenger of God when ill to come to him, and Umar forbade it, and the Prophet of God came to him, and when he entered, the Prophet of God said "your love of the Jews has destroyed you", and he said "Prophet of God, I did not send for you to rebuke me but to ask forgiveness for me", and asked for his shirt to be shrouded in, and he gave it to him, and the Messenger of God asked forgiveness for him, and he died and was shrouded in the shirt of the Messenger of God, who breathed on his skin and lowered him into his grave, and God, blessed and exalted, sent down "never pray over any of them who dies", the verse. He said, it was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God was spoken to about it and said "what will my shirt avail him against God, or my Lord", and he prayed over him, "and I hope a thousand of his people will become Muslims because of it". The editor notes that the words "and he prayed over him" are so in the manuscript and form a statement by Qatada or another, inserted in the Prophet's words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 410}

Report 17059, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul, when ill, sent to the Prophet, and when he entered, the Prophet said "your love of the Jews has destroyed you", and he said "Messenger of God, I sent for you only to ask forgiveness for me and not to rebuke me", and Abdallah then asked him to give him his shirt to be shrouded in, and he gave it to him, prayed over him and stood at his grave, and God, mighty is His mention, sent down "never pray over any of them who dies, nor stand at his grave". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 410}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
