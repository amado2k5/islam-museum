## Summary
Verse 41 of [[passage/quran-13|Surat ar-Ra'd]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 41 of 43 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 41 (pp. 493-498)**

On "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders? God judges; none can repel His judgment, and He is swift in reckoning", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its interpretation. Some said its meaning is: do these polytheists of Mecca who ask Muhammad for signs not see that We come to the land and open it for him, land after land around their own land? Do they not fear that We open their land for him as We opened others for him? Report 20514, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn al-Sabbah, Hushaym, Husayn and Ikrima: "that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", he said, have they not seen that We open the land for Muhammad, land after land? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 493}

Report 20515, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", he means by that what God opened for Muhammad, saying that is its diminishing. Report 20516, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', his father and Salama ibn Nubayt: what you overcame of the enemy's land. Report 20517, from Ma'mar, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Ibn Thawr: al-Hasan used to say of "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", it is the Muslims' prevailing over the polytheists. Report 20518, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", meaning that the lands around the Prophet of God were being diminished for him, they were looking at that and not taking heed; God said in Sura al-Anbiya', "We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders; are they then the victors?" [[passage/quran-21-44|21:44]]; rather the Prophet of God and his Companions are the victors. Others said its meaning is rather: do they not see that We come to the land and ruin it, or do they not fear that We do the like of that with them and their land, destroying them and ruining their land? Report 20519, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ali ibn Asim, Husayn ibn Abd al-Rahman and Ikrima: "that We come to the land, diminishing it {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 494}

from its borders", he said, have they not seen the village being ruined until the habitation is in one corner? Report 20520, from Mujahid, through Hajjaj ibn Muhammad, Ibn Jurayj and al-A'raj: "We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", he said, its ruin. Report 20521, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and al-A'raj: the same; he said, and Ibn Jurayj said, its ruin and the destruction of its people. Report 20522, from Ikrima, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il and Abu Ja'far al-Farra': "diminishing it from its borders", he said, We ruin from its borders. Others said the meaning is rather: We diminish its blessing, its fruit and its people by death. Report 20523, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: "diminishing it from its borders", he says, the diminishing of its people and its blessing. Report 20524, from Mujahid, through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and Layth: "diminishing it from its borders", he said, in persons, in fruits and in the ruin of the land. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 495}

Report 20525, from al-Sha'bi, through Ibn Waki', his father and Talha al-Qannad: were the earth diminished, your garden would be too narrow for you, but persons and fruits are diminished. Others said its meaning is: We come to the land diminishing it of its people, taking them from their edges by death. Report 20526, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "diminishing it from its borders", he said, the death of its people. Report 20527, from Mujahid, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya, Sufyan and Mansur: "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", he said, death. Report 20528, from Ikrima, through al-Muthanna, Muslim ibn Ibrahim, Harun al-Nahwi and al-Zubayr ibn al-Khirrit: "diminishing it from its borders", he said, it is death; then he said, were the earth diminished we would find no place to sit. Report 20529, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Ikrima used to say, it is the taking of people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 496}

Report 20530, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: Ikrima was asked about the diminishing of the earth and said, the taking of people. Report 20531, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Jarir ibn Hazim and Ya'la ibn Hakim: "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", he said, were it as they say, none of you would find a hole to relieve himself in. Report 20532, from Abu Raja', through al-Fadl ibn al-Sabbah and Isma'il ibn Ulayya: Ikrima was asked while I was listening about this verse and said, death. Others said "diminishing it from its borders" is by the passing of its jurists and best people. Report 20533, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Talha ibn Amr and Ata': the passing of its scholars, its jurists and the best of its people; the editor notes that al-Hakim related it in the Mustadrak and said the chain is sound, and that al-Dhahabi objected that Talha ibn Amr is abandoned according to Ahmad. Report 20534, from Mujahid, through Abu Ahmad and Abd al-Wahhab: the death of the scholars. Al-Tabari says: the best of the views on its interpretation is that of whoever said that "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders" means the Muslims among Muhammad's Companions prevailing over it and subduing its people; do they not take heed of that and fear their prevailing {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 497}

over their land and subduing them? For God threatened those polytheists of his people who asked His Messenger for signs with His saying "whether We show you part of what We promise them, or We cause you to die, your duty is only to convey and Ours is the reckoning", then rebuked them, mighty be His praise, for their poor heed to what they see of God's doing with their like among the disbelievers while they still ask for signs, saying "do they not see that We come to the land, diminishing it from its borders", by subduing its people and prevailing over it from its edges and sides, while they take no heed of what they see of that. As for "God judges; none can repel His judgment", he says, God is the One who judges, so His judgment is carried out, and decrees, so His decree takes effect; and when God's judgment and decree come to these polytheists of Mecca, they will not be able to turn it back. By "none can repel His judgment" he means none can reverse His judgment, "al-mu'aqqib" in Arab speech being the one who returns upon a thing. As for "and He is swift in reckoning", he says, God is swift in reckoning, counting the deeds of these polytheists, nothing hidden from Him, and He is behind their requital for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 498}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
