## Summary
Verse 63 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 63 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: who delivers you from the darknesses of land and sea, when you call on him humbly and secretly: if he delivers us from this we shall be among the thankful" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to those who hold others equal to their Lord and call to worship their idols: who delivers you from the darknesses of the land when you go astray in it and are bewildered and guidance and the road are dark to you, and from the darknesses of the sea when you ride it and miss the way and the path is dark to you and you are not guided to it, other than God, to whom you then turn in prayer; "humbly" from you to him, in submission, openly; "and secretly" means at times hiding the prayer and at times declaring and showing it; you say: if you deliver us from this, Lord, meaning these darknesses we are in, "we shall be among the thankful", of those who affirm your oneness in thanks and make worship pure for you, apart from those we used to join with you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 414}

The editor says the printed text has a wording in "to whom you turn" that the manuscript corrects; that the verse text in print and manuscript reads "anjaytana", the reading of the rest of the Seven, while the Qur'an copy current with him has the Kufans' "anjana", and that Abu Jafar followed the common reading and did not mention the Kufans'; he thinks Abu Jafar abridged the commentary here very sharply and left out much one would have expected him to say; and refers for "tadarru" to p. 355.

13342, from Ibn Abbas: when a man loses the road he calls on God: if you deliver us from this we shall be among the thankful; 13343, from Qatada: from the distress of land and sea. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 415}

The editor notes that he left report 13342 on the common reading and not that of his own Qur'an copy.

Verse 63 closes here, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-64|6:64]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
