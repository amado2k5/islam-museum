**Commentary on verse 55 (pp. 361-363)**

On [[passage/quran-11-55|11:55]] al-Tabari says: 'so plot against me, all of you' means scheme, you and your gods together, to harm and trouble me, 'then give me no respite' means do not delay it, but see whether you and they can reach me with what you claim your gods reached me with of harm. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'plotting' and 'respite'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 361}

The remaining reports on 'we say only that some of our gods have afflicted you with evil' of verse 54: report 18272, from Ibn Abbas: our gods strike you with madness; report 18273, from Qatada: what leads you to revile our gods is only that evil has struck you from them; report 18274, from him: you do this to our gods because they struck you with harm; report 18275, from Abd Allah b. Kathir: our gods have struck you with evil; report 18276, from al-Dahhak: we fear some evil from our gods may strike you; report 18277, from Ibn Zayd: your mind is confused and this has struck you from what our gods did to you. His word 'i'tarak' is the 'ifta'ala' form from 'it befell me', when it strikes you, and he cites a line of Abu Khirash al-Hudhali from a poem in which he tells of his fleeing his pursuers, which the editor explains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 363}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
