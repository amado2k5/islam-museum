**Commentary on verse 115 (pp. 26-27)**

Al-Tabari says "they said, Moses" means the sorcerers said to Moses: choose whether you will throw your staff or we shall throw ours. "That" is introduced with "either" because it stands where a choice is commanded, so "that" takes the accusative sense: choose that you throw or that we throw. When the construction with "either" is in the form of a command, "that" is needed, as when you say to a man "either that you go or that you sit", meaning "go or sit". When it is in the form of a report, it has no "that", as in "either He punishes them or turns to them" ([[passage/quran-9-106|9:106]]); the former is called "choosing", and "either" is spelled with a kasra throughout. The editor notes that the first case is "choosing" and that the grammarians call the reporting form "vagueness", that al-Tabari should have put his remark "this is called choosing" earlier to avoid a misunderstanding, and he points to a very good chapter in al-Farra's "Ma'ani al-Qur'an". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 26}
