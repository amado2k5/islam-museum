## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 26 to 27 in a single segment. He explains the phrase "And the day the sky split open with clouds and the angels descended in descent": Reciters differed in the reading of the phrase "tashaqq." The majority of Hijaz readers read it as "and the day it splits" with a shadda meaning 'it cracks.' They merged one of the two ta's into the shin and stressed it, as he said: they do not hear until the highest gathering. Most of the reciters of Kufa read this: 'and the day it splits' with a lightened 'sh' and a truncation with one of the 'ta's' from the other. My view on this is that they are extensive readings in the recitation of the cities with the same meaning; whichever the reader reads, it is correct. The interpretation of the phrase is: 'the day the sky splits open from the clouds.' It was said: that is a white cloud like the clouds that shaded the Children of Israel, and I made the 'b' in His saying: 'with the clouds' instead of 'an' as you say: 'I threw from the bow and with the bow, and on the bow,' meaning the same. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Ikrimah.
