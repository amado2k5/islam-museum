## In al-Tabari's commentary

The interpreters differed over the occasion for "or do you wish to ask your Messenger as Moses was asked before". Some said, from Ibn Abbas (in Ibn Ishaq's report), that Rafi' ibn Huraymila and Wahb ibn Zayd said to the Messenger of God: bring us a book which you send down to us from heaven for us to read, and cause rivers to gush forth for us, and we will follow and believe you; and the verse was revealed about what they said (the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham). Others, from Qatada and al-Suddi, said it meant to ask that God show them openly, as Moses was asked, and the Arabs asked the Messenger of God to bring God to them so they might see Him. Others, from Mujahid, said Quraysh asked Muhammad to turn al-Safa into gold; he said yes, it would be for them like the table of the Children of Israel if they disbelieved, and they refused and went back, and the verse was revealed. Others, from Abu al-Aliya, said a man said: Messenger of God, if only our expiations were the expiations of the Children of Israel; and the Prophet said what God has given you is better than what He gave the Children of Israel (relating that for them a sin and its expiation were written on one's door, and that the five prayers and one Friday to the next expiate what lies between, and that whoever intends a good deed and does not do it has one written for him), and the verse was revealed. The editor says this is a mursal report, one of Abu al-Aliya's, and that a mursal is no proof in his view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 490} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 491}

On the meaning of "am" the grammarians differed. Some Basrans said it is interrogative: "do you wish to ask your Messenger?"; others of them said it is a new question cut off from what came before, not meaning doubt but made to show the ugliness of their act, citing al-Akhtal's line "your eye lied, or did you see at Wasit". Some Kufan grammarians said you may take it as a question on speech that preceded, as at the start of Surat al-Sajda, "or do they say he has invented it", that "am" answers a question in two ways, and that were a speech begun with nothing before it, the question would be only by hamza or "hal" (the editor traces this to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 492}
