## In al-Tabari's commentary

The interpreters differed over the occasion for "or do you wish to ask your Messenger as Moses was asked before". Some said, from Ibn Abbas (in Ibn Ishaq's report), that Rafi' ibn Huraymila and Wahb ibn Zayd said to the Messenger of God: bring us a book which you send down to us from heaven for us to read, and cause rivers to gush forth for us, and we will follow and believe you; and the verse was revealed about what they said (the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham). Others, from Qatada and al-Suddi, said it meant to ask that God show them openly, as Moses was asked, and the Arabs asked the Messenger of God to bring God to them so they might see Him. Others, from Mujahid, said Quraysh asked Muhammad to turn al-Safa into gold; he said yes, it would be for them like the table of the Children of Israel if they disbelieved, and they refused and went back, and the verse was revealed. Others, from Abu al-Aliya, said a man said: Messenger of God, if only our expiations were the expiations of the Children of Israel; and the Prophet said what God has given you is better than what He gave the Children of Israel (relating that for them a sin and its expiation were written on one's door, and that the five prayers and one Friday to the next expiate what lies between, and that whoever intends a good deed and does not do it has one written for him), and the verse was revealed. The editor says this is a mursal report, one of Abu al-Aliya's, and that a mursal is no proof in his view. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 490} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 491}

On the meaning of "am" the grammarians differed. Some Basrans said it is interrogative: "do you wish to ask your Messenger?"; others of them said it is a new question cut off from what came before, not meaning doubt but made to show the ugliness of their act, citing al-Akhtal's line "your eye lied, or did you see at Wasit". Some Kufan grammarians said you may take it as a question on speech that preceded, as at the start of Surat al-Sajda, "or do they say he has invented it", that "am" answers a question in two ways, and that were a speech begun with nothing before it, the question would be only by hamza or "hal" (the editor traces this to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 492}

Al-Tabari prefers, on the strength of the reports, that "am" in "am turiduna" is a new question meaning "do you wish, people, to ask your Messenger", that "am" can open a question when preceded by speech, like the start of Surat al-Sajda, and that it can mean "bal" after a question (citing an Arab usage and a verse whose author the editor could not identify). He relays the objection of one who denied that it is a detached question, because the first part is a statement and the second a question, and a question cannot be in a statement. The sense, to him, is: do you wish to ask your Messenger for things like what the people of Moses asked before you, so that you disbelieve if refused what God's wisdom does not allow Him to give you, or perish if given it and then disbelieve, as perished the nations before you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 492} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 493} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 494}

On "whoever exchanges faith for disbelief" he says: whoever takes denial of God and His signs in exchange for belief in God and His signs and confession of Him. He mentions a view from Abu al-Aliya that disbelief here is hardship and faith is ease, and says he knows no sense of hardship in kufr nor of ease in iman unless what is meant is the hardships God prepared for the unbelievers and the bliss for the believers, a sense remote from the plain address. He finds in "he has strayed from the right path" a sign that these verses from "do not say ra'ina" are addressed to the believers among the Prophet's Companions and chide them for something that pleased the Jews, informing them that the Jews are people of deceit, envy and transgression, forbidding them to take their counsel, and telling them that whoever of them apostatizes has erred from the right path. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 494} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 495}

On "dalla" he says it means went off and turned aside, the root sense of "dalal" being going away from something, later used for the perished and the unregarded, and he cites a verse of al-Akhtal. "Sawa' al-sabil" is the middle and the course; the root of "sawa'" is the middle, and he cites Isa ibn Umar the grammarian ("I kept writing until my middle gave out") and a verse of Hassan ibn Thabit. "Sabil" is the travelled road. So the verse means: whoever takes unbelief in exchange for belief in God and His Messenger and leaves his religion has turned aside from the course of the road and its clear middle. He says the plain sense is a report of the one who exchanged faith for unbelief leaving the road, while what is meant is that he left the religion God chose for His servants as a road to His good pleasure, love and gardens; God made the road a likeness of His religion, and the one who turns from it is like one who turns from the road's course and only gets farther from his goal (the editor explains the form "masbul"). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 495} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 496} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 497}

Al-Tabari says that this road, of which God reported that whoever exchanges faith for unbelief has strayed from its middle, is "the straight path" for guidance to which we are told to ask in "guide us to the straight path". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 497} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 498}
