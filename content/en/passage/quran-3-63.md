## Summary
Verse 63 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 63 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 63.** Al-Tabari says it means: if those who argued with you about Jesus turn away from the truth that came to you from your Lord about Jesus and the rest of the guidance and clarification, and do not accept it, then God knows those who disobey and do in His earth and lands what He forbade them, which is their spreading of corruption; He knows them and their deeds, counts and preserves them until He repays them for it. The editor refers to earlier explanations of turning away and of corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 476} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 477}

**Al-Tabari on the mutual invocation (report 7180).** Al-Tabari says that when God had separated His prophet from the delegation of Najran's Christians by decisive judgment and a just ruling, He commanded him, should they turn away from what he called them to (affirming God's oneness, that He has no son or consort, and that Jesus is His servant and messenger) and insist on dispute and quarrel, to call them to mutual invocation of a curse; he did, and when he did they held back, declined and asked for a settlement. He then gives report 7180 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mughira, from Amir): the Prophet was commanded to invite the people of Najran to the mutual invocation; they agreed on it and set the next morning; they went to al-Sayyid and al-Aqib, their most prudent men, who agreed with them, and consulted a wise man among them, who rebuked them and said: if he is a prophet and prays against you, God will never turn His anger from you; and if he is a king and prevails over you, he will never spare you. They asked what to do, having made the appointment; he told them that when they went the next morning and it was put to them, they should say "we seek refuge in God", and that perhaps he would excuse them. At dawn the Prophet came carrying al-Hasan, holding al-Husayn by the hand, with Fatima walking behind him; he invited them to what they had agreed the day before, and they said "we seek refuge in God", several times. He said: if you refuse, then enter Islam, with what Muslims have and what they owe; if you refuse, pay the tribute in humility. They said they owned nothing but themselves. He said: then I withdraw the agreement. They said they had no strength for war with the Arabs but would pay tribute. He set on them two thousand garments a year, a thousand in Rajab and a thousand in Safar. The Prophet said that the herald had brought news of the destruction of the people of Najran, even the birds on the trees, had they persisted in the mutual invocation. Jarir adds that he asked al-Mughira that people report that Ali was with them; he answered that al-Shabi did not mention him, and he did not know whether this was because of the Umayyads' view of Ali or because he was not in the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 478} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 479}

**The editor's notes.** He says the printed and manuscript "and He commanded him", with the conjunction, is an extra that spoils the sentence, so he dropped it (the sentence is "commanded him ... to call them", with an interjected clause between), that the verb meaning he made them regret is old Arabic and rarely found in dictionaries, that the manuscript's form of the verb "spare" is correct, that the Prophet's remark has "has come to me" in the manuscript, that the verb for persisting means to carry on with something, and that the last paragraph about Ali is part of the report and so has no number of its own. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 478} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 479}

**Report 7181 (its start).** Al-Tabari gives (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr): the Prophet called them to fairness and cut off their argument; when the word came to him from God with the decisive judgment and the command to invoke mutual cursing if they rejected it, he invited them to it, and they said: Abu al-Qasim, let us consider our matter and then we shall come to you. They withdrew and went apart with al-Aqib, who was their man of counsel, saying: Abd al-Masih, what do you think? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 479}

**The rest of report 7181.** Al-Tabari completes Ibn Ishaq's report from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr. Al-Aqib tells his people that they know Muhammad is a sent prophet, that he has brought them the decisive account about their companion, that no people ever called down a curse on a prophet without the elders surviving and the young growing up, and that this would mean their uprooting. If they insist on keeping their religion and what they say of their companion, he advises them to leave the man and return to their lands until time shows them how his affair ends. They come to the Prophet and say they have decided not to take part in the mutual invocation and to leave him to his religion while they keep theirs, and they ask him to send with them a man of his Companions acceptable to them to judge between them in matters of their property over which they differ, for the Muslims are well regarded among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 479} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 480}

**The editor's notes on the report.** He notes that the printed text reads a corrupt form of the sentence about Muhammad being a sent prophet, that he restored the manuscript reading, and that it agrees with Ibn Hisham's biography; that the clause about time showing them the outcome of his affair is not in Ibn Hisham and means that time passes and conditions turn, so they will see the end of his matter; and that he quotes al-Suhayli in al-Rawd al-Unuf saying the Najran account has many additions from Ibn Ishaq beyond Ibn Hisham's transmission. He locates the report in Ibn Hisham (vol. 2, pp. 232-233) as the remainder of the reports ending with 7176, and explains that the word used of the Muslims is a verbal noun used as an adjective, like the phrase for a just man, meaning well-regarded, citing a line of Zuhayr for that sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 480}

**Report 7182.** Al-Tabari gives (Ibn Humayd, from Isa ibn Farqad, from Abu al-Jarud, from Zayd ibn Ali) who was with the Prophet at the mutual invocation: the Prophet, Ali, Fatima, al-Hasan and al-Husayn. The editor comments on the two transmitters: Isa ibn Farqad al-Marwazi, Abu Mutahhir, from whom Amr ibn Rafi and Ibn Humayd narrated and of whom Abu Hatim said "a shaykh"; and Abu al-Jarud, Ziyad ibn al-Mundhir al-Hamdani, whom he quotes Ibn Main as calling a liar who fabricated reports against the Companions and reports without basis in praise of the Prophet's household, one of the extreme Shia and the founder of the sect called the Jarudiyya. These are Ibn Main's judgements as the editor quotes them; the museum gives none. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 480}

**Report 7183.** Al-Tabari gives (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) that the Prophet took al-Hasan, al-Husayn and Fatima by the hand and told Ali to follow, and he went out with them. The Christians did not come out that day; they said they feared he might be the prophet and that a prophet's supplication is not like others, so they held back. The Prophet said that had they come out they would have burned. They made peace with him for eighty thousand, any shortfall in dirhams being made up in goods with a robe valued at forty, and for thirty-three coats of mail, thirty-three camels and thirty-four war horses each year, the Prophet standing surety for these until he delivered them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 481}

**Reports 7184 and 7185.** In 7184 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) it was mentioned to them that the Prophet called a delegation from Najran who had argued with him about Jesus, and they drew back and were afraid; and that he used to say, by Him in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, that the punishment had hung over the people of Najran and had they done it they would have been uprooted from the face of the earth, the editor glossing the expression as the surface of the earth. In 7185 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada) it reached them that the Prophet went out to hold the mutual invocation with the people of Najran, and when they saw him come out they were afraid, fled and went back. Mamar adds from Qatada that when the Prophet intended the people of Najran he took the hands of al-Hasan and al-Husayn and told Fatima to follow, and when the opponents saw this they turned back. The editor says the printed text has a corrupt reading here and that he reads the verb from the root of invocation, meaning mutual invocation and mutual cursing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 481}

**Reports 7186-7189.** Report 7186 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Abd al-Karim al-Jazari, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas): had those who were to invoke with the Prophet come out, they would have returned finding neither family nor property. Report 7187 (Abu Kurayb, from Zakariya, from Adi, from Ubaydallah ibn Amr, from Abd al-Karim, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) is the same. Report 7188 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) has the Prophet say, by Him in whose hand is my soul, that had they cursed me not a year would have passed with one of them present before God destroyed the liars. In report 7189 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) the Prophet is asked whom he would have brought, had he gone through with the invocation, when the call spoke of the sons, and he answers al-Hasan and al-Husayn. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 482}

**Report 7190.** Al-Tabari gives (Muhammad ibn Sinan, from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from al-Mundhir ibn Thalaba, from Alba ibn Ahmar al-Yashkuri) that when the verse came down the Prophet sent for Ali, Fatima and their two sons and called the Jews to the mutual invocation, whereupon a young Jew said: woe to you, was it not only yesterday that your brothers were turned into apes and swine? Do not take part in it. So they desisted. The editor notes that al-Mundhir ibn Thalaba ibn Harb al-Tai is listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy and that Alba ibn Ahmar al-Yashkuri narrated from Ikrima, of whom Ahmad said "no harm in him, I know only good", Ibn Hibban also listing him. The editor adds that the reports of this section are all mursal, as one sees, except the report of Ibn Abbas. These reports are given as transmitted; the museum gives no verdict on any account of who accompanied the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 482}
