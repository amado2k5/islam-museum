## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "righteousness is not that you turn your faces toward the east and the west, but righteousness is whoever believes in God, the Last Day, the angels, the Book and the prophets" al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said righteousness is not prayer alone but the qualities He sets out; from Ibn 'Abbas and al-Dahhak, it was revealed at Medina when the obligations were sent down and the limits set, and from Mujahid, righteousness is what is firm in the heart of obedience to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 336} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 337} Others said God meant the Jews and Christians, the Jews praying toward the west and the Christians toward the east, and He revealed it to tell them righteousness is other than the act they do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 337}

From Qatada and al-Rabi' ibn Anas, the Jews prayed toward the west and the Christians toward the east, and this was revealed; from Qatada, a man asked the Prophet about righteousness and it was revealed and he recited it to him, and before the obligations a man who bore witness that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger and died so was hoped to receive good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 337} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 338} Al-Tabari chooses the view of Qatada and al-Rabi' that the Jews and Christians are meant, since the verses before were reproaching them and reporting about them, so the sense is: righteousness is not, O Jews and Christians, that some of you turn your face to the east and some to the west. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 338} Asked how "righteousness is whoever believes in God" can be said when righteousness is an act and "whoever" a person, he answers that the sense is "righteousness is the righteousness of whoever believes", the noun standing for the act as in "generosity is Hatim" and "ask the town", meaning its people; he allows also "the righteous one is whoever believes in God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 339}

On "and gives wealth, for love of it, to the near of kin, the orphans, the poor, the wayfarer, those who ask, and for freeing slaves" he says: gives his wealth while loving it and being tight with it; from 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, that he gives it while healthy and miserly, hoping for life and fearing poverty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 340} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 341} He cites that al-Sha'bi, asked whether a man owes something from his wealth besides zakat, said yes and recited this verse, and in another report from him on the authority of Fatima bint Qays the Prophet said "in wealth there is a due besides zakat"; from 'Ata', asked by a Bedouin about a due on his camels after the alms, yes: lending the bucket, the stud and the milk; and from al-Suddi, this is an obligation on the owner of wealth besides zakat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 342} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 343}

Al-Tabari says the sense of the verse is: he gave wealth, loving it, eager for it and tight with it, to his kin, thereby joining their kinship; he argues that "the near of kin" are the giver's own relatives from what is reported from the Prophet about his order to Fatima bint Qays and from his saying, when asked which charity is best, "the effort of the one with little, to a hostile relative". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 344} "The wayfarer" is one passing by a man, and they differed on his description: some said the guest, from Qatada, who mentioned what is reported from the Prophet about hospitality of three nights; others said the traveller passing by you, from Abu Ja'far, Mujahid and Qatada; he is called "son of the road" for keeping to it, as a water bird is called "son of water". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 345} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 346} "Those who ask" are those who seek food, from 'Ikrima; "for freeing slaves" means in freeing necks from bondage, the mukatabun striving to pay their contract. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 347} "Established prayer" means kept up its practice within its limits, and "gave zakat" means gave it as God made it obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 347}

Asked whether any duty in wealth is owed beside zakat, he gives two views. Some said there are dues beside zakat and argued from this verse, since God mentions giving wealth to kin and then, after it, giving zakat; if it were one wealth, the repetition would have no meaning. Others said the first wealth is the zakat itself, God describing those who are given it to teach where zakat is to be placed, and then reporting that zakat was given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 348} "Those who fulfil their covenant when they make it" are those who do not break God's covenant after making it but fulfil it; from al-Rabi' ibn Anas, whoever gives God's covenant and breaks it, God will take vengeance on him, and whoever gives the Prophet's pledge and betrays it, the Prophet is his opponent on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 348} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 349} "The patient in hardship and affliction" are those who restrain themselves from what God dislikes and hold them to obedience; from Ibn Mas'ud, "ba'sa'" is poverty and "darra'" sickness, with other reports from him of hunger or need and illness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 349}
