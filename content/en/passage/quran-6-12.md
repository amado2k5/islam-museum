## Summary
Verse 12 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 12 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these who hold others equal to their Lord, "to whom belongs what is in the heavens and the earth?", that is, who owns it? Then tell them that it belongs to God, who made every thing his servant and overpowered every thing by his dominion and rule, not to the idols and rivals, nor to the images they worship and take as gods that own no benefit for themselves and ward off no harm from themselves. "He has prescribed mercy for himself" means he decreed that he is merciful to his servants, not hastening punishment on them and accepting their turning and repentance; this is God's appeal to those who turn away from him to turn to him in repentance. He says: if these who hold others equal to me and reject your prophethood repent and turn, I shall accept their repentance, and I have decreed in my creation that my mercy encompasses everything. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 274}

The editor refers for "prescribed" to volume 10 and his note there, and in p. 273 adds in brackets what the sentence needs.

Report 13096, from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet: when God finished creation he wrote a writing: my mercy has outstripped my anger. 13097, from Salman: when God created the heavens and the earth he created a hundred mercies, each filling what lies between heaven and earth; with him are ninety-nine, and one he divided among creatures, by which they show tenderness and by which wild beasts and birds drink water; on that day he limits it to the God-fearing and adds the ninety-nine to them. 13098, from Salman, the like, but Ibn Abi Adi did not mention the wild beasts and birds drinking water. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 274}

The editor says the chain of 13096 is sound and the report well known, that Dhakwan is Abu Salih, traces it to al-Bukhari in the Fath in other words, keeps "on that day" in 13097 from the manuscript since "that" points to the known day of resurrection, and identifies Abu Uthman al-Nahdi, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mull.

Report 13099, from Salman: we find in the Torah two points of tenderness: God created the heavens and the earth, then created a hundred mercies before he created creation, then created creation and put one mercy among them and kept ninety-nine with himself; by it they have mercy on one another, give to one another, show tenderness, visit one another, the she-camel yearns, the cow lows, the sheep bleats, birds and fish in the sea follow one another; on the day of resurrection God gathers that mercy to what he has, and his mercy is greater and wider. 13100, from him, the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 275}

The editor notes that in the manuscript the letter "ta" stands over "visit one another" to mark doubt or error, that "tathuj" means cries out and the Durr al-Manthur has "tantaj", both right in sense, that "ta'ar" means bleats, that he is in doubt about "birds follow one another" and "fish follow one another", and traces the two reports to the Durr al-Manthur, which names Abd al-Razzaq, Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn Jarir, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim.

Reports 13101 and 13102, from Tawus: when God created creation nothing had tenderness for anything until he created a hundred mercies and put one among them, so creatures became tender to one another. 13103 and 13104, from Ikrima, the narrator thinking he traced it to the Prophet: when God finishes judging between his creatures he brings out a writing from under the throne in which is: my mercy has outstripped my anger and I am the most merciful of the merciful; then there come out of the Fire as many as the people of Paradise, written here "God's freed ones"; a man said to Ikrima: but God says in [[passage/quran-5-37|5:37]] that they want to come out of the Fire and are not coming out; he said: woe to you, those are its own people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 276}

Report 13105, from Abu Hurayra, from the Prophet: when God decreed creation he wrote in a book that is with him above the throne: my mercy has outstripped my anger. 13106 and 13107, from Abdullah ibn Amr: God has a hundred mercies and sent down one to the people of the world, by which jinn, men, birds of heaven, fish of the water, beasts of earth and creeping things show mercy to one another, and he stored ninety-nine with himself, until on the day of resurrection he draws back the mercy he sent down and gathers it to what he has, and puts it in the hearts of the people of Paradise and over them. 13108, from Ka'b to Umar, asked what was the first thing God began of creation: God wrote a writing, not with pen or ink but with his finger: I am God, no god but me, my mercy has outstripped my anger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 277}

The editor says Ahmad relates 13105 with "has overcome my anger", glosses "ikhtalaja" as pulled and took away, traces 13106 to the Durr al-Manthur, says he doubts a letter in Ka'b's report, that it comes from Ka'b al-Ahbar and is mixed with his Israelite reports, and identifies its narrators, among them Abu al-Mukhariq Zuhayr ibn Salim al-Ansi and what al-Daraqutni said of him.

Then "he will gather you to the day of resurrection, no doubt in it": the lam in "he will gather you" is the lam of oath; the grammarians then differed on what brings it. A Kufan grammarian said: if you like, make "mercy" the end of the sentence and begin "he will gather you" afresh; or you may put it in the accusative, meaning "he prescribed that he will gather you", as in [[passage/quran-6-54|6:54]], "your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself, that whoever of you does evil in ignorance", meaning prescribed that whoever does; and the Arabs, with verbs that admit an oath's reply, use the open "an" or the lam, saying "I sent to him that he stand" and "I sent to him that he surely stand"; so too [[passage/quran-12-35|12:35]], "they would surely imprison him for a time", which is frequent in the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 278}

The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and says manuscript and print have "reply of speech" where "reply of oaths", as in al-Farra, is better.

A Basran grammarian used to say that the lam of "he will gather you" is raised because the sense of "kataba" is made obligatory and binding, with the sense of an oath, as if he said "by God, he will gather you". Al-Tabari holds the right view to be that "he has prescribed mercy for himself" is the end of a statement and "he will gather you" a fresh sentence, the sense then being: God will gather you, you who hold others equal to God, to the day of resurrection about which there is no doubt, to take vengeance on you for your unbelief in him. He says it is better than making "kataba" govern "he will gather you", since "kataba" has already governed "mercy" and cannot also govern "he will gather you", for it does not take two objects. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 279}

If someone asks what he says of the reading of [[passage/quran-6-54|6:54]], "your Lord has prescribed mercy for himself, that" with "anna", the answer is that so read, "anna" explains and expresses "mercy", since the sense is: he prescribed mercy for himself, that he be merciful to whoever of his servants repents after doing evil in ignorance, and forgive; mercy is explained by its description, and "he will gather you to the day of resurrection" is no description of mercy that could explain it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 279}

The editor says the two bracketed additions in this passage are his inference from the commentary and the verse, since they are missing from manuscript and print, and refers to the commentary on that verse at pp. 392 and 393 further on.

As for "no doubt in it", it means no uncertainty that God will gather you to the day of resurrection, raising you all to him, then give every doer the reward of what he did, good or bad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 280}

Then "those who have lost themselves, they do not believe": by "those who have lost themselves" he means those who hold idols and images equal to him, that is, who ruined themselves and cheated themselves by claiming a rival and equal to God, bringing them to perdition by earning God's wrath and his painful punishment in the return. The root of "khasar" is being cheated; one says "the man lost in the sale" when he was cheated, as al-A'sha said in a line from his poem satirising Alqama ibn Ulatha and praising Amir ibn al-Tufayl, describing a judge who takes no bribe in his ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 280}

The editor refers for "doubt" and "loss" to earlier volumes, corrects "by making obligatory God's wrath" to "by earning", and says the line is in al-A'sha's Diwan and Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, that the Diwan reads "ghabn al-khasir" and that Abu Jafar's "khasira al-khasir" is correct in language.

The case of "those who" in "those who have lost themselves" is accusative, in apposition to the "kum" of "he will gather you", as an explanation of it, since those who lost themselves are the ones addressed by "he will gather you". And "so they do not believe" means: through their ruin and cheating of themselves of their share they do not believe, that is, they do not affirm God's oneness, do not trust his promise and threat and do not acknowledge Muhammad's prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 281}

Verse 12 closes here at p. 281, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-13|6:13]].
