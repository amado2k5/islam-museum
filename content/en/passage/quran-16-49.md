## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "To Allah prostrates whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth of creatures and angels, and they are not arrogant": to Allah submit and submit to His command: whatever is in the heavens and the earth descends, and the angels in the heavens, and they are not arrogant in submitting to Him in obedience. "And those who do not believe in the Hereafter have hearts in denial and they are arrogant," and their shadows are cast away from the right and the shawls in prostration to Allah, while they humble themselves. Some grammarians of Basra used to say: I separate the mention of one of the animals from the mention of the whole. The meaning of the speech is: To Allah prostrate whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth of animals and angels, as it is said: 'Whatever has come to me from a man,' meaning: what has come to me of men.
