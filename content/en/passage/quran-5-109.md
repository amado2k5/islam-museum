## Summary
Verse 109 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 109 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "on the day God gathers the messengers": fear God, people, and listen to his warning and reminder, and beware of the day God gathers the messengers; then "beware" is dropped, and "fear God and listen" suffices without it, as a rajaz poet says he fed his beast straw and cold water, meaning and watered it with cold water, the feeding sufficing without saying watering because the hearer knows the sense; so "beware" is dropped here because the hearer knows, it being a warning from God's command to his creatures of his punishment for their disobedience. As for "what answer were you given", it means: what answer did your peoples give you, when... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 209}

The editor refers to the earlier note on the rajaz line in volume 1, corrects the printed "until she became" from the manuscript, and refers to earlier places for "what".

The rest of "what answer were you given": what answer did your peoples give when you called them to my oneness, to acknowledge me, to act in obedience to me and to refrain from disobeying me? "They say: we have no knowledge." The interpreters differed on this. Some said: their "we have no knowledge" was not a denial by the messengers that they knew what their peoples did, but they were stunned out of answering by the terror of that day, and answered once their minds returned, by bearing witness against their peoples. Report 12986, from al-Suddi: they came to a station where minds were dazed, and when asked said we have no knowledge; then they came to another station and bore witness against their people. Report 12987, from al-Hasan: out of the terror of that day. Report 12988, from Mujahid: they are terrified, and when asked what answer they were given, say we have no knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 210}

In 12986 the editor keeps the manuscript reading and drops a "lamma" that has no place, and notes that the chain of 12987 is plainly defective between Anbasa and al-Hasan al-Basri, so he put dots in its place until he can find the full chain.

Others said the meaning is: we have no knowledge except what you taught us; report 12989, from Mujahid: they say we have no knowledge except what you taught us, you are the knower of the unseen. Others said: no knowledge except knowledge that you know better than we; report 12990, from Ibn Abbas, the same. Others said that "what answer were you given" means what did they do after you and what did they invent; report 12991, from Ibn Jurayj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 211}

Al-Tabari holds the soundest to be the saying "no knowledge except knowledge that you know better than we", because God reports them as saying "we have no knowledge, you are the knower of the unseen", that is, nothing of what we know or anything else, hidden or plain, is hidden from you; the messengers deny only that they have knowledge, of what they were asked, that God does not have, not that they knew what they witnessed. How could it be otherwise, when God reports that they tell what answer their peoples gave and call witnesses to their delivering the message, as in [[passage/quran-2-143|2:143]] on the middle community? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 211}

As to Ibn Jurayj's saying that it means what the peoples did after you and what they invented, it is an interpretation without meaning, because the prophets had no knowledge of what would happen after them except what God told them, and if asked about what the peoples did after them, they would be told: what did we make known to you would come from them after you? The plain wording of God's report of his question points elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 212}

The editor corrects the printed "they call as witnesses to their kinship" from the manuscript.

Verse 109 closes here at p. 212, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-5-110|5:110]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
