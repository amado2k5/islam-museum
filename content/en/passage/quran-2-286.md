## Summary
Verse 286 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 286 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-286|2:286]]), pp. 129 to 133. Al-Tabari explains the first sentence as saying that God does not charge a soul by laying worship on it except with what it can bear, so He neither narrows it nor strains it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 129} The editor says the manuscript and print repeat the verse's wording in the middle of the explanation and he left it out, as the sentence requires; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 129} Al-Tabari recalls having shown earlier that the word for capacity is a noun from the saying 'this matter encompassed me', like the words for effort and means; the editor refers to part 5. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 130} In 6502, Ibn Abbas (through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Muawiya and Ali): they are the believers; God made the matter of their religion broad, as He said ([[passage/quran-22-78|22:78]]) that He laid no hardship on them in religion, and ([[passage/quran-2-185|2:185]]) that He wants ease for them and not hardship, and ([[passage/quran-64-16|64:16]]) that they should be mindful of Him as far as they can. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 130} The editor says manuscript and print give the wording of one verse differently from the reading text and he set the reading's wording; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 130} In 6503, Ibn Abbas (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and al-Zuhri): when it came down the believers cried out about it and said: Messenger of God, we repent of the work of hand, foot and tongue; how do we repent of whispering thoughts, how do we refrain from them? Gabriel came with this verse, meaning that they cannot refrain from whispering thoughts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 130} The editor says the phrase of surprise they used is eloquent Arabic, found in poetry, but he could not recall where; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 130} In 6504, al-Suddi (through Musa, Amr and Asbat): capacity is ability, and the soul's talk was something they could not bear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 130}

Then he explains the sentence that a soul has what it did of good and bears what it did of evil: 'has' refers to the soul he said He charges only within its capacity, so every soul has what it acquired and did of good, and 'upon it' means upon every soul is what it did of evil; the editor refers to the earlier commentary on earning and acquiring. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 131} In 6505, Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said): of good, and upon it, of evil or ill. In 6506, al-Suddi (through Musa, Amr and Asbat): what it did of good, and upon it what it did of evil. In 6507, Qatada (through Ammar, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father) the same. In 6508, Ibn Abbas (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and al-Zuhri): the work of hand, foot and tongue. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 131} Abu Jafar says: so the sense of the verse is that God charges a soul only with what it can bear, neither straining it nor narrowing it in the matter of its religion, so He does not hold it to account for a resolve if it resolves, nor a whisper if one comes to it, nor a passing thought if one crosses its heart; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 131}

Next al-Tabari explains the supplication not to be held for forgetting and error as a teaching from God to His believing servants of how to call on Him and what to say in their prayer; its sense is: say, do not hold us if we forget something You made obligatory for us and did not do it, or err in doing something You forbade us, not intending to disobey You but through ignorance and error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 132} In 6509, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): if we forget something You made obligatory for us, or err and [thereby] commit something You forbade us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 132} The editor says the bracketed word is almost required for the sentence to stand; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 132} In 6510, Qatada (through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Mamar): it reached me that the Prophet said that God overlooked for this community its forgetting and what its selves told it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 132} The editor traces it to Muslim's Sahih (by several routes from Qatada, Zurara ibn Awfa and Abu Hurayra, in the wording that God has overlooked for his community what their selves told them as long as they do not speak or act); this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 132} In 6511, al-Suddi (through Musa, Amr and Asbat): when this verse came down Gabriel told him: say that, Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 132}

He raises the question whether God may hold His servants for what they forgot or erred in, so that they should ask Him not to. He answers that forgetting is of two kinds: one is by the servant's neglect and negligence; the other by the forgetful one's inability to keep what he was charged with and his mind's weakness to bear it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 133} The first is a leaving of what he was commanded to do, and is what a servant asks God not to hold him to; it is the forgetting for which God punished Adam and removed him from the garden ([[passage/quran-20-115|20:115]]), and the forgetting God speaks of in [[passage/quran-7-51|7:51]]; the servant's plea not to be held for forgetting in this way is allowed so long as his leaving was not a negligence and neglect amounting to unbelief in God, for if it is unbelief then the plea not to be held to it is not allowed, since God told His servants that He does not forgive associating partners with Him, so asking Him to do what He said He would not do is a mistake; the request for forgiveness is for things like forgetting the Qur'an after learning it through being busy away from it and from reciting it, or forgetting a prayer or fast through being occupied with other things until he lost them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 133} The second, for which the servant is not held, is due to the weakness of his constitution to keep it and his mind's lack of capacity for what he was charged to watch; this is no disobedience in the servant and he is not sinful for it, so there is no ground for the servant to ask his Lord to forgive it, since that would be asking forgiveness of what is no sin; it is like a matter that overcomes him though he is keen to remember and keep it. This is al-Tabari's explanation and choice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 133}

These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices and arguments are his, and the editor's notes and references are his, with no museum verdict.

The rest of p. 134: for what may not be the object of a plea for forgiveness, al-Tabari gives the example of one who strives to memorise the Qur'an, recites it, and then forgets it, not because he was busy with something else but because of the weakness of his constitution to keep it and the small capacity of his mind for what was lodged in his heart, and the like; it is not permitted to ask the Lord to forgive this, since the servant has no sin in it for which to be forgiven on the ground of having acquired it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 134} Error too, he says, has two kinds. One is that the servant does what he was forbidden, with intent and will; that is an error of his for which he is held, and one says of him that he erred in the deed he did and was sinful when he committed what is sinful; he cites a line saying that people blame the commander when they miss the right course and the guide is not blamed. This is the kind where the servant asks his Lord to turn away from him the sin that came from him, except what was unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 134} The other kind is what happens through ignorance of it and the thought that he may do it, like one who eats at night in Ramadan thinking the dawn has not risen, or puts off a prayer on a cloudy day awaiting its time and then its time goes while he thinks it has not entered; this is the error laid aside from the servant, in which God has removed sin from His servants, so there is no ground for the servant to ask his Lord not to hold him to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 134} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 135} This is al-Tabari's explanation and choice, reported without any museum legal ruling.

In the notes to p. 134 the editor says the print has a wording different from the manuscript and that the manuscript's is right, and refers to part 2; that the poet is Ubayd ibn al-Abras al-Asadi, whom al-Buhturi's Hamasa calls 'Ubayd ibn Mansur al-Asadi', apparently a corruption; that the line is in his diwan, the Hamasa and Lisan al-Arab, with a wording in the diwan different from Lisan, whose wording is as al-Tabari has it; he glosses the verb for blaming as censure and rebuke, the commander as the one with authority whom they obey, and the guide as one whom God has guided to what is right, and sets beside it a line of al-Qutami of close sense; and he says that al-Tabari's use of the word for pardon here in the sense of turning away is good and sound, since if it were from the phrase 'he pardoned his sin' the wording would need to be otherwise. All of this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 134}

On p. 135 al-Tabari says that some people claimed the servant's plea to his Lord not to hold him for forgetting or error is only an act of his in obedience to what his Lord commanded or urged of humbling oneself to Him and submissiveness in asking, but as a plea for pardon it has no ground in their view; he says that a book will explain their case adequately; the editor refers to al-Sharif al-Murtada's Amali. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 135}

Then he explains the supplication not to be laden with a burden: the burden is the covenant, as in [[passage/quran-3-81|3:81]], so the plea is not to be laden with a covenant they would fail to keep and could not bear, as it was laid on the Jews and Christians who were charged with deeds and whose covenants and pledges were taken to uphold them, and did not, and were swiftly punished; God taught Muhammad's community to ask that He not lay on them, of His covenants and pledges, deeds that, if they neglected, erred in or forgot them, would bring on them for their error and neglect what He brought on those before them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 135} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 136} In 6512, Qatada (through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Mamar): a covenant and pledge as laid on those before, meaning as made heavy on those before us. In 6513 and 6514, Mujahid (through Ibn Waki, his father and Musa ibn Qays al-Hadrami; and through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih): a covenant. In 6515, Ibn Abbas (through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Muawiya and Ali): a covenant. In 6516, al-Suddi (through Musa, Amr and Asbat): the burden is the covenant that was on those before us of the Jews. In 6517, Ibn Jurayj (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj): a covenant we cannot bear nor keep, as laid on the Jews and Christians who did not keep it and were destroyed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 136} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 137} In 6518, al-Dahhak (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir): pledges. In 6519, al-Rabi' (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn Abi Jafar and his father): the covenant, citing the same word in [[passage/quran-3-81|3:81]] and saying 'my covenant'. In 6520, Ibn Abbas (through Muhammad ibn Sad, his father, uncle, father and grandfather): 'my covenant'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 137} On 6513 the editor notes: Musa ibn Qays al-Hadrami al-Farra al-Kufi, nicknamed 'bird of paradise', narrated from Salama ibn Kuhayl, Muhammad ibn Ajlan, Muslim al-Batin and others, and Waki, Yahya ibn Adam and Abu Nuaym from him; Ahmad said: I know only good of him; Ibn Sad: he had few reports; Ibn Main held him reliable; and al-Uqayli said he was among the extremists of the Rafida and related objectionable or false reports; this is the editor's citation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 136}

Others said the sense is that He should not lay on them sins and wrongdoing as He laid them on earlier nations, transforming them into apes and pigs as He transformed those. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 137} In 6521, Ata ibn Abi Rabah (through Said ibn Amr al-Sakuni, Baqiyya ibn al-Walid, Ali ibn Harun and Ibn Jurayj): do not transform us into apes and pigs. In 6522, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): do not lay on us a sin for which there is no repentance or expiation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 137} The editor says Said ibn Amr al-Sakuni's biography has been given, that he cannot find 'Ali ibn Harun' and thinks the right name is 'Yazid ibn Harun', since Baqiyya narrates from Yazid ibn Harun and died before him; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 137}

Others said the word, with a kasra on its first letter, means weight. In 6523, al-Rabi' (through Ammar, Ibn Abi Jafar and his father): the strictness He imposed on those before us of the People of the Book. In 6524, Malik (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb, who asked him): the burden is the heavy matter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 138} Al-Tabari adds that the same word with a fatha on its first letter means what inclines a man to another out of kinship; one says 'the kinship between me and so-and-so inclined me to him' and 'there is a bond of kinship between us'; and the editor says manuscript and print have a different wording and that the sense of the explanation requires what the dictionaries confirm, which he set. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 138}

Then he explains the supplication not to be burdened beyond strength as: say also, do not charge us with deeds we cannot bear for the weight of carrying them; the company of interpreters understood it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 138} In 6525, Qatada (through Bishr, Yazid and Said): a severity such as was laid on those before you. In 6526, al-Dahhak (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir): do not burden us with deeds we cannot bear. In 6527, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): do not make obligatory on us of religion what we have no strength for, so that we fail. In 6528, Ibn Jurayj (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj): the transformation into apes and pigs. In 6529, Salim ibn Shabur (through Sallam ibn Salim al-Khuzai, Abu Hafs Umar ibn Said al-Tanukhi and Muhammad ibn Shuayb ibn Shabur): strong sexual desire. In 6530, al-Suddi (through Musa, Amr and Asbat): the severity and the shackles of prohibition that were on them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 139} On 6529 the editor says: Sallam ibn Salim al-Khuzai's biography has been given; Abu Hafs Umar ibn Said al-Tanukhi is Umar ibn Said ibn Sulayman Abu Hafs al-Qurashi al-Dimashqi, a narrator of Said ibn Abd al-Aziz al-Tanukhi, so called as if attributed to him, who narrated from Muhammad ibn Shuayb ibn Shabur, in al-Tahdhib and Tarikh Baghdad; Muhammad ibn Shuayb ibn Shabur al-Dimashqi was one of the great, narrated from al-Awzai and Said ibn Abd al-Aziz and others, lived in Beirut, was listed by Ibn Hibban among the reliable, and died in 200; and he glosses the word as the surge of sexual desire in man or woman; this is the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 139}

Abu Jafar says: we say the sense is that He should not charge us with deeds we cannot keep up because it follows the believers' plea not to be held if they forget or err and not to be laden with a burden as He laid on those before them; so joining it to the sense of what precedes, the plea for ease in religion, is better than what departs from that sense; this is his choice and argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 139} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 140}

On the plea for pardon, he says that this too, in God's report from the believers of what they asked of Him, is clear proof that they asked Him to ease His obligations for them, because they followed it with a plea to pardon any falling short they have in some of the obligations He commanded, so that He overlooks it and does not punish them for it, even if what He charged them with of the obligations is light on their bodies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 140} In 6531, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): pardon us if we fall short in anything of Your command that You gave us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 140} As for the plea for forgiveness, it means: cover for us a slip if we commit one between us and You, and do not expose it or shame us by showing it; he has shown the sense of forgiveness earlier, and the editor refers to the nearby note and the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 140} In 6532, Ibn Zayd: if we violate anything You forbade us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 141}

Then he explains the plea for mercy as: wrap us in a mercy from You by which You save us from Your punishment, for none is saved from Your punishment except by Your mercy to him and not by his deed, and our deeds will not save us if You do not have mercy on us, so grant us success in what pleases You. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 141} In 6533, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): we do not attain doing what You commanded us, nor leaving what You forbade us, except by Your mercy, and none is saved but by Your mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 141} The editor says the print has 'we do not leave' and that he kept the manuscript's, which is right, with the verb in the subjunctive joined to 'doing'; this is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 141}

Then al-Tabari begins on the last clause of the verse: God's being described as their protector means that He is their ally by His help, not those who oppose Him and disbelieve in Him, for they believe in Him and obey Him in what He commanded and forbade, so He is the ally of whoever obeys Him and the enemy of whoever disbelieves in Him and disobeys Him; then the plea for help, because they are His party. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 141}

We stop at the end of p. 141, in the explanation of the last clause of the sura. These are reports as transmitted, al-Tabari's choices and arguments are his, and the editor's notes and references are his, with no museum verdict.
