## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "but whoever fears from a testator partiality or sin and sets things right between them, there is no sin on him; God is Forgiving, Merciful" al-Tabari reports the interpreters' differences. Some said: whoever attends a sick man making a bequest as death nears and fears he will err in it or deliberately wrong, there is no blame on him for reconciling him with his heirs by telling him to be just; from Mujahid, if he goes beyond they bid him be just, and if he falls short they say give so-and-so such. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 399} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 400} Others said: whoever, of the dead man's guardians or the one in charge of Muslims' affairs, fears partiality in the dead man's bequest and reconciles his heirs and the beneficiaries, returning the bequest to justice and truth, there is no blame or sin; from Ibn 'Abbas, if the dead man erred or was unjust in his bequest, the guardians do not sin by returning his error to the right; from Qatada, if a man bequeathed wrongfully or unfairly and the dead man's guardian or a Muslim ruler returned it to God's Book and to justice, that is his to do; from al-Rabi' and Ibrahim, the executor returned it to the right after his death; and from Ibrahim, asked about one who bequeathed more than a third, "return it". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 400} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 401}

Others said: whoever fears from a testator partiality or sin in his gift, on the approach of his death, to some of his heirs and not others, there is no blame on one who reconciles them, from 'Ata', whom Ibn Jurayj asked about giving an heir at death when there is no bequest for an heir, and he said that is in what is divided among them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 401} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 402} Others said his partiality and sin are in a bequest to a non-heir whose benefit returns to an heir, from Tawus: such as a man bequeathing to his son's sons so the wealth goes to their father, or bequeathing the whole third while heirs are many and wealth little; the executor or the ruler reconciles them after his death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 402} Others said: whoever fears from a testator for his parents and kin partiality of some to others and reconciles them, there is no sin on him; from al-Suddi, "janaf" is an error in his bequest and "ithm" is a deliberate wrong, and he reconciles them as he sees right, and Ibn Zayd said much the same and that God removed that from them and set the fixed shares. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 402} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 403}

Al-Tabari chooses that the sense is: whoever fears that a testator will incline away from the truth by mistake or deliberately sin in his bequest, by bequeathing to his parents and kin who do not inherit more than is permitted him, beyond the third or the whole third while the wealth is little and the heirs many, there is no blame on one present who reconciles those bequeathed to with the heirs by bidding the dying man do what is customary, telling him what God permitted and forbidding him to exceed it; and likewise for one with much wealth and few heirs who wants to bequeath less than a third, the one present reconciles by telling him to increase his bequest up to the third God allowed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 403} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 404} His ground is that God said "whoever fears", and fear is before the partiality or sin occurs, whereas if it had occurred He would have said "whoever becomes clear about". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 404} Asked how there can be reconciliation when heirs were not mentioned, he answers that those whom God ordered bequests for were mentioned, and reconciling between him and them is reconciling them and the testator's heirs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 404} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 405}

He notes two readings of "musin", light and heavy on the sad, both well-known Arab usages; "janaf" is injustice and swerving from the truth, with a line of poetry; and the sense is: whoever fears from a testator partiality in the place of the bequest and a swerve from what is right, or sin by doing it knowingly, and reconciles them, there is no sin on him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 405} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 406} From Ibn 'Abbas, 'Ata', al-Dahhak, al-Suddi, Mujahid, al-Rabi' and Ibrahim, "janaf" is error and "ithm" is deliberate; from 'Ata' and Tawus, "janaf" is inclination; and from Ibn Zayd, "janaf" is injustice and "ithm" his inclining to some over others, all coming to one thing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 406} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 407}

The reports continue: from Mujahid "error or deliberately", from Ibn Jurayj on Ibn 'Abbas that "janaf" is error and "ithm" deliberate, and from al-Dahhak the same; the editor notes in a footnote that a narrator in the chain of the al-Dahhak report is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 407} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 408} On "God is forgiving, merciful", al-Tabari takes it that God forgives the testator what he privately resolved of partiality and sin if he left off from it in his bequest, passing over what he had meant since he did not carry it out, and is merciful to the one who reconciles between the testator and whoever wanted to wrong him in favour of another or sin in it for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 408}
