## Summary
Verse 127 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 127 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 192).** Abu Ja'far says: God helped you at Badr so as to destroy a party of those who disbelieved in God and His Messenger and denied their Lord's oneness and their Prophet's prophethood; by the word for a "part" he means a group or band. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 192}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7796, number unreadable, inferred): at Badr God cut off a part of the unbelievers, killing their chiefs, leaders and commanders in evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 192}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7797): like it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 192}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, Abbad, al-Hasan (7798): this is Badr; God cut off a group of them and a group remained. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 192}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7799): to cut off a part of the polytheists by a killing in which He takes vengeance on them. The editor says this is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 114), following report 7794, and that the manuscript and printed text dropped "from Ibn Ishaq", which he restored because it is a recurring chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 192}

**Another view of the verse (p. 193).** Others said the sense is that victory comes only from God, so that He may destroy a part of the unbelievers, and that they meant those killed at Uhud. From Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7800): God mentioned the polytheists killed at Uhud, who were eighteen men, then mentioned the martyrs in another verse, [[passage/quran-3-169|3:169]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 193}

**Shaming and turning back (pp. 193-194).** Abu Ja'far says the word means humbling them with the failure of what they hoped to gain over the Muslims; it was also said to mean throwing them down on their faces, and someone reported hearing the Arabs say that in the sense of "God threw him down", which the editor ascribes to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 103). He then summarises the sense: God helped you at Badr to destroy a party of the unbelievers by the sword, or to humble them by the failure of what they coveted, so that they turn back from you having got nothing of what they hoped for. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 193}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7801): or to send them back empty, that is, those of them who remain return routed, having got nothing of what they hoped for. In two notes the editor says the manuscript and printed text read "those who remain" without the word for the routed, that he restored it from Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 114), that the printed text had dropped it because the copyist crossed it out with an incomplete stroke which the publisher took as a deletion mark, and he explains the word as meaning those put to flight, used for one or many; the report follows the earlier ones ending with 7799. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 193}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7802): He shames them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 194}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7803): the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 194}
