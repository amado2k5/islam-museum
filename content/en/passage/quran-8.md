## Summary
Surat al-Anfal: surah 8 in the order of the Mushaf, Medinan, 75 verses, and revelation order 88 among the surahs. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Content
Its verses are recorded in the museum one by one under the records passage/quran-8-‹verse number›.

## In Tantawi's al-Tafsir al-Wasit
In his introduction to the sura in [[work/al-tafsir-al-wasit|al-Tafsir al-Wasit]], [[person/muhammad-sayyid-tantawi|Tantawi]] says that it is eighth in the order of the mushaf, coming after the Meccan al-Fatiha, four long Medinan suras and two long Meccan ones, and that it has 75 verses in the Kufan mushaf, 76 in the Hijazi and 77 in the Syrian. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2328} It is called al-Anfal because it speaks of the anfal, the spoils, in more than one place; some Companions called it "the sura of Badr", for Abu al-Shaykh reports from Sa'id ibn Jubayr that [[person/abdullah-ibn-abbas|Ibn Abbas]] was asked about it and said: that is the sura of Badr. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2328}

He holds it to be wholly Medinan, as did [[person/zayd-ibn-thabit|Zayd ibn Thabit]], Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, [[person/ata-ibn-abi-rabah|Ata']], [[person/al-hasan-al-basri|al-Hasan]] and [[person/ikrima-mawla-ibn-abbas|Ikrima]]. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2328} He quotes the author of al-Manar on views that exempt some verses — verse 64, reported from Ibn Abbas to have come down when Umar embraced Islam, and verse 30, which Muqatil exempted because it concerns Quraysh's plot against the Prophet just before [[event/hijra|the Hijra]] — and al-Manar's reply that describing the unbelievers of Mecca does not mean revelation in Mecca. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2328} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2329} Tantawi prefers that the whole sura is Medinan, its descriptions of the idolaters before the Hijra being reminders to the Messenger and his Companions. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2329}

He notes that al-Zamakhshari and others said it came down after al-Baqara, and takes this to mean after some verses of al-Baqara, since al-Baqara came down in instalments until shortly before the Prophet's death. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2329} He quotes al-Alusi's correspondences between it and al-A'raf, but holds that many such correspondences "are not free of strain", and that the order of the suras is fixed by revelation. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2329} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2330} He sees the sura as a whole as speaking of [[event/battle-of-badr|the battle of Badr]], citing al-Bukhari's report from Ibn Abbas that it came down about Badr, and then sets out its themes: the division of spoils, the qualities of believers, the reluctance of some to fight at first, the glad tidings of reinforcement, drowsiness and rain, six addresses to the believers, the captives and their ransom, treaties, and the standing of the Emigrants and the Helpers. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2330} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2331} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2332} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2333} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2334} {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2335} He ends the introduction with a summary of the Badr story from Ibn Hisham's Sira, because he holds that knowing "the historical atmosphere" helps one understand the verses. {cite:tafsir-tantawi-wasit p.2336}
