**Commentary on verse 59 (pp. 154-155)**

On "and when he had provided them with their provisions he said, bring me a brother of yours from your father; do you not see that I give full measure and that I am the best of hosts?", al-Tabari says: when Joseph loaded his brothers' camels with food, a full load for each man's camel, he said to them "bring me a brother of yours from your father" so that I may load you another camel and you gain another camel's load; "do you not see that I give full measure", so that I wrong no one; "and I am the best of hosts", that is, the best of those who take a guest into their house among the people of this town, so I will host you. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "ifa'" and "kayl". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 154}

Report 19466, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: "and I am the best of hosts", Joseph says it, I am the best of those who host in Egypt; the editor corrects "yaquluhu" from the manuscript. Report 19467, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when Joseph provided the people, he loaded each of them with one camel for their number, then said to them "bring me a brother of yours from your father" and I will give you another camel; "do you not see that I give full measure", that is, I wrong people of nothing; "and I am the best of hosts", that is, better for you than others, for if you bring him I will honour your standing and be good to you, and you will gain a camel beyond your number, since I give each man only one camel. Report 19468, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "a brother of yours from your father" means Benjamin, who is Joseph's brother by his father and mother. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 155}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
