## Summary
Verse 1 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 1 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The start of the commentary on the sura of al-Nisa (pp. 511-513).** Page 511 carries the title "Commentary on the sura of al-Nisa" and p. 512 has no text; p. 513 begins with the heading "The commentary on the sura in which women are mentioned", the basmala and the prayer for ease with which the copyist opens.

**Verse 1: one origin of creation (pp. 513-514).** Al-Tabari says the address to the people means: beware your Lord, lest you disobey Him in what He commanded and forbade, so that there falls on you from His punishment what you cannot withstand. God then described Himself as the One alone in creating all humankind from a single person, making known to His servants how the beginning of their coming into being from that one origin was, and alerting them that all of them are children of one man and one mother, that they are of one another, and that the right of each over the other is binding as a brother's right over his brother, because they meet in descent from one father and one mother; and that what binds them to respect one another's right, though the meeting in descent from their common father is distant, is like what binds them in the nearer kinship; and inclining them towards one another, so that they deal fairly and do not wrong each other, and the strong gives the weak his due with kindness as God obliged him to. In the editor's note the printed edition and the manuscript read "and He made known to His servants", and he concluded from al-Tabari's manner of exposition, and from his later "and alerting them" and then "and inclining", that the right reading is "making known", as the flow of the sentence requires, and that "inclining" is joined to "making known" and what follows. Al-Tabari then says the creation from the one origin is from Adam.

**Reports 8400-8402 (p. 514).** Al-Suddi (8400), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal from Asbat: as for the creation from the one origin, it is from Adam. Qatada (8401), through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid ibn Zuray' from Sa'id: he means Adam. Mujahid (8402), through Sufyan ibn Waki' from his father from Sufyan from a man: Adam. In two notes the editor says the printed edition added a formula of blessing on the Prophet after Adam's name in the first two reports and he set what the manuscript has.

**The feminine form of the word for "soul" (p. 514).** Al-Tabari says a parallel, where a man is meant, is the saying of the poet, in a line addressed to a praised man saying that his father was a caliph borne by another woman (in the feminine) while he means the man, using the feminine because of the word "caliph"; God used the feminine description here because the word for "soul" is feminine, the sense being "from one man"; had it been said in the masculine, giving the wording the masculine for the sense, it would have been right; the line is described and not reproduced. The note refers to the line and its tracing in volume 6, and says this is al-Farra's remark in Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 252).

**The creation of the mate (p. 515, reports 8403-8406).** Al-Tabari says the mate is the second of the pair for the one origin, and according to the interpreters she is his wife Eve. In the editor's note, for "mate" he refers to the earlier explanation and says that by "second" al-Tabari meant that "zawj" is the single one that has a partner, so that each of the two companions is called a "zawj", and then the wife of a man and the man who is the woman's partner are each called "zawj". Mujahid (8403), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr from Abu 'Asim from 'Isa from Ibn Abi Najih: Eve, from the short rib of Adam while he slept; he woke and said in Nabatean "atha", that is, a woman; and the editor says the short rib is the lowest of the ribs, or the rib next to the flank between the side and the belly. Mujahid (8404), through al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl from Ibn Abi Najih, the same. Qatada (8405), through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid from Sa'id: Eve, created from Adam, from one of his ribs. Al-Suddi (8406), through Musa ibn Harun from 'Amr ibn Hammad from Asbat: God settled Adam in paradise and he walked in it alone (that is, by himself, as the note says), having no mate to find rest in; he slept a sleep and woke to find a woman sitting by his head, whom He created ... and p. 515 breaks off here, with the text of the report continuing on p. 516.
