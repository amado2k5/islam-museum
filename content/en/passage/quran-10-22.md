**Commentary on verse 22 (pp. 50-53)**

He then explains "It is He who makes you travel on land and sea, until, when you are on the ships and they sail with them in a good wind and they rejoice in it, a stormy wind comes upon them and waves come at them from every side, and they think they are encircled, they call on God, sincere to Him in religion: if You save us from this, we shall be among the thankful". God is the One who makes you travel, people, on land on the back and on the sea in ships, "and they sailed with them" means the ships ran with the people in a good wind at sea, "and they rejoiced in it" means the ship's passengers rejoiced in the good wind by which they travel, the pronoun referring to the good wind; "a stormy wind came upon them" means a violent wind came upon the ship. The editor refers to the gloss on "ship" in the twelfth volume. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 50}

The Arabs say "a stormy wind" in two forms, and "the wind blew hard" in two verbs, one of them used among the Banu Asad, it is said; he cites a line by one of the Banu Dubayr of Asad describing a violent shaking wind with rain and a loud, echoing thunder (described, not quoted), and the editor says he did not identify the poet and explains three words in it. "Waves came at them from every side" means the waves came to the ship's passengers from every side; "they thought they were encircled" means they thought destruction had surrounded and encompassed them; "they called on God, sincere to Him in religion" means they made their supplication sincere to God there, without their idols and gods, their refuge then being to God alone. The editor refers to the gloss on "encompassing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 51}

Report 17595, from Qatada: when harm touches them at sea they make their supplication sincere to Him. Report 17596, from Abu Ubayda, on "sincere to Him in religion": the words "hayya shrahayya" (a foreign phrase), whose explanation is "O Living, O Self-Subsisting"; the editor says the phrase is undoubtedly non-Arabic and that he could not identify it. Report 17597, from Ibn Zayd: these polytheists call on whatever they call on besides God, but when harm comes they call on none but God, and when He saves them they associate others again. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 52}

"If You save us from this" means from this hardship we are in, "we shall be among the thankful" to You for Your favours and for delivering us from our plight, by being sincere in worship of You and giving obedience to You alone, not to gods and rivals. The readers differed on "He makes you travel": most readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read "yusayyirukum" from travelling, and Abu Ja'far the reader read "yanshurukum" from spreading, taking it to mean that God sends out His servants and spreads them over land and sea, close in meaning to making them travel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 52}

He notes that the verse says "they sailed with them in a good wind" and elsewhere "in the laden ship", using the singular; "ship" is a name for one and for many, and is treated as masculine or feminine, the editor referring to al-Farra. The verse also says "they sailed with them" after "makes you travel", addressing and then turning to speak of those absent, a shift he has explained in many places. The answer to "until, when you are on the ships" is "a stormy wind came upon them", and the answer to "they thought they were encircled" is "they called on God, sincere to Him in religion". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 53}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
