## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And the Day We will raise in every nation a witness against them from themselves, and We will bring you as a witness against those and send down to you the Book as an explanation of all things, and a guidance and mercy and good tidings for the Muslims": "And the Day We will raise in every nation a witness against them from themselves," meaning: We will ask their Prophet whom We sent to them to call to obey Us. He said: "From themselves," because He, the Exalted, used to send to nations whose prophets were among them: What did they answer you, and what did they respond to you? "We have brought you as a witness against these," he says to His Prophet Muhammad: We have brought you, O Muhammad, as a witness over your people and your nation to whom You have sent you for what they responded to you and what they did in what You sent you to them. And His saying: "And We have sent down to you the Book as an explanation of all things," means: This Qur'an was revealed to you, Muhammad, clarifying everything people need, such as knowing what is lawful and unlawful, reward, and punishment. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid, Ibn Jurayj, and Al-A'mash.
