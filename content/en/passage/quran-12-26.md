Verse 26 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 26 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 26 (pp. 53-55)**

Al-Tabari says: Joseph said, when the Aziz's wife accused him of what she accused him of, wanting the indecency from her, denying her charge and repelling what was ascribed to him: I did not solicit her; rather she solicited me. It is also said that Joseph would not have mentioned it had she not accused him before her master as she did; report 19098, from Nawf al-Shami, through Muhammad ibn Umara, Ubayd Allah ibn Musa, Shayban and Abu Ishaq: Joseph did not want to mention it until she said "what is the reward of one who intended evil toward your wife", and he grew angry and said, she solicited me. The editor refers to the explanation of "soliciting" and notes that Nawf's name is corrupted in the printed text, the right form being Nawf ibn Fadala al-Bikali al-Himyari al-Shami. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 53}

On "and a witness from her household bore witness" the people of knowledge differed on the witness's description. Some said he was an infant in the cradle. Report 19099, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', al-Ala ibn Abd al-Jabbar, Hammad ibn Salama, Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: four spoke in the cradle while small: the son of Pharaoh's hairdresser, Joseph's witness, Jurayj's companion, and Jesus son of Mary. Report 19100, from Abu Hurayra: Jesus, Joseph's companion and Jurayj's companion spoke in the cradle. Reports 19101 to 19106, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Hilal ibn Yasaf: an infant in the cradle. The editor notes that al-Ala ibn Abd al-Jabbar al-Attar is sound in hadith, that Abu Bakr al-Hudhali was a liar whose hadith is abandoned, though Abu Hurayra's long report was recorded by al-Bukhari in his Sahih, by Muslim and by Ahmad with a sound chain, and that Ayyub ibn Jabir al-Yamami is weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 54}

Report 19107, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad, Abu Marzuq and Juwaybir: an infant whom God made speak; it is also said a man of judgement who spoke from his judgement. Report 19108, from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet, through Affan, Hammad, Ata ibn al-Sa'ib and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: "four spoke while small," and he named among them Joseph's witness. The editor notes that the hadith is long, recorded by Ahmad in the Musnad with a sound chain, and that its last statement is a saying of Ibn Abbas not traced back to the Prophet. Report 19109, from al-Dahhak: they claim he was an infant in the house. Report 19110, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father and his uncle, and it continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 55}

Report 19110 continues, from Ibn Abbas: he was an infant in the cradle. Others said: he was a bearded man; report 19111, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima: he was bearded. Report 19112, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Abi Mulayka: he was one of the king's intimates. Reports 19113 and 19114, from Ikrima, through Imran ibn Hudayr and then Sawwar ibn Abd Allah and Abd al-Malik ibn al-Sabbah: he was not an infant but a wise man. Reports 19115 to 19118, from Mujahid and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a man. Reports 19119, 19121 and 19122, from Ibn Abbas: bearded. Report 19120, from al-Suddi: her cousin was the witness from her household. Report 19123, from Ibn Abi Mulayka: one of the king's intimates. Reports 19124 and 19125, from Qatada: a wise man of her household. The editor notes that Abd al-Malik ibn al-Sabbah al-Misma'i is trustworthy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 56}

Report 19126, from Mujahid: he was a man. Report 19127, from al-Hasan: a man of judgement who gave his opinion. Report 19128, from Ibn Ishaq: it is said the witness was only an adviser, a man of Itfir's household whose opinion he sought, and he said: I testify that if his shirt was torn from the front she has spoken the truth and he is among the liars. It is also said the meaning of "a witness bore witness" is "a judge judged", report 19129, from al-Farra', from Mujahid. Others said the witness meant is the torn shirt; reports 19130 and 19131, from Mujahid: his shirt was torn from the back, and that was the testimony. Report 19132, from Mujahid: he was not of mankind, and 19133: he was by God's decree and not human. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 58}

Al-Tabari said: the right view is that of the one who said he was an infant in the cradle, for the report we mentioned from the Messenger of God, who listed those who spoke in the cradle and named Joseph's witness among them. As for what Mujahid said, that it was the torn shirt, it has no sense, for God reported that the witness was from the woman's household, saying "from her household", and a shirt is not said to be of a man's or a woman's household. On "if his shirt is torn from the front, she has spoken the truth and he is among the liars" he said: when someone pursued is fleeing he is caught from behind, so it was known that if the tear were in front he would not have been fleeing and pursued but pursuing and repelled, and that would be testimony to his lying. Report 19134, from Ibn Ishaq: when Itfir saw his shirt torn from behind he knew it was her scheming and said, "it is of your scheming, your scheming is great." Report 19135, from Qatada: the shirt decides between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 59}

Al-Tabari said: the "anna" that normally follows "testimony" is dropped because "testimony" is taken in the sense of "saying", as if he had said, "a speaker of her household said, if his shirt...", as in the verse of the bequest in inheritance [[passage/quran-4-11|4:11]], where "bequest" is taken in the sense of "saying". On "when he saw his shirt torn from behind" it is a report about the woman's husband, who is the one who said to her that this deed is of your scheming, that is, of the doing of women, your scheming is great; it is also said the report is about the witness as the speaker. The editor refers to the explanation of "scheming". This passage covers verses 26, 27 and 28 together, which al-Tabari gave as one section. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 60}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
