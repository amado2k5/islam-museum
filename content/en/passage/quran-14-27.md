## Summary
Verse 27 of [[passage/quran-14|Surat Ibrahim]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 27 of 52 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 27 (pp. 589-604)**

Al-Tabari says on this verse: by making firm those who believe, He means that He confirms their deeds and their faith, and by the firm word, the true word, which is said to be the testimony that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger. On "in the life of this world", the interpreters differed. Some said it means that God makes them firm in their graves before the Hour. Report 20758, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Abu al-Sa'ib Salm ibn Junada, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: the making firm in this life is when the two angels come to him in the grave and ask, who is your Lord? He says, my Lord is God. They ask, what is your religion? He says, my religion is Islam. They ask, who is your prophet? He says, my prophet is Muhammad. That is the making firm in this life. In a note the editor says Abu Ja'far related the hadith of al-Bara' by fourteen chains at this place, that he gathered them so they can be traced easily, and that they come by three routes: that of Sa'd ibn Ubayda, that of Zadhan and that of Khaythama. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 589}

Report 20759, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Abu Kurayb, Jabir ibn Nuh, al-A'mash and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: like it in meaning. Report 20760, from al-Bara', through Abd Allah ibn Ishaq al-Naqid al-Wasiti, Wahb ibn Jarir, Shu'ba, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: the Prophet mentioned the believer and the disbeliever and said, when the believer is asked in his grave he says, my Lord is God; that is His saying about making firm those who believe with the firm word in this life and the next. In the notes the editor sets out the routes from Sa'd ibn Ubayda: that of al-A'mash, with Abu Mu'awiya's report 20758 and Jabir ibn Nuh's report 20759, and that of Alqama ibn Marthad, which Abu Ja'far has by three chains, through Wahb ibn Jarir (20760), Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik (20761) and Affan (20773); he identifies the narrators, and the printed note here is partly garbled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 590}

Report 20761, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, Shu'ba, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: the Messenger of God said, when the Muslim is asked in the grave he testifies that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger; that is His saying in this verse. Report 20762, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, through al-Husayn ibn Salama ibn Abi Kabsha, Muhammad ibn Mamar al-Bahrani, Abu Amir Abd al-Malik ibn Amr, Abbad ibn Rashid, Dawud ibn Abi Hind and Abu Nadra: we were with the Messenger of God at a funeral and he said, and the report continues on the next page. The editor notes that the hadith of Shu'ba from Alqama was related by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, that it is a sound hadith, and he points out small differences between the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 591}

The rest of report 20762, from Abu Sa'id: O people, this community is tried in its graves. When a person is buried and his companions disperse, an angel comes with a hammer in his hand, sits him up and says, what do you say of this man? If he is a believer he testifies that there is no god but God alone with no partner and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, and the angel says, you have spoken truly. A door to the fire is opened for him and it is said, this would have been your dwelling had you disbelieved in your Lord, but since you believed, God has replaced it with this; then a door to paradise is opened and he wishes to rise to it, and he is told, be still; then his grave is widened for him. As for the disbeliever or hypocrite, he is asked what he says of this man and says, I do not know; and it is said, you did not know and you did not seek to know and you were not guided. Then a door to paradise is opened and it is said, this would have been your dwelling had you believed, but since you disbelieved God has replaced it with this; then a door to the fire is opened, and the angel strikes him with the hammer a blow that all God's creation hears except the two weighty ones, jinn and humans. One of his companions said, O Messenger of God, none of us has an angel standing at his head with a hammer in his hand without being terrified; and the Messenger of God recited the verse. In the notes the editor identifies the narrators and says the chain is sound, that Ahmad related it and that al-Haythami cited it in Majma' al-Zawa'id. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 592}

Report 20763, from al-Bara', through Abu Kurayb, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, al-A'mash, al-Minhal and Zadhan: the Messenger of God mentioned the taking of the believer's soul and said, his soul is returned to his body and two angels come and sit him up in his grave and ask, who is your Lord? He says, my Lord is God. They ask, what is your religion? He says, my religion is Islam. They ask, who is this man who was sent among you? He says, he is the Messenger of God. They ask, how do you know? He says, I read God's book, believed in it and affirmed it. Then a caller calls from the sky, my servant has spoken truly. In a long note the editor discusses the word "mitraq" (hammer), which is firm in all the versions and sound Arabic, and the phrase "la darayta wa la tadarayta", preferring the manuscript's wording; he sets out what Yunus, Ibn al-Athir and al-Zamakhshari said about the variant "la talayta" used by the hadith scholars and what it means, and notes that Ahmad's version adds, I do not know; I heard people saying something. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 593}

The report 20763 ends: that is God's saying about making firm those who believe. Report 20764, from al-Bara', through Abu al-Sa'ib, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash, al-Minhal and Zadhan: like it. Report 20765, from al-Bara', through Ibn Humayd, Ibn Waki', Jarir, al-A'mash, al-Minhal and Zadhan: like it. In the notes the editor says this is the second route, that of Zadhan from al-Bara', which Abu Ja'far related by two chains, that of al-A'mash from al-Minhal ibn Amr from Zadhan and that of Yunus ibn Khabbab from al-Minhal, and that he has it from al-A'mash by five routes (reports 20763 to 20767); he gives what was said about al-Minhal ibn Amr, whom some criticised and a group held reliable, and about Zadhan al-Kindi, a trustworthy Successor, and notes that al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak collected the routes and supporting reports, that Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Hakim related it, and that al-Haythami said it is in the Sahih and elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 594}

Report 20766, from al-Bara', through Ibn Waki', Ibn Numayr, al-A'mash, al-Minhal ibn Amr and Zadhan: like it. Report 20767, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Ibn Humayd, al-Hakam ibn Bashir, Amr ibn Qays, Yunus ibn Khabbab, al-Minhal and Zadhan: the like. Report 20768, from al-Bara', through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar, and also through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Sa'id ibn Mansur and Mahdi ibn Maymun, all from Yunus ibn Khabbab, from al-Minhal ibn Amr, from Zadhan: the Messenger of God mentioned the seizing of the believer's soul and said, someone comes to him in his grave and asks, who is your Lord, what is your religion, who is your prophet? He says, my Lord is God, my religion is Islam and my prophet is Muhammad. The questioner rebukes him and asks again, who is your Lord, what is your religion? This is the last trial presented to the believer, and that is when God speaks of making firm those who believe with the firm word; and he says, my Lord is God, my religion is Islam and my prophet is Muhammad; the report continues on the next page. The editor notes that Abd Allah ibn Numayr's report from al-A'mash was related by Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Hakim, and that Yunus ibn Khabbab is very weak, citing the verdicts of Yahya al-Qattan, Ibn Ma'in, al-Bukhari, Ibn Hibban and Abu Hatim on him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 595}

The end of report 20768: it is said to him, you have spoken truly; the wording is Ibn Abd al-A'la's. Report 20769, from Abu Hurayra, through Muhammad ibn Khalaf al-Asqalani, Adam, Hammad ibn Salama, Muhammad ibn Amr and Abu Salama: the Messenger of God recited the verse and said, that is when it is asked in the grave, who is your Lord, what is your religion? He says, my Lord is God, my religion is Islam and my prophet is Muhammad, who came with the clear proofs from God, so I believed in him and affirmed him; it is said, you have spoken truly, on this you lived, on this you died and on this you will be raised. Report 20770, from Abu Hurayra, through Mujahid ibn Musa, al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Yazid, Muhammad ibn Amr and Abu Salama: the dead person hears the beat of the sandals as people turn away from him. If he is a believer, prayer is at his head, almsgiving on his right, fasting on his left, and the doing of good, of charity, kinship, kindness and goodness to people, at his feet. He is approached from his head and prayer says, there is no way in from my side; from his right and almsgiving says the same; from his left and fasting says the same; from his feet and the doing of good says the same. In the notes the editor says that Hammad ibn Salama and Muhammad ibn Amr are trustworthy, that the chain is sound, and that al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 596}

The rest of report 20770: he is told, sit up, and he sits while the sun appears to him close to setting, and is told, tell us about what we ask you. He says, let me pray first. It is said, you will, but tell us what we ask. He says, what do you ask about? It is said, this man who was among you, what do you say of him and what do you testify of him? He says, Muhammad? It is said, yes. He says, I testify that he is the Messenger of God and that he brought the clear proofs from God, and we believed him. It is said to him, on this you lived, on this you died and on this you will be raised, God willing. Then his grave is widened seventy cubits and lit for him; a door to paradise is opened and he is told, look at what God has prepared for you in it, and his delight grows; a door to the fire is opened and he is told, look at what God has turned away from you had you disobeyed, and his delight grows. Then his spirit is placed among the good spirits, which are green birds hanging on the trees of paradise, and his body returns to the dust from which it began, and that is God's saying about making firm those who believe. Report 20771, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Abu Qatan, al-Mas'udi and Abd Allah ibn Mukhariq from his father: when the believer, and the report continues on the next page. The editor notes that al-Hakim related the first report and judged it sound on Muslim's condition, which al-Dhahabi followed, that al-Haythami says al-Tabarani related it in al-Awsat with a good chain, and that al-Suyuti attributes it to several, among them Ibn Abi Shayba, Ibn al-Mundhir, Ibn Hibban and al-Bayhaqi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 597}

The rest of report 20771: when the believer dies he is sat up in his grave and asked, who is your Lord, what is your religion, who is your prophet? God makes him firm and he says, my Lord is God, my religion is Islam and my prophet is Muhammad; then Abd Allah recited the verse. In a long note the editor discusses the men of this chain: he says Abu Qatan and al-Mas'udi are known, that al-Mas'udi grew confused late in life but that the early narrators from him are sound, and then examines Abd Allah ibn Mukhariq and his father, setting out what al-Bukhari, Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Hajar said, with their disagreement over whether the father was of Sulaym or of Shayban, and noting that Abd Allah ibn Mukhariq al-Shaybani is the poet known as Nabigha of Banu Shayban, brother of Qabus. He calls the report very unsettled, but says al-Haythami cites it in Majma' al-Zawa'id as related by al-Tabarani in al-Kabir with a good chain, and that al-Suyuti attributes it to Ibn Jarir, al-Tabarani and al-Bayhaqi in his book on the punishment of the grave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 598}

Report 20772, from al-Bara', through al-Hasan, Abu Khalid al-Qurashi and Sufyan from his father, and also through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad and Sufyan from his father from Khaythama: on "God makes firm those who believe with the firm word in the life of this world", he said, the punishment of the grave. Report 20773, from al-Bara', through al-Husayn, Affan, Shu'ba, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: from the Prophet on the verse; Shu'ba said something I did not memorise, then said, in the grave. Report 20774, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: when death comes to the believer the angels attend him, greet him and give him good news of paradise; when he dies they walk in his funeral procession, and the report continues on the next page. The editor notes that Khaythama's report is the third route of the hadith of al-Bara', that Abu Khalid al-Qurashi, Amr ibn Khalid, is discarded, that Sufyan is al-Thawri, that Muslim and al-Nasa'i related the report through Sufyan from his father, and that Affan was one of Ahmad's teachers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 599}

The end of report 20774: then they pray over him with the people, and when he is buried he is sat up in his grave and asked, who is your Lord? He says, my Lord is God. Who is your messenger? He says, Muhammad. What is your testimony? He says, I testify that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger. Then his grave is widened as far as his sight reaches. Report 20775, from Tawus, through al-Hasan, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Ibn Tawus: I think he said only that it concerns the trial of the grave. Report 20776, from al-Musayyab ibn Rafi', through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and al-Ala' ibn al-Musayyab: it concerns the one in the grave. Report 20777, from al-Musayyab ibn Rafi', through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and al-Awwam: it was revealed about the one in the grave. Report 20778, from al-Musayyab ibn Rafi', through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Abbad ibn al-Awwam and al-Ala' ibn al-Musayyab: like it. Report 20779, from al-Rabi', through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Sa'd and Abu Ja'far al-Razi: it has reached us that this community is questioned in its graves and God makes the believer firm in his grave when he is questioned. Report 20780, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through al-Muthanna, Abu Rabi'a Fahd, Abu Awana, al-A'mash, al-Minhal ibn Amr and Zadhan: the Messenger of God said, and the report continues on the next page. The editor says the chain of report 20774 is very weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 600}

The rest of report 20780, which mentions the seizing of the believer's soul: his soul returns to his body and God sends him two angels of stern rebuke, who sit him up and rebuke him, asking, who is your Lord? He says, God. What is your religion? Islam. What is this man, or the prophet, who was sent among you? He says, Muhammad, the Messenger of God. They ask, how do you know? He says, I read God's book, believed in it and affirmed it; that is God's saying about making firm those who believe. Report 20781, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: it was revealed about the dead person who is questioned in his grave about the Prophet. Report 20782, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it has reached us that this community is questioned in its graves and God makes the believer firm where he is questioned. Report 20783, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Shurayk and Ibrahim ibn Muhajir, begins here and its text continues on the next page. The editor notes that the hadith of al-Bara' by the route of Zadhan, then of al-A'mash from al-Minhal ibn Amr, is as stated before, that Abu Rabi'a Fahd is disputed, that Abu Awana al-Wadah ibn Abd Allah is trustworthy, and that Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi related it in his Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 601}

The end of report 20783, from Mujahid: on "in the life of this world" he said, this is in the grave when he is addressed, and in the hereafter likewise. Others said: the meaning is that God makes those who believe firm through faith in this life, which is the firm word, and in the hereafter through the questioning in the grave. Report 20784, from Tawus, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar and Ibn Tawus: the firm word is that there is no god but God, and in the hereafter it is the questioning in the grave. Report 20785, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: as for this life, He makes them firm by good and righteous deed, and the hereafter means in the grave. Abu Ja'far says: the right view is what the report from the Messenger of God establishes, that the meaning is that God makes those who believe firm with the firm word in this life, by faith in God and in His messenger Muhammad, and in the hereafter by the like of what He made them firm with in this life, which is in their graves when they are asked about the oneness of God and faith in His messenger. As for "and God leads astray the wrongdoers", it means that God does not grant success to the hypocrite and the disbeliever, in this life and in the hereafter at the questioning in the grave, in what He guided the believer to of faith in God and His messenger. In the notes the editor records differences between the printed text and the manuscript in the word for the questioning and in the order of a phrase, and holds that the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 602}

The interpreters said the same as we have said. Report 20786, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: as for the disbeliever, the angels descend when death comes to him and stretch out their hands, which means striking, striking his face and back at death; when he is put in his grave he is sat up and asked, who is your Lord? He gives them nothing back and God makes him forget it; when asked who is the messenger sent to you, he is not guided to him and returns nothing; God says, "and God leads astray the wrongdoers". Report 20787, from al-Bara', through al-Muthanna, Fahd ibn Awf Abu Rabi'a, Abu Awana, al-A'mash, al-Minhal ibn Amr and Zadhan: the Messenger of God mentioned the disbeliever when his soul is seized and said, his soul is returned to his body, and two angels of stern rebuke come, sit him up and rebuke him and ask, who is your Lord? He says, I do not know. They ask, what is your religion? He says, I do not know. It is said to him, who is this prophet who was sent among you? He says, I heard people say so, I do not know. They say, you did not know; and that is God's saying about leading astray the wrongdoers and doing what He wills. On "and God does what He wills" he means, mighty be His mention, that guidance and misguidance are in God's hand, so do not deny, O people, His power, nor the guidance of one of you who was astray, nor the misguidance of one who was guided, for the turning of His creatures and the turning of their hearts are in His hand, and He does with them what He wills. The editor notes that report 20787 is the last of the hadith of al-Bara' ibn Azib, and points to a difference between the printed text and the manuscript in the wording of report 20786. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 603}

Here ends the sixteenth volume of al-Tabari's commentary in this edition, and with it the commentary on Surat Ibrahim closes at verse 27; the seventeenth volume follows, beginning with the commentary on verse 28, on those who changed God's favour into disbelief and led their people down to the abode of ruin, which is hell. After this page come the volume's indexes, among them the index of verses cited elsewhere in the commentary; they are not part of the commentary and are not carried over here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 604}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
