## Summary
Verse 27 of [[passage/quran-14|Surat Ibrahim]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 27 of 52 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 27 (pp. 589-594)**

Al-Tabari says on this verse: by making firm those who believe, He means that He confirms their deeds and their faith, and by the firm word, the true word, which is said to be the testimony that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger. On "in the life of this world", the interpreters differed. Some said it means that God makes them firm in their graves before the Hour. Report 20758, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Abu al-Sa'ib Salm ibn Junada, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: the making firm in this life is when the two angels come to him in the grave and ask, who is your Lord? He says, my Lord is God. They ask, what is your religion? He says, my religion is Islam. They ask, who is your prophet? He says, my prophet is Muhammad. That is the making firm in this life. In a note the editor says Abu Ja'far related the hadith of al-Bara' by fourteen chains at this place, that he gathered them so they can be traced easily, and that they come by three routes: that of Sa'd ibn Ubayda, that of Zadhan and that of Khaythama. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 589}

Report 20759, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Abu Kurayb, Jabir ibn Nuh, al-A'mash and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: like it in meaning. Report 20760, from al-Bara', through Abd Allah ibn Ishaq al-Naqid al-Wasiti, Wahb ibn Jarir, Shu'ba, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: the Prophet mentioned the believer and the disbeliever and said, when the believer is asked in his grave he says, my Lord is God; that is His saying about making firm those who believe with the firm word in this life and the next. In the notes the editor sets out the routes from Sa'd ibn Ubayda: that of al-A'mash, with Abu Mu'awiya's report 20758 and Jabir ibn Nuh's report 20759, and that of Alqama ibn Marthad, which Abu Ja'far has by three chains, through Wahb ibn Jarir (20760), Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik (20761) and Affan (20773); he identifies the narrators, and the printed note here is partly garbled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 590}

Report 20761, from al-Bara' ibn Azib, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, Shu'ba, Alqama ibn Marthad and Sa'd ibn Ubayda: the Messenger of God said, when the Muslim is asked in the grave he testifies that there is no god but God and that Muhammad is God's messenger; that is His saying in this verse. Report 20762, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, through al-Husayn ibn Salama ibn Abi Kabsha, Muhammad ibn Mamar al-Bahrani, Abu Amir Abd al-Malik ibn Amr, Abbad ibn Rashid, Dawud ibn Abi Hind and Abu Nadra: we were with the Messenger of God at a funeral and he said, and the report continues on the next page. The editor notes that the hadith of Shu'ba from Alqama was related by Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Majah, that it is a sound hadith, and he points out small differences between the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 591}

The rest of report 20762, from Abu Sa'id: O people, this community is tried in its graves. When a person is buried and his companions disperse, an angel comes with a hammer in his hand, sits him up and says, what do you say of this man? If he is a believer he testifies that there is no god but God alone with no partner and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger, and the angel says, you have spoken truly. A door to the fire is opened for him and it is said, this would have been your dwelling had you disbelieved in your Lord, but since you believed, God has replaced it with this; then a door to paradise is opened and he wishes to rise to it, and he is told, be still; then his grave is widened for him. As for the disbeliever or hypocrite, he is asked what he says of this man and says, I do not know; and it is said, you did not know and you did not seek to know and you were not guided. Then a door to paradise is opened and it is said, this would have been your dwelling had you believed, but since you disbelieved God has replaced it with this; then a door to the fire is opened, and the angel strikes him with the hammer a blow that all God's creation hears except the two weighty ones, jinn and humans. One of his companions said, O Messenger of God, none of us has an angel standing at his head with a hammer in his hand without being terrified; and the Messenger of God recited the verse. In the notes the editor identifies the narrators and says the chain is sound, that Ahmad related it and that al-Haythami cited it in Majma' al-Zawa'id. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 592}

Report 20763, from al-Bara', through Abu Kurayb, Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, al-A'mash, al-Minhal and Zadhan: the Messenger of God mentioned the taking of the believer's soul and said, his soul is returned to his body and two angels come and sit him up in his grave and ask, who is your Lord? He says, my Lord is God. They ask, what is your religion? He says, my religion is Islam. They ask, who is this man who was sent among you? He says, he is the Messenger of God. They ask, how do you know? He says, I read God's book, believed in it and affirmed it. Then a caller calls from the sky, my servant has spoken truly. In a long note the editor discusses the word "mitraq" (hammer), which is firm in all the versions and sound Arabic, and the phrase "la darayta wa la tadarayta", preferring the manuscript's wording; he sets out what Yunus, Ibn al-Athir and al-Zamakhshari said about the variant "la talayta" used by the hadith scholars and what it means, and notes that Ahmad's version adds, I do not know; I heard people saying something. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 593}

The report 20763 ends: that is God's saying about making firm those who believe. Report 20764, from al-Bara', through Abu al-Sa'ib, Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash, al-Minhal and Zadhan: like it. Report 20765, from al-Bara', through Ibn Humayd, Ibn Waki', Jarir, al-A'mash, al-Minhal and Zadhan: like it. In the notes the editor says this is the second route, that of Zadhan from al-Bara', which Abu Ja'far related by two chains, that of al-A'mash from al-Minhal ibn Amr from Zadhan and that of Yunus ibn Khabbab from al-Minhal, and that he has it from al-A'mash by five routes (reports 20763 to 20767); he gives what was said about al-Minhal ibn Amr, whom some criticised and a group held reliable, and about Zadhan al-Kindi, a trustworthy Successor, and notes that al-Hakim in al-Mustadrak collected the routes and supporting reports, that Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Hakim related it, and that al-Haythami said it is in the Sahih and elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 594}

We stop here at the foot of p. 594, after report 20765 in the commentary on verse 27; the next batch begins at p. 595 (marker 603). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 594}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
