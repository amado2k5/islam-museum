**Commentary on verse 87 (pp. 450-453)**

On [[passage/quran-11-87|11:87]] al-Tabari says: Shu'ayb's people said, Shu'ayb, does your prayer tell you that we should leave the worship of what our fathers worshipped of idols and images, 'or do what we wish with our property', such as breaking and clipping coins and cheating people in measure and weight? 'You are the forbearing one', he who is not led by anger to do what he would not do when pleased, 'the right-minded', meaning right-minded in his command to them to leave idol worship. Report 18487, from Zayd b. Aslam: among what he forbade them was clipping coins, or he said cutting coins, the doubt being Hammad's; the editor gives notes on narrators in the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 450}

Report 18488, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi: it reached me that Shu'ayb's people were punished for cutting coins, and I found that in the Qur'an, 'does your prayer tell you that we should leave what our fathers worshipped or do what we wish with our property'; 18489, from him, the same; 18490, from Zayd b. Aslam: among what he forbade them was clipping coins; 18491, from Ibn Zayd: he forbade them to cut dinars and dirhams and they said, they are our property, we do with them what we wish, cut them if we like, alter them if we like, throw them away if we like; 18492, from Zayd: among that was cutting coins. As for 'your prayer', al-A'mash said in report 18493, your recitation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 451}

If someone asks, how can it be said 'does your prayer tell us to leave what our fathers worshipped or do what we wish with our property', when Shu'ayb had forbidden them to do with their property what you mentioned? The answer is that the sense is not what you suppose, and the grammarians differ. One of the Basrans said it means does your prayer tell you that we leave what our fathers worshipped or that we leave doing what we wish with our property, not that we are told to do what we wish, since that was not their command. Some Kufans said much the same, and that there is another way, making the command like a prohibition, as if he said does your prayer command this and forbid us that, the second 'an' being joined to 'what' in 'what they worship'. Some readers are reported to have read 'what you wish', and for them there is no difficulty, the second 'an' being joined to the first. As for 'you are the forbearing, the right-minded', they are God's enemies and said it to him in mockery, calling him foolish and ignorant by these words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 452}

The interpreters said as we have said; report 18494, from Ibn Jurayj: they are mocking; 18495, from Ibn Zayd: the mockers mock, you are the forbearing, the right-minded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 453}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
