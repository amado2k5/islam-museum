## Summary
Verse 109 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 109 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "and they swore by God their strongest oaths that if a sign came to them they would believe in it", al-Tabari says: these who set up equals to God swore by God with the full force of their swearing, which is the most binding, hardest and strongest oath they could manage; they said: we swear by God that if a sign comes to us confirming what you say, Muhammad, like what came to the nations before us, we will believe in it, that is, we will credit its coming through you, and that you are a messenger sent by God and that what you have brought us is the truth from God. The phrase "believe in it" puts the report in terms of the sign while the meaning is the coming of the sign. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "aqsama" and "jahd aymanihim". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 37}

"Say: signs are with God", that is, He alone, of all His creatures, is able to bring them to you; "and what will make you perceive", that is, what tells you (the editor refers to Abu Ubayda) "that when it comes they will not believe?" It is said that those of his people who asked him for a sign were those of the polytheists of his people of whose belief God had made His prophet despair. Report 13744, from Mujahid: Quraysh asked Muhammad to bring them a sign and made him swear that they would believe in it; report 13745 is the same from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 38}

Report 13746, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi (through Hannad, Yunus ibn Bukayr, Abu Ma'shar): the Messenger of God spoke with Quraysh, and they said: "Muhammad, you tell us that Moses had a staff with which he struck the rock and twelve springs burst out of it, and that Jesus raised the dead, and that Thamud had a she-camel; so bring us some of the signs, so that we may believe you." He said: "What would you like me to bring you?" They said: "Turn al-Safa into gold." He said: "If I do, will you believe me?" They said: "Yes, by God, if you do it we will all follow you." The Prophet stood and prayed, and Gabriel came to him and said: "You have what you wish: if you wish it will become gold, but if a sign is sent and they do not believe after that, We will punish them; or if you wish, give them leeway until the one of them who will repent repents." He said: "Rather let the repentant repent." So God sent down "and they swore by God" to "they are ignorant". The editor's footnote says the manuscript's word for "give them leeway" is without dots and that he preferred reading it from "nadahtu the thing", to widen it, meaning to make room for them until the repentant repents. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 38}

On "and what will make you perceive that when it comes they will not believe?", al-Tabari records the interpreters' difference over who is addressed. Some said it is the polytheists who swore by God that if a sign came they would believe; the report ends at "what will make you perceive", and then a fresh judgment begins on them, that they will not believe when it comes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 39}

The reports: 13747, from Mujahid: "what will make you perceive" is "what tells you", and then He reports of them that they will not believe; 13748, from him: He made it binding on them that when it comes they will not believe; 13749, from Abd Allah ibn Yazid: "signs are with God alone", and then he resumes and says that when it comes they do not believe; 13750, from Mujahid: what tells you that you will believe when it comes, and then it turns to report of them. This interpretation goes with the reading with a kasra on the hamza of "innaha", as a fresh statement cut off from the first, and among those who read so were some of the readers of Mecca and Basra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 40}

Others of them said that it is rather an address from God to His prophet and his companions. They said that those who asked the Messenger of God to bring a sign were the believers in him, and the reason for their asking was that the polytheists had sworn that when the sign came they would believe and follow the Messenger; so the companions said, "Ask your Lord for that, Messenger of God." He asked, and God sent down about them and their request: say to the believers in you, "signs are with God, and what will make you perceive", you believers, that when signs come to these polytheists they will not believe? They therefore read "annaha" with fatha on the hamza. Among those who read so were most of the readers of Medina and Kufa, and they said that "la" in "la yu'minun" was inserted as a redundant particle, as in "what prevented you from not prostrating" (7:12) and in "it is forbidden for a town We destroyed that they not return" (21:95), where the sense is "what prevented you from prostrating" and "it is forbidden for them to return". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 40}

A group who read "annaha" with fatha on the hamza took it in the sense of "perhaps it" and mentioned that this is in the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b; and it is recorded that the Arabs said, as heard from them, "go to the market, anaka you will buy me something", meaning "perhaps you will buy" (the editor refers to al-Farra' and Abu Ubayda). It is also said that a line of Adi ibn Zayd al-Ibadi, in which he asks his reproacher what tells her that his death may come within an hour of today or in the forenoon tomorrow, is evidence for this (the editor's footnote traces the line to the Jamharat Ash'ar al-Arab and the Lisan and gives the context of the poem). The page ends before the rest of the discussion of this evidence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 41}
