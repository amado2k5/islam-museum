**Commentary on verse 114 (pp. 502-508)**

On [[passage/quran-11-114|11:114]] God says to His Prophet Muhammad: 'establish prayer', Muhammad, that is, pray at 'the two ends of the day', meaning morning and evening. The interpreters differ on which evening prayer is meant, after all agreeing that the morning one meant is the dawn prayer. Some said it is the noon and afternoon prayers, which belong to the evening; reports 18609, 18610 and 18611, from Mujahid: the dawn and the two evening prayers, meaning noon and afternoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 502}

Reports 18612 and 18613, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi: dawn, noon and afternoon; 18614, from al-Dahhak: dawn, noon and afternoon. Others said it is the sunset prayer; report 18615, from Ibn Abbas: the morning prayer and the sunset prayer, and so 18616, from al-Hasan, and 18617, from Ibn Zayd: morning and sunset. Others said it is the afternoon prayer; report 18618, from al-Dahhak: dawn and afternoon; 18619, from Muhammad b. Ka'b: dawn and afternoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 503}

Reports 18620 and 18621, from al-Hasan, 18622, from Qatada, 18623, from al-Hasan, 18624, from Muhammad b. Ka'b and 18625, from al-Hasan: morning and afternoon. Some said the two ends of the day are noon and afternoon and 'part of the night' means sunset, night and dawn. Al-Tabari says: the soundest of these with me is the view that it is the sunset prayer, as we reported from Ibn Abbas, because all agree that the prayer of one of the two ends is the dawn prayer, which is prayed before sunrise, so it must be that the prayer of the other end is sunset, being prayed after the sun sets; and were it required that the prayer of one end be before sunset, the prayer of the other end would need to be after sunrise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 504}

No one we know says that, except whoever says it means noon and afternoon, whose unsoundness is not obscure, for they are nearer to both belonging to one end than to belonging to the two ends of the day; noon is prayed after midday in the second half, so it cannot belong to the first end when it is in the last. As for 'part of the night', it means hours of the night, plural of 'zulfa', which is an hour, a station, a nearness; it is said Muzdalifa and Jam' are so called as a station after 'Arafa, or from Adam's drawing near to Eve from 'Arafa; he cites a line of al-'Ajjaj describing a camel, which the editor explains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 505}

The readers differ: most readers of Medina and Iraq read 'zulafan' with damma on the zay and fatha on the lam; some of Medina with damma on both, as if taking it as singular; some Meccans with damma and sukun. Al-Tabari says: the reading I like best is with damma on the zay and fatha on the lam, as a plural of 'zulfa' like 'ghurfa, ghuraf' and 'hujra, hujar', since the last evening prayer is prayed after parts of the night have passed, and that is the one meant by 'part of the night'. Reports 18626, 18627 and 18628, from Mujahid: hours of the night, the 'atama prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 506}

Report 18629, from Ibn Abbas: the 'atama prayer; 18630, from al-Hasan: the 'isha'; 18631, from 'Ubaydallah b. Abi Yazid: Ibn Abbas liked to delay the 'isha' and would recite the verse; 18632, from Mujahid: an hour of the night, the 'atama prayer; 18633, from Ibn Zayd: the 'atama, and I never heard any of our jurists and elders say 'isha', they say only 'atama. Some said the prayers the Prophet was told to establish as parts of the night are the sunset and night prayers; reports 18634 and 18635, from al-Hasan: they are two parts of the night, the sunset and night prayers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 507}

Report 18636, from al-Hasan: God said to His Prophet 'establish prayer at the two ends of the day and part of the night', the parts of the night being sunset and night, and the Prophet said they are the two parts of the night, sunset and night; 18637, 18638 and 18639, from Mujahid: sunset and night; 18640, from al-Hasan: God has made clear the times of prayer in the Qur'an, saying in [[passage/quran-17-78|17:78]] 'establish prayer from the sun's decline to the darkness of night', its decline being when it passes the middle of the sky and has a shadow on the earth, and 'at the two ends of the day', morning and afternoon, 'and part of the night', sunset and night, and the Prophet said they are the two parts of the night; 18641, from Qatada, and 18642 and 18643, from Muhammad b. Ka'b: sunset and night. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 508}
