**Commentary on verse 114 (pp. 502-526)**

On [[passage/quran-11-114|11:114]] God says to His Prophet Muhammad: 'establish prayer', Muhammad, that is, pray at 'the two ends of the day', meaning morning and evening. The interpreters differ on which evening prayer is meant, after all agreeing that the morning one meant is the dawn prayer. Some said it is the noon and afternoon prayers, which belong to the evening; reports 18609, 18610 and 18611, from Mujahid: the dawn and the two evening prayers, meaning noon and afternoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 502}

Reports 18612 and 18613, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi: dawn, noon and afternoon; 18614, from al-Dahhak: dawn, noon and afternoon. Others said it is the sunset prayer; report 18615, from Ibn Abbas: the morning prayer and the sunset prayer, and so 18616, from al-Hasan, and 18617, from Ibn Zayd: morning and sunset. Others said it is the afternoon prayer; report 18618, from al-Dahhak: dawn and afternoon; 18619, from Muhammad b. Ka'b: dawn and afternoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 503}

Reports 18620 and 18621, from al-Hasan, 18622, from Qatada, 18623, from al-Hasan, 18624, from Muhammad b. Ka'b and 18625, from al-Hasan: morning and afternoon. Some said the two ends of the day are noon and afternoon and 'part of the night' means sunset, night and dawn. Al-Tabari says: the soundest of these with me is the view that it is the sunset prayer, as we reported from Ibn Abbas, because all agree that the prayer of one of the two ends is the dawn prayer, which is prayed before sunrise, so it must be that the prayer of the other end is sunset, being prayed after the sun sets; and were it required that the prayer of one end be before sunset, the prayer of the other end would need to be after sunrise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 504}

No one we know says that, except whoever says it means noon and afternoon, whose unsoundness is not obscure, for they are nearer to both belonging to one end than to belonging to the two ends of the day; noon is prayed after midday in the second half, so it cannot belong to the first end when it is in the last. As for 'part of the night', it means hours of the night, plural of 'zulfa', which is an hour, a station, a nearness; it is said Muzdalifa and Jam' are so called as a station after 'Arafa, or from Adam's drawing near to Eve from 'Arafa; he cites a line of al-'Ajjaj describing a camel, which the editor explains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 505}

The readers differ: most readers of Medina and Iraq read 'zulafan' with damma on the zay and fatha on the lam; some of Medina with damma on both, as if taking it as singular; some Meccans with damma and sukun. Al-Tabari says: the reading I like best is with damma on the zay and fatha on the lam, as a plural of 'zulfa' like 'ghurfa, ghuraf' and 'hujra, hujar', since the last evening prayer is prayed after parts of the night have passed, and that is the one meant by 'part of the night'. Reports 18626, 18627 and 18628, from Mujahid: hours of the night, the 'atama prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 506}

Report 18629, from Ibn Abbas: the 'atama prayer; 18630, from al-Hasan: the 'isha'; 18631, from 'Ubaydallah b. Abi Yazid: Ibn Abbas liked to delay the 'isha' and would recite the verse; 18632, from Mujahid: an hour of the night, the 'atama prayer; 18633, from Ibn Zayd: the 'atama, and I never heard any of our jurists and elders say 'isha', they say only 'atama. Some said the prayers the Prophet was told to establish as parts of the night are the sunset and night prayers; reports 18634 and 18635, from al-Hasan: they are two parts of the night, the sunset and night prayers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 507}

Report 18636, from al-Hasan: God said to His Prophet 'establish prayer at the two ends of the day and part of the night', the parts of the night being sunset and night, and the Prophet said they are the two parts of the night, sunset and night; 18637, 18638 and 18639, from Mujahid: sunset and night; 18640, from al-Hasan: God has made clear the times of prayer in the Qur'an, saying in [[passage/quran-17-78|17:78]] 'establish prayer from the sun's decline to the darkness of night', its decline being when it passes the middle of the sky and has a shadow on the earth, and 'at the two ends of the day', morning and afternoon, 'and part of the night', sunset and night, and the Prophet said they are the two parts of the night; 18641, from Qatada, and 18642 and 18643, from Muhammad b. Ka'b: sunset and night. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 508}

Reports 18644, from Muhammad b. Ka'b, 18645, from al-Hasan, 18646, from al-Dahhak, 18647, from al-Hasan, 18648, from al-Dahhak and 18649, from al-Hasan: 'part of the night' means sunset and night. As for 'good deeds drive away evil deeds', it means turning to obedience to God and doing what pleases Him removes the sin of disobeying God and wipes away wrongs; the editor explains that 'athaam' is the penalty of sin and 'aathaam' the plural of 'ithm'. Then the interpreters differ on the good deeds meant that drive away evil deeds: some said they are the five obligatory prayers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 509}

Report 18650, from Ka'b: by Him in whose hand Ka'b's soul is, the five prayers are the good deeds that drive away evil deeds as water washes away grime; 18651, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi, 18652, from Ibn Abbas, 18653, from Mujahid, 18654, from al-Hasan and 18655, from Ibn Abbas: the five prayers. The editor gives notes on some narrators and discusses a name in the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 510}

Reports 18656, from al-Dahhak, 18657, from al-Hasan, 18658, from Abdallah b. Mas'ud, 18660, from Ibn Abbas and 18661, from Masruq: the five prayers; 18659, from Salman: by Him in whose hand my soul is, the good deeds by which God wipes out evil deeds as water washes away grime are the five prayers. The editor says he found no mention in the books on narrators of one in the chain of report 18661. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 511}

Report 18662, from al-Harith, freedman of Uthman b. Affan: Uthman sat one day and we sat with him, and the muezzin came; Uthman called for water in a vessel of about a mudd and made ablution, then said, I saw the Messenger of God make ablution as I have, and he said, whoever makes ablution as mine and then rises and prays the noon prayer is forgiven what lies between it and the dawn prayer, then prays the afternoon prayer and is forgiven what lies between it and noon, then the sunset prayer and is forgiven what lies between it and the afternoon, then the night prayer and is forgiven what lies between it and sunset, then perhaps spends the night turning in bed, then if he rises, makes ablution and prays dawn he is forgiven what lies between it and the night prayer; these are the good deeds that drive away evil deeds. The editor judges the chain sound and refers to Ahmad's Musnad and Majma' al-Zawa'id, noting an addition in the Musnad about the lasting good deeds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 512}

Reports 18663 and 18664, from al-Harith, Uthman's freedman, are similar, saying that Uthman sat one day at the Maqa'id and then related much the same from the Messenger of God, adding that these are the good deeds, 'good deeds drive away evil deeds'; report 18665, from Abu Malik al-Ash'ari: the Messenger of God said the prayers were made expiations for what lies between them, since God said 'good deeds drive away evil deeds'. The editor weakens the chain of the last because of Muhammad b. Isma'il b. 'Ayyash, who did not hear from his father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 513}

Report 18666, from Abu 'Uthman al-Nahdi: I was with Salman under a tree and he took a dry branch and shook it until its leaves fell, then said, so the Messenger of God did, I was with him under a tree, he took a dry branch and shook it until its leaves fell and said, will you not ask me why I do this, Salman? I said, why do you? He said, when a Muslim makes ablution well and then prays the five prayers, his sins fall away as these leaves fell; then he recited the verse. Others said it means saying glory be to God, praise be to God, there is no god but God, and God is greatest; report 18667, from Mujahid, says so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 514}

Al-Tabari says: the better of the two interpretations is that they are the five prayers, for the soundness of the reports from the Messenger of God and their multiple transmission that he said the five prayers are like a flowing river at the door of one of you in which he plunges five times a day, what then remains of his grime; and because it is in the course of God's command to establish prayers and promise of abundant reward after establishing them, which is likelier than a promise for the unmentioned good deeds of other works, singling out some over others. As for 'that is a reminder for those who remember', it means that what I threatened for inclining to wrongdoing and promised for establishing the prayers that drive away evil deeds is a reminder for people who remember God's promise, hope for His reward and fear His threat, not whoever's heart is sealed so he answers no caller and hears no warner. It is also said that this verse came down about a man who took from a woman not his wife or his slave something forbidden to him and then repented. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 515}

Report 18668, from Abdallah b. Mas'ud: a man came to the Prophet and said, I dealt with a woman in a corner of Medina and took from her all short of touching her, so here I am, decide on me what you wish; Umar said, God had covered you, had you covered yourself; the Prophet gave no answer, the man rose and went, the Prophet sent a man after him and called him, and when he came recited the verse to him; a man of the people said, is this his alone, Messenger of God? He said, no, for all the people. The editor notes that the report is related by two routes in al-Tabari, cites Muslim, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, and refers to the discussion of the man's name. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 516}

Report 18669, from Abdallah: a man came to the Prophet and said, I met a woman in the garden and took all but intercourse with her; the Prophet was silent, then the verse came down, he called him and recited it to him, and Umar said, Messenger of God, is it his alone or for all people? He said, no, for all people; report 18670, from him, similar, in which Umar said God had covered him had he covered himself, the Prophet said bring him back and recited the verse, and Mu'adh b. Jabal asked, for him alone, Prophet of God, or for all people? He said, for all people; report 18671, from him, likewise, where the Prophet was silent and when he left called him and recited this verse to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 517}

Report 18672, from Abdallah: a man met a woman on a street in Medina and took short of intercourse, then came to the Prophet and told him, and the verse came down, and Mu'adh b. Jabal said, Messenger of God, is this for him alone or for us all? He said, for you all; reports 18673 and 18674, from Ibn Mas'ud, similarly, that a man met a woman in Medina and took short of intercourse. The editor gives notes on some narrators and explains that 'hash' in Medinan usage is a garden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 518}

Report 18675, from Ibrahim: so-and-so b. Mu'attib, a man of the Ansar, came and said, Messenger of God, a woman came to me and I took from her what a man takes from his wife except that I did not have intercourse with her; the Messenger of God did not know what to answer until this verse came down, and he called him and recited it to him; the editor discusses the man's name, quoting Ibn Hajar that it was said to be Ka'b b. 'Amr, Abu al-Yusr al-Ansari. Report 18676, from Ibn Mas'ud: a man took something from a woman short of fornication, came to the Prophet and told him, and the verse came down, and the man said, is this for me, Messenger of God? He said, for whoever of my community takes it up, or acts by it; the editor notes that Ahmad relates it by this route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 519}

Report 18677, from Abu 'Uthman: I was with Salman and he took a dry branch and shook it and said, I heard the Messenger of God say, whoever makes ablution well, his sins fall away as these leaves fall, then recited the verse; 18678, from Mu'adh: a man came to the Prophet and said, Messenger of God, what do you say of a man who met a woman he did not know, and did with her all a man does with his wife except that he did not have intercourse with her? Then God sent down this verse, and the Messenger of God said to him, make ablution and pray; Mu'adh said, I said, Messenger of God, is it for him alone or for all the believers? He said, for all the believers. The editor judges the chain of 18678 sound and refers to al-Bukhari, Muslim, Ahmad, Ibn Majah and al-Tirmidhi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 520}

Reports 18679 and 18680, from 'Abd al-Rahman b. Abi Layla: a man took from a woman short of intercourse and came to the Prophet asking about it, and the verse was recited or came down, and Mu'adh said, Messenger of God, is it for him alone or for all people? He said, it is for all people; 18681, from Abu Umama: a man came to the Messenger of God and said, Messenger of God, carry out God's penalty on me, once and twice; he turned away from him, then the prayer was called, and when he finished the prayer he said, where is the one who said carry out God's penalty on me? He said, here I am. He said, did you complete your ablution and pray with us just now? He said, yes. He said, then you are from your wrong as your mother bore you, do not repeat it; and God then sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 521}

The editor judges the chain of 18681 good and refers to Muslim, Abu Dawud and Ahmad. Report 18682, from Mu'adh b. Jabal: he was sitting with the Prophet when a man came and said, Messenger of God, a man took from a woman what is not lawful to him, leaving nothing that a man does with his wife that he did not do except that he did not have intercourse with her? He said, let him make ablution well and then pray; and God sent down this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 522}

Mu'adh said, is it for him alone, Messenger of God, or for the Muslims at large? He said, for the Muslims at large. Report 18683, from Yahya b. Ja'da: a man among the Prophet's Companions mentioned a woman while sitting with the Prophet and asked leave for a need and was given it; he went to look for her and did not find her, and as he returned wishing to bring the Prophet good news of rain he found the woman sitting by a pool and went beyond bounds with her, then rose in remorse and came to the Prophet and told him what he had done; the Prophet said, ask your Lord's forgiveness and pray four rak'as, and recited the verse to him. Report 18684, from Abu al-Yusr b. 'Amr al-Ansari: a woman came to buy dates from me for a dirham and I said, there are better dates in the house; she came in and I leaned to her and kissed her. I went to Abu Bakr and asked him, and he said, cover yourself, repent and seek God's forgiveness; I came to the Messenger of God, who said, you betrayed a man fighting in God's way, in his family, with such a thing, until I thought I was of the people of the Fire, and wished I had become Muslim only then; he bowed his head a while, then Gabriel came down, and he said, where is Abu al-Yusr? I came, and he recited the verse to me up to 'a reminder for those who remember', {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 523}

and someone said, is it for him alone, Messenger of God, or for all people? He said, for all people. Report 18685, from Abu al-Yusr: I met a woman and embraced her, though I did not have her, and went to Umar b. al-Khattab, who said, fear God, cover yourself and tell no one; I could not hold back and went to Abu Bakr and asked him and he said the same; I went to the Messenger of God and he said what they had said, and the verse came down, and his Companions said, is this for him alone or for all people? He said, for all people. Report 18686, from Qatada: a man took a kiss from a woman and came to the Prophet and said, Prophet of God, I am ruined, and God sent down 'good deeds drive away evil deeds, that is a reminder for those who remember'. The editor notes that al-Tirmidhi relates it and calls it good but strange, and that Qays b. al-Rabi' was weakened by Waki' and others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 524}

Report 18687, from Sulayman al-Taymi: a man struck a woman on her hips, then came to Abu Bakr and Umar, and each time he asked one of them about the expiation for it he said, is she a woman whose husband is on campaign? He said yes; he said I do not know; then he came to the Prophet and asked, and he said, is she a woman whose husband is on campaign? He said yes; he said I do not know, until God sent down the verse; the editor explains that a 'mughziya' is a woman whose husband went on campaign and was left alone at home, and quotes a saying of Umar warning against sitting with such women. Report 18688, from 'Ata': a woman came to a man who sold flour and he kissed her, then was remorseful, came to Umar and told him, and he said, fear God and do not be the wife of a fighter; the man said, she is the wife of a fighter; he went to Abu Bakr, who said the same as Umar; they all went to the Prophet, who said likewise, then was silent and did not answer them, and God sent down the verse, meaning the obligatory prayers; 18689, from 'Ata' b. Abi Rabah, similarly, 'Ata' being asked, is it the obligatory prayer, and saying, yes, the obligatory one, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 525}

and Ibn Jurayj said 'Abdallah b. Kathir said they are the obligatory prayers. Ibn Jurayj said, from Yazid b. Ruman: a man of Banu Ghanm, to whom a woman came, kissed her and touched her, then came to Abu Bakr, then to Umar, then to the Prophet, and this verse came down; the man who kissed the woman kept remembering, and that is God's saying 'a reminder for those who remember'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 526}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
