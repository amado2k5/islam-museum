## Summary
Verse 77 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 77 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The occasion, as mentioned (p. 547).** Al-Tabari says it was mentioned that this verse came down about a group of the Messenger's companions who believed in him and trusted him before fighting was made obligatory, when prayer and zakat already were; they used to ask God to prescribe fighting, and when he did, it weighed on them and they said what God reports of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 547}

**Commentary on the verse (p. 548, notes 1-8).** Al-Tabari says the address is to the Prophet: look with your heart and know the case of those told, from among your companions when they asked you to ask your Lord to prescribe fighting for them, to keep their hands back from fighting the polytheists, to perform the prayer God had made obligatory within its limits, and to give zakat to those entitled, as purification of their bodies and wealth. They disliked what they were ordered, the restraint, and it weighed on them. When the fighting they had asked for was prescribed, a party of them feared people, fearing they would fight them, as they feared God or more, and out of dismay at the fighting prescribed for them they complained of its being prescribed, inclining to this world and preferring ease and soft living to the dislike of meeting the enemy and the hardship of war with them. They asked to be put off for a short term, meaning until they died in their beds in their homes. The editor refers to earlier interpretations of "have you not seen", "he prescribed", "party", "fear", "term", performing prayer and giving zakat (some page numbers garbled in the scan), and says manuscript and print are both wrong in a word before "on the dislike", which he corrects to the word for soft, easy living, and that the phrase "on the dislike of meeting the enemy" belongs with "preferring ease". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 548}

**Reports 9951-9954 (pp. 549-550, note 1).** Muhammad b. Ali b. al-Hasan b. Shaqiq (9951, from his father, from al-Husayn b. Waqid, from Amr b. Dinar, from Ikrima) reports from Ibn Abbas that Abd al-Rahman b. Awf and some companions came to the Prophet and said that they had been mighty when polytheists and became humbled when they believed; he said he had been ordered to pardon, so they should not fight; and when God moved him to Medina and ordered fighting, they held back, and the verse came down. The editor says the print is wrong in the name of the narrator's grandfather, refers to earlier places for the two narrators (report numbers garbled), and says al-Hakim gave the report in the Mustadrak with different wording and called it sound by the condition of al-Bukhari though the two did not include it, and that al-Dhahabi agreed (this grading is al-Hakim's and al-Dhahabi's, as the editor relays it); al-Bayhaqi gave it in the Sunan, Ibn Kathir in his tafsir from Ibn Abi Hatim, and al-Suyuti traced it in al-Durr al-Manthur to these and added al-Nasa'i. Al-Qasim (9952, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ikrima that it came down about some companions of the Messenger, and from Ibn Jurayj that the near term means dying a death. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9953, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that some companions, while the Prophet was in Mecca before the hijra, hurried to fight and said: let us take picks and fight the polytheists in Mecca; he forbade them and said he had not been ordered; when the hijra came and he was ordered to fight, the group disliked it and did what you have heard, and God answered with the clause that follows in the verse on the slightness of this world's pleasure. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9954, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that they were people who accepted Islam before fighting was prescribed, having only prayer and zakat, and asked God to prescribe fighting, and the near term is death. The editor says print and manuscript omit the second "to" in the abbreviated citation and the sense calls for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 550}

**Another view: it came down about the Jews (p. 550).** Al-Muthanna (9955, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that what lies between this place and the closing clause of [[passage/quran-4-83|4:83]] is about the Jews. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9956, from his father, his uncle, his father, from his father) reports from Ibn Abbas that God forbade this community to do as they did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 550}

**The second part of the verse (p. 551, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: say, Muhammad, to those who complained of the fighting being prescribed, your life in this world and your enjoyment of it is slight, because it is passing and what is in it passes, and the bliss of the hereafter surpasses it, since it lasts and its bliss is lasting. He says the hereafter is described as better while its bliss is meant, the mention of the hereafter indicating the meaning. The good is for the one who keeps his duty to God by fulfilling his obligations, avoiding disobedience and obeying in all of it, and God will not reduce the reward of their deeds by the thinnest thread; he has explained the meaning of this word earlier, enough to spare repetition (the editor refers to earlier pages), and the editor also refers to earlier interpretation of the word for enjoyment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 551}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 551}
