## Summary
Verse 122 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 122 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 122, "is he who was dead and We gave him life and a light by which he walks among people like one in darknesses from which he does not emerge", al-Tabari says this speech points to God's forbidding the believers in His Messenger at that time to obey some idolaters who disputed with them over eating carrion, and commanding them to obey a believer from among them who had been an unbeliever whom God guided and enabled to believe. He says to them: is obeying one who was dead, meaning an unbeliever, the same? Because of his turning away from obedience, his ignorance of monotheism and his leaving his share of the work for God, He set him in the position of the dead who cannot benefit himself; "We gave him life" means We guided him to Islam and revived him so he came to know what harms and benefits himself and works for his deliverance from God's wrath and punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 88}

He made his seeing the truth after being blind to it, and his knowing God's oneness and the laws of his religion after ignorance of them, a life and a light by which he walks the straight path among people. "Like one in darknesses" does not know which way to turn or which road to take for the depth of the night's darkness; so the astray unbeliever in the darknesses of unbelief sees no right guidance and knows no truth. The sense is: is obeying this one whom We guided to the truth like obeying one in the likeness of a man hesitating in darkness, in the first calling to forbid what God forbade and permit what He permitted, and the second calling to permit what God forbade and forbid what He permitted? The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "death" and "giving life". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 89}

It is mentioned that this verse was sent down about two named men, one a believer and the other an unbeliever, and the commentators then differed about them. Some said the one who was dead and whom God brought to life is Umar ibn al-Khattab, and the one in the darknesses is Abu Jahl ibn Hisham; this is reported (13836) from al-Dahhak. The notes give a biography of Sulayman ibn Abi Hawdha and state that the editor found no entry for Shu'ayb al-Sarraj in the books to hand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 89}

Others said the dead man whom God brought to life is Ammar ibn Yasir, and the one in the darknesses is Abu Jahl ibn Hisham; this is given (13837 and 13838) from Ikrima, and the editor's note discusses the name Bishr ibn Tayyim or Bashir ibn Qays and how al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim differ over it. Commentators spoke in line with al-Tabari's reading of the verse; he gives (13839) Mujahid: "was dead and We gave him life" means astray and We guided him, "a light" means guidance, and "like one in the darknesses" means in error for ever. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 90}

The reports on "is he who was dead and We gave him life" continue. Reports 13840 and 13841, from Mujahid, give astray and We guided him; 13842, from Ibn Abbas: the unbeliever whom We guided, the light being the Qur'an for whoever believes in it and acts by it, and the darknesses unbelief and error; 13843, from him: the light is guidance, the unbeliever whom God guides to Islam; 13844, from Qatada: the believer has from God a light and clear proof by which he acts and which is the Book of God, while the unbeliever is like one in error, bewildered and wandering, finding no way out; and 13845, from al-Suddi: the light is Islam and the darknesses are idolatry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 91}

Report 13846, from Ibn Zayd, says the light is Islam to which God guided him, and "like one in the darknesses" is one who is not among the people of Islam; he cited "God is the ally of those who believe; He brings them out of the darknesses into the light" (2:257) and compared it to a person lighting a lamp in his house and seeing what is in it, so one to whom God gave this light is lit by it in his religion, while one in the darknesses does not know what is coming or what will befall him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 92}

On the sentence "thus was made attractive to the unbelievers what they used to do", al-Tabari says: as I abandoned this unbeliever who disputes with you, believers, over eating what I forbade you, away from the truth, so that I adorned for him the evil of his deed and he saw it as good, so that he earns the painful punishment I prepared, so I adorned for others of like unbelief what they did of disobedience to God, that they merit the exemplary punishment awaiting them with their Lord. He holds that this is the clearest statement disproving those who claim God delegated matters to His creatures in their deeds so that He has no part in their acts, and that He made all equal in the means by which they reach obedience or disobedience; had it been so, He would have adorned error and unbelief for His prophets and friends as He did for His enemies, and adorned faith for unbelievers as for His prophets; and in His telling that He adorned for every doer his deed lies an indication of adorning unbelief, wickedness and disobedience for His enemies in particular and making faith and obedience hateful to them. The editor's note says that "delegation" is the position of the Qadariyya, Mu'tazila and Imamiyya that the matter was delegated to the servant so his will suffices to bring about his act. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 93}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
