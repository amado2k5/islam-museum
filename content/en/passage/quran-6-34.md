## Summary
Verse 34 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 34 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

This is God's consolation to his prophet Muhammad and comfort for the distress he suffered from his people's denying what they were brought of the truth from God. He says: if these polytheists of your people deny you, Muhammad, rejecting your prophethood and denying that God's signs are from him, do not grieve at that, and be patient with their denial of you and the hurt you meet from them for God's sake until God's help comes; messengers were denied before you whom I sent to their peoples, and the peoples harmed them, and they were patient with their peoples' denial and it did not deter them from carrying out God's command to call their people to him, until God judged between them and their peoples; "and none can change the words of God" means none can alter his words, and his words are what God sent his prophet Muhammad of his promise of help against those who oppose and resist him and victory over those who turn away from him; "and there has come to you some of the news of the messengers" means the report of the messengers before you and their peoples, and what I did to them when they rejected my signs and persisted in their error; the word "news" is omitted because "some" points to it. He says: so wait too for the like of the help and victory that I gave to those among... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 335}

The editor says the manuscript has "until God's palace came to them", a slip of the copyist, the printed wording being right, and refers for "news" to p. 262.

We stop here at the foot of p. 335, within the explanation of "and there has come to you some of the news of the messengers", at "the like of what I did among those", which continues on p. 336.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
