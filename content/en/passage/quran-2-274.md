## Summary
Verse 274 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 274 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-274|2:274]]), pp. 601 and 602, the last in volume 5. Report 6232: a man of Ghafiq relates that Abu al-Darda used to look at horses tethered among the draught and mixed-breed horses and say, the owners of these, meaning the horses, are among those this verse describes; the editor gives the biography of the narrator Ayman ibn Nabil al-Makki, calling him reliable with an impediment in his speech, and explains the two terms for non-Arabian horses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 601}

Others said it means a people who spent in God's way without waste or stinginess. 6233, Qatada: these are the people of paradise, and they were told that the Prophet used to say the wealthy are the lowest, they said except whom, O Prophet of God, and he repeated it three times until he said except whoever says to wealth thus and thus, on his right, left, before him and behind him, and few are they; then Qatada says these are people who spent in God's way, which He made obligatory and approved, without extravagance, penury, squandering or corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 601} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 602} The editor notes he added a word in brackets since what follows is clearly Qatada's own speech, that al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-manthur, that the beginning of the report is a mursal report, and explains the word for penury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 602}

It was also said that these verses, from the one on charity to the end of ([[passage/quran-2-274|2:274]]), were acted upon before what is in Surat Bara'a (al-Tawba) came down with the details of zakat, and when Bara'a came down they were confined to it. 6234, Ibn Abbas: this was acted on before Bara'a came down with the obligatory charities and their detail, and charity ended with it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 602} The reference to ([[passage/quran-9-60|9:60]]) is the museum's pointer, not in al-Tabari's text here.

So volume 5 of al-Tabari's commentary in Shakir's edition ends at ([[passage/quran-2-274|2:274]]), and volume 6 opens with the commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 602} The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.
