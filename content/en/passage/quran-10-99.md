**Commentary on verse 99 (pp. 211-213)**

On [[passage/quran-10-99|10:99]] al-Tabari says God tells His prophet: had your Lord willed, Muhammad, all who are on the earth would have believed in you together and affirmed that you are God's messenger and that what you brought and call them to of God's oneness and pure servitude to Him is true; but He does not will that, since it was foreordained by God's decree before He sent you as a messenger that none will believe in you and follow you and affirm what God sent you with of guidance and light except one for whom happiness was set in the first book before the heavens and the earth and what is in them were created. Report 17909, from Ibn Abbas on this verse and on [[passage/quran-10-100|10:100]] and the like in the Qur'an: the Prophet was eager that all people believe and follow him in guidance, and God told him that none believes but one for whom happiness was set in the first remembrance, and none goes astray but one for whom misery was set in the first remembrance. The editor says the printed text adds 'of his people', which is not in the manuscript, so he removed it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 212}

If someone asks what is the point of 'all of them together', since 'all' implies 'together' and 'together' implies 'all', and each makes the other unnecessary, the reply is that the people of Arabic differed. Some of the Basran grammarians said 'together' is used for emphasis, as in [[passage/quran-16-51|16:51]], where 'two gods' already points to two. Another said 'together' is put after 'all of them' because 'together' occurs only as emphasis while 'all of them' occurs as both emphasis and noun, and that if it were said that he joined them to show the two have one meaning it would be allowed here. He then compares numbers and the saying 'I have a dirham and two dirhams', and says that the one and the two are not ambiguous as the plural is. The editor says he is left unsatisfied about the explanation of this passage and fears something has dropped from it, since it is not clear to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 213}

His words 'will you then compel people until they are believers?' mean God tells His prophet that none will affirm you, Muhammad, nor follow you and acknowledge what you brought, except those whom your Lord wills to affirm you, not by your compelling them nor by your eagerness for it. 'Will you compel people until they become believers' in you, affirming what you brought from your Lord? So proclaim what you are commanded and turn away from the polytheists against whom your Lord's word has been fulfilled that they will not believe. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 213}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
