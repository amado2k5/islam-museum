## Summary
Verse 143 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 143 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 248).** Abu Ja'far says God tells the Companions of Muhammad that they used to wish for the means of death, which is fighting, and had now seen what they wished for; the pronoun returns to death and the sense is fighting, and the looking means they saw it from close by. The editor says the printed text had a corrupt sentence and the manuscript a blank the size of a word, so he put in brackets what he gathered from Abu Ja'far's words. Some grammarians held that the mention of looking is for emphasis, as one says "I saw him plainly" or "I saw him with my own eye". As al-Tabari gives the occasion, some Companions who had missed Badr used to wish, before Uhud, for a day like Badr on which to prove themselves and gain a reward like the people of Badr; when Uhud came, some fled and some stood fast until they fulfilled what they had pledged to God, so He rebuked those who fled and praised those who stood firm and kept their pledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 248}

**Reports (pp. 248-250).**
- Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid (7930): men were absent from Badr and used to wish to meet a day like it and gain the good and reward the people of Badr gained; at Uhud some turned back and God rebuked them for it. Abu Asim was unsure of the exact verb among three close ones. Al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa from Shibl through Ibn Abi Najih from Mujahid (7931) is the same, except that he settled on "rebuked" without doubt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 248}
- Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id from Qatada (7932): some believers had not been at Badr and had seen what God gave the people of Badr of favour, honour and reward, so they wished for a fight; the fight was brought to them at the edge of Medina at Uhud, and God spoke as you hear, down to the verse on the thankful ([[passage/quran-3-144|3:144]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 249}
- Al-Hasan ibn Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq from Ma'mar from Qatada (7933): they wished to meet the polytheists and fight them, and when they met at Uhud they turned away. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 249}
- Al-Muthanna from Ishaq from Ibn Abi Ja'far from his father from al-Rabi' (7934): like Qatada's report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 249}
- Muhammad ibn Bashshar from Hawdha from Awf from al-Hasan (7935): it reached him that some Companions used to say that if they met the enemy with the Prophet they would do such and such; they were tried by it, and not all of them were true to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 250}
- Al-Suddi (7936): some Companions who had missed Badr, seeing the standing of its people, asked God to show them a day like Badr on which to prove themselves; they were shown Uhud. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 250}
- Ibn Ishaq (7937): those who urged the Messenger to take them out against the enemy, having missed the earlier day at Badr, in their desire for martyrdom; the sense is that they saw death by swords in men's hands, with the way left open between them and their enemy, as they looked, and you turned from them. In notes the editor says the printed text has a word the publisher altered, that the manuscript has an undotted word he reads as a form of a root meaning to hurry someone, and that he would have preferred it had Ibn Hisham not given "urged"; that the Sira (3: 117) continues 7929; and that the Sira differs at one point ("then he turned them from you" for "you turned from them"), two different meanings, but being a report he cannot prefer one without a reason, so both are acceptable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 250}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
