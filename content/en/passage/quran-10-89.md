**Commentary on verse 89 (pp. 185-188)**

On [[passage/quran-10-89|10:89]] al-Tabari says: this is God's report of His answering Moses and Aaron's prayer against Pharaoh, the nobles of his people and their wealth. God said to them: 'your prayer has been answered' regarding Pharaoh, his chiefs and their wealth. If someone asks how the answer is attributed to two when the prayer came from one, the reply is that though the one praying was one, the other said 'amin', and that was Aaron, so the answer is attributed to both, since one who says 'amin' is a supplicant, and so the interpreters said. Report 17847, from Ikrima: Moses used to pray and Aaron to say amin. Some of the people of Arabic claimed that the Arabs address one person as two, citing a line of Mufris b. Rib'i al-Asadi in which the poet tells his two companions not to hurry him as he pulls up the roots of a bush to light a fire for roasting; the editor gives the variants of the line and its setting. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 185}

Reports 17848, from Abu Salih, and 17849 and 17850, from Muhammad b. Ka'b: Moses prayed and Aaron said amin; report 17851, from Abu al-Aliya, and report 17852, from al-Rabi' b. Anas, likewise; report 17853, from Ikrima: Moses used to pray and Aaron to say amin; report 17854, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abbas and Ikrima: Aaron said amin to Moses' prayer and God said 'your prayer has been answered, so be steadfast'; report 17855, from Ibn Zayd: Aaron used to say amin and God said 'your prayer has been answered', so the amin became a prayer and he a partner in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 186}

As for 'so be steadfast', it is a command from God to Moses and Aaron to stay steadfast and firm in their charge of calling Pharaoh and his people to God's oneness and obedience until God's punishment comes, the punishment in which He told them He had answered them. Report 17856, from Ibn Abbas: 'be steadfast' means go on with My command, and that is steadfastness; Ibn Jurayj said they say Pharaoh stayed forty years after this prayer, and the editor says the manuscript has 'after this verse'. His words 'and do not follow the way of those who do not know' mean do not walk the road of those ignorant of the truth of My promise, so as to hasten My decree, for My promise has no breach, My threat will fall on Pharaoh, and My punishment will strike him and his people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 187}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
