## Summary
Verse 275 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 275 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), pp. 7 to 10 of volume 6, opening the commentary on usury. Al-Tabari explains who is meant by those who take usury: ira' is adding to a thing, one says so-and-so exceeded so-and-so when he added to him, the addition is riba, and a thing grew when it increased over what it was and became big; a mound is so called for its extra height and rising over the ground around it, and from this one says so-and-so is at the raised place of his people, meaning high standing among them, so the root of riba is rising and increase. The one who takes usury is called so for doubling the money he was owed by his debtor as due, or adding to it because of the term he postpones it to; this is al-Tabari's explanation, and he cites the verse on doubled multiples at ([[passage/quran-3-130|3:130]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 7} The editor notes two bracketed additions to make the sentence run, corrects a phrase on the raised place of his people from al-Zamakhshari's Asas al-balagha, and observes the manuscript copyist's haste and many omissions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 7}

Al-Tabari says the interpreters said likewise. 6235 and 6236, Mujahid: the usury God forbade was, in the pre-Islamic period, that a man was owed a debt by another and said, you get so much and give me time, and was given time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} 6237, Qatada: the usury of the pre-Islamic people was that a man sold goods for a stated term, and when the term came and his partner could not pay he increased it and gave time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} These reports are given as transmitted and are not a legal ruling by the museum.

Abu Jafar then says those who take the usury he described in this world will not rise in the next life from their graves except as rises the one whom the devil has driven mad in this world, who is seized and thrown down, the touch being madness; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} The editor notes that the print replaced the wording by the verse's own words, so it was no longer an explanation, and that he chose the manuscript reading meaning choking, relying on report 6242 from Ibn Abbas and report 6247, adding that choking is among the signs of madness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8}

The interpreters said likewise. 6238 and 6239, Mujahid: that is on the day of resurrection, for taking usury in this world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6240, Ibn Abbas: that is when he is raised from his grave; the editor gives the biography of the narrator Rabi'a ibn Kulthum al-Basri, quoting al-Nasa'i that there is no harm in him and in the weak narrators that he is not strong, and Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in that he is reliable, and for his father Kulthum ibn Jabr Ahmad's remark that he is reliable and al-Nasa'i's that he is not strong, with death in 130; these assessments are the editor's and those he quotes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6241, Ibn Abbas: on the day of resurrection it is said to the usurer, take up your weapon for war, and he recited the verse, saying that is when he is raised from his grave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6242, Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the eater of usury is raised on the day of resurrection mad and choking. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6243, Qatada: that is the sign of the people of usury on the day of resurrection, raised with a derangement from the devil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6244, Qatada: it is the derangement the devil brings about from madness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6245, al-Rabi ibn Anas: they are raised on the day of resurrection with derangement from the devil, and in some readings there is a wording that mentions the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6246, al-Dahhak: whoever died while taking usury is raised on the day of resurrection staggering like the one the devil touches. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6247, al-Suddi: it means madness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6248, Ibn Zayd: this is their likeness on the day of resurrection, they do not stand among people but as one stands who is choked, as though mad; the editor notes a garbling of the sentence in print and manuscript and a place where the copyist struck out his own writing, so he omitted a phrase from the sentence to bring it nearer the sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} We stop at 6248. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.
