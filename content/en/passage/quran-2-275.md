## Summary
Verse 275 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 275 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), pp. 7 to 10 of volume 6, opening the commentary on usury. Al-Tabari explains who is meant by those who take usury: ira' is adding to a thing, one says so-and-so exceeded so-and-so when he added to him, the addition is riba, and a thing grew when it increased over what it was and became big; a mound is so called for its extra height and rising over the ground around it, and from this one says so-and-so is at the raised place of his people, meaning high standing among them, so the root of riba is rising and increase. The one who takes usury is called so for doubling the money he was owed by his debtor as due, or adding to it because of the term he postpones it to; this is al-Tabari's explanation, and he cites the verse on doubled multiples at ([[passage/quran-3-130|3:130]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 7} The editor notes two bracketed additions to make the sentence run, corrects a phrase on the raised place of his people from al-Zamakhshari's Asas al-balagha, and observes the manuscript copyist's haste and many omissions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 7}

Al-Tabari says the interpreters said likewise. 6235 and 6236, Mujahid: the usury God forbade was, in the pre-Islamic period, that a man was owed a debt by another and said, you get so much and give me time, and was given time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} 6237, Qatada: the usury of the pre-Islamic people was that a man sold goods for a stated term, and when the term came and his partner could not pay he increased it and gave time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} These reports are given as transmitted and are not a legal ruling by the museum.

Abu Jafar then says those who take the usury he described in this world will not rise in the next life from their graves except as rises the one whom the devil has driven mad in this world, who is seized and thrown down, the touch being madness; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} The editor notes that the print replaced the wording by the verse's own words, so it was no longer an explanation, and that he chose the manuscript reading meaning choking, relying on report 6242 from Ibn Abbas and report 6247, adding that choking is among the signs of madness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8}

The interpreters said likewise. 6238 and 6239, Mujahid: that is on the day of resurrection, for taking usury in this world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6240, Ibn Abbas: that is when he is raised from his grave; the editor gives the biography of the narrator Rabi'a ibn Kulthum al-Basri, quoting al-Nasa'i that there is no harm in him and in the weak narrators that he is not strong, and Ahmad and Ibn Ma'in that he is reliable, and for his father Kulthum ibn Jabr Ahmad's remark that he is reliable and al-Nasa'i's that he is not strong, with death in 130; these assessments are the editor's and those he quotes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6241, Ibn Abbas: on the day of resurrection it is said to the usurer, take up your weapon for war, and he recited the verse, saying that is when he is raised from his grave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6242, Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the eater of usury is raised on the day of resurrection mad and choking. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} 6243, Qatada: that is the sign of the people of usury on the day of resurrection, raised with a derangement from the devil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6244, Qatada: it is the derangement the devil brings about from madness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6245, al-Rabi ibn Anas: they are raised on the day of resurrection with derangement from the devil, and in some readings there is a wording that mentions the day of resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6246, al-Dahhak: whoever died while taking usury is raised on the day of resurrection staggering like the one the devil touches. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6247, al-Suddi: it means madness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} 6248, Ibn Zayd: this is their likeness on the day of resurrection, they do not stand among people but as one stands who is choked, as though mad; the editor notes a garbling of the sentence in print and manuscript and a place where the copyist struck out his own writing, so he omitted a phrase from the sentence to bring it nearer the sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 10} We stop at 6248. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), pp. 11 to 13. Al-Tabari explains the devil's staggering by touch as his driving him mad by touching him; one says a man was touched and seized with a fit, so he is touched or fitted, when the visitation comes upon him and he goes mad, and he cites a verse at ([[passage/quran-7-201|7:201]]) and a line of al-A'sha (mentioned, not reproduced). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 11} The editor in notes gives the line's source and wording and explains words in the lines before it, such as the wide waste, the strong she-camel and madness; these are his remarks on the poetry, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 11}

Al-Tabari raises a question: what of one who does what God forbade of usury in his trade but does not eat it, does he deserve this warning? He answers yes, for what is meant by usury in the verse is not eating alone; but those about whom these verses came down, when they came down, took their food from usury, so He described them by their trait, magnifying the matter of usury and making their state ugly to them; he cites the two verses at ([[passage/quran-2-278|2:278]]) and ([[passage/quran-2-279|2:279]]) as showing that the prohibition covered all senses of usury and that doing it, eating it, taking it and giving it are alike, as reports from the Prophet agree. This is al-Tabari's explanation and argument, not a legal ruling by the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 12} In 6249 he gives, without a chain, that the Prophet cursed the one who eats usury, who gives it to eat, the one who writes it and its two witnesses, when they know of it; the editor notes that al-Tabari gave it in short and without a chain, that Ibn Kathir fully documented it, and that al-Suyuti gives it in al-Durr al-manthur from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and ascribes it to Ahmad, Abu Ya'la, Ibn Khuzayma and Ibn Hibban. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 12}

In the first note on p. 12 the editor adds, in his own voice, a remark about his time, saying that people of fitna try to make usury light for people though God made it grave and ugly, quoting a saying in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan that a time will come when people eat usury and whoever does not eat it will be reached by its dust, and he prays to God; this is the editor's personal remark, neither al-Tabari's nor the museum's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 12}

Al-Tabari then explains the clause beginning that is because they said sale is like usury: the thing pointed to is the rising from their graves he described, like the rising of one driven mad; what befalls them of the ugliness of their state, the terror of rising and the evil that comes to them is because in this world they lied and invented and said that the sale God made lawful to His servants is like usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 12} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 13} He says that those of the pre-Islamic people who took usury, when one's money fell due on his debtor, the debtor would say give me more time and I will add to your money, and they were told this is usury, not lawful, and they would say it is the same to us whether we add at the start of the sale or when the money falls due, and God belied them in what they said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 13}

Al-Tabari then explains what God made lawful of sale and forbade of usury: God made profits lawful in trade, buying and selling, and forbade the addition added for the owner of the money for giving his debtor more time and postponing his debt; the two additions are not alike, one coming from sale, the other from postponing the money and extending the term; God forbade the second and allowed the first, which is the extra over the capital with which the seller bought his goods, so he takes their surplus; the addition from sale is not the like of the addition from usury, for the command is His and the creation His, deciding among them what He wills, and none may object to His judgement or oppose His command, they have only to obey and submit. This is al-Tabari's explanation, attributed to him and not a ruling by the museum. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 13} The editor refers to earlier commentary on sale and corrects a phrase in the print from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 13} We stop at the end of p. 13. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-275|2:275]]), p. 14. Al-Tabari explains the admonition as the reminding and warning with which He reminded and warned them in the verses of the Qur'an and threatened them with punishment for taking usury. Whoever receives it and stops taking usury, restrains himself from it and desists, keeps what he ate and took and which passed before the admonition and the prohibition came. His affair after the admonition and the prohibition came, and after he stopped taking it, is with God in being kept from it and granted success; if He wills He keeps him from taking it and holds him firm in stopping, and if He wills He leaves him. Whoever returns to taking usury after the prohibition and says what he said before the admonition came, likening sale to usury, their end is the fire, lasting, meaning those who do it and say it are people of the fire of Gehenna; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 14} Report 6250, al-Suddi: the admonition is the Qur'an and what is past is what he ate of usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 14} The editor refers to earlier commentary on admonition, stopping, the people of the fire and abiding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 14}
