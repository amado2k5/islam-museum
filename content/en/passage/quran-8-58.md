**Commentary on verse 58 (pp. 25-27)**

Al-Tabari says: "if you fear", Muhammad, from an enemy with whom you have a pact and treaty that he will break his covenant, undo his treaty and betray you, which is treachery and perfidy, "then throw it back to them on equal terms", meaning confront them with war and tell them, before you fight them, that you have annulled the covenant between you because of the signs of treachery that appeared from them, so that you and they are alike in knowing that you are fighting them, and they can take up the gear of war while you are clear of perfidy; "God does not love the treacherous", those who betray one who is under security and covenant by fighting him before telling him he is at war with him. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of treachery and "throwing back", and notes how "signs" reads in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 25}

Al-Tabari raises a question: how can a covenant be broken for fear of treachery, when fear is a surmise and not certainty? He answers that it is not as the questioner thought; the meaning is that when signs of treachery appear from your enemy and you fear they will fall on you, you hand them the reins of peace and notify them of war. That is like what the Qurayza did when they answered Abu Sufyan and the idolaters with him by agreeing to support them against the Messenger of God and fight alongside them, after the covenant by which they had pledged peace; their answering him gave the Prophet cause to fear treachery against him and his companions. So too is the rule for any people in truce with the believers from whom the Muslims' imam sees signs of treachery like those that appeared from the Qurayza: it is his duty to throw the pact back on equal terms and notify them of war. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 25}

The meaning of "on equal terms" is that your knowledge and theirs be equal that each party is at war with the other, not at peace. It is said the verse was sent down about the Qurayza; report 16221, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "throw it back to them on equal terms", he said, the Qurayza. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 26}

Some said "equal terms" here means leisure; report 16222, from al-Walid ibn Muslim, through Ali ibn Sahl: it is clear to us that "on equal terms" means at leisure, as Bukayr told us from Muqatil ibn Hayyan on the verse "a release from God and His messenger to those of the idolaters with whom you made a covenant, so travel in the land four months" (9:1-2). The authorities on Arab speech differ on its meaning. Some said it means on justice, until your knowledge and theirs is balanced about the war each owes the other, and cited a line of rajaz about striking the faces of treacherous enemies until they respond to "sawa'", meaning justice. Others said it means the middle, citing a line of Hassan lamenting the helpers of the Messenger, with the sense of the middle of the grave niche. The editor says the author of the rajaz is unknown, that the publisher misread the line, and explains the word for the treacherous as the plural of a habitual betrayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 27}

Al-Tabari then says these senses are close, because justice is a middle that neither rises above the truth nor falls short of it, the middle is justice, and the equality of both parties' knowledge of what each owes the other after the truce is justice in action and a middle; as for al-Walid ibn Muslim's claim that it means leisure, he knows no basis for it in Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 27}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
