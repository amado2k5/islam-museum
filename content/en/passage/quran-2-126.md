## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when Abraham said: my Lord, make this a safe land" al-Tabari says: remember when Abraham said, my Lord, make this land a safe land. By "safe" he means safe from tyrants and others having power over it and from God's punishment reaching it as it reaches other lands, of swallowing by the earth, flood and the like. From Qatada: we were told that the sanctuary was made sacred directly beneath the Throne, and that the House came down with Adam, who circled it and the believers after him, until at the time of the flood God raised it and purified it so the punishment of the people of the earth did not reach it; Abraham then looked for its trace and built it on an old foundation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 44} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 45}

He then asks whether the sanctuary was safe only after Abraham asked his Lord for it, and says they differed. Some said it had always been safe from God's punishment and from the tyrants of His creation since the heavens and the earth were created, and they argued from the report of Abu Shurayh al-Khuza'i that when Mecca was conquered the Prophet addressed the people and said that God made Mecca sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth; and from a report from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet said of Mecca at its conquest that it is a sanctuary God made sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth, not lawful to anyone before or after him, and made lawful to him for an hour of the day. The editor's footnotes give the sources for these two reports and discuss the chain of the second. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 45} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 46}

Al-Tabari goes on with the argument of those who say the sanctuary was always safe: that Mecca since its creation has been a safe sanctuary from God's punishment and from tyrants, and that Abraham did not ask his Lord for that but asked that its people be safe from drought and dearth and that its dwellers be given fruits, from "and provide its people with fruits" and "our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring in a valley without cultivation by Your sacred House", since by saying "sacred" on arriving he described it as already made sacred before him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 47} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 48}

Others said the sanctuary was lawful before Abraham's prayer like other lands and became sacred by his making it so, as Medina was lawful before the Prophet made it sacred. They argued from reports including one from Jabir from the Prophet: Abraham made God's House sacred and safe, and I have made Medina sacred between its two lava tracts; one from Abu Hurayra from the Prophet that Abraham was God's servant and friend and that he made Mecca sacred and the Prophet made Medina sacred; and one from Rafi' ibn Khadij to the same effect. The editor's footnotes give the sources of these reports and assess their chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 48} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 49}

He mentions their saying that God reported Abraham saying "my Lord, make this a safe land" without saying he asked for safety from some things and not others, and that the reports of Abu Shurayh and Ibn Abbas are no proof because of their chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 50}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice. God made Mecca a sanctuary when He created it, as the Prophet reported that He made it sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth, without that being declared sacred through any of His prophets, but by His preventing anyone who wished it ill and by His averting from it and its dwellers the calamities and punishments that befell other places; so it remained until God settled Abraham there and he placed Hajar and Ishmael in it. Abraham then asked his Lord to make its sanctity a duty on His servants through his tongue, so that it be a custom for those after him, and He answered him; so the prohibition became a duty on creation through Abraham, and that is why its sanctification is ascribed to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 50} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 51}
