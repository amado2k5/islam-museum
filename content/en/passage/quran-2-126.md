## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when Abraham said: my Lord, make this a safe land" al-Tabari says: remember when Abraham said, my Lord, make this land a safe land. By "safe" he means safe from tyrants and others having power over it and from God's punishment reaching it as it reaches other lands, of swallowing by the earth, flood and the like. From Qatada: we were told that the sanctuary was made sacred directly beneath the Throne, and that the House came down with Adam, who circled it and the believers after him, until at the time of the flood God raised it and purified it so the punishment of the people of the earth did not reach it; Abraham then looked for its trace and built it on an old foundation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 44} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 45}

He then asks whether the sanctuary was safe only after Abraham asked his Lord for it, and says they differed. Some said it had always been safe from God's punishment and from the tyrants of His creation since the heavens and the earth were created, and they argued from the report of Abu Shurayh al-Khuza'i that when Mecca was conquered the Prophet addressed the people and said that God made Mecca sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth; and from a report from Ibn Abbas that the Prophet said of Mecca at its conquest that it is a sanctuary God made sacred on the day He created the heavens and the earth, not lawful to anyone before or after him, and made lawful to him for an hour of the day. The editor's footnotes give the sources for these two reports and discuss the chain of the second. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 45} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 46}
