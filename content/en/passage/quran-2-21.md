## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari takes "O people, worship your Lord" as addressed to both groups just described, those whose hearts were sealed and the deceiving hypocrites, and to everyone who is accountable, and he defines worship as submission to God by obedience and humble devotion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 362} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 363}
He reports from Ibn Abbas that "worship" means "declare His oneness", which al-Tabari understands as giving obedience and worship to the Lord alone, to the exclusion of the rest of creation. He also reports from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Mas'ud and others of the Companions that "and those before you" means that God created them and those before them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 363}
He counts this verse among the strongest evidence against those who hold that commanding what can be done only with God's help is permissible only after God has given the one commanded that help, since God commanded worship and repentance of people He had told would not believe or turn back from their error. This is his own argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 363}
On "that you may be mindful" he says it means that by worship, obedience and devotion to God alone they may guard against His wrath and be among those with whom He is pleased. He gives Mujahid's gloss, "that you may obey", which al-Tabari thinks means guarding against the Lord through obeying Him and leaving error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 364}
He takes up the objection that "perhaps" implies doubt in God, and answers that here it carries no doubt but the sense of "so that", citing two verses by a poet whose name he does not know. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 365}
