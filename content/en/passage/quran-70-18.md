## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 18 to 21 as a single unit. He interprets His saying, “Nay, indeed, it is a blazing Fire”: No, that is not so; nothing will save him from God’s punishment. Then He begins the account of what He, may His praise be exalted, has prepared for him there, saying, “Indeed, it is a blazing Fire.” Laza is one of the names of Hell, and therefore it is not permissible to use it in this way. The scholars of Arabic differed on its grammatical function. Some grammarians of Basra said: It is in the accusative case as a substitute for the pronoun “it,” and the predicate of “inna” is “striking away.” He said: If you wish, you can make “laza” the nominative case as the predicate of “inna,” and “striking away” the subject of the sentence. Some who rejected this said: The apparent meaning should not be followed by the implied meaning except in rare cases. He said: The preferred reading is “It is a blazing Fire, tearing away the skin.” “Blazing Fire” is the predicate, and “tearing away” is the circumstantial qualifier. He said: Whoever raises the subject is beginning a new sentence, because it is praise or condemnation. He said: It cannot begin a new sentence except in this way. The passage also quotes statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Sufyan.
