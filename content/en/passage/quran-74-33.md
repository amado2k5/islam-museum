## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 32 to 37 as a single unit. He interprets the phrase “And the night when it departs” as meaning “when it turns away.” Others have related it in a chain of transmission: “My father told me, who said: My uncle told me, who said: My father told me, on the authority of his father, on the authority of Ibn Abbas: ‘And the night when it departs’ means its darkening.” The reciters differed in their reading of this. The majority of the reciters of Medina and Basra read it as “when it departs,” while some reciters of Mecca and Kufa read it as “when it departs.” The correct view, in our opinion, is that both are well-known and correct readings in meaning, so whichever reading a reciter uses is acceptable. Scholars of Arabic have differed on this matter. Some Kufans said that they are two dialects, as it is said: “The day departed” and “the summer departed.” They said: Similarly, “before” and “approached.” So, when they say “The rider approached” and “departed,” they only use the alif. The passage quotes statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, and Ibn Zayd.
