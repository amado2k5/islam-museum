## Summary
Verse 156 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 156 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "lest you say: the Book was sent down only to two groups before us, and we were unaware of their study", al-Tabari says the grammarians differ on what governs "an" and on the meaning. Some Basran grammarians said it means "then We gave Moses the Book, completing it, lest you say..." (the editor thinks the right wording is "this is a blessed Book We sent down lest you say", which is what al-Tabari chooses later). Some Kufan grammarians said it is accusative by an implied verb, the sense being "follow it and be mindful, that you may receive mercy, be mindful lest you say", like the words of [[passage/quran-49-2|49:2]], "lest your deeds be void while you do not perceive". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 239}

Others of them said it is accusative from two places: one, "We sent it down lest they say the Book was sent down only to two groups before us", the other from the command "be mindful"; and he said it does not fit in the place of "an" as in [[passage/quran-4-176|4:176]], "God makes clear to you lest you go astray". Al-Tabari says the soundest view is that of those who said "an" is accusative through its attachment to the sending down, for the sense is: this is a blessed Book We sent down lest you say that the Book was sent only to two groups before us. The two groups are the Jews and the Christians; God sent His Book to His Prophet Muhammad so that the idolaters should not say, no Book was sent to us to follow, we were not commanded or forbidden, so there is no argument against us in what we do or leave, since no book or messenger came to us from God; the argument is only on the two groups to whom the Book was sent before us. So the commentators said, and he gives (14180) from Ibn Abbas that they are the Jews and the Christians. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "group". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 240}

He gives (14181) and (14182) from Mujahid that the two groups are the Jews and the Christians and that it was feared Quraysh would say it; and (14183) from Qatada and (14184) from al-Suddi the same. "And we were unaware of their study" means: lest you say that we were heedless of the two groups' recitation of the Book sent down to them, not knowing what it is, what they read or say, or what was sent to them in their book, because they were its people and not us; it did not concern us, we were not commanded by it and it was not in our tongue, so that they could take that as an argument; and God cut off that argument by sending down the Qur'an to His Prophet Muhammad. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "study" and "heedlessness", and notes that the printed "what it is" is supported by report 14288 later. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 241}

He gives (14185) from Ibn Abbas that "their study" means their recitation; (14186) from Qatada that it means their reading; (14187) from Ibn Zayd that "study" is reading and knowledge, and he recited [[passage/quran-7-169|7:169]], "they studied what is in it", saying that they knew what is in it and did not come to it in ignorance; and (14188) from al-Suddi that it means their reading, which we did not know. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 242}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
