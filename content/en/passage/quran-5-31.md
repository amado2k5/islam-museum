**Commentary on verse 31: "then God sent a raven scratching in the earth", pp. 224-226**

Al-Tabari says this too is evidence that the matter of Adam's sons is contrary to what 'Amr reported from al-Hasan: had they been of the Children of Israel the killer would not have been ignorant of burying his brother and covering his body, but they were Adam's own sons and the killer did not know God's custom with the dead and what to do with his brother; it is said he carried him on his shoulder for a while until the corpse smelled, so God wished to teach him the custom and sent the two ravens.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 224}

Report 11752, from Ibn 'Abbas, says he carried his brother in a sack on his neck for a year until God sent the ravens and he saw them scratching and said: am I unable to be like this raven? and buried his brother; the editor notes a narrator's weakness. Report 11753, from Ibn 'Abbas, says God sent a living raven to a dead one, and the living one covered the dead one's body. Report 11754, from al-Suddi, from several Companions, says God sent two brother ravens that fought and one killed the other, dug for him and heaped earth on him; seeing it he said: woe to me, am I unable to be like this raven?  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 225}

Reports 11755 to 11758, from Mujahid and Ibn 'Abbas, say a raven dug a hole beside a dead raven and buried it while the killer watched, one adding that it killed it and heaped earth on it. Report 11759, from 'Atiyya, says that when he killed him he regretted it and held the body to himself until it smelled, and birds and beasts gathered round waiting for it to be thrown so they could eat it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 226}

Reports 11760, 11761, 11763 and 11764, from Qatada, Abu Malik and al-Dahhak, say a raven killed a raven or came to a dead raven and heaped earth on it while the killer watched, and then he said: woe to me, am I unable to be like this raven? Report 11762, from Mujahid, says he carried his brother on his shoulder for a hundred years not knowing what to do until he saw the raven bury the raven.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 227}

Report 11765, from Ibn Ishaq, from some people of the earlier scripture, says that when he killed him he was at a loss and did not know how to bury him, since he was the first slain and the first dead of Adam's sons. The people of the Torah claim that God asked Cain where his brother was, and he said he did not know and had not been his keeper; God said the voice of his brother's blood was crying out from the earth, that he was cursed from the earth that had opened its mouth to take it, and that it would no longer yield him its crops, so he would be fearful and wandering. Cain said his sin was too great to be forgiven and that whoever met him would kill him; God said it would not be so, whoever killed Cain would be repaid sevenfold, and set a sign on him so that none who found him would kill him, and he left God's presence east of Eden. The editor says this is another old translation of a passage of Genesis chapter 4, and that al-Tabari calls Cain "Qayn" in his history.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 228}

Report 11766, from Khaythama, says that when Adam's son killed his brother the earth drank his blood and was cursed, so it never drank blood again.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 229}

Al-Tabari gives the sense: God stirred up for the killer, who did not know what to do with his brother, a raven scratching in the earth and turning up its soil to show him how to cover his brother's corpse; the word may possibly mean the private parts, but the corpse is the stronger sense, and the interpreters took it so. Something is omitted here: God showed him by the raven digging in the earth for another dead raven and burying it, and then the killer said: am I unable to be like this raven? and buried his brother, and became one of those who regretted his disobedience of God in killing him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 229}

He adds that all that God mentions in these verses is a parable He gave for Adam's descendants, urging the believers among the Prophet's companions to forgive and overlook the Jews of Banu al-Nadir who had meant to kill the Prophet, when they came asking help with the blood-money for the two men killed by 'Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri; God showed them the baseness of their forebears' nature and their poor steadfastness on the path of truth despite His many favours, and likened their treachery, and the believers' fidelity and forgiveness, to Adam's two sons who made offerings, as a parable to follow the better one and not the worse.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 230}

Reports 11767, 11768 and 11769, from Bakr ibn 'Abdallah and al-Hasan, give with no chain to a Companion the Prophet's saying that God gave the two sons of Adam as a parable, so take the better of them and leave the worse; the editor says these are reports with a gap in the chain which he did not find in the books of the Sunna.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 230}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
