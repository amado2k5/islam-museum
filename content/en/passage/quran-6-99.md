## Summary
Verse 99 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 99 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "it is he who sent down water from heaven; with it we brought out the growth of everything, and from it we brought out green, from which we bring out grain piled one on another" al-Tabari says: God, to whom worship belongs pure, with no partner in it for anything else, is the God who sent down water from heaven, "with it we brought out the growth of everything", we brought out by the water we sent down from heaven the food of cattle, beasts, birds and wild animals and the provision and sustenance of the children of Adam, what they feed on and eat, grow on and flourish. The sense of "with it we brought out the growth of everything" is: we brought out with it what everything grows by, flourishes on and is made sound. Were it said that the sense is we brought out with it the growth of all kinds of plant, so that "everything" is the kinds of plants, it would be an allowable view, though the sound way is the first. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 573}

The editor says the printed text has "no sharing in it for anything else", differing from the manuscript through bad judgement, and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

"We brought out from it green" says: we brought out from it, meaning from the water we sent down from heaven, "khadir", fresh of crops. "Khadir" is "green", as the Arabs say "show her to me as a streak, I'll show her to you as rain", and one says "the earth turned green", "khadaran" and "khadara", and "khadir" is the fresh of herbs; one says "a green date-palm" when its unripe dates are seen green before they ripen, and "a man was cut down green" when he dies young and sound, and "it is yours, khadiran madiran", meaning pleasant and wholesome. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 574}

The editor says "show her to me as a streak, I'll show her to you as rain" is a proverb that the author of Lisan ascribes in "ghamar" to Abu Dhu'ayb al-Hudhali and does not ascribe in "khadir", and which al-Maydani in al-Amthal and Abu Hilal in Jamharat al-Amthal give without ascribing it to him; that "namra" is a cloud with black and white, said as a proverb for the soundness of a sign of a thing and the soundness of the indication of it; that "khadara" is a verbal noun like "ghadara" not given in the dictionaries under its root; and that "midr" is the soft, bare.

"We bring out from it grain piled one on another" says: we bring out from the green grain, meaning what is in the ear, the ear of wheat, barley, rice and the like, ears whose grains pile on one another. 13661, from al-Suddi: "from it green from which we bring out grain piled one on another", this is the ear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 574}

The editor refers for "grain" to p. 550.

On "and from the palm, from its spathe, clusters within reach" he says: and from the palm, from its spathe, clusters of it within reach, and that is why "clusters" is in the nominative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 574}

The editor says print and manuscript have "and from the palm, from its spathe, clusters within reach", which is the verse text, and that this explanation does not stand; the right form is as he set, inferred from al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

We stop here at the foot of p. 574, after the explanation of the nominative "clusters" in verse 99, and al-Tabari continues on p. 575.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
