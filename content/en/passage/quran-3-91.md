## Summary
Verse 91 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 91 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 91: the unbelievers who died in their disbelief.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: those who denied Muhammad's prophethood and did not confirm him and what he brought from God, of every community, its Jews, Christians, Magians and others, and died in that denial of his prophethood and of what he brought, will have nothing received in the hereafter from anyone of that description as exchange or bribe for leaving off his punishment for his disbelief, or as payment for pardoning him, even if he had gold enough to fill the earth from its east to its west and offered it as a bribe and recompense for leaving his punishment and pardoning his disbelief in return for what God brings on him of His punishment. For a bribe is received only by one in need of what he is bribed for; but how would He receive a ransom who owns this world and the next and is the creator of every ransom a ransomer offers for himself or another? He says he has shown that the ransom is the exchange and recompense from the one ransomed from, and the editor refers to volume 3. In a note he corrects 'khilaf' in the manuscript as a misreading, and says the printed text has a word that suggests the publisher found Abu Jafar's Arabic strange and turned it into his own; he explains 'recompense' here as exchange and atonement and 'payment' as the wage for a thing.

**The stated recompense.** Then He reports what they have with Him: those who denied and died in that have with God in the hereafter a punishment that pains, and have no relative, friend or companion to help them and rescue them from God and from His punishment as they used to be helped in this world against whoever tried to harm or displease them. The editor says the shifting of pronouns in this sentence is permissible and good even though it troubles some who read it. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, who said that Anas ibn Malik told us that the Prophet used to say: the unbeliever will be brought on the Day of Resurrection and told: if you had gold enough to cover the earth, would you ransom yourself with it? He will say: yes; and it will be said: you were asked for what is much easier than that (7384). The editor says the report was recorded by al-Bukhari in his Sahih (al-Fath 11: 348-350) by two routes, that of Hisham al-Dastuwa'i from Qatada and that of Said ibn Abi Aruba from Qatada, like al-Tabari's report here; Muslim has it (17: 148-149) by the route of Hisham and points to the route of Said and notes its difference; and the report has other routes in other wording in al-Bukhari and Muslim. Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan that every unbeliever is meant (7385).

**The construction of the word for gold (p. 586).** Al-Tabari reports that some Kufan grammarians said the word for gold is accusative as a departure from the measure before it and an explanation of it, the measure being 'the fill of the earth', as one says 'I have the amount of a skin of ghee' and 'the amount of a ratl of honey', where the honey explains the measure mentioned, an indefinite accusative explaining the measure. The Basran grammarians claimed it is accusative because the word for fill is occupied by the earth and the word for gold comes after them, so its accusative is like that of a circumstantial noun, since the circumstantial noun comes after a verb already occupied by its subject and is accusative as the object that follows a verb occupied by its subject; the like in speech is 'I have your like as a man' for 'among men', where 'man' is accusative because the annexation occupies the noun. The conjunction was introduced in the phrase about ransoming for an omitted clause after it which the conjunction shows, like the conjunction in the verse of al-An'am [[passage/quran-6-75|6:75]], the sense being, in his account, that a clause has been omitted which the conjunction indicates; so here, had there been no conjunction in the speech it would still be sound and nothing would be omitted. The editor explains that 'explanation' is specification, also called 'clarification', refers to volume 2, and notes what al-Farra set out in Maani al-Quran 1: 225-226. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 586}
