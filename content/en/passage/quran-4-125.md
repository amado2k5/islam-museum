## Summary
Verse 125 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 125 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 125: the best religion and the friend of God (pp. 250-252, notes 2-5 on p. 250, notes 1-2 on p. 251 and note 1 on p. 252).** Al-Tabari says this is God's judgement for Islam and its people with excellence over every other religion and its people: who, people, has a better and more correct religion and a better-guided way than one who submits himself to God, obeying Him and affirming His prophet Muhammad in what he brought from his Lord, and who does good, meaning acts on what his Lord commanded, treating as forbidden what He forbade and as permitted what He permitted, and follows the religion that Abraham, the friend of the Merciful, followed and bequeathed to his sons after him, upright on his path. He says he has already shown the differences over the word for upright and the proof for the right view. Report 10538 (Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak) says that God preferred Islam over every religion, that no deed is accepted in anything but Islam, and that this is the way of Abraham. The editor refers to earlier explanations of submitting oneself, of doing good, of the word for creed or community, and of the word for upright. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 252}

On the last clause, that God took Abraham as a friend, he says it means that God took Abraham as an ally. If one asks the sense of the friendship given to Abraham, the answer is that on Abraham's side it was enmity for God's sake, hatred for His sake, and loyalty and love for His sake; on God's side it was helping him against anyone who sought to harm him, as He saved him from the fire when Nimrod wished to burn him, or giving him the better of the argument when Nimrod disputed with him, as He did when the king of Egypt sought his wife, together with enabling him to do what he loved and making him an imam for those after him and a model for those who followed him in obedience and worship. Another account says that God named him friend because famine struck his region, and he travelled to a friend of his, of Mosul according to some and of Egypt according to others, to get provisions for his family, but did not find what he needed at his friend's. When he drew near his family he passed through a sandy waste and said that he would fill his sacks with sand so that his family would not be grieved by his returning without supplies and would think he had brought what they liked. He did so, and the sand in his sacks became flour. When he reached his house he slept; his family opened the sacks, found flour, kneaded and baked, and when he woke and asked about the bread they said it was from the flour he had brought from his friend. He knew, and said that it was from his friend, God. They said that this is why God called him friend. The editor notes that this shows how al-Tabari abridged his commentary in places, since otherwise he would have told the stories of Abraham, which have been given in scattered places earlier, and corrects a missing letter before the king of Egypt, and explains the word for sacks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 252}
