**Commentary on verse 63 (pp. 158-159)**

On "when they returned to their father they said, O our father, the measure has been withheld from us, so send our brother with us that we may get our measure, and we shall guard him", al-Tabari says: when Joseph's brothers returned to their father they said "the measure has been withheld from us", that is, whatever was above the measure measured for us was withheld, and only a camel's measure was measured for each man; "so send our brother with us", Benjamin, "that we may get our measure", that he may measure for himself another camel's load in addition to the load of our camels, "and we shall guard him" from any harm reaching him on his journey. The interpreters said the like: report 19474, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: they said, O our father, the king of Egypt honoured us with an honour that, had he been a man of Ya'qub's descendants, he could not have exceeded; he took Shim'un as a pledge and said, bring me this brother of yours to whom your father clings after your brother who perished, and if you do not bring him do not come near my land. The rest of the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 158}

Ya'qub said "shall I entrust him to you except as I entrusted you with his brother before? God is the best guardian and He is the most merciful of the merciful", and then told them: when you come to the king of Egypt, give him my greetings and say, our father blesses you and prays for you for what you have done for us. Report 19475, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: they set out until they reached their father; their dwelling, as some scholars told him, was at al-'Arabat in the land of Palestine in the Ghawr of al-Sham, and others say at al-Awlaj by the Sha'b below Hisma; he was a bedouin with sheep and camels. They said, O father, we came to the best of men; he lodged us and treated our lodging generously, measured for us and gave full measure without cheating us, and he has ordered us to bring him a brother of ours from our father, saying if you do not, do not come near me or enter my land. Ya'qub said to them "shall I entrust him to you except as I entrusted you with his brother before?". The editor corrects "Hisma" from the printed text against "Hisw" in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 159}

The readers differed on "naktal". Most readers of Medina and some of Mecca and Kufa read it with a nun, "we measure", meaning we and he; most readers of Kufa read it with a ya', "he measures", meaning he measures for himself as we measure for ourselves. Al-Tabari says the correct view is that these are two known readings with the same meaning, and whichever one a reader uses is right, because they were telling their father that an increase of measure beyond the number of their heads had been withheld from them, and then asked him to send their brother with them to measure for himself; when he measures for himself and they measure for themselves, the brother has entered their number, so it is the same whether the report concerns him alone or all of them in the plural form, since the meaning of the speech and what is meant by it are understood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 159}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
