## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "is it not that every time they made a covenant, a party of them threw it away? Rather, most of them do not believe", al-Tabari relates the grammarians' dispute about the "waw": some Basrans said it is a waw that accompanies interrogatives and is redundant, like the "fa" in "afa-kullama", while some Kufans said it is a conjunction onto which the question has been entered; he prefers the latter and rejects redundancy, since God's book cannot hold a particle with no meaning. The covenant is the pledge the Children of Israel gave their Lord, again and again, to act on the Torah, which some of them then broke time after time; God rebuked them and shamed their sons, who took the same path in the covenant to believe in Muhammad. He reports from Ibn Abbas that when the Prophet was sent and the pledge taken from them was mentioned, Malik ibn al-Sayf said: by God, no covenant was made with us about Muhammad and no pledge taken for him, and the verse came down. "Nabdh" originally means throwing away, hence a foundling is "mandhub" and nabidh is so called, as in a verse of Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali; "a party of them threw it away" means they cast it off and broke it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 399} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 400} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 401}

Al-Tabari continues, from Qatada and Ibn Jurayj, that "nabadhahu" means broke it, and that there was no covenant on earth they made that they did not break, making one today and breaking it tomorrow; in 'Abd Allah's reading it is "naqadahu". The pronoun in "nabadhahu" refers to the covenant, and "fariq" is a group with no singular from its own root, like "jaysh" and "rahṭ". On "rather, most of them do not believe" there are two senses: that the majority, not the few, are the breakers, or that most of them do not give credence to God, His messengers, His promise and His threat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 402} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 403}
