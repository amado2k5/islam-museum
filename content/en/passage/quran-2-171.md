## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "the likeness of those who disbelieve is as one who shouts at what hears nothing but a call and a cry" al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. Some said the likeness of the unbeliever, in his little understanding of what is recited to him from God, is that of the beast which hears the sound when called but does not understand what is said to it, from 'Ikrima, Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Rabi' and 'Ata'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 308} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 309} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 310} In a report from Mujahid, 'Ata' and al-Suddi, "the one who shouts" is the shepherd calling his flock, and so the Prophet calls those who hear only the sound of speech; al-Suddi links this to "deaf, dumb, blind". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 310} Al-Tabari explains that a word is left out: the likeness of the preaching to the unbelievers, and of their preacher, is like a caller shouting at his sheep, as one says "honour him with the honour due to a sultan"; it may also mean that their likeness, in their little understanding, is that of the beast called, which grasps only the sound, and that the sentence may be cast on the caller while its sense is for the one called, for which he cites Arabic usage. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 310} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 311} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 312} Others said the likeness of the unbelievers in calling on their gods and idols, which neither hear nor understand, is like one who shouts at what hears nothing but a call and a cry, the echo, which hears the caller's voice but understands nothing from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 312}

From Ibn Zayd: it is the man who shouts into the hollows of the mountains and a voice answers him, called the echo (suda); the likeness of their gods is like that, of no benefit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 313} Al-Tabari mentions another possibility, that their likeness in calling their gods is that of one shouting at his flock out of earshot, who gains nothing from his shouting but toil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 313} He chooses the first interpretation, said by Ibn 'Abbas and those who agreed, that the likeness of the preaching to the unbeliever, and of the preacher, is that of the caller to his sheep, because the verse was revealed about the Jews, who were not idol-worshippers revering them; he argues from the verses before and after and the reports that the Jews are meant, and from 'Ata' they are the Jews about whom "those who conceal what God sent down of the Book" was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 313} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 314} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 315} In a footnote the editor remarks that this wording does not fully fit al-Tabari's own earlier reading of the preceding verses (on the Arab polytheists, 2:163 to 2:169), and reads it as excepting those verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 314} "Yan'iq" means calling out to sheep, with a line of al-Akhtal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 315} On "deaf, dumb, blind, so they do not understand" he says they are deaf to the truth, not hearing it, mute to saying the truth, acknowledging it and making clear Muhammad's matter to people, and blind to guidance, from Qatada, al-Suddi and Ibn 'Abbas; the nominative is a fresh start. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 316}
