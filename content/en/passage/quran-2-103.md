## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari says that "had they believed and been God-fearing" means: had those who learn from the two angels what separates man and wife believed God and His messenger and feared His punishment, fulfilling His obligations and avoiding His disobediences, God's reward for them would have been better than magic and what they gained by it, if they knew the measure of God's reward; what is denied of them is knowledge of the extent of the recompense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 457} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 458}

"Mathuba" in Arabic is a verbal noun of "I rewarded you" (ithaba, thawab, mathuba), from "thaba ilayka al-shay'", the thing returned to you; to reward someone for a gift is to return something to him in exchange, hence God's reward of His servants, giving them a return so that a recompense comes back to them for what they did. He reports that some Basran grammarians held the answer to "law" is omitted because the sense indicates it, meaning "they would have been rewarded"; others of Basra denied this and held the answer is "mathuba" itself, "law" being answered as "la-in" is, because the two are close in meaning, so the sense is "if they believe and fear God, a reward from God is better". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 458}

For "mathuba" as reward he gives reports from Qatada (a reward from God), al-Suddi and al-Rabi'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 459}
