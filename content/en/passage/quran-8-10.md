**Commentary on verse 10 (pp. 417-418)**

Then al-Tabari says on "God made it only as good news and so that your hearts might be at rest by it; help comes only from God; God is Mighty, Wise": God did not make the following of the angels one after another and their coming one after another to you, believers, as a reinforcement for you "except as good news" for you, that is, a glad tidings… {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 417}

…glad tidings for you, announcing to you God's help for you against your enemies; "and so that your hearts might be at rest by it" meaning that your hearts be calmed by their coming to you and sure of God's help for you; "help comes only from God" meaning you are not helped against your enemy, believers, except that God helps you against them, not by the strength of your might and your powers but by God's help to you, since that is in His hand and to Him, He helps whom He wills of His creatures; "God is Mighty, Wise" meaning God who helps you, and in whose hand is the help of whom He wills of His creatures, is mighty, nothing overcoming Him and none prevailing against Him, rather He overcomes and prevails over everything because He created it, and wise in His ordering and in His helping whom He helps and abandoning whom He abandons of His creatures, no weakness or flaw entering His ordering. It is reported from Abd Allah ibn Kathir from Mujahid, report 15757: the Prophet was reinforced, of what God mentioned, with only a thousand angels following one another; His mention of the three and the five was good news, and they were reinforced with no more than this thousand that God mentioned in al-Anfal, while the three and the five were good news. Al-Tabari has dealt with that sufficiently in Surat Al Imran. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "good news", "rest", "mighty" and "wise". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 417} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 418}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
