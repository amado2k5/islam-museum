## Summary
Verse 91 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 91 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Another party of hypocrites (p. 26, note 1).** Al-Tabari says these are a different party who showed Islam to the Prophet and his Companions to be safe from killing, captivity and seizure of property while remaining unbelievers, which their own people knew; when they met their people they were with them and worshipped what they worshipped, so as to be safe from them too, and whenever their people called them to idolatry they relapsed and became idolaters like them. The editor says the bracketed addition is needed for the sense (repeated on p. 27). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 26}

**First view: people of Mecca (p. 27).** Some said they were people of Mecca who professed Islam out of caution while being unbelievers, to be safe on both sides. Report 10078 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) from Mujahid: people who came to the Prophet and professed Islam for show, then went back to Quraysh and fell back into idols, seeking safety on both sides; the Prophet was ordered to fight them unless they withdrew and made peace. Report 10079 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid is the same. Report 10080 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, same chain) from Ibn Abbas: whenever they wished to leave a trial they were thrown back into it; a man known to have spoken of Islam would be brought before a stick, a stone, a scorpion or a beetle, and the idolaters told him to say that this was his lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 27}

**Second view: idolaters who sought security (pp. 27-28).** Report 10081 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: a tribe in Tihama said, "Prophet of God, we will neither fight you nor fight our people", wanting safety from both; God refused them, and the sense is that whenever a trial came to them they perished in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 28}

**Third view: Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud al-Ashja'i (p. 28).** Report 10082 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: Nu'aym ibn Mas'ud al-Ashja'i was mentioned; he was trusted by both Muslims and idolaters and carried words between the Prophet and the idolaters; the trial is idolatry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 28}

**The sense of being sent back to the trial (p. 28, note 1).** Report 10083 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi') from Abu al-Aliya: whenever they were tested by it they went blind in it. Report 10084 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada: whenever a calamity befell them they perished in it. Al-Tabari says the matter is as he explained before: the word for trial means testing in Arabic and the word for casting back means returning, so the sense is that whenever they were put to the test to return to disbelief and idolatry, they returned to it. The editor refers to the earlier treatment of both words and to pp. 7, 15 and 16. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 28}

**If they do not withdraw or make peace (p. 29, notes 1-5).** Al-Tabari: if these people do not withdraw from you or surrender, handing you the reins and making peace (report 10085: al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi': the word means peace), and do not hold back their hands from fighting you, then seize them wherever you meet them on earth and kill them, for their blood is then lawful to you; and God has given you a proof for killing them wherever you meet them, because they persist in unbelief and have not emigrated from the abode of idolatry. The word "clear" means that the proof shows they deserve this from you and that you are right to kill them. The editor says both manuscript and print have a form the context does not call for, so he printed another, and that a conjunction has dropped out in another phrase and is better restored; he refers to earlier treatments of surrender, holding back and finding. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 29}

**The meaning of authority (p. 30, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the word for authority means proof. Report 10086 (al-Muthanna, from Qabisa, from Sufyan, from an unnamed man) from Ikrima: wherever the Qur'an has this word, it means a proof. Report 10087 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi: the clear authority is the proof. The editor refers to earlier treatments of both words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 30}


These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 30}
