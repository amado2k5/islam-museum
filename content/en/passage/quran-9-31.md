**Commentary on verse 31 (pp. 208-212)**

Al-Tabari says the Jews took their rabbis, who are the scholars, an explanation already given, the singular being either with a kasra or with a fatha on the first letter. Yunus al-Jarmi is reported to have claimed he had heard it only with the kasra, arguing from the saying "this is a scholar's ink"; al-Farra reported hearing both. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 208}

The Christians took their monks, those of the hermitages and the devout among them. Report 16630, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki, his father and Salama: "they took their rabbis and monks" means their reciters and scholars. "As lords besides God" means as masters besides God whom they obey in disobedience to God, making lawful what those masters made lawful of what God had forbidden them, and forbidding to themselves what those masters forbade of what God had made lawful. Report 16631, from Adi ibn Hatim, through al-Husayn ibn Yazid al-Tahhan, Abd al-Salam ibn Harb, Ghutayf ibn A'yan and Mus'ab ibn Sa'd: I came to the Prophet as he was reciting in the sura of disavowal "they took their rabbis and monks as lords besides God", and he said they did not worship them, but they used to make things lawful for them and they would treat them as lawful. The editor notes that al-Tabari gives it by three chains, all through Abd al-Salam ibn Harb, that al-Tirmidhi called it good and strange, known only through Abd al-Salam, with Ghutayf not well known in hadith, and that al-Suyuti cites it with further sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 209}

Report 16632, from Adi ibn Hatim, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Waki, Malik ibn Isma'il, Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Abd al-Salam ibn Harb: I came to the Messenger with a gold cross on my neck and he said "Adi, throw this idol off your neck", and I did; I came to him as he recited the sura of disavowal, and when he read this verse I said we do not worship them; he asked whether they do not forbid what God made lawful so that they forbid it, and make lawful what God forbade so that they treat it as lawful; I said yes, and he said that is their worship. Report 16633, from Adi, through Sa'id ibn Amr al-Sakuni, Baqiyya, Qays ibn al-Rabi', Abd al-Salam, Ghudayf and Mus'ab: I heard the Messenger reading the sura, and when he reached the verse I said they did not pray to them; he said you are right, but they made lawful what God had forbidden and so it was treated as lawful, and forbade what God had made lawful and so it was forbidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 211}

Report 16634, from Hudhayfa, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi, Sufyan, Habib ibn Abi Thabit and Abu al-Bakhtari: asked about the verse whether they worshipped them, he said no, when they made something lawful for them they treated it as lawful and when they forbade something they treated it as forbidden; report 16635 is similar; report 16636: they neither fasted nor prayed for them, but when they made something lawful they treated it so, and when they forbade what God had made lawful they held it forbidden, and that was their lordship. Report 16637, from Abu al-Bakhtari: they went to what God made lawful and made it forbidden, and to what God forbade and made it lawful, and were obeyed in that, so God made their obedience a worship; had they said to them "worship us", they would not have. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 212}

Report 16638, from Hudhayfa, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and al-Thawri: a man asked whether they worshipped them, and he said no, when they made something lawful for them they treated it as lawful, and when they forbade something they treated it as forbidden. Report 16639, from al-Hasan: in obedience. Report 16640, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: they made obedience to them seem good to them. Report 16641, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat and al-Suddi: they did not order them to prostrate to them, but ordered disobedience to God and were obeyed, and God called them lords by that. Report 16642, from Abu al-Aliya, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Numayr, Abu Ja'far al-Razi and al-Rabi' ibn Anas: I asked him how the lordship among the Children of Israel was, and he said that whatever their scholars ordered they did and whatever they forbade they left, on their word, while they found in God's book what they were ordered and forbidden, but they took counsel of men and cast God's book behind their backs. The editor notes that a bracketed sentence in the report is from the manuscript and he does not know what it means, so he kept it as it came. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 212}

We stop here at the foot of p. 212, in the middle of the commentary on verse 31 (after report 16642); the next batch begins at p. 213. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 212}
