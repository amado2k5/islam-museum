## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "evil is that for which they sold themselves", al-Tabari says it means "bad is what they sold themselves for"; the root of "bi'sa" is ba'isa, from bu's, lightened by moving the vowel, as with zillat and kibd, or it may be the Tamim dialect of moving the vowel of the middle letter to the first. He then sets out the grammarians' disagreement on "ma". Some Basrans said it is a noun on its own with "that they disbelieve" as its explanation, as in "na'ima rajulan Zayd". Some Kufans said "ma" is the subject of "bi'sa" and "that they disbelieve" the second noun, in the nominative or the genitive, citing al-Ma'ida 5:80 and the Arab saying "la-bi'sama tazwijun wa-la mahr". Al-Tabari rejects this view because "ma" with its clause "they sold" becomes a definite noun, making the sense "evil is their buying disbelief", which he holds is not allowed; he then reviews further views. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 338} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 339}
