## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "evil is that for which they sold themselves", al-Tabari says it means "bad is what they sold themselves for"; the root of "bi'sa" is ba'isa, from bu's, lightened by moving the vowel, as with zillat and kibd, or it may be the Tamim dialect of moving the vowel of the middle letter to the first. He then sets out the grammarians' disagreement on "ma". Some Basrans said it is a noun on its own with "that they disbelieve" as its explanation, as in "na'ima rajulan Zayd". Some Kufans said "ma" is the subject of "bi'sa" and "that they disbelieve" the second noun, in the nominative or the genitive, citing al-Ma'ida 5:80 and the Arab saying "la-bi'sama tazwijun wa-la mahr". Al-Tabari rejects this view because "ma" with its clause "they sold" becomes a definite noun, making the sense "evil is their buying disbelief", which he holds is not allowed; he then reviews further views. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 338} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 339}

Al-Tabari also notes a view that "bi'sama" is a single expression governing what follows, as in "bi'sama tazwijun wa-la mahr", and then prefers that "bi'sama" is governed by the pronoun returning in "they sold themselves for it", with "that they disbelieve" as its explanatory apposition. The sense is then: evil is that for which the Jews sold themselves, their disbelief in what God sent down, out of envy that God should send down His bounty, "an yunazzil" being accusative as a reason, "because God sends down". On "ishtaraw" he says it means "sold", reporting al-Suddi and Mujahid, and notes that Arabs say "sharaytu" for "I sold" and "ishtaraytu" for "I bought", citing verses of Yazid ibn Mufarrigh and al-Musayyab ibn 'Alas. On "baghyan" he says it means transgression and envy, from Qatada, al-Suddi and Abu al-'Aliya; al-Suddi reports they said the messengers had been from the Children of Israel, so why was this one from the Children of Ishmael. To the question how anything can be bought with disbelief, he answers that selling and buying among the Arabs is giving up what one owns for a return, and the words are used of anyone who earns by his own act good or ill; "evil is what they bought" then means evil is what they earned and evil the exchange they took, losing God's reward and incurring the Fire. He treats the verse as a parallel to al-Nisa' 4:51-54 on the envy of some of the People of the Book at what God gave the House of Abraham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 339} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 340} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 341} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 342} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 343}

On "that God should send down of His bounty on whom He wills of His servants" he repeats the sense with a report from Ibn Ishaq, from elders of the Ansar, that it was envy that God should place it in others than themselves, and similar reports from Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya, al-Suddi and 'Ali al-Azdi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 344}
