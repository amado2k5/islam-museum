**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 561-562)**

On [[passage/quran-12-7|12:7]] al-Tabari says: in Joseph and his eleven brothers there were signs, meaning lessons and reminders, for those who ask, meaning those who ask of their reports and stories, and by that He meant His Prophet Muhammad; for it is said God sent down this chapter to His Prophet to teach him what Joseph met from his nearest people and brothers in envy, along with God's honouring him, to console him for what he meets from his nearest kin among the polytheists of Quraysh, as Ibn Ishaq used to say; the editor corrects the manuscript's wording and explains that 'adaniyyahu' means those nearest to him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 561}

Report 18794, from Ibn Ishaq: God related to Muhammad the news of Joseph and his brothers' wrong and envy of him when he told of his dream, because the Messenger of God saw the wrong and envy of his people when God honoured him with prophecy, so that he should follow Joseph's example. The readers differ on 'signs for those who ask': most readers of the cities read 'ayat' in the plural, and it is related of Mujahid and Ibn Kathir that they read it in the singular. Al-Tabari says: the better of the two readings is the plural, for the consensus of the authorities among the readers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 562}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
