## Summary
Verse 96 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 96 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of the ranks and the views on it (pp. 97-98, note 1 on p. 97 and notes on p. 98).** Abu Ja'far says the ranks are merits from God and stations of honour. The commentators differed. Some, as in report 10256 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada), said it used to be said: Islam is a rank, migration within Islam a rank, jihad within migration a rank, and being killed in jihad a rank. Others, as in report 10257 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, who asked Ibn Zayd), said the ranks are the seven mentioned in sura at-Tawba at [[passage/quran-9-120|9:120]] and [[passage/quran-9-121|9:121]] (the wording is not given here); Ibn Zayd adds on p. 98 that at first the rank of jihad was undifferentiated and the one who strove with his property had a share in it, but when the ranks came in detail he was excluded and kept only the matter of expenditure, that the first items are not for the one who spends, and that what is said of spending is the expenditure of the one who sits. Others said the ranks are those of Paradise, with report 10258 (Ali ibn al-Hasan al-Azdi, from al-Ashja'i, from Sufyan, from Hisham ibn Hassan, from Jabala ibn Suhaym, from Ibn Muhayriz): the ranks are seventy, and between two ranks lies the running of a swift, trained thoroughbred horse for seventy years. The editor says he found no biography of Ali ibn al-Hasan al-Azdi, a teacher of al-Tabari who is cited by him in the History in several places; al-Ashja'i is Ubayd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan is al-Thawri, and Hisham ibn Hassan al-Qurdusi, Jabala ibn Suhaym and Ibn Muhayriz (Abd Allah) have all been dealt with earlier; he glosses the horse terms as its height in running, running hard, and a horse prepared for racing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 98}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the rest of the verse (pp. 98-99, notes 2 on p. 98 and 1-2 on p. 99).** Abu Ja'far holds (his choice) that the most apt reading is that the ranks are the ranks of Paradise, as Ibn Muhayriz said, because the mention of ranks is a rendering and explanation of the large reward, and the reward is the recompense (note 2 refers to his explanation of "reward" in volume 8). Since the ranks, forgiveness and mercy explain the reward, he sees no ground for those who take the ranks as deeds exceeding the deeds of the sitters, as Qatada and Ibn Zayd said. The sense of the verse for him is that God preferred the strivers over the sitters who are not disabled by a large reward and ample recompense, which is ranks given in the hereafter from the ranks of Paradise, raising them for what they endured for God. Forgiveness is pardon for their sins and the favour of leaving their punishment, and mercy is compassion toward them; the closing words describe God as one who has always covered the sins of His believing servants, pardoning the punishment for them, and who shows them compassion, favouring them with His blessings despite their going against His command and prohibition and committing disobedience. The editor says the printed text adds a conjunction before the verb of pardoning, and that he kept the manuscript's, which is better, and he refers to the language indexes for forgiveness and mercy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 99}
