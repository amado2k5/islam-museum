## Summary
Verse 123 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 123 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning (p. 169).** Abu Ja'far says: if you are steadfast and godwary, their scheming will not harm you at all and your Lord will help you; He helped you at Badr against your enemies when you were humbled, meaning few in number and without protection from people, until He gave you the upper hand over your enemy despite their numbers and your fewness. You are more numerous today than you were then, so if you are steadfast for God's command He will help you as He did that day. He then bids them godwariness, by obeying Him and avoiding what He forbade, so that they may thank Him for the help, for making their religion prevail and for guiding them to the truth from which their opponents strayed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 169}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7733): fewer in number and weaker in strength; the bidding to godwariness means to be wary of Him, for that is thanks for His favour. The editor says it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 113), the remainder of the reports ending in 7732, and that Abu Ja'far's wording in relating it is sounder than Ibn Hisham's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 170}

**Why Badr is called Badr (pp. 170-171).** Al-Tabari notes disagreement on the reason for the name. Some said it was water belonging to a man called Badr and was named for him:
- From Ibn Waki', his father, Zakariyya, al-Sha'bi (7734): it belonged to a man called Badr, so it was named for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 170}
- From Ya'qub, Hushaym, Zakariyya, al-Sha'bi (7735): it was a well of a man called Badr, so it was named for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 170}

Others rejected this and said it is the name of the spot, as other places have their names:
- From al-Harith ibn Muhammad, Ibn Sa'd, al-Waqidi, Mansur, Abu al-Aswad, Zakariyya, al-Sha'bi (7736): it was named because it was the water of a man of Juhayna called Badr. Al-Waqidi then says he mentioned this to Abdullah ibn Ja'far and Muhammad ibn Salih, who rejected it and said: why then are al-Safra, al-Hamra and Rabigh so called? It is the name of the place. He mentioned it to Yahya ibn al-Nu'man al-Ghifari, who said he heard the elders of Banu Ghifar say it was their water and halting-place, that no man called Badr ever owned it, and that it is not Juhayna country but Ghifar's; al-Waqidi says: this is what is known among us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 170} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 171}
- From al-Husayn ibn al-N-m-r-j (so printed), Abu Mu'adh, Ubayd ibn Sulayman, al-Dahhak (7737): Badr is water to the right of the Mecca road, between Mecca and Medina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 171}

**The meaning of being humbled (p. 171).** He says the word in the verse is the plural of "humble", as "the mighty" is the plural of "mighty" and "the wise" of "wise". Abu Ja'far says God so described them because of their few numbers, being some three hundred and ten or more against an enemy of between nine hundred and a thousand, as he explained earlier, and that the exegetes spoke as he did: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 171}
- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7738): Badr is water between Mecca and Medina where the Prophet and the polytheists met, and it was the first fight he fought; it was mentioned that he told his Companions that day that they were as many as the companions of Talut when he met Jalut, three hundred and some ten men, while the polytheists were a thousand or near it. The editor says part of it came earlier under number 5730 (as printed in the scanned text), refers to the numbers of the men of Badr in reports 5724-5732, and glosses a verb in the report as "approached". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 171}
- From Muhammad ibn Sinan, Abu Bakr, Abbad, al-Hasan (7739): it means few, being three hundred and some ten that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 172}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi' (7740): like Qatada's saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 172}
- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7741): fewer in number and weaker in strength; the note says it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 113) and has the sense of 7733 (the printed note gives its number as 7749, evidently 7741). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 172}

**The bidding to godwariness (p. 172).** Abu Ja'far says its sense is as explained, and Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq (7742) give it as: be wary of Me, for that is thanks for My favour. The editor says it is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 113) and part of 7733, and that the printed text and manuscript have a different form of the word for favour here, so he kept what stood in the earlier report and in Ibn Hisham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 172}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 172}
