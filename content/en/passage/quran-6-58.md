## Summary
Verse 58 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 58 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "say: if I had what you hasten, the matter between me and you would have been decided; God knows best the wrongdoers" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to these who hold gods and idols equal to their Lord, who deny what you brought them and ask you to bring them a sign out of their haste for punishment: if I had in my hand what you hasten of the punishment, the matter between me and you would have been decided, and it would have been settled with utmost speed by my hastening what you ask of it and hasten; but that is in the hand of God, who knows best the time of sending it on the wrongdoers, who put their worship, which should be only God's, in the wrong place and worship gods and images besides him; and he knows best the time of vengeance on them and the state of decision between me and them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 400}

It was also said that the sense of "the matter between me and you would have been decided" is the slaughter of death. Report 13304, from Ibn Jurayj: it reached me about "the matter would have been decided", he said: the slaughter of death. Al-Tabari supposes that the one who said this was drawing on [[passage/quran-19-39|19:39]], "warn them of the day of regret, when the matter is decided and they are heedless", for a story is related from the Prophet on it that shows the sense of this speaker for "the deciding of the matter"; but "the matter between me and you would have been decided" has nothing to do with it; it is a command from God to his prophet to say to one who hastened the decision between him and them by asking for a sign: if the punishment and signs were in my hand and with me, I would hasten for you what you ask of me, but it is in the hand of one who knows better than I and all his creation what is good for his creatures. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 401}

The editor says the printed text and manuscript have "that the speaker of this kind drew", which is strange speech he does not think Abu Jafar would come near, the right reading being as he gave it; and that Abu Jafar related in his commentary the report that on the day of resurrection death is brought like a white ram and made to stand between Paradise and the Fire, and the people of Paradise and the Fire are called and asked whether they know it; they say no, and it is said this is death, then it is taken and slaughtered, and it is called out: people of the Fire, eternity and no death, and people of Paradise, eternity and no death.

Verse 58 closes here at p. 401, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-59|6:59]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
