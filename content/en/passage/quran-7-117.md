**Commentary on verse 117 (pp. 29-30)**

Al-Tabari says: We inspired Moses, "Throw your staff", and he threw it, and it swallowed up what they falsely and vainly produced by sorcery; one says "laqiftu the thing, I laqaf it", meaning to snatch and swallow. The editor refers to al-Farra's "Ma'ani al-Qur'an". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 29}

Report 14942, from Qatada through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Moses threw his staff and it turned into a snake that ate up all their sorcery. Report 14943, from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Sufyan, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: it was a snake that swallowed their falsehoods; it passed nothing of their ropes and wood without swallowing it, so the sorcerers knew that this was a matter from heaven and not sorcery, and fell down in prostration saying they believed in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Moses and Aaron. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 29}

Report 14944, from al-Suddi through Musa ibn Harun, Amr and Asbat: God inspired Moses, do not fear, throw what is in your right hand and it will swallow what they forge; he threw his staff and it ate every snake of theirs; when they saw it they prostrated and said they believed in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Moses and Aaron. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 29}

Report 14945, from Ibn Ishaq through Ibn Humayd and Salama: God inspired him to throw what was in his right hand; he threw his staff and it went toward what they had thrown, which in the sight of Pharaoh and the people were snakes running, and it swallowed them one by one until nothing, little or much, of what they had thrown could be seen in the valley. Then Moses took it and it was his staff in his hand as before, and the sorcerers fell prostrate saying they believed in the Lord of the worlds, the Lord of Moses and Aaron, and that had this been sorcery they would not have been beaten. The editor notes it is part of al-Tabari's report in his History, continuing report 14940. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 30}

Report 14946, from al-Qasim ibn Abi Bazza through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya and Hisham: it became a serpent with its mouth open and swallowed their ropes and staffs; the sorcerers then fell prostrate and did not lift their heads until they had seen paradise and the fire and the reward of their people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 30}

Reports 14947 and 14948, from Mujahid: "they forge" means they lie. Report 14949, from al-Hasan through Ibrahim ibn al-Mustamir, Uthman ibn Umar and Qurra ibn Khalid: their ropes and staffs, it gulped them down in one swallow, meaning swiftly and without choking. In his footnote the editor gives the biography of Ibrahim ibn al-Mustamir and says he fears that an explanation of "forging" as lying fell out before these reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 30}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
