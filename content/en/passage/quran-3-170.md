## Summary
Verse 170 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 170 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 170 (with verse 169, pp. 384-385).** Al-Tabari treats verses 169 and 170 together under one heading (p. 384); see [[passage/quran-3-169|3:169]] for the commentary through p. 385 (reports 8204 and 8205); the commentary continues on the following pages.

**Verse 170, the clause on being glad for those who have not yet joined (pp. 395-397, reports 8226-8231).** Al-Tabari explains that the martyrs are glad for the brothers they left alive in the world, still on their path of fighting God's enemies alongside His Messenger, because they know that if these are martyred and join them they will come to the same honour from God. On the absence of fear he says: they are secure from God's punishment and certain of His pleasure, so they are safe from what they feared in the world, and they do not grieve over what they left behind of worldly means and hard living, because of the ease and nearness they have reached. He takes the particle "anna" in the sentence to mean that they rejoice for them that no fear is upon them, and says that a group of the interpreters spoke along these lines. In the editor's notes on p. 396, "khafd" is softness, breadth and plenty of life, and "zulfa" is nearness, rank and standing with God; he refers to al-Farra' (1: 247), to earlier passages whose numbers are garbled in the extracted text, and to the earlier comment on "za'ama" (p. 392, note 1). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 395} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 396}

Reports: Qatada (8226), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: they are their brothers who left them on their religion and affair, and the martyrs are glad at the honour, favour and bliss they have come to; Ibn Jurayj (8227), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: they say that our brothers will be killed as we were, join us and receive of God's honour what we received; al-Rabi' (8228), through 'Ammar from Ibn Abi Ja'far from his father, from some who had been quoted to him: these are the slain of Badr and Uhud, of whom they claimed that when God took their spirits and admitted them to Paradise, their spirits were placed in green birds that graze in it and lodge in golden lamps beneath the Throne; seeing the honour they wished their brothers after them knew their state and would hasten to it when they met in battle; God told them He would send down word to their Prophet and inform their brothers, and they rejoiced. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 396} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 397}

Ibn Ishaq (8229), through Ibn Humayd from Salama: they are pleased at the joining of those from their brothers who follow them on the course of their fighting, so that they share the reward they have, and God has removed fear and grief from them; the note says this is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 126), completing the reports ending at 8220, and that Ibn Hisham's wording is "God has removed", which is better. Ibn Zayd (8230), through Yunus from Ibn Wahb: they are their brothers among the martyrs who will be martyred after them. Al-Suddi (8231), through Muhammad from Ahmad from Asbat: the martyr is brought a book listing those of his brothers and family who will come to him, and is told that so-and-so arrives on such a day and so-and-so on another; he rejoices at their arrival as the family of an absent man rejoice at his arrival in the world. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 397}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 397}
