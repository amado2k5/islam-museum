## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "Do you hope that they will believe you", al-Tabari says the address is to the companions of Muhammad and the believers: do you expect the Jews of the Israelites to accept what your prophet brought you, which he reports from al-Rabi' and Qatada. "Fariq" is a collective like "ta'ifa", with no singular from its own word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 244}

Al-Tabari says "among them" means among the Israelites, and that the Jews of Moses' time are counted among those addressed because they were their fathers and forebears. On "who used to hear the word of God, then alter it after they had understood it", the commentators differ about who is meant: Mujahid says those who alter and conceal are their scholars; al-Suddi, that it is the Torah, which they altered; Ibn Zayd, that they made lawful unlawful and unlawful lawful according to bribes; al-Rabi', that they heard as the people of prophethood hear and then altered it; Ibn Ishaq, that it is those who asked Moses to see their Lord and were struck by lightning, and then a report from some of the learned that they asked to hear His speech, purified and fasted, went out to al-Tur and heard it, after which a group altered what they were commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 244} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 245} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 246} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 247}
In his own choice al-Tabari prefers al-Rabi's view and what Ibn Ishaq reports, that those meant heard His speech as Moses did and then altered it, since singling out the alterers as hearers would be meaningless if the Torah was meant, which the alterer and others alike had heard; and it cuts off the believers' hope for the remnant. "They alter it" means turn it from its sense to another, "while they know" that they are in the wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 247} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 248} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 249}
On "when they meet those who believe, they say: we believe", it reports of these Jews that when they met the believers they said "we believe", taking on the ways of the hypocrites, as he relates from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 249}
