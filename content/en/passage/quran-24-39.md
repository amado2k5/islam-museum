## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "And those who disbelieve, their deeds are like a mirage of a certain place, which the thirsty think of as water, until when they come to it, they find it nothing. And Allah is with them, and He will fulfill His account. And Allah is swift in reckoning." This is a parable God gave to the deeds of the disbelievers, saying: "And those who denied the oneness of their Lord and denied this Qur'an and those who brought it, like their deeds that they did are like a mirage." He says: like a mirage, and a mirage is what sticks to the earth, and that happens at midday when the heat intensifies. And 'al-al' is what is like water between the sky and the earth, and that is the beginning of the day, raising everything by midday. And His saying: 'Baqi'ah' is the plural of 'qa'a', like the neighboring plural of a genitive form, and 'al-qa'a' is what spreads and expands from the earth, and in it is a mirage. And His saying: 'The thirsty think it is water' means that the thirsty among people think the mirage is water. In the Maqta', he quotes from Ibn Abbas and Al-Rabi'.
