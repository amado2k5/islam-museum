**Commentary on verse 40 (pp. 257-262)**

Al-Tabari says this is an announcement from God to the Messenger's Companions that He is the one charged with helping His messenger against the enemies of his religion and making him prevail over them without them, whether or not they protect him, and a reminder from Him of His doing so for him when he was few in number and the enemy many; how then when he is many and the enemy few? He says to them: if you do not go out, believers, with My messenger when he calls you out, so as to help him, God is his helper and supporter against his enemy and makes him independent of you and of your help and support, as He helped him "when the unbelievers drove him out", the unbelievers in God of Quraysh, from his homeland and home, "second of two", meaning they drove him out as one of the two. The Arabs say "he is the second of two" meaning one of the two, and "third of three" and "fourth of four" meaning one of the three or four, which differs from their saying "brother of six" or "boy of seven", since the brother and the boy are not among the six and the seven. By "second of two" God meant the Messenger and Abu Bakr, who left fleeing from Quraysh when they planned to kill the Messenger and hid in the cave. His words "when the two were in the cave" mean when the Messenger and Abu Bakr were in the cave. The cave is a great hollow in a mountain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 258}

"When he said to his companion" means when God's messenger said to his companion Abu Bakr "do not grieve", which was because Abu Bakr feared the seekers would learn of their place and was distressed, and the Messenger told him not to grieve, for God was with them and was their helper, so the polytheists would not know of them and would not reach them. God says that He helped him against his enemy in this state of fear and few numbers, so how could He forsake him and make him need you, when He has multiplied his helpers and the number of his troops? Report 16725, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "if you do not help him" recalls the early part of his affair when God sent him, God saying I will do that for him and help him as I helped him then, when he was the second of two; report 16726 is similar through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. Report 16727, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: his companion was Abu Bakr, and the cave was a mountain at Mecca called Thawr. Report 16728, from Urwa, through Abd al-Warith ibn Abd al-Samad, his father, Aban al-Attar and Hisham ibn Urwa: when the Prophet and Abu Bakr left, Abu Bakr had a milch flock that went out to his family's, and he sent Amir ibn Fuhayra with the flock to Thawr, and Amir used to bring the flock to the Prophet at the cave in Thawr, the cave God named in the Qur'an; the editor says this is part of Urwa's letter to Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 259}

Report 16729, from Anas, on what Abu Bakr told them, through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Affan, Hibban, Hammam and Thabit: while I was with the Messenger in the cave, with the feet of the polytheists above our heads, I said Messenger of God, if one of them lifted his foot he would see us, and he said Abu Bakr, what do you think of two whose third is God? The editor lists those who give it, among them Ibn Sa'd, Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and al-Tirmidhi, who called it good, sound and strange. Report 16730, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki, his father, Sharik and Ibrahim ibn Muhajir: Abu Bakr stayed with the Prophet in the cave three days; report 16731, from al-Zuhri, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the mountain called Thawr, in which the Messenger and Abu Bakr stayed three nights. Report 16732, from Amr ibn al-Harith through his father, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: when Abu Bakr al-Siddiq preached he asked which of them read the sura of repentance; a man said I do, and he said read; when he reached "when he said to his companion, do not grieve" Abu Bakr wept and said "by God, I am his companion". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 260}

As for "then God sent down His tranquillity on His messenger" (it was also said on Abu Bakr) "and supported him with hosts you did not see, and made the word of those who disbelieved the lowest while the word of God is the highest, and God is mighty and wise", he sent down His calm and stillness on His messenger and strengthened him with hosts from Him, of angels you did not see; He made the word of those who disbelieved, which is the word of polytheism, the lowest, because it was overpowered, humbled, nullified by God, and its people destroyed, every one overpowered and defeated being below the victor, who is the highest; and the word of God, His religion, His oneness and the saying that there is no god but God, is the highest over polytheism and its people, prevailing. Report 16733, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the word of those who disbelieved is polytheism with God, and the word of God is that there is no god but God. The words "the word of God is the highest" are a new statement and not joined to "made the word of those who disbelieved the lowest", since if they were joined to the first "word" it would be in the accusative; the editor refers to al-Farra. "God is mighty and wise" means mighty in His vengeance on those who disbelieve in Him, whom no conqueror overcomes, no victor defeats and no helper aids against His punishment, and wise in His management of His creation and His disposing of them as He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 261}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
