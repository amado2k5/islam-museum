## Summary
Verse 40 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 40 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Zechariah's question and the barren one.** Al-Tabari says Zechariah, when the angels called to him with the glad tidings, asked how a boy could be his when he had reached such an age, for one who has reached it has no child, and his wife was barren. A barren woman is one who bears no child; the word is used of a woman and of a man, and he cites a line of Amir ibn al-Tufayl on a youth one-eyed, barren and cowardly. The editor notes the line is in Amir's diwan, Majaz al-Qur'an and the Hamasa of al-Shajari, that Amir was one of the noble one-eyed whose eye was lost at Fayf al-Rih when Musahir ibn Yazid al-Harithi speared him, receiving twenty wounds that day, quotes Alqama ibn Ulatha's remark that he had no child, and explains the line as saying who would excuse him if he feared his enemy and held back from the charge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 381}

**The sense of reaching old age.** Al-Tabari says the word for old age is the verbal noun of 'he grew old', that the verse says old age reached him though elsewhere it says he reached old age ([[passage/quran-19-8|19:8]]), because what reaches you you have reached, and the sense is that he had grown old, like saying 'hardship has reached me' meaning I am in hardship. The editor notes the printed text added an unneeded conjunction, and the correct reading is the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 381} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 382}

**Did Zechariah doubt? Al-Tabari's question and answer.** Al-Tabari raises a question: how could Zechariah, a prophet of God, say this after the angels had given him good news by God's command? Did he doubt their truthfulness, which cannot be said of believers, let alone prophets and messengers? Or was it a denial of his Lord's power, which would be graver still? He replies that it was not as supposed, and reports: 7001 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says that when he heard the angels' call with the news of John, Satan came and told him the voice was not from God but from Satan mocking him, since had it been from God He would have inspired it to him as in other matters; so at that moment he wavered and asked how a boy could come. Report 7002 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abu Bakr, from Ikrima) says Satan came wishing to cloud his Lord's favour and asked if he knew who had called him; he answered that the angels of his Lord had; Satan said no, it was Satan, and had it been from his Lord He would have hidden it as he hid his own supplication; so he asked for a sign. Al-Tabari says Zechariah's words and his turning to his Lord came from the whisper that mingled with his heart, making him imagine the call was from other than angels, so he asked for confirmation, that it might be settled in him by a sign God would show that it was glad tidings from God on the tongues of His angels. He adds that it is also possible he was asking from which side the promised child would come, from his wife, who was barren, or from another woman, in which case it would not be as Ikrima, al-Suddi and those who spoke like them said. These are reports and theological questions given with their chains, and al-Tabari offers two possibilities; the museum gives no verdict. The editor explains 'he wavered on the spot' as at that moment, and notes differences between the printed text and manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 382} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 383}

**The sense of 'thus', and creating a child.** Al-Tabari says the sense is that God is as He described Himself: it is easy for Him to create a child from the aged man who despaired of a child and from a barren woman from whom birth is not hoped, as He created Zechariah before, when he was nothing, for nothing He wills is hard for Him and nothing He wishes is barred to Him, since His power is unlike any power. Report 7003 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says that God had created him before when he was nothing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 383} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 384}
