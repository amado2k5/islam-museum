## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "say: if the abode of the Hereafter with God is yours alone, to the exclusion of others, then wish for death, if you are truthful", al-Tabari says this verse is among those by which God argued for His prophet against the Jews around his place of emigration and exposed their rabbis, for He told him to call them to a fair settlement, as he called the Christians to mutual imprecation over Jesus; if they were in the right, wishing for death could not harm them, since they would go to rest and to God's neighbourhood, and if they did not wish it, people would know they were in the wrong. They refused, knowing that if they wished for death they would perish and lose their world. He reports from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet, that had the Jews wished for death they would have died and seen their seats in the Fire, and that had those who were to imprecate gone out, they would have come back finding neither family nor wealth (the editor judges the chain sound and refers to Ahmad's Musnad), and from Ibn Abbas also that had they wished it, no Jew would have remained on earth. He notes that they had said "we are God's children and His beloved" and "none will enter Paradise except Jews or Christians", so their refusal showed their claim false. On the reason for the command there are two views: that it was a prayer for death upon the falser of the two parties, from Ibn Abbas, or that it was to show their claim false, from Qatada, Abu al-'Aliya and al-Rabi'. "Khalisa" means pure, that is, belonging to them alone; Ibn Abbas is reported to read it as "khassa", particular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 361} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 362} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 363} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 364} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 365}
