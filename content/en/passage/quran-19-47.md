## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 47 to 48 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "He said, 'Peace be upon you, I will ask your forgiveness for you, my Lord; indeed, He was with me barefoot.'" Abraham said to his father when he threatened him with his advice and his prayer to God with bad words and punishment: Peace be upon you, my father. He said: It is safe from me for you to return to what you disliked, and for your prayer to me for what you promised me punishment. But I will ask my Lord to forgive you, he says: But I will ask my Lord to cover your sins by forgiving you from your punishment for them. "Indeed, he was barefoot with me." He says: My Lord has entrusted me with gentleness; he answers my prayers when I call upon him, and it is said of him: So-and-so has been blessed with me. I have explained this with previous evidences, which would make it necessary to repeat it here. And similar to what we have said about this, the scholars of interpretation said. In the passage, statements from Ibn Abbas, Ibn Zayd, and Ibrahim are also quoted.
