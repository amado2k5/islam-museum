**Commentary on verse 183 (pp. 287-288)**

Al-Tabari says: "and I grant them respite" means I put off those who denied Our signs; the root of "imla" is the saying "a long while of time passed over him", the word meaning a span, from which one says "I waited for you a long while", so that by their disobedience to their Lord they reach the measure of punishment and torment He has written for them, then He takes them to Himself. "My plan is firm": "kayd" is scheming, and "firm" means strong and severe. He cites a line describing a hard, lasting journey that does not break off; the editor says he did not know its author, found no sense in the first half of the line, and that it describes she-camels or horses, explaining the words for the mixed gaits, the fiery run and the gallop. He refers to the earlier explanations of "respite" and "plan", and says the bracketed words are supplied from Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an and that the passage is disjointed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 287} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 288}
