## Summary
Verse 44 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 44 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 44: "We sent down the Torah, with guidance and light, by which the prophets who submitted judged for those who were Jews", p. 338**

Al-Tabari says: We sent down the Torah with clarity on what these Jews asked you of the ruling on the two married adulterers, "and light", that is, with the clearing of what was dark to them and the illumination of the uncertain in the ruling, "by which the prophets who submitted judged", that is, they judge by the Torah's ruling in what they brought to the Prophet about the two adulterers; "the prophets who submitted" being those who bowed to God's ruling and confessed it. God means by this our Prophet Muhammad in his ruling of stoning on the married Jewish adulterers and in making equal the blood of the slain of al-Nadir and Qurayza in retaliation and blood-money, and the prophets before Muhammad judging by God's ruling in it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 338}

Report 12006, from al-Suddi, says it means the Prophet. Report 12007, from Qatada, says it was told that when this verse was revealed the Prophet used to say: we judge over the Jews and others of the religions. Report 12008, from al-Zuhri, from a man of Muzayna in Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab's company, begins.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 338}

Report 12008, from Abu Hurayra, says a Jewish man and woman committed adultery, and some said to others: let us go to this prophet, who was sent with leniency; if he gives a ruling short of stoning we will accept it and cite it with God as a prophet's ruling. They came as he sat in the mosque and asked what he said about a man and woman of theirs who committed adultery; he said nothing to them until he reached their house of study, stood at the door and said: I ask you by God who revealed the Torah to Moses, what do you find in the Torah for one who commits adultery when married? They said he is blackened, paraded and lashed, the parading being that the two are placed on a donkey back to back and led round, and a young man of them kept silent; he pressed the oath on him, and he said: since you ask us, we find stoning in the Torah. He asked what was the first thing they relaxed, and he said a kinsman of one of our kings committed adultery and stoning was put off for him; then a man of the common people did, and he wished to stone him, but his people stood between and said: you will not stone our man until you bring yours and stone him; so they settled on this penalty. The Prophet said: I rule by what is in the Torah, and ordered them stoned. Al-Zuhri said it reached them that this verse was revealed about them and the Prophet was among those. The editor discusses the reading of the phrase "among the common people" and prefers the sense that he was of the ordinary people.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 339}

Report 12009, from 'Ikrima, says "the prophets who submitted" are the Prophet and those before him, judging by the truth in it; report 12010, from al-Hasan, says it is the Prophet, "for those who were Jews", so judge among them and do not fear them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 340}

**Commentary on verse 44, continued: "and the rabbis and the scholars, by what they were entrusted of God's book, and were witnesses to it", pp. 341-344**

Al-Tabari says: and judging by the Torah and its rulings which God sent down in it, in every age as commanded in it, along with the prophets who submitted, are the rabbis and the scholars. The rabbis, plural of "rabbani", are the learned, wise and discerning in running people's affairs and attending to their interests; the scholars are the learned, plural of "habr", the one who has mastered something, from which Ka'b is called Ka'b al-Ahbar; and al-Farra' said the Arabs most often say "hibr", with a kasra on the first letter, for the singular.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 341}

Some interpreters said the rabbis and scholars here were the two sons of Suriya who confessed to the Prophet God's ruling in the Torah on the married adulterers. Report 12011, from al-Suddi, says they were two Jewish brothers who followed the Prophet without converting, and promised him that they would tell him whatever he asked of the Torah; one was a rabbi and the other a scholar; he asked them and they told him what had happened when the noble and the poor man had committed adultery and how they changed it. Al-Tabari chooses that God reported that the Torah is judged by the submitting prophets for the Jews, and by His rabbis and scholars; it may be that the two sons of Suriya and others are meant, but the apparent text takes in the submitting prophets and every rabbi and scholar, and nothing in the text or in any proof shows that a particular group is meant.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 342}

Reports 12012 to 12017, from al-Dahhak, al-Hasan, Mujahid, Qatada, 'Ikrima and Ibn Zayd, say the rabbis and scholars are the readers, jurists and learned; Mujahid says the rabbis are above the scholars, and Ibn Zayd says the rabbis are the rulers and the scholars the learned. "By what they were entrusted of God's book" means the submitting prophets judge by the Torah's ruling and the rabbis and scholars by the knowledge of God's book, the Torah, entrusted to them, the preposition belonging to "scholars". "And were witnesses to it" means the rabbis and scholars judge by the Torah with the prophets, and were witnesses to the prophets' judgement that they ruled over them by God's book.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 343}

Report 12018, from Ibn 'Abbas, says "witnesses" means the rabbis and scholars are witnesses for Muhammad to what he said, that it is the truth that came from God.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 344}

**Commentary on verse 44, continued: "so do not fear people but fear Me, and do not sell My verses for a small price", pp. 344-345**

Al-Tabari says God tells the Jewish scholars and rabbis: do not fear people in carrying out My ruling that I made on My servants, for they cannot harm or benefit you except by My leave, and do not hide the stoning I made the ruling in the Torah for married adulterers; fear Me before anyone of My creatures and fear My punishment for hiding what you were entrusted of My book. Report 12019, from al-Suddi, says so. "Do not sell My verses for a small price" means do not take for leaving the ruling by the verses of My book sent to Moses a vile return, which is the small price.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 344}

He meant to forbid them taking unlawful gain for distorting God's book and changing its ruling on the married adulterers and other rulings they altered for bribes. Report 12020, from Ibn Zayd, says do not eat unlawful gain on My book or take a bribe for it, and report 12021, from al-Suddi, says do not take a little for hiding what I sent down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 345}

**Commentary on verse 44, continued: "whoever does not judge by what God sent down, those are the unbelievers", p. 345**

Al-Tabari says: whoever hides God's ruling that He sent down in His book and made a judgement among His servants, hides it and judges by another, like the Jews' ruling for the married adulterers by parading and blackening and hiding stoning, and like their ruling for some of their slain with full blood-money and for others half, retaliation for the nobles and blood-money for the lowly, though God made them all equal in the Torah, those are the unbelievers.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 345}

We stop here at the foot of p. 345, at the start of al-Tabari's commentary on "those are the unbelievers"; the rest of verse 44 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
