## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "yet here you are, killing one another and driving a party of you from their homes, aiding against them in sin and enmity", al-Tabari gives two readings: either "then you, O these", with "ya" dropped as in "Yusuf, turn away from this", or "you" as subject with "these" placed between it and its predicate. "Tazahur" is mutual aid, since each leans his back on another's. He mentions the Basran view that "ha'ula'" is an alert and emphasis. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 303} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 304}

On whom this verse concerns, al-Tabari reports from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Ishaq, that the Banu Qaynuqa' were allies of the Khazraj and the Banu al-Nadir and Qurayza allies of the Aws; when war broke out between the Aws and Khazraj, each side fought with its allies against its own brethren until they shed each other's blood, though they held the Torah forbidding it, and when the war ended they ransomed their captives in confirmation of the Torah; the verse was sent down about this, the editor noting that the last sentence is Ibn Ishaq's own words. Al-Suddi has a close account that names the war of Sumayr, saying they fought and then ransomed, and when the Arabs taunted them they replied that they were commanded to ransom and forbidden to fight, yet were ashamed to see their allies humbled. Ibn Zayd says Qurayza and al-Nadir were brothers who split, and Abu al-'Aliya that when they found a people weak they drove them from their homes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 305} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 306} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 307}
"'Udwan" is going beyond the limit through wrong and aggression, and readers differ on "tazahurun" between a lightened and a doubled "za", with the same meaning, so he prefers neither. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 307} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 308}
On "if they come to you as captives, you ransom them, though their expulsion is forbidden to you: do you then believe in part of the Book and disbelieve in part?", al-Tabari says God rebukes them for killing one another and then ransoming the captive, though killing and expelling are as forbidden as leaving captives with the enemy, so how could they allow one and refuse the other, and disbelieving in part of the Book breaks the covenant. He reports from Qatada that ransoming them was belief and expelling them unbelief, and similar from Ibn Abbas and Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 308} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 309}
