## Summary
Verse 83 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 83 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the opening of the verse (p. 568, notes 2-3).** Al-Tabari says the pronoun for "them" refers to the group that plots by night against what the Messenger says, and that the sense is: when word reaches them about a raiding party of the Muslims, that it has been made safe from its enemy by defeating it, or word of fear from the enemy because of harm done to the Muslims, they spread and publicize it among the people before the Messenger knows of it and before the commanders of the detachments arrive. The pronoun in the verb phrase returns to the "matter". The editor notes a muddled manuscript reading in the words for "the commanders of its detachments" and says he preferred his own reading. Al-Tabari illustrates the verb with a line by Abu al-Aswad al-Du'ali about news spread among people until it was like a fire on a height; the editor traces the line to the poet's divan, al-Aghani and Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 568}

**The story behind the lines (p. 569, note 3).** The editor says the lines were composed when Abu al-Aswad proposed to a woman of the Abd al-Qays named Asma, daughter of Ziyad: he confided the matter to a friend, the friend told a cousin of hers who was also seeking her, the cousin went to her family and asked them to prevent the match, they did so and pressed her until she married her cousin. The lines (described, not reproduced) speak of the friend who was trusted with the secret and did not keep it. The editor explains the word for "that which kindles a fire". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 569}

**Reports 9990-9992 (p. 569, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters spoke along these lines. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9990, from Yazid b. Zuray', from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that they hastened to it and spread it. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9991, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that when word reached them that they were made safe from their enemy, or that they were afraid of them, they spread the talk until it reached the enemy. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9992, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) reports from Ibn Abbas that they spread it and informed against people with it. The editor says the print has a different verb and the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 569}

**Reports 9993-9995 (p. 570, notes 1-4).** Al-Qasim (9993, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) reports from Ibn Jurayj that this concerns news: when a Muslim detachment went out, people asked one another about it and said the Muslims had received such and such from their enemy and the enemy such and such from them, and spread it among themselves without the Prophet being the one who told them; Ibn Jurayj also reports from Ibn Abbas that the verb means they proclaimed and spread it. Yunus (9994, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that it means they published it, and that those who did so were people who were either hypocrites or others of weak faith. A report (9995, al-Tabari relates it on the authority of al-Husayn b. al-Faraj) has Abu Mu'adh say they spread it and informed on people, and they are the people of hypocrisy. The editor says the print altered the manuscript in the first three places because it did not understand the text, that a certain verb means "to ask about the news", and that in the fourth place he kept what was said on p. 569. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 570}

**The second clause of the verse (pp. 570-571, notes 5, 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: if they had referred the matter that reached them about their enemy [and the Muslims] to the Messenger and to their commanders, and stayed silent without spreading it until the Messenger or those in command were the ones to speak about it, after its truth or falsity was established with them, confirming it if true and voiding it if false, then those who investigate and extract it, meaning those in command, would know the truth of the report. The editor says the bracketed words "and the Muslims" stand so in both manuscript and print, that he does not know what they are, that he fears something fell out, and that without them the sentence runs correctly; that the phrase for "they report the news" in both is a sick passage corrected by him as a copyist's slip; and that the print misread the unpointed manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 571}

**Meaning of extraction and the poetry (p. 571, notes 3-5).** Al-Tabari says anyone who brings out something hidden from the eyes or from the minds of hearts is its "extractor"; one says a well is extracted when its water is brought out, and the extracted water of the earth is called by a particular noun. He cites a line by a poet (the editor attributes it to Ka'b b. Sa'd al-Ghanawi or Ghuraiqa b. Musafi' al-Absi and refers to al-Asma'iyyat) describing a generous man near moist earth whose enemy can reach none of his water because of his might and protection, frowning in the face of evil and not staying in disgrace; the line is described, not reproduced. The editor glosses the word for a dug well, for moist earth and for frowning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 571}

**Reports 9996-10000 (p. 572, notes 1-3).** Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9996, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the sense is: had they stayed silent and referred the talk to the Prophet and their commanders until he spoke of it, those who search out news would know it. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9997, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that it means their scholars, those who examine it and care about it. Al-Qasim (9998, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) reports from Ibn Jurayj that "those in command" means those of understanding in religion and intellect; and (9999, same chain, from Hajjaj, from Abu Ja'far, from al-Rabi', from Abu al-Aliya) that it means people of knowledge, and that those who extract are those who follow it up and probe for it. Abu Kurayb (10000, from Ibn Idris, from Layth) reports from Mujahid that they are those who ask about it and probe for it. The editor says the manuscript has a slip in a verb in report 9997, and that both the print's added word in 9998 and the manuscript reading are right in the early commentators' manner of abbreviating, and likewise the manuscript reading in 9999. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 572}

**Reports 10001-10006 (pp. 572-573, note 1).** Muhammad b. Amr (10001, from Abu Asim, from Isa) reports from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid, that it means their asking "what was it?" and "what did you hear?". Al-Muthanna (10002, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) reports the same from Mujahid. Ibn Waki' (10003, from his father, from Abu Ja'far, from al-Rabi') reports from Abu al-Aliya that they probe for it. Muhammad b. Sa'd (10004, by the usual chain back to Ibn Abbas) reports from Ibn Abbas that they are those who probe for it. In report 10005 (al-Tabari relates it on the authority of al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) al-Dahhak says they follow it up. Yunus (10006, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that those in command are the governors who command them in war, who consider whether the news that came is true or false, whether it is vain and they void it or right and they confirm it; he says this concerns war, and that had they done otherwise they would have referred it to God, the Messenger and those in command. The editor says the print changed a phrase in the manuscript because it could not read it well. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 573}

**Commentary on the last part of the verse and the question of the exempted few (p. 574, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: had God not bestowed His grace, success and mercy on you, believers, rescuing you from what afflicted these hypocrites who tell the Messenger, when he commands them, that they will obey and then a group of them plots otherwise, you would have been like them and gone after Satan, all but a handful, as these did; and that the address is to those addressed at [[passage/quran-4-71|4:71]]. He then records the interpreters' disagreement about who the few are and from what they are excepted. Some said they are the extractors among those in command, the exception being from the knowing, so that the few would not know by extraction what others know; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and says the exception there is from the extraction, not from the spreading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 574}

**Reports 10007-10010 (pp. 574-575).** Bishr b. Mu'adh (10007, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that the sentence in truth runs: those who extract it would know it, all but a handful of them, and then the clause on grace, mercy and Satan is a separate sentence. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (10008, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar) reports from Qatada that it means all of you would have gone after Satan, and that the exception is like the exception in the knowing clause. Al-Muthanna (10009, from Suwayd b. Nasr, from Ibn al-Mubarak by recitation, from Sa'id) reports the same from Qatada. Al-Qasim (10010, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) reports from Ibn Jurayj like Qatada's view, adding that he said they would have known it all but a handful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 575}

**Second view (p. 575).** Others said the few are the group God described as telling the Messenger "obedience" and then plotting by night otherwise, so that the sense is: when word of reassurance or alarm came to them they spread it, all but a handful of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 575}

**Reports 10011-10012 (pp. 575-576, note 1).** Al-Muthanna (10011, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that the exception in the first part of the verse concerns the hypocrites' news and the few are the believers, and he compares the passage in the Kahf sura ([[passage/quran-18-1|18:1]]) about there being no crookedness in the Book, which involves a transposition. The editor says the print's text of this report is taken from al-Durr al-Manthur and is defective, that the manuscript agrees with his text except at the end, that he put the mention of the Kahf passage in brackets, and that he thinks his preferred reading is right. Yunus (10012, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the verse has a transposition, the sense being: they spread it, all but a handful of them, and had it not been for God's grace and mercy neither few nor many would have been saved. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 576}

**Third view (p. 576).** Others said the exception is from following Satan: the few are people who had not intended what the others intended, so God made those He rescued know the place of His favor and excepted the others who did not do what the rest did. Al-Tabari relates (10013, on the authority of al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) that al-Dahhak b. Muzahim said they were the companions of the Prophet, who had told themselves some of Satan's affairs, except a group of them. This is given as transmitted; the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 576}

**Start of the fourth view (p. 576).** Others said the sense is that, were it not for God's grace and mercy, all of them would have gone after Satan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 576}

**End of the fourth view (p. 577, notes 1-2).** Al-Tabari reports that the holders of this view said the exception is exceptional in wording only and serves as an indicator of totality and inclusion, so that, had it not been for God's grace and mercy toward them, no one would have escaped error. They cited a line by al-Tirimmah b. Hakim in praise of Yazid b. al-Muhallab that calls him one of few faults; they said it seems to describe the man as having faults, yet it is known to mean that he has none, since to describe a man as having few faults is a criticism, not praise; likewise in the verse the sense is that all of them would have followed Satan. (The line is described, not reproduced.) The editor refers to what al-Tabari said earlier on the sense of "few" (the page numbers in the print's cross-reference are garbled and are not given) and to what he wrote in volume 1; he traces the line to al-Tirimmah's divan, p. 139, glosses the words for a lofty man, for faults that wound and for faults that damage lineage and character (named after the worm that eats teeth or trees), and notes that the manuscript has a meaningless reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 577}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 577).** Abu Ja'far al-Tabari says the soundest of these views in his opinion is that of those who made the exception from the spreading, so that the sense is: when word of reassurance or alarm reached them they spread it, except a few, though they would have referred it to the Messenger. He reasons that the matter must fall under one of the views he listed, and that the exception cannot be from following Satan, since one whom God has favored with His grace and mercy cannot be among Satan's followers. This is al-Tabari's choice; the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 577}

**His argument against the other views (p. 578, notes 1-5).** Al-Tabari says it is not permissible to carry the meanings of God's book on other than the predominant sense apparent in Arab speech, and that a way exists to carry it on the predominant sense, so there is no need to treat the exception as an indicator of inclusion of all, which also departs from the interpreters' interpretation. Nor can the exception be from the clause on knowing: once the matter is referred to the Messenger and those in command and they have made it plain, every extractor is equal in knowing its reality, so there is no ground for excepting some. Since these three views suffer these flaws, the sound one is "the fourth", the one he had judged correct, the exception from the spreading. (The numbering as printed does not match the order of the views as given here, and the editor does not remark on it.) The editor says the print made the opening of one sentence a fresh start, which is a corruption, the manuscript is right, and that because of that error it added a phrase meaning "no basis for it", which he removed; that "every extractor of reality" stands so in print and manuscript but the context requires his reading; that a particular "so" is meaningless and his reading is right; and he refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 578}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 578}
