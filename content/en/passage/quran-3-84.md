## Summary
Verse 84 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 84 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 84: faith in the earlier prophets and scriptures.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: do you seek other than the religion of God, People of the Jews? If they seek other than God's religion, Muhammad, say to them that we have faith in God, the conditional clause being left out because what is apparent in the speech indicates it. The editor says that the printed text has 'and' for 'or' and that the manuscript's reading is correct. The words 'say, we have faith in God' mean: say to them, Muhammad, we confirm God, that He is our lord and our God, there is no god but He and we worship none other; and we confirm also what was sent down on us of His revelation and message, and acknowledge it; and what was sent down on Abraham, the friend of God, and on his two sons Ishmael and Isaac, and the son of his son Jacob; and what was sent down on the Asbat, who are the twelve sons of Jacob, whose names he has set out before, the editor referring to the place. He says that what was given to Moses and Jesus, of which God ordered Muhammad to confirm them and to believe in it, is the Torah that God gave Moses and the Gospel He gave Jesus, and he confirms also what was sent down on the prophets from Him.

He explains the statement that they set none of them apart from the rest as meaning that they do not confirm some and deny others, nor believe in some and disbelieve in others, as the Jews and the Christians disbelieved in some of God's prophets and confirmed others, but they believe in all of them and confirm them. He explains the statement that they submit to Him in Islam as meaning that they adhere to Islam for God and no other religion, rather disavowing before Him every religion beside it and every community other than it, yielding to Him in obedience, humble in servitude, acknowledging His divinity and lordship and that there is no god but He. He says he gave the report of this sense earlier and disliked repeating it; the editor refers to pp. 109-111 of volume 3 for the parallel verse and to the language indexes, and keeps 'ubuda' as in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 570}
