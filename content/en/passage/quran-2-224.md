## Summary
Verse 224 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 224 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reports the interpreters' differences over the prohibition on making God an object of exposure for oaths in this verse ([[passage/quran-2-224|2:224]]). Some said it means do not make Him a pretext for your oaths: when one of you is asked for something good or for reconciling people, he says he has sworn not to, using the oath to excuse leaving the good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 419} From Tawus: a man swears about a thing not fit to do and then pleads his oath, whereas it is better for him to expiate the oath and do what is better (4351, 4352). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 420} From Ibn 'Abbas through al-Suddi: a man swears not to speak to his kin or give charity, or swears not to reconcile two who are estranged and says he has sworn; he should expiate his oath (4353). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 420} From Qatada: do not make God your pretext by saying you have sworn not to keep kinship, work for good or give alms, for the Qur'an came to leave the Devil's command, so do not carry out any command of his in your vows or oaths (4354). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 420} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr (4355), and from 'Ata': a person swears not to do good, and he should do it and expiate his oath (4356). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 420} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 421} From al-Dahhak: a man forbids himself what God made lawful to him and says he has sworn; God ordered them to expiate their oaths and come to what is lawful (4357); the note corrects its chain from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 421} From al-Suddi: the word for exposure means a matter arises between you and a man and you swear not to speak to him; the word for "do good" means swearing not to be good to a relative; and the word for "make peace" means swearing not to reconcile two people; and he adds that this was before the expiations came down (4358); the editor refers to al-Tabari's later discussion of this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 421} From Ibrahim al-Nakha'i: he swears not to fear God, not to keep kinship and not to reconcile two people, and his oath must not stop him (4359). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 421}

Others said it means: do not interpose the oath by God in your talk among yourselves and make it an argument for leaving the good. From Ibn 'Abbas: do not make your oath a barrier against doing good, but expiate your oath (4360, 4361). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 422} From Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (4362, 4363, 4364) and Sa'id ibn Jubayr (4364), likewise; in al-Nakha'i's report: do not swear not to fear God, nor not to do good, nor not to keep kinship, nor not to reconcile people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 422} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 423} From Mujahid: they were commanded to keep kinship, to do what is right and to reconcile people, and whoever swears not to do so should do it and leave his oath (4365); from al-Rabi' (4366); from 'A'isha: do not swear by God even if you keep your oath (4367). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 423} From Ibn Jurayj, who was told that the verse came down about Abu Bakr over Mistah (4368), and from Ibrahim (4369). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 423} The note records that the editor compared the two forms of the chain of 4365 in the manuscript and the printed text and preferred the printed one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 423}

From Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (4370) and from Makhul: a man swears not to do good, keep kinship or reconcile people, and God forbade it (4371). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 424} The editor says 4370 is not in the manuscript at this place and repeats 4359, and that he kept it to show the difference between copies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 424} Al-Tabari's choice: the better of the two interpretations is that of those who said the sense is, do not make the oath by God an argument for not doing good between yourselves and God and between you and people, because in Arabic the word for "exposure" means strength and force, and one says "this matter is a strength for you", meaning strength against your affairs; he cites a line of Ka'b ibn Zuhayr describing she-camels, whose words the editor explains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 424} So the sense for him is: do not make God a strength for your oaths against doing good, keeping fear of God and reconciling people; whoever swears and then sees something better than what he swore should break the oath, do the good, fear God, reconcile people and expiate his oath. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 425} He notes that the negative is omitted from the wording because the sense shows it, and illustrates with a line of Imru' al-Qays. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 425} On the word for "do good", some said it is all good deeds and others that it is kindness to kin; al-Tabari prefers the first, because the term is general and kindness to kin is one of its meanings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 425} The word for "fear" means to be wary of one's Lord and His punishment in His obligations and limits; there is another report from Ibn 'Abbas on the verse's sense (4372). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 426} To "make peace between people" is to reconcile them in what is right, in what contains no sin and what God loves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 426} Al-Tabari rejects al-Suddi's statement that the verse came down before the expiations for oaths, since nothing in the Book or the Sunna shows it, and an account of what happened is only established by a truthful report; the verse may well have come down after the expiations were set out in Surat al-Ma'ida ([[passage/quran-5-89|5:89]]), the mention there being enough. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 426} This is al-Tabari's own choice.

On the close of the verse, al-Tabari says God hears what the one who swears says and knows what he intends by his oath, good or otherwise, and that this is a warning and threat to fear what He forbade, whether outwardly or inwardly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 427}
