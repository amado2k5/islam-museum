## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "when his Lord said to him: submit, he said: I have submitted to the Lord of the worlds" al-Tabari says: when his Lord said to him, be sincere to Me in worship and humble yourself to Me in obedience, and Abraham answered his Lord, I have bowed in obedience and been sincere in worship to the Lord and Director of all creatures and no other; he refers to the earlier explanation of "islam". He asks what "when" is attached to, and says it is a link to "We chose him in this world", so the sense is: We chose him in this world when We said to him, submit, and he said, I have submitted to the Lord of the worlds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 92}

Al-Tabari adds that the wording "to the Lord of the worlds" comes as a report about an absent party after it had come as a report about himself, as Khufaf ibn Nudba said, "look at Khufaf, I am that one". He asks whether God called Abraham to Islam, and says yes, when he said "my people, I am free of what you associate; I have turned my face to Him who created the heavens and the earth, upright", which was the time his Lord said to him "submit", after having tried him with the stars, the moon and the sun. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 92} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 93}
