## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "mankind was one community; then God sent the prophets as bearers of good news and warners, and sent down with them the Book in truth to judge between people in what they differed over": the interpreters differed over the meaning of "umma" here and over who the "people" are whom God so described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 275} Some said they were those between Adam and Noah, ten generations all on a law of truth, who differed afterwards; from Ibn 'Abbas: between Noah and Adam were ten generations all on a law of truth; they differed and God sent the prophets, and so it is in the reading of 'Abdallah, "mankind was one community, then they differed" (4048), the editor adding that al-Hakim narrated it in the Mustadrak and graded it sound on the condition of al-Bukhari, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; from Qatada: they were all on guidance and then differed, so God sent the prophets, and the first prophet sent was Noah (4049). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 275} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 276} Al-Tabari says "umma" on this view means religion, as in al-Nabigha's line "can one who has a religion be trusted while obedient", meaning one of religion; the root sense of "umma" is a group gathered on one religion, and the report about the umma suffices for a report about the religion, as in "had God willed, He would have made you one community" (5:48); so Ibn 'Abbas directed the verse to people having been of one religion until they differed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 276}

Others said Adam was on the truth, an imam to his descendants, and God sent the prophets among his children; they took "umma" as obedience to God and calling to His oneness, as in "Abraham was an umma, devoted to God, upright" (16:120), meaning a leader in good who is followed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 276} From Mujahid: "mankind was one community", he said, Adam (4050, 4051), and in one report: between Adam and Noah there were ten prophets, and God sent the prophets as bearers of good news and warners; Mujahid said Adam was a community by himself (4052). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 277} Al-Tabari remarks that whoever said this allowed naming one person by the name of a group for the gathering in him of the good qualities found in a scattered group, as one says "so-and-so is a community by himself", or because he was the cause of gathering on his religion those of his descendants who gathered on it until they differed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 277}

Others said mankind was one community on one religion on the day God brought Adam's descendants out of his loins and presented them to him; from Ubayy ibn Ka'b: they were one community when they were presented to Adam, when He created them on Islam and they acknowledged servanthood to Him, then they differed after Adam; Ubayy read "mankind was one community, then they differed, then God sent the prophets", and God sent messengers and revealed books only at the time of difference (4053); from Ibn Zayd: when He brought them out of Adam's back, they were never one community other than on that day, and "God sent the prophets" is when the communities split up (4054). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 277} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 278} Al-Tabari says the sense of the verse on this view is like Ibn 'Abbas's, except that the time when people were one community differs from the time Ibn 'Abbas gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 278}

Others said "mankind was one community" means they were on one religion, then God sent the prophets, from Ibn 'Abbas (4055). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 278}

Al-Tabari says the soundest interpretation is that God told His servants that people were one community on one religion and one creed. From al-Suddi: one religion, the religion of Adam; they differed and God sent the prophets as bearers of good news and warners (4056). The religion they were on was the true religion, as Ubayy ibn Ka'b said, and al-Suddi reports that in the reading of Ibn Mas'ud it is "they differed from it", that is from Islam (4057); they differed in their religion, so at their difference God sent the prophets as bearers of good news and warners and sent down the Book with them to judge between people in what they differed over, as mercy to His creatures and as an excuse before them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 278} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 279} He allows that the time they were one community may have been from Adam to Noah, as 'Ikrima reported from Ibn 'Abbas and as Qatada said, or when Adam was shown his offspring, or another time, and there is no indication in God's Book or an established report as to which, so nothing may be said beyond what God said, that people were one community and when they differed God sent among them prophets and messengers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 279}

Al-Tabari says not knowing the time does us no harm, as knowing it would not benefit us unless knowledge of it were obedience to God; the editor comments that this is the argument of a pious, scrupulous and discerning man who knows where minds slip. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 279} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 280} But whichever it was, the Qur'an's evidence is clear that those God said were one community were one on faith and the religion of truth, not on disbelief and polytheism, since in Surat Yunus He said "mankind was but one community, then they differed" (10:19) and threatened over the difference, not the unity; had their union before the difference been on disbelief and some then moved to belief, promise rather than threat would better suit His wisdom, and it is absurd that He would threaten at the point of repentance and not at the point of all being united on disbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 280} "Bearers of good news and warners" means messengers who give good news of ample reward to the obedient and warn the disobedient of severe punishment and everlasting stay in the Fire; "to judge between people" means that the Book, the Torah, judges, and he ascribes judging to the Book because any prophet who judges does so by what the Book directs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 280}

On "none differed over it except those who were given it, after the clear proofs came to them, through envy among themselves": by "it" he means the Book God revealed, the Torah; by "those who were given it" the Jews of the Children of Israel; by "the clear proofs" God's proofs that the Book is from Him and is the truth over which they may not differ. He reports that they opposed the Book and differed over it knowingly and deliberately, and that this was "baghy" among themselves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 281} "Baghy" is the verbal noun of "baghā so-and-so against so-and-so", when he transgresses and exceeds his limit; it is also said of a wound that festers, of the sea when it overflows, and of a cloud falling on land that then becomes fertile, all meaning excess and exceeding the limit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 281} So the meaning for him is that their difference was not from ignorance of the Book but, after the proof was established against them, out of envy among themselves, seeking leadership and humbling one another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 281} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 282} From al-Rabi': he returned to the Children of Israel, "except those who were given the Book and knowledge, after the proofs came to them, through envy among themselves", envy over this world, seeking its dominion, glitter and ornament, and which of them would have kingship and awe among people; so they transgressed against one another and struck one another's necks (4058). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 282}

The grammarians then differed on "min" in "after the proofs came to them". Some said it belongs to those given the Book and what follows is its relative clause, the meaning being "none differed over it but those given it, through envy among themselves, after the proofs came to them"; another objected, since "baghy" is a verbal noun and a clause cannot precede its verbal noun, saying "those who" is excepted and "after the proofs came" is excepted by a further exception, as if repeating for emphasis. Al-Tabari holds the second closer to the verse, because they differed only after the proof was established and only out of envy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 282}

On "so God guided those who believed to the truth about which they differed, by His leave; and God guides whom He wills to a straight path": "God guided" means He gave success to those who believed in God and His messenger Muhammad, accepting him and what he brought, when those given the Book differed over it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 283} Here comes the report of Abu Hurayra: "We are the last, the first; yet they were given the Book before us and we after them, and this is the day they differed over, so God guided us to it; tomorrow for the Jews and the day after for the Christians", about earlier communities straying from Friday, which was prescribed for them as for us, and making it Saturday (4059); the editor says this is a sound, well-known report from Abu Hurayra, established from him by several routes and narrated by the two Shaykhs and others, and that 'Iyad ibn Dinar al-Laythi is a reliable Successor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 283} From Abu Hurayra: we are the last and the first on the Day of Resurrection, the first to enter Paradise, though they were given the Book before us and we after them, and God guided us to what they differed over of the truth by His leave (4060). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 283} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 284}

From Ibn Zayd, on things they differed over: He guided them to Islam; they differed in prayer, some praying to the east and some to Jerusalem, and He guided us to the qibla; in fasting, some fasting part of a day and some part of a night; in Friday, the Jews taking Saturday and the Christians Sunday; in Abraham, the Jews saying he was Jewish and the Christians saying he was Christian, and God cleared him and made him a hanif and a Muslim; and in Jesus, the Jews making him a slander and the Christians a lord, and God guided us to the truth about him (4061). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 284} The editor separates al-Tabari's own words from the report. Al-Tabari says God's guidance of those who believed in Muhammad and what he brought, in what these factions of the Children of Israel given the Book differed over, was to enable them to hit upon what those before the differing had been on when they were one community, the religion of Abraham the upright Muslim, and thereby they became a middle community, to be witnesses over people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 284} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 285} From al-Rabi': God guided them at the time of difference in that they stayed on what the messengers brought before the difference, on sincerity to God alone and worship without partner, establishing prayer and paying zakat, and kept away from the differing, so they would be witnesses over people on the Day of Resurrection, over the people of Noah, Hud, Salih, Shu'ayb and Pharaoh's family that their messengers had conveyed and they had denied; in Ubayy's reading it is "that they may be witnesses over people on the Day of Resurrection", and Abu al-'Aliya used to say that in this verse is the way out of doubts, errors and trials (4062). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 285} From al-Suddi: the disbelievers differed over it and God guided the believers to the truth in it; in Ibn Mas'ud's reading it is "about what they differed from", that is from Islam (4063). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 285}

"By His leave" means by His knowledge of what He guided them to; he has explained elsewhere the sense of "idhn" when it means knowledge. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 286} "And God guides whom He wills to a straight path" means He directs whom He wills of His creatures and guides them to the right way on the truth in which there is no crookedness, as He guided those who believed in Muhammad when those given the Book differed. Al-Tabari says this verse is clear evidence of the soundness of what the people of truth said, that every favour to servants in religion or worldly life is from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 286} He raises a question: what is the meaning of "God guided those who believed to what they differed over"? Did He guide them to the truth or to the difference? He answers that the meaning is that God guided the believers to the truth in what those given God's Book differed over, some disbelieving by altering it and some holding to the truth in it, namely the people of the Torah who altered it, so God guided to the truth against what they altered and distorted those who believed from the community of Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 286} To one troubled that "min" attaches to the truth and "lam" to the difference, so that on his reading they are reversed, he replies that this is found widely in Arab speech, citing the line of al-Nabigha al-Ja'di, "the obligation of what you say was as the obligation of stoning for adultery", where stoning is in fact the obligation for adultery, and the line "Siraj is noble in his pride, the eye is adorned by him when you look at him", where it is Siraj who adorns the eye. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 286} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 287} Some said the meaning is that the people of the earlier Books differed, some disbelieving in the Book of others though all are from God, and God guided the believers in Muhammad to accept them all; al-Tabari holds the first view sounder because God spoke of their difference over one Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 287}
