## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "Whoever disbelieves in Allah after his faith, except for one who is forced and whose heart is secure in faith. But whoever opens his heart with disbelief, upon them is wrath from Allah, and for them is a great punishment": The Arabs differed regarding the 'min' (min) from the phrase: 'whoever disbelieves in Allah' and from the phrase: 'But whoever opens his heart with disbelief.' Some grammarians in Basra said: The phrase 'so upon them' becomes a predicate for his saying: 'But whoever opens his heart with disbelief,' and his saying: "Whoever disbelieves in Allah after his faith," he informed them of one report, which indicated the meaning. Some grammarians of Kufa said: These are only two parts that have come together, one that is agreed upon by the other, so their answer is the same, like the saying: Whoever comes to us, whoever does good, we honor him, meaning: whoever does good among those who comes to us, we honor him. He said: Likewise, every two parts that come together are the second and the first, so the answer is the same. In the section, he quotes from Ibn Abbas.
