**Commentary on verse 2 (pp. 12-18)**

Al-Tabari then explains "Is it a wonder to the people that We revealed to a man among them: warn the people?": was it a wonder to them that We sent down the Qur'an to a man from among them to warn them of God's punishment for disobedience, as though they did not know that God had revealed before him to men like him, so that they marvelled at the revelation to him? The editor refers to the glossary entries on "revelation" and "warning". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 12}

The interpreters said the same. Report 17527, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Uthman ibn Sa'id, Bishr ibn Umara, Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak: when God sent Muhammad as a messenger, the Arabs, or some of them, denied it, saying God is too great for His messenger to be a human like Muhammad; so the verse came down, and God added that He had sent only men before him. Report 17528, from Ibn Jurayj: Quraysh marvelled that a man of theirs was sent, as with Hud sent to Ad and Salih to Thamud, and as God asks, "Do you wonder that a reminder from your Lord has come to a man among you?" {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 13}

Next he takes "and give good news to those who believe that they have a footing of truth with their Lord": was it a wonder that We revealed to a man among them to warn the people and to give good news to those who believe in God and His messenger that they have a footing of truth? The particle "that" is joined to "warn". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 13}

They differed over "a footing of truth". Some said it is a good reward for the righteous deeds they sent ahead. Here are report 17529, from al-Dahhak: a reward of truth; report 17530, from Mujahid: righteous deeds; report 17531, from Ibn Abbas: a good reward for what they sent ahead of their deeds; and report 17532, from Mujahid: their prayer, fasting, almsgiving and glorifying. In the note the editor says the narrator is Zayd ibn al-Hubab al-Tamimi, whose name the printed text garbled, calls Ibrahim ibn Yazid al-Khuzi weak and al-Walid ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Mughith trustworthy. Reports 17533 and 17534, from Mujahid: good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 14}

Report 17535, from Mujahid, is the same through Ibn Jurayj; report 17536, from al-Rabi ibn Anas: a reward of truth; report 17537, from al-Rabi, the same; and report 17538, from Ibn Zayd: the footing of truth is the reward of truthfulness for the deeds they sent ahead. Others said it means a prior record of truth for them in the Preserved Tablet, namely happiness; report 17539, from Ibn Abbas: happiness was decreed for them in the first remembrance. Others said it means that Muhammad is an intercessor for them, a footing of truth; report 17540, from Qatada or al-Hasan: Muhammad is an intercessor for them, and the editor says he found no biography of Fudayl ibn Amr ibn al-Jawn. Report 17541, from Qatada, then begins. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 15}

Al-Tabari completes the views on "a footing of truth": report 17541, from Qatada, a forerunner of truth with their Lord, and report 17542, from Zayd ibn Aslam, that it is Muhammad. Then he says the view he holds nearest to the truth is that they have righteous deeds with God by which they earn His reward. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 16}

His proof is that the Arabs are reported to say "these are the people of the foot in Islam", meaning those who sent good ahead in it and so had precedence, and "he has a foot of good or of evil with me" for what he has put forward of good or of evil. He cites a line by Hassan ibn Thabit that speaks of the high footing of the one praised, with their allies following their forebears in obeying God, and a line by Dhu al-Rumma in praise of Bilal ibn Abi Burda ibn Abi Musa al-Ash'ari saying that they hold a footing the people do not deny, together with noble lineage (both described, not quoted). The editor adds that the following lines in the poem name the caliphs, that the diwan's wording at the end differs from the one here, and for Hassan's line refers to its tracing in the thirteenth volume. So the meaning is: give good news to those who believe that they have a forward store of good from righteous deeds with their Lord. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 16}

Then "the unbelievers said: this is plain sorcery". The readers differed. Most readers of Medina and Basra read it as "sihr", meaning that what you have brought us, they mean the Qur'an, is sorcery; Masruq, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and a group of the Kufans read "sahir", a sorcerer. Al-Tabari says everything described by a quality points to that quality and the quality points back to it, so the reader may choose either: they called him a sorcerer and called what he brought sorcery, and the two readings agree in meaning. In the note the editor records a difference between the printed text and the manuscript and says which he adopted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 17}

There is an omission in the wording, which what is stated makes unnecessary to spell out: "when he gave them good news, warned them and recited the revelation to them, the unbelievers said: what he has brought us is plain sorcery". The meaning is then: was it a wonder that We revealed to a man among them, warn the people and give good news to the believers; and when he brought them God's revelation and recited it to them, those who denied God's oneness and His messenger's mission said that what Muhammad brought was plain sorcery, that is, it was clear to you that he is a falsifier in what he claims. The editor refers to the glossary on "sorcery" and "plain". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 18}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
