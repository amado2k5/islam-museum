**Commentary on verse 111 (pp. 494-499)**

On [[passage/quran-11-111|11:111]] the readers differ on 'wa inna kullan lamma': a group of readers of Medina and Kufa read 'inna' with shadda and 'lamma' with shadda. The grammarians differ on its sense: one of the Kufan grammarians said that read thus it means 'and each of them, from what, your Lord will pay them their deeds', but when the m's came together one was dropped, two were left and one merged into the other, citing a verse whose author is unknown, and then it was lightened, as some readers read 'and oppression admonishes you' lightening the ya against the ya, and al-Kisa'i recited a line in which a ya was dropped; the editor explains that this 'lamma' is not the 'lamma' he discusses except in the gathering of m's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 494}

He gives another example where the lam was dropped before a lam, in a line describing a path in Najd. Others said that read thus it means 'each, truly and surely, your Lord will pay them their deeds', and what is meant when read 'lamma' with shadda and tanwin, but the reader dropped the tanwin and made it the form of a verb, likening it to 'illa' as someone says by God lamma you got up from us and by God illa you got up from us; this is the reading of al-Zuhri, as will come. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 495}

Al-Tabari says: I found most scholars of Arabic reject this view and refuse that 'lamma' could mean 'illa' except in an oath, saying that were it possible one could say the people stood lamma your brother, meaning except your brother. He says: I think it is unsound by a clearer route than what they said, namely that 'inna' is affirmation and 'illa' is also affirmation, entering only to break a negation that precedes it, so by the interpreter's account 'inna' must be negation for 'illa' to break it, and anyone who said that, his ignorance would be plain, unless a reader lightened 'in' and made it the negative 'in', and then his reading would be spoiled another way, since he would make 'kull' accusative by 'layuwaffiyannahum', and Arabic does not let a verb after 'illa' put the noun before it in the accusative. Some Kufan readers read 'wa in kullan' with 'in' lightened and 'kull' accusative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 496}

Some grammarians claimed the reader meant the heavy 'inna' and lightened it, and from Abu Zayd al-Basri it is reported that he heard 'ka-an' with the noun accusative, the nun lightened from 'ka-anna', and cites a line whose author is unknown. Some Medinans read 'in' lightened with 'kull' accusative and 'lamma' lightened; the reader may have intended the sense reported of the Kufan reader, or 'each, He will pay them', that is, pay each, in which case there is the ugliness I mentioned. Some of the people of the Hijaz and Basra read 'inna' with shadda, 'kullan', 'lama' lightened, 'layuwaffiyannahum', and this reading has two senses, one that the reader meant 'each of them, from those whom your Lord will pay their deeds', directing 'ma' to 'man', as in [[passage/quran-4-3|4:3]], though Arabs mostly use it for what is not human. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 497}

And the lam in 'lama' is meant as the lam that answers 'inna', and the lam in 'layuwaffiyannahum' the oath lam, placed between 'ma' and its clause, as in [[passage/quran-4-72|4:72]]. The other way is to make the 'ma' in 'lama' the 'ma' that enters speech as an addition, its lam being the one that answers, and the lam in 'layuwaffiyannahum' also an answering lam, repeated since that is its place, and he cites an unattributed line. Al-Zuhri is reported to have read 'wa inna kullan' with 'inna' heavy and 'lamman' with tanwin, meaning severely, truly and wholly. Al-Tabari says: the soundest of these readings by Arabic usage is the one reading 'inna' with shadda, 'kullan lama' with 'ma' lightened, 'layuwaffiyannahum rabbuka', meaning that all those whose stories We told you, Muhammad, in this chapter, your Lord will pay them their deeds, the good with abundant reward and the bad with severe punishment, so 'ma' means 'man', the lam in it answers 'inna', and the lam in 'layuwaffiyannahum' is an oath lam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 498}

As for 'He is aware of what they do', it means your Lord, Muhammad, is aware of what these polytheists of your people do; nothing of their deeds is hidden from Him but He is informed of all of it, knows it and encompasses it, until He repays them for all that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 499}
