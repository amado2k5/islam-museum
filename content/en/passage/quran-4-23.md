## Summary
Verse 23 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[concept/jahili-marriage-forms|the four forms of marriage in the Jahiliyya]], and [[concept/social-condition-jahiliyya|the social condition of the Arabs before Islam]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 23 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Forbidden by lineage and by marriage (pp. 141-143, reports 8944-8950).** Abu Jafar says the sense is: marriage with mothers is forbidden to you; the word for marriage is left out because the sentence shows it. Ibn Abbas used to say seven are forbidden by lineage and seven by marriage. Abu Kurayb (8944, from Ibn Abi Zaida, al-Thawri, al-Amash, Ismail ibn Raja, Umayr, a client of Ibn Abbas) reports this from Ibn Abbas, who then recited the verse to its mention of the two sisters and the exception, and said the seventh is the prohibition in [[passage/quran-4-22|4:22]]; Ibn Bashshar gives it (8945, from Muammal, Sufyan, al-Amash, reciting to [[passage/quran-4-24|4:24]]) and again (8946, from Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, Sufyan), and from al-Zuhri (8947, from Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Ibn Abi Dhib) and from Said ibn Jubayr (8948, from Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Habib); Ibn Waki gives it (8949, from his father, Ali ibn Salih, Simak ibn Harb) from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas; and Ibn Humayd (8950, from Jarir, Mutarrif) reports from Amr ibn Salim, a client of the Ansar, the list: by lineage mothers, daughters, sisters, paternal aunts, maternal aunts, and the daughters of brothers and of sisters; by marriage the nursing mothers, the sisters by nursing, the mothers of wives, the stepdaughters, the wives of one's own sons and taking two sisters together, then what follows and the fathers' wives. In the notes on pp. 142-143 the editor calls Ismail ibn Raja al-Zubaydi reliable, says he used to gather the schoolboys and tell them hadith so as not to forget it, and calls Umayr ibn Abdallah al-Hilali, a client of Umm al-Fadl, reliable; says al-Hakim gives the report in al-Mustadrak (2:304) as sound on the conditions of the two shaykhs, with al-Dhahabi agreeing, that Ibn Hajar attributes it to al-Tabarani (Fath 5:133) and Ibn Kathir has it (2:390); that for 8948 al-Bukhari has it in his Sahih (Fath 5:132) by this chain in other wording and al-Bayhaqi in al-Sunan al-Kubra (7:158) in this wording; and that Amr ibn Salim is Abu Uthman al-Ansari, judge of Marw, disputed, with much dispute over his father's name, and that al-Tabari names him instead of using the kunya by which he is famous. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 141} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 142} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 143}

**The mothers of wives: the first view (pp. 143-144).** Abu Jafar says all these women God named as forbidden in the verse are forbidden to the men for whom he forbade them, by the consensus of the whole community, except for the mothers of wives with whom the husbands have not consummated; among some early Companions there was disagreement when the daughter parted before consummation: are they among the unqualified prohibitions or among those conditioned on consummation with the daughters? All scholars, early and late, said they are unqualified, and a man who marries a woman may never marry her mother, whether or not he consummated. They said the condition of consummation belongs to the stepdaughter and not to the mother; if the condition could return to the mothers, the exception in the following verse could return to all the prohibitions, and the agreement of all that the exception applies only to what it directly follows is clearer evidence that the condition applies to what directly precedes it, the stepdaughters. In the note the editor explains "unqualified" as prohibitions lawful by no means or cause, like the mother or sister, quotes al-Qurtubi (5:107) that they are called "mubham", with no door or road to them since the prohibition is shut tight, and quotes al-Azhari that many scholars wrongly take the word as meaning obscurity, whereas it comes from the word for a horse colour without any other mark; and that when Ibn Abbas was asked about the mothers of wives, since God did not state consummation for them, he said it is the unqualified prohibition, whether or not you consummated, whereas stepdaughters have two aspects, lawful in one and forbidden in the other, forbidden if the man consummated with their mothers and not otherwise. He says Ibn al-Athir commented that this was an explanation of stepdaughters and mothers, not of wives, which the editor calls a poor comment, and refers to a book called al-Insaf (pp. 28-29), whose author's name is not clear in the scan. On p. 144 he says the manuscript and printed text have a senseless word in the sentence on the condition and that he conjectured that the condition returns to the mothers of wives and the stepdaughters together, as the other side would have it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 144}

**The view reported from some early authorities (pp. 144-145, reports 8951-8955).** Abu Jafar says it was reported that some early authorities held it lawful to marry the mothers of wives not consummated with, their ruling being that of stepdaughters. Ibn Bashshar (8951, from Ibn Abi Adi and Abd al-Ala, Said, Qatada, Khallas ibn Amr) reports from Ali, asked about a man who marries a woman and divorces her before consummating, whether he may marry her mother, that she is like the stepdaughter; Humayd ibn Masada (8952, from Yazid ibn Zurai, Said) gives it from Khallas, from Ali. Humayd (8953, same chain) reports from Qatada, from Said ibn al-Musayyab, that Zayd ibn Thabit used to say that if the wife died while with him and he took her inheritance, he disliked succeeding to her mother, but if he divorced her before consummating, he could if he wished; Ibn Bashshar (8954, from Yahya ibn Said, Qatada) reports from Said ibn al-Musayyab, from Zayd, that there is no harm in marrying her mother when he divorced before consummating. Al-Qasim (8955, from Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj) reports from Ikrima ibn Khalid that Mujahid told him the entering is meant for both. In the notes the editor calls Khallas ibn Amr al-Hajari reliable but says people spoke about his hearing from Ali, his reports from him being from a sheet he had, as al-Bukhari says in al-Tarikh al-Kabir; quotes al-Qurtubi that Khallas's report from Ali is no proof and that what is sound from Ali agrees with the community; calls Ikrima ibn Khalid al-Makhzumi reliable and says some called him disowned in hadith because he was confused with another; and refers to Ibn Kathir (2:392-394). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 144} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 145}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the report of Amr ibn Shuayb (pp. 145-147, reports 8956-8957).** Abu Jafar chooses the first view, that the mother is among the unqualified prohibitions, because God did not attach the condition of consummation with their daughters to the mothers as he did for the stepdaughters, and that is a consensus of the authority which may not be opposed. A report from the Prophet also reached him, though its chain deserves a look: al-Muthanna (8956, from Hibban ibn Musa, Ibn al-Mubarak, al-Muthanna ibn al-Sabbah) reports from Amr ibn Shuayb, from his father, from his grandfather: when a man marries a woman it is not lawful for him to marry her mother, whether or not he consummated with the daughter; and if he marries the mother, does not consummate and divorces her, he may marry the daughter if he wishes. Abu Jafar says that, whatever is in its chain, the consensus of the authority makes citing another proof unnecessary. In the note the editor says al-Muthanna ibn al-Sabbah al-Abnawi al-Makki has an entry at 4611, that he thinks his report good since he became confused only late, as he detailed in his commentary on the Musnad, that al-Bayhaqi has it in al-Sunan al-Kubra (7:160) from Ibn al-Mubarak and said al-Muthanna is not strong, but that al-Bayhaqi also gave it by Ibn Lahia from Amr ibn Shuayb in similar words, a strong corroboration; that Ibn Kathir copies it (2:394) and al-Suyuti (2:135) adds Abd al-Razzaq and Abd ibn Humayd. Al-Qasim (8957, from Hajjaj) reports Ibn Jurayj asking Ata about a man who marries a woman and does not see her or lie with her until he divorces her, whether her mother is lawful for him; Ata said no, she is "mursala", and, asked whether Ibn Abbas recited the condition of consummation for the mothers, said no, "tatra", which Ibn Jurayj explained as meaning no, no. In the notes the editor says he took the wording of the question from al-Durr al-Manthur (2:135), that the first publisher could not read an undotted word in the manuscript, that "tatra" means successive, and that the question about it occurs in a report in Ibn Sad with the gloss "scattered", and he refers to 8941. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 145} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 146} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 147}

**The stepdaughters and the meaning of entering (pp. 147-148, reports 8958-8959).** Abu Jafar says rabaib is the plural of rabiba, the daughter of a man's wife, so called for his rearing her; its root is "marbuba", turned into rabiba as "qatila" from "maqtula"; and a woman's husband may be called "rabib" of his wife's son, meaning the one who rears him, as one says khabir and khabir, shahid and shahid. The editor notes the manuscript and printed text have "qabila" for "qatila", an error, and "jabir" for "khabir" with the knowing sense. Abu Jafar then says the interpreters differed on the meaning of entering. Some said intercourse, and al-Muthanna (8958, from Abdallah ibn Salih, Muawiya ibn Salih, Ali ibn Abi Talha) reports from Ibn Abbas that the entering is marriage. Others said it is baring, and al-Qasim (8959, from al-Husayn, Hajjaj) reports Ibn Jurayj asking Ata, who said that she is brought to him, he uncovers her, touches her and sits between her legs, and that this forbids her daughter to him even in her family's house, and that the same holds for a slave woman he uncovers, whose mother and daughter he forbids. The editor says the manuscript and printed text have a different verb and that the right one means to touch in order to find out, a word the dictionaries do not explain, though its meaning shows in their talk. Abu Jafar then chooses Ibn Abbas's view that entering means intercourse and marriage, since it can only be one of two things: the apparent customary sense among people, reaching her by being alone with her, or intercourse; and the agreement of all that a man's being alone with his wife does not forbid her daughter if he divorces her before touching her or before looking at her private part with desire shows that the meaning is reaching her by intercourse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 147} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 148}

**The close of the verse: stepdaughters, sons' wives and marrying two sisters together (pp. 149-150, report 8960).** Abu Jafar says the sound interpretation is the one he gave, that the mother of a wife is unconditionally forbidden. He explains the condition about the mothers of stepdaughters as meaning the man did not consummate with them but divorced them, so there is then no objection to marrying the daughter. He explains the wives of sons as the wives of sons of the loin, the plural of "halila"; it was said she is so called because she lies with her husband in one bed. He says there is no disagreement among scholars that a man's son's wife is forbidden to him on the son's contract alone, whether or not the son consummated. If asked about the wives of sons by milk, he says they are like those of the loin in prohibition, and that the qualifier excludes sons a man adopted, not those his wife nursed. On this al-Qasim (8960, from al-Husayn, Hajjaj) reports that Ibn Jurayj asked Ata, who said they used to be told, and God knows best, that the verse came down when the Prophet married the wife of Zayd ibn Haritha and the polytheists talked about it; so this verse came, together with what is in sura al-Ahzab on adopted sons ([[passage/quran-33-4|33:4]]) and on Muhammad not being the father of any of the men ([[passage/quran-33-40|33:40]]). The editor notes that the manuscript and printed text read "we used to narrate", an error, since Ata reports what he heard from his teachers, and refers to Ibn Kathir. Abu Jafar then explains that marrying two sisters together is forbidden to them, with the particle in the nominative position, as if the sentence were "and doing so with both of them"; the editor refers to al-Farra's Maani al-Quran. The exception means: but what had already happened among you before the prohibition. God, he says, covers the sins of his servants when they repent and is kind in the duties he laid on them and then lightened. By this he informs that he pardons whoever combined two sisters in marriage in his pre-Islamic life, before the prohibition, if after it he feared God and obeyed by avoiding it. The editor adds that the manuscript and printed text have a different opening particle, which he corrected to follow al-Tabari's method of giving the verse's text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 150}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 150}
