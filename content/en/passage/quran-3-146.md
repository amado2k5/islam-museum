## Summary
Verse 146 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 146 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Two readings of the first word (p. 263).** Abu Ja'far says the reciters differed: some read it with a hamza on the alif and a doubled ya, others with a long alif and a light ya. Both are famous among Muslim reciters and both are known dialect forms with no difference in meaning, so whichever the reader uses, he is right; the sense is "how many a prophet". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 263}

**Two readings of the verb (pp. 264-265).** The reciters differed: a group from the Hijaz and Basra read it with a damma on the qaf (the passive), and another group from the Hijaz and Kufa with a fatha and a following alif (the active, "fought"). Those who chose the active reasoned that if they had been killed, the denial of weakness would have no known sense, since it is impossible to describe them as not having weakened after being killed. Those who read the passive said that the killing meant the prophet and some of those with him, not all of them, and the denial of weakness is for those who remained and were not killed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 264}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 264-265).** Abu Ja'far says the sounder reading is the damma, because God rebuked by this verse and those before it, from [[passage/quran-3-142|3:142]], those who were routed at Uhud and left the fighting or heard the crier say that Muhammad was killed. He blamed them for fleeing and abandoning the fight: if Muhammad dies or is killed, will you leave your religion? He then told them what many followers of earlier prophets did and said, in effect: why did you not do as the people of virtue and knowledge among the followers of prophets before you did when their prophet was killed, continuing on his path and fighting the enemies of God's religion for it as they had fought beside their prophet, without faltering or weakening, but staying patient against their enemies until God judged between them? The interpreters' explanations follow this. The editor notes where he followed the manuscript against the printed text (for example in the wording of the reading and a plural), and corrected the context in one place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 265}

**The grammar and meaning of "ribbiyyun" (p. 265).** Abu Ja'far says the word is in the nominative because of "with him", not because of the verb, and the sense is: how many a prophet was killed with whom were many ribbiyyun who did not weaken at what befell them; a circumstantial "and" is understood, as in "the commander was killed, a great army with him", meaning "and with him". On the meaning the grammarians differed: some Basrans said they are those who worship the Lord, the singular being ribbi; some Kufans said that if they were attributed to worship of the Lord the word would have a fatha on the ra, and that they are scholars and thousands. Abu Ja'far's own view is that the ribbiyyun are large groups, the singular being ribba, a group; the editor notes how the printed text and manuscript differ in its wording. The interpreters differed, and some said as he does. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 265}

**Reports: thousands, groups, scholars (pp. 266-268).**
- Abd Allah (ibn Mas'ud) by several chains from Asim through Zirr (7957-7960, 7963): the ribbiyyun are thousands. The chains run from Ibn Bashshar through Abd al-Rahman and Sufyan; al-Muthanna through Abu Nu'aym and al-Thawri; al-Hasan ibn Yahya through Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri and Ibn Uyayna from Asim ibn Abi al-Najud and Zirr ibn Hubaysh; Ibn Humayd through Hukkam and Amr; and Humayd ibn Mas'ada through Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal and Shu'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 266}
- Ibn Abbas (7961, 7962): large groups; the editor says the verb in 7962 appears in the active form and that he left al-Tabari's reports as they are because all their senses fit the passive reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 266}
- Ibn Abbas through Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ata' (7964): many scholars. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 267}
- Al-Hasan (7965, 7968): jurists and scholars; in another report (7966): large groups, and Ya'qub says Isma'il read the verb in the passive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 267}
- Qatada (7967): large groups. Likewise Ikrima (7969, 7970), Mujahid (7971, 7972), al-Rabi' (7973), al-Dahhak (7974 and 7976, adding that their prophet was killed), al-Suddi (7977) and Ibn Abbas (7979): large groups. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 267}
- Al-Hasan in a report (7975) through Ibn al-Mubarak and Ja'far ibn Hayyan, with the active reading: Ja'far said patient scholars, and Ibn al-Mubarak said pious and steadfast (the editor notes the plural form meaning the very patient). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 268}
- Ibn Ishaq (7978): how many a prophet met death with groups beside him; the report is in Ibn Hisham (3: 118), continuing 7955 with some difference in wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 268}

**The view that they are followers (p. 269).** Yunus from Ibn Wahb from Ibn Zayd (7980): the ribbiyyun are followers, the rabbaniyyun are the rulers, and the ribbiyyun the subjects; by this God rebuked them when they fled at the Devil's cry that Muhammad was killed, and the rout came with his cry... The editor says the place-name in the report is undotted in the manuscript, that the printed text read it in a way that has no sense, and that he could not trace it despite effort; he thinks it is a corruption of a place name, and mentions what the biographies relate, that Iblis took the likeness of a man at al-Aqaba and cried out, and that two men testified that the man was beside them fighting when the cry was made; he hopes to find the right reading later. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 269}

**The last part of the verse (pp. 269-270).** Abu Ja'far says they did not fail from the pain of the wounds they received in God's cause, nor at the killing of those killed among them, from war against God's enemies, nor shrink from fighting them; their strength did not weaken at the killing of their prophet, and they did not abase themselves, humbling themselves before the enemy by entering their religion or flattering them in it from fear, but went on in their insight and their prophet's way, patient in God's command, obedient to Him and following what He sent down. God loves these and those like them in patience in His command, not one who lost heart and fled the enemy, nor one who turned back and became abject because his prophet was killed or died, nor one entered by weakness at his loss. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the root for weakening (p. 234). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 270}
- Qatada (7981) and al-Rabi' (7982): they did not fail or lose heart at the killing of their prophet, and did not turn back from their insight or their religion, but fought for what the Prophet of God fought for until they joined God. The editor explains "insight" as the heart's belief and knowledge held with firmness and certainty, and says the word "their support" in the printed text and manuscript is an error and the right word is the one he gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 270}
- Al-Suddi (7983): the ribbiyyun did not weaken at the killing of the Prophet and did not falter for it, and did not abase themselves when the Messenger said... and the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 270}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 270 in the commentary on verse 146 and continue from p. 271. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 270}
