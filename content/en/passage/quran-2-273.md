## Summary
Verse 273 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 273 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-273|2:273]]), p. 590. Al-Tabari says the verse is God's explanation of the way and manner of spending; the sense is that whatever good you spend, you spend for yourselves, for the poor who were held back in God's way; the lam in the word for the poor is returned to the place of the lam in for yourselves, and since the particle fa, the answer to the condition, was interposed, repeating it was left out as the sense is clear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 590} Report 6211, al-Suddi: the guidance meant is of the polytheists, while the spending's recipients were specified; the editor refers to report 6208. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 590}

It was said that the poor whom God mentions are the poor of the Emigrants in general, not other poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 590} We stop at the end of p. 590; those who hold this are named only on the next page, so we pause at the last complete report. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-273|2:273]]), pp. 591 to 593. Report 6212, Mujahid: it means the Emigrants of Quraysh in Medina with the Prophet, who was ordered to give charity to them; 6213, al-Rabi ibn Anas: the poor of the Emigrants in Medina; 6214, al-Suddi: the poor of the Emigrants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 591}

Al-Tabari then explains those held back in God's way as those whose fighting their enemy made them confine themselves and hold themselves back from moving about, so they cannot earn; he says he showed earlier that being held back is a man's coming, through illness, want, or fighting his enemy or other causes, to a state in which he keeps himself from managing his affairs; the editor explains the word for moving about as earning, refers to the earlier places, and notes a slight difference in the manuscript wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 591}

He then says the interpreters differed, and some said much as he did. 6215, Qatada: they confined themselves in God's way for fighting; 6216, Ibn Zayd: the whole earth was unbelief so none could go out seeking God's bounty without going out in unbelief, and it was said the whole earth was at war with the people of that town and wherever they turned they met an enemy, so they were here in God's way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 592}

Others said what is meant is those whom the polytheists held back and denied freedom to move. 6217, al-Suddi: the polytheists confined them in Medina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 592} Al-Tabari answers this with a linguistic argument: if the interpretation were as al-Suddi took it, the wording would be the transitive verb form rather than the form meaning being held back; so it shows that their fear of the enemy brought them to the state in which they confined themselves while in God's way, and not that the enemy confined them; for one the enemy holds one says the enemy confined him, and for one held by fear one says fear of the enemy held him back. This is al-Tabari's choice and argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 592}

Al-Tabari then explains that they cannot go about in the land and travel in the countries seeking a livelihood and gains so as to do without charity, from dread of the enemy and fear for themselves; the editor corrects a manuscript word, taking it as evidence of the copyist's carelessness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 593} 6218, Qatada: they confined themselves in God's way against the enemy and cannot trade; 6219, al-Suddi: it means trade; 6220, Ibn Zayd: none of them could go out seeking God's bounty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 593}

Al-Tabari adds that the one who does not know their state thinks them rich from their abstaining from asking and leaving what people hold, patient in hardship, and then begins the reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 593} We stop at 6220, as the next report begins on the same page and carries over to the next. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's choice and linguistic argument are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-273|2:273]]), pp. 593 to 596. Report 6221, Qatada: the one who does not know their state thinks them rich from their abstaining; the editor corrects the chain in print and manuscript by adding the name Bishr, which the copyist dropped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 594} Al-Tabari explains abstaining as leaving off asking people, from the idea of chastity from a thing, meaning leaving it, and cites a line of Ru'ba for the sense of being free of and avoiding (mentioned, not reproduced); the editor refers to where it was earlier documented. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 594}

He then explains sima as the mark and traces, citing a verse in ([[passage/quran-48-29|48:29]]); he says this is the Quraysh form, some Arabs lengthen it, and Thaqif and part of Asad say another form. He gives a line by Ibn Anqa' al-Fazari for the sense (mentioned, not reproduced). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 594} The editor goes on at length in notes on Ibn Anqa' al-Fazari: the disagreement over his name among al-Qali, al-Amidi and al-Marzubani; that he lived long in the pre-Islamic period and, reaching Islam, became Muslim; and his story from al-Qali's Amali, that he was among the richest of his time until misfortune struck, then Umayla ibn Kalda al-Fazari passed by, asked his state and promised to change it, and came at dawn with his camels, sheep and horses and shared his wealth with him, and Ibn Anqa' praised him in verses; he notes the variant wording of the line which al-Tabari gives in volume 8, what Abu Riyash says, and explains the phrase on not wearying the eye; all this is the editor's remarks, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 594} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 595}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over the mark which God said these poor have and by which they are known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 596} Some said it is humility and meekness. 6222 and 6223, Mujahid: humility; 6224, Layth: Mujahid used to say it is humility. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 596} Others said you know them by the mark of poverty and the strain of need on their faces. 6225, al-Suddi: the mark of poverty on them; 6226, al-Rabi ibn Anas: strain from need on their faces. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 596} Others said you know them by the shabbiness of their clothes, hunger being hidden; report 6227 from Ibn Zayd begins and runs on to the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 596} We stop at 6226. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's explanation is his, and all the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
