## Summary
Verse 5 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/battle-of-badr|Battle of Badr]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 5 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 391-397)**

Al-Tabari says, on "as your Lord brought you out of your house with the truth, though a party of the believers were averse" [[passage/quran-8-5|8:5]] and "they dispute with you about the truth after it became clear, as though they were being driven to death while they looked on" [[passage/quran-8-6|8:6]]: the interpreters differed over what brings in the "as" in "as your Lord brought you out" and what is likened to God's bringing His prophet out of his house with the truth. Some said what is likened to it is the good for the believers in their meeting their Lord, their setting right what is between them and their obeying God and His Messenger, and said the sense is: set right what is between you, for that is better for you, as God brought Muhammad out of his house with the truth and it was better for him. Report 15700, from Ikrima: fear God and set right what is between you… as your Lord brought you out of your house with the truth, that is, this is better for you as your being brought out of your house with the truth was better for you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 391}

Others said the sense is: as your Lord brought you out of your house with the truth, Muhammad, against the dislike of a party of the believers, so they dislike fighting and dispute with you about it after it became clear to them. Report 15701, from Mujahid: so they dispute with you about the truth; report 15702, from Mujahid: so they dispute with you about the truth, the fighting; report 15703, from Mujahid: so your Lord brought you out, the editor thinking the right reading is "so they dispute with you"; report 15704, from al-Suddi: God sent down about his going out to Badr and their disputing with him "as your Lord brought you out of your house with the truth, though a party of the believers were averse" at the pursuit of the polytheists, "they dispute with you about the truth after it became clear". The people of Arabic differed over it: some Kufan grammarians said it is a command from God to His Messenger to carry out His command about the spoils against the dislike of his companions, as he carried out His command in his going out of his house in pursuit of the caravan while they were averse, the editor referring to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an; others of them said the sense is: they ask you about the spoils in dispute, as they disputed with you on the day of Badr and said you brought us out for the caravan and did not tell us of a fight for which we might prepare. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 391} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 392}

Some Basran grammarians said it is possible that this "as" in "as your Lord brought you out" is attached to His words "those are the believers truly": as your Lord brought you out of your house with the truth, and said the "as" has the sense of "on"; another of them said it has the sense of an oath, the meaning being "by Him who brought you out, your Lord". The editor says the first is perhaps al-Akhfash, who said the "as" means "on" and claimed it is Arab usage that when someone is asked how he has risen in the morning he says "as good", meaning "on good", and that the author of the second is Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna. Al-Tabari chooses: the best of these views to my mind is that of those who spoke as Mujahid, whose sense is: as your Lord brought you out with the truth against the dislike of a party of the believers, so they dispute with you about the truth after it became clear, because both things happened, I mean the going out of some of those who went out of Medina unwillingly and their dispute about meeting the enemy when the two sides drew near each other, so likening one of them to the other, with one close to the other, is better than likening it to what is far from it. Mujahid said of the "truth" they disputed with the Prophet after it became clear to them that it is the fighting, in reports 15705, 15706 and 15707. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 393} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 394}
As for "from your house", some said it means from Medina; reports 15708, from Ibn Abi Najih, and 15709, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far: from Medina to Badr. As for "though a party of the believers were averse", their aversion was as in report 15710, from Ibn Abbas by the chain of Ibn Ishaq from al-Zuhri, Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada, Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr and Yazid ibn Ruman from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr and others: when the Messenger of God heard of Abu Sufyan coming from Syria he called the Muslims to them and said, this is the caravan of Quraysh with their wealth in it, so go out to it, perhaps God will give it to you as a bonus; the people responded, some light and some heavy, because they did not think the Messenger of God would meet war; report 15711, from al-Suddi: at the pursuit of the polytheists. The editor explains "intadabahu" as calling him so that he answered and hurried, and "ir" as a caravan, and refers to Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 394}
The interpreters then differed over whom "they dispute with you about the truth after it became clear" means. Some said it means the believers among the companions of the Messenger of God who were with him when he set out for Badr to meet the polytheists. Report 15712, from Ibn Abbas: when the Prophet consulted about meeting the people, and Sa'd ibn Ubada said what he said, on the day of Badr, he commanded the people and they drew up for battle and he commanded them to the armed fight and the people of belief disliked it, so God sent down the verse; report 15713, from Ibn Ishaq: then He mentioned the people, the companions of the Messenger of God, and their march with the Messenger of God when the people knew that Quraysh had set out against them and that they had gone out wanting the caravan, hoping for booty, and He said "as your Lord brought you out" up to "averse", that is in dislike of meeting the people and disapproval of Quraysh's march when it was mentioned to them. Others said it means the polytheists; report 15714, from Ibn Zayd: these are the polytheists, who disputed with him about the truth, "as though driven to death" when called to Islam "while they looked on", and this is not of the description of the others but a description begun afresh of the people of unbelief; report 15715, from the nephew of al-Zuhri: a man of the companions of the Messenger of God used to interpret the verse of the going out of the Messenger of God to the caravan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 395} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 396}
Al-Tabari chooses: the right view is what Ibn Abbas and Ibn Ishaq said, that it is a report from God about a party of the believers who disliked meeting the enemy, their dispute with the Prophet of God being their saying, he did not tell us that we would meet the enemy so that we might prepare to fight them, we only went out for the caravan. What shows it sound is His words "when God promised you one of the two parties, that it would be yours, and you wished that the one without the thorn would be yours", [[passage/quran-8-7|8:7]]; in that is the clear proof, for whoever understands from God, that the people disliked the armed fight and that their dispute was over fighting, as Mujahid said, from their dislike of it; and that Ibn Zayd's saying has no sense, because what precedes "they dispute with you about the truth" is a report about the believers and what follows is a report about them, so that it be a report about them is better than that it be about people not mentioned. The editor identifies the men of the chain of 15715. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 396} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 397}
As for "after it became clear", the interpreters differed over its sense. Some said it means after it became clear to them that you do only what God commanded you, report 15716, from al-Suddi. Others said it means they dispute with you about fighting after you were commanded to it, report 15717, from al-Kalbi from Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas. As for "as though driven to death while they looked on", the sense is: as if those who dispute with you about meeting the enemy, from their dislike of meeting them when called to meet them to fight, were being driven to death. The interpreters said the like, and report 15718, from Ibn Ishaq: that is, in dislike of meeting the people and disapproval of Quraysh's march when it was mentioned to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 397}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
