**Commentary on verse 18 (pp. 550-555)**

On "the likeness of those who disbelieve in their Lord: their deeds are like ashes on which the wind blows hard on a stormy day; they have no power over anything of what they earned; that is the far error", al-Tabari says: the scholars of Arabic differed over what puts "mathal" in the nominative. One of the Basran grammarians said: it is as if He said, and of what We relate to you is the likeness of those who disbelieve, then proceeded to explain it, as He said "the likeness of the Garden" [[passage/quran-13-35|13:35]], and this is frequent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 550}

One of the Kufan grammarians said: the likeness belongs only to the deeds, but the Arabs put the nouns first because they are better known and then bring the predicate being told of along with its subject; the sense of the speech is: the likeness of the deeds of those who disbelieve in their Lord is like ashes, as it was said "on the Day of Resurrection you will see those who lied against God, their faces blackened" [[passage/quran-39-60|39:60]], the sense of the speech being: on the Day of Resurrection you will see the faces of those who lied against God blackened. He said, and were "the deeds" put in the genitive it would be allowed, as He said "they ask you about the sacred month, fighting in it" [[passage/quran-2-217|2:217]], the verse, and His saying "the likeness of the Garden promised to the God-fearing, beneath which rivers flow" {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 551}

[[passage/quran-13-35|13:35]] He said, "tajri" is in the place of the predicate, as if he said "that it flows and that it is such and such"; had "an" been put in, it would be allowed. He said, and of it is the poet's saying, Adi ibn Zayd's, in a verse saying, let me be, your affair will not be obeyed, and you did not find my forbearance wasted; he said, "al-hilm" is in the accusative by "alfaytu" by repetition, and had he put it in the nominative it would be right. He said, this is a parable God struck for the deeds of the disbelievers, saying: the likeness of the deeds of those who disbelieve on the Day of Resurrection, which they did in the world claiming that they wanted God by them, is like ashes on which the wind blew hard on a stormy day, scattering it and carrying it off; so the deeds of {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 553}

the people of disbelief in Him on the Day of Resurrection, they find none of them to benefit them with God and save them from His punishment, because they did not do them purely for God but associated idols and images in them. God, mighty and exalted, says "that is the far error", meaning their deeds that they did in the world, in which they associated partners with God, are deeds done without guidance and uprightness but in far deviation from guidance and a path taken without uprightness to a great degree. It was said "on a stormy day", the day being described by stormy, though it is the wind's attribute, because the wind is in it, as one says "a cold day, a hot day", because cold and heat are in it, and as the poet said in a verse describing two cloudy days and a sunny day, describing the two days as cloudy when the cloud is in them. It may also be that "on a day stormy of wind" was meant, with "wind" dropped because it was mentioned before, which would be like Miskin al-Darimi's saying "when comes a day dark of sun, eclipsed", meaning "eclipsed of sun". It was said it is an attribute of the "wind" specifically, except that {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 554}

when it came after "the day" it was made to follow its grammatical case, since the Arabs follow a genitive with a genitive in descriptions, as Dhu al-Rumma said in a verse describing the face of his beloved Mayy, "the look of a face unblemished", putting "ghayr" in the genitive following the case of "face" though it is an attribute of "look", the meaning being "the look of a face, unblemished", and as they say "this is a hole of a ruined lizard". The interpreters said the same: report 20636, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "like ashes on which the wind blows hard", he said, the wind carried it on a stormy day. Report 20637, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "the likeness of those who disbelieve in their Lord: their deeds are like ashes on which the wind blows hard on a stormy day", he says, those who disbelieved in their Lord and worshipped another, their deeds on the Day of Resurrection are like ashes on which the wind blows hard on a stormy day; they have no power over any of their deeds to benefit them, as one has no power over ashes once they are let go on a stormy day. And "that is the far error" means the evident mistake, far from the path of truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 555}
