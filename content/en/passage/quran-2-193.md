## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and fight them until there is no persecution and the religion is God's", al-Tabari says God tells His Prophet: fight the polytheists who fight you until there is no fitna, meaning until there is no shirk toward God and none is worshipped besides Him, the worship of idols, gods and rivals fades, and worship and obedience are to God alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 570} From Qatada, Mujahid, al-Suddi and Ibn 'Abbas: fitna is shirk, and fight until there is no shirk. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 570}

From al-Rabi', "until there is no fitna" means no shirk. From Ibn Zayd, until there is no kufr, and he recited "you will fight them or they will submit" from al-Fath. From Ibn 'Abbas, shirk. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 571} Al-Tabari explains that din in this place is worship and obedience to God in His command and prohibition, citing a verse of al-A'sha, "he subdued al-Rabab when they disliked the din"; the editor gives the story of the poem and the disagreement over whom it praises. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 571} From al-Rabi' on "and the religion is God's": until none is worshipped but God, which is "there is no god but God", for which the Prophet fought and to which he called; he relates from the Prophet, "I have been commanded to fight people until they say there is no god but God, establish the prayer and pay the zakat, and when they do that they have protected from me their blood and property except by its right, and their reckoning is with God". From Qatada, the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 572}

On "but if they desist, there is to be no aggression except against the wrongdoers", al-Tabari says: if those of the unbelievers who fight you cease fighting you, enter your community, acknowledge the obligations God laid on you and abandon the worship of idols, then leave off aggression against them and fighting them, since there should be no aggression except against the wrongdoers, who are those who associate others with God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 572} To the question whether aggression against a wrongdoer is permitted, he answers that the meaning is by way of requital for the aggression of the polytheists, that is, do to them like what they did to you, as one says "if you take a wrong from me I will take it from you", the second not being a wrong; he cites a verse of 'Amr ibn Shas al-Asadi and likens it to "God mocks them"; the editor says he could not find the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 573} From Qatada, the wrongdoer is one who refused to say there is no god but God; from al-Rabi', they are the polytheists; from 'Ikrima, they are those who refused to say there is no god but God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 573} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 574}

Others said the meaning is: fight only those who fight. From Mujahid, fight only those who fight you; from al-Suddi, God does not love aggression against the wrongdoers or others, but says do to them like what they did to you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 574} Al-Tabari reports that some Basran grammarians said "if they desist" can only be said knowing that only some of them will desist, as if it said "if some of them desist, there is no aggression except against the wrongdoers among them", with an ellipsis, while others denied the ellipsis and read it as "if they desist, God is forgiving and merciful to one who desists, and there is no aggression except against the wrongdoers who do not desist". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 574}
