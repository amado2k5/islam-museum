## Summary
Verse 96 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 96 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 96: the first house (pp. 19-21).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over its interpretation.

**First view: the first house in which God is worshipped (7422-7427).** Some said the sense is that it is the first house in which God is worshipped, as a place of blessing and direction for all nations, the one at Bakka, and they said it is not the first house placed on the earth because many houses were before it. Hannad ibn al-Sari relays from Abu al-Ahwas, from Simak, from Khalid ibn Ar'ara that a man stood before Ali and said: will you not tell me about the House, is it the first house placed on the earth? He said: no, but it is the first house in which blessing was placed, the Abraham's place of standing, and whoever enters it is secure (7422). The editor says it is an abridgement of an earlier report; that the manuscript and printed text have here, as there, 'placed in the blessing'; that he corrected it from al-Mustadrak and al-Durr al-Manthur to 'the blessing in it', but returns to judge that what stood there is right and that 'the Abraham's place of standing' is in the genitive as a substitute for 'the blessing'. Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna relays from Muhammad ibn Jafar, from Shu'ba, from Simak that he heard Khalid ibn Ar'ara say that he heard Ali, who was asked whether the house placed first for people is the first house that was on the earth, say: no; where were Noah's people then, and where were Hud's people? but it is the first house set up for the public as a place of blessing and direction (7423). Yaqub relays from Ibn Ulayya, from Abu Raja that Hafs asked al-Hasan, and I was listening, and he said: it is the first mosque in which God was worshipped on the earth (7424). Abd al-Jabbar ibn Yahya al-Ramli relays from Damra, from Ibn Shawdhab, from Matar that there were houses before it but it is the first house placed for worship (7425); the editor says he found no entry for al-Tabari's teacher Abd al-Jabbar, who will come again as 7446. Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad, from al-Hasan that it is the first house in which God is worshipped (7426), and al-Muthanna from al-Himmani, from Sharik, from Salim, from Said that it was placed for worship (7427).

**Second view: it is the first house set up for the public, and their differing on how it was placed.** Others said that it is indeed the first house set up for the public. Those who said so then differed on the manner of its being placed first.

*Created before the earths, which were then spread from beneath it (7428-7432).* Some said it was created before all the earths and then the earths were spread out from under it. Muhammad ibn Umara al-Asadi relays from Ubayd Allah ibn Musa, from Shayban, from al-A'mash, from Bukayr ibn al-Akhnas, from Mujahid, from Abd Allah ibn Amr that God created the House two thousand years before the earth, and, when His throne was on the water, it was a white foam, and the earth was spread out from beneath it (7428); the editor says that the foam is the portion of the white froth of water that rises on it. Muhammad ibn Abd al-Malik ibn Abi al-Shawarib relays from Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad, from Khusayf that he heard Mujahid say that the first thing God created was the Ka'ba, then He spread the earth from beneath it (7429). Muhammad ibn Amr relays from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that it is like the wording of [[passage/quran-3-110|3:110]] (that is, that 'for people' means for all) (7430). Muhammad relays from Ahmad, from Asbat, from al-Suddi that on the day the earth was water there was a foam on the earth, and when God created the earth He created the House with it, so it is the first house placed on the earth (7431). Al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada that it is the first house God placed, around which Adam circumambulated and those after him (7432).

*The site of the Ka'ba is the site of the first house (7433).* Others said that the site of the Ka'ba is the site of the first house God placed on the earth. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada, who said: it was mentioned to us that the House came down with Adam when he came down, and God said: a house of Mine has come down with you, to be circled as My throne is circled; so Adam circled it and those after him of the believers, until in the time of the Flood, when God drowned Noah's people, God raised it and purified it from the punishment of the people of the earth touching it, and it became a frequented house in the heaven. Then Abraham followed some trace of it afterwards and built it on an ancient foundation that had been before it (7433). (These are views and reports al-Tabari transmits, and the museum presents them as they were reported, without a verdict.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 21}

**Al-Tabari's choice and the report of Abu Dharr (p. 22, 7434).** Al-Tabari says the correct view is what God Himself said: that the earliest house made for the people was made so that God might be worshipped in it, a house of blessing and guidance, that is a place of return for the rites of those performing them and the circling of those circling it, in glorification and reverence of God, and that it lies at the place called Bakka, and he gives as his reason the soundness of the report from the Prophet. Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna relays from Ibn Abi Adi, from Shuba, from Sulayman, from Ibrahim al-Taymi, from his father, from Abu Dharr, who said: I asked, Messenger of God, which mosque was founded first? He said: the Sacred Mosque. I asked: then which? He said: the Farthest Mosque. I asked: how long between them? He said: forty years (7434). Al-Tabari says this report shows that the Sacred Mosque is the earliest mosque God established on the earth; as for its being a house in the sense of a house of worship, guidance and blessing, there is disagreement, part of which he mentioned here and part in Surat al-Baqara and other surahs, where he also stated his choice, so he does not repeat it. The editor says Sulayman is al-A'mash; Ibrahim al-Taymi is the son of Yazid ibn Sharik, and he and his father appeared earlier; the report is in Ahmad's Musnad by several routes from al-A'mash and in Muslim by way of Ali ibn Mushir from al-A'mash; Ibn Kathir cites it and says al-Bukhari and Muslim both give it from al-A'mash's report; al-Suyuti cites it and adds that it is attributed to Ibn Abi Shayba, Abd ibn Humayd and al-Bayhaqi in the Shuab; and the printed text altered a reading of the manuscript that is correct. (These are reports and choices relayed by the museum attributed to their owners, without a verdict.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 22}

**The meaning of Bakka and the origin of the name (p. 23).** Al-Tabari says the place called Bakka is the house at the point where people crowd as they circle in their pilgrimage and lesser pilgrimage. The root means crowding: one says that a man bakka another when he pressed and jostled him, and that they yatabakkun when they press and collide, so Bakka, on the pattern fa'la, was named for the act of those crowding in it. He argues that no circling is valid outside the mosque, so what surrounds the Ka'ba inside the mosque is Bakka and what lies outside the mosque is Makka and not Bakka, since nothing outside it obliges people to crowd; on this basis he holds that the view making Bakka a name for the inner part of Makka, and Makka a name for the sacred precinct, is unsound. The editor says a part of the old division of the book ends here; the manuscript has a closing formula of praise and blessing on the Prophet and his family, then a new opening with the basmala and a new chain through Abu Bakr Muhammad ibn Dawud ibn Sulayman al-Baghdadi from Muhammad ibn Jarir, repeating the chain of the manuscript that was copied, as noted in his comment on volume 6; this is the second place where that chain occurs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 23}

**Early views on Bakka and Makka (7435-7442, p. 24).** Yaqub ibn Ibrahim relays from Hushaym, from Husayn, from Abu Malik al-Ghifari that Bakka is the site of the House and Makka is everything else (7435); from Hushaym, from Mughira, from Ibrahim the same (7436). Ibn Humayd relays from Hakkam, from Amr, from Ata, from Abu Jafar that a woman passed in front of a man praying while she was circling the House and he pushed her, and Abu Jafar said: it is Bakka, where people press one another (7437). Ibn al-Muthanna relays from Abd al-Samad, from Shuba, from Salama, from Mujahid that it was called Bakka because people press one another in it, men and women (7438). Ibn Waki relays from his father, from Sufyan, from Hammad, from Said, who was asked why it was called Bakka and said that they crowd in it (7439); the editor says the printed text has another word and he kept the manuscript's. Ibn Waki relays from his father, from Sufyan, from al-Aswad ibn Qays, from his father, from Ibn al-Zubayr that it was called Bakka because they come to it as pilgrims (7440); the editor says al-Aswad ibn Qays al-Abdi narrated from his father and others and Shuba, al-Thawri and Sharik narrated from him, that his father Qays al-Abdi of Kufa has an entry in al-Bukhari's Great History, and that the manuscript and the printed text read "from his brother", a slip he corrected. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that God made all people press on one another there, so that women pray in front of men, which is not fitting in any other town (7441); al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada nearly the same: men and women press one another and pray in front of one another, which is fitting only in Makka (7442). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 24}

**The rest of the views on Bakka (7443-7447, p. 25).** Ibn Waki relays from his father, from Fudayl ibn Marzuq, from Atiyya al-Awfi that Bakka is the site of the House and Makka is what surrounds it (7443). Yunus relays from Ibn Wahb, from Yahya ibn Azhar, from Ghalib ibn Ubayd Allah that he asked Ibn Shihab about Bakka and was told: the House and the mosque; he asked about Makka and was told: the whole sacred precinct (7444). Al-Husayn relays from Hushaym, from Hajjaj, from Ata and Mujahid that in Bakka men and women press one another (7445). Abd al-Jabbar ibn Yahya al-Ramli, a teacher of al-Tabari who appeared at 7425, relays that Damra ibn Rabia said Bakka is the mosque and Makka the houses (7446). Another group, in the report Yahya ibn Abi Talib relays from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak, said it is Makka (7447). It was also said that it is a place of blessing because circling it is forgiveness of sins; the editor says this is the saying of al-Farra in his Meanings of the Qur'an. **Al-Tabari on the grammar of the word for blessing.** He says its accusative is by exclusion from the passive verb, because that verb carries a pronoun returning to the house, which is definite, while the word for blessing is indefinite and cannot follow it in case; the editor says "exclusion" here is like the circumstantial accusative, with similar remarks in volumes 5 and 6. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 25}

**The accusative on the view that the earliest house is meant (p. 26).** Al-Tabari says that on the view of those whose sayings he has given, the accusative is circumstantial, from the relative clause attached to the place called Bakka, because the sense on their view is that the earliest house for the people is the house in Bakka, in a state of blessing; the house is described by the relative clause, which is definite, while the word for blessing is indefinite, so it is put in the accusative by "cutting" in the view of some and as a circumstantial in the view of others, and the word for guidance is joined to it. The editor says "cutting" is like a branch of the circumstantial and refers to earlier places and the glossary of terms. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 26}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
