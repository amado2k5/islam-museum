## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "Is He who is obedient during the night, prostrating and standing, warning of the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord? Say, are those who know equal to those who do not know? Only those of understanding remember." Reciters differed in the reading of the phrase "Aman." Some Meccans, some Medinans, and most Kufans read it as "Aman" with a lightened meem, and their pronunciation is similarly twofold: one is that the alif in "Aman" means supplication, meaning: O you who is devout with the vessels of the night, and the Arabs pronounce with the alif as they pronounce with a bay, saying: I will increase and come, O Zayd, I will come. From this is the saying of Aws ibn Hajar: My son, you are in no hand except a hand without a support. And if you direct the alif to the call, the meaning is: Say, 'Enjoy your disbelief a little, O disbeliever. Indeed, you are among the companions of the Fire. O you who are obedient to the vessels of the night, prostrating and standing, you are among the people of Paradise. And in Hell, the disbelieving side will be blinded by Allah from the reward in the Hereafter.' Al-Saffaya regarding a statement to the believing team, as it was known but was disrupted.
