**Commentary on verse 70 (pp. 72-75)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses His prophet: say to those in your hands and your companions' hands among the polytheist captives from whom ransom was taken, that if God knows Islam in their hearts He will give them better than what was taken as ransom and will pardon them the punishment for their offence in fighting the Prophet of God and his companions and disbelieving in God; God is forgiving to His servants' sins when they repent, and merciful in not punishing them after repentance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 72}

It is reported that al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib used to say the verse was revealed about him. Report 16321, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki, Ibn Idris, Ibn Ishaq, Ibn Abi Najih and Mujahid: al-Abbas said it came down about him; he told the Prophet of his Islam and asked him to reckon against him the twenty ounces taken from him, but he refused, and God gave him in their place twenty slaves, each a merchant with al-Abbas's money in his hands. Report 16322, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, al-Kalbi and Abu Salih, from Ibn Abbas on the authority of Jabir ibn Abdullah ibn Ri'ab, is similar to Ibn Waki's. The editor notes that al-Haythami cites both reports in Majma al-Zawa'id, attributing them to al-Tabarani, that the second chain has al-Kalbi, that al-Wahidi mentions the report in Asbab al-nuzul, and that he corrected the name Jabir ibn Abdullah ibn Ri'ab against the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 73}

Report 16323, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was told that when the money of Bahrain, eighty thousand, came to the Prophet after he had made ablution for the noon prayer, he turned away no one who complained and no one who asked that day, and prayed only after distributing it; he told al-Abbas to take from it and carry it off, and al-Abbas did, saying it was better than what had been taken from them and that he hoped for forgiveness. Report 16324, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: al-Abbas was captured at Badr and ransomed himself with forty ounces of gold; when the verse came down he said God had given him two things he would not exchange for the world, that he was captured and ransomed himself with forty ounces and God gave him forty slaves, and that he hoped for the forgiveness promised. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 74}

Report 16325, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: it means those captured at Badr; if you act in obedience to Me and are sincere to My messenger, I will give you better than was taken and forgive you. Report 16326, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj and Ata al-Khurasani: al-Abbas and his companions told the Prophet they believed what he brought, testified that he was God's messenger and would advise him sincerely against their people, and the verse came down; the "good" is faith and belief, with God replacing better than what was lost, and the forgiveness is of the polytheism they had held. Al-Abbas would say he would not wish this verse unrevealed about them even for the world, for God gave him a hundredfold better than was taken and he hoped he had been forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 74}

Report 16327, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it means al-Abbas and his companions who were captured at Badr; if you obey Me and are sincere to Me and My messenger, I will give you better than was taken and forgive you. Al-Abbas would say God had given them two things better than anything, twenty slaves, and as to the second, they were in the promise of the Truthful, awaiting God's forgiveness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 75}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
