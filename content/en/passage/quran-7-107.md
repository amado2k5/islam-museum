## Summary
Verse 107 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 107 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 107 (pp. 15-17)**

Al-Tabari says Moses threw down his staff and it was a "plain serpent", that is a snake, "plain" meaning that whoever saw it could tell it was a snake. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 15}

Report 14909, from Qatada through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it turned into a huge snake, and another said it was like a city. Report 14910, from Qatada through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: a snake that almost leapt at him, almost scaling him. The editor notes that the printed text has the feminine form "almost" in both places and he keeps the manuscript's masculine, since "snake" takes either gender. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 15}

Report 14911, from al-Suddi through Musa ibn Harun, Amr and Asbat: the thu'ban is the male snake; it opened its mouth, set its lower jaw on the ground and its upper on the palace wall, then headed for Pharaoh to seize him. When he saw it he was terrified, jumped up and fouled himself, though he had never done so before, and cried: Moses, take it and I believe in you and will send the Children of Israel with you. Moses took it and it became a staff again. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 15}

Report 14912, from Ibn Abbas through Abd al-Karim ibn al-Haytham, Ibrahim ibn Bashshar, Sufyan ibn Uyayna, Abu Sa'd and Ikrima: he threw down the staff and it became a snake that put one jaw at the foot of the dome and the other at its top; Sufyan showed with his thumb and forefinger something like an arch. When it was about to seize Pharaoh, Pharaoh said, Moses, take it; Moses took it in his hand and it was a staff again as at first. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 16}

Report 14913, from Ibn Abbas through al-Abbas ibn al-Walid, Yazid ibn Harun, al-Asbagh ibn Zayd, al-Qasim ibn Abi Ayyub and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: it turned into a huge snake, mouth open, rushing at Pharaoh, who threw himself from his couch and begged Moses to hold it off, and he did. Report 14914, from Ibn Abbas through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: the thu'ban is the male snake. The editor's footnotes gloss the "jaw", the "arch" and "threw himself from his couch" (he cast himself down and fell). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 16}

Report 14915, from Wahb ibn Munabbih through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Isma'il ibn Abd al-Karim and Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil: when Moses came in to Pharaoh, Pharaoh asked, do I know you? He said yes; Pharaoh said, did We not bring you up among us as a child ([[passage/quran-26-18|26:18]])? Moses gave the reply he gave, and Pharaoh said, seize him; Moses was quicker and threw his staff, and it was a plain serpent. It attacked the people and they fled; twenty-five thousand of them died, killing one another, and Pharaoh ran off until he entered the house. The editor says the printed text and the manuscript read "Moses said to him: do I know you", which is certainly wrong, and the correct wording comes from Ibn Kathir's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 17}

Report 14916, from Mujahid through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Abu Sa'd, on the verse about the "running snake" ([[passage/quran-20-20|20:20]]): forty cubits lay between its jaws. Report 14917, from al-Dahhak through Waki', Abda ibn Sulayman and Juwaybir: the thu'ban is the male snake. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 17}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
