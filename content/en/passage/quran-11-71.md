**Commentary on verse 71 (pp. 389-397)**

On [[passage/quran-11-71|11:71]] al-Tabari says: 'his wife' was Sarah bt. Haran b. Nahor b. Sarug b. Ra'u b. Peleg, Abraham's cousin; 'standing': it is said she was standing behind a screen listening to the messengers' words and Abraham's, and it is said she was standing serving the messengers while Abraham sat with them. His words 'and she laughed': the interpreters differed on the meaning of 'she laughed' and the reason she did. Some said she laughed the ordinary laugh, in wonder that she and her husband Abraham were serving their guests themselves in honour of them while they held back from their food and did not eat; report 18314, from al-Suddi, begins at the foot of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 389}

Report 18314, from al-Suddi: God sent the angels to destroy the people of Lot; they came walking in the form of young men until they alighted at Abraham's and were his guests. When Abraham saw them he honoured them, went to his family and brought a fat calf, slaughtered it and roasted it on hot stones, and that is the 'hanidh' when he roasted it; he came and sat with them and Sarah stood serving them, which is 'and his wife standing while he was sitting' in Ibn Mas'ud's reading. When he set it before them he said, will you not eat? They said, Abraham, we eat no food except for a price; he said, this has a price; they said what is its price? He said, you mention God's name at its start and praise Him at its end; Gabriel looked at Michael and said, it is right that his Lord take this one as a friend. When he saw their hands did not reach it, that is, they were not eating, he was alarmed by them and felt fear, and when Sarah saw that he had honoured them and she stood serving them she laughed and said, how strange are these guests of ours, we serve them ourselves in honour of them and they do not eat our food. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 390}

Others said rather she laughed that the people of Lot were heedless when God's messengers had come for their destruction. Report 18315, from Qatada: when Abraham felt fear in himself they told him what they had come for, and his wife laughed and wondered that a people were visited by punishment while heedless, and she laughed and wondered at that; 'so We gave her glad tidings of Isaac and, after Isaac, of Jacob'; report 18316, from him, likewise. Others said rather she laughed thinking they intended the deed of Lot's people; report 18317, from Muhammad b. Qays: when the angels came she thought they meant to do what Lot's people do. Others said rather she laughed at the alarm she saw in her husband Abraham; report 18318, from al-Kalbi: she laughed when they alarmed Abraham, from the fright she saw in him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 391}

Others said rather she laughed when she was given the glad tidings of Isaac, wondering that she should have a child at her age and her husband's. Report 18319, from Wahb b. Munabbih: when the angels came to Abraham he saw them and was struck by their appearance and beauty; they greeted him and sat by him; he stood and ordered a fat calf, which was roasted for him, and he set the food before them; 'when he saw their hands did not reach it he mistrusted them and felt fear of them' while Sarah was behind the house listening; they said do not fear, we bring you glad tidings of a forbearing, blessed boy, and they gave his wife Sarah the tidings of him, and she laughed and wondered how she could have a child when she was an old woman and he a very old man; they said do you wonder at God's command, He is able to do what He wills. Some who took this interpretation said that this is an advancing of what is meant to come later, as if the sense were: his wife was standing, so We gave her glad tidings of Isaac and after Isaac of Jacob, and she laughed and said, woe is me, shall I bear when I am old? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 391}

Others said rather that 'she laughed' here means she menstruated. Report 18320, from Mujahid: she menstruated; she was in her seventies and Abraham was a hundred; the editor says this is a ruined report on every side because of its weak narrators. Others said rather she laughed in joy at being safe from them when they said to Abraham do not fear, for he had feared them and she feared them as Abraham did, so when she was reassured she laughed, and they followed it with the glad tidings of Isaac. A Kufan of the people of Arabic claimed he had not heard 'dahikat' for 'menstruated' from a reliable source, and a Basran said some people of the Hijaz told him the Arabs say 'the woman laughed' for menstruated; some said 'laughter' is the teeth; he cites lines of Abu Dhu'ayb, al-Kumayt and others using the word, says the Arabs say 'the palm laughed' when it put out its spathe and 'I made a trough laugh' when filled until it overflowed, and that the senses are near one another as it is something that fills and overflows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 393}

Al-Tabari says the soundest of the views mentioned is that of those who said 'she laughed' means she wondered at the heedlessness of Lot's people toward the punishment of God that had encircled them; he says so because it is mentioned immediately after their words to Abraham 'do not fear, we were sent to the people of Lot', and since there is no ground for laughing and wondering at their saying 'do not fear' to Abraham, the laughing and wondering were at the affair of Lot's people. His words 'so We gave her glad tidings of Isaac and after Isaac of Jacob' mean We gave Sarah, Abraham's wife, as a reward from Us for her disapproval and wonder at the deed of Lot's people, the tidings of Isaac as her son, 'and after Isaac, Jacob', that is, behind Isaac, from her son Isaac. 'Wara'' in Arab speech is the child's child, and so the interpreters took it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 394}

Reports 18321, 18323, 18324, 18325 and 18326, from Amir al-Sha'bi: 'wara'' is the child's child; report 18322, from al-Hasan, who, asked by Abu al-Mughira about a youth, was told he is my son of my 'wara'', and al-Hasan recited 'We gave her glad tidings of Isaac and after Isaac of Jacob'; report 18327, from Ibn Abbas to a man who came with his son's son, saying this is your child from 'wara'', which seemed hard on the man, so Ibn Abbas said God says 'and after Isaac, Jacob', so children's children are 'wara''. Report 18328, from al-Suddi: when Sarah laughed and said how strange our guests are, we serve them ourselves in their honour and they do not eat our food, Gabriel said to her, rejoice in a child named Isaac and after Isaac Jacob; she struck her face in wonder, which is 'she struck her face' in [[passage/quran-51-29|51:29]], and said 'woe is me, shall I bear when I am old and this my husband is an old man, that is a strange thing'; they said do you wonder at God's command, the mercy of God and His blessings are on you, people of the house, He is Praiseworthy, Glorious; Sarah said what is the sign of that? He took a dry stick in his hand, twisted it between his fingers and it quivered green; and Abraham said, it is then for God as a sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 395}

Report 18329, from Ibn Ishaq: 'she laughed' means Sarah, from what she knew of God's command and what she knew of the people of Lot; they gave her glad tidings of Isaac and after Isaac of Jacob, a son and a son's son; she said, and struck her face, it is said she struck her brow, woe is me, shall I bear when I am old, to 'He is Praiseworthy, Glorious'. The readers differed on 'and after Isaac, Jacob': most readers of Iraq and the Hijaz read 'Ya'qubu' in the nominative, restarting the speech with 'after Isaac, Jacob', and although this is a fresh statement it indicates the sense of glad tidings. Some readers of Kufa and Syria read 'Ya'quba' in the accusative; the Syrian of the two is said to have taken it as accusative through an understood verb like the tidings, as if he said 'and We granted him, after Isaac, Jacob', and when 'We granted' was not shown the 'giving glad tidings' acted on it and was joined to the place of 'Isaac', since 'Isaac' though genitive is in the sense of the accusative governed by 'We gave glad tidings', as the poet said; the commentary on the verse continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 396}

The editor cites Jarir's line against al-Akhtal in which he boasts of the tribes of Qays Aylan over Taghlib. As for the Kufan of the two, he read it as genitive in sense but put it in the accusative because it is not inflected for the genitive; the people of knowledge of Arabic rejected that, because of the interposing of the 'sifa', meaning the preposition, between the conjunction and the noun, saying it is wrong to say 'I passed by Amr in the house and in the house Zayd' with Zayd joined to Amr unless the preposition is repeated; if it is not repeated the right form for them is the nominative, and the accusative is allowed; if the noun is put before the preposition the genitive is allowed, as when you say 'I passed by Amr in the house and Zayd in the room', and some Basran grammarians allowed the genitive even with the preposition between the conjunction and the noun. Al-Tabari says the soundest of the two readings for him is the nominative, because that is the known speech of the Arabs which the people of Arabic do not reject, and what the readers of the cities hold; the accusative has a ground, but he does not like to read it so because God's Book came down in the most eloquent tongue of the Arabs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 397}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
