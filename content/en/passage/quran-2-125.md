## Summary
Verse 125 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "and when We made the House a resort for the people" al-Tabari says "when" is joined to "and when his Lord tested Abraham with words", which is itself joined to "Children of Israel, remember My favour", and that the House is the Sacred House. On the sense of "mathaba" he reports a difference among the grammarians: some Basra grammarians said the feminine ending was added because of the many who return to it, while some Kufa grammarians said mathab and mathaba are one, like maqam and maqama. In his reading it means a place of return, that He made the House a resort and refuge for the people, who come to it every year and return to it without ever having had enough of it; he cites a line attributed to Waraqa ibn Nawfal describing the sanctuary, whose wording and attribution the editor discusses in a footnote. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 25} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 26}

On "mathaba" al-Tabari reports from Mujahid, al-Suddi, Ibn Abbas, 'Abda ibn Abi Lubaba, 'Ata', 'Atiyya, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Qatada, al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd that people return to it, from every place, every year, and never have enough of it, so no one leaves it thinking he has had his fill. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 27} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 28} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 29}

On "and a place of safety" he says "amn" is the verbal noun of "amina, ya'manu, amnan", and that God called it so because in pre-Islamic times it was a refuge for whoever sought refuge in it: a man might meet the killer of his father or brother there and not touch him until he left it. He cites "have they not seen that We made a safe sanctuary while people are snatched away around them", and from Ibn Zayd, al-Suddi, Mujahid, al-Rabi' and Ibn Abbas that whoever enters it is safe and that its sanctity is such that whoever enters is not afraid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 29} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 30}

On "take the station of Abraham as a place of prayer" he notes the disagreement of the readers: some read "ittakhidhu" with a kasra on the kha', as a command, which is the reading of most of the people of Kufa and Basra, most Meccan readers and some Medinan readers, and they relied on a report from Anas ibn Malik that 'Umar ibn al-Khattab said: "I said, Messenger of God, if only you took the station as a place of prayer", and the verse was revealed; they said that being a command it may not be read as a report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 30} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 31}

He mentions that one of the Basra grammarians claimed the phrase is joined to "Children of Israel, remember My favour", so the command would be to the Jews of the Prophet's time; and from al-Rabi' ibn Anas that among the words with which Abraham was tested is "take the station of Abraham as a place of prayer", so that they pray behind the station. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 31}

Al-Tabari says the report from 'Umar ibn al-Khattab from the Prophet indicates the opposite of what those said who made the command one to the Jews: it is a command from God to the Messenger, the believers and all creation held to account. Some readers of Medina and Syria read "ittakhadhu" with a fatha, as a report, and there is disagreement about what it is joined to: some Basra grammarians took it as "and when they took", some Kufa grammarians as joined to "We made". Al-Tabari prefers "ittakhidhu" with a kasra, as a command, because of the report established from the Prophet, and he relates from Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah that the Prophet read it so. In a footnote the editor thinks some words have dropped out of al-Tabari's text at one point. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 31} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 32} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 33}

He then sets out the interpreters' disagreement on "the station of Abraham". Some said it is the whole pilgrimage, from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid and 'Ata'. Others said it is 'Arafa, al-Muzdalifa and the pillars, from 'Ata', Mujahid and Ibn Abbas; al-Sha'bi said "today I have perfected for you your religion" was revealed to the Prophet while he stood at 'Arafa. Others said it is the sanctuary, from Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 33} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 34}

Others said it is the stone on which Abraham stood when the building rose and he grew too weak to lift the stones, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas: Abraham built and Ishmael handed him stones, and when the building rose he stood on a stone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 34} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 35}

Others said it is his station in the Sacred Mosque; Qatada said they were told to pray beside it and not to wipe it, and it was mentioned to him that someone who saw the mark of Abraham's heels and fingers in it said people kept wiping it until it wore away. From al-Rabi': they pray behind the station. From al-Suddi: the prayer is at the station in the pilgrimage, and the station is the stone that Ishmael's wife placed under Abraham's foot when she washed his head, so that his foot sank in it, and God made it one of His rites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 35} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 36}

Al-Tabari chooses that "the station of Abraham" is the station known by that name in the Sacred Mosque, by the report from 'Umar and by Jabir's report of the Prophet's pilgrimage: he kissed the corner, trotted three rounds and walked four, then went forward to the station of Abraham and recited the verse, put the station between himself and the House and prayed two rak'as. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 36}

Al-Tabari says that even had there been no report from the Messenger to support his choice, the right course would be this view, because speech is taken in its known apparent sense unless something shows the opposite, and what is known among people as "the station of Abraham" is the place of prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 36} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 37}

On "musalla" the interpreters differed. Some said it is the place of supplication, from Mujahid, and others that the meaning is: take a place of prayer to pray beside, from Qatada and al-Suddi. Al-Tabari explains the first view as taking "musalla" from "sallaytu" in the sense of "I called on", held by those who said the station of Abraham is the whole pilgrimage, so the meaning would be: take 'Arafa, al-Muzdalifa, the pillars and the other places of pilgrimage as places of supplication and follow Abraham in them. He chooses the other view: take from the station of Abraham a place of prayer, praying beside it as worship from you and honour from Him to Abraham, by the reports from 'Umar and Jabir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 37} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 38}

On "and We made a covenant with Abraham and Ishmael to purify My House for those who circle it" he says "We made a covenant" means "We commanded", from 'Ata' and Ibn Zayd, and that the purification is from idols, the worship of images and associating partners. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 38}

He asks whether there was a house before Abraham's building which could be purified of polytheism, and gives two readings, each held by a group of interpreters. The first is that it means: build My House free of polytheism and doubt, like "is he who founded his building on piety toward God better", from al-Suddi. The second is that they were told to purify the place of the House before it was built and the House after, of the idols that polytheists placed there in the time of Noah and before, so that this would be a custom for those after them, from Ibn Zayd; and from 'Ubayd ibn 'Umayr, Mujahid and Qatada, from idols, polytheism and doubt, with Qatada adding false speech. In a footnote the editor says this answer rests on there having been idols worshipped at the House before Abraham, which needs proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 39} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 40}

On "those who circle it" they differed: some said they are the strangers who come to the House from afar, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, others that they are those who circle it, strangers or its own people, from 'Ata'. Al-Tabari holds the better reading is 'Ata''s, because the one who circles is the one who goes round a thing, and a newcomer from afar does not deserve the name unless he circles. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 40} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 41}

On "and those who stay" he says the one who stays by something is the one who remains at it, citing a verse of al-Nabigha al-Dhubyani. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 41}

Al-Tabari says the one in retreat (mu'takif) is so called for staying in the place where he confined himself for God, then sets out the disagreement over whom God meant by "those who stay": some said the one sitting in the Sacred House without circling or praying, from 'Ata'; some, those in retreat who dwell beside it, from Mujahid and 'Ikrima; some, the people of the Sacred Land, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Qatada; and others, those who pray, from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 41} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 42} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 43}

Al-Tabari chooses 'Ata''s view, that the one who stays is the one remaining at the House as a neighbour without circling or praying, since the verse also names those who circle and those who bow and prostrate, which shows that the state of the one who stays is other than theirs. On "those who bow and prostrate" he says they are the groups of bowers and prostraters, and it was also said that those who pray are meant, from 'Ata' and Qatada; he refers to the earlier explanation of bowing and prostration. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 43} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 44}
