**Commentary on verse 90 (pp. 188-193)**

On [[passage/quran-10-90|10:90]] al-Tabari says: We took the Children of Israel across the sea until they had crossed it, 'and Pharaoh and his hosts followed them', and one says 'atba'tuhu' and 'tabi'tuhu' in one sense. Al-Kisa'i, as Abu Ubayd reports, used to say that when it is meant that he followed them with good or ill the word is 'atba'ahum' with a hamza, and when it is meant that he followed their trail or imitated them it is from 'ittaba'tu' with doubled ta and no hamza. 'In rebellion' against Moses and Aaron and the Israelites with them, and 'aggression', that is, hostility against them, a verbal noun from 'so-and-so was aggressive against so-and-so in wrong', like 'ghazā'. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'crossing', 'rebellion' and 'aggression'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 188}

It is reported of some that they read the word for aggression in another form, also a verbal noun, like 'uluww' from 'ala'. 'Until drowning overtook him' means until drowning surrounded him; a phrase is left out in the speech, its mention dropped because the plain words show it: Pharaoh and his hosts followed them in rebellion and aggression, so We drowned him, until drowning overtook him. His words 'he said: I believe that there is no god but the One in whom the Children of Israel believe, and I am of those who submit' are a report of Pharaoh's saying when he was at the edge of drowning and certain of death: I acknowledge that there is no god but He in whom the Children of Israel believe. The readers differed: some, the reading of most of Medina and Basra, read 'annahu' with fatha, as governed by 'I believe'; others, the reading of most Kufans, read 'innahu' with kasra, as the start of a fresh report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 189}

Al-Tabari says that in his view the two are readings close in meaning and whichever the reader uses is right, and the interpreters said as he did. Report 17857, from Abd Allah b. Shaddad: Jacob and his sons joined Joseph, seventy-two in all, and they left Egypt with Moses, when they went out, six hundred thousand. When Pharaoh caught up with them and they saw him, they said: Moses, where is the way out? God inspired Moses to strike the sea with his staff, and it parted, and each part was like a great mountain; the sea dried for them and God uncovered the face of the ground. Pharaoh rode out on a black stallion, with eight hundred thousand behind him on horses of that colour besides all other colours; Gabriel was on a mare in heat, the only mare among them, and Michael drove them on. When the last of the Israelites had come out Gabriel came near him, the stallion smelled the mare, and Pharaoh could not control it; he said: go on, those people have no more right to the sea than you. He followed them until the first of them wished to come out, and then he plunged and called out what the verse reports, and was answered with the words 'now, when you had disobeyed before?'. The editor refers to the verse of Surat al-Shu'ara' worked into the report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 190}

Report 17858, from Ibn Abbas, raised to the Prophet by one of the two narrators, that Gabriel was thrusting into Pharaoh's mouth; it begins at the foot of the page and runs on to the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 190}

Report 17858, from Ibn Abbas, raised to the Prophet by one of the two narrators, that Gabriel was thrusting mud into Pharaoh's mouth for fear he would say there is no god but God; report 17859, from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet: Gabriel began to thrust, or to stuff, mud into Pharaoh's mouth for fear that mercy would reach him. The editor says Ahmad relates it in his Musnad by the route of Shu'ba from Adi b. Thabit and Ata b. al-Sa'ib, as does Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi; that al-Hakim called it sound by the standard of the two Shaykhs, except that most of Shu'ba's companions made it stop at Ibn Abbas, and al-Dhahabi agreed; and that al-Tirmidhi relates it and calls it good, strange and sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 191}

Report 17860, from Abu Hurayra, who said the Prophet said: Gabriel said to me, Muhammad, if you had seen me pushing and thrusting black mud into his mouth for fear that God's mercy would reach him and He would forgive him, meaning Pharaoh. The editor says the report is very weak because of Kathir b. Zadhan, of whom Ibn Ma'in said I do not know him and Abu Hatim and Abu Zur'a said an unknown shaykh; that al-Haythami cites a similar report from Abu Hurayra through al-Tabarani's al-Awsat, with Qays b. al-Rabi' in it; and he glosses 'aghuttuhu' as dunk him in the water and 'hal' as the black mud and silt. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 191}

Report 17861, from Ibn Abbas, that the Prophet said: when God drowned Pharaoh he said 'I believe that there is no god but the One in whom the Children of Israel believe', and Gabriel said, Muhammad, if you had seen me taking from the sea mud and thrusting it into his mouth for fear that mercy would reach him. The editor says Ali b. Zayd b. Jud'an was weakened by the imams, though not accused of lying, and his reports may be noted but are not relied on, and that Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi relate the report and the latter calls it good. Report 17862, from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet: when Pharaoh said there is no god but God, Gabriel began stuffing mud and dust into his mouth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 192}

Report 17863, from Maymun b. Mihran: Gabriel took mud from the sea and struck his mouth with it, or filled his mouth with it, for fear that God's mercy would reach him. Report 17864, from Maymun b. Mihran, that al-Dahhak b. Qays gave a sermon and said: Pharaoh was a rebellious servant who forgot the remembrance of God, and when drowning overtook him he said 'I believe', and God said 'now, when you had disobeyed before?'. Report 17865, from Ibn Abbas, as a saying stopping at him: when drowning overtook Pharaoh Gabriel began stuffing dust into his mouth for fear he would be forgiven. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 193}

Report 17866, from Ibrahim al-Taymi, that Gabriel said: I envied no one of the children of Adam mercy except Pharaoh, for when he said what he said I feared it would reach the Lord and He would have mercy on him, so I took mud and foam of the sea and struck his eyes and his face with it; report 17867, from Ibn Abbas: Gabriel said I stuffed his mouth with mud for fear that mercy would reach him. The editor corrects readings in the printed text from the manuscript in these reports. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 193}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
