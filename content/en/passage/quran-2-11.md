## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari records a difference over "and when it is said to them: do not spread corruption in the land". [[person/salman-al-farisi|Salman al-Farisi]] is reported to have said: these have not come yet. Others, including Ibn Abbas in al-Suddi's report and al-Rabi' ibn Anas, say they are the hypocrites, and that corruption is disbelief and acting in disobedience, since whoever disobeys God in the land or commands disobedience has spread corruption in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 287} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 288}
Al-Tabari prefers that the verse was revealed about the hypocrites of the Prophet's time, though its sense reaches any who share their description until the Day of Resurrection. Salman's words could mean he spoke after those of that time had passed, reporting on those who would come after, not denying that anyone of that description existed. His reason is the agreement of the exegetes that it concerns the hypocrites of that time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 289}
He defines corruption in the land as doing what God forbade and neglecting what He ordered to be kept, and lists disobedience, neglect of obligations, doubt about the religion, lying to the believers and backing the deniers against God's friends. That they thought themselves reformers did not lift their punishment, which he takes as a reply to those who say punishment is deserved only by one who is obstinate after knowing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 289} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 290}
On "we are only reformers" he reports Ibn Abbas that they meant making peace between the believers and the People of the Book, and Mujahid that when they committed disobedience and were told to stop, they said they were on guidance and were reformers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 290}
