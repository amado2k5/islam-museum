## Summary
Verse 83 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 83 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 83: the reading of the person of the verbs (pp. 563-564).** Al-Tabari says the reciters differed. Most reciters of the Hijaz from Mecca and Medina and the reciters of Kufa read both verbs with the second-person letter, addressing them. Some people of the Hijaz read both with the third-person letter, as a report about those absent. Some people of Basra read the first with the third-person letter and the second with the second-person. Al-Tabari chooses the second-person letter for both, because the verse before it is an address to them, so following the address with its like is better than turning the speech to something unlike it, though the other form is permissible, as he has explained that the narrated speech sometimes comes out wholly in address, sometimes as a report about the absent, and sometimes partly in address and partly in the absent form. The editor refers to p. 464 and its note and to the index of Arabic topics.

**The sense of the verse according to al-Tabari.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: People of the Book, do you seek and wish for anything other than obedience to God, when all in the heavens and the earth have humbled themselves to Him, yielding to Him in servitude, acknowledging His singular lordship and submitting to Him in the sincerity of oneness and divinity, whether by choice or by compulsion? Those whose submission is willing, like the angels, the prophets and the messengers, have submitted willingly, and among them are those who were unwilling. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on religion, seeking, submission and compulsion and keeps the word 'ubuda' as in the manuscript rather than 'ubudiyya' of the printed text.

**The views of the interpreters on the submission of the unwilling.** The interpreters differed over the sense and manner of the submission of the one who submits unwillingly.

*Acknowledging that God is his creator and lord (7342-7344).* Some said his submission is his acknowledging that God is his creator and lord, even if he associates another with Him in worship. Abu Kurayb relays from Waki, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Mujahid that it is like the sense of a verse of al-Zumar about their being asked who created the heavens and the earth (7342), and Ibn Bashshar relays it from Abu Ahmad, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Mujahid (7343). Al-Muthanna relays from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi, from Abu al-Aliya that every human has acknowledged against himself that God is his lord and he is His servant, so whoever associates in His worship is the one who submitted unwillingly, and whoever is sincere in servitude is the one who submitted willingly (7344).

*When the covenant was taken (7345).* Others said the submission of the unwilling was when the covenant was taken from him and he acknowledged it. Abu Kurayb relays from Waki, from Sufyan, from al-A'mash, from Mujahid, from Ibn Abbas that it was when the covenant was taken (7345).

*The prostration of the shadow (7346-7349).* Others said that what is meant by the submission of the unwilling is the prostration of his shadow. Sawwar ibn Abd Allah relays from al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, from Layth, from Mujahid that the willing is the believer and the unwilling is the shadow of the unbeliever (7346); Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid that it is the prostration of the believer willingly and of the unbeliever while he is unwilling (7347); al-Muthanna from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid: the believer's prostration willingly and the prostration of the unbeliever's shadow while he is unwilling (7348); and al-Qasim from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abd Allah ibn Kathir, from Mujahid: the prostration of his face willingly and of his shadow unwillingly (7349). The editor says the manuscript and the printed text have 'the prostration of his face and his shadow willingly', which does not stand, and that from the earlier reports of Mujahid he concluded that what he put is the true sense.

*The heart's yielding to God's will (7350).* Others said his submission is by his heart within God's will and his yielding to His command, though he denies His divinity by his tongue. Abu Kurayb relays from Waki, from Isra'il, from Jabir, from Amir that all of them yielded to Him (7350). The editor says that Jabir is Jabir ibn Yazid al-Ju'fi, who related from Abu al-Tufayl, Abu al-Duha, Ikrima, Ata and Tawus, and from whom Shu'ba, al-Thawri, Isra'il and a group related; that Amir is al-Sha'bi; and that the manuscript and the printed text had 'Jabir ibn Amir', but there is no narrator of that name.

*Those who submitted fearing the sword (7351 and 7352).* Others said what is meant is the submission of those among the people who submitted unwillingly, fearing the sword for themselves. Muhammad ibn Sinan relays from Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, from Abbad ibn Mansur, from al-Hasan that some people were compelled to Islam and some came willingly (7351). Al-Hasan ibn Qaza'a al-Bahili relays from Rawh ibn Ata, from Matar al-Warraq that the angels submitted willingly, the Ansar willingly, Banu Sulaym and Abd al-Qays willingly, and all the people unwillingly (7352).

*The believer willingly and the unbeliever at the sight of the punishment (7353 and 7354).* Others said that the people of faith submitted willingly and the unbeliever submitted when he saw the thing with his eyes, when submission no longer profits him. Bishr relays from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada that the believer submitted willingly and it profited him and was accepted from him, whereas the unbeliever submitted unwillingly when it no longer profits or is accepted from him (7353); al-Hasan ibn Yahya relays from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada that the unbeliever submitted when he saw God's might, citing a verse of Ghafir that their faith did not profit them when they saw the might (7354).

*The worship of creation to God (7355).* Others said that the sense is the worship of creation to God. Al-Muthanna relays from Abd Allah ibn Salih, from Muawiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas that the sense is their worship of Him, all of them, by choice and under compulsion, which is His statement in a verse of al-Ra'd about the prostration of those in the heavens and the earth (7355). The editor says the printed text has 'in the worship of creation' and the manuscript 'that the worship of creation', and that he preferred the reading he gave.

**The return to Him.** Al-Tabari says the sense is: to God, People who seek a religion other than Islam among the Jews, the Christians and the rest of the people, you will go after your death, and He will repay you for your deeds, the doer of good for his good and the doer of ill for his ill. This is a warning from God to His creation that any of them should return to Him after his death on other than the community of Islam. (This is what al-Tabari transmits, and the museum presents it without a verdict.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 568}
