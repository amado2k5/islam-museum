## Summary
Verse 265 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 265 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

In ([[passage/quran-2-265|2:265]]) from p. 530, al-Tabari says the likeness is of those who spend their wealth, giving it in charity, mounting others with it in God's way, supporting the needy among the fighters and combatants in His way and in other obedience to God, seeking His pleasure. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 530} The editor says in a note that the manuscript's wording is disordered, that the copyist repeated himself, and that he deleted and added words to make the sentence stand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 530} Al-Tabari then explains the confirming of their souls: it means strengthening them in spending that in obedience to God, and making it sure, from the saying I confirmed so-and-so in this matter when you made his resolve sound and firm and strengthened his opinion in it, citing a line of Abd Allah ibn Rawaha praying that God confirm what He gave of good with confirmation as with Moses and with help as they were helped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 531} The editor gives in a note the sources for the line in Ibn Hisham's Sira, Ibn Sa'd, al-Amidi, al-Isti'ab and Tabaqat Fuhul al-Shu'ara', says al-Amidi and Ibn Hisham give the second half-line differently, and quotes the report that when the Prophet heard this line he turned to him smiling and said to him that God confirm you also; this is the editor's report from those sources, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 531}

Al-Tabari says God meant that their souls were certain and trusting in His promise for what they spent in obedience to Him without reproach or harm, so they confirmed them in spending their wealth seeking God's pleasure and made their resolve and opinions sound, out of certainty in it and trust in God's promise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 531} That is why some interpreters glossed this word as trust and some as certainty, since the confirming of the souls of those who spend their wealth seeking God's pleasure came from their certainty and trust in God's promise; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 531} Al-Sha'bi (6063): trust and certainty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 531} The editor's notes correct readings of the manuscript and printed text and explain two words in the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 531} We stop here. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's explanation is attributed to him, and the corrections are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-265|2:265]]) from p. 531. Al-Tabari cites those who glossed the confirming from their souls as trust and certainty. Al-Sha'bi (6064): trust from their souls, steadfastness and help; Qatada (6065): certainty from their souls, saying that confirmation is certainty; Abu Salih (6066): certainty from within themselves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 532} Others said it means that they used to verify the place where they put their charity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 532} Mujahid (6067, 6068, 6069): they verify where they put their wealth; al-Hasan (6070): they used to verify where they put their wealth, meaning their zakat, and (6071) that when a man intended a charity he checked, and if it was for God he went ahead and if doubt mixed in he held back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 532} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 533} The editor notes that the numbering skips report 6072 at one point, and that it dropped out of the numbering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 534}

Al-Tabari says this interpretation, which he has reported from Mujahid and al-Hasan, is far from the meaning shown by the plain recitation, and his argument is that they took the noun of confirmation as meaning verification, claiming the people used to verify where they put their wealth; were that so, the verbal noun would follow the pattern of the reflexive verb, as one says he honoured himself and he spoke, as in ([[passage/quran-16-47|16:47]]) with the verbal noun of a man's taking fright; so if it came from the people's verifying where they put their charity, the noun would follow that pattern and not the causative one; rather the sense for him is that their souls confirmed them by soundness of resolve and certainty in God's promise; this is his argument and construction. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 533} The editor refers in a note to a difference between the printed text and the manuscript in a phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 533}

Al-Tabari raises an objection: why do you not allow that this is like ([[passage/quran-73-8|73:8]]), where the verbal noun was not given on the pattern of the verb? He answers that this is different, for the verbal noun there was allowed because the verb was evident before it, indicating the omitted speech: he devoted himself and God devoted him to Him with devotion; the Arabs sometimes produce verbal nouns not matching the preceding verbs when the verbs before indicate what they are taken from, as in ([[passage/quran-71-17|71:17]]) and ([[passage/quran-3-37|3:37]]), where plant growth is the verbal noun of growing because of the verb before it, the sense being He made you grow and you grew from the earth; but in the clause under discussion there is no preceding verb that would suggest the noun was shifted from its pattern; this is al-Tabari's answer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 533} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 534} The editor explains in a note the manuscript's and printed text's different parsing and that the correct one is what he printed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 534}

Others said the sense of confirmation is seeking reward from themselves; Qatada (6073): reckoning for reward from their own souls. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 534} Al-Tabari says this view too is far from the sense of confirmation, because confirmation is not known in any speech in the sense of reckoning for reward, unless its interpreter meant that the souls of those who spend were reckoning for reward in confirming their owners; but if that is the sense, reckoning for reward is not a meaning of confirmation to be used to translate it; this is al-Tabari's judgement and argument. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 534} We stop here. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's critique is attributed to him with no verdict from the museum, and the corrections are the editor's.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-265|2:265]]) from p. 534, on the likeness of those who spend their wealth seeking God's pleasure and out of their souls' trust in His promise. Al-Tabari says the likeness is of one who gives his wealth in charity and makes it a path in obedience to God without reproach to the recipient or harm, and that it is likened to a garden, which means an orchard, as he has already shown. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 535} He says the raised ground is land that rises and is lifted above the flood, and God described it so because ground raised above the channels and valleys is firmer, and the gardens of firm ground are finer and better in fruit, planting and crops than those of soft ground; he cites a line of al-A'sha describing a meadow among the meadows of al-Hazn, green, watered by constant rain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 535} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536} In notes the editor gives lines before and after it in al-A'sha's poem with sources, and long explanations of words, among them al-Hazn as a place in the land of Banu Asad and Banu Yarbu', the musk pods, the flower, the cloak of growth and the evening; these explanations are the editor's, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 535} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536}

The word for the raised ground has three dialect forms, and a group of reciters has read each of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536} One has the first letter with a damma, which most reciters of Medina, the Hijaz and Iraq read; one with a fatha, read by some of Syria and some of Kufa and said to be a dialect of Tamim; and one with a kasra, which Ibn Abbas is reported to have read. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536} Al-Tabari's choice. Abu Jafar says it is not permitted for him to read it except in one of two forms, fatha or damma, since people's reading in their cities is in one of them, and he much prefers the damma, as the better known of the two dialects among the Arabs; as for the kasra, the abandonment of reading with it shows it is not permissible. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536} It is called the raised ground because it rose and grew thick and high, from the saying that a thing rose if it swelled and grew large; this is al-Tabari's account. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536}

Interpreters spoke along the lines of al-Tabari. Mujahid (6074): the raised ground is the open, level place, and (6075) the level, elevated land. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 536} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 537} Qatada (6076): on a rise of the land; al-Dahhak (6077): the elevated place in which rivers do not run and in which there are gardens; al-Suddi (6078): on a hillock of the land; al-Rabi' (6079): a rise of the land; Ibn Abbas (6080): the elevated place in which rivers do not run. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 537} Others used to say it is level ground; al-Hasan (6081): level land that rises above the waters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 537} The editor notes that in al-Dahhak's report he kept the printed text's wording because it agrees with al-Durr al-Manthur, gives the right sense and comes right in report 6080. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 537} Al-Tabari's explanation of the downpour striking begins at the foot of p. 537 and we stop before it. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's choice and account are attributed to him, and the verse explanations and corrections are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-265|2:265]]) from p. 537. Al-Tabari says that the downpour struck the garden on the rise of the land as heavy, great drops of rain, and that its yielding its fruit twofold means it doubled its fruit when the downpour struck it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 538} The word for fruit eaten is the thing eaten, on the noun pattern with a damma on each of the first two letters, like terror and mockery, whereas the same word with a fatha on the first letter and no vowel on the second is the act of the eater, as one says I ate an eating and one single meal; he cites a line in which its speaker says that a meal is not worth plunder and a hunger no punishment, and explains that the fatha is there because it means the act, as the mention of hunger shows, and that were the first letter given a damma it would mean the food one ate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 538} In notes the editor says that al-Tabari did not mention the damma on the second letter of the word, which is the reading of his own copy of the Qur'an, that the verse's speaker was Abu Mi'ras of Hudhayl and that it is in al-Shajari's Hamasa with lines before it boasting of generosity, points out a copyist's mistake in a word, and explains the word for punishment as lasting torment; these remarks are the editor's, not al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 538}

Al-Tabari explains the next clause: the light rain is dew and the gentle rain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 539} Ibn Abbas (6082), via Ata al-Khurasani: dew; al-Suddi (6083): dew; Qatada (6084): a drizzle, meaning light rain; al-Dahhak (6085): the drizzle of rain, meaning its gentle kind; al-Rabi' (6086): drizzle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 539}

Al-Tabari then says God means by this likeness that as the fruit of this garden He described was doubled when the downpour poured, if the downpour missed it the dew would do the same; so God doubles the charity of the giver and the one who spends his wealth seeking His pleasure and out of his own soul's trust, without reproach or harm, whether his spending was little or much, it does not fail or go unrepaid, as the garden described is doubled whether little or much rain strikes it, its good never failing in any case; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 539} A group of interpreters said the like. Al-Suddi (6087): as the fruit of that garden was doubled, so the fruit of this spender is doubled twice; Qatada (6088): this is a likeness God drew for the believer's work, whose good has no failure in any case, whether downpour or dew; al-Dahhak (6089): this is the likeness of one who spent his wealth seeking God's pleasure; al-Rabi' (6090): this is a likeness God drew for the believer's work. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 539} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 540}

Al-Tabari raises a question: how was it said in the form of a condition when this is a report about a past matter? He answers that a verb to be is understood in it, so the sense is: it gave its fruit twofold, and if the downpour did not strike it, dew struck it; like someone saying I kept two horses, and if I did not keep two then one at its value, meaning unless I keep two, a verb to be having to be implied since it is a report; and he cites a line of Za'ida ibn Sa'sa'a al-Faq'asi about lineage; this answer is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 540} The editor refers in notes to al-Farra''s Ma'ani al-Qur'an and to the earlier attribution of the line. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 540} These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's explanation and answer are attributed to him, and the notes are the editor's, with no verdict from the museum.

The close of ([[passage/quran-2-265|2:265]]) on p. 541. Al-Tabari says the sense of the verse's closing description of God is that He sees what you do, people, in your spending, nothing of it or of your deeds in it or elsewhere being hidden from Him; He knows the one among you who spends with reproach and harm and the one who spends seeking His pleasure and out of his soul's trust, and He reckons it all for you until He requites every one of you for his deed, good for good and evil for evil. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 541} By this He means a warning against His punishment in spending and other acts, that any of His creatures do what was earlier forbidden or neglect what was commanded, since that is within God's sight and hearing, He knows and counts it against them, and He is watching His creatures; this is his explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 541} The editor notes that he printed a different word from the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 541}
