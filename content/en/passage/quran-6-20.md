## Summary
Verse 20 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 20 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari says: those "to whom we gave the book", the Torah and the Gospel, know that he is one god, not a company of gods, and that Muhammad is a sent prophet, "as they know their sons". The words "who have lost themselves" are an epithet of the first "alladhina", and by "lost themselves" he means they ruined themselves and cast themselves into the fire of Hell by denying Muhammad as God's sent messenger while knowing the truth of it, "so they do not believe", that is, through that loss of themselves they do not believe. It has been said that "their losing themselves" means that every servant has a dwelling in Paradise and a dwelling in the Fire; on the day of resurrection God gives the people of Paradise the dwellings of the people of the Fire in Paradise, and the people of the Fire the dwellings of the people of Paradise in the Fire, and that is the loss of those who lose, for they sold their dwellings in Paradise for the dwellings of the people of Paradise in the Fire through what they neglected in the world of disobeying God and wronging themselves; and that is the sense of [[passage/quran-23-11|23:11]], on those who inherit Paradise, abiding in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 294}

The editor refers for "lost" to p. 281 and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

The interpreters said the like: report 13130, from Qatada: they know that Islam is God's religion and Muhammad God's messenger, finding him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. 13131, from him: the Christians and Jews know God's messenger in their book as they know their sons; al-Tabari refers to the interpretation of the like of this verse in [[passage/quran-2-146|2:146]]. 13132, from al-Suddi: they know the Prophet as they know their sons, because his description is with them in the Torah. 13133, from Ibn Jurayj: he means the Prophet; the people of Medina claim, from People of the Book who became Muslim, that they said: by God, we know him better than our sons, from the description and attribute we find... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 295}

At 13132 the editor notes that the report is cut short in both manuscript and print, that the bracketed addition comes from the Durr al-Manthur, from al-Suddi's commentary as transmitted by Abu al-Shaykh, and that the lack appears old in the copies of Abu Jafar's commentary, since al-Suyuti's copy was also cut short here.

We stop here at the foot of p. 295, within report 13133 from Ibn Jurayj on "those to whom we gave the book know him as they know their sons", at "from the description and attribute we find", which continues on p. 296.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
