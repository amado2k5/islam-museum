**Commentary on verse 41 (pp. 94-95)**

On [[passage/quran-10-41|10:41]] he says to His Prophet: if these polytheists deny you and reject what you brought from your Lord, say to them, I have my religion and my deed and you have yours; your deed does not harm me and mine does not harm you, and each doer is repaid for his deed. "You are free of what I do" means you are not held to account for its offence, and "I am free of what you do" means I am not held to account for yours; this is like what is said in [[passage/quran-109-1|109:1]] to [[passage/quran-109-3|109:3]]. The editor refers to the gloss on "free of". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 94}

It is said that this verse was abrogated by fighting and the command to fight. Report 17662, from Ibn Zayd: He commanded him this, then abrogated it and commanded him to strive against them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 95}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
