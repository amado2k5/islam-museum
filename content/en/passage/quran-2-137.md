## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "if they believe in the like of what you believe in, they are guided" al-Tabari says: if the Jews and Christians hold true God, what was sent down to you, what was sent down to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the tribes, what was given to Moses and Jesus and what was given to the prophets from their Lord, as you, believers, have held true, they have been granted success and have kept to the path of truth, and are then of you and you of them by entering your religion. By this verse God showed that He accepts no work from anyone except with belief in these things; from Ibn Abbas, belief is the firmest handhold and God accepts no deed without it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 113}

He mentions a reading reported from Ibn Abbas which the Muslims' copies do not have and which the readers agreed to leave: he said do not say "bi-mithli ma amantum bihi", for God has no like, but say "bi'lladhi amantum bihi" or "bi-ma amantum bihi". Al-Tabari says that understood as believing in a like of God this would be plain polytheism, if the report is sound from Ibn Abbas, but the sense is otherwise: the likeness falls between the two acts of belief and acknowledgement, theirs and yours, not between the things believed in, as one says "'Amr passed your brother like my passing him". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 114}

On "and if they turn away, they are only in schism" he says: if those who said "be Jews or Christians" turn away and do not believe like your belief, and divide between God's messengers, believing some and disbelieving others, know, believers, that they are in disobedience, separation and war against God, His Messenger and you; from Qatada and al-Rabi', "shiqaq" is separation, and from Ibn Zayd, separation and war, reciting "and whoever breaks with the Messenger". For al-Tabari the root of "shiqaq" is "it was hard on him", when something distressed and harmed him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 115} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 116}

On "God will suffice you against them, and He is the Hearing, the Knowing" he says: God will suffice you, Muhammad, against those among the Jews and Christians who said to you and your companions "be Jews or Christians" if they turn away, whether by the sword, by exile from your neighbourhood or other punishments, for God hears what they say to you with their tongues and knows the envy and hatred they hide. He says God did that to them soon and fulfilled His promise, sufficing His Prophet by giving him power over them, so that some were killed, some exiled and some humbled with the jizya and abasement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 116}
