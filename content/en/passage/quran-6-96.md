## Summary
Verse 96 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 96 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "splitter of the dawn; he made the night for rest and the sun and moon for reckoning; that is the ordaining of the mighty, the knowing" al-Tabari says: by "splitter of the dawn" he means splitter of the pillar of the morning from the night's darkness and blackness, "isbah" being a verbal noun from "we reached the morning". 13595, from al-Dahhak: the lighting of the morning; 13596 and 13597, from Mujahid: the lighting of dawn; 13598, from Qatada: splitter of the morning; 13599, from Ibn Abbas: by "isbah" he means the light of the sun by day and the light of the moon by night; 13600 and 13601, from Mujahid: splitter of the morning, the lighting of the morning; 13602, from Ibn Zayd: the splitting of the dawn from the night; 13603, from al-Dahhak: creator of light, the light of day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 555}

The editor refers for "splitting" to a recent earlier passage.

Others said the sense is creator of night and day. 13604, from Ibn Abbas: he created night and day. It is reported from al-Hasan al-Basri that he read "faliq al-asbah" with a fatha on the alif, as though he took it as a plural of "subh", meaning the morning of every day, making it "asbah", and we have not heard of any other who read so; the reading we do not allow ourselves to pass beyond is with kasra on the alif, "faliq al-isbah", for the consensus of the authority of readers and interpreters on its soundness and rejection of the contrary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 556}

The editor says "faliq al-isbah" is the reading of the Hijazis as will be mentioned later, so he left the report on their reading, and that the printed text has "we do not allow other than it" in place of the manuscript's entirely right wording.

As for "he made the night for rest" the readers differed in its reading. Most readers of the Hijaz and Medina and some Basrans read "ja'il al-layl" with an alif in the form of the noun, nominative joined to "faliq", with "the night" genitive by annexation and "the sun and moon" accusative joined to the place of "the night", since the night, though genitive in form, is in the place of the accusative as the object of "ja'il", and it is good to join to the sense of the night and not its form because of the entry of "rest" between it and "the night"; the poet al-Farazdaq says of men sitting at the doors seeking a need, either a middling need or a fresh one, putting the second need in the accusative joined to the sense of the first need and not its form, because its sense is accusative though genitive in form. Something like this also comes with the second joined to the sense of what is before it and not its form even without anything between them, as another said in a line describing that while they were looking for a man he came to them with a milk-skin and a herdsman's flint. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 557}

The editor says print has "most readers of the Hijaz" and he kept the manuscript; that the line is al-Farazdaq's and its tracing and commentary came earlier, adding here Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, and that "sitting" in the nominative is reported there; that the second line is by a man of Qays Aylan, also attributed to Nusayb, in Sibawayh, al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, al-Sahibi and Sharh Shawahid al-Mughni, Sibawayh's version being "while we were seeking him" and "a hung milk-skin and a bag", print having "so while" with fa which he replaced with the manuscript's and "his flesh" an error; that "nirqibuh" means we wait for him, a "shakwa" a vessel like a bucket or small waterskin in which water is cooled and milk kept, a "wafda" a satchel like a quiver in which a herdsman carries his gear and provisions, and that he did not find the rest of the poem.

Most readers of Kufa read "ja'ala al-layla sakanan wa al-shams" in the form "fa'ala", the past verb, with the night accusative. Abu Jafar said: the right for us is to say that these are two widespread readings among the readers of the cities, agreeing in sense and not differing, so whichever the reader reads he is right in grammar and sense. He reported that he made the night for rest because everything that moves by day rests in it and is still in it, settling in its dwelling and shelter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 557}

On "and the sun and moon for reckoning" he says the interpreters differed. Some said the sense is he made the sun and moon run in their orbits by a reckoning. 13605, from Ibn Abbas: the number of days, months and years; 13606, from him: they run to a term set for them; 13607, from al-Suddi: by reckoning; 13608, from al-Rabi: the sun and moon are in a reckoning, and when their days are done that is the end of time and beginning of the greatest terror, "that is the ordaining of the mighty, the knowing"; 13609, from Qatada: they turn in a reckoning; 13610, from Mujahid: it is like [[passage/quran-36-40|36:40]], "each swims in an orbit", and like [[passage/quran-55-5|55:5]], "the sun and moon are by reckoning". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 559}

Others said the sense is he made the sun and moon light. 13611, from Qatada: that is, light. Abu Jafar said: the better of the two views in interpretation for being right is that of those who interpreted it as he made the sun and moon run by a reckoning and number until their affair and the end of their terms is reached, and turn for the benefit of creation for which they were made. We said this the better of the two for the verse because God mentioned before it his favours to his creation and the greatness of his authority in splitting the dawn for them and bringing out plants and shoots from the grain and stone, and followed that with the creation of the stars for their guidance on land and sea, so his describing the running of the sun and moon for their benefit is likelier here than a mention of their lighting, which he already described before by "splitter of the dawn", and there is no point in repeating it in one verse for no meaning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 559}

"Husban" in Arab speech is the plural of "hisab", as "shuhban" is of "shihab", and it is said that "husban" here is a verbal noun from "I reckoned the reckoning, I reckon it, hisaban and husbanan", and it is reported from the Arabs "God has the reckoning of so-and-so", that is his reckoning. I think Qatada, in his interpretation as light, went to what is reported from Ibn Abbas on [[passage/quran-18-40|18:40]], "or send on it a husban from heaven", that it is fire, and so directed the interpretation of "the sun and moon for reckoning" to that, though this has nothing of that sense. As for "hibban" with a kasra on the ha, it is the plural of "husbana", a small pillow, and is not from the first two either; one says "I seated him on it". "Husbanan" is accusative by "he made". A Basran said the sense is "the sun and moon by reckoning", dropping the particle "bi" as dropped from [[passage/quran-6-117|6:117]], "God knows best who goes astray from his way", that is, knows better those who go astray from his way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 560}

The editor says Abu Jafar said "with kasra on the ha" while the dictionaries agree that it is with damma and do not mention a kasra here, and that the one who said what is reported of the Basran is al-Akhfash, as Lisan al-Arab shows.

On "that is the ordaining of the mighty, the knowing" he says: this act that he described himself as doing, splitting the dawn, making the night for rest and the sun and moon for reckoning, is the ordaining of him whose authority is mighty, so none who wants to harm, punish or take vengeance on him can escape him, the knowing of the good of his creatures and their governance, not the ordaining of the images and idols that neither hear nor see, nor understand anything nor reason, neither harm nor benefit, and which, if harm is intended, cannot... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 560}

We stop here at the foot of p. 560, in the middle of al-Tabari's sentence on "that is the ordaining of the mighty, the knowing" in verse 96, and he continues on p. 561.
