## Summary
Verse 32 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 32 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "Say: who has forbidden God's adornment which He brought out for His servants, and the good things of provision?", God tells His Prophet to say to these ignorant Arabs who strip when circling the House and forbid themselves what I made lawful of good provision: who has forbidden you, people, God's adornment which He created for His servants to adorn themselves with and beautify themselves with its clothing, and the lawful of God's provision with which He provided His creation for their food and drink? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 395}

The commentators differ on the meaning of "the good things of provision", after agreeing that "adornment" is what al-Tabari said. Some said "the good things of provision" here is meat, since they did not eat it while in ihram: he gives (14534) from al-Suddi that it is fat; (14535) from Ibn Zayd that when they performed hajj or umra they forbade themselves the sheep and what comes from it; (14536) from him that some people forbade what comes from the sheep, its milk, its clarified butter and its meat, and God said "say: who has forbidden God's adornment", the adornment being garments; and (14528) from Ibn Zayd that when they came to the House and circled it, the clothes they circled in became forbidden to them, and if they found someone to lend them clothes, well, otherwise they circled naked, and He said "who has forbidden God's adornment", that is, God's clothes which He brought out for His servants. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 396}

He gives (14537) from al-Hasan that when God sent Muhammad He said, this is a prophet, this is my chosen one, take him as your model: go the way of his path and manner; no doors were shut before him, no chamberlains stood before him, no platters were carried to him morning and evening, he sat on the ground and ate his food on the ground and licked his hand, wore coarse clothes, rode a donkey and took a rider behind him, and he used to say, whoever turns away from my sunna is not of me; al-Hasan said, how many turn away from his sunna and leave it! Then some rough wicked men, eaters of usury and plunder, whom my Lord has made foolish and hated, claim there is no blame on them in what they eat and drink and in decorating these houses, interpreting this verse, "who has forbidden God's adornment", whereas it was made for the allies of Satan, who have made it playgrounds of their bellies and private parts; this is from speech Sufyan did not memorise. The note says the report is in Abu Nu'aym's Hilyat al-Awliya in similar form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 397}

Others said rather that what is meant is what the Jahiliyya forbade of the bahira and sa'iba animals: he gives (14538) from Qatada that it is what the people of the Jahiliyya forbade themselves of their property, the bahira, sa'iba, wasila and ham; and (14539) from Ibn Abbas that the Jahiliyya forbade things God had made lawful, of clothing and other things, which is God's word in [[passage/quran-10-59|10:59]], "say: have you considered what God sent down to you of provision, of which you made some forbidden and some lawful?", and God sent down "say: who has forbidden God's adornment". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 398}

Then, on "Say: it is for those who believe in the life of this world, exclusively theirs on the Day of Resurrection", God tells His Prophet to say, Muhammad, to those I commanded you to say to, "who has forbidden God's adornment which He brought out for His servants, and the good things of provision?", when they were at a loss for an answer and did not know what to reply: God's adornment which He brought out for His servants and the good things of His provision are, in this world, for those who believed God and His Messenger and followed what was sent down to you from your Lord, though those who disbelieve in God and His Messenger and go against his Lord's command share in it in this world; and they are exclusively for those who believed in God and His Messenger on the Day of Resurrection, with no one who disbelieved in God and His Messenger and went against his Lord's command sharing in it then. The commentators said the like: he gives (14540) from Ibn Abbas that the Muslims shared the good things with the disbelievers, so they ate of the good foods and wore the best of the clothes and married the good women among them, and they will have them exclusively on the Day of Resurrection; (14541) the like from him, that the Muslims share the good things with the idolaters in the life of this world, and then God makes the good things exclusive in the Hereafter for those who believed, with the idolaters having nothing in them; and (14542) from him that the sense is: say, it is exclusively in the Hereafter for those who believed in Me in the world, no one sharing it with them in the Hereafter, since adornment in this world is for all children of Adam, and He made it exclusively for His friends in the Hereafter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 399}

He gives (14543) from al-Dahhak that the Jews and Christians share it with you in the world and it is exclusively for those who believed on the Day of Resurrection; (14544) from al-Hasan that it is exclusively for the believers in the Hereafter, the disbelievers not sharing it with them, while in the world they share it; (14545) from Qatada that whoever acted with faith in the world, God's honour is made exclusively his on the Day of Resurrection, and whoever left faith in the world comes to his Lord with no excuse; (14546) from al-Suddi that the idolaters share it with them and it is exclusively for those who believed on the Day of Resurrection; (14547) from al-Dahhak that the idolaters share with the believers in the world in clothing, food and drink, and on the Day of Resurrection clothing, food and drink become exclusively the believers' and the idolaters have no share in any of it; (14548) from Ibn Jurayj that of the world the believer and the disbeliever both receive, but the good of the Hereafter is exclusively the believers' and the disbeliever has no share in it; and (14549) from Ibn Zayd that this is for those who believed on the Day of Resurrection, the people of disbelief not sharing it with them, though they share it with them in the world, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 400}

and when the Day of Resurrection comes they have in it neither little nor much. Sa'id ibn Jubayr said of it, and (14550) is given from him, that it means they benefit from it in the world and its sin does not follow them; the note says Isma'il ibn Aban al-Warraq is trustworthy and truthful in narration. The readers differ on "exclusively": some readers of Medina read it in the nominative, meaning say, it is exclusively for those who believed, and the other readers of the lands read it in the accusative, as a state from "they", its mention being left out as the evident wording suffices, as he described in explaining the speech: say, it is for those who believed in the life of this world shared, and for them in the Hereafter exclusive; and whoever reads it so makes the predicate of "it" the words "for those who believed". Al-Tabari says the better of the two readings to him is the accusative, since the Arabs prefer the accusative for what follows a noun and its qualifier, though the nominative is permissible, but the accusative is more frequent in their speech. The note refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 401}

Then, on "thus We explain the signs for a people who know", God says: as I made clear to you what is required of you about clothing and adornment, and the lawful and forbidden of foods and drinks, and distinguished between them for you, people, so I make clear all My proofs, evidences, signs of My lawful and forbidden and My rulings, for a people who know what is made clear to them and understand what is distinguished for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 402}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
