**Commentary on verse 5 (pp. 517-523)**

On "We sent Moses with Our signs: bring your people out of the darknesses into the light, and remind them of the days of God; surely in that are signs for every steadfast, grateful one", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: We sent Moses with Our proofs and arguments before you, Muhammad, as We sent you to your people with the like of them in proofs and arguments. Then report 20561 begins with its chain, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih; the editor notes that the symbol for a change of chain is used for the first time in this manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 517}

After the chain of report 20561: from Mujahid on God's saying "We sent Moses with Our signs", he said, with the clear proofs. Report 20562, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: "We sent Moses with Our signs", he said, the nine signs, the flood and what came with it. Reports 20563 and 20564, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj, and al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "We sent Moses with Our signs", he said, the nine clear proofs. On "bring your people out of the darknesses into the light", as We sent down this Book to you, Muhammad, that you may bring people out of the darknesses into the light by their Lord's leave; and by "bring your people out of the darknesses into the light" He means call them from error to guidance and from disbelief to faith. Report 20565, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "We sent Moses with Our signs: bring your people out of the darknesses into the light", he says, from error to guidance. Report 20566, from Qatada, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Hisham, Amr and Sa'id: the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 518}

On "and remind them of the days of God" He says, mighty and exalted: exhort them with the favours past upon them in the days gone by; the mention of "the days" suffices for the favours meant, because those were days known to them in which God had favoured them with great favours, rescuing them from Pharaoh's people after they had been in humiliating torment, drowning their enemy Pharaoh and his people and making them inherit their land, homes and wealth. One of the Arabic scholars said: the meaning is, frighten them with the punishment that fell on Ad, Thamud and their like, and with the pardon of the others; he said it is in sense like your saying "take them with severity and gentleness". Another of them said: we have found in their speech evidence for naming favours "days", and then cited a verse of Amr ibn Kulthum in which he says his people have bright, long days in which they refused to submit to the king; he said it may be that he made them bright and long for their kindnesses to the people in them, and said this is evidence for whoever says "remind them of the days of God" means by God's favours; then he said it may be that the naming of them as bright is for their rising above the king and their refusal of him, so their days are bright for them and long for their enemies. The editor notes that this is undoubtedly the saying of Abu Ubayda, which Ibn al-Anbari transmits from him in his commentary on the seven long poems, and that the copy of Majaz al-Qur'an has lost something at the beginning of the commentary on Sura Ibrahim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 519}

Abu Ja'far says: the one who said this has no ground for holding that this verse is evidence that "the days" means favours, because Amr ibn Kulthum described the days he described as "bright" for the might of his clan in them and their refusing the king submission to him in obedience, like people's saying "so-and-so never had a white day", meaning he never had a day remembered for good. As for his describing them as long, days are described as long only in hardship, as al-Nabigha said at the opening of his poem, asking to be left to a toilsome care and a night he endures whose stars are slow; Amr described them as long only for the severity of what they brought against his people's enemies, and there is no other ground for it than what I said. The interpreters said the same: report 20567, from Mujahid, through Yahya ibn Talha al-Yarbu'i, Fudayl ibn Iyad and Layth: "remind them of the days of God", he said, by God's favours. Report 20568, from Mujahid, through Ishaq ibn Ibrahim, Yahya ibn Yaman, Sufyan and Ubayd al-Muktib: "remind them of the days of God", he said, by God's favours. Report 20569, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Ubayd al-Muktib: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 520}

the same. Report 20570, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Abthar and Husayn: the same. Reports 20571 to 20573, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, al-Harith, al-Husayn and Waraqa; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "the days of God", he said, God's favours. Report 20574, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl: "remind them of the days of God", he said, of the favours with which He favoured them: He rescued them from Pharaoh's people, split the sea for them, shaded them with the clouds and sent down on them manna and quails. Report 20575, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad and Habib ibn Hassan: by God's favours; the editor notes that this Habib is Habib ibn Abi al-Ashras or Habib ibn Abi Hilal, rejected in hadith and abandoned. Report 20576, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "remind them of the days of God", he says, remind them of God's favours upon them. Report 20577, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 521}

"remind them of the days of God", he said, by God's favours. Report 20578, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "remind them of the days of God", he said, His days on which He took vengeance on the disobedient among the nations, frightening them with them, warning them of them and reminding them that what befell those before them might befall them. Report 20579, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b from the Prophet, through al-Muthanna, al-Himmani, Muhammad ibn Aban, Abu Ishaq, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibn Abbas: "remind them of the days of God", he said, God's favours; in a long note the editor says this is Abu Ja'far's chain, that Abd Allah ibn Ahmad related it in his additions to the Musnad through Yahya ibn Abd Allah, freedman of Banu Hashim, who has been spoken against, that Abd Allah ibn Ahmad also related it as a statement of a Companion, that Ibn Kathir said the latter is the more likely, and that the basis of these chains is Muhammad ibn Aban al-Ju'fi, of whom his poor memory and weakness have been said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 522}

Report 20580, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri and Ubayd Allah: "remind them of the days of God", he said, by God's favours. On "surely in that are signs for every steadfast, grateful one" he says: in the days that passed with His favours upon them, meaning upon Moses's people, "signs", meaning lessons and admonitions, "for every steadfast, grateful one", for everyone patient in obedience to God and grateful to Him for the favours He gave. Report 20581, from Qatada, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Hisham, Amr and Sa'id: "surely in that are signs for every steadfast, grateful one", he said, an excellent servant is a servant who, when tried, is patient and, when given, is grateful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 523}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
