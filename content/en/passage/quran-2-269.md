## Summary
Verse 269 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 269 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-269|2:269]]), pp. 576 to 578. Al-Tabari explains hikma here as hitting on the right in word and deed, which God gives to whom He wills among His servants, and whoever is given it has been given much good; he then says the interpreters differed on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 576}

First view: hikma is the Qur'an and understanding of it. 6177, Ibn Abbas: knowledge of the Qur'an, its abrogating and abrogated, firm and ambiguous, earlier and later, lawful and forbidden, and its parables; 6178, Qatada: the Qur'an and understanding in the Qur'an; 6179, Qatada: understanding in the Qur'an; 6180, Abu al-Aliya: the Book and comprehension of it, the editor giving biographies of its narrators al-Hilali, Muslim ibn Ibrahim, Mahdi ibn Maymun and Shu'ayb ibn al-Habhab with the imams' remarks on them, and noting that the print had a different wording from the manuscript, both being sound; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 576} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 577} 6181, Mujahid: it is not prophethood but the Qur'an, knowledge and understanding; 6182, Ibn Abbas: understanding in the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 577}

Second view: hikma is hitting on the right in word and deed. 6183, 6184 and 6185, Mujahid: it is that, and in 6185 it is the Book, He gives its hitting on the right to whom He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 577}

Third view: knowledge of the religion. 6186, Ibn Zayd: reason in religion, and he recited the verse; 6187, Ibn Zayd: reason; and Ibn Wahb asked Malik what wisdom is (6188), and he said knowing the religion, understanding it and following it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 578}

Fourth view: hikma is comprehension. Ibrahim al-Nakha'i (6190): comprehension; the editor gives the narrator Abu Hamza al-A'war al-Qassab, a companion of Ibrahim, quoting al-Bukhari that he is not strong and is weak, and Ibn Adi that his reports from Ibrahim in particular are not corroborated; these assessments are the editor's and those he quotes, not al-Tabari's. The printed number of the report between 6188 and 6190 is not legible in the scan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 578}

Fifth view: it is fear of God. 6191, al-Rabi ibn Anas: the fear of God, because the head of everything is fear of God, and he cited the verse on the scholars in ([[passage/quran-35-28|35:28]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 578} We stop at 6191; al-Tabari's choice among these views is not reached in the pages read. The sayings are transmitted as they came, the biographies and gradings are the editor's, with no museum verdict.
