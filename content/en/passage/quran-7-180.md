**Commentary on verse 180 (pp. 281-285)**

Al-Tabari says: "to God belong the most beautiful names", which are as Ibn Abbas said in report 15451: among His names are the Mighty and the Compeller, and all His names are beautiful. Report 15452, from Abu Hurayra from the Messenger of God: God has ninety-nine names, a hundred less one; whoever counts them all will enter Paradise. The editor says its chain is sound, identifies Hisham ibn Hassan al-Qardusi as trustworthy and narrated from by the compilers of the group, and notes that al-Bukhari gave it by the route of Abu al-Zinad from al-A'raj from Abu Hurayra, with Ibn Hajar explaining it exhaustively, that Muslim gave it, and that Ahmad gave it in his Musnad by several routes, in some of which there is the addition "God is odd and loves the odd". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 281} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 282}

As for "and leave those who deviate in His names", it means the polytheists, and their deviation in God's names was that they turned them from what they are, naming their gods and idols by them, adding to them and subtracting from them: they named some of them al-Lat, derived by them from the name of God, and some al-Uzza, derived from the name of God al-Aziz. Report 15453, from Ibn Abbas: the deviation of the deviators was that they called al-Lat among God's names; report 15454, from Mujahid: they derived al-Uzza from al-Aziz and al-Lat from Allah. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 282} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 283}

The interpreters differed over "yulhidun". Some said it means they deny, and report 15455, from Ibn Abbas, says deviation is denial; others said they associate, and report 15456, from Qatada, says they associate. Al-Tabari says the root of "ilhad" in Arabic speech is turning from the middle course, swerving from it and turning away, then it is used for anything crooked and not straight, which is why the niche of the grave is called a "lahd", since it is to one side and not in the middle; one says "alhada" and "lahada". The editor says the last verbal noun is seldom found in the dictionaries, so it should be noted. It is reported of al-Kisa'i that he distinguished between "ilhad" as turning from the middle course and "lahd" as leaning on something, and read everywhere in the Qur'an with damma on the first letter and kasra on the ha, except the verse in al-Nahl, [[passage/quran-16-103|16:103]], which he read with fatha on both, claiming it means leaning; while the other masters of Arab speech hold that the two have one meaning and are two dialects of one word. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 283} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 284}

The reciters differed over the reading: most reciters of Medina and some Basrans and Kufans read "yulhidun" with damma and kasra, from "alhada", and most Kufan reciters read "yalhadun" with fatha on both, from "lahada". Al-Tabari says they are two dialects of one meaning so whichever the reader uses is right, but he prefers the reading with damma, the dialect of those who say "alhada", as the better known and more eloquent. Ibn Zayd used to say that "leave those who deviate in His names" is abrogated, and report 15457 says: these are the people of unbelief, and it was abrogated by fighting. The editor says the verse in al-Nahl is on al-Kisa'i's reading with fatha, which is the reading of most Kufan reciters. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 284}

Al-Tabari answers Ibn Zayd: there is no sense in his saying it is abrogated, for "leave those who deviate in His names" is not a command from God to His prophet to leave the polytheists to say it until He permits him to fight them, but a threat from God to those who deviate in His names and a warning to them, as He says elsewhere "leave them to eat and enjoy and be diverted by hope", [[passage/quran-15-3|15:3]], and "that they may disbelieve in what We gave them and enjoy themselves, for soon they will know", [[passage/quran-29-66|29:66]]; it is speech in the form of a command with the sense of warning and threat, and its meaning is: give respite, Muhammad, to those who deviate in God's names until a term they will reach, and soon they will be repaid, when the term God set for them comes, for their deeds before that, unbelief in God, deviation in His names and denial of His messenger. In a note the editor remarks that al-Tabari slipped here from the plural pronoun to the singular and that he left it as it stands. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 285}
