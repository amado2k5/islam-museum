## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "originator of the heavens and the earth" al-Tabari says "badi'" is "mubdi'", a "mufil" form turned into "fa'il", like "mu'lim" into "alim"; the "mubdi'" is the one who brings into being and originates what no one preceded him in; hence one who innovates in religion is called a "mubtadi'". He cites a verse of al-A'sha and one of Ru'ba ibn al-Ajjaj. He says the sense is: glory be to God, how could He have a son when He owns what is in the heavens and the earth, which bear witness to Him by their pointing to His oneness and confess obedience to Him, He being their maker and creator without a source or a model, and that the One who originated the heavens and the earth without a source and without a model is the One who originated the Messiah without a father, by His power. He reports this from al-Rabi' (He originated their creation and none shared in it) and al-Suddi (He originated them and created them, nothing before them to take as a model), the editor noting a correction in the text of al-Suddi's report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 540} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 541}

On "when He decrees a matter, He only says to it 'Be', and it is" al-Tabari says: "qada amran" means He made firm and decided a matter; the root of "qada'" is firmness and completion, whence the judge is called "qadi" for his deciding the case and finishing it, and the dead is said to have "qada", having finished with the world, and "the day was spent" when it ended; he gives from the Qur'an "your Lord decreed that you worship none but Him", meaning He decided the ruling by commanding them, and "We decreed to the Children of Israel in the Book", meaning We informed them and finished with them, and cites Abu Dhu'ayb's verse on the two coats of mail "which Dawud made, or Tubba' made the full ones", meaning he made them firm, and a verse said in praise of Umar ibn al-Khattab (the editor gives in a note the attributions and differences over this verse and explains its words). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 542} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 543}

He continues: "He only says to it 'Be', and it is" means that when He makes a matter firm He says to it "be" and it is as God commanded and willed. Al-Tabari puts the question: at what point does He say "be" to the matter, when it does not exist, when a command can only be to one commanded, or when it exists, being already there? He then says the interpreters disputed it, and sets out their views and reasons. Some said it is a report of His decided command for those of His existing creatures He decrees upon, that when He commands them His decree takes effect on them, like His command to those of the Children of Israel to be apes, abased, they being present when so commanded, and like the one swallowed with his house by the earth; these take the verse as particular rather than general. Others said the verse is general in its plain sense and no one may turn it to an inner sense without proof, God knowing all that will be before it is, so things not yet existing but certain by His knowledge are like those that exist, and He may say to them "be" and bid them come out of non-existence into existence, for their being pictured to Him and known to Him in non-existence. Others said it is general in form but particular in meaning, since a command can only be to one commanded, so it means: when He decrees a matter such as giving life to the dead or death to the living, He says to a living thing "be dead" or to a dead one "be alive". Others said it reports of all that He brings forth that when He decrees, creates and brings it forth, it comes to be and exists, with no speech there by this group's view but the existence of the created and the coming about of the decreed, like one saying "so-and-so spoke with his head" or "with his hand" when he moved it and said nothing, citing verses of Abu al-Najm and Amr ibn Humama al-Dawsi and the rajaz "the basin filled and said 'enough'". The editor refers to explanations of these verses. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 543} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 544} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 545} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 546}
