**Commentary on verse 1 (pp. 511-512)**

Here al-Tabari begins the commentary on the sura in which Ibrahim is mentioned. On "Alif Lam Ra. A Book We have sent down to you that you may bring people out of the darknesses into the light, by their Lord's leave, to the path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy", he says: we have already explained the meaning of "Alif Lam Ra", which spares repeating it here. As for "a Book We sent down to you", its meaning is: this is a Book We sent down to you, Muhammad, meaning the Qur'an, "that you may bring people out of the darknesses into the light", that is, that you may guide them by it from the darknesses of error and disbelief to the light of faith and its brightness, and that by it you may show the ignorant and blind the ways of right guidance. "By their Lord's leave" means by their Lord's enabling them to that and His kindness to them, "to the path of the Almighty, the Praiseworthy" means to God's straight way, which is His religion that He approved and laid down for His creation. The editor notes that he passed over the explanation of "the Almighty". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 511}

"Al-Hamid" is a "fa'il" form turned from "maf'ul", and its meaning is the One praised for His favours. He, mighty be His praise, attributed the bringing out of people from the darknesses into the light by their Lord's leave to His Prophet, who is the guide of His creation and the one who enables those of them he loves to faith, since the calling to it and the setting out of what is for them and against them come from him. This shows the soundness of the saying of the Ahl al-Ithbat, who attribute the acts of servants to the servants as acquisition and to God, mighty be His praise, as bringing into being and ordering, and the unsoundness of the saying of the Ahl al-Qadar, who denied that God has any making in it; the editor explains that the Ahl al-Ithbat are the Sunnis who affirm the attributes and that the Ahl al-Qadar are the Mu'tazila and those who denied destiny. The interpreters said the same: report 20559, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "that you may bring people out of the darknesses into the light", that is, from error to guidance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 512}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
