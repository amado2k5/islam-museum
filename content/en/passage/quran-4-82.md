## Summary
Verse 82 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 82 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse (p. 567).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: will those who plan other than what you say to them, Muhammad, not reflect on God's book, so as to know God's proof against them in obeying you and following your command, and that what you brought them of revelation is from their Lord, through the harmony of its meanings, agreement of its rulings, the way part supports part by confirmation and part bears witness to part by verification; were it of any origin but God its rulings would differ, its meanings clash, and part of it would show up the corruption of another part. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 567}

**Reports 9987-9988 (p. 567, notes 1-3).** Bishr b. Mu'adh (9987, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that God's word does not differ and is true with no falsehood in it, while the word of people differs. Yunus (9988, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the Qur'an does not give the lie to itself or contradict itself, and what people fail to understand of it comes from the shortfall of their minds and their ignorance; he recited the clause on differing, and then said it is right for the believer to affirm that it all comes from God and to believe in what is ambiguous in it, and not to strike one part against another; and when he is ignorant of a matter and does not know it, he should say that what God said is true, and know that God did not say a thing and then contradict it, and it is fitting... (the report breaks off at the foot of p. 567 and continues on the next page). The editor says the print has a wrong word for "of a matter" and the manuscript is right, that print and manuscript drop the conjunction in "and when he is ignorant", which is coordinated with "it is right for the believer to say", and that the print has the wrong form of "contradict" and the manuscript is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 567}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
