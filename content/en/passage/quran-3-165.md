## Summary
Verse 165 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 165 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 165: the calamity and its cause (8179-8191, pp. 371-376).** Abu Ja'far: the meaning is: when a calamity struck you, believers, which was those of you who were killed at Uhud and the wounded, the idolaters having killed seventy that day, you had inflicted on the idolaters twice as much, namely what the Muslims inflicted on the idolaters at Badr, killing seventy of them and capturing seventy; you said, when the calamity at Uhud struck you: from what direction is this, and how did this reach us, when we are Muslims and they are idolaters, and God's prophet is among us receiving revelation from heaven, and our enemy are people of disbelief and association? Say, Muhammad, to the believers among your companions: this came upon you from yourselves, by your opposing my command and leaving obedience to me, not from anyone else; and God has power over all He wills for His creation of pardon, punishment, favour and vengeance, "powerful" meaning possessor of power (pp. 371-372). The notes refer to earlier explanations of "anna" and "qadir" (the numbers are garbled in the scanned text). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 371} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 372}

**The disagreement over "from yourselves" (pp. 372-376).** After unanimity on the rest of the verse the scholars differed on this clause. Some said it means your opposing the Prophet of God when he advised you not to go out to your enemy but to stay until they entered your city and were among your fortresses, and you refused and said, take us out to them so that we fight them outside the city. The note says "atam" is the plural of "utum", a fort built of stone in which the people of Medina lived and took shelter, and that "asharu" means they went out into open country. Qatada (8179), through Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id: the Muslims were struck at Uhud and seventy of them were killed, and they had inflicted twice that at Badr, killing seventy idolaters and capturing seventy; and it was mentioned to us that the Prophet said to his companions on the day of Uhud, when Abu Sufyan and the idolaters came: "I am in a strong shield", meaning Medina, "so leave the people to enter upon us and we fight them" (the note defines the "junna" as whatever armour one shelters behind, like any protection); some of the Ansar said to him: Prophet of God, we dislike being killed in the streets of Medina, and we used to refuse fighting in the pre-Islamic age, so in Islam we have more right to refuse it; so go out with us to them. The Messenger of God went and put on his armour (the editor states that the wording of the printed text and the manuscript in an earlier clause differs from what he adopted, and that his choice is as in al-Durr al-Manthur 2: 94). The men then blamed each other and said: the Prophet of God hinted at one thing and you at another; go, Hamza, and tell him our affair follows yours. Hamza came and told him, and the Messenger of God said: it is not for a prophet who has put on his armour to lay it down until he fights, and there will be a calamity among you. They asked: specific or general? He said: you will see it. (The note explains "la'ma" as the stout coat of mail and other implements of war.) Qatada then said: it was mentioned to us that the Prophet saw in a dream a cow being slaughtered, which he interpreted as killing among his companions; that his sword Dhu al-Fiqar was broken, which was the killing of his uncle Hamza, who was killed that day and was called Lion of God; and that a ram was slaughtered, which he interpreted as the ram of the battalion, 'Uthman ibn Abi Talha, who was struck down that day and carried the idolaters' banner (pp. 372-373). The editor notes that the manuscript and printed text have a meaningless word where "slaughtered" now stands, that he deduced the correct word and that the copyist had distorted it, that he did not find the report with this wording elsewhere, but that it is reported that the Prophet saw himself riding behind a ram and interpreted it as meaning he would kill the ram of the people, their protector and standard-bearer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 372} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 373}

Al-Rabi', as passed on by 'Ammar from Ibn Abi Ja'far from his father (8180), has it similarly, saying that "twice" means twice what was inflicted on them, and that the clause means by what you disobeyed. Qatada (8181), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar: the Muslims were struck at Uhud, having inflicted twice that at Badr in killed and captured. 'Ikrima (8182), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from 'Amr ibn 'Ata': the Muslims killed seventy idolaters at Badr and captured seventy, and the idolaters killed seventy Muslims at Uhud; they asked whence this came when they were Muslims fighting for God's sake and the others idolaters, and the answer was a punishment for their disobeying the Prophet when he said what he said (pp. 373-374). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 373} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 374}

Al-Hasan (8183), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Mubarak: they said this struck us only because we accepted ransom from the captives at Badr and disobeyed the Prophet at Uhud, so whoever of us was killed was a martyr and whoever remained was purified; we were content (the closing words are unclear in the scan, and the editor notes that the printed text's wording differs from the manuscript, as if its publisher had not understood it). Al-Hasan and Ibn Jurayj (8184), by the same route: their disobedience was that he told them not to pursue the enemy on the day of Uhud and they pursued. Al-Suddi (8185), through Muhammad, from Ahmad, from Asbat: he mentioned what befell the believers at Uhud, seventy killed, that at Badr they had captured seventy and killed seventy, their asking whence this was, and the answer that they had disobeyed. Ibn 'Abbas (8186), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, from his uncle: you inflicted on the idolaters at Badr twice what they inflicted on you at Uhud (p. 374). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 374}

Ibn Ishaq (8187), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: if a calamity struck you in your brethren, it was through your sins, and you had inflicted twice that on your enemy the day before at Badr in killed and captured, and you forgot your disobedience and your opposing what your Prophet commanded, and you brought it on yourselves; and God has power over whatever He wills for His servants of vengeance or pardon. In the notes: the printed wording of "from your enemy" is disturbed and the proofreader put a number for his doubt, the manuscript has an undotted word, and the right reading is from Ibn Hisham's Sira; the wording of the manuscript and the printed text in the clause on bringing it about differs from Ibn Hisham's transmission, which the editor preferred as better in the context; and the report is in the Sira (3: 125), a continuation of reports ending with 8178. Al-Dahhak (8188), through al-Husayn, from Abu Mu'adh, from 'Ubayd: you inflicted on the idolaters at Badr twice what they inflicted on you at Uhud (p. 375). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 374} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 375}

**The second view: the captives and the ransom (pp. 375-376).** Others said it means their taking the idolaters captive at Badr, accepting ransom and leaving off killing them (in the note the editor corrects the printed word for "your capturing", saying the copyist of the manuscript had run letters together). 'Ubayda al-Salmani (8189), through Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Fudayl, from Ash'ath ibn Siwar, from Ibn Sirin: the Muslims captured seventy idolaters and killed seventy, and the Messenger of God said: choose to take ransom from them, strengthening yourselves with it against your enemy and having seventy of you killed, or to kill them; they said: rather we take the ransom and have seventy of us killed; he said: so they took the ransom and seventy of them (the Muslims) were killed (the last clause is unclear in the scan); and 'Ubayda said: they asked for both good things. 'Ubayda (8190), through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Ibn 'Ulayya, from Ibn 'Awn, from Ibn Sirin, on the captives of Badr: the Messenger of God said: if you wish you kill them, and if you wish you ransom them and as many of you will be martyred; they said: rather we take the ransom and enjoy it, and as many of us will be martyred. 'Ali (8191), through al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Isma'il, from Ibn 'Awn, from Muhammad, from 'Ubayda al-Salmani, and by a second route through Hajjaj (the next name in this chain is garbled in the scan): Jibril came to the Prophet and said that God disliked what his people had done in taking captives, and had commanded him to give them a choice between two things, that they be brought forward and have their necks struck, or take ransom on condition that as many of them be killed; the Messenger of God summoned the people and told them, and they said: our clans and brothers! rather we take their ransom and strengthen ourselves for fighting our enemy and as many of us will be martyred, and there is nothing in that we dislike; and seventy men were killed of them, the number of the captives of Badr; the scanned text of p. 376 then breaks off in mid-sentence (the words "and there were killed of them on the day of U...") so the rest of the report does not appear in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 375} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 376}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 376}
