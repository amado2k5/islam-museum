## Summary
Verse 19 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 19 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 19 (pp. 168-173)**

Al-Tabari says this is a rebuke from God to a people who boasted of the watering and the custodianship of the House; He told them that boasting lies in belief in God and the last day and striving in His way, not in the custodianship and watering they boasted of, as the reports and the interpreters show. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 168}

Report 16557, from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir al-Ansari, through Abu al-Walid al-Dimashqi, al-Walid ibn Muslim, Mu'awiya ibn Sallam and his grandfather Abu Sallam al-Aswad: I was by the Messenger's pulpit with a group of his Companions, and one said he did not care to do no deed after Islam except to water the pilgrims, another said rather the tending of the Sacred Mosque, and another said striving in God's way is better than what you say; Umar rebuked them, saying not to raise their voices by the Messenger's pulpit, it being Friday, and that after the Friday prayer he would go in to the Messenger and ask about their dispute; he did so, and God sent down the verse. The editor gives the narrators, says Muslim gives it in his Sahih, Ibn Kathir attributes it to Abu Dawud and al-Suyuti adds further sources, and notes that another chain follows in report 16560. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 169}

Report 16558, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib said when captured at Badr that if the Muslims had preceded them in Islam, emigration and striving, they had tended the Sacred Mosque, watered the pilgrims and freed captives, and God said what He said, meaning that was in polytheism and He does not accept what was done in polytheism. Report 16559, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: the polytheists said that tending God's House and attending to the watering was better than one who believed and struggled, and they boasted of the sanctuary and were arrogant because they were its people and tenders; God recalled their arrogance and turning away at [[passage/quran-23-67|23:67]], saying they were arrogant about it, spending nights in talk and shunning the Qur'an and the Prophet; so He set belief in God and striving with His prophet above the polytheists' tending of the House and watering, and it did not profit them with God while they associated others with Him, and He called them wrongdoers for their polytheism, so the tending availed them nothing. Report 16560, from al-Nu'man ibn Bashir, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar and Yahya ibn Abi Kathir: a man said he did not care to do no deed after Islam except to water the pilgrims, another except to tend the Sacred Mosque, and another that striving was better; Umar rebuked them as before and the verse came down. The editor notes it is a report passed on by Yahya from al-Nu'man or from Abu Sallam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 171}

Report 16561, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar and Amr: it came down about Ali, al-Abbas, Uthman and Shayba, who spoke on this; al-Abbas said he would give up the watering, and the Messenger said to keep to it, for there was good in it for them; report 16562, from al-Sha'bi: about Ali and al-Abbas. Report 16563, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb and Abu Sakhr: Talha ibn Shayba of Banu Abd al-Dar, al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib and Ali ibn Abi Talib boasted; Talha said he was keeper of the House, with its key, and could spend the night in it if he wished; al-Abbas said he was in charge of the watering and could spend the night in the mosque; Ali said he did not know what they meant, he had prayed toward the qibla six months before the people and was a man of striving; and God sent down the verse. Report 16564, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: when it came down al-Abbas said he would give up their watering, and the Prophet told them to keep it, for there was good in it for them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 172}

Report 16565, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: Ali, al-Abbas and Shayba ibn Uthman boasted; al-Abbas said he was the best of them, watering the pilgrims of God's House, Shayba said he tended God's mosque, and Ali said he had emigrated with the Messenger and strove with him in God's way; and God sent down what is at [[passage/quran-9-20|9:20]]. Report 16566, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: the Muslims turned on al-Abbas and his companions captured at Badr, reproaching them for polytheism, and al-Abbas said by God they used to tend the Sacred Mosque, free captives, keep the House and water the pilgrims, and God sent down the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 172}

Al-Tabari says the sense of the sentence is: have you, people, made the watering of the pilgrims and the tending of the Sacred Mosque like the belief of one who believed in God and the last day and strove in God's way? These are not equal, nor are their conditions and standing with God balanced, for God accepts no deed without belief in Him and the last day; "and God does not guide the wrongdoing people" means He does not grant success in righteous deeds to one who disbelieves in Him and denies His oneness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 172}

Al-Tabari then explains that the noun is put in place of the verbal noun in the words "like one who believed in God" because the sense is known, citing a line by an unknown poet whose sense is that manhood is not the sprouting of down on the cheek but every generous young man, making "that" the predicate, as one says "generosity is Hatim, poetry is Zuhayr"; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 173}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
