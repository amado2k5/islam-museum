**Commentary on verse 94 (pp. 200-203)**

On [[passage/quran-10-94|10:94]] al-Tabari says God speaks to His prophet Muhammad: if you, Muhammad, are in doubt about the truth of what We chose you for and sent down to you, that the Children of Israel did not differ about your prophethood before you were sent as a messenger to His creation, because they find you written with them and know you by the description with which you are described in their book, the Torah and the Gospel, then 'ask those who read the Book before you', among the people of the Torah and Gospel such as Abd Allah b. Salam and the like of the truthful and believing among them, not the liars who deny you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 200}

Report 17886, from Ibn Abbas: the Torah and the Gospel, those of the People of the Book who reached Muhammad and believed in him; he says ask them if you are in doubt that you are written with them. Report 17887, from Ibn Zayd: it is Abd Allah b. Salam, who was of the People of the Book and believed in the Messenger of God. Report 17888, from Mujahid: they are the People of the Book. Report 17889, from al-Dahhak: meaning those of piety and belief among the People of the Book who reached the Prophet. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 201}

If someone says: was the Prophet in doubt about God's report that it is true and certain, for him to be told this? The reply is no, as a group of the people of knowledge said. Report 17890, from Sa'id b. Jubayr: the Prophet did not doubt and did not ask; report 17891, from him: he did not doubt and did not ask; report 17892, from Sa'id b. Jubayr and from Mansur from al-Hasan, likewise; reports 17893 and 17894, from Qatada: it was reported to us that the Prophet said, I do not doubt and I do not ask. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 202}

If it is then asked how this speech comes out, the reply is that al-Tabari has shown in several places in this book that the Arabs allow a man to say to his slave 'if you are my slave, then come to my command', the slave so commanded being one the master does not doubt is his slave; likewise a man says to his son 'if you are my son, be dutiful to me' without doubting that he is his son, and that this is sound and common in their speech, including [[passage/quran-5-116|5:116]], though God knew Jesus had not said that. This is of that kind: the Prophet was not in doubt about the truth and soundness of God's report, and God knew of his state, but He addressed him as his people address one another, since the Qur'an came down in their tongue. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 202}

As for 'the truth has come to you from your Lord', it is a fresh report from God: I swear that the certain truth has come to you of the report that you are God's messenger, and that these Jews and Christians know that this is so and find your description with them in their books; 'so do not be among the doubters' means those in doubt about its soundness and truth. And if someone said that this verse is addressed to the Prophet but means some whose insight into his prophethood was not sound though they professed belief with the tongue, to direct them to the place where the truth of his affair can be known, which removes the confusion from the heart, as in [[passage/quran-33-1|33:1]], the saying would not be rejected as unsound. The editor refers to al-Farra' and the earlier commentary on 'doubt'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 203}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
