**Commentary on verse 88 (pp. 177-184)**

On [[passage/quran-10-88|10:88]] al-Tabari says: Moses said: our Lord, You gave Pharaoh and the great men of his people and their nobles, the chiefs, adornment from the goods and furnishings of the world and wealth in the very gold and silver, in the life of this world, 'our Lord, that they may lead astray from Your path'; that is, You gave them what You gave so that they might stray from Your path. The readers differed. Some read it so that they lead people astray from Your path and bar them from Your religion; others read it so that they themselves stray from Your path and swerve from the way of guidance. The editor refers to al-Farra' and to the earlier commentaries on 'the chiefs' and 'adornment'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 177}

If someone asks: did God give Pharaoh and his people what He gave of the world's adornment and wealth so that they would lead people away from His religion, or so that they would stray from it? If He gave it for that, then they did what they were given it for, and they are not to be blamed for it. The reply: the meaning is other than what you imagine. The people of Arabic differed on the meaning of this lam: some of the Basran grammarians said the meaning is 'our Lord, so they strayed from Your path', as in [[passage/quran-28-8|28:8]], where Pharaoh's household did not pick him up in order that he become an enemy and a grief but picked him up and he became so, and this lam comes in the sense of outcome and end. Some of the Kufan grammarians, al-Farra' the editor says, said it is the lam of purpose, and the meaning is: our Lord, You gave them what You gave so that they would stray; then he prayed against them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 178}

Another said these lams in 'so that they lead astray' and 'to become for them an enemy' and the like are in the sense of the genitive: You gave them what You gave for their straying, and they picked him up for his becoming so, since matters ended there; the Arabs put the lam of purpose in the sense of the lam of the genitive and the lam of the genitive in the sense of the lam of purpose because the meanings are close, as in [[passage/quran-9-95|9:95]], meaning for your turning away, since they did not swear for the sake of your turning away, and he cites a line by a poet whose name is not known, of a man who rose without being fit to rise, though the one who wastes himself may be struck. Al-Tabari says the right view for him is that it is the lam of purpose, and the meaning is: our Lord, You gave them what You gave of the adornment of this life and wealth so that You try them with it and so that Your servants stray from Your path, as a punishment from You, as in [[passage/quran-72-16|72:16]] and [[passage/quran-72-17|72:17]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 179}

His words 'our Lord, efface their wealth and harden their hearts' are a prayer by Moses against Pharaoh and his chiefs that their wealth be changed from its form and altered to another state than the one it was in, as in [[passage/quran-4-47|4:47]], meaning before We change them from the form they have. One says 'I effaced his eye' of putting it out, and the Arabs use 'effacing' also for obliteration and decay, and for crumbling and wearing away; he cites a line of Ka'b b. Zuhayr about a she-camel on a road whose landmarks are wiped out and unknown. The interpreters differed here. A group said what al-Tabari said. Report 17820, from Abd Allah b. Kathir: it has reached us from al-Qurazi that he said make their sugar stones; report 17821, from Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi likewise. The editor says the number 17819 was dropped from the numbering by oversight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 179}

Report 17822, from Abu al-Aliya: make it stones; report 17823, from al-Rabi' b. Anas: it became stones; reports 17824 and 17825, from Qatada: it has reached us that their crops, or their tilled land, turned to stone; the printed text has a plural form of the word for tilled land and the editor kept the manuscript's; report 17826, from Sufyan: they say it became stones; report 17827, from Abu Salih: it became stones; report 17828, from Qatada: it has reached us that tilled lands of theirs became stones. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 180}

Report 17829, from al-Dahhak: God made it carved stones in the shape of what it had been; report 17830, from Ibn Zayd: it was done and it befell them: He effaced their wealth and it became stones, their gold and their silver coins and their lentils and everything. Others said rather the meaning is: destroy it. Report 17831, from Mujahid: destroy it; reports 17832 and 17833, from him likewise; report 17834, from Ibn Abbas: ruin them and destroy their wealth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 181}

As for 'harden their hearts', it means seal them so that they neither soften nor open to belief. Report 17835, from Ibn Abbas: Moses said before Pharaoh came 'our Lord, harden their hearts so that they do not believe until they see the painful punishment'; God answered him and came between Pharaoh and belief until the drowning overtook him, and belief did not help him; report 17836, from Ibn Abbas: seal their hearts until they see the painful punishment, which is drowning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 181}

Reports 17837, 17838 and 17839, from Mujahid on 'harden their hearts': with misguidance; report 17840, from al-Dahhak: destroy them as disbelievers. As for 'so that they do not believe until they see the painful punishment', the meaning is that they do not affirm God's oneness and acknowledge His unity until they see the agonising punishment. Reports 17841, 17842 and 17843, from Mujahid: that they do not believe in God in what they see of signs until they see the painful punishment. Report 17844 begins at the foot of the page. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on 'painful'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 182}

Report 17844, from Ibn Jurayj from Mujahid, likewise; report 17845, from al-Munqari on 'so that they do not believe': he prayed against them. The editor says the name is unpointed in the manuscript and he could not tell whom it means. The people of Arabic differed on the grammatical place of 'they believe': some of the Basran grammarians said it is in the subjunctive, since it answers a command with the particle fa', or since it is a prayer against them for having disobeyed, and it is reported of this speaker that he made it subjunctive by joining it to 'so that they lead astray from Your path'. Another of them, which is the view of the Kufan grammarians and of Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an, said its place is the jussive, as a prayer by Moses against them, meaning 'may they not believe', and he cites a line of al-A'sha, in a satire on Yazid b. Mus-hir al-Shaybani, praying that what has knotted between the addressee's eyes may not ease and that he meet him only with his nose in the dust. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 183}

Some Kufan grammarians, al-Farra' the editor says, held it is a prayer, as if he said 'O God, so let them not believe', and that if you like you may make it an answer to his request, since the request came in the form of a command, putting 'so let them not believe' in the subjunctive as an answer, though that is not easy; he cites a line of Abu al-Najm in which the poet bids his she-camel go on at an easy wide pace to Sulayman, so that they may rest, and says that an answer is not easy in a prayer since it is not a condition. Al-Tabari says the right view is that it is jussive as a prayer, meaning 'so let them not believe', and he chose it because what comes before is a prayer, 'our Lord, efface their wealth and harden their hearts', so that joining 'so let them not believe' to it, since it stands in its sequence, suits its meaning better. As for 'until they see the painful punishment', Ibn Abbas used to say the meaning is until they see the drowning; report 17846, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abbas: the drowning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 184}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
