## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "when they meet those who believe, they say: we believe", it reports of these Jews that when they met the believers they said "we believe", taking on the ways of the hypocrites. Ibn Abbas is reported to say they were a group of Jews who said this to the Prophet; through al-Dahhak, that they were the hypocrites among the Jews; in another report that they said they believed in your prophet but that he was sent to you alone; al-Suddi says they were people who believed and then dissembled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 249} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 250}
On "Do you tell them what God has opened to you?", the views differ: Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak, what God commanded you; through 'Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, that they said do not tell the Arabs this, for you used to seek victory over them by it; Abu al-'Aliya and Qatada, what God sent down in their book describing Muhammad, since if they said it the Arabs would use it against them; Mujahid has reports linked to the Jews of Banu Qurayza, in which they asked, when the Prophet addressed them with a harsh description, who told Muhammad this, the editor noting the speaker was the Prophet and not 'Ali; al-Suddi, what they were punished with; Ibn Zayd, that when asked about a matter in the Torah they would confirm it, and their leaders would ask why they told them, since they would use it against them, adding that they would come to Medina in the morning saying "we believe" and go back after the afternoon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 250} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 251} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 252} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 253}

Al-Tabari explains that "fath" in Arabic originally means victory, judgement and decision, as in "judge between us and our people in truth", so "what God has opened to you" means what God has judged and decreed upon you, among it their covenant to believe in Muhammad. By his own reasoning he prefers that it means what God opened to them in the sending of Muhammad, because the verse's first part reports their saying "we believe in Muhammad", so its last part parallels it, and their mutual blame was over telling the Muslims of his description in their books, which would be used against them. "Will you not reason?" is said by those blaming their fellows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 254} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 255} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 256}
