## Summary
Verse 145 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 145 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The written term (pp. 260-261).** Abu Ja'far says the meaning is that Muhammad and any other creature dies only after reaching the term God made the limit of his life; when he reaches what God wrote for him and God permits him to die, he dies, but before that he does not die by any plotter's scheme or trickster's ruse. Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq (7954): Muhammad has a term he will reach, and it will be when God permits (Ibn Hisham 3: 118, continuing 7940). It was also said the sense is that no soul would die without His permission (the editor attributes this to Abu Ubayda, Majaz al-Qur'an 1: 104). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 260}

**The accusative of the verbal noun (pp. 261-262).** The grammarians differ over what governs it.
- Some of the Basran grammarians: it is emphasis, in the accusative as "God wrote a writing with a set term"; the same applies to everything of this kind in the Qur'an (the editor lists the places where such examples occur: [[passage/quran-4-122|4:122]], [[passage/quran-10-4|10:4]], [[passage/quran-31-9|31:9]], [[passage/quran-18-82|18:82]], [[passage/quran-28-46|28:46]], [[passage/quran-44-6|44:6]], [[passage/quran-27-88|27:88]], [[passage/quran-4-24|4:24]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 261}
- Some of the Kufans: the sense is that God wrote the terms of souls, and the following word is brought out as an accusative from the meaning in the speech, since the clause already conveyed "wrote". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 261}
- Others of them: saying "Zayd is standing truly" means "I say Zayd is standing truly", since every speech is a saying, so the thing said stands in for the saying and what follows comes out of it; the same for "supposing" and "certainly". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 261}
- Abu Ja'far's own choice: all of these are accusative as verbal nouns from the meaning of the preceding speech, because the verbal nouns, whose wording differs from what precedes them, have their sense in what comes before, so their accusative comes from its meaning, not its wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 262}

**Seeking recompense in this world or the next (p. 262).** Abu Ja'far says the sense is: whoever of you believers seeks by his deed a reward of some worldly goods apart from the honour with God, God gives him from it what He apportioned him as provision for the days of his life, and then he has no share in the honour God prepared for those who obey Him and seek what is with Him in the hereafter; and whoever seeks the reward of the hereafter is given from the honour God reserved for the people of obedience. The wording thus goes out on this world and the next, and the meaning is what is in them. Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq (7955): whoever among you wants this world, with no desire for the hereafter, is given what was apportioned him of provision, and has no share in... and the report continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 262}

**Close of Ibn Ishaq's report (p. 263).** (7955) The report ends: whoever wants the reward of the hereafter is given what God promised him, along with what is sent to him of provision in this world. The editor says the report is in Ibn Hisham (3: 118), continuing 7954, with a large difference in wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 263}

**The reward of the thankful (p. 263).** Abu Ja'far says: I shall reward the one who thanked me for the kindness I showed him, through obeying me, stopping at my command and avoiding what I forbade, in the hereafter with the like of what I promised my friends of honour for their thanks. Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq (7956): that is the reward of the thankful, meaning God's giving him what He promised in the hereafter along with the provision sent to him in this world. The editor says the report is not in Ibn Hisham in this wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 263}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
