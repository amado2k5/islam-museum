## Summary
Verse 39 of [[passage/quran-7|Surat al-A'raf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 39 of 206 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "The first of them will say to the last: you have no superiority over us, so taste the punishment for what you used to earn", God says: the first of every nation and creed that preceded in the world will say to the last of them, those who came after them and arose after their time in it and took their way and followed their custom: "you have no superiority over us"; you knew what befell us of God's punishment for our disobedience of Him and our disbelief in His signs, after the messengers and warners came both to us and to you with that; so did you turn to obedience of God and restrain yourselves from your error and misguidance? The people's argument ran out and they were bested and could not give an answer by saying, our superiority over you is that we took warning from you and believed in God and affirmed His messengers; God said to all of them, taste, all of you, disbelievers, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 419}

His punishment for what you earned in the world of sins and acts of disobedience and committed of offences and crimes. The commentators said the like of what he said: he gives (14599) from Abu Mijlaz that it means, what is your superiority over us when it was shown you what was done to us and you were warned? and (14600) from al-Suddi that it means you have gone astray as we went astray. Mujahid used to say of this, as (14601) and (14602) are given from him, that "superiority" is lightening of the punishment. Al-Tabari says this saying reported from Mujahid has no meaning, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 420}

because the saying of those who said "you have no superiority over us" is only a reproach for what they had done before that saying, as the sense of "was" in the sentence shows. Had it been a reproach for their saying to their Lord, "give them double the punishment of the Fire", the reproach would have been "what superiority do you have over us in lightening punishment from you, when what befell us has befallen you?", and not "you had no superiority over us". The note adds that some words on the photographed manuscript page are unreadable, so the explanation here follows the evident force of the text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 421}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
