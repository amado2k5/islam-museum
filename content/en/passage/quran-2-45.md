## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "Seek help through patience and prayer" al-Tabari says the sense is: seek help to fulfil My covenant that you made in your scripture, of obeying Me, following My command and giving up what you desire of leadership and love of the world, through patience in it and prayer. He mentions a view that "patience" here means fasting, but holds that fasting is only one of the senses of patience and that the command is to be patient in all that the soul dislikes of obeying God and leaving disobedience. The root of "sabr" is restraining the soul from what it loves and holding it back from desire, which is why Ramadan is called the month of patience. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 11}
He then asks how prayer can be a help to obedience and giving up leadership, and answers that prayer contains the recitation of God's Book, whose verses call to forsake the world, console the soul about its adornments and remind of the Hereafter. He reports from Hudhayfa, by two chains, that the Prophet turned to prayer whenever a matter distressed him; the editor discusses the name of two narrators in them and traces the report to Ahmad and Abu Dawud. He mentions that the Prophet saw Abu Hurayra lying on his belly and told him to get up and pray, for prayer brings healing; the editor notes that al-Tabari gives it without a chain and that al-Bukhari and Ibn al-Asbahani found the raised chain faulty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 12} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 13}
Al-Tabari holds that God told the rabbis of the Children of Israel to take refuge in patience and prayer in keeping His covenant, as He told His prophet in surah Ta Ha. He reports that when Ibn Abbas was told on a journey of the death of his brother Qutham, he said the formula of return to God, prayed two rak'as, and recited the verse; the editor calls the chain sound. From Abu al-Aliya he reports the meaning "seek help through patience and prayer". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 14}

Al-Tabari reports from Ibn Jurayj that patience and prayer are two aids towards God's mercy, and from Ibn Zayd that the polytheists told Muhammad he was calling them to a great matter, meaning prayer and belief in God. On "and it is indeed heavy" he takes the pronoun to refer to prayer and rejects the view that it refers to responding to Muhammad, since no response was mentioned and one does not leave the plain sense of a statement for a hidden one with no indication. He explains "heavy" as hard and weighty, with the same report from al-Dahhak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 14} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 15}
On "except for the humble" he says those who submit to obeying Him, fear His might and believe in His promise and warning. He reports from Ibn Abbas that they are those who believe what God sent down, from Abu al-Aliya the fearful, from Mujahid the true believers, and from Ibn Zayd that humility is fear and awe of God. The root of "khushu'" is lowliness and submission, and he cites a line of Jarir. For him the verse means: seek help, rabbis, by holding yourselves to obeying God and by establishing prayer, which is heavy except for those humble before God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 15} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 16} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 17}
