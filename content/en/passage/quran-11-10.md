**Commentary on verse 10 (pp. 256-257)**

On [[passage/quran-11-10|11:10]] al-Tabari says: if We spread out the world for man and give him ease in his living and wide provision, which are the favours God means by 'and if We give him a taste of favour', 'after hardship has touched him' meaning after the narrowness of living and the difficulty he was struggling with, 'he will surely say: the evils have gone from me', that is, at that point he will say the narrowness and hardship have gone from me and the troubles and dislikes have passed, 'he is surely exultant, boastful', that is, man is exultant in the favours he is given; the commentary on the verse continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 256}

'Boastful' means full of pride in the ease he has gained in the world and the life spread out for him in it, forgetting its turns and the sourness of its hard times and giving up the search for the lasting bliss and the joy that endures and does not pass. Report 18005, from Ibn Jurayj on 'the evils have gone from me': conceit toward God and boldness against Him; 'he is exultant', and God does not love the exultant; 'boastful' after he is given and does not thank God. The editor refers to the earlier commentaries on 'exultant' and 'boastful', and says 'al-awa'is' is the plural of 'a'isa', meaning hardship, difficulty and need. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 257}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
