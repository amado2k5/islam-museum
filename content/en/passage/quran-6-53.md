## Summary
Verse 53 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 53 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

By "and so we have tried some of them by others, that they say: are these the ones God has favoured among us? Is not God best informed of the thankful?" al-Tabari means: and so we tested and tried. Report 13289, from Qatada: we tried some of them by others. He has shown earlier the sense of "fitna", that it is testing and trial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 388}

The editor refers for "fitna" to p. 297.

God's trial of some of his creatures by others is his making them differ in what he apportioned to them of provision and character, making some rich and some poor, some strong and some weak, so that he made some need others, as a test from him of them by it. A group of interpreters said as we said: report 13290, from Ibn Abbas: he made some of them rich and some poor, and the rich said to the poor "are these the ones God has favoured among us?", meaning God guided them; they said that only in mockery and ridicule. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 389}

The editor keeps "mockery and ridicule" from the manuscript and refers for "favour" to volume 9, p. 71.

As for "that they say: are these the ones God has favoured among us?" he says: we tested people with wealth and poverty, honour and humiliation, strength and weakness, guidance and error, so that those whom God led astray and blinded to the way of truth would say of those God guided and gave success: are these the ones God favoured with guidance and right direction, who are poor, weak and lowly, "among us", we being rich and strong? in mockery of them and enmity to Islam and its people. And he says "is not God best informed of the thankful?", an answer to these polytheists who denied that God guided the poor and weak to the truth and let down them, the rich, and an affirmation to them: I know better who of my creatures is thankful for my favour and who is ungrateful for it; so my favour to those on whom I bestowed guidance is the reward for their thanks for my favour, and my letting down those I let down from the path of right guidance is the penalty for their ingratitude for my favour, not because the rich is rich or the poor poor, for reward and punishment are due to none but as recompense for the deed he earned, not for wealth or poverty, since wealth, poverty, weakness and strength are not among the acts of my creatures. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 390}

Verse 53 closes here at p. 390, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-54|6:54]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
