## Summary
Verse 27 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 27 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "if you could see when they are made to stand before the Fire and say: if only we were sent back and not deny the signs of our Lord and be among the believers" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: if you could see, Muhammad, these who hold images and idols equal to their Lord and reject your prophethood, whose description I gave you, "when they are made to stand", that is, when they are detained "on the Fire", meaning in the Fire, "on" being put for "in", as in [[passage/quran-2-102|2:102]], "they followed what the satans recited on Solomon's kingdom"... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 316}

The rest: "on" for "in". It is said "if you could see when they are made to stand" with the sense of "when they will be made to stand", for the reason given before, that the Arabs sometimes put "idh" for "idha" and "idha" for "idh", though the portion of "idh" is to accompany reports of what has happened and that of "idha" what has not, but it is as the rajaz poet Abu al-Najm says in a line praying for God's reward of gardens of Eden in the high chambers, putting "idh" for "idha". It is said "wuqifu" and not "uqifu" because that is the eloquent form in Arab speech; one says "waqaftu al-dabba" without alif when you hold it, and "waqaftu al-ard" when you make it a lasting endowment. Report 13179, from Abu Amr: I never heard any of the Arabs say "awqaftu al-shay'" with alif, though if I saw a man in a place and asked "what made you stand here?" with the alif, I would find it good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 317}

The editor refers for "on" meaning "in" to earlier volumes 11, 2 and 1 and the grammar indexes, for "idh" and "idha" to p. 236, notes that two lines of the rajaz appeared on p. 235 and the first is in Lisan al-Arab under "taha", and corrects "al-Harith from Abu Ubayd" from the manuscript.

"They say: if only we were sent back" means: these polytheists who held others equal to their Lord say, when held in the Fire, if only we were sent back to the world to repent and return to obeying God, "and not deny the signs of our Lord", his proofs, nor reject them, "and be among the believers", those who trust God, his proofs and messengers and follow his command and prohibition. The readers differed: most readers of the Hijaz, Medina and the two Iraqs read "yalaytana nuraddu wa-la nukadhdhibu bi-ayati rabbina wa-nakunu min al-mu'minin" with both verbs raised, meaning if only we were sent back and we are not denying our Lord's signs but will be among the believers; some Kufan readers read both "nukadhdhiba" and "nakuna" in the accusative, meaning if only we were sent back, and not deny, and be believers; report 13180 has this in Ibn Mas'ud's reading, "if only we were sent back, then we would not deny", with fa. Some Syrian readers are reported to read "nukadhdhibu" raised and "nakuna" accusative, as if taking it that they wished to be sent back and to be believers, and reported that they would not deny if sent back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 318}

The grammarians differed. A Basran grammarian said the accusative is because it answers a wish and what follows "wa" is like what follows "fa"; if you like you may raise it and take it as not a wish, as though they said "and, by God, we do not deny, and, by God, we are among the believers", cut off from the first; the raised form is the standard, for when accusative "wa" is a conjunction and they would then be wishing not to deny and to be believers, and this cannot be, since they did not wish this, they wished to be sent back and reported that they would not deny. A Kufan grammarian said that accusative "nukadhdhiba" and "nakuna" as an answer with "wa" would be right, since the Arabs answer with "wa" and "thumma" as with "fa", saying "if only I had money, so I would give you" and "and I would give you" and "then I would give you", and it may be accusative by "sarf", as in "nothing contains me and is too narrow for you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 319}

Another said: I do not like the accusative here, since it is not a wish from them but a report they gave about themselves; do you not see that God called them liars, saying "and if they were sent back they would return to what they were forbidden"? Denial applies to a report, not a wish. Some denied that the answer can be with "wa" or any particle other than "fa", saying "wa" marks a circumstance, as in "nothing contains me and is too narrow for you", that is, "while it is too narrow for you"; and "fa" gives the answer of a condition, "you did not stand so we come to you" meaning had you stood we would have come; and said "do not deny" and "be" are allowed because they said "if only we were sent back" in a state other than that in which they stood before the Fire; their standing was in that, and they wished not to have been stood in that state. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 319}

The editor refers for "sarf" to earlier volumes 1, 3 and 7.

Al-Tabari takes the sense of the author of this view to be: if you could see when they are made to stand before the Fire and say "we have been made to stand before it denying the signs of our Lord, unbelievers; if only we were sent back to it and made to stand before it not denying or unbelieving"; and this is an interpretation the plain revelation rejects, namely God's words "if they were sent back they would return to what they were forbidden, and they are liars", which tell that they are liars in what they said, while a wish cannot be called a lie; but he thinks that the author of this view did not reflect on the interpretation and stuck to the paths of Arabic. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 320}

The reading he chooses is "yalaytana nuraddu wa-la nukadhdhibu bi-ayati rabbina wa-nakunu min al-mu'minin" with both raised, meaning if only we were sent back, and we are not denying our Lord's signs if sent back but will be among the believers, as a report of what they will do if returned to the world and not a wish not to deny and to be believers; for God has reported that if sent back they would return to what they were forbidden and that they are liars in what they said, and had it been a wish their being called liars would be impossible, since a wish is not called a lie, truth and lie belonging only to reports. As to the accusative, he thinks its reader aimed at the interpretation of Abdullah's reading with fa, as an answer to a wish, which is sound in its grammar read so, its meaning: had we been sent back to the world we would not have denied our Lord's signs and would be among the believers. If what some report of hearing the answer with "wa" and "thumma" like the answer with "fa" is sound, there is no doubt the accusative reading is sound; otherwise it is far in sense from the interpretation of the revelation; he does not know such hearing to be sound from the Arabs, for what is known in their speech is the answer with fa and the "sarf" with wa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 321}

The editor corrects "that he aimed at the interpretation" against the printed and manuscript readings.

Verse 27 closes here at p. 321, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-28|6:28]].
