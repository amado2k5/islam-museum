## Summary
Verse 109 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 109 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**God's dominion and no need to wrong (pp. 98-99).** Al-Tabari says the sense is that He punishes those who disbelieved after believing with the great punishment and darkened faces mentioned, and rewards the believers who stayed firm in affirmation and fidelity to their covenants with abiding in His gardens, wronging neither group because He has no need to wrong. The wrongdoer wrongs another to add might to his might, authority to his authority or dominion to his dominion, or to make up a lack in some of his means; whoever owns all that lies between the east and west, and this world and the next, has no reason to wrong anyone, being without any deficient means needing completion; exalted is God above that. That is why, after denying that He wishes wrong, God says that what is in the heavens and earth is His. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 98} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 99}

**The grammarians' dispute over repeating the name (pp. 99-100).** The people of Arabic differed over why God's name is mentioned openly again in the closing clause when it was already mentioned openly in the first. A Basran said it is like the Arabs' saying "as for Zayd, Zayd went" and like a line of a poet, where the name is stated in place of a pronoun. The line is by Adi ibn Zayd, sometimes ascribed to his son Sawada ibn Adi, and perhaps to Umayya ibn Abi al-Salt; the editor gives its places in al-Buhturi's Hamasa, Shu'ara al-Jahiliyya, Sibawayh's Kitab, Khizanat al-Adab, Ibn al-Shajari's Amali and Sharh Shawahid al-Mughni, says it comes from scattered lines of Adi's reflections on life and death, describes their sense, and notes that in the manuscript the line was corrupt. A Kufan grammarian said this is not like the line, since in the line the second mention stands where a pronoun would stand and is a single word, while in the verse the first statement is a report with no tie to the second; each of the two accounts differs in sense from the other and suffices on its own, whereas the poet's line needs the report on it completed. The editor adds that the Kufans call the pronoun "kinaya". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 100}

**Al-Tabari's choice (p. 100).** Al-Tabari says the second view is the sounder to him, because the meanings and clarity of God's book are not to be directed to rare speech and senses, and in eloquent speech and the plain understood sense it has a valid, existing sense. As for the return of matters to God, he says every creature's affair, righteous or wicked, good or bad, ends with Him, and He repays each according to what they deserve, wronging none. The editor says he corrected al-Tabari's comparison with the poet in the manuscript and printed text, and that the copyist often miswrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 100}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 100}
