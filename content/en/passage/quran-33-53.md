## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying: "O you who have believed, do not enter the houses of the Prophet except that he has been permitted to you to food without looking at it. But when you are invited, enter; and when you have eaten, disperse, and you are not accustomed to the hadith. Indeed, that used to harm the Prophet, so he should be shy of you, but Allah is not ashamed of the truth. And when you ask them for provisions, ask them from behind a veil. That is purer for your hearts and their hearts, and it is not for you to harm." The Messenger of Allah, and never marry his wives after him ever. Indeed, that is great with Allah." Allah says to the companions of the Messenger of Allah: O you who have believed in Allah and His Messenger, do not enter the houses of the Prophet of Allah except to invite to food you feed "without looking at it," meaning: not waiting for its perception and reach, which is derived from their saying: 'This thing has become a mean, a me, a vessel, and a vessel.' Al-Hutay'ah said: And I approached the evening prayer to Suhail or Al-Sha'ara, and the vessels were extended to me, and it has another language. It is said: 'Qa inna lakh' means it became clear to you, and he attained for you, and from it is the saying of Ru'bah ibn al-'Ajjaj: 'I raged and I am like me, we will bring him to the pigeon that has swallowed a pigeon in rhyming.' In the maqta, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Qatadah.
