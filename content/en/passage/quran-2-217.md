## Summary
Verse 217 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/expedition-of-nakhla|Expedition of Nakhla]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:ibn-hisham-sira}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 217 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "they ask you about the sacred month, about fighting in it": it means your companions ask you, Muhammad, about the sacred month, which is Rajab, about fighting in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 299}

The lowered "fighting" is on the repetition of "about" before it, and so was the reading of 'Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, it is said; from al-Rabi': "they ask you about fighting in it", and he used to read it so (4080). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 300} Al-Tabari says: say, Muhammad, "fighting in it", that is in the sacred month, "is a great matter", meaning that treating it as lawful and shedding blood in it is grave in God's sight. He says this because the Arabs did not lance spears in it, so that a man would meet his father's or brother's killer in it and not stir him, out of respect; Mudar called it "the deaf" for the silence of weapons in it, meaning Rajab; from Jabir: the Messenger of God did not campaign in the sacred month unless attacked, or if he campaigned and it arrived he stayed until it passed (4081). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 300} "Sadd" is hindering, "and disbelief in Him" means in God, and he reads the sentence as: hindering from God's way and disbelief in Him, and from the Sacred Mosque, and expelling the people of the Sacred Mosque, its people and guardians, are greater in God's sight than fighting in the sacred month; "hindering" is raised by "greater in God's sight" and "expelling its people" is joined to it, and then He begins the report about "fitna": "fitna is worse than killing", meaning polytheism is greater than killing, that is the killing of Ibn al-Hadrami which they found objectionable in the sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 300} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 301} Some grammarians, al-Farra' as the editor shows, claimed that "and the Sacred Mosque" is joined to "fighting", the meaning being "they ask you about the sacred month, about fighting in it and about the Sacred Mosque". Al-Tabari rejects this: besides departing from what the scholars said, it has no basis, because the people had no doubt about the gravity of what the polytheists did in expelling them from their homes in Mecca that they should ask about it; they asked only about what they were uncertain of, the killing of Ibn al-Hadrami; and there is no disagreement among the interpreters that the verse was revealed to the Messenger of God because of the killing of Ibn al-Hadrami and his killer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 301} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 302}

Al-Tabari reports (4082) from 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, through Ibn Ishaq, that the Prophet sent 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh in Rajab on his return from the first Badr, with eight men of the Emigrants, and wrote him a letter and ordered him not to look at it until he had travelled two days and not to compel any of his companions; his companions were of the Emigrants: Abu Hudhayfa ibn 'Utba, 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh, the commander, 'Ukkasha ibn Mihsan, 'Utba ibn Ghazwan, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, 'Amir ibn Rabi'a, Waqid ibn 'Abdallah, Khalid ibn al-Bukayr and Suhayl ibn Bayda'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 302} After two days he opened the letter, which said: go on until you camp at Nakhla between Mecca and Ta'if, keep watch there for Quraysh and learn their news for us; he told his companions, whoever wants martyrdom and desires it, let him go, and whoever dislikes it, let him return; he went on and they went with him and none stayed behind; at a mine above al-Fur' called Buhran, Sa'd and 'Utba lost a camel they rode by turns and stayed behind looking for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 302} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 303} Ibn Jahsh camped at Nakhla and a Quraysh caravan passed carrying raisins, leather and goods, including 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami, 'Uthman ibn 'Abdallah ibn al-Mughira, his brother Nawfal and al-Hakam ibn Kaysan; 'Ukkasha looked out at them with his head shaved and they felt safe and said, pilgrims on 'umra, no harm from them. The men consulted, on the last day, which the editor says is "of Jumada" in the printed text while the text of Ibn Hisham and al-Tabari's History has "of Rajab", the sounder; they said, if you leave them tonight they will enter the sanctuary and be safe from you, and if you kill them you kill them in the sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 303} They hesitated, then took heart and agreed to kill whom they could and take what they had; Waqid ibn 'Abdallah shot 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami with an arrow and killed him, 'Uthman ibn 'Abdallah and al-Hakam ibn Kaysan were taken captive and Nawfal escaped. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 304} They brought the caravan and two captives to the Messenger of God at Medina; some of the family of 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh said he told his companions that the Messenger of God had a fifth of their booty, before the fifth was made obligatory, and set aside a fifth of the caravan and divided the rest; when they arrived the Prophet said, I did not order you to fight in the sacred month, held the caravan and the captives and refused to take anything, and the men were dismayed and thought they were ruined, and the Muslims rebuked them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 304} Quraysh said, Muhammad and his companions have made the sacred month lawful, shedding blood in it, taking property and capturing men; those of the Muslims in Mecca who answered said they had acted in Jumada (the editor notes the variant "in Sha'ban"); and the Jews, taking an omen against the Prophet, said, 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami was killed by Waqid ibn 'Abdallah: "'Amr", war has been stirred, "al-Hadrami", war has come, "Waqid", war has been kindled; and God turned that against them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 304} When people talked much of this, God sent down "they ask you about the sacred month", meaning that if you killed in the sacred month, they hindered you from God's way along with disbelief in Him and from the Sacred Mosque, and your expulsion from it, when you are its people and guardians, is greater in God's sight than killing whom you killed; "fitna is worse than killing" means they used to turn a Muslim from his religion until they returned him to disbelief after belief, which is greater in God's sight than killing; "they will not cease fighting you until they turn you from your religion if they can" means they persist in the vilest and gravest of that, neither repenting nor desisting. When the Qur'an came down with this and God relieved the Muslims of their fear, the Messenger of God took the caravan and the two captives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 304} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 305} The editor notes this is the text of Ibn Hisham's Sira from Ibn Ishaq and that al-Tabari narrated it in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 305}

From al-Suddi (4083): the Messenger of God sent a raiding party of seven under 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh al-Asadi, among them 'Ammar ibn Yasir, Abu Hudhayfa ibn 'Utba, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, 'Utba ibn Ghazwan, Suhayl ibn Bayda', 'Amir ibn Fuhayra and Waqid ibn 'Abdallah al-Yarbu'i, and he wrote Ibn Jahsh a letter not to be read until he reached Batn Malal; when he reached it he opened it and it said to go on until he reached Batn Nakhla, and he said to his companions, whoever wants death let him go and make his will, for I am making mine and going on; Sa'd and 'Utba stayed behind because they lost a mount and went to Buhran looking for it; Ibn Jahsh went on to Batn Nakhla and met al-Hakam ibn Kaysan, 'Abdallah ibn al-Mughira, al-Mughira ibn 'Uthman and 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami; they fought, took al-Hakam and 'Abdallah ibn al-Mughira captive, al-Mughira escaped, and 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami was killed by Waqid ibn 'Abdallah; it was the first booty the companions of Muhammad took. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 305} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 306} When they returned with the two captives and what they took, the Meccans wanted to ransom them and the Prophet said, until we see what became of our two companions; when Sa'd and his companion returned he ransomed the two captives; the polytheists then reviled him, saying, Muhammad claims to follow obedience to God and he is the first to make the sacred month lawful and killed our companion in Rajab; the Muslims said, we killed him in Jumada, and it was said, on the first night of Rajab and the last night of Jumada, and the Muslims sheathed their swords when Rajab began. God sent down, rebuking the people of Mecca, "fighting in it is a great matter", not lawful, and what you did, polytheists, is greater than killing in the sacred month when you disbelieved in God and hindered Muhammad and his companions from Him, and expelling the people of the Sacred Mosque from it when they expelled Muhammad is greater in God's sight than killing, and fitna, which is polytheism, is greater in God's sight than killing in the sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 306}

From Jundub ibn 'Abdallah, from the Prophet (4084): he sent a group and appointed Abu 'Ubayda over them; when he set out he wept from longing for the Messenger of God, so the Prophet sent another man in his place, 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh, wrote him a letter and ordered him not to read it until he reached such and such a place and not to compel any of his companions to go with him; when he read the letter he said "we belong to God and to Him we return" and "we hear and obey God and His Messenger", told them the news and read them the letter; two men returned and the rest went on, met Ibn al-Hadrami and killed him, not knowing that day whether it was of Rajab or Jumada; the polytheists told the Muslims, you did such and such in the sacred month; they came to the Prophet and told him, and God sent down the verse, "fitna" being polytheism. Someone who was in the party, I think he said, said, by God only one man killed him, and he said, if it is good then I have taken charge of it, and if it is sin then I did it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 306} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 307} The editor notes that al-Tabari narrated it in his History and its remainder comes as 4102. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 307}

From Mujahid (4085): a man of Banu Tamim whom the Prophet sent on a raid passed Ibn al-Hadrami carrying wine from Ta'if to Mecca, shot him with an arrow and killed him; there was a pact between Quraysh and Muhammad, and he killed him on the last day of Jumada al-Akhira and the first day of Rajab; Quraysh said, in the sacred month and we have a pact; God sent down the verse about the killing of Ibn al-Hadrami, and "fitna", disbelief in God and idol worship, is greater than all this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 307} From al-Zuhri, 'Uthman al-Jazari and Miqsam, the freedman of Ibn 'Abbas (4086): Waqid ibn 'Abdallah met 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami on the first night of Rajab thinking it was of Jumada and killed him, the first polytheist killed; the polytheists reproached the Muslims and God sent down the verse; al-Zuhri added that the Prophet, as far as had reached them, held fighting in the sacred month forbidden and then it was made lawful for him. The editor notes the report is a "mursal", narrated by two chains from two Successors, and that Miqsam is the son of Bujra, freedman of 'Abdallah ibn al-Harith ibn Nawfal, called "freedman of Ibn 'Abbas" for his attachment to him, a reliable Successor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 308}

From Ibn 'Abbas (4087): the polytheists had hindered the Messenger of God and turned him back from the Sacred Mosque in a sacred month, and God gave His Prophet victory in a sacred month the next year; the polytheists faulted him for fighting in a sacred month, and God said that hindering from God's way and the rest is greater than fighting in it; Muhammad sent a raiding party who met 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami coming from Ta'if on the last night of Jumada and first night of Rajab, his companions thinking it was Jumada, and one man among them killed him; the polytheists sent to reproach him, and the expulsion of the people of the Sacred Mosque is greater than what Muhammad's men did and polytheism most grave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 308} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 309} From Abu Malik: when the verse came down they thought it grave, and He said, fitna, the polytheism you persist in, is greater than what you thought grave (4088); in another report from him: the Messenger of God sent 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh with a force, who met some polytheists at Batn Nakhla while the Muslims reckoned it the last day of Jumada, which was the first day of Rajab, and the Muslims killed Ibn al-Hadrami; the polytheists said, do you not claim you hold the sacred month and the sacred land sacred, and you killed in the sacred month; and God sent down the verse (4089). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 309} From Qatada: Waqid ibn 'Abdallah al-Tamimi met 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami at Batn Nakhla and killed him (4090), the editor suggesting a copyist's error in the printed word before it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 310} From Ibn Jurayj: I asked 'Ata' about whom it was revealed; he said, I do not know; 'Ikrima and Mujahid said, about 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami, and so from al-Zuhri (4091); from Mujahid: all of this is greater than killing Ibn al-Hadrami, and fitna, disbelief in God and idol worship, is greater than all of it (4092); from al-Dahhak: Muhammad's companions killed Ibn al-Hadrami in the sacred month, the polytheists reproached them, and God said fighting in the sacred month is grave, and greater than that is hindering from God's way, disbelief in Him and expelling the people of the Sacred Mosque from it (4093). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 310}

Al-Tabari says these two reports from Mujahid and al-Dahhak show the soundness of what he said on the raising of "hindering" and "disbelief in Him", that what raises it is "greater in God's sight", and confirm what he narrated from Ibn 'Abbas, and show the error of whoever claims it is raised by joining to "great", and of whoever claims that "and expelling its people from it is greater in God's sight" is a report detached from what precedes and a new beginning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 310} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 311} From al-Sha'bi: "fitna is worse than killing" means disbelief (4094); from Qatada: expelling its people is greater in God's sight than that, then He reproached the polytheists for their evil deeds: fitna is worse than killing, that is, associating partners with God is worse than killing (4095). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 311} Similar is reported from Ibn 'Abbas: when the Messenger of God's companions killed 'Amr ibn al-Hadrami on the last night of Jumada and the first night of Rajab, the polytheists sent to reproach him, and God said "fighting in it is a great matter", and other things are greater than it (4096). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 311}

The grammarians differed over what raises "and hindering from God's way"; some Kufan grammarians, al-Farra' as the editor shows, said there are two ways: "hindering" may be joined to "great", that is, say fighting in it is great and hindering from God's way and disbelief in Him; or "hindering" may itself be "great", that is, great is hindering from God's way and disbelief in Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 311} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 312} Al-Tabari says he erred in both. If "hindering" is raised by joining to "great", the meaning becomes that fighting in the sacred month is great and hindering from God's way and disbelief in God, which is contrary to what all the people of Islam hold, since no one claimed God made fighting in the sacred months disbelief in God; and how could it be, when God says after it "and expelling its people from it is greater in God's sight"? Were it so, expelling the people of the Sacred Mosque would be greater in God's sight than disbelief in Him, and the established proof that nothing is greater in God's sight than disbelief in Him shows the error. On the other reading, the meaning becomes that expelling the people of the Sacred Mosque is greater in God's sight than disbelief in God and hindering from His way, and its holder falls into the same error as the first, making one of the parts of disbelief greater in God's sight than disbelief itself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 312} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 313} The editor remarks that al-Tabari rebutted al-Farra' firmly. Some grammarians of Basra held the first view on raising "hindering" and made "and expelling its people" raised as a new start; he has shown its error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 313}

The interpreters then differed whether "they ask you about the sacred month, about fighting in it; say, fighting in it is a great matter" is abrogated or stands. Some said it is abrogated by "and fight the polytheists all together as they fight you all together" (9:36) and "kill the polytheists" (9:5); from 'Ata' ibn Maysara: fighting in the sacred month was made lawful in Surat Bara'a (4097), the editor noting disagreement over his identification; from al-Zuhri: the Prophet, as far as had reached them, held fighting in the sacred month forbidden and then it was made lawful (4098). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 313} Others said it is a standing ruling, and by this verse fighting is not lawful for anyone in the sacred months; from Ibn Jurayj: I said to 'Ata', what of them, since at that time it was not lawful for them to raid the polytheists in the sacred month, and then they raided in it? 'Ata' swore by God that it is not lawful for people to raid in the sacred month or to fight in it, nor is it recommended; and he said they are not called to Islam before being fought, nor to the jizya, they leave that (4099). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 314} Al-Tabari says the correct view is what 'Ata' ibn Maysara said, that the prohibition on fighting the polytheists in the sacred months is abrogated by "the number of months with God is twelve months in God's Book…" (9:36), because the reports from the Messenger of God are widespread that he campaigned against Hawazin at Hunayn and Thaqif at Ta'if, and sent Abu 'Amir to Awtas to fight the polytheists there, in some of the sacred months, in Shawwal and part of Dhu al-Qa'da, and had fighting in them been forbidden and disobedience he would have been the furthest of people from doing it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 314} Further, the scholars of his biography do not dispute that the Pledge of Ridwan to fight Quraysh was in Dhu al-Qa'da, that he pledged to meet the people in battle until 'Uthman returned with the message, and then the truce was made and he held back from fighting them, in Dhu al-Qa'da, a sacred month. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 315} If someone thinks that the prohibition came after the Prophet had made them lawful by his campaigns, he thinks in ignorance, for this verse was revealed about 'Abdallah ibn Jahsh and his companions at the end of Jumada al-Akhira in the second year after the Messenger of God's arrival at Medina, whereas Hunayn and Ta'if were in Shawwal of the eighth year, a gap no one can miss. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 315}

On "they will not cease to fight you until they turn you back from your religion, if they can": it means the polytheists of Quraysh will not cease to fight you until they turn you back from your religion if they are able; from 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr: they persist in the vilest and gravest of that, neither repenting nor desisting, that is in tempting the Muslims away from their religion until they return them to disbelief, as they used to do to whomever of them they could before the migration (4100), the editor noting that the words from "that is, in tempting" are not in Ibn Hisham's Sira or al-Tabari's History; from Mujahid: the disbelievers of Quraysh (4101). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 316}

On "whoever of you turns back from his religion and dies a disbeliever, their deeds have come to nothing in this world and the next, and they are the people of the Fire, abiding in it": it means whoever returns from his religion, as in "so they went back on their tracks" (18:64), meaning returned. The doubled letter appears in "yartadid" because the final letter is silent by the jussive. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 317} "And dies a disbeliever" means dies before repenting of his disbelief, and they are those whose deeds "have come to nothing", that is, gone for nothing, their nullity being the loss of reward and recompense for them in this world and the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 317} They are called the "people" of the Fire because they do not leave it but are its resident dwellers, and "abiding" means remaining a stay without end or limit. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 317}
