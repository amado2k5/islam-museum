## Summary
Verse 100 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 100 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 100 (pp. 267-276)**

On "and he raised his parents upon the throne", it means upon the couch. Report 19883, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: the couch. Report 19884, from al-Dahhak, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Muhammad ibn Yazid al-Wasiti and Juwaybir: "al-arsh" is the couch. Reports 19885 to 19892, from Mujahid, through Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the couch. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 267}

Reports 19893 and 19894, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, and Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: his couch; on the couch. Report 19895, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: he raised his parents on the couch. Report 19896, from Sufyan, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq and Abu Ahmad: on the couch. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 268}

Report 19897, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: his seat. Report 19898, from Zayd ibn Aslam, through Ibn Abd al-Rahim al-Barqi and Amr ibn Abi Salama: I asked him about God's words "and he raised his parents upon the throne", and said, has it reached you that she was his aunt? He said: some of the people of knowledge say so, saying that his mother had died before that and this was his aunt. On "and they fell down before him in prostration" he says: Ya'qub, his children and his mother fell down before Joseph in prostration. Report 19899, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: he raised his parents on the couch and they prostrated to him, and his brothers prostrated to him. Report 19900, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Ya'qub set out with his family until they came to Joseph; when his sons were gathered to Ya'qub they went in to Joseph, and when they saw him they fell down before him in prostration, which was the greeting of kings at that time: his father, his mother and his brothers. Report 19901, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "and they fell down before him in prostration", it was the greeting of those before you, with which they greeted one another, and God gave this community the salaam, the greeting of the people of Paradise, as an honour from God, blessed and exalted, which He hastened for them, and a favour from Him. Report 19902, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the greeting of people then was that they prostrated to one another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 269}

Report 19903, from Sufyan, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq and Abu Ishaq: "and they fell down before him in prostration", he said, it was a greeting among them. Report 19904, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: his parents and brothers; that was their greeting, as people do today. Report 19905, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', al-Muharibi and Juwaybir: a greeting among them. Report 19906, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: that prostration was for his eminence, as the angels prostrated to Adam for his eminence; it was not a prostration of worship. Those who said it was a greeting among them meant that it was a custom of theirs and not by way of worship of one another. Evidence that this was long an ancient custom among people before Islam, not by way of worshipping one another, is a verse of al-A'sha of Banu Tha'laba in which he says that when the one he praises came to them shortly after sleep they prostrated to him and raised the 'amar. The editor explains that the verse is from his poem in praise of Qays ibn Ma'dikarib, who rescued al-A'sha when he nearly became a captive in one of his raids, that La'la' is a place between Kufa and Basra, and that "'amar" is variously explained: said to be the turban or cap, or the basil raised for a king to greet him, or raising our voices with "'amraka Allah"; and that he keeps the manuscript reading rather than the printed one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 270}

On "O my father, this is the interpretation of my vision of before; my Lord has made it true", he says: Joseph said to his father: O father, this prostration that you, my mother and my brothers made to me is "the interpretation of my vision of before", that is, what my vision came to, the vision he had seen before his brothers did to him what they did, that eleven stars, the sun and the moon were prostrating to him; "my Lord has made it true" means my Lord has verified it by the coming of its interpretation in truth. The people of knowledge differed over the length of time between Joseph's vision and its fulfilment. Some said it was forty years: report 19907, from Salman al-Farisi, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, al-Mu'tamir, his father and Abu Uthman: between Joseph's vision and his seeing its interpretation was forty years. Report 19908, from Uthman al-Nahdi, through Ya'qub ibn Burhan, Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, Ibn Ulayya and Sulayman al-Taymi: he mentioned forty years; the editor thinks "Ya'qub ibn Burhan" is a miscopying of "Ya'qub ibn Mahan", a teacher of al-Tabari. Report 19909, from Salman, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Ulayya, al-Taymi and Abu Uthman: forty years. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 271}

Report 19910, from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, through al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Sufyan and Abu Sinan: he saw the interpretation of his vision after forty years. Report 19911, from Salman, through Sufyan, Sulayman al-Taymi and Abu Uthman, is the same. Report 19912, from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, through Abu al-Sa'ib, Ibn Fudayl and Dirar: he heard people disputing about a vision one of them had seen while he was praying; when he finished he asked them about it and they hid it from him, and he said: the interpretation of Joseph's vision came after forty years. Report 19913, from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Isra'il and Dirar ibn Murra: between Joseph's vision and its interpretation were forty years. Report 19914 is like Abu al-Sa'ib's from Ibn Fudayl. Report 19915, from Salman, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan, Sulayman al-Taymi and Abu Uthman: he saw the interpretation of his vision after forty years. Report 19916, from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ibn Uyayna and Abu Sinan: Joseph's vision was fulfilled after forty years, and to that the furthest of visions extends; the editor notes that the printed wording is garbled because the manuscript is unpointed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 272}

Reports 19917 to 19921, from Salman and Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, say the same: between Joseph's vision and his seeing its interpretation, or its explanation, was forty years, through Mu'adh ibn Mu'adh, Sulayman al-Taymi and Abu Uthman; Abd al-Wahhab ibn Ata'; Sa'id ibn Sulayman and Hushaym; and Amr ibn Muhammad al-Anqazi, Isra'il and Abu Sinan. Others said the period was eighty years. Report 19922, from al-Hasan, through Amr ibn Ali, Abd al-Wahhab al-Thaqafi and Hisham: from the time Joseph left Ya'qub until they met was eighty years, grief never leaving his heart and his tears running down his cheeks, and there was then no servant on the face of the earth more beloved to God than Ya'qub. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 273}

Report 19923, from Jasr ibn Farqad, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: between Ya'qub's losing Joseph and the day he was returned to him was eighty years; the editor notes that Jasr ibn Farqad, Abu Ja'far al-Qassab, is "not so strong". Report 19924, from Fudayl ibn Iyad, through Ibn Waki' and Hasan ibn Ali: I heard that between Joseph's parting from Ya'qub's lap and their meeting was eighty years. Report 19925, from al-Hasan, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Dawud ibn Mihran, Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad and Yunus: Joseph was cast into the pit at seventeen and between that and his meeting Ya'qub was eighty years; he lived afterwards twenty-three years and died aged a hundred and twenty. Report 19926, from al-Hasan, through Sa'id ibn Sulayman, Hushaym and Yunus, is similar except that it says eighty-three years. Report 19927, through Dawud ibn Mihran, Ibn Ulayya and Yunus from al-Hasan: Joseph was cast into the pit at seventeen and was eighty years in servitude, prison and kingship, then God brought his family together, and he lived afterwards twenty-three years. Report 19928, from al-Hasan, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Mubarak ibn Fadala: Joseph was cast into the pit at seventeen and was absent from his father eighty years, then lived, after God united his family and he saw the interpretation of his vision, twenty-three years, and died aged a hundred and twenty. Report 19929, from al-Hasan, through Mujahid, Yazid and Hushaym: Joseph was absent from his father in the pit and prison until they met eighty years, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 274}

and Ya'qub's eyes dried up, and there was none on earth more honoured to God than Ya'qub. The editor notes that this Mujahid is Mujahid ibn Musa ibn Farukh al-Khwarizmi, a teacher of al-Tabari. Others said the period was eighteen years. Report 19930, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: I was told, and God knows best, that Joseph's absence from Ya'qub was eighteen years; he said that the People of the Book claim it was forty years or about that, and that Ya'qub stayed with Joseph seventeen years after coming to him in Egypt, and then God took him to Himself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 275}

On "and He was good to me when He took me out of the prison and brought you from the desert", he says, reporting Joseph's words: God was good to me in taking me out of the prison where I was held and in bringing you from the desert. This is because the dwelling of Ya'qub and his sons was, as it is related, in the desert of Palestine. Report 19931, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the dwelling of Ya'qub and his sons was, as some scholars told him, at al-'Arabat in the land of Palestine in the Ghawr of al-Sham, and some say at al-Awlaj by the Sha'b; he was a bedouin with camels and sheep. Report 19932, through Ibn Waki', Amr and an elder of theirs: Ya'qub was in the desert of Palestine. Report 19933, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: Ya'qub and his sons were in the land of Canaan, people of livestock and the wilderness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 275}

Report 19934, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "and brought you from the desert", they were people of the desert and of livestock. "Al-badw" is a verbal noun from "bada fulan" when he comes to be in the desert, "yabdu badwan". It is reported that Ya'qub entered Egypt with his sons, families and children on the day they entered, fewer than a hundred, and that they left it on the day they left, numbering over six hundred thousand. Report 19935, from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad, through Ibn Waki', Zayd ibn al-Hubab, Amr ibn Muhammad, Musa ibn Ubayda and Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: the house of Ya'qub gathered to Joseph in Egypt, eighty-six persons, small and great, male and female, and left Egypt on the day Pharaoh drove them out, six hundred thousand and more. Report 19936, from Abd Allah, through Amr, Isra'il, Abu Ishaq and Abu Ubayda: Joseph's people left Egypt six hundred and seventy thousand, and Pharaoh said "these are a small band", in Sura al-Shu'ara'. Report 19937, from Ibn Mas'ud, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Isra'il, al-Mas'udi, Abu Ishaq and Abu Ubayda: the Children of Israel entered Egypt sixty-three persons and left it six hundred thousand; Isra'il said in his report six hundred and seventy thousand. Report 19938, from Masruq, through Ibn Waki', Amr, Isra'il and Abu Ishaq: Joseph's people entered Egypt three hundred and ninety, men and women together. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 276}

We stop here at the foot of p. 276, in the middle of the commentary on verse 100 (after report 19938, on the number who entered Egypt); the next batch begins at p. 277. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 276}
