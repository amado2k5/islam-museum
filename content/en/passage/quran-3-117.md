## Summary
Verse 117 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 117 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The parable of the disbelievers' spending (pp. 134-135).** Al-Tabari says the likeness is of what the disbeliever gives of his property, giving it to whom he gives in seeking nearness to his Lord while denying God's oneness and denying Muhammad, in that it will not benefit him with his disbelief and will dissolve when he needs it, gone after the benefit he hoped for, to a wind carrying bitter cold that struck the tilth of a people who had hoped to reach it and hoped for its yield and benefit; the owners of the tilth had disobeyed God and transgressed His limits, and the wind destroyed their crop after the hope they had. So God does with the disbeliever's spending and alms, when he meets Him, voiding their reward and disappointing his hope. The parable is put in terms of the spending though what is meant is God's dealing with the spending, as explained in the parable at [[passage/quran-2-17|2:17]] and the like. So the sense is: the likeness of God's voiding the reward of what they spend in this present life is that of a wind with bitter cold, and the mention of God's voiding was left out because the end of the sentence shows it and the hearer knows its meaning. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of spending and tilth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 134} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 135}

**What is meant by spending (7667, 7668, pp. 135-136).** The interpreters differed on the spending. Some said it is the ordinary spending among people: from Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7667), the disbeliever's spending in the world. Others said it is the words he says with his tongue that his heart does not confirm: from Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, Asbat, al-Suddi (7668), that what he says is not accepted from him, like a crop the wrongdoing people sowed, which a wind with cold struck and ruined, so their polytheism ruined what they spent. Al-Tabari says he showed earlier which is sounder, and explained the present life earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 135} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 136}

**The meaning of the bitter cold (7669-7675, p. 136).** Al-Tabari says the word means severe cold, from a north wind with the blast of dew and moisture on a cloudy morning after a clear night; the editor says one seldom finds such an account of the word in the lexicons. He then relates: from Humayd ibn Mas'ada, Yazid ibn Zurayʿ, Uthman ibn Ghiyath, Ikrima (7669): severe cold; from al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Abbas (7670): severe cold and zamharir; from Ali ibn Dawud, Abdullah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya, Ali, Ibn Abbas (7671): cold; from Ibn Waki, his father, Sufyan, Harun ibn Antara, his father, Ibn Abbas (7672): the word means cold; from Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7673): severe cold; from Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7674): the same; from Muhammad, Ahmad, Asbat, al-Suddi (7675): severe cold. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the present life in volume 1. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 136}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 136 in the commentary on verse 117 and continue from p. 137. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 136}
