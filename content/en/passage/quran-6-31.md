## Summary
Verse 31 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 31 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

By "those who deny the meeting with God have lost, until when the Hour comes on them suddenly, they say: alas for us for what we neglected in it" al-Tabari means: ruined and cheated in their exchange of belief for unbelief are those who denied the meeting with God, that is, who denied the raising after death, the reward and punishment, and Paradise and the Fire, of the polytheists of Quraysh and those who followed their way in it; "until when the Hour comes on them" means the Hour in which God raises the dead from their graves; the article was put on "Hour" because its meaning is known to those addressed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 324}

By "suddenly" he means unexpectedly, without the one it takes by surprise knowing the time of its coming; one says "baghatahu abghatuhu baghtatan" when you take him so. "They say: alas for us for what we neglected in it": those who denied the meeting with God have been ruined by selling their dwellings in Paradise for the dwellings of those from the people of Paradise whose dwellings in the Fire they bought; when the Hour comes on them suddenly and they see what they sold and bought and recognise the loss of the bargain they made in the world, they say, in regret and grief at the great cheating they did themselves and the vast loss than which none is greater: alas for us for what we neglected in it, that is, our regret for what we wasted in it, meaning that bargain. The pronoun in "in it" refers to the bargain, though mention of it is spared by the sense of "those who denied the meeting with God have lost", since loss can only be in a bargain that took place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 325}

The sense is: ruined are those who denied the meeting with God by selling belief, which earns God's pleasure and Paradise, for unbelief, which earns his wrath and punishment, not perceiving the loss on them in that until the Hour comes; when it comes suddenly and they see the loss they suffered in their bargain, they say, in regret: alas for us for what we neglected in it. The interpreters said so: report 13185, from al-Suddi: "ya hasratana" is our regret, "for what we neglected in it" what we wasted of the work of Paradise. 13186, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, from the Prophet, on "alas for us": the people of the Fire see their dwellings in Paradise and say alas for us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 326}

The editor refers for "regret" to volumes 7 and 3, corrects the printed "they lost" to the manuscript, identifies Yazid ibn Mihran al-Asadi the baker, Abu Khalid, truthful, and says al-Suyuti traced the report in the Durr al-Manthur and said Ibn Jarir, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tabarani, Abu al-Shaykh, Ibn Mardawayh and al-Khatib related it by a sound chain from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri.

Then "they bear their burdens on their backs; evil is what they bear": these who denied the meeting with God "bear their burdens on their backs", their sins and offences, "wizr" in the singular; one says "wazara al-rajul yazir" when he sinned, as in God's "evil is what they bear"; if it is meant that they were charged with sin, one says "wuzira al-qawm". Some claimed "wizr" is weight and load, but he does not know that to be so from any witness or trusted report from the Arabs. He said "on their backs" because a load may be on the head, shoulder or elsewhere, so he made clear where they bear what they bear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 326}

It is reported that their bearing their burdens on their backs that day is as in 13187 from Amr ibn Qays al-Mula'i: when a believer comes out of his grave, the fairest and sweetest-smelling thing meets him and says: do you know me? He says: no, except that God has sweetened your scent and beautified your form. It says: so was I in the world, I am your good deed, long did I ride you in the world, so ride me today; and he recited [[passage/quran-19-85|19:85]], "the day we gather the God-fearing to the Merciful as a delegation". The unbeliever is met by the ugliest and foulest-smelling thing, which says: do you know me? He says: no, except that God has made your form ugly and your smell foul. It says: so was I in the world, I am your bad deed, long did you ride me in the world, so today I ride you; and he recited "they bear their burdens on their backs; evil is what they bear". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 327}

The editor notes that the printed text has "his deed met him in the fairest form", which is feeble talk, while in the manuscript the copyist dropped "thing", which he supplied from the later "ugliest thing"; he identifies al-Hakam ibn Bashir ibn Salman al-Nahdi, trusted, and corrects "Sulayman", identifies Amr ibn Qays al-Mula'i, and traces the report to the Durr al-Manthur and Ibn Kathir's commentary.

Report 13188, from al-Suddi: every wrongdoing man who dies and enters his grave is met by a man of ugly face, black colour and foul smell, in filthy clothes, who enters the grave with him. When he sees him he says: how ugly your face! He says: so was your deed ugly! He says: how foul your smell! He says: so was your deed foul! He says: how filthy your clothes! He says: your deed was filthy. He says: who are you? He says: I am your deed. So he is with him in his grave; when he is raised on the day of resurrection, he says: I used to carry you in the world with pleasures and desires, so today you carry me; and he rides on his back and drives him until he enters the Fire; that is God's word "they bear their burdens on their backs". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 328}

As for "evil is what they bear", it means: evil is the burden they bear, that is, the sin they commit against their Lord. Report 13189, from Qatada: evil is what they do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 328}

On p. 328 the editor keeps "the sin they commit against their Lord" from the manuscript and drops the word "their unbelief" that the printed text added and so spoiled the sentence, and refers for "sinning against his Lord" to what he explained before.

Verse 31 closes here at p. 328, and verse 32 begins on p. 329 and is not yet filed.

We stop here at the foot of p. 328, with verse 31 complete, and al-Tabari begins the commentary on verse 32 on p. 329.
