## Summary
Verse 74 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 74 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "when Abraham said to his father Azar: do you take idols as gods? I see you and your people in manifest error" al-Tabari says to his prophet Muhammad: remember, Muhammad, for your argument with which you argue with your people and your dispute with them over their gods and your replies to them in it, from the proof and sign we cast to you and teach you of the falsity of what your people persist in and the soundness of the religion you hold and the truth of what you argue against them, the argument of Abraham, my friend, with his people and his replies to them over the falsity of the idol worship they persisted in, and his devotion to God and contentment with him as friend and helper against the idols; so take him as a leader, follow him, and make his course among his people a model for yourself, when he said to his father, parting from his religion and faulting his worship of idols apart from his maker and creator: Azar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 465}

The editor says the printed text and manuscript have a corruption in "the truth of what you argue with" which the printed edition's publisher treated as he did similar words, and that the likely cause is that the copyist ran two words together; and he sets out the construction: remember, Muhammad, the argument of Abraham, when he said to his father: Azar.

The people of knowledge then differed on the sense of "Azar" and what it is, a name or an attribute, and if a name, whose. Some said it is the name of his father. 13434, from al-Suddi: the name of his father is Azar; 13435, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq: Azar was Abraham's father, and was, as we were told, God knows best, a man of Kutha, a village in the Sawad, the Sawad of Kufa; 13436, from Sa'id ibn Abd al-Aziz: he is Azar and he is Tarih, like Israel and Jacob. Others said he is not Abraham's father; 13437, from Mujahid: Azar was not Abraham's father; 13438 and 13439, from him: Azar was not his father, he is an idol, the name of an idol. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 466}

13440, from al-Suddi: it is the name of his father, and it is said no, his name was Tarih and the idol's name Azar, he says: do you take Azar as idols and gods? Others said it is an insult and fault-finding in their speech, meaning crooked, as though he took it that Abraham faulted him for his swerving and turning from the truth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 467}

The editor says the manuscript has "do you take idols as gods" without Azar, and the printed version is near the right as will appear in Abu Jafar's criticism of al-Suddi's saying, and refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

The readers also differed in how they read it. The readers of the main cities read "li-abihi Azara" with a fatha on the ra, following the father in the genitive, but since it is a foreign name they gave it a fatha, not inflecting it though it is in the genitive; and it is reported from Abu Yazid al-Madani and al-Hasan al-Basri that they read "Azaru" with a damma as a vocative, meaning "Azar". As for what is reported from al-Suddi, that Azar is an idol's name in the accusative, meaning do you take Azar as idols and gods, it is a view far from right in Arabic, since the Arabs do not put a noun in the accusative by a verb after an interrogative particle; they do not say "your brother did I speak to" meaning "did I speak to your brother". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 467}

Abu Jafar said: the right reading for me is the reading of those who read with a fatha on the ra, following the father's case in the genitive, fatha-ed because it is not inflected, being a foreign name; I chose this for the consensus of the readers' authority. This being the right reading, and it being impossible that it be accusative by the verb after the interrogative, its fatha is sound in one of two ways: either it is the name of Abraham's father, in the genitive in apposition to the father but fatha-ed since foreign names are not inflected as the Arabs do with foreign names, or it is an attribute of him, also genitive, but not inflected since it came out on the pattern of "ahmar" and "aswad", so that the sense would be: when Abraham said to his crooked father: do you take idols as gods. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 468}

The editor says the printed text has "the reading was allowed", which is faulty, the right form being the manuscript's unpointed word; that "as the Arabs fatha-ed" is an error with the manuscript's right form; that "repeating the matter on it" in the manuscript has its right form in the printed text; and that the construal as attribute is the one he gave because it is what Abu Jafar set out earlier.

Since it has no right sense but one of these two, the better of the two views for me is the saying that it is his father's name, because God reported that he was his father, and it is the preserved saying of the people of knowledge, rather than the other saying that claims it is an attribute. If someone asks: the genealogists trace Abraham to Tarih, so how can Azar be a name of his when the name he is known by is Tarih? he is told: it is not impossible that he had two names, as many people in our time do and many did before; and it may have been a title he was called by. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 469}

On "do you take idols as gods? I see you and your people in manifest error" he says: this is God's report of what Abraham said to his father Azar: do you take idols as gods, worshipping them and taking them as lords apart from God who created you, shaped you and provided for you? "Asnam" is the plural of "sanam", an image of stone, wood or other material in the form of a person, which is the "wathan"; the picture drawn in a person's form on a wall or elsewhere is also called "sanam" and "wathan". "I see you and your people in manifest error" says: I see you, Azar, and your people who worship idols with you and take them as gods, in error, that is, turned from the path of truth and away from the road of right, "manifest" clear to whoever looks that it is a swerve from the right way and turning from the straight road; he means that he and they had gone astray from God's oneness and worship, which his favours to them required them to make pure for him and not for other gods and idols. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 469}

The editor says the printed text has "it is allowed to be a title, God knows best", leaving out "he is called by" which is in the manuscript, and adding what the manuscript lacks; and refers for "error" and "manifest" to earlier passages.

Verse 74 closes here on p. 469, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-75|6:75]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
