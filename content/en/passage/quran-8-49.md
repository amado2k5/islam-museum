**Commentary on verse 49 (pp. 12-13)**

Al-Tabari says: God says "God is hearing and knowing" in these circumstances, "and when the hypocrites said", joined to "when God showed them to you in your dream as few"; "and those in whose hearts is a sickness" means doubt in Islam, whose certainty was not sound and whose breasts were not opened to faith; "their religion has deluded these" means their religion deluded these companions of Muhammad who fight the idolaters, namely Islam. It is reported that those who said this were some of the idolaters of Quraysh who had spoken the words of Islam but whose hearts it had not taken hold of. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of "sickness" and "delusion". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 12}

Report 16193, from Amir al-Sha'bi, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Abd al-A'la and Dawud: some people of Mecca had spoken the words of Islam and went out with the idolaters on the day of Badr; when they saw how few the Muslims were they said "their religion has deluded these"; report 16194 is the same from him. Report 16195, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Yahya ibn Zakariyya and Ibn Jurayj: a group of Quraysh, Abu Qays ibn al-Walid ibn al-Mughira, Abu Qays ibn al-Fakih ibn al-Mughira, al-Harith ibn Zam'a ibn al-Aswad ibn al-Muttalib, Ali ibn Umayya ibn Khalaf and al-As ibn Munabbih ibn al-Hajjaj, who went out from Mecca with Quraysh in doubt, and their doubt held them back; when they saw how few the companions of the Messenger of God were they said "their religion has deluded these", until they reached what they reached. The editor discusses in the notes the reading of the names in the manuscript and says Abu Qays ibn al-Walid was at Badr and was killed by Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 13}

Report 16196, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they were a people who did not take part in the fighting at Badr and were called hypocrites; some said they were people who had professed Islam in Mecca and went out with the idolaters at Badr, and when they saw how few the Muslims were they said "their religion has deluded these". Report 16197, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they saw a band of believers who had left their homes at God's command; and we were told that Abu Jahl, when he looked down on Muhammad and his companions, said, by God, God will not be worshipped after today, out of hardness and arrogance. The editor discusses in the note how the word is read in the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 14}

Report 16198, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: some of the hypocrites in Mecca said it at Badr, when the Muslims were three hundred and some ten men; report 16199, from Ibn Jurayj: when the two sides drew near, God made the Muslims look few in the eyes of the idolaters and the idolaters few in the eyes of the Muslims, and the idolaters said "their religion has deluded these" because of how few they appeared, and thought they would surely defeat them; God then said "whoever relies on God, God is mighty, wise". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 14}

On "whoever relies on God" al-Tabari says it means whoever hands his affair to God, trusts in Him and is content with His decree: God protects and helps him, because He is "mighty", overcome by nothing and subdued by none, so His protected one is secure and whoever relies on Him is sufficed. This is a command from God to the believers, the companions of the Messenger and others, to entrust their affair to Him and submit to His decree so that He suffices them against their enemies; and He is "wise", with no flaw in His ordering. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of reliance, "mighty" and "wise". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 15}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
