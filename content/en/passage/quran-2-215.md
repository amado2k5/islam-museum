## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "they ask you what they should spend; say, whatever good you spend is for parents, relatives, orphans, the poor and the wayfarer; whatever good you do, God knows it": al-Tabari means, your companions ask you, Muhammad, what of their wealth they should spend in charity and on whom; say to them, whatever of your wealth you spend and give in charity, give it to your fathers and mothers, relatives, the orphans, the poor and the wayfarer, for whatever good you bring and do for them, God knows it and counts it for you until He repays your reward on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 291} The "good" is the wealth about which the Prophet's companions asked, and God answered them with this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 292} "Madha" has two grammatical analyses. One is that it means "what thing" and is accusative by "they spend", not by "they ask you". The other is nominative, one form being that "dha" means "which", so that "ma" is raised by "dha" and "they spend" is the relative clause of "dha", as Yazid ibn Mufarrigh al-Himyari said, "'Adas! 'Abbad has no authority over you; you are safe, and this one you carry is free", where "you carry" is the clause of "this"; so the sense is "they ask you what is that which they spend". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 292}

The other nominative analysis is that "madha" means "what thing" and is raised even though "they spend" acts on it, because the operator in it cannot be moved before it, since in a question the verb may not precede the interrogative particle; he cites Labid's line "will you not ask the man what he is after, is it a vow to be fulfilled or error and vanity" and Muzahim al-'Uqayli's "and not everyone who comes to Mina do I know", with the editor explaining the point of each. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 292} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 293} Al-Tabari says this verse was revealed, it is reported, before God prescribed zakat on wealth; from al-Suddi: on the day it came down there was no zakat, only the spending a man does on his family and the charity he gives, and zakat abrogated it (4068). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 293} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 294} From Ibn Jurayj: the believers asked the Messenger of God where to put their wealth and the verse came down, which is voluntary spending, while zakat is separate from all that, and Mujahid said they asked and he gave them a ruling (4069), likewise Ibn Abi Najih (4070); from Ibn Zayd: this is from supererogatory acts, they have more right to your bounty than others (4071). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 294} Al-Tabari says al-Suddi's statement may be as he said or otherwise, and the verse gives no indication of its correctness, since it may be God's urging to spend on those whose maintenance is not obligatory, parents, relatives and the others named, and a teaching of where spending finds its merit, as in "and gives wealth, though loving it, to relatives, orphans, the poor…" (2:177); and this, he says, is how he reads Ibn Jurayj's words. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 294} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 295}
