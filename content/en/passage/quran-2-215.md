## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "they ask you what they should spend; say, whatever good you spend is for parents, relatives, orphans, the poor and the wayfarer; whatever good you do, God knows it": al-Tabari means, your companions ask you, Muhammad, what of their wealth they should spend in charity and on whom; say to them, whatever of your wealth you spend and give in charity, give it to your fathers and mothers, relatives, the orphans, the poor and the wayfarer, for whatever good you bring and do for them, God knows it and counts it for you until He repays your reward on the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 291} The "good" is the wealth about which the Prophet's companions asked, and God answered them with this verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 292} "Madha" has two grammatical analyses. One is that it means "what thing" and is accusative by "they spend", not by "they ask you". The other is nominative, one form being that "dha" means "which", so that "ma" is raised by "dha" and "they spend" is the relative clause of "dha", as Yazid ibn Mufarrigh al-Himyari said, "'Adas! 'Abbad has no authority over you; you are safe, and this one you carry is free", where "you carry" is the clause of "this"; so the sense is "they ask you what is that which they spend". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 292}
