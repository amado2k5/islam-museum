## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "to God belong the East and the West, so wherever you turn there is the face of God", al-Tabari says: to God belong their dominion and ordering, as one says "this house is so-and-so's", meaning it is his possession; so the sense is that they are His in dominion and creation. "Mashriq" is the place of sunrise. Asked whether God has only one East and one West, he answers that the meaning is: to God belong the East from which the sun rises each day and the West in which it sets each day, that is what lies between the two ends of the East and between the two ends of the West, since the sun rises each day from a place to which it returns only after a year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 526}

Asked why the mention of the East and West as God's is singled out here, al-Tabari gives the interpreters' differing views on the occasion. Some said: because the Jews prayed facing Jerusalem and the Prophet did so for a time and was then turned to the Ka'ba, which the Jews objected to, saying "what turned them from their direction", and God said all the Easts and Wests are His and He turns the faces of His servants as He wills. He reports from Ibn Abbas (via Ali ibn Abi Talha) that the first thing abrogated of the Qur'an was the direction of prayer, the Prophet having faced Jerusalem for some sixteen months while loving the direction of Abraham, until the turning was revealed, the Jews doubted, and this verse came; al-Suddi has the like. The editor notes the chain is weak through a break between Ali ibn Abi Talha and Ibn Abbas, while the sense is established from Ibn Abbas by a sound route in Abu Ubayd and al-Hakim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 527} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 528}

Others said the verse came before facing the Sacred Mosque was made obligatory, to inform the Prophet and his Companions that they might face wherever they willed in prayer since God is in every direction they face and no place is void of Him (as in Surat al-Mujadila), and that this was then abrogated by the obligation to face the Sacred Mosque, from Qatada (the Prophet prayed toward Jerusalem sixteen months, then was turned to the Ka'ba, the later verse of al-Baqara abrogating this) and Ibn Zayd (the Prophet disliked the Jews' saying that Muhammad and his Companions did not know their direction until the Jews guided them, and raised his face to the sky). In a note the editor gives Ibn Kathir's comment on al-Tabari's words "no place is void of Him": if he means His knowledge it is correct, but His essence is not confined in any of His creation; the editor says Ibn Kathir's words are al-Tabari's own belief, which he made clear in his commentary on Surat al-Mujadila, so there is no ground for doubting him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 528} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 529} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 530}

Others said it came as God's permission to His Prophet to pray the voluntary prayer wherever his face turned, east or west, while travelling, and in the heat of fighting and extreme fear, from Ibn Umar (who prayed wherever his mount turned, saying the Prophet did so and that the verse was revealed about this, and that on returning from Mecca the Prophet prayed on his mount, nodding his head toward Medina); the editor gives its routes in the Musnad and Muslim and says the verse was not revealed about this but serves as evidence. Others said it came about people who could not tell the direction of prayer and prayed in different directions, God telling them "there is My face, and it is your direction", their prayer standing, from Amir ibn Rabi'a (that they were with the Prophet on a dark night and a man gathered stones to make a place of prayer, and at dawn they found they had prayed in the wrong direction, and the verse was revealed); the editor shows the chain of this report is weak, with the remarks of al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Kathir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 530} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 531}
