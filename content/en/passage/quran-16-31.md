## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "Gardens of Eden they will enter, beneath which rivers flow; they will have therein whatever they wish. Thus Allah rewards the righteous": meaning, Allah mentioned by saying: "Gardens of Eden" means gardens for the Divine Place. We have explained the differences among interpreters regarding the meaning of Eden in the past that suffices to repeat it. "They will enter it" means: they enter the Gardens of Eden. There are three aspects to the nominative "gardens": first, it is nominative at the beginning, the second is by returning from the remembrance in the phrase "they enter it"; and the third: it is the predicate of blessings. The meaning if you make the predicate of blessings, it is: And indeed the dwelling of the righteous is the gardens of Eden, and "they enter it" in the case of the state, as it is said: "Yes, the abode is a dwelling place you dwell." It may also be that if the statement with this interpretation is "they enter it" from the connection to "gardens of Eden." And His saying: "rivers flow beneath it" means: rivers flow beneath its trees.
