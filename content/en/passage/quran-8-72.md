## Summary
Verse 72 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 72 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 72 (pp. 77-83)**

Al-Tabari says: those who believed in God and His messenger and emigrated, that is, left their people, clan and homes and went out from them as their people shunned them, and strove in God's way, wearing themselves out in fighting God's enemies among the unbelievers for God's religion, which He made a road to His mercy and rescue from His punishment, and those who sheltered the Messenger and the emigrants with him, giving them lodging in their homes when their own people drove them out, and helped them against the polytheist enemies: these, the Emigrants and the Helpers, are allies of one another, one hand against whoever disbelieves in God, and brothers to each other rather than to their unbelieving relatives. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of emigration, striving, God's way and sheltering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 78}

Some said the sense is that they were nearer to one another in inheritance, God having made them heirs of one another through emigration and aid rather than kinship, and that He later abrogated this in [[passage/quran-8-75|8:75]] and [[passage/quran-33-6|33:6]]. Report 16331, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: it means in inheritance, which He gave to the Emigrants and Helpers rather than to blood relatives; they acted on that until the verse on those bound by kinship came down on inheritance, abrogating the earlier one, and inheritance passed to blood relatives. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 78}

Report 16332, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his grandfather: there is no emigration after the conquest, only martyrdom; in the Messenger's time the believers held three positions: the believing emigrant who parted from his people and went to believers in their homes and property; those who sheltered and aided, announced what the emigrants announced and drew swords on those who denied; these two God made allies who inherited from one another, the Helper inheriting from the Emigrant through religious alliance; and the one who believed and did not emigrate, who did not inherit because he neither emigrated nor aided, God having declared the emigrant believers free of his inheritance. Those who sheltered and aided owed it to those others, when they asked for help in religion, to help them, except against a people with whom the Prophet had a treaty. Then God sent down the joining of every kinsman to his kin among believers, emigrant or not, giving each believer a fixed share in 8:75 and in [[passage/quran-9-71|9:71]]. The editor flags a place where the printed text adds what the manuscript lacks. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 79}

Report 16333, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the three closing verses of al-Anfal mention the alliance the Messenger set between the Emigrants and the Helpers in inheritance, abrogated by the last of them. Report 16334, from Abdullah ibn Kathir, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: it reached us that this concerned inheritance, the emigrant and non-emigrant believers not inheriting from one another; then the verse on those bound by kinship came down and they inherited though they had not emigrated; the same report adds Mujahid's statement above. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 80}

Report 16335, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: for a time Muslims inherited by emigration, a Muslim bedouin inheriting nothing from an emigrant; this was abrogated and God joined those bound by kinship in 33:6, the allowance for kindness to friends being toward the polytheists, so bequest was allowed but there was no inheritance for them; inheritance followed religion, Muslims inheriting from one another, and people of two religions not inheriting. The editor notes differences between the printed text and the manuscript in the wording of "joined" and of "bequest". Report 16336, from Ikrima and al-Hasan, through Ibn Humayd and Yahya ibn Wadih: the bedouin did not inherit from the emigrant nor the emigrant from him, and God abrogated this with the verse on those bound by kinship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 80}

Report 16337, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: the alliance concerned inheritance; those who believed and did not emigrate are the bedouin, with no share in inheritance; "if they seek your help in religion" because they are Muslims, help is owed except against a people with whom there is a treaty; the unbelievers' alliance with one another is likewise in inheritance; then the fixed shares and inheritance rules abrogated it, and those bound by kinship are those who had inherited on emigration, so bedouin and Emigrants inherited from one another. The editor explains a transposition in this sentence between the printed text and the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 81}

Al-Tabari then explains the second half of the verse: those who believed in God and His messenger but did not emigrate from their unbelieving people or leave the land of unbelief for the land of Islam: you, believing emigrants, have nothing of their alliance, that is their aid and inheritance, until they emigrate from the land of war to the land of Islam. He notes that he had already given the view of those who say alliance here means inheritance and says he will give what he has of it later. The editor refers to the explanation of emigration on p. 77. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 81}

He continues: if these who believed and did not emigrate ask your help in religion, because they are of your religion, against your enemies and theirs among the polytheists, then, Emigrants and Helpers, aid is owed, unless they ask it against a people with whom you have a treaty, a covenant by which one side bound the other not to fight; and God sees what you do in what He commanded and forbade of your alliance with one another, abandoning alliance with those who believed and did not emigrate, and helping them when they ask in religion, and the other obligations; nothing of it is hidden from Him. The editor refers to the explanation of "covenant" and "seeing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 82}

Report 16338, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: Muslims inherited by emigration and the Prophet made them brothers, so they inherited by Islam and emigration, while a man who became Muslim and did not emigrate did not inherit his brother; this was abrogated by 33:6. Report 16339, from al-Zuhri, through Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: the Prophet took a pledge from a man entering Islam to establish prayer, give zakat, perform the pilgrimage and fast Ramadan, and never to see the fire of a polytheist without being at war with him; the editor explains this as living far enough from a polytheist not to see his fire, a prohibition of neighbouring polytheists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 83}

Report 16340, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: if the Muslim bedouin ask your help against their enemy, Emigrants and Helpers, you must help them except against a people with whom you have a treaty. Report 16341, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: when the Prophet died he left people in four positions: the believing emigrant; the Helpers; the believing bedouin who had not emigrated, whom the Prophet helped if he asked and whom he left at his leave otherwise, and whom it was a duty to help if he sought help in religion, which is the meaning of the verse; and the fourth, those who followed in goodness. Report 16342, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman, on the passage from the start of the verse to the end of the sura: the Messenger died leaving people in four positions: the believing emigrant, the Muslim bedouin, those who sheltered and aided, and those who followed in goodness. The editor records the manuscript's reading in two places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 83}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
