## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "He said, O Adam, tell them their names" al-Tabari holds that God showed the angels they were ignorant of the workings of His plan until He disclosed it, just as they were ignorant of the names shown to them, and that they and all others know only what their Lord teaches them, He granting knowledge to whom He wills. "Tell them" refers to the angels, and "their names" to those shown. When Adam told them and they were certain their statement had been mistaken, their Lord said "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth", a reproof for their question; the unseen is what is hidden from sight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 496} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 497}
He reports from Ibn Abbas that the address is to the angels in particular and that no one else knows the unseen, and from Ibn Zayd that God told them that as they did not know the names they had no knowledge of what He kept from them, that some in the earth would disobey and some obey, and that the filling of Hell had already been decreed; when they saw what knowledge Adam was given they acknowledged his excellence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 497}
On "I know what you reveal and what you were concealing" he reports from Ibn Abbas, al-Suddi, Said ibn Jubayr and Sufyan that what they revealed was their saying "will You place in it one who corrupts", and what they concealed was what Iblis kept secret in himself, his pride and his refusal to prostrate to Adam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 498} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 499}
He then gives another view from al-Hasan al-Basri and Qatada: seeing Adam's wondrous creation, some angels whispered to others that God would never create anyone more honoured by Him than they. The editor cautions that the questioner in the first report, al-Hasan ibn Dinar, is a discredited narrator, while the chain by which Mahdi ibn Maymun heard al-Hasan's answer directly is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 499}

Al-Tabari reports from al-Rabi' ibn Anas that what they revealed was their saying "will You place in it one who corrupts", and what they hid among themselves was that their Lord would create nothing but they would be more knowing and more honoured. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 500}
Al-Tabari's own choice is Ibn Abbas's reading: God knows what they showed with their tongues and what they hid within, and what they hid was the opposition and pride that Iblis held inside. He gives as reasons that the view of al-Hasan and Qatada has no support from the Book or from a report that carries authority, while the Qur'anic account of Iblis, who refused and grew arrogant when called to prostrate, supports Ibn Abbas. To the objection that the plural wording rules this out, he answers that the Arabs speak of part of a group in the form used for all, as in "the army was killed" when only some were, and cites the verse of surah al-Hujurat said to concern one man of Banu Tamim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 501}
