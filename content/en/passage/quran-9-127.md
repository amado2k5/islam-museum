**Commentary on verse 127 (pp. 582-584)**

Al-Tabari says: when a surah of the Qur'an comes down that exposes the faults of these hypocrites whom God described in this surah, and they are with the Messenger of God, they look at one another, wondering whether anyone sees them if they speak or whisper about the faults of the people so that he might tell them, then they rise and leave the Messenger, not listening to the recitation of the surah containing their faults. Then He begins afresh "God has turned their hearts away", turning the hearts of these hypocrites away from good, success and belief in God and His Messenger, "because they are a people who do not understand", that is, God did this abandonment to them and turned their hearts from good things because they are a people who do not grasp God's admonitions, out of arrogance and hypocrisy. The editor refers to earlier discussion of turning and understanding, and corrects the printed reading by the manuscript in a note. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 582}

The grammarians differed about what brings in the interrogative. Some Basran grammarians said that "they looked at one another, 'does anyone see you?'" is as though he said "they said to one another", since their looking in this place was a gesture or something like it; some Kufan grammarians said it is simply: when a surah came down they said to one another, does anyone see you; the views continue on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 582}

Another of them said that "looking" here does not mean "saying" but the looking that brings in the question, as the Arabs say "look together which of them knows most" and "gather, which of them is the better jurist", meaning gather to consider; this is what brings in the question. Reports 17498 to 17500 are from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', his father, Shu'ba and Abu Hamza; through Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Umayr ibn Tamim; and through Abu Mu'awiya, al-A'mash and Abu al-Duha: do not say "we turned away from the prayer", for some people turned away and God turned their hearts away; say instead "we have completed the prayer". The editor says he found no notice of Umayr ibn Tamim al-Tha'labi except in al-Jarh wa al-Ta'dil and that the name differs in the copies. Report 17501 is from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father and uncle: they are the hypocrites. Report 17502 is from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "does anyone see you" asks whether anyone heard your report and saw you and told him, when something came down that told of their talk; they are the hypocrites. Report 17503 is from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Adam, Shu'ba and Abu Ishaq: do not say "we turned away from the prayer", for God rebuked a people, saying they turned away and God turned their hearts; say "we have prayed". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 583}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
