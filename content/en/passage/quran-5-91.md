## Summary
Verse 91 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 91 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 91: "Satan only wants to cause enmity and hatred between you", p. 565**

Al-Tabari says: Satan only wants for you the drinking of wine and gambling with arrows, and makes it seem good to you, wishing to cause enmity and hatred between you through them, so that you oppose and hate one another and he scatters your affairs after God joined you in faith and in the brotherhood of Islam; he would turn you from the remembrance of God by wine's intoxication overcoming you and by your being busy with gambling, and from the prayer your Lord made obligatory.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 565}

**Commentary on verse 91, continued: "So will you not abstain?" and the occasions of revelation, pp. 566-572**

Al-Tabari says: will you not abstain from drinking wine and gambling with arrows, and act on what your Lord commanded of keeping the prayer at its times and keeping His remembrance. The interpreters differed on the occasion. Some said it came down over Umar ibn al-Khattab, who told the Prophet the harm that comes of drinking and asked God to forbid it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 566}

Report 12512, from Abu Maysara, says Umar said "O God, give us a full explanation about wine", and the verse of the surah of the cow came down; he was called and it was read to him, and he repeated his prayer; then the verse of the women came down, when the Prophet's herald called that a drunken man must not approach prayer; he was called, it was read to him, and he repeated his prayer, and the verse of the table came down, and when the reader reached "so will you not abstain" Umar said "we have abstained, we have abstained". Report 12513 adds that wine takes away mind and wealth, and 12514 to 12516 give the like. The editor traces the report to Ahmad, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Tirmidhi and al-Hakim, and notes that some judged it sound and that Abu Zur'a's doubt whether Abu Maysara heard from Umar is answered.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 567}

Report 12517, from Muhammad ibn Qays, says when the Prophet came to Medina, people asked him about wine and gambling and the verse of the cow came down; they said there was a concession in it and went on drinking and asking forgiveness, until a man led the sunset prayer, recited the chapter of the unbelievers, and did not know what he was reciting; the verse of the women came down, and they left drinking when prayer time came, until the verse of the table came down and they said "we have abstained, Lord".  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 569}

Others said it came down over Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. Report 12518, from Mus'ab ibn Sa'd on his father, says a man of the Ansar made food and invited them, they drank until merry, and the Ansar and Quraysh boasted against one another; an Ansari took a camel's jawbone and struck Sa'd's nose, splitting it, and the verse came down. Reports 12519 to 12521, from Sa'd and Salim ibn Abdullah, give the like, with Sa'd and his companions drinking, fighting and breaking his nose. The editor traces the report to Ahmad, Muslim, Abu Dawud al-Tayalisi and al-Bayhaqi and explains its wording.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 570}

Others said it came down over two tribes of the Ansar. Report 12522, from Ibn Abbas, says two tribes of the Ansar drank until drunk and played roughly with one another; when they woke, each saw marks on his face and beard and said his brother had done this to him, though they were brothers with no grudge in their hearts, until grudges arose, and the verse came down. Some said their brothers had died with it in their bellies, killed at Badr and Uhud, and the verse "no blame on those who believe and do good for what they ate" came down.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 571}

Report 12523, from Buraida, says that he was with three or four men drinking wine on a sand patch when he got up to greet the Prophet, and wine had been forbidden; he went back and read the two verses to them to "so will you not abstain", some having drunk part and left part in the vessel, and they poured out what was in their vessels and said "we have abstained, our Lord". The editor traces the report to al-Bayhaqi, al-Hakim, al-Haythami and others.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 572}

**Commentary on verse 91, continued: enmity through gambling, and al-Tabari's choice, pp. 573-574**

Others said the enmity and hatred came from gambling and not from drunkenness; report 12524, from Qatada, says that in the pre-Islamic period a man would gamble away his family and property, then sit stripped of his wealth watching it in another's hands, and this bred enmity and hatred between them, so God forbade it. Al-Tabari holds that God named these things abomination and commanded avoiding them; that it is possible the verse came down over Umar's prayer, or over what befell Sa'd from the Ansari when they were drunk, or over the enmity that comes to a man who lost his property gambling toward the one who beat him; and that he has no decisive report on which it was. Whichever it was, the ruling binds all those under obligation and ignorance of the occasion does not harm them: avoiding wine, gambling, altars and arrows is an obligation on everyone to whom the verse comes.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 574}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
