**Commentary on verse 53: "Those who believe will say: are these the ones who swore by God their strongest oaths that they were with you? Their deeds have come to nothing and they have become losers", pp. 407-408**

Al-Tabari says the reciters differed over "and those who believe will say". The people of Medina read "those who believe say" without the conjunction, and their copies have it so; the sense is that the hypocrites will wake up regretful when God brings the conquest or a command from Him, and the believers say in amazement at their hypocrisy, lying and boldness against God in their false oaths: are these the ones who swore to us by God that they were with us, while lying in their oaths? Mujahid went to this meaning in report 12176, from Ibn Jurayj.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 407}

Some Basrans read it with the conjunction and the verb in the subjunctive, joined to "perhaps God will bring the conquest"; the reader said the sense is: perhaps God will bring the conquest, and perhaps those who believe will say, comparing it to "I ate bread and milk" and to a line of verse. The sense then is: perhaps God brings the conquest or a command from Him, and the hypocrites regret what they hid, and perhaps the believers then say: are these the ones who swore falsely by God? It is also in the copies of the people of Iraq with the conjunction, and the Kufans read it with the conjunction and the verb in the indicative, as a future, free of what makes a verb subjunctive or jussive; its sense is: they will wake up regretting, and those who believe will say, with the verb beginning a fresh clause. Al-Tabari then begins to state that the reading on which they themselves recite is the one keeping the conjunction.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 408}

We stop here at the foot of p. 408, at the start of al-Tabari's choice of the reading "and he says" with the conjunction kept; the rest of verse 53 follows in the next batch, from p. 409.
