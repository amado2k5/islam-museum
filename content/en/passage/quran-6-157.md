## Summary
Verse 157 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 157 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "or lest you say: had the Book been sent down to us, we would have been better guided than they", God says that this is a blessed Book He sent down so that the idolaters of Quraysh should not say that the Book was sent only to two groups before them, or should not say that had the Book been sent to them as to those two groups, with commands and prohibitions and the error of their way shown against what is right, they "would have been better guided than they", that is, more upright on the way of truth, more faithful to the Book and better in deed than the two groups to whom the Book was sent before. God says "a clear proof has come to you from your Lord": a Book in your own clear Arabic tongue, a plain proof against you from your Lord; "guidance" is a clarification of the truth and a criterion between right and wrong; and "mercy" is for whoever acts on it and follows it. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "guidance" and "proof". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 243}

He gives (14189) from al-Suddi that "a clear proof has come to you" means a clear Arabic tongue, when you did not know the study of the two groups and when you said that had a book come to you, you would have been better guided than they; and (14190) from Qatada that this is the saying of the Arab disbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 243}

Then, on "who does greater wrong than one who denies God's signs and turns away from them?", he says: who is more mistaken in deed and more transgressing than you, idolaters, who deny God's proofs and evidences, which are His signs; "turned away from them" means he turned away from them after they came to him, neither believing in them nor affirming their reality. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "wrong" and "sign". The report is put in the form of speaking of an absent party, though those meant are the idolaters of Quraysh addressed by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 244}

He gives (14191) from Ibn Abbas that "turned away from them" means turned aside from them; (14192) from Mujahid that "they turn away" means they turn aside, and "sadf" is turning aside; (14193) from Qatada the same; and (14194) from al-Suddi that it means he barred himself from them. As for "We shall repay those who turn away from Our signs with an evil punishment", it means God will requite those who turn away from His signs and proofs, do not reflect on them and do not recognise their reality so as to believe in what they point to of God's oneness, the truth of His Prophet's prophethood and the truth of what he brought from his Lord; "evil punishment" is severe retribution, the punishment of the Fire God prepared for those who disbelieve in Him; and "for what they turned away" means God does this as requital for their turning away from His signs in this world and not accepting what their Prophet Muhammad brought them. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "turning away" and "requital". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 245}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
