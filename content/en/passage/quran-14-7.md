**Commentary on verse 7 (pp. 526-528)**

On "and when your Lord proclaimed: if you are grateful I will surely increase you, and if you disbelieve, My punishment is severe", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: remember also when your Lord informed you. "Ta'adhdhana" is the "tafa''ala" form from "adhana", and the Arabs sometimes put "tafa''ala" in place of "af'ala", as they say "aw'adtuhu" and "taw'adtuhu" with one meaning. "Adhana" is to inform, as al-Harith ibn Hilliza said at the opening of his famous poem, that Asma' announced to us her parting, and that many a one who stays wearies of the staying; by "announced to us" he means informed us. It is reported of Ibn Mas'ud that he used to read "wa-idh qala rabbukum". Report 20583, from Ibn Mas'ud, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Sufyan and al-A'mash: so. Report 20584, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "when your Lord proclaimed", when your Lord said; that is the "ta'adhdhun". On "if you are grateful I will surely increase you" he says: if you are grateful to your Lord by obeying Him {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 526}

in what He commanded and forbade you, I will surely increase you in His benefactions with you and His favours on you, over what He has given you of rescue from Pharaoh's people and deliverance from their torment. Something else has been said on this, which is what is related in report 20585, from Ali ibn Salih, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, al-Husayn ibn al-Hasan and Ibn al-Mubarak: "if you are grateful I will surely increase you", he said, that is, from My obedience. Report 20586, from Ali ibn Salih, through al-Muthanna, Yazid and Ibn al-Mubarak: similarly. Report 20587, from Sufyan, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq and Abu Ahmad: "if you are grateful I will surely increase you", he said, from My obedience. Report 20588, from al-Hasan, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Malik ibn Mighwal and Aban ibn Abi Ayyash: "if you are grateful I will surely increase you", he said, from My obedience. Abu Ja'far says: no sense can be understood for this view, because obedience has not been mentioned in this place so that it be said, if you thank Me for it I will increase you in it; what has run is the report of God's favouring Moses's people in His saying "remember God's favour upon you", then He told them that God had let them know that if they thank Him for this favour He will increase them; so what is needed for understanding is that the sense is that He will increase them of His favours, not of obedience, which has not been mentioned, unless it is meant, if you are grateful and obey Me in thanks I will increase you in the means to thanks that help you, which would be a ground. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 527}

On "and if you disbelieve, My punishment is severe" he says: if you disbelieve, people, God's favour and deny it by leaving off thanking Him for it, going against His command and prohibition and riding His disobedience, "My punishment is severe", I punish you as I punish whoever of My creation disbelieves in Me. One of the Basrans used to say, of the meaning of "wa-idh ta'adhdhana rabbukum", that it is "wa-ta'adhdhana rabbukum", and that "idh" is among the redundant particles; we have shown the unsoundness of that earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 528}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
