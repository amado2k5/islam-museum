## Summary
Verse 119 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/repentance-of-the-three-accepted|Repentance of the Three Accepted]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 119 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 119 (pp. 558-561)**

Al-Tabari says: God tells the believers the way of deliverance from His punishment: "you who believe in God and His Messenger, fear God and be mindful of Him by performing His obligations and avoiding His limits, and be in this world of the people of God's friendship and obedience, so that in the next you will be with the truthful in Paradise", meaning those who were truthful to God in believing in Him, their deeds confirming their words, and not of the hypocrites whose deeds belie their words. The sense is: be with the truthful in the hereafter by fearing God in this world, as in verse 69 of Surat al-Nisa, which is [[passage/quran-4-69|verse 4:69]]. He says this because a hypocrite's being with the believers does him no good unless he does as they do, and if he does what they do he is one of them. Hence some interpreters explained it as with Abu Bakr and Umar, or with the Prophet and the emigrants. The editor corrects a printed sentence by the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 558}

Reports 17451 and 17452 are from Nafi, through Ibn Humayd, Ya'qub and Zayd ibn Aslam, and through Ibn Waki', Habwayh Abu Yazid and Ya'qub al-Qummi: the three who were left behind were told "fear God and be with the truthful", meaning with Muhammad and his companions. Reports 17453 and 17454 are from al-Dahhak and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: with Abu Bakr and Umar and their companions; report 17455 is from Ibn Jurayj: with the truthful emigrants. The editor says Abu Hashim al-Rummani is trustworthy though his name is disputed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 559}

It is reported that Ibn Mas'ud read it "be of the truthful" and took it as a prohibition by God of lying. Report 17456 is from Abu Ubayda ibn Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, through al-Muthanna, Adam al-Asqalani, Shu'ba and Amr ibn Murra: Ibn Mas'ud said lying is not permitted in earnest or in jest, recite the verse if you wish, and that is his reading "of the truthful"; do you see any licence for lying? Reports 17457 to 17461 give the same from him through several chains, in one of which the transmitter is unsure whether he said "of" or "with" the truthful, though in his book it is "with". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 560}

Al-Tabari says the sound interpretation is the one given from Nafi and al-Dahhak, since the copies of the mushaf all agree on "with the truthful", the reading he does not allow anyone to depart from; Abdullah's interpretation on his reading is sound, but the reading is otherwise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 561}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
