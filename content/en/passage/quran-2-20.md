## In al-Tabari's commentary
This verse is bound up with al-Tabari's commentary on "the lightning almost snatches away their sight; whenever it lights for them they walk in it", set out under [[passage/quran-2-19|verse 19]] (pp. 356-358) because he reads the two verses as one parable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 356} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 358}
On "and when it darkens over them they stand still" he says that whenever the hypocrites see nothing in Islam to please them in this world, when the believers are tried with hardship, they stay in their hypocrisy and hold fast to their error, like the traveller in the rainstorm bewildered when the lightning's light dies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 358} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 359}
On "and had God willed, He would have taken away their hearing and sight" he says God singled out hearing and sight because they were mentioned in the two verses by way of parable, and that this is a warning that He is able to bring His vengeance on them, so that they fear His might and hasten to repent; he gives Ibn Abbas that it is for leaving the truth after knowing it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 359} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 360}
He explains "dhahaba bi-sam'ihim" as meaning "He would have removed", the Arabs saying "dhahabtu bi-basarihi" when they add the preposition and "adhhabtu basarahu" when they omit it. To the question why hearing is singular and sight plural, he records a Kufan grammarian's view that hearing is singular as a verbal noun, a Basran's that it is singular in form and plural in sense, and holds that singular or plural would be sound for either given what the context shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 360} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 361}
He explains "God has power over all things" as God describing Himself as able because He is warning the hypocrites of His might, "qadir" meaning "capable". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 361} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 362}
