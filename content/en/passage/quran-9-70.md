**Commentary on verse 70 (pp. 344-346)**

Al-Tabari says: has the news not come to these hypocrites who hide unbelief in God and forbid belief in Him and His Messenger of those before them, the nations before them, when they disobeyed Our messengers and opposed Our command, and what befell them of Our punishment? Then the Almighty made clear who those nations were, saying "the people of Noah", the word for people put in the genitive because it is in apposition to "those", which is in the genitive. The sense is: has the news not come to these hypocrites of the people of Noah and what I did to them when they denied My messenger Noah and opposed My command, did I not drown them in the flood; of 'Ad, when they disobeyed My messenger Hud, did I not destroy them with a furious, violent wind; of Thamud, when they disobeyed My messenger Salih, did I not destroy them with the quake, leaving them lifeless in their courtyards; of the people of Abraham, when they disobeyed him and rejected what came to them from God of the truth, did I not strip them of the favour and destroy their king Nimrod; of the people of Madyan, son of Abraham, did I not destroy them with the punishment of the day of shadow when they denied My messenger Shu'ayb; and of the cities turned upside down, whose highest part became their lowest, when they disobeyed My messenger Lot and denied what came to them from Me of the truth. The Almighty says: do these hypocrites who mock God, His verses and His Messenger feel safe that the way of their predecessors among the nations be taken with them in taking vengeance, in hastening disgrace and penalty to them in this world, and that there befall them for denying My messenger Muhammad what befell those for denying Our messengers when they came to them with clear proofs? The editor refers to earlier explanations of tidings, the overturning and the clear proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 345}

Report 16936, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "the overturned cities" are the people of Lot, whose land was turned over so that its highest became its lowest; report 16937, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they are the people of Lot. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 345}

If someone asks: if "the overturned cities" means the people of Lot, why is it in the plural and not the singular? The answer is that they were three villages, so it was put in the plural, and with the feminine plural ending, as in God's words in al-Najm, "and the overturned city He brought down", [[passage/quran-53-53|53:53]]. If he asks: how is it said "their messengers came to them with clear proofs" when only one was sent to them? The answer is that a messenger came to each village of the overturned cities calling them to God, so that the messengers of the Messenger of God whom he sent to them to call to God on his message were messengers to them, as the Arabs call a people attributed to Abu Fudayk the Kharijite "the Fudaykat", though Abu Fudayk is one man, but since his companions were attributed to him, he being their chief, they were called so. It may also be said that the sense is that their messengers from God came to the people of Noah, 'Ad, Thamud and the rest of the nations God mentioned in this verse with the clear proofs. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 346}

As for "so God did not wrong them", the Almighty says God destroyed these nations He mentions only for their crimes and their wronging themselves and their deserving of great punishment from God, not as an injustice from God to them nor by His placing a penalty on one not fit for it, for God is wise, with no flaw in His management and no error in His decree; the people He destroyed wronged themselves by disobeying God and denying His messengers until they angered their Lord against them, and the word of punishment became due on them and they were punished. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 346}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
