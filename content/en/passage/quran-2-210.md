## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "are they waiting for anything but that God come to them in canopies of cloud, and the angels": al-Tabari says it means, are those who deny Muhammad and what he brought waiting for anything but that God come to them in canopies of cloud and the angels? The readers then differed in the reading of "and the angels". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 260}

The readers differed over "and the angels". Some read it raised, joined to the name of God: are they waiting for anything but that God and the angels come to them in canopies of cloud; from Abu al-'Aliya on the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b, "God and the angels come in canopies of cloud": the angels come in canopies of cloud and God comes in what He wills (4032), and Abu Ja'far al-Razi gives the same, citing the verse of al-Furqan (25:25) (4033). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 261} Others read it lowered, joined to "canopies", meaning in canopies of cloud and in the angels. They also differed between "zulal" and "zilal"; those who read "zulal" take it as the plural of "zulla", and those who read "zilal" also take it as the plural of "zulla", as "khulla" has the plural "khilal", though it may be the plural of "zill". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 261} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 262} Al-Tabari chooses "in zulal of cloud" on the strength of a report from the Prophet that "in the cloud there are arches in which God comes, surrounded", the singular of zulal being zulla, which is the arch, and by following the script of the codex; and in general, where the meanings agree but the readings differ and nothing separates them except the script of the codex, the reading that fits the codex's spelling is preferred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 262} He holds the raised "and the angels", joined to the name of God, to be the sounder, as reported from Ubayy, because God says in other places that the angels come to them, as in "and your Lord comes with the angels, rank upon rank" (89:22) and "are they waiting for anything but that the angels come to them or your Lord comes" (6:158). He answers the supposition that "the angel" in the singular differs from "the angels" by noting that the Arabs use the singular for the plural, as in "so-and-so has much dirham and dinar" or "the camel and the sheep perished". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 263}

The interpreters then differed whether "canopies of cloud" belongs to God's coming or to the angels'. Some said to God's coming; from Mujahid: it is other than ordinary cloud, which existed only for the Children of Israel in their wandering, and God comes in it on the Day of Resurrection (4034); from Qatada: God comes to them and the angels come to them at death (4035); from 'Ikrima: arches of cloud with the angels around it, and another said, the angels with death (4036). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 263} Al-Tabari remarks that if 'Ikrima meant "around it" to refer to the cloud, the reading would have to be lowered, and if he meant around the Lord, it matches the others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 264} Others said it belongs to the angels' coming while the Lord comes in what He wills; from al-Rabi': that is the Day of Resurrection, the angels come in canopies of cloud and the Lord comes in what He wills (4037). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 264} Al-Tabari prefers the first, that it belongs to the Lord's coming, so the meaning is: are they waiting for anything but that God come to them in canopies of cloud and the angels come; he cites from Ibn 'Abbas the Prophet's saying that in the cloud there are arches in which God comes, surrounded (4038); the editor notes that al-Tabari's chain includes Zam'a ibn Salih, who is weak, and Salama ibn Wahram, who is reliable, the fault lying with Zam'a, that the report is weak, and that the version stopping at Ibn 'Abbas is probably sounder. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 264} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 265} the opening of [[passage/quran-2-210|2:210]] means "they are not waiting". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 265}

They then differed over how the Lord's coming is described. Some said there is no description beyond what He described of Himself, of coming, arriving and descending, and it is not permitted to anyone to elaborate on it except by a report from God or from a sent messenger, since speech about God's attributes and names is not permitted by deduction. Others said His coming is like the coming of someone going from place to place and moving from one location to another. Others said the meaning is "are they waiting for anything but that God's command comes to them", as one says "we feared that the Umayyads would come to us", meaning their rule. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 265} Others said that His reward, reckoning and punishment come, as in "rather, the scheming of night and day" (34:33), and as one says "the governor cut off the thief's hand" when his aides did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 266} Al-Tabari says the sense of the sentence is: those who refuse to enter al-silm entirely and follow the footsteps of Satan are waiting for nothing but that God come to them in canopies of cloud and decide their affair as He decides. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 266}

At this point (4039) al-Tabari reports from Abu Hurayra a long report from the Prophet about the standing place on the Day of Resurrection: people are made to stand in one place for about seventy years, not looked at and not judged, until they weep tears and then blood, and they seek someone to intercede with their Lord so that He judges between them; they go to Adam and then to the prophets one by one, who each decline, until they come to Muhammad, who prostrates before the Throne and then is permitted to intercede; then the people of the first heaven descend, then the second, the third and the rest, each in multiples of those before, and when asked whether their Lord is among them they say no, He is coming, until the Almighty descends in canopies of cloud with the angels, a caller calls, and God judges between His creatures, jinn, humans and animals, until He takes retribution for the hornless sheep from the horned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 266} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 267} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 268} The editor comments that the report is weak on two counts: Isma'il ibn Rafi' al-Madani, who is very weak, graded so by Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in, Abu Hatim, Ibn Sa'd and others, of whom Ibn Hibban said he was a righteous man who turned reports about until rejected narrations came to dominate his hadith; and an unnamed Ansari man; and the wording has a rejected quality. He adds that Ibn Kathir referred to it and to al-Tabarani's longer report through the same man, calling it famous yet very strange with some wording rejected, and noting it was said to have been assembled from many reports into one account; and that Sadr al-Din ibn Abi al-'Izz in his commentary on al-Tahawiyya treated the two as one report and attributed it to several authorities, which the editor finds open to question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 267} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 268}

Al-Tabari says this report shows the error of Qatada's view that the angels meant are those who come at death, because the Prophet said they come after the Hour has risen, in the place of reckoning when the heavens split, and similar is reported from a group of the Companions and Successors, whom he skips to avoid lengthening the book. It also supports his choice of the raised reading "and the angels" and the error of the lowered one, since the angels come to the people of the Resurrection before their Lord comes to them in canopies of cloud, unless the reader of the lowered form meant "and among the angels who come to the people of the standing place", which would be a possible interpretation though far from the scholars' view, the indication of the Book and the established reports of the Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 268} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 269}

On "and the matter is decided, and to God all matters return": it means that judgement is settled in justice between creatures, as in what is reported from Abu Hurayra of taking right for every wronged person from every wrongdoer, even retribution for the hornless from the horned. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 269} To God goes the judgement between His creatures on the Day of Resurrection and the ruling on their affairs from this world, of their wronging one another, the transgressor's overstepping God's limits and the good-doer's good conduct; He judges between wrongdoers, rewards the good with good and the evil-doers as He sees fit, and shows favour by pardoning those who were not disbelievers. Though all matters of this world and the next begin with Him and return to Him, people wrong one another in this world and some of His servants judge between them, one unjustly, another justly, a ruling being carried out on some and not on others; so He informed His servants that all of it returns to Him at the Resurrection, where there is no wrong and none who can resist the execution of His ruling, and weak and strong, poor and rich are equal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 269} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 270} The definite article is attached to "matters" because He means all matters, as in "honey pleases me" and "the mule is stronger than the donkey". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 270}
