**Commentary on verse 90 (pp. 416-419)**

Al-Tabari says: the excuse-makers of the Bedouin came to the Messenger of God to be given leave to stay behind, and those who denied God and His Messenger sat, failing to come to the Messenger of God and strive with him, those of them who lied and made excuse with falsehood; a painful punishment will strike those among them who denied the oneness of God and the prophethood of His prophet Muhammad. If someone says: "the excuse-makers came", yet you know that in the speech of the Arabs a mu'adhdhir is one who makes an excuse in a matter without pressing it or making it firm, and that is not the description of these, whose description is that they had strained to find what would let them march with the Messenger of God against his enemy and were eager for it but found no way to it, so that they are more fitting and deserving to be described as having "had an excuse"; and if they are so described the correct reading is what Ibn Abbas read. Report 17073, from al-Dahhak, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Ibn Abi Hammad, Bishr ibn Umara and Abu Rawq: Ibn Abbas used to read "the excuse-makers came" with the light form, saying they are the people of excuse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 416}

It is said that the meaning is other than what you assumed and that it means "those who make excuse came from the Bedouin", but the "t", when it came next to the "dh", was assimilated into it and the two became a doubled "dh" because of the closeness of their points of articulation, as one says "they remember" for "they are reminded", and the "ayn" moved to the fatha because the fatha of the "t" in the form "those who make excuse" was transferred to it. The Arabs sometimes turn the sense of making an excuse to having an excuse, saying "so-and-so made excuse in such a matter" meaning he had an excuse, and from that is the line of Labid in which he says that whoever weeps a full year has made excuse, that is, has had an excuse. The interpreters nonetheless differed over the description of these people whom God described as having come to the Messenger of God excusing themselves. Some said they were lying in their excuse, so God did not excuse them. Report 17074, from Qatada, through Abu Ubayda Abd al-Warith ibn Abd al-Samad, his father and al-Husayn: Qatada used to read "the excuse-makers of the Bedouin came", saying they excused themselves with lies; report 17075, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Yahya ibn Zakariyya and Ibn Jurayj: a band from Banu Ghifar came and made excuse, and God did not excuse them. The editor refers to al-Farra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 417}

So those whom we have mentioned reported that these people were people of an excuse made with falsehood and not with truth, and it is not permissible to describe them as having an excuse except as having had an excuse in excusing themselves with falsehood, and as for an excuse with truth, as those whose words we cited said, it is not permissible to describe them so. Some also said they came as excuse-makers without seriousness, offering what they did not intend to do; whoever takes it so has no difficulty in it, though I know of none of the scholars of Qur'anic interpretation who took its interpretation to that, so as to favour the view. Moreover, the reading of the reciters of the great cities, with the doubling of the "dh", suffices in place of "the excuse-makers" and is a proof of the soundness of the interpretation of those who took it as making excuse, because the people so described were not charged with a duty in which they were excused, but were two groups, either a diligent, obedient one or a hypocritical transgressor opposing God's command; so in neither group is there one described as a making of excuse in going out with the Messenger of God, only one who made an excuse with pressing, or one who excused himself. Since that is so, and the authority of the reciters is agreed on the doubling of the "dh" in the word, it is known that its meaning is the interpretation we described. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 418}

It is also reported of Mujahid that he agreed with Ibn Abbas on this, in report 17076, from Humayd, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr and Ibn Uyayna: Mujahid read "the excuse-makers came" in the light form and said they are the people of excuse. Report 17077, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the excuse-makers, as it reached me, were a band of Banu Ghifar, among them Khufaf ibn Ayma ibn Rahda, then the story went on about the people of excuse until it came to the words "nor those who, when they came to you for mounts", the verse. The editor notes that this report was cut short in the manuscript and the printed text and that he completed it from Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 419}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
