**Commentary on verse 2 (pp. 512-514)**

On "God, to whom belongs what is in the heavens and what is in the earth; and woe to the disbelievers from a severe punishment", al-Tabari says: the readers differed in reading it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 512}

Most of the readers of Medina and Syria read it "Allahu alladhi lahu ma fi al-samawat", with the name "Allah" in the nominative as the subject and "alladhi lahu ma fi al-samawat" as its predicate. Most of the readers of Iraq, Kufa and Basra read it "Allahi alladhi", with the name "Allah" in the genitive, following "the Almighty, the Praiseworthy", both being in the genitive. The scholars of Arabic differed over its interpretation when so read. It is reported of Abu Amr ibn al-Ala' that he read it in the genitive and said its meaning is "by their Lord's leave to the path of God, the Almighty, the Praiseworthy, to whom belongs what is in the heavens"; he said it is of the postponed that means to be put first, and gave as an example someone's saying "I passed by the elegant Abd Allah", speech in which the description is put in place of the noun and then the noun is put in place of the description, following the grammatical case of the description that was put in place of the noun, as one of the poets said in a verse saying that if he had arrows and a bow he would not fear the wicked wolf's attacks. As for al-Kisa'i, it is reported of him that whoever used the genitive meant to make it one speech, following a genitive with a genitive, and he read in the genitive. Abu Ja'far says: the correct view in my opinion is that they are two well-known readings, each read by leading readers, with one meaning, so whichever the reader reads he is correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 513}

It is possible that the one who read it in the nominative meant the meaning of the one who used the genitive in the connection of the speech with itself but used the nominative because of its separation from the verse before it, as He, mighty be His praise, said "God has bought from the believers their lives and their wealth" to the end of the verse and then said "the repentant, the worshippers" [[passage/quran-9-111|9:111-112]]. The meaning of "God, to whom belongs what is in the heavens and what is in the earth" is: God, who owns all that is in the heavens and on the earth. He says to His Prophet Muhammad: We sent down this Book to you that you may call My servants to the worship of the One of this description and to leave the worship of those who own neither harm nor benefit for them or for themselves, of gods and idols. Then He, mighty be His praise, threatened whoever disbelieved in Him and did not respond to His Messenger's call to what he called them to, of devoting oneness to Him, saying "and woe to the disbelievers from a severe punishment", he says, the valley that flows with the pus of the people of Hell, for whoever denied His oneness and worshipped another beside Him, from God's severe punishment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 514}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
