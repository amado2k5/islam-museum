**Commentary on verse 49: "Judge between them by what God sent down, do not follow their desires, and beware lest they tempt you away from part of what God sent down to you", pp. 392-394**

Al-Tabari says the sense of "judge between them by what God sent down" is: We sent down to you, Muhammad, the Book confirming what was before it, and that you judge between them, so "that" is in the accusative, joined to "the Book". "What God sent down" means God's ruling given to him in His book. "Do not follow their desires" is God's prohibition to His prophet against following the wishes of the Jews who came to him over their slain man and their two adulterers, and a command to keep to the book. The editor says the "two adulterers" are the Jewish man and woman who committed adultery, whom the Prophet had stoned.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 392}

"Beware lest they tempt you away from part of what God sent down" means beware of these Jews who came for judgment turning you from part of the ruling of the Book, so that you leave it and follow their wishes. "If they turn away, know that God only wishes to afflict them for some of their sins" means that if they turn from your ruling and do not act on it after you ruled with truth, know they refused to accept it only because God wishes to hasten their punishment in this world for some of their past sins; "many people are transgressors" means many of the Jews, who abandon the book of God and leave His obedience for disobedience.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 393}

Report 12150, from Ibn 'Abbas, says Ka'b ibn Asad, Ibn Suriya and Shas ibn Qays said to one another: let us go to Muhammad, perhaps we can tempt him from his religion. They came and said: you know we are the rabbis, nobles and chiefs of the Jews, and if we follow you the Jews will follow and not oppose us; we have a quarrel with our people, and we would bring it to you, you rule for us against them, and we will believe and trust you. The Prophet refused, and God revealed the verse about them. The notes say Ibn Hisham gives the names a little differently and that the story is in his Sira. Report 12151, from Ibn Zayd, says the temptation is that they say "in the Torah it is so", though God has shown him what is in the Torah, and he recited the verse of retaliation, one part with another. Report 12152, from al-Sha'bi, says the Magians were included with the People of the Book in this verse.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 393}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
