## Summary
Verse 279 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 279 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-279|2:279]]), pp. 24 to 26. Al-Tabari explains the condition as if they do not leave what remains of usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 24} He notes the readers' difference over the reading of the warning: most readers of Medina read it with a short alif and fatha on the dhal, meaning be aware and notified; others, the reading of most Kufans, read it with the alif lengthened and a kasra on the dhal, meaning inform others and tell them you are at war with them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 24}

Al-Tabari's choice and argument. Abu Jafar says the better of the two readings is the short alif with fatha, meaning know it, be sure of it, and be on notice from God of that; he chose it because God commanded His Prophet to cast off whoever stays in the polytheism he is not allowed to remain in, and to kill the apostate among them in every case unless he returns to Islam, whether or not the polytheists gave him notice that they are at war with him; the one so addressed is either a polytheist remaining in a polytheism he is not allowed to remain in or a Muslim who apostatised and gave notice of war, and either way war is simply cast at him, not that he is ordered to give notice of it if he intends it; for if the matter were up to him, and he stayed taking usury while holding it lawful and the Muslims were not given notice of war, they would not be bound to fight him, which is not his rule in either case; so it is known that he is the one notified of war, not the one who gives notice of it. This is al-Tabari's choice and argument, attributed to him, not to the museum and not a legal ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 24} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 25} The editor notes that the print mis-spelt a phrase here and that the manuscript has the right spelling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 24} He says the interpreters interpreted it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 25}

6261, Ibn Abbas: whoever keeps on with usury and will not desist, it is the duty of the Muslims' leader to ask him to repent, and if he desists, well, and if not to strike off his neck. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 25} 6262 and 6263, Ibn Abbas: on the day of resurrection it is said to the usurer, take your weapon for war. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 25} 6264 and 6265, Qatada: God threatened them with killing, as you hear, and made them fair game wherever they are found; the editor explains the word for fair game as lawful and the nullifying of blood as making it void. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 25} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 26} 6266, al-Rabi ibn Anas: He threatened the usurer with killing; 6267, Ibn Abbas: be sure of war from God and His Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 26} These reports are transmitted as they came, show what their narrators said, and are not a legal ruling by the museum.

Abu Jafar says all these reports show that what is meant is God's notice to them of war and killing, not an order to them to give notice to others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 26} Al-Tabari then explains the next condition: if they repent, leave taking usury and turn to God, the capital is theirs, of the debts owed you by people, without the addition you made to it as usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 26} We stop before report 6268, which begins at the foot of p. 26. The sayings are transmitted as they came, al-Tabari's choice and argument are his, the notes are the editor's, with no museum verdict and no legal ruling.

More on ([[passage/quran-2-279|2:279]]), pp. 26 to 28. Report 6268 concludes, from Qatada: the money owed them by men was left to them as their capital when this verse came down, while profit and surplus are not theirs and they should take none of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 27} 6269, al-Dahhak: God set aside usury and left them their capital; 6270, Qatada: whatever debt they had, they may take their capital and add nothing to it; 6271, al-Suddi: they have the capital they lent and the usury falls. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 27} 6272, Qatada: they were told the Prophet said in his sermon on the day of the conquest, all usury of the pre-Islamic period is set aside, and the first usury I begin with is that of al-Abbas ibn Abd al-Muttalib; 6273, al-Rabi ibn Anas: he said in his sermon that all usury is set aside and the first usury set aside is al-Abbas's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 27} The editor notes that the sermon is given in the farewell pilgrimage in Muslim in Jabir ibn Abd Allah's report and in al-Bayhaqi's Sunan, and that al-Suyuti documents it in al-Durr al-manthur from Amr ibn al-Ahwas, saying Abu Dawud, al-Tirmidhi who graded it sound, al-Nasa'i, Ibn Majah, Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bayhaqi relate it, and refers to Ibn Kathir; this is the editor's documentation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 27}

Al-Tabari then explains the close of the verse: the denial of wrong means that you are not wronged by taking back your capital, owed you by your debtors before the charging of usury, without the profits you added as usury, so that you take what you had no right to take, and that the debtor who gives it you without the usury you imposed for extending the term does not cheat you of a right you have against him by withholding it, since what exceeded your capital was not a right you had against him, so his withholding it would not wrong you; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 28} 6274, Ibn Abbas: so you would charge usury, and you are not wronged, so you are diminished; 6275, Ibn Zayd: you are not reduced from your wealth and you do not take what is false and unlawful for you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 28}
