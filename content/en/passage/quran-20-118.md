## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 118 to 120 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Indeed, you shall not hunger therein nor be naked": He, the Exalted, says, informing about His saying to Adam when He made him settle in Paradise: "Indeed, you have," O Adam, "that you neither starve therein nor be naked." And "inna" in the phrase "do not hunger therein" is in the accusative case with the letter 'in' in the phrase: "Indeed, you have it." And His saying: "And that you do not thirst therein" differed among readers; some reciters of Medina and Kufa read it with a kasra: 'and innak,' with a conjunction on the phrase: 'Indeed, you have it.' Some reciters of Medina and the general reciters of Kufa and Basra read this: 'And anuk,' with a fatha on its alif, as a conjunction with the 'an' in His saying: 'that you do not starve in it.' In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas and Qatadah.
