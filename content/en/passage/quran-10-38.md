## Summary
Verse 38 of [[passage/quran-10|Surat Yunus]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 38 of 109 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 38 (pp. 91-92)**

On [[passage/quran-10-38|10:38]] he says: do these polytheists say that Muhammad invented this Qur'an from himself and fabricated it? Say to them, if it is as you say, you are Arabs like me, my tongue is like yours and my speech like yours, so bring a surah like this Qur'an. The editor notes that the manuscript drops words that he has restored by inference and that the printed text spoiled them. The pronoun in "like it" refers to the Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 91}

Some Basran grammarians said the sense is "a surah like its surah", the word for surah being dropped and "like" attached to what the surah was attached to, as in [[passage/quran-12-82|12:82]], where the sense is the people of the town. Others rejected this and said it means: bring a Qur'an like this Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 91}

Al-Tabari says the correct view is that a surah is a surah of the Qur'an and is Qur'an even though it is not all of it; so they were told "a surah like it" with the masculine pronoun and not the feminine, because the pronoun follows the sense of "surah" and not its wording. "And call on whom you can besides God": call, polytheists, those of your allies and partners you can call to produce a surah like it, from anyone other than God; exert yourselves, for you will never be able to bring a surah like it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 92}

"If you are truthful": if you are truthful that Muhammad invented it, bring a surah like it with the help of all who will assist you; if you do not, there is no doubt you are lying in your claim, since Muhammad can be no more than a human being like you, and if all of creation cannot bring a surah like it, one person is the more unable to bring the whole of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 92}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
