## Summary
Verse 68 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 68 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 68: "Say, People of the Book, you stand on nothing", p. 473**

Al-Tabari says: this is God's command to His prophet to tell the Jews and Christians who lived around Medina that they stand on nothing of what they claim from the religion of Moses and Jesus until they uphold the Torah, the Gospel and what was sent down to them from their Lord, namely what Muhammad brought of the Furqan. They must act on all of it, believe what it holds about believing in Muhammad and accepting him, and not separate between God's messengers, believing some and rejecting others, for rejecting one of them is rejecting all, since God's books confirm one another.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 473}

Report 12284, from Ibn Abbas, begins with a group of Medinan Jews, among them Rafi ibn Haritha, Sallam ibn Mishkam, Malik ibn al-Sayf and Rafi ibn Hurayma, who came to the Prophet and asked whether he did not claim to follow the creed and religion of Abraham.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 473}

**Commentary on verse 68, continued: "It will increase many of them in rebellion and unbelief", pp. 473-476**

Report 12284 continues: the Prophet answered that he was indeed on Abraham's way and believed in the Torah, but that they had introduced changes, rejected the covenant taken from them and hidden what they were told to make plain, and that he was free of their innovations. They replied that they would keep to what they had, that they were on the truth, and that they would neither believe in him nor follow him; and God sent down this verse and what follows. Report 12285, from Ibn Zayd, says the Torah belongs to the Jews, the Gospel to the Christians, and what was sent down from their Lord is what was sent down to us; the sense is that they stand on nothing until they act on what is in it.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 474}

Al-Tabari says God swore that the book He sent down to His prophet would increase many of these Jews and Christians in transgression and excess in denying him, beyond what they had shown before the Furqan came down, and in rejection of his prophethood. "Do not grieve" means do not be sad; he cites a verse of rajaz for the usage. God tells His prophet not to be sad at their denial, for this was their habit with their own prophets, so how much more with him.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 475}

Report 12286, from Ibn Abbas, says that what was sent down means the Furqan, and report 12287, from al-Suddi, that "do not grieve" means do not be sad.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 476}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
