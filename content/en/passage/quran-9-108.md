## Summary
Verse 108 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/demolition-of-masjid-al-dirar|Demolition of Masjid al Dirar]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:ibn-hisham-saqqa}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 108 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 108 (pp. 475-490)**

Al-Tabari says to His Prophet Muhammad: do not stand, Muhammad, in the mosque these hypocrites built for harm and division among the believers and as an ambush for whoever fought God and His Messenger. Then the Almighty swore, saying "a mosque founded on piety from the first day is worthier of your standing in it", you. By "founded on piety" He means its foundation and root begun on fear of God and obedience to Him; "from the first day" means from when its building was begun; "worthier of your standing in it" means more fitting for you to stand in it praying. It was also said that "from the first day" means from the beginning of the first day, as the Arabs say "I have not seen him since such a day", meaning its beginning, and that "from the first day" is meant for "from the first of the days", as someone says "I met every man" meaning all men. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 475}

The interpreters differed over the mosque He meant by "a mosque founded on piety from the first day". Some said it is the mosque of the Messenger of God, in which are his pulpit and his grave today. Report 17201, from Uthman ibn Ubaydallah ibn Abi Rafi', through Ibn Waki', Abu Mu'awiya and Ibrahim ibn Tahman: Muhammad ibn Abi Hurayra sent me to Ibn Umar to ask him about the mosque founded on piety, which mosque it is, the mosque of Medina or the mosque of Quba, and he said "no, the mosque of Medina". The editor says he doubts the name of the sender and thinks it more likely al-Muharrar ibn Abi Hurayra. Report 17202, from Ibn Umar, Zayd ibn Thabit and Abu Sa'id, through al-Qasim ibn Amr al-Anqazi, al-Darawardi and Uthman ibn Ubaydallah: the mosque founded on piety is the mosque of the Messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 476}

Report 17203, from Uthman ibn Ubaydallah ibn Abi Rafi', through Rabi'a ibn Uthman: I asked Ibn Umar about the mosque founded on piety, and he said it is the mosque of the Messenger; report 17204, from Zayd, through Ibn Uyayna, Abu al-Zinad and Kharija ibn Zayd: it is the mosque of the Prophet; report 17205, from Zayd, through Abd al-Rahman ibn Abdallah ibn Dhakwan, his father and Kharija: it is the mosque of the Messenger. Report 17206, from Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Rahman, through Ibn Bashshar, Yahya ibn Sa'id and Humayd al-Kharrat al-Madani: Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Sa'id passed by me and I said "how did you hear your father speak of the mosque founded on piety?", and he said, my father said, "I came to the Messenger of God and went in to him in the house of one of his wives and said 'Messenger of God, which mosque is the one founded on piety?', and he took a handful of pebbles and struck the ground with it, and said 'it is this mosque of yours'"; I said "this is how I heard your father tell it". The editor notes that the report is related by Muslim and by Ahmad by this same route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 477}

Report 17207, from Abu Sa'id, through Ibn Waki', his father, Usama ibn Zayd and Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Sa'id: the mosque founded on piety is the greatest mosque of the Prophet. Report 17208, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, through Humayd ibn Mas'ada, Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal and Dawud: the mosque founded on piety from the first day is the great mosque of Medina; report 17209, from him, the same except that he said "the greatest"; report 17210, from him, through Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Qattan and Ibn Harmala: it is the mosque of the Prophet; report 17211, from Kharija ibn Zayd, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ibn Uyayna and Abu al-Zinad: the mosque of the Prophet that was founded on piety. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 478}

Others said rather that what is meant is the mosque of Quba. Report 17212, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abu Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "a mosque founded on piety from the first day", meaning the mosque of Quba; report 17213, from him, is similar, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father; report 17214, from Atiyya, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Fudayl ibn Marzuq: it is the mosque of Quba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 478}

Report 17215, from Ibn Burayda, through Ibn Waki', Abu Usama and Salih ibn Hayyan: the mosque of Quba, which was founded on piety, was built by the Prophet of God; the editor notes that Salih ibn Hayyan al-Qurashi is weak in hadith. Report 17216, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the mosque founded on piety is the mosque of Quba. Report 17217, from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Ma'mar and al-Zuhri: those among whom the mosque founded on piety was built were Banu Amr ibn Awf. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 479}

Al-Tabari holds the better of the two views to be that of those who said it is the mosque of the Messenger, because of the soundness of the report to that effect from the Messenger of God. Report 17218, from Sahl ibn Sa'd, through Abu Kurayb, Ibn Waki', Waki', Rabi'a ibn Uthman al-Taymi and Imran ibn Abi Anas: two men disagreed in the time of the Messenger of God over the mosque founded on piety, one of them saying "it is the mosque of the Prophet" and the other "it is the mosque of Quba", and they came to the Messenger of God and asked him, and he said "it is this mosque of mine". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 479}

Report 17219, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b, through Ibn Waki', Abu Nu'aym, Abdallah ibn Amir al-Aslami, Imran ibn Abi Anas and Sahl ibn Sa'd: the Prophet was asked about the mosque founded on piety and said "this mosque of mine"; the editor notes that Abdallah ibn Amir al-Aslami is weak. Report 17220, from Abu Sa'id, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb, al-Layth and Imran ibn Abi Anas: two men argued over the mosque founded on piety from the first day, one saying "it is the mosque of Quba" and the other "it is the mosque of the Messenger of God", and the Messenger of God said "it is this mosque of mine". The editor notes the hadith is sound, related by al-Tirmidhi and Ahmad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 480}

Report 17221, from Sa'id ibn Abi Sa'id al-Khudri, through Bahr ibn Nasr, Shu'ayb ibn al-Layth, his father and Imran ibn Abi Anas: two men argued, and he gave the like. Report 17222, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb, Suhayl ibn Muhammad, Unays ibn Abi Yahya and his father: the Messenger of God said "the mosque founded on piety is this mosque of mine, and in each is good"; report 17223 is similar through al-Muthanna, al-Himmani, Abd al-Aziz and Unays. Report 17224, from Abu Sa'id, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Safwan ibn Isa and Unays: a man of Banu Khudra and a man of Banu Amr ibn Awf disputed over the mosque founded on piety, the Khudri saying "it is the mosque of the Messenger of God" and the Awfi saying "it is the mosque of Quba", and they came to the Prophet and asked him, and he said "it is this mosque of mine, and in each is good". The editor says the chain is sound and Ahmad relates it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 481}

As for His words "in it are men who love to purify themselves, and God loves those who purify themselves", al-Tabari says: in those present at the mosque founded on piety from the first day are men who love to clean their seats with water when they have come from the privy, and God loves those who purify themselves with water. What the interpreters said agrees with what we said. Report 17225, from Shahr ibn Hawshab, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abu Dawud, Hammam ibn Yahya and Qatada: when "in it are men who love to purify themselves" came down, the Messenger of God said "what is this purification for which God has praised you?", and they said "Messenger of God, we wash away the trace of excrement". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 482}

Report 17226, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God said to the people of Quba "God has praised you well in purification, so what do you do?", and they said "we wash off ourselves the trace of excrement and urine". Report 17227, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: when "in it are men who love to purify themselves" came down, the Prophet said "company of the Helpers, what is this purification for which God has praised you?", and they said "we clean ourselves with water when we come from the privy". Report 17228, from Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Salam, through Jabir ibn al-Kurdi, Muhammad ibn Sabiq, Malik ibn Mighwal, Sayyar Abu al-Hakam and Shahr ibn Hawshab: the Messenger of God stood before us and said "will you not tell me, for God has praised you well for purity?", and they said "Messenger of God, we find it written with us in the Torah, cleaning oneself with water". The editor notes that there is disagreement on Shahr ibn Hawshab in the chain of this report and that Ahmad and al-Bukhari in his larger History relate it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 483}

Report 17229, from Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Salam, through Sufyan ibn Waki', Malik ibn Mighwal, Sayyar and Shahr ibn Hawshab: when the Prophet came to the people of Quba he said "God has praised you well for purity", meaning His words "in it are men who love to purify themselves", and they said "we find it written with us in the Torah, cleaning oneself with water". The editor notes that the name Yahya ibn Rafi' in the chain is most likely Yahya ibn Adam. Report 17230, from Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Salam, through Abu Hisham al-Rifa'i, Yahya, Malik ibn Mighwal, Sayyar and Shahr, Yahya saying "I know it only from his father": the Prophet said to the people of Quba "God has praised you well in purity", and they said "we find it written for us in the Torah, cleaning oneself with water", and about it came down "in it are men who love to purify themselves". The editor goes into the disagreement over the chain of this report, holding that the name Yahya ibn Rafi' in it should be Yahya ibn Adam, quoting Ibn Hajar, Abu Zur'a and al-Haythami on whether the father is named, and observing that those God praised for purifying themselves were of Banu Amr ibn Awf ibn Malik ibn al-Aws at Quba, Arabs who are not recorded as having been Jews, whereas the plain sense of this report suggests their religion was Judaism, and that Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Salam and his father were of the Jewish Banu Qaynuqa', allies of the Qawaqil of the Khazraj and not of these; he judges the wording of the report to be badly confused and the following report of Uwaym ibn Sa'ida clearer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 485}

Report 17231, from Uwaym ibn Sa'ida, who was one of the men of Badr, through Abd al-A'la ibn Wasil, Isma'il ibn Subayh al-Yashkuri, Abu Uways al-Madani and Sharahbil ibn Sa'd: the Messenger of God said to the people of Quba "I hear that God has praised you in purification, so what is this purification?", and they said "Messenger of God, we know nothing, except that we saw some Jewish neighbours of ours wash their rears after excrement, so we washed as they washed". The editor notes that there is doubt about Sharahbil's hearing from Uwaym and that the report is related by Ahmad and Ibn Khuzayma and cited by Ibn Kathir and al-Haythami. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 486}

Report 17232, from Khuzayma ibn Thabit, through Muhammad ibn Umara, Muhammad ibn Sa'id, Ibrahim ibn Muhammad and Sharahbil ibn Sa'd: this verse came down, "in it are men who love to purify themselves, and God loves those who purify themselves", he said, they used to wash their rears after excrement. Report 17233, from Amir al-Sha'bi, through Ibn Waki', his father and Ibn Abi Layla: some people of Quba used to clean themselves with water, and the verse came down. Report 17234, from Muslim al-Qurri, through al-Hasan ibn Arafa, Shababa ibn Sawwar and Shu'ba: I said to Ibn Abbas, "may I pour water over my head?", he being in the state of pilgrimage, and he said "have you not heard God say 'God loves those who turn in repentance and loves those who purify themselves'?", which is [[passage/quran-2-222|2:222]]. Report 17235, from al-Sha'bi, through Ibn Waki', Hafs, Dawud and Ibn Abi Layla: when "in it are men who love to purify themselves" came down, the Messenger of God said to the people of Quba "what is this for which God has praised you?", and they said "there is none of us who does not clean himself after the privy". Report 17236, from Ibrahim ibn Isma'il al-Ansari, through al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn, Hushaym and Abd al-Hamid al-Madani: the Messenger of God said to Uwaym ibn Sa'ida "what is this for which God has praised you, 'in it are men who love to purify themselves, and God loves those who purify themselves'?", and he said "we are nearly always washing the rear with water". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 487}

Report 17237, from Musa ibn Abi Kathir, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd al-Rahman ibn Sa'd, Abu Ja'far and Husayn: the beginning of the story of this verse concerns men of the Helpers of Quba, "in it are men who love to purify themselves", and the Prophet asked them and they said "we clean ourselves with water". Report 17238, from Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, through al-Muthanna, Asbagh ibn al-Faraj, Ibn Wahb, Yunus and Abu al-Zinad: Uwaym ibn Sa'ida of Banu Amr ibn Awf, Ma'n ibn Adi of Banu al-Ajlan and Abu al-Dahdah; as for Uwaym ibn Sa'ida, it has reached us that the Messenger of God was asked who are those about whom God said "in it are men who love to purify themselves", and he said "good men, among them Uwaym ibn Sa'ida", and it has not reached us that any man of them was named but Uwaym. The editor notes that this is part of the hadith of the Saqifa related by al-Bukhari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 488}

Report 17239, from al-Hasan, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd ibn Nasr, Ibn al-Mubarak and Hisham ibn Hassan: when this verse came down the Messenger of God said "what is this by which God has mentioned you in the matter of purity and praised you?", and they said "we wash off the trace of excrement and urine". Report 17240, from Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn Salam, repeats what has gone before, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd, Ibn al-Mubarak, Malik ibn Mighwal, Sayyar and Shahr, Malik saying he meant His words "in it are men who love to purify themselves". Report 17241, from Atiyya, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Fudayl ibn Marzuq: when this verse came down, the Messenger of God asked them "what is this purification of yours that God mentioned?", and they said "Messenger of God, we used to clean ourselves with water in the Jahiliyya, and when Islam came we did not give it up", and he said "then do not give it up". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 489}

Report 17242, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: in the mosque of Quba there were men of the Helpers who washed their rears with water, entering the palm groves where water flowed and washing, and God praised them for this, saying "in it are men who love to purify themselves", the verse. Report 17243, from Ata, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad and Talha ibn Amr: a group of the people of Quba introduced washing with water, and about them came down "in it are men who love to purify themselves, and God loves those who purify themselves". It was said "those who purify themselves" when it is really the other form, but the "t" was assimilated into the "t" and became a doubled letter because their points of articulation are close. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the purifier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 490}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
