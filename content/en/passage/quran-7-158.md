**Commentary on verse 158 (pp. 170-172)**

**On "Say: O people, I am the Messenger of God to you all"**

Al-Tabari says, addressing His Prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to all the people: I am God's Messenger to you all, not to some of you and not others, as those messengers before me were sent to some people and not others; my message is not to some of you but to all of you. The word "who" is an epithet of the name of God, and the sense is: say, O people, I am the Messenger to you of God, to whom belongs dominion of the heavens and the earth. By "to whom belongs dominion of the heavens and the earth" he means the one who holds the authority of the heavens and the earth and what is in them and their governance and direction; "there is no god but He" means divinity and worship are fitting only for Him, apart from anything else, rivals and idols, as He is the one with authority over everything, able to bring into being the creation of whatever He wills, give it life and bring it to an end when He wills its death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 170}

"So believe in God and His Messenger" means: say to them, affirm the signs of God who has this description, acknowledge His oneness and that divinity and worship belong to Him, and affirm His Messenger Muhammad, that he is sent to His creatures, calling to His oneness and obedience. The editor refers to the explanation of "dominion". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 170}

**On "the unlettered Prophet who believes in God and His words"**

Al-Tabari says: "the unlettered Prophet" is an epithet of the Messenger of God, and he has explained the meaning of "prophet" and "unlettered" earlier. "Who believes in God and His words" means who affirms God and His words. The interpreters then differed on "His words". Some said it means His signs; report 15247, from Qatada, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh, Yazid and Sa'id: His signs. Others said Jesus son of Mary is meant; reports 15248 and 15249, from Mujahid through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj, and from al-Suddi through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad and Asbat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 171}

Al-Tabari says: the right view in our opinion is that God commanded His servants to affirm the prophethood of the unlettered Prophet who believes in God and His words, and the report did not specify some of God's words for his belief and not others but told them of all the words, so the truth is that the saying is general, for the Messenger of God believed in all of God's words as the plain sense of God's Book has it. As for "and follow him that you may be guided", be guided by him, people, and act on what He commanded you to do of obedience to God, so that you may be rightly guided and reach the truth by following him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 172}
