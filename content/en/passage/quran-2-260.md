## Summary
Verse 260 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 260 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari's commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-260|2:260]]), whose first page in volume 5 is p. 485. He says the connection to the earlier passages ([[passage/quran-2-258|2:258]]) and ([[passage/quran-2-259|2:259]]) is sound because seeing there is with the heart, not the eye, meaning have you not known and so recalled, and so it is joined sometimes by what matches its wording and sometimes by what matches its sense; this is al-Tabari's explanation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 485}

He then gives the interpreters' differing accounts of why Abraham asked his Lord to show him how He revives the dead. Some said he saw an animal carcass divided among beasts of prey and birds and asked to see how God would revive it despite its flesh being scattered in the bellies of birds and beasts, to see it plainly and add certainty by sight to what he knew by report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 485} Qatada (5963): we were told that Abraham, the friend of God, came upon a carcass divided among animals and beasts, made his request, and his Lord asked whether he did not believe, and he answered yes but sought reassurance. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 485} Al-Dahhak (5964): Abraham passed a dead animal, decayed and divided among the winds and beasts, stood looking, though he already knew God is able to do it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 486} Ibn Jurayj (5965): it reached me that Abraham, walking on the road, came upon the carcass of a donkey with beasts and birds over it, its flesh torn apart and its bones left; when the beasts went and the birds flew to hills and mounds he stood amazed and said he knew God would gather it from the bellies of those beasts and birds, and asked to be shown; his Lord asked whether he did not believe and he said yes, but report is not like seeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 486} Ibn Zayd (5966): Abraham passed a fish half on land and half in the sea, the sea creatures eating the part in the sea and the beasts the part on land, and the wicked one, whom the editor's note says is Iblis, asked when God would gather this from the bellies of these; and he made his request. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 486} The editor's notes correct readings of the manuscript and explain a verb meaning they divided it up among themselves. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 486}

Others said the cause was the debate and dispute between him and Nimrod. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 486} Ibn Ishaq (5967): after what passed between Abraham and his people, which God relates in ([[passage/quran-21-51|21:51]]), Nimrod, as they report, asked Abraham about the God he worshipped and described the power of; Abraham answered that He gives life and death; Nimrod said he too gives life and death, and the dispute went as God relates; and then Abraham asked his Lord to show him the reviving of the dead, and his Lord asked whether he did not believe, and he said yes, with no doubt in God or in His power, but he wished to know it and his heart longed for it, so that it might be at rest in what it longed for once known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 487} Al-Tabari says the two accounts are close in sense in that the request was to see plainly what he already knew by report; this is his assessment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 487}

Others said the request came at the glad tidings that God had taken him as a friend, so he asked to be shown a sign at once so that his heart would be at rest that God had chosen him as friend; report 5968 from al-Suddi begins at the foot of p. 487 and we stop before it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 487} These reports are given as transmitted, without a verdict from the museum.

More on why Abraham asked in ([[passage/quran-2-260|2:260]]). Report 5968 from al-Suddi, for the view that the request came at the tidings of friendship: when God took Abraham as a friend the angel of death asked his Lord for leave to bring him the news, and was allowed; he came to Abraham, who was out, and entered his house; Abraham was the most jealous of men and locked the door when he went out; when he came and found a man in his house he rose to seize him and asked who permitted him to enter his house, and the angel said the Lord of this house; Abraham said you have spoken truly and knew it was the angel of death, who told him God had taken him as a friend, and he praised God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 487} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 488} Abraham then asked to be shown the form in which he takes the souls of unbelievers; the angel said he could not bear it, but Abraham insisted, turned his face away and looked, and saw a black man whose head reached the sky, flame coming from his mouth and ears, and Abraham fainted, then recovered with the angel back in his first form. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 488} He said that if the unbeliever met at death only your form in affliction and grief it would suffice, and asked to see how he takes the souls of believers; he turned away, then looked and saw a young man of the fairest face and sweetest scent in white clothes, and said that if the believer had with his Lord only this form of yours as comfort and honour it would suffice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 488} The angel left and Abraham stood praying that his Lord show him how He revives the dead so that he would know he was His friend; his Lord asked whether he believed in His friendship, and he said yes, but so that he be at rest in that friendship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 488} This report is given as transmitted from al-Suddi, without a verdict from the museum. In notes the editor gives a reading of a phrase, explains the Arabic singular masculine pronoun for a collective, and explains the word for friendship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 488}

Said ibn Jubayr (5969): the reassurance sought was reassurance in the friendship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 489} In a note the editor gives an account of Amr ibn Thabit, a transmitter in this chain, and of his father, quoting what Ibn al-Mubarak, Abu Zur'a, Ibn Ma'in, al-Bukhari and Abu Dawud said of him and his death in 172; this assessment is the editor's, not al-Tabari's or the museum's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 489}

Others said Abraham said this to his Lord because he doubted God's power to revive the dead; this is given as attributed to its holders. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 489} From Ayyub (5970), Ibn Abbas said there is no verse in the Qur'an more hopeful to me than this; the editor notes that al-Suyuti has it in al-Durr al-Manthur, ascribed to Abd al-Razzaq and Ibn Jarir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 489} From Said ibn al-Musayyib (5971): Abd Allah ibn Abbas and Abd Allah ibn Amr arranged to meet, and one asked the other which verse in God's book is most hopeful for this community; Ibn Amr mentioned ([[passage/quran-39-53|39:53]]) to its end, and Ibn Abbas said that if you say so, more hopeful for this community still is Abraham's request in ([[passage/quran-2-260|2:260]]). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 490} In a note the editor lists who relates the report in al-Suyuti, the al-Hakim version in al-Mustadrak with different wording and his statement that it meets the condition of the two Shaykhs, al-Dhahabi's reply that it has a break, and the reason he gives for that break, namely that Abd al-Aziz ibn Abi Salama did not meet Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir; he also explains an omitted predicate in Ibn Abbas's words; these references and judgements are the editor's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 490} From Ibn Jurayj (5972): I asked Ata ibn Abi Rabah about the verse, and he said some of what enters people's hearts entered Abraham's heart, so he asked, and his Lord told him to take four birds to show him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 490} Report 5973 from Abu Hurayra, a saying of the Prophet, begins at the foot of p. 490 and we stop before it. These reports are given as transmitted, without a verdict from the museum.
