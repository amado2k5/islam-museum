## Summary
Verse 159 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 159 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Then, on "those who split up their religion and became sects, you have nothing to do with them; their affair is with God, then He will tell them what they used to do", al-Tabari says the readers differ on how to read "split". It is reported that Ali ibn Abi Talib read "left their religion": he gives (14252) from Amr ibn Dinar, (14253) from Hamza al-Zayyat and (14254) from Qatada; as if Ali meant that they went out of it and apostatised, from "leaving". Abdullah ibn Mas'ud read it with a doubled ra, meaning "split up" (14255, from Abu Ishaq), and the readers of Medina and Basra and most Kufans follow this reading; as if Abdullah took it that God's religion is one, the religion of Abraham, the upright submitter, and the Jews and Christians divided it, some becoming Jews and others Christians, making it scattered sects. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 268}

Al-Tabari says the sound view is that these are two well-known readings recited by imams of the readers, agreeing in sense and not differing, since every one who strays leaves his religion; and the factions did divide the religion God approved for His servants, some turning Jew, others Christian and others Magian, which is division itself and becoming scattered sects who are not united, so they leave God's true religion and divide it; whichever the reader reads, he is right, though he prefers the reading most readers hold, the doubled ra in "farraqu". The commentators then differ over who is meant. Some said the Jews and Christians: (14256) and (14257) from Mujahid that "became sects" refers to the Jews; (14258) and (14259) from Qatada that they are the Jews and Christians; and (14260) from al-Suddi that they are the Jews and Christians and "left their religion" means abandoned their religion. The note says the printed text and the manuscript have "split" in both places, while the explanation requires "left". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 269}

He gives (14261) from Ibn Abbas that the Jews and Christians differed before Muhammad was sent and split up, and when Muhammad was sent God revealed this verse; and (14262) from al-Dahhak and (14263) from Qatada that they are the Jews and Christians. Others said those meant are the people of innovation in this community, who followed the ambiguous parts of the Qur'an rather than the precise: (14264) from Abu Hurayra that the verse was revealed about this community; (14265) from him that they are the people of prayer (the note says the printed text has "the people of misguidance", the manuscript clearly "the people of prayer", meaning the believers among the qibla); and (14266) from him that the Prophet said of this verse {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 270}

that they are not of you, they are the people of innovation, doubts and misguidance in this community. The note says this report, raised to the Prophet, is not sound because of Abbad ibn Kathir's weakness, that Ibn Kathir says he did not fabricate it but erred in raising it, since al-Thawri narrated it from Layth from Tawus from Abu Hurayra as his own statement that it was revealed about this community, and that al-Haythami ascribes it to al-Tabarani in al-Awsat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 271}

Al-Tabari chooses the view that God told His Prophet that he is free of those who left his true religion and divided it, becoming sects and parties in it, and that he is not of them nor they of him, because the religion God sent him with is Islam, the religion of Abraham the upright, as his Lord told him to say in [[passage/quran-6-161|6:161]]. So whoever leaves the religion he was sent with, whether idolater, pagan, Jew, Christian or pretended hanif, an innovator who invented in the religion what led him astray from the straight path and the upright religion, the creed of Abraham the submitter, is free of Muhammad and Muhammad of him, and falls under the generality of the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 271}

Then, on "you have nothing to do with them; their affair is with God", the commentators differ. Some said it was revealed with the command to leave off fighting the idolaters before fighting them became an obligation, and then was abrogated by the command to fight them in Surat Bara'a, the words of [[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]]; he gives (14267) from al-Suddi that he was not told to fight them, then it was abrogated and he was commanded to fight them in Surat Bara'a. Others said it was rather revealed as God's notice to him that some of his community would innovate in his religion after him, and it is not abrogated because it is a report, not a command, and abrogation applies only to command and prohibition; he gives (14268) from Abu al-Ahwas that he recited the verse and said your Prophet is free of them, and (14269) the like by other routes; the note says the chain of 14268 is sound. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 272}

We stop here at the foot of p. 272, in verse 159 after report 14269; the next batch continues from report 14270, which begins on p. 272.
