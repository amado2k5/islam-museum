## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains 'And We sent the winds to the winds' in the plural: it presents the general reading of the Quran as plural, while some Kufans read it as monotheistic, 'the wind,' which is described in the plural, since the wind is one word and means plural because it blows from every direction, as in their saying 'the land of Sabasib' and 'garment of morals.' He presents the disagreement among the Arabs regarding its description as 'pollinated': The winds are pollinated, not 'fertile' because they pollinate clouds and trees — some grammarians of Basra say 'pollinated' is fertile, and some from Kufa say pollinated because it passes over soil and water, and they cite the description of 'wind of punishment' as baraneous because it does not pollinate — and some of them use the passive participle meaning the subject like flowing water. Al-Tabari suggests that it is fertile as God described it: fertile and pollinated, fertilized by water and clouds pollinated by its effect. He narrates from Ibn Mas'ud: He sends winds and carries water, causing clouds to flow and circulate as fertilization does, then rain. And "and you are not in store for him": you cannot store or store him.
