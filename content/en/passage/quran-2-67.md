## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads this verse as one of those by which God rebuked the Israelites addressed, for their forebears' breach of the covenant, when they disputed over the slain man. "Huzu" is play and mockery; he says they thought Moses was mocking even though he told them God had commanded it, and that Moses cleared himself by saying he seeks refuge in God from being among the ignorant, those who ascribe falsehood to God. He explains the omitted particle in "Do you take us in mockery?" as unnecessary here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 182} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 183}
Three accounts of the cause are given. Through Ibn Sirin, 'Ubayda al-Salmani says a childless man was killed by his kinsman, who left him among another tribe; they nearly fought, and the prophet told them to slaughter a cow, which was taken only for its weight in gold, though any cow would have served. Abu al-'Aliya says a rich childless man was killed by a relative-heir who left the body at a crossroads and complained to Moses; had they taken any cow it would have sufficed, but they pressed hard and God pressed them, and without their "if God wills" they would never have been guided; the cow was found with an old woman who kept orphans and who raised the price, and a bone from it was struck on the dead man, who named the killer. Al-Suddi says a poor nephew, refused his rich uncle's daughter, killed the uncle at night and claimed his wealth. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 183} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 184} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 185}

To al-Suddi's account: the youth killed his uncle, then came looking for him and seized the tribe gathered over the body, demanding the blood-money; they took the matter to Moses, who ruled the payment, and they asked him to pray that God show who was the killer, as they were ashamed to be reproached with it. In a report through Ibn Abbas by way of 'Ubayda, the cow's description is spelled out and they could not find it; a man most dutiful to his father refused to wake him to sell pearls at a large price, and God compensated him with that cow, which he sold to them for ten times its weight in gold. The dead man was struck with the piece between the shoulders and lived, named his killer, and the youth was put to death. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 185} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 186} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 187}
Al-Tabari notes that the narrators from Qatada, Mujahid, Wahb, Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, Muhammad ibn Qays and Ibn Abbas agree on the cause of Moses' command, though they differ whether the killer was the victim's brother, nephew, or a group of heirs impatient for his death, and all agree the command arose from the slain man. Ibn Zayd adds that a body was thrown into one tribe, who were accused; when Moses spoke they asked whether he mocked them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 187} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 188} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 189}
