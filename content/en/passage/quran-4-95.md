## Summary
Verse 95 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 95 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The opening of the verse and the reading of the exception word (pp. 85-86, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari begins [[passage/quran-4-95|4:95]]: those believers who stay behind from the fight are not on a level with the strivers, they who prefer ease and staying in their homes to the hardship of journeys and to meeting God's enemies in obedience to Him, except those with an excuse such as loss of sight or other conditions that leave them no way to fight; and the strivers, so that God's word may be highest, who spend their strength against God's enemies, by spending their property in what weakens the plotting of the enemies of the believers, and with themselves in joining the fight. Then he says the reciters differed on the word of exception: most reciters of Medina, Mecca and Syria read it in the accusative, meaning "except", and most reciters of Kufa and Basra in the nominative, as a qualifier of the stay-behinds. Al-Tabari chooses the accusative because the reports agree that the exception came down after the first part of the verse, as an exception to it (his choice). The editor says the printed text has "the people of Iraq, Kufa and Basra" and he adopted the manuscript wording, notes a variant of "fearing" of equal sense, and refers to earlier volumes for the phrase on striving. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 86}

**Report 10233 from al-Bara and its note (p. 86, note 2).** Report 10233 (Nasr ibn Ali al-Jahdami, from al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, from his father, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara): the Prophet said: bring me the shoulder-blade and the tablet, and he had the first part of the verse written, with Amr ibn Umm Maktum behind him, who said: is there an allowance for me, Messenger of God? Then the exception came down. The editor says this is the hadith of al-Bara ibn Azib on the occasion of the exception; that al-Tabari gives it with seven chains, five in sequence (10233-10237) and two after; that Abu Ishaq in all of them is al-Sabi'i; that al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i narrated it from Nasr ibn Ali by this chain, as did Ibn Hibban in his Sahih from Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Yusuf from Nasr; and that the verb "wrote" means he ordered it written, which agrees with the printed text and with those books, whereas the manuscript has the other conjunction, so he adopted the agreeing one. Report 10234 (Ibn Waki', from Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash) begins at the foot of the page, and its chain continues on the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 86}


These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
