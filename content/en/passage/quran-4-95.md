## Summary
Verse 95 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 95 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The opening of the verse and the reading of the exception word (pp. 85-86, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari begins [[passage/quran-4-95|4:95]]: those believers who stay behind from the fight are not on a level with the strivers, they who prefer ease and staying in their homes to the hardship of journeys and to meeting God's enemies in obedience to Him, except those with an excuse such as loss of sight or other conditions that leave them no way to fight; and the strivers, so that God's word may be highest, who spend their strength against God's enemies, by spending their property in what weakens the plotting of the enemies of the believers, and with themselves in joining the fight. Then he says the reciters differed on the word of exception: most reciters of Medina, Mecca and Syria read it in the accusative, meaning "except", and most reciters of Kufa and Basra in the nominative, as a qualifier of the stay-behinds. Al-Tabari chooses the accusative because the reports agree that the exception came down after the first part of the verse, as an exception to it (his choice). The editor says the printed text has "the people of Iraq, Kufa and Basra" and he adopted the manuscript wording, notes a variant of "fearing" of equal sense, and refers to earlier volumes for the phrase on striving. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 86}

**Report 10233 from al-Bara and its note (p. 86, note 2).** Report 10233 (Nasr ibn Ali al-Jahdami, from al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman, from his father, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara): the Prophet said: bring me the shoulder-blade and the tablet, and he had the first part of the verse written, with Amr ibn Umm Maktum behind him, who said: is there an allowance for me, Messenger of God? Then the exception came down. The editor says this is the hadith of al-Bara ibn Azib on the occasion of the exception; that al-Tabari gives it with seven chains, five in sequence (10233-10237) and two after; that Abu Ishaq in all of them is al-Sabi'i; that al-Tirmidhi and al-Nasa'i narrated it from Nasr ibn Ali by this chain, as did Ibn Hibban in his Sahih from Muhammad ibn Umar ibn Yusuf from Nasr; and that the verb "wrote" means he ordered it written, which agrees with the printed text and with those books, whereas the manuscript has the other conjunction, so he adopted the agreeing one. Report 10234 (Ibn Waki', from Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash) begins at the foot of the page, and its chain continues on the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 86}

**Reports 10234-10236 (pp. 87-88, notes 1-3).** Report 10234 (Ibn Waki', from Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara): when the first sentence of the verse was sent down, Ibn Umm Maktum, who was blind, came and said: how, Messenger of God, when I am blind? He had not left before the exception was sent down. The editor says this repeats the report before it; one remark about Sufyan ibn Waki' is garbled in the OCR and is not asserted. He calls the report sound in itself by this route and traces it to al-Nasa'i through Muhammad ibn Ubayd from Abu Bakr ibn Ayyash. (The number is printed damaged in the note and is read as 10234 from the sequence.) Report 10235 (Ibn Waki', from his father, from Sufyan, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara): Amr ibn Umm Maktum came, blind, and said: what do you command me, for I am blind? God sent down the exception, and the Prophet then said: bring me the shoulder blade and the inkwell, or the tablet and the inkwell. The editor notes that Sufyan ibn Waki' was not alone in giving it from his father from Sufyan al-Thawri: Ahmad has it in the Musnad from Waki' from al-Thawri, and al-Tirmidhi from Mahmud ibn Ghaylan from Waki', saying, as the editor quotes, that it is a good and sound report, that the man is called Amr or Abd Allah ibn Umm Maktum, that he is Abd Allah ibn Za'ida, and that Umm Maktum was his mother. Report 10236 (Isma'il ibn Isra'il al-Dallal al-Ramli, from Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn al-Mughira, from Mis'ar, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara): Ibn Umm Maktum spoke to him when the first sentence was sent down, and the exception followed. In note 3 (ending on p. 88) the editor writes that Isma'il ibn Isra'il is trustworthy, one of the teachers of Ibn Abi Hatim, who quotes him as truthful; but Ibn Abi Hatim has al-Sallal for al-Dallal, and finding nothing to prefer, the editor keeps what the manuscript and printed text give, after removing from the printed text an extra word in the name that the manuscript lacks, supported by al-Tabari's own citation of him in his History. Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn al-Mughira al-Kufi, who settled in Egypt, was called not strong by his own father, and the editor reports from his biography criticism so severe that it voids his transmission; yet the report is sound through Mis'ar without him, since Muslim gave it from Abu Kurayb from Muhammad ibn Bishr al-Abdi from Mis'ar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 88}

**Report 10237 and its long note (pp. 88-90, note 1).** Report 10237 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Abu Ishaq, who heard al-Bara): the Prophet told Zayd, who came with a shoulder blade and wrote on it; Ibn Umm Maktum complained of his blindness and the exception was sent down. Shu'ba adds: Sa'd ibn Ibrahim told me, from his father, from a man, from Zayd, like al-Bara's report. The editor (pp. 88-89, with its tail on p. 90; some lines are out of order in print) says Abu Ishaq is al-Sabii and that the printed "Ibn Ishaq" is certainly an error. He lists who gave al-Bara's report: Ahmad in the Musnad from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Muslim from Ibn al-Muthanna and Ibn Bashshar, al-Tayalisi, Ahmad by other routes, al-Bukhari and al-Darimi from Abu al-Walid al-Tayalisi, Ibn Hibban, al-Bukhari again from Hafs ibn Umar, and al-Bayhaqi in the Sunan al-Kubra by two chains. The attached chain (Shu'ba, Sa'd, his father, a man, Zayd, meaning Ibn Thabit) is in his view a different report with a different chain containing an unnamed man, so its chain is weak, while Zayd's report is established in itself and comes at 10239 and 10240. Muslim placed it after al-Bara's report as al-Tabari does, which the editor takes to mean completeness of Shu'ba's transmission, not that it meets Muslim's own standard. He is surprised that al-Nawawi did not discuss it and that al-Mizzi did not list the man among the unnamed; Sa'd is Sa'd ibn Ibrahim ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, whose father was a senior Follower, so the unnamed man may well be a Companion, but the editor cannot say. He sees no flaw in one report from the other, since the story is famous: Zayd ibn Thabit told it, al-Fultan ibn Asim, a Companion, told it at length (al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawa'id names Abu Ya'la, al-Bazzar and al-Tabarani, and says Abu Ya'la's men are trustworthy; Ibn Hajar in al-Isaba names the Musnad of al-Hasan ibn Sufyan, Ibn Abi Shayba, Abu Ya'la and Ibn Hibban; al-Suyuti adds Abd ibn Humayd), and Ibn Abbas told it, as at 10242. (Volume and page numbers in these references are not given because of print damage.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 90}

**Reports 10238-10240 (pp. 89-91).** Report 10238 (Abu Kurayb, from Ishaq ibn Sulayman, from Abu Sinan al-Shaybani, from Abu Ishaq, from Zayd ibn Arqam): Ibn Umm Maktum came and asked: have I no concession? The answer was: no! He said: O God, I am blind, so grant a concession! God sent down the exception, and the Prophet ordered it written (the text adds a gloss that this means the scribe). The editor: Ishaq ibn Sulayman al-Razi al-Abdi is trustworthy, as shown earlier; Abu Sinan is the younger al-Shaybani, Sa'id ibn Sinan al-Burjumi, trustworthy though criticised for some mistakes; Ibn Hajar in the Fath made a serious slip in taking him for Dirar ibn Murra, the elder, since the one who transmits from Abu Ishaq al-Sabii and from whom Ishaq ibn Sulayman transmits is the younger, as al-Mizzi's Tahdhib al-Kamal shows; the printed "Ibn Ishaq" is again an error; al-Haythami gives it from al-Tabarani with trustworthy men; and Ibn Hajar judged the preserved report to be Abu Ishaq from al-Bara, on which the two Sheikhs agree through Shu'ba. Report 10239 (Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Bazi' and Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Ishaq, from al-Zuhri, from Sahl ibn Sa'd): I saw Marwan ibn al-Hakam sitting and he told us from Zayd ibn Thabit that revelation came to the Prophet; Ibn Umm Maktum came while Zayd was writing at the Prophet's dictation and said: if I could fight I would fight! Revelation then came, the Prophet's thigh on Zayd's, so heavy that Zayd feared it would be crushed; it lifted, and the Prophet gave the exception. The editor traces it to al-Nasa'i from Ibn Bazi', and to Ahmad, al-Bukhari, al-Tirmidhi, Ibn al-Jarud and al-Bayhaqi; al-Suyuti adds Ibn Sa'd, Abd ibn Humayd, Abu Dawud, Ibn al-Mundhir and Abu Nu'aym; he glosses "crush" as break and "it lifted" as the strain that seized him during revelation being removed. Report 10240 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from al-Zuhri, from Qabisa ibn Dhu'ayb, from Zayd ibn Thabit): I used to write for the Prophet, who said to write the first sentence; Abd Allah ibn Umm Maktum came: I love jihad but I have the disability you see, my sight has gone! Zayd's thigh again grew heavy under the Prophet's until he feared it would be crushed; then the Prophet had him write the sentence with the exception placed before the mention of the strivers. The editor says it has the sense of the previous one by Qabisa's route, calls Qabisa a trustworthy senior Follower, finds it in the Tafsir of Abd al-Razzaq and in Ahmad's Musnad, and notes that Ibn Kathir cites it and ascribes it to Ibn Abi Hatim and Ibn Jarir while Ibn Hajar ascribes it to Ahmad alone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 91}

**Reports 10241-10242 (pp. 91-92, notes 2 and 1-2).** Report 10241 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ibn Juraij, from Abd al-Karim, that Miqsam, freed slave of Abd Allah ibn al-Harith, told him from Ibn Abbas): the first sentence of the verse concerns those who stayed back from Badr and those who went out to Badr. The editor says it is not in the Tafsir of Abd al-Razzaq, so perhaps in his Musannaf, and he did not find it in the Musnad; al-Bukhari gave it abridged in this way through Hisham from Ibn Juraij and through Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn Juraij; Ibn Kathir says al-Bukhari alone gave it, not Muslim; al-Suyuti adds Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim; a longer form follows. Report 10242 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Abd al-Karim, who heard Miqsam relate from Ibn Abbas): it concerns those who stayed back from Badr and those who went out when the Badr expedition was sent down; Abd Allah ibn Umm Maktum and Abu Ahmad ibn Jahsh ibn Qays al-Asadi said: Messenger of God, we are two blind men, have we a concession? Then the verse came down with the exception. In note 1 the editor says the printed text has a different form of the verb of descent and he kept the manuscript's; in note 2 that this is the longer form of the previous report, with the story of Ibn Umm Maktum already given from al-Bara, Zayd ibn Arqam and Zayd ibn Thabit with some additions. He traces it to al-Tirmidhi (who calls it good and rare by this route, as he quotes), to Ibn Hajar in the Fath, Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti (who adds al-Nasa'i, Ibn al-Mundhir and al-Bayhaqi). Al-Tirmidhi and those who copy him have Abd Allah ibn Jahsh instead of Abu Ahmad ibn Jahsh; Ibn Hajar holds al-Tabari's wording right, since Abd Allah, the brother, was not blind and died at Uhud. For Abu Ahmad the editor cites al-Isaba and Ibn Sa'd: Ibn Hajar holds his name to be Abd with no addition, Ibn Sa'd has Abd Allah, and the editor fears a printer's or copyist's slip; from al-Isaba he quotes that Abu Ahmad was blind, went round Mecca without a guide, was married to a daughter of Abu Sufyan ibn Harb, and took part at Badr and the other battles. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 92}

**Reports 10243-10247 (pp. 92-94, note 1 on p. 93).** Report 10243 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas): Abd Allah ibn Umm Maktum the blind man heard what was sent down on jihad and came to the Prophet: I cannot fight, is there a concession from God if I stay? The Prophet said: I have been commanded nothing about you and do not know whether there will be a concession for you and your companions. Ibn Umm Maktum said: O God, I ask you by my sight! Afterwards God sent down the verse with the exception. Report 10244 (Ibn Humayd, from Hakkam, from Amr, from Ata, from Sa'id): a blind man said: Prophet of God, I love jihad but cannot go; the exception was sent down (the last narrator is given only as Sa'id and is not identified here). Report 10245 (Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Husayn, from Abd Allah ibn Shaddad): Ibn Umm Maktum said: I am blind as you see! The exception was sent down. The editor says Husayn is Ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Sulami and that the report is mursal, since Abd Allah ibn Shaddad ibn al-Had al-Laythi, a senior trustworthy Follower, names no one above him, though its substance is sound from the earlier reports; al-Suyuti has it mursal too and adds Sa'id ibn Mansur and Abd ibn Humayd. Report 10246 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): God excused the people of excuse by the exception, and Ibn Umm Maktum was among them; the mention of the strivers follows. Report 10247 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): when the merit of jihad was mentioned, Ibn Umm Maktum said: I am blind and cannot bear it! God sent down the exception for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 94}

**Reports 10248-10249 (p. 94, notes 1-2).** Report 10248 (al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Abd Allah al-Nufayli, from Zuhayr ibn Mu'awiya, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara): I was with the Prophet, who said: call Zayd for me and tell him to bring the shoulder blade and inkwell (or the tablet and inkwell; the doubt is Zuhayr's) and write the first sentence. Ibn Umm Maktum said: there is harm in my eyes! The exception came down before he left. Report 10249 (al-Muthanna, from Abd Allah ibn Raja al-Basri, from Israil, from Abu Ishaq, from al-Bara, similarly), except that the Prophet said: call Zayd and let him come with a shoulder blade and inkwell, or a tablet. The editor says both are among the versions of al-Bara's report given at 10233-10237, that Ahmad has the first in the Musnad from Hashim ibn al-Qasim from Zuhayr, that Israil is the son of Yunus ibn Abi Ishaq al-Sabii and transmits from his grandfather, that al-Bukhari gave 10249 from Muhammad ibn Yusuf from Israil and again from Ubayd Allah ibn Musa from Israil, and that Ibn Hibban gave it in his Sahih through Muhammad ibn Uthman al-Ijli from Ubayd Allah ibn Musa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 94}

**Reports 10250-10251 and the clause on preference (p. 95, notes 1-2).** Report 10250 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ubayd Allah ibn Musa, from Israil, from Ziyad ibn Fayyad, from Abu Abd al-Rahman): when the first sentence of the verse was sent down, Amr ibn Umm Maktum said: Lord, you have tried me, so what do I do? Then the exception was sent down (this report gives another form of the exception word, which is not repeated here). The editor says Ziyad ibn Fayyad al-Khuza'i al-Kufi was authenticated earlier; he could not identify the shaykh Abu Abd al-Rahman, a very common kunya, who is most likely a Follower, so the report is mursal; al-Suyuti gave it as mursal and ascribed it to Ibn Sa'd, Abd ibn Humayd and al-Tabari. Abu Ja'far then says that Ibn Abbas said about the exception something like what he himself said. Report 10251 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas): the people of harm are meant. Abu Ja'far then explains the clause on preference: God gave those who strove with means and body a standing above the disabled sitters of a single measure, that is one merit (note 2 refers back to his treatment of "degree" in volume 7), through the merit of striving in person; in all else the two are equal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 95}

**Reports 10252-10255 (pp. 96-97, note 1).** Report 10252 (al-Muthanna, from Suwayd, from Ibn al-Mubarak, who heard Ibn Juraij): the preference is over the people of harm. Abu Ja'far then explains that God's promise of the finest outcome covers all, the strivers and the disabled sitters, and that this outcome is Paradise (note 1 refers to his earlier remarks on the word for "each"). Report 10253 (Bishr ibn Mu'adh, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada): it is Paradise, and God gives every possessor of merit his merit. Report 10254 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): Paradise. Abu Ja'far then says that God preferred the strivers over the sitters who are not disabled by a large recompense. Report 10255 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Juraij, finished on p. 97): over the sitters among the believers other than the disabled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 97}
