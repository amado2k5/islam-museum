**Commentary on verse 59 (pp. 28-31)**

Al-Tabari says the readers differed on how to read this. Most readers of the Hijaz and Iraq read it with "ta" in "reckon" and a kasra on the "inna" of "inna-hum", meaning: do not think, Muhammad, that those who disbelieved have outstripped Us and escaped us by themselves; then the report begins afresh on God's power over them: these unbelievers cannot frustrate their Lord when He seeks them and wishes to punish and destroy them. Some readers of Medina and Kufa read "ya" in "yahsabanna" with the same kasra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 28}

Al-Tabari holds this reading unpraiseworthy for two reasons: it departs from the readers' reading and is anomalous, and it is far from eloquent Arabic, because "reckon" calls for its object and predicate, and the reader gave "reckon" a predicate with no subject mentioned. He thinks what led him to it was regard for Ibn Mas'ud's reading, reported as "let not those who disbelieve think that they have outstripped, they cannot frustrate", which is sound and eloquent once "that they" is included since "reckon" governs it. The editor notes that the reading al-Tabari rejects is the one we read today, and corrects a passage in the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 28}

The reader with "ya" has two grammatical defences, though both are far from the most eloquent usage. One is that the meaning is "let not those who disbelieve think that they outstripped", with "that" or "that they" dropped, as in the verse "and among His signs He shows you the lightning in fear and hope" (30:24), meaning "that He shows you"; and a verse of Dhu al-Rumma is cited for this. In the note the editor gives the occasion of that verse: a dispute between Dhu al-Rumma and Utayba ibn Tarthuth over an old well, and the ruling of the governor's agent for Ibn Tarthuth before the dispute was settled, after which the poet composed his poem praising al-Muhajir ibn Abd Allah, governor of al-Yamama; he also corrects a name in the printed text and explains the words for "old", "lying" and "bribes". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 29}

The second defence is that an object of "reckon" is implied, as if he had said "let not those who disbelieve think that they outstripped", with "that they" dropped and implied. Some also explained "it is only Satan who frightens his friends" (3:175) as meaning Satan frightens the believer with his friends, the believer being implied. Some Syrian readers read "ta" with a fatha on "anna-hum"; al-Tabari finds no intelligible sense for this unless the "la" in "they cannot frustrate" is a redundant filler, and no word of God's Book should be turned into redundancy without proof. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an and corrects a phrase in the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 30}

Al-Tabari chooses the reading with "ta" and kasra on "inna-hum", meaning: do not think, Muhammad, that those who rejected God's proofs and denied them have outstripped Us by themselves and escaped Us; they cannot frustrate Us, that is they cannot escape Us or flee from Us. Report 16223, from al-Suddi, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal and Asbat: they will not escape. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 31}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
