**Commentary on verse 53 (pp. 142-146)**

On "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil, except what my Lord has mercy on; my Lord is forgiving, merciful", al-Tabari says Joseph is saying: I do not clear myself of error and slip so as to purify it; "the self commands evil", that is, the selves of servants command them what they desire, even if their desire lies outside what pleases God; "except what my Lord has mercy on", that is, unless my Lord shows mercy to whom He wills among His creatures and saves him from following its desire and obeying it in the evil it commands. "Ma" in "illa ma rahima" is in the accusative, because it is an exception disconnected from what precedes, like the words in Sura Ya Sin "nor will they be rescued, except as a mercy from Us", meaning unless they are shown mercy; and "an", when it has the sense of a verbal noun, is close to "ma". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 142}

By "my Lord is forgiving, merciful" he means that God overlooks the sins of whoever repents of them by not punishing or exposing him for them, and is merciful to him after his repentance by not punishing him for them. It is reported that Joseph said this because when he said "that he may know that I did not betray him in his absence", an angel said: not even on the day you intended her? And then Joseph said "and I do not absolve myself". It is also said that the one who said to Joseph "not even on the day you intended her, and loosened your trousers" was the Aziz's wife, and Joseph answered her thus. And it is said that Joseph said it on his own account from the start. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 143}

Report 19428, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima: when the king gathered the women and asked whether they had sought Joseph, they said "God forbid, we know no evil of him", and the Aziz's wife said "now the truth has become clear", and Joseph said "that he may know that I did not betray him in his absence"; Gabriel said to him: not even on the day you intended what you intended? and he said "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil". Report 19429, through Ibn Waki', his father, Isra'il, Simak and Ikrima from Ibn Abbas, is like the report of Abu Kurayb from Waki'. Report 19430 begins at the end of the page, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Amr and Isra'il. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 143}

Report 19430 continues: when Pharaoh gathered the women (the printed text has "the king"; the editor keeps the manuscript reading) he said: you sought Joseph; then he mentions the like, except that Gabriel nudged him and said: not even when you intended her? and Joseph said "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil". Report 19431, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Mis'ar and Abu Husayn: when Joseph said "that he may know that I did not betray him in his absence", Gabriel, or an angel, said: not even on the day you intended what you intended? and he said "and I do not absolve myself". Reports 19432 and 19433 are similar, with the speaker given as the angel, or Gabriel. Reports 19434 and 19435, from Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Sufyan and Abu Sinan: Gabriel said to him: not even on the day you intended what you intended? and he said "and I do not absolve myself". Report 19436 begins at the end of the page, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Amr and Mis'ar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 144}

Report 19436 concludes, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, like the report of Ibn Waki'. Reports 19437 and 19438, from al-Hasan, through Ibn Waki', al-Ala ibn Abd al-Jabbar, Zayd ibn Hubab, Hammad ibn Salama and Thabit, and through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Affan: Gabriel said to him, remember your intention, and he said "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil". Reports 19439 and 19440, from Abu Salih, through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Isma'il ibn Salim, and through al-Muthanna and Amr ibn Awn: this is Joseph's saying, and Gabriel said to him: not even when you loosened your trousers? and Joseph said "and I do not absolve myself". Report 19441, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: we were told that the angel who was with Joseph said to him, remember what you intended, and God's prophet said "and I do not absolve myself". Report 19442, from Qatada: it reached me that the king said to him when he said what he said, do you remember your intention? and he said "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil, except what my Lord has mercy on". Report 19443, from Ikrima, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: the king poked his side and said, O Joseph, not even when you intended? and he said "and I do not absolve myself". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 145}

Among those who said the speaker was the Aziz's wife: report 19444, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: Joseph said "that he may know" when he was brought, so that the Aziz would know he had not betrayed him in his absence regarding his household, and that God does not guide the plot of the betrayers; the Aziz's wife said, O Joseph, not even the day you loosened your trousers? and Joseph said "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil". Among those who said Joseph said it to himself, with no one reminding him but recalling what had passed: report 19445, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: it is Joseph's saying to his king when God showed his excuse; he recalled that he had intended her and she had intended him, and Joseph said "and I do not absolve myself; the self commands evil". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 146}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
