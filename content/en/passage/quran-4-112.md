## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 112 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 112: error, sin and the one cast as guilty (pp. 197-199, notes 1-4 on p. 197, notes 1-4 on p. 198 and notes 1-2 on p. 199).** Al-Tabari says the sense is: whoever commits a fault, the word for a sin, or a sin, which is what is not lawful of disobedience. He explains that God distinguished the two because a fault may come from intent or without intent, while a sin comes only from intent; so the sense is that whoever commits a fault without intent or a sin with intent, then casts it on an innocent, that is attributes to one innocent of what he attributed to him his own error or deliberate sin, has taken on himself a calumny, meaning a falsehood and lie, and a great sin and a great crime, committed knowingly and deliberately. The interpreters differed over who the innocent is, after all of them agreed that the one who cast the sin was Ibn Ubayriq, whose affair he had described. Some said God meant a Muslim called Labid ibn Sahl (as in report 10411); others said a Jew called Zayd ibn al-Samin (reports 10416 and 10412 earlier), and among those who said that he was a Jew is Ibn Sirin. Report 10425 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Ghundar, from Shu'ba, from Khalid al-Hadhdha', from Ibn Sirin) says: a Jew. Report 10426 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Badal ibn al-Muhabbar, from Shu'ba, from Khalid, from Ibn Sirin) is the same. It is also said that casting it on an innocent means casting the sin this traitor committed on one innocent of what he accused him of, so that the pronoun refers to the sin; and if it were made to refer to both the sin and the fault that would be allowed, because acts, though the expressions for them differ, return to one sense, being an act. On the closing clause, he who casts it has borne a calumny, which is falsehood and lie, and a manifest sin, meaning a manifest burden that makes plain the matter of its bearer, his boldness against his Lord and his advance against what He forbade him, to anyone who knows his affair. The editor's notes: references to earlier explanations of the word for fault (volume 2, pp. 284-285 and 110) and of calumny (volume 8, p. 124 and volume 5, p. 432); that the printed text had an addition which he removed because it spoiled the commentary, and he followed the manuscript, and that the printed text reads "then describes what he committed of his error" where the manuscript is right; that Badal ibn al-Muhabbar al-Tamimi al-Yarbu'i narrated from Shu'ba and from al-Khalil ibn Ahmad, the author of the science of prosody, that al-Bukhari and the four collectors narrate from him through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, and that he is reliable, his name being vocalised with two short vowels; that the view recorded as a saying "it was said" is an abridgement of al-Farra' in his commentary on the Qur'an (1:286-287); and, on p. 199, a reference to the explanation of the word for manifest and that the printed text had "makes plain the matter of his deed", where the manuscript's form is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 199} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 199}
