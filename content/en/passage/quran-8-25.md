## Summary
Verse 25 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 25 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 25 (pp. 473-476)**

Al-Tabari says on "and fear a trial that will not befall only those of you who did wrong; know that God is severe in punishment" [[passage/quran-8-25|8:25]]: he says to the believers, fear a trial, that is a test from God and an affliction He tries you with, which will not befall only those who did wrong, they being those who did what was not for them to do, of crimes they committed and sins between them and God which they rode, and he warns them against riding a disobedience or doing a sin by which they earn a punishment. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "trial", "befalling" and "particularity". It was said that the verse came down about a group of the Messenger's Companions who are those meant; report 15903, from al-Hasan: it came down about Ali, Uthman, Talha and al-Zubayr; reports 15904, 15905 and 15906, from al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam: it came down and we did not see any of us falling into it, then it befell us particularly, and I read it for a time not thinking we were of its people, then we were the ones meant. The editor gives the manuscript's reading in 15904 and weakens narrators in the chains of 15905 and 15906: Zayd ibn Awf Abu Rabi'a is weak and al-Salt ibn Dinar is abandoned, and says he found no report by Uqba ibn Sahban from al-Zubayr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 473} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 474}

Reports 15907 and 15908, from al-Suddi: it came down about the people of Badr in particular and befell them on the Day of the Camel, and they fought each other. Report 15909, from Ibn Abbas: God commanded the believers not to let wrongdoing stand among them, lest God afflict them all with punishment. Report 15910, from Mujahid: it is for you as well. Report 15911, from Ibn Zayd: the trial is misguidance. Report 15912, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud: none of you but is wrapped in a trial, and he cites [[passage/quran-8-28|8:28]], so let him seek refuge in God from misleading trials; the editor says the likely right wording there is "whoever of you seeks refuge, let him seek refuge". Report 15913, from al-Hasan: al-Zubayr said we were made afraid by it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 474} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 475}

Then al-Tabari says the people of Arabic differed on the interpretation of this: some Basran grammarians said "will not befall" is not an answer but a prohibition after a command, for were it an answer the emphatic nun would not have entered. Some Kufan grammarians said He commanded them and then forbade them, with a tinge of the conditional though it is a prohibition, and similar is the ant's saying in [[passage/quran-27-18|27:18]]; this is al-Farra's statement in his Ma'ani al-Qur'an. The editor corrects the printed edition's wording on "a tinge of the conditional", saying what he gives matches al-Farra. The text of p. 476 is badly damaged in the scan, so we keep to what can be made out: the words "know that God is severe in punishment" are a warning from God and a threat to whoever falls into the trial He warned against, meaning know, believers, that your Lord's punishment is severe for whoever is tried by wronging himself and goes against His command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 475} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 476}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
