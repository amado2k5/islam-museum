## Summary
Verse 144 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/change-of-qibla|Change of Qibla]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 144 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "We see the turning of your face toward the sky, and We shall turn you to a qibla that pleases you; so turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque" al-Tabari says it means We see, Muhammad, the turning of your face, that is its shifting, in the sky, that is toward the sky and its direction; it was said to him because, before his qibla was changed, he lifted his gaze to the sky awaiting God's command to turn to the Ka'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 172} He reports from Qatada and al-Rabi' that he turned his face to the sky, loving that God would turn him to the Ka'ba, until God did, and from al-Suddi that when he prayed he raised his head to the sky to see what he was commanded, before the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 173}

He then notes the differing accounts of why he longed for the Ka'ba's direction. Some said he disliked the Jerusalem qibla because the Jews said he follows our qibla and differs from us in our religion; from Mujahid, he used to pray to God and ask for the qibla to be prescribed, so the verse was revealed and the Jews' saying ended. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 173}

In the report of Mujahid that he continues, the verse came down during the noon prayer, and the men took the women's places and the women the men's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 173} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 174} From Ibn Zayd, the Prophet said of the Jews that they face one of God's houses, so he faced it for sixteen months; word reached him that the Jews said Muhammad and his companions did not know where their qibla was until we guided them; he disliked that and raised his face to the sky, and the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 174} Others said he longed for it because it was the qibla of his forefather Abraham; from Ibn 'Abbas, when he migrated and most of Medina were Jews, God commanded him to face Jerusalem and the Jews were glad, but he loved the qibla of Abraham, prayed and looked to the sky, and the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 174}

On "We shall turn you to a qibla that pleases you" al-Tabari says: We shall turn you from Jerusalem to a qibla you are pleased with, that is, love and desire. "Turn your face" means direct and change your face. "Toward the Sacred Mosque": "shatr" means direction, aim and facing, and he cites a line of a Hudhali and one of Ibn Ahmar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 175} He reports from Abu al-'Aliya, Ibn 'Abbas, Mujahid, Qatada, al-Rabi' and Ibn Jurayj from Ibn 'Abbas that it means facing it or toward it, from al-Bara' that it means in front of it, and from Ibn Zayd that it means its side, edge, and its sides are "shutur". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 176} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 177}

He then records their disagreement over the place in the Sacred Mosque he was told to face. Some said the qibla he was turned to is opposite the Ka'ba's water spout (mizab); from 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, who said "this is the qibla", and it is reported he sat in the Sacred Mosque opposite the spout and recited the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 177} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 178} Others said the whole House is the qibla and the House's qibla is the door, and he begins to name those who said so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 178}
