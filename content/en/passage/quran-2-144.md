## Summary
Verse 144 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/change-of-qibla|Change of Qibla]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq} {cite:sahih-al-bukhari}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 144 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "We see the turning of your face toward the sky, and We shall turn you to a qibla that pleases you; so turn your face toward the Sacred Mosque" al-Tabari says it means We see, Muhammad, the turning of your face, that is its shifting, in the sky, that is toward the sky and its direction; it was said to him because, before his qibla was changed, he lifted his gaze to the sky awaiting God's command to turn to the Ka'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 172} He reports from Qatada and al-Rabi' that he turned his face to the sky, loving that God would turn him to the Ka'ba, until God did, and from al-Suddi that when he prayed he raised his head to the sky to see what he was commanded, before the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 172} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 173}

He then notes the differing accounts of why he longed for the Ka'ba's direction. Some said he disliked the Jerusalem qibla because the Jews said he follows our qibla and differs from us in our religion; from Mujahid, he used to pray to God and ask for the qibla to be prescribed, so the verse was revealed and the Jews' saying ended. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 173}
