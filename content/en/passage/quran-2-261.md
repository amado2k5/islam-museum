## Summary
Verse 261 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 261 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari's commentary on ([[passage/quran-2-261|2:261]]) in volume 5, from p. 512. Abu Jafar says this verse returns to what is in ([[passage/quran-2-245|2:245]]) on the goodly loan, and that the verses between, the stories of the Children of Israel and their account with Talut and Jalut, the report of the one who disputed with Abraham, the matter of the one who passed the ruined village, and the story of Abraham's request, are an interposition by God with their stories: partly as argument against the polytheists who denied resurrection and the Hour, partly as exhortation to the believers to fight in His way as He commanded in ([[passage/quran-2-249|2:249]]), telling them that He supports them though few against many foes and teaching them His way that He backs those on their path and abandons the unbelievers on the path of their enemies; and partly to cut off the excuse of the Jews living in the migration town of the Messenger of God, by what He disclosed to His prophet of their hidden matters and the concealed secrets of their forebears, that they might know that what Muhammad brought was from God and no conjecture or invention, and to warn the hypocrites among them, that their doubt about Muhammad's matter might bring on them the like of the might He brought on their forebears in the village He destroyed; this is al-Tabari's account and understanding of the verses' arrangement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 512} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 513} The editor explains in a note the sentence's subject and predicate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 512}

Al-Tabari returns to the report of the one who lends God a goodly loan and the reward with Him. He says the sense is: the likeness of those who spend their wealth on themselves in fighting God's enemies, with their persons and wealth, is that of a grain of wheat or barley or another plant of the earth whose crop puts forth ears from a sower's seed, which put forth seven ears with a hundred grains in each; so the one who spends his wealth on himself in God's way has a reward of seven hundredfold on each one of his spending. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 513} Al-Suddi (6028): this is for the one who spends in God's way and has his reward at seven hundred. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 513} Ibn Zayd (6029): this is the one who spends on himself in God's way and goes out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 513} In notes the editor explains the increase of seed as growth and gain, corrects phrases in the printed text and manuscript, and completes a phrase from al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 513} Al-Rabi' (6030): whoever pledged allegiance to the Prophet on migration and kept watch with him in Medina and went nowhere without his leave had his good deed at seven hundredfold, and whoever pledged to Islam had it at tenfold. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 514} The editor adds a note on the wording and a reference to al-Durr al-Manthur. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 514}

Al-Tabari raises a question: have you seen an ear with a hundred grains, or heard of one, that the likeness of one who spends is drawn from it? He answers that if it exists, so be it; if not, the sense may be like an ear that put forth seven ears if God places that in it; or it may mean that when sown it puts forth a hundred grains; this answer is his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 514} The editor reports in a note that the margin of the manuscript carries a comment on this question in a hand other than the copyist's, apparently Maghribi, the first such comment he has found, and gives its text: that this is established and observed in his country, where the ears of grain have many grains and shoots, that he counted sixty-three shoots from one grain and saw nearly as much several times, and it is signed, per the editor, Muhammad ibn Mahmud al-Jazari al-Hanafi; he refers to al-Qurtubi and others. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 514} We stop here, as al-Tabari's discussion runs onto the next page. These reports are given as transmitted, al-Tabari's account and answer are attributed to him, and the marginal comment is a later reader's, reported by the editor, with no verdict from the museum.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-261|2:261]]) from p. 514. Al-Tabari says that some interpreters took the grain to mean that when sown it puts forth a hundred grains, the product being added to the original; al-Dahhak (6031): each ear put forth a hundred grains, and this is for the one who spends in God's way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 515}

Al-Tabari then explains the clause on multiplying for whom He wills. The interpreters differed on it. Some said: He multiplies the reward of the good deeds of whom He wills among His servants, beyond what He gave one who did not spend in His way, short of what He promised one who spends in His way of seven hundredfold; as for the one who spends in His way, He does not reduce him from what He promised. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 515} Al-Dahhak (6032): this is multiplied for one who spent in God's way, meaning the seven hundred, and the multiplication for whom He wills refers to one who did not spend in His way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 515} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 516} In a note the editor explains that the printed text's wording was altered from the manuscript's because it was corrupt, and that from the sequence of the commentary he judges the passage to contain a slip and an omission, which he completed in line with these interpreters' sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 515}

Others said the sense is that God multiplies for whom He wills among those who spend in His way beyond seven hundred up to two million times; al-Tabari says this was reported from Ibn Abbas by a route whose chain he did not find, so he left it out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 516} Al-Tabari's choice. Abu Jafar says the better interpretation of the clause is that God multiplies beyond seven hundred to whatever multiplication He wills for whom He wills among those who spend in His way, because no reward and multiplication was mentioned for one who does not spend in His way, so it would not be proper to take the multiplication promised in the verse as a promise for work done other than in His way; this choice and argument are his. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 516} In a note the editor says he added a bracketed phrase required by the sense of the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 516}

Al-Tabari then explains the close of ([[passage/quran-2-261|2:261]]), on the two attributes of God in it, vastness and knowledge: the first means that He can increase those He wills of His creatures who spend in His way beyond the sevenfold-hundred He promised, the second that He knows which of them merits the increase, and the editor refers to earlier explanation of the two attributes and to the language indexes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 516} Ibn Zayd (6033): the first, that He can increase from His vastness; the second, He knows whom He increases. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 516} Others said the first means He is vast enough for those multiples, and the second that He knows what those spend who spend their wealth in obedience to God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 517}
