## Summary
Verse 131 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 131 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 131, "that is because your Lord would not destroy the towns unjustly while their people were unaware", al-Tabari says: We sent the messengers, Muhammad, to those whose case I described, the idolaters of mankind and the jinn, relating My signs to them and warning them of meeting Me on their return, because your Lord would not destroy the towns unjustly. "Unjustly" can be taken in two ways. First, by the idolatry of those of their people who joined others with God and the unbelief of those who disbelieved, as Luqman said "idolatry is a great wrong" (31:13); "while their people were unaware" means He would not hasten punishment on them until He sent them messengers to alert them to God's proofs and warn them of His punishment, so they could not say "no bringer of good news or warner came to us". Second, that He would not destroy them without reminding and alerting them through messengers, signs and lessons, thereby wronging them, and God is not unjust to His servants. Al-Tabari's choice: the first of the two is sounder, that He would not destroy them for their idolatry without sending messengers to them and giving them notice, because "that is because" comes straight after "did not messengers come to you", which clearly shows that the sense is: we did that because we do not destroy towns without reminder and warning. The editor notes that this answers al-Farra', who held the second saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 124}

As for "dhalika", it may be in the accusative, meaning "we did that", or in the nominative as a subject, as if to say "that is so"; as for "an", it stands in the accusative, meaning we did that because your Lord would not destroy the towns, for once the preposition governing it is dropped, the verb takes hold of it and puts it in the accusative (the editor refers to al-Farra'). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 125}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
