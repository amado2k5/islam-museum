## Summary
Verse 72 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 72 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 72: the plan to believe at the opening of the day and disbelieve at its end.** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed on the nature of what this group was told to do: to believe at the start of the day and disbelieve at its end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 506}

**First view: an outward profession, then disbelief.** Some said they were told to affirm the Prophet outwardly in his prophethood and in what he brought, with no resolve or conviction of the heart, and then to reject him and deny it all at the day's end. The editor says the printed text has a different word from the manuscript's, that it is sound and firmly established, and that the phrase about outward affirmation links to what precedes it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 506} Al-Tabari gives report 7231 (al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Mamar, from Qatada): they said to one another, show them approval of their religion at the start of the day and disbelieve at its end, for that is likelier to win their trust, make them think you saw in them something you dislike, and make them more likely to turn from their religion. In report 7232 (al-Muthanna, from Mualla ibn Asad, from Khalid, from Husayn, from Abu Malik) the Jews said: believe with them at the start of the day and reject at its end, so that they may come back with you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 507}

**Report 7233 (al-Suddi).** Al-Tabari gives (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) that the rabbis of the villages of Urayna, twelve of them, said to one another: enter Muhammad's religion at the start of the day and say we testify he is right and truthful, then at the end of the day disbelieve and say we went back to our scholars and rabbis and asked them, and they told us Muhammad is a liar and that you have no ground; we have gone back to our religion, which is dearer to us than yours. Perhaps they would doubt, saying: these were with us at the start of the day, what is the matter with them? God informed His messenger of this. The editor says the printed text read the village name differently, a corrupt reading of an unpointed manuscript, that the right form is in al-Durr al-Manthur (2: 42), and that it is a place name (Mujam ma ustajam, p. 929). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 507}

**Report 7234.** In report 7234 (told to the compiler from Ammar, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from Husayn, from Abu Malik al-Ghifari) the Jews said to one another: submit at the start of the day and renounce at its end, perhaps they will go back; and God disclosed their secret. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 507}

**Second view: the prayer.** Others said that what they were told to do was the prayer: to attend it with the Muslims at the start of the day and leave it at the end. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 508}

**Reports 7235-7237.** In report 7235 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) the Jews said this: they prayed the dawn prayer with Muhammad and disbelieved at the end of the day as a ruse, so that people would see that error had appeared to them from him after they had followed him. Report 7236 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. In report 7237 (Muhammad ibn Sad, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn Abbas) a group of Jews said: when you meet the companions of Muhammad at the start of the day, believe, and when it is the end, pray your own prayer, so that they may say these are people of a scripture who know more than we do and turn back from their religion; and trust only one who follows your religion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 508}

**The start of al-Tabari's own reading.** He says the meaning of the speech is therefore: a group of the People of the Book, meaning the Jews who read the Torah, said: affirm what Muhammad brought of the true religion, its laws and his practice, at the opening of the day, the opening meaning the first part of the day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 508}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at the beginning of al-Tabari's own reading on p. 508 and continue from p. 509.
