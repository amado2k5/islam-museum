**Commentary on verse 176 (pp. 261-264)**

Al-Tabari says: had We willed, We would have raised this man to whom We gave Our signs by those signs We gave him, "but he clung to the earth", meaning he settled into the life of this world on earth and what belongs to it and preferred its pleasure and desires to the hereafter, "and followed his desire", meaning he cast off obedience to God and went against His command. He says the story of the one whose case God describes, with the scholars differing about his case, is what follows. Report 15420, from Sayyar: Bal'am had been given prophethood and was answered in prayer; Moses came with the Children of Israel making for the land where Bal'am was, or he said Syria, and the people were struck with great fear of him, so they went to Bal'am and said pray against this man and his army. He said: until I take counsel with my Lord; and he was told, do not pray against them, for they are My servants and their prophet is among them. He told his people that he had taken counsel with his Lord about praying against them and had been forbidden. They sent him a gift, which he accepted, and came back to him, and he said again until I take counsel, but nothing came back to him; they said, had your Lord disliked your praying against them He would have forbidden you as He did the first time. He began to pray against them, but when he prayed against them the prayer ran on his tongue against his own people, and when he meant to pray for victory for his people he prayed for victory for Moses and his army; they said we see you pray only against us, and he said nothing runs on my tongue but this, and had I prayed against him I would not have been answered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 261} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 263}

Then he said: but I shall show you a matter in which their destruction may lie: God hates fornication, and if they fall into fornication they will perish, so send out the women to meet them, for they are travellers and perhaps they will fornicate and perish. They did so. The king had a daughter, and her father, or Bal'am, told her to yield herself to no one but Moses. They fell into fornication, and the head of a tribe of the Children of Israel came to her and wanted her, and she said I yield myself to no one but Moses; he said my standing is such and such, and she sent to her father to consult him and he said let him have you. A man of the sons of Aaron came to them with a spear and thrust through them, God having strengthened him, and he pierced them both and lifted them on his spear, and the people saw them. God sent the plague on them and seventy thousand died. Sayyar also related that Bal'am rode a she-donkey of his and when he came to al-Fulul, or a road among al-Fulul, he began to beat her and she would not go forward; she stood under him and said why do you beat me, do you not see what is before you? and there was Satan before him, so he got down and prostrated to him. God said the words of the story, up to [[passage/quran-7-176|7:176]]. Al-Mu'tamir says: Sayyar told me this, and I do not know whether something of another's report has entered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

In the notes the editor explains that the printed edition meddled with the wording of this report and he keeps the manuscript; that "nothing came back to him" is from a verb meaning to return; and that "al-Fulul" he takes to be the remnant of Moses's army after the punishment that killed seventy thousand of it. He identifies al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman al-Taymi, the well-known imam, and his father Sulayman ibn Tarkhan, and Sayyar as Sayyar ibn Salama Abu al-Minhal al-Riyahi, the well-known trustworthy narrator, and says Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti gave the report abridged. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

Report 15421, from al-Mu'tamir from his father: a report reached me from a man of the People of the Book that Moses asked God to overcome him and make him one of the people of the Fire, and God did; and I was told that Moses killed him afterwards. Report 15422, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq from Salim Abu al-Nadr: when Moses camped in the land of the Canaanites in Syria, and Bal'am was at Bala'a, a village of al-Balqa, Bal'am's people came to him and said Bal'am, this is Moses son of Imran with the Children of Israel, who has come to drive us from our land, kill us and settle the Children of Israel in it, and you are answered in prayer, so go out and pray against them. He said: woe to you, a prophet of God with the angels and the believers, how can I go and pray against them when I know of God what I know? They said we have no home. They kept on, softening him and pleading with him, until they tempted him and he was tempted, and he rode a she-donkey of his toward the mountain that gives a view. The editor notes that the bracketed words are from al-Tabari's History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

We stop here at the foot of p. 264, in report 15422 where he rides the she-donkey toward the mountain; the next batch begins at p. 265. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
