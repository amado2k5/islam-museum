**Commentary on verse 176 (pp. 261-275)**

Al-Tabari says: had We willed, We would have raised this man to whom We gave Our signs by those signs We gave him, "but he clung to the earth", meaning he settled into the life of this world on earth and what belongs to it and preferred its pleasure and desires to the hereafter, "and followed his desire", meaning he cast off obedience to God and went against His command. He says the story of the one whose case God describes, with the scholars differing about his case, is what follows. Report 15420, from Sayyar: Bal'am had been given prophethood and was answered in prayer; Moses came with the Children of Israel making for the land where Bal'am was, or he said Syria, and the people were struck with great fear of him, so they went to Bal'am and said pray against this man and his army. He said: until I take counsel with my Lord; and he was told, do not pray against them, for they are My servants and their prophet is among them. He told his people that he had taken counsel with his Lord about praying against them and had been forbidden. They sent him a gift, which he accepted, and came back to him, and he said again until I take counsel, but nothing came back to him; they said, had your Lord disliked your praying against them He would have forbidden you as He did the first time. He began to pray against them, but when he prayed against them the prayer ran on his tongue against his own people, and when he meant to pray for victory for his people he prayed for victory for Moses and his army; they said we see you pray only against us, and he said nothing runs on my tongue but this, and had I prayed against him I would not have been answered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 261} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 263}

Then he said: but I shall show you a matter in which their destruction may lie: God hates fornication, and if they fall into fornication they will perish, so send out the women to meet them, for they are travellers and perhaps they will fornicate and perish. They did so. The king had a daughter, and her father, or Bal'am, told her to yield herself to no one but Moses. They fell into fornication, and the head of a tribe of the Children of Israel came to her and wanted her, and she said I yield myself to no one but Moses; he said my standing is such and such, and she sent to her father to consult him and he said let him have you. A man of the sons of Aaron came to them with a spear and thrust through them, God having strengthened him, and he pierced them both and lifted them on his spear, and the people saw them. God sent the plague on them and seventy thousand died. Sayyar also related that Bal'am rode a she-donkey of his and when he came to al-Fulul, or a road among al-Fulul, he began to beat her and she would not go forward; she stood under him and said why do you beat me, do you not see what is before you? and there was Satan before him, so he got down and prostrated to him. God said the words of the story, up to [[passage/quran-7-176|7:176]]. Al-Mu'tamir says: Sayyar told me this, and I do not know whether something of another's report has entered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

In the notes the editor explains that the printed edition meddled with the wording of this report and he keeps the manuscript; that "nothing came back to him" is from a verb meaning to return; and that "al-Fulul" he takes to be the remnant of Moses's army after the punishment that killed seventy thousand of it. He identifies al-Mu'tamir ibn Sulayman al-Taymi, the well-known imam, and his father Sulayman ibn Tarkhan, and Sayyar as Sayyar ibn Salama Abu al-Minhal al-Riyahi, the well-known trustworthy narrator, and says Ibn Kathir and al-Suyuti gave the report abridged. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 262} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 263} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

Report 15421, from al-Mu'tamir from his father: a report reached me from a man of the People of the Book that Moses asked God to overcome him and make him one of the people of the Fire, and God did; and I was told that Moses killed him afterwards. Report 15422, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq from Salim Abu al-Nadr: when Moses camped in the land of the Canaanites in Syria, and Bal'am was at Bala'a, a village of al-Balqa, Bal'am's people came to him and said Bal'am, this is Moses son of Imran with the Children of Israel, who has come to drive us from our land, kill us and settle the Children of Israel in it, and you are answered in prayer, so go out and pray against them. He said: woe to you, a prophet of God with the angels and the believers, how can I go and pray against them when I know of God what I know? They said we have no home. They kept on, softening him and pleading with him, until they tempted him and he was tempted, and he rode a she-donkey of his toward the mountain that gives a view. The editor notes that the bracketed words are from al-Tabari's History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264}

Report 15422 is completed: when she had gone a little way with him she lay down under him, so he got down and beat her until she was worn out, and she rose and he rode her; this happened three times, and then God permitted her to speak to him as a proof against him, and she said: woe to you, Bal'am, where are you going? Do you not see the angels before me turning me from my way? Are you going to a prophet of God and the believers to pray against them? He did not stop beating her, and God let her go; she went on until she brought him up to the top of Mount Husban over the camp of Moses and the Children of Israel. He began to pray against them, but whatever he prayed against them his tongue turned to his own people, and whatever good he prayed for his people his tongue turned to the Children of Israel. His people said: do you know what you are doing? You are praying for them and against us! He said: this is beyond my power, God has overcome me in it. And his tongue lolled out and fell on his chest. The editor notes that the mountain's name is Hasan in the printed edition and unpointed in the manuscript, that he follows the spelling that agrees with the History and found it in no gazetteer, explains the verb for being worn out as being pushed to exhaustion, and that al-Tabari gave the report in his History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 264} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 265}
Then he said to his people: this world and the hereafter are now gone from me and only cunning and stratagem remain, so I shall plot for you: adorn the women, give them goods, send them to the camp to sell them there, and tell them that no woman is to refuse herself to any man who wants her, for if one of them fornicates you will be spared them. They did so. When the women entered the camp a Canaanite woman named Kozbi daughter of Zur, head of her people, passed a man of the great ones of the Children of Israel, Zimri son of Salum, head of the tribe of Simeon son of Jacob; he rose to her and took her by the hand when her beauty pleased him, then brought her and stood with her before Moses and said: I suppose you will say she is forbidden to you? He said: yes, she is forbidden to you, do not go near her. He said: by God, we shall not obey you in this. He went into his tent with her and lay with her, and God sent the plague among the Children of Israel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 265} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 266}
Phinehas son of Eleazar son of Aaron, who was in charge of Moses's affairs, a man given stature and strength of grip, was away when Zimri did what he did, and came while the plague was sweeping through the Children of Israel and was told. He took his spear, which was all of iron, went into the tent where the two lay together, and ran them through with his spear; then he came out lifting them toward the sky, the spear held in his arm, his elbow braced against his flank and the spear resting against his jaws, he being the firstborn of Eleazar, and he kept saying: God, thus we do to whoever disobeys You. The plague was lifted. Those who died of the Children of Israel between Zimri's meeting the woman and Phinehas's killing him were counted and found to be seventy thousand, those who say fewer saying twenty thousand, in one hour of the day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 266}
From that came the custom that the Children of Israel give the descendants of Phinehas son of Eleazar from every beast they slaughter the stomach part, the foreleg and the jaws, because of his bracing the spear against his flank, taking it in his arm and resting it against his jaws, and the firstborn of all their goods and persons, because he was Eleazar's firstborn. About Bal'am son of Ba'ur God sent down to Muhammad "recite to them the tidings of him to whom We gave Our signs and he stripped himself of them", meaning Bal'am, "and Satan followed him and he was among the misled", up to "that they may reflect". The editor notes that al-Tabari gave this in his History, that "qiba" is a part of the stomach, that the History and manuscript read "his beard" but the printed reading is right since the Children of Israel are said to give the "jaws", and that "yahus" with the unpointed ha means moving through and scattering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 266} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 267}
Report 15423, from al-Suddi: a man of the Children of Israel called Bal'am went to the giants and said do not fear the Children of Israel, for when you go out to fight them I will pray against them and they will perish. Joshua went out to fight the giants with the people, and Bal'am went out with the giants on his she-donkey wanting to curse the Children of Israel; whenever he meant to pray against the Children of Israel he prayed against the giants, and they said you are praying only against us, and he said I meant the Children of Israel. When he reached the city gate an angel took hold of the donkey's tail and held her; he tried to move her and she did not move, and when he beat her much she spoke and said: you lie with me by night and ride me by day! Woe to me from you; had I been able to go out I would have, but this angel holds me back. About Bal'am God says "recite to them the tidings of him to whom We gave Our signs". The editor says part of it came as report 15411 and that it is in the History. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 267}
Report 15424, from Ikrima: a woman among them said show me Moses and I will tempt him; she perfumed herself and passed a man who resembled Moses, and he lay with her. A son of Aaron was told, took a sword and thrust it through the pair, then lifted them so that the people saw them and knew it was not Moses. So the house of Aaron was preferred in the offering over the house of Moses with the liver, the upper arm and the thigh. He said: this is the one to whom We gave Our signs and who stripped himself of them, meaning Bal'am. The editor says he doubts a phrase of the report and that the manuscript has a word he thinks should read "the shoulder-meeting". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 267} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 268}
The interpreters differed over "had We willed, We would have raised him by them". Some said it means We would have raised him by his knowledge of them, and report 15425, from Ibn Abbas, says God would have raised him by his knowledge. Others said it means We would have lifted from him the state he came to of disbelief in God, through Our signs, and reports 15426 and 15427, from Mujahid, say "We would have warded it off from him", the editor noting that the printed edition had "raised" and the right reading is with the letter dal as in the manuscript. Al-Tabari then chooses that God made the report general: had He willed He would have raised him by the signs He gave him; "raising" covers many senses, raising in rank with Him, in worldly honour and noble qualities, and in good repute and high praise, and it may be that God meant all of it, that had He willed He would have raised him and given him all this by enabling him to act on His signs. So it is right not to restrict any of it, there being no indication of restriction from report or reason. As to "by them", Ibn Zayd said as we said, report 15428: by those signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 268} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 269}
As to "but he clung to the earth", the interpreters said much as we have: report 15429, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, he leant on the earth; report 15430, from him, he was drawn to the earth; report 15431, from Mujahid, he settled; report 15432, from Ibn Abbas, there was among the Children of Israel Bal'am ibn Ba'ur, who was given a book and clung to the desires of the earth, its pleasure and its wealth, and gained nothing from what the book brought; report 15433, from al-Suddi, he followed this world and leant on it. Then al-Tabari says: the root sense of "ikhlad" in Arabic speech is delay and staying, so one says someone "akhlada" in a place when he stays there, and "akhlada himself" to a place when he comes to it from another. The editor says this last explanation is found in none of the dictionaries, so it should be noted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 269} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 270}
Al-Tabari cites the opening of a poem by Zuhayr, asking about the abodes he passed, whose traces are like writing on a stone in a watercourse, in which the word for the dweller means one who stays, and a verse of Malik ibn Nuwayra describing the sons of clans of the tribes of Malik and Amr ibn Yarbu' as having stayed and settled. The editor notes that the printed edition had a different place-name in Zuhayr's line and the right form is that of the manuscript and the divan, explains the words for the rugged ground, the writing and the stone of the watercourse, and says Malik's line is from a poem composed about the day of Mukhattat, in the Asma'iyyat, giving the lines before it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 270}
Some Basrans, whom the editor identifies as Abu Ubayda Ma'mar ibn al-Muthanna, said that "akhlada" means he clung, hung back and lingered, and that a "mukhlid" is also a man whose grey hair comes late, or among beasts one whose front teeth remain until the fourth teeth come out. As to "and followed his desire", Ibn Zayd said of its sense, in report 15434: his desire was with the people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 271}
Then comes the sentence "his likeness is as the likeness of the dog: if you drive it away it pants, or if you leave it it pants", and al-Tabari says: the likeness of this man to whom We gave Our signs and who stripped himself of them is that of the dog that pants whether you drive it off or leave it. The interpreters differed over the reason God made his likeness that of the dog. Some said He likened him to it in panting for his abandoning action on God's book and signs that He had given him and turning away from His admonitions, so that he is the same whether admonished by God's signs or not, in that he is not admonished and does not give up unbelief, as the dog's state is the same in its panting whether driven off or not. Report 15435, from Mujahid: you drive it off, it is like the man who reads the book and does not act on it; report 15436, from Mujahid, the same, and from Ibn Jurayj: the dog is cut off in its heart, it has no heart, so his likeness is that of the one who leaves guidance and has no heart; report 15437, from al-Kalbi: that is the unbeliever, astray whether you admonish him or not. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 271} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 272}
Report 15438, from Ibn Abbas: if you load wisdom on him he does not bear it, and if left he is not guided to good, like the dog that pants if it lies still and pants if driven; report 15439, from him: God gave him His signs and he left them, so God made his likeness that of the dog; report 15440, from Qatada: this is a likeness God struck for one to whom guidance was offered and who refused to accept it and left it, and al-Hasan used to say he is the hypocrite, while Qatada said this is the likeness of the unbeliever, dead of heart. The editor identifies Ibn Abd al-A'la as Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la and Ibn Thawr as Muhammad ibn Thawr, and says that "from some of them" means al-Kalbi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 272} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 273}
Others said He likened him to the dog only because he panted as the dog pants, and report 15441, from al-Suddi, says Bal'am panted as the dog pants and "you load on him" means you charge him. Al-Tabari then chooses: the better of the two interpretations is that of those who said it is a likeness for his abandoning action on God's signs that He gave him, meaning he is the same whether admonished or not in that he does not give up what he is in of opposing his Lord's command, as it is the same whether the dog is charged and driven off or left undriven in that it does not stop panting in either state. He says so from the indication of the words "that is the likeness of the people who denied Our signs", which make it a likeness for those who deny His signs; we know that panting is not in the make of every denier, and that it is a likeness God struck for them, so it is known that it is a likeness for the one described in this verse as it is for all who deny God's signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 273}
On "that is the likeness of the people who denied Our signs; so relate the story, that they may reflect", al-Tabari says: this likeness I struck for the one to whom We gave Our signs and who stripped himself of them is the likeness of the people who denied Our proofs, marks and evidences and took the way of this one who stripped himself of the signs in abandoning action on what We gave. As for "so relate the story", He says to His prophet Muhammad: relate, Muhammad, this story I have related to you, of the tidings of him to whom We gave Our signs and the reports of the nations whose reports I told you in this surah, and what befell them of My punishment when they denied My messengers, to your people of Quraysh and to the Jews of the Children of Israel before you, that they may reflect on it and take warning and turn to obedience to Us lest there befall them what befell those before them, and that the Jews of the Children of Israel may ponder it and know the truth of your affair and the soundness of your prophethood, since the tidings of the one to whom We gave Our signs belong to their hidden knowledge and concealed reports that only their rabbis know, and those of them who have read the books; and in your knowing it, unlettered as you are, neither writing nor reading nor studying books nor sitting with people of knowledge, is the clear proof for you against them that you are God's messenger and that you knew it only by revelation from heaven. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "story". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 273} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 274}
Abu al-Nadr said much the same, and report 15442, from Salim Abu al-Nadr, says of "so relate the story, that they may reflect": it means the Children of Israel, since you have brought them the report of what was among them which they conceal from you, "that they may reflect" and know that none would bring this report of what passed among them but a prophet to whom news from heaven comes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 275}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
