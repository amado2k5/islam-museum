## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains the phrase "And whatever blessing you have is from Allah. Then when harm touches you, to Him you turn to Him": The Arabs differed regarding the inclusion of the letter 'fa' in the phrase 'fa' (from Allah). Some of the Basranis said: the fa is included, because 'ma' is equivalent to 'min,' so the predicate is made with the fa. Some Kufans said: 'Ma' means recompense, and it has an implicit verb, as if you said: Whatever blessing you have is from Allah, because the reward must be a jussive verb; if it appears, it is a definite verb; if it is apparent, it is a jussive, and if not, it is implied, as the poet said: 'We do not strain an arm with reason in our wealth, even if it is patience, we know patience.' He said: He meant: If there is reason, then conceal it. In the passage, he quotes from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, and Ibn Jurayj.
