## Verse 15 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 259-261)

Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-15|3:15]]) says the meaning is: say, Muhammad, to the people for whom the love of the desires was made beautiful ([[passage/quran-3-14|3:14]]), shall I tell you of something better and more excellent than what was made lovely to you in this world, of women, sons, stored wealth and the other kinds of property that are the provision of this world. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on the verb for 'announce'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 259-260}

He then records that the grammarians differed over where the question ends, in three views. Some said it ends at the words 'than that', after which a new statement begins about what is for the God-fearing with their Lord; hence the word for the gardens is in the nominative, and those who say this allow only the nominative, since it is the predicate of a new subject and not a repetition of the word 'better', for which the genitive would be allowed; yet it also explains the 'better' that God told His prophet to ask people about, and it is raised by the particle in the phrase for those who fear. Others said much the same but held that if the particle there is made part of the announcing, both cases are allowed, the genitive as a substitute for the 'better' and the nominative if the phrase is the predicate of a new subject. Others said the question ends at 'with their Lord', and then the gardens begin, as if someone had asked 'what is for them?' or 'what is that?' and it was answered that they are gardens. In a note the editor says that the manuscript and print have after this a pointless repetition, which he judged the slip of a copyist given to many slips, and dropped from the text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 260}

Al-Tabari's own choice is the view that the question ends at what is better than that, and that what follows is a fresh statement about whom the Gardens belong to, so that its form is that of a report and it explains the 'better' he asked about, and the sentence then needs no implied pronoun. The editor refers to al-Farra, Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 195-198), and corrects a word in the manuscript and print in the verb for 'announce'. In a further note he says that the last part of the old division from which his manuscript was copied ends here, with a line closing the part and the opening invocation of the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 261}

Al-Tabari then says the word for abiding is in the accusative as a circumstantial of state, and the editor refers to his explanation under al-Baqara. He glosses the phrase for those who fear as those who feared God and obeyed Him by doing His obligations and avoiding His disobediences, and the phrase for 'with their Lord' as: for them gardens with their Lord. The gardens are orchards, which he showed earlier with citations; the rivers flowing from beneath them are rivers beneath the trees; abiding is lasting stay; and the companions are the women of paradise who were purified. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 261}

Al-Tabari adds, on the companions named in the verse, that they are the women of paradise who were purified of every harm that comes with the women of this world, of menses, seminal fluid, urine, postnatal bleeding and the like, which he says he explained earlier, so that he need not repeat it. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on the gardens, on abiding and on the companions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 261-262}

He says that God's good pleasure means God's being pleased, a verbal noun from 'God was pleased with so-and-so', with the forms 'ridwan', 'rudwan' and 'mardat'; the one with the first letter dammed is the dialect of Qays and the way Asim read it. God mentions His pleasure among what He prepared for the God-fearing, al-Tabari says, because it is the highest of the stations of honour for the people of Paradise, and he gives report 6751 from Jabir ibn Abdullah (Ibn Bashshar, Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, Sufyan, Muhammad ibn al-Munkadir): when the people of Paradise enter it, God says He will give them better than this; they ask what could be better; He says, My pleasure. The editor notes that this report does not go back to the Prophet, but that a supporting report that does is the one al-Bukhari gave from Abu Said al-Khudri, in which God addresses the people of Paradise and asks whether they are pleased; they answer that they have been given what He gave to none of His creatures; He says He will give them better, and makes His pleasure descend on them so that He is never displeased with them again. He adds that Ibn Hajar pointed to Jabir's report in al-Fath (11: 364), saying that al-Bazzar has it and Ibn Hibban graded it sound, and that he did not find its wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 262}

Of the verse's close, al-Tabari says that God sees those of His servants who fear Him, fear and obey Him, and prefer what is with Him of what He prepared for the God-fearing to the love of what was made beautiful for them in the present world, of the desires for women, sons and the rest He listed. He also sees those who do not fear Him, who disobey Him, obey Satan and prefer what was made beautiful to them in the world to the lasting bliss; He knows each party and will repay each when they return to Him, the doer of good for his good and the doer of wrong for his wrong. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on the word for All-Seeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 262-263}
