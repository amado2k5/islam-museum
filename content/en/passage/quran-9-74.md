## Summary
Verse 74 of [[passage/quran-9|Surat at-Tawba]], a Medinan surah. Scholars connect its revelation to [[event/hypocrites-ambush-at-the-pass|Hypocrites Ambush at the Pass]]. {cite:tanzil-quran} {cite:mubarakpuri-raheeq}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 74 of 129 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 74 (pp. 361-369)**

Al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over the person about whom this verse came down and the statement that God says he swears by God he did not make. Some said it came down about al-Julas ibn Suwayd ibn al-Samit. Report 16967, from Urwa, through Ibn Waki', Abu Mu'awiya and Hisham ibn Urwa: "they swear by God that they said nothing, though they said the word of unbelief", it came down about al-Julas ibn Suwayd ibn al-Samit, who said "if what Muhammad brought is true, we are worse than donkeys", and his wife's son said to him "by God, enemy of God, I shall tell the Messenger of God what you said, for if I do not I fear a calamity will strike me and I shall be held to account for your sin"; the Prophet called al-Julas and said "al-Julas, did you say such and such?", and he swore he had not said it, and God sent down the verse. The editor refers to earlier uses of the comparative "worse" and notes that the printed text has "donkeys" in another form where the manuscript is as given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 361}

Report 16968, from Urwa, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abu Mu'awiya and Hisham ibn Urwa: it came down about al-Julas ibn Suwayd ibn al-Samit; he and his wife's son Mus'ab came from Quba, and al-Julas said "if what Muhammad brought is true, we are worse than these donkeys we are riding", and Mus'ab said "by God, enemy of God, I shall tell the Messenger of God what you said"; Mus'ab said, I went to the Prophet, fearing that something would be revealed in the Qur'an or that a calamity would strike me or that I would be mixed up in his sin, and I said "Messenger of God, I came with al-Julas from Quba and he said such and such, and had I not feared being mixed up in his sin or that a calamity would strike me I would not have told you"; he called al-Julas and said "did you say what Mus'ab said?", and he swore, and God sent down the verse. The editor says the mention of the sin he took from the rest of the report and from Ibn Kathir's commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 362}

Report 16969, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: the one who said those words, as it reached me, was al-Julas ibn Suwayd ibn al-Samit, and a boy who was in his care called Umayr ibn Sa'id reported it of him, and he denied it and swore by God he had not said it; when the Qur'an came down about him he repented and gave it up and his repentance was good, as it reached me. The editor notes that the name is Sa'd in Ibn Hisham's Sira and that he left the manuscript's form since Ibn Hajar mentioned the difference in al-Isaba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 362}

Report 16970, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: "the word of unbelief", one of them said "if what Muhammad says is true, we are worse than donkeys", and a man of the believers said "what he said is true, and you are worse than a donkey", and the hypocrites then plotted to kill him, which is the sense of "they plotted what they did not attain"; reports 16971 and 16972, from Mujahid, are similar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 363}

Report 16973, from Ibn Abbas, through Ayyub ibn Ishaq, Abdallah ibn Rajaa, Isra'il, Simak and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the Messenger of God was sitting in the shade of a tree and said "a person will come to you who will look at you with the eyes of a devil, so when he comes do not speak to him"; before long a blue-eyed man appeared, and the Messenger of God called him and said "why do you revile me, you and your companions?", and the man went and brought his companions, and they swore by God that they had not said or done it, until he overlooked it, and God sent down "they swear by God they said nothing", and described them all to the end of the verse. The editor says the chain is sound, that al-Suyuti cites it in al-Durr al-Manthur with al-Tabarani and others, and that the Arabs took a blue-eyed man as an ill omen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 363}

Others said rather that it came down about Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. Report 16974, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that two men fought, one of Juhayna and one of Ghifar, Juhayna being allies of the Helpers, and the Ghifari got the better of the Juhani, and Abdallah ibn Ubayy said to the Aws "help your brother, for by God our likeness and Muhammad's is only as the saying goes, fatten your dog and he will eat you", and he said "if we return to Medina the mighty will drive out the weak", which is [[passage/quran-63-8|63:8]]; a man of the Muslims reported it to the Prophet of God, who sent for him and asked him, and he kept swearing by God that he had not said it, and God sent down "they swear by God that they said nothing, though they said the word of unbelief". Report 16975, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: it came down about Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 364}

Al-Tabari holds that the sound view is to say that God told of the hypocrites that they swear by God falsely, regarding a word of unbelief they spoke, that they did not say it; it is possible that the saying was what was reported from Urwa, that al-Julas said it, and possible that its speaker was Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and the saying what Qatada reports he said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 364}

We have no knowledge which of these it was, since there is no report of either that establishes proof and by which certain knowledge of it is reached, and it is not something known by the nature of reason; so the right is to say of it as God, glorious is His praise, said: "they swear by God that they said nothing, though they said the word of unbelief and disbelieved after their Islam". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 365}

As for "and they plotted what they did not attain", the interpreters differed over who plotted it and what it was. Some said it was a man of the hypocrites, and what he plotted was to kill his wife's son, who heard from him what he said and whom he feared would divulge it. Report 16976, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: the hypocrite plotted to kill him, meaning the believer who said to him "you are worse than a donkey", and that is God's saying "they plotted what they did not attain"; report 16977, from Mujahid, is the same, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa. The editor notes that he supplied the bracketed words at the start of the passage because the original has a gap, and that the hypocrite's name stays unstated in the reports, as is Abu Ja'far's habit in his chapter headings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 365}

Others said the one who plotted was a man of Quraysh and what he plotted was to kill the Messenger of God. Report 16978, from Mujahid, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Shibl and Jabir: a man of Quraysh plotted to kill the Messenger of God, called al-Aswad. Others said the one who plotted was Abdallah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and his unattained plot was his saying "if we return to Medina the mighty will drive out the weak", on the authority of Qatada, which we have given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 366}

As for "and they resented only that God and His Messenger had enriched them of His bounty", it is mentioned to us that the hypocrite of whom God said he spoke the word of unbelief was poor, and God enriched him through the killing of a client of his, for whom the Messenger of God gave him the blood-money; and when he said what he said God said "they resented", meaning they objected to nothing in the Messenger of God "except that God and His Messenger enriched them of His bounty". Report 16979, from Urwa, through Ibn Waki', Abu Mu'awiya and Hisham ibn Urwa: a client of al-Julas had been killed, and the Messenger of God ordered the blood-money for him and he became rich, which is the meaning of "they resented only that God enriched them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 366}

Report 16980, from Ikrima, through Ibn Uyayna and Amr: the Prophet adjudged the blood-money at twelve thousand for a client of Banu Adi ibn Ka'b, and about this the verse came down; report 16981, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: Abdallah ibn Ubayy had blood-money due and the Messenger of God brought it out for him. Report 16982, from Ikrima, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Amr: a client of Banu Adi ibn Ka'b killed a man of the Helpers and the Messenger of God adjudged the blood-money at twelve thousand, and about this the verse came down; Amr said he had not heard this, the twelve thousand, from the Prophet except from Ikrima. Report 16983, from Ibn Abbas, through Salih ibn Misman, Muhammad ibn Sinan al-Awfi, Muhammad ibn Muslim al-Ta'ifi, Amr ibn Dinar and Ikrima: the Prophet set the blood-money at twelve thousand, and that is God's saying "they resented only...", he said, by taking the blood-money. The editor notes that Abu Ja'far did not record this report in the chapter on blood-money in his commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 367}

As for "if they repent it will be better for them", the Almighty says: if these who spoke the word of unbelief repent of what they said and return from it, their return and repentance from it will be better for them than hypocrisy; "and if they turn away" means if they turn from repentance and do not come to it, persisting in their unbelief, "God will punish them with a painful punishment", an agonizing one in this world, either by being killed or by hastened disgrace for them in it, and He will punish them in the hereafter with the Fire. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 368}

As for "and they have no protector or helper in the earth", it means these hypocrites, if God punishes them in this present world, have no protector to stand by them to prevent God's punishment and no helper to support them against God and rescue them from His punishment. They had been people of might and protection through their clans and kin, by whom they protected themselves from anyone who wished them ill, and the Almighty told that those who protected them from anyone who wished them ill, from their clans and allies, will not protect them from God or help them against Him, should they need their help. It is mentioned that the one about whom this verse came down repented of the hypocrisy he was in. Report 16984, from Urwa, through Ibn Waki', Abu Mu'awiya and Hisham ibn Urwa: "if they repent it will be better for them", he said, al-Julas said "God has allowed me repentance, so I repent", and the Messenger of God accepted it from him; report 16985, from Urwa, through al-Muthanna and Ishaq: al-Julas said "Messenger of God, I see that God has allowed me repentance, so I repent", and he repented and the Messenger of God accepted it from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 369}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
