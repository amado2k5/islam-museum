**Commentary on verse 44 (pp. 117-119)**

On "they said: confused dreams, and we are not learned in the interpretation of dreams" al-Tabari says the chiefs the king asked to interpret his vision said, this vision of yours is a jumble of dreams, meaning mixtures and a false vision without reality. "Adghath" is the plural of a bundle, originally a bundle of grass, which he likens to mixed dreams that have no interpretation; "ahlam" is the plural of a dream, meaning one that is not true. He cites a verse of Ibn Muqbil describing a soft young woman whose bed seems strewn with bundles of fragrant herb on a cold north-wind morning, and another verse by someone the editor could not identify and whose wording he could not explain well. The editor says he found the verse of Ibn Muqbil nowhere but here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 117}

Report 19332, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: confused; report 19333 from him, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, uncle and father from his father: false. Report 19334, from Qatada: when the king told his companions his vision they said confused dreams, the doing of dreams; report 19335: mixtures of dreams. Reports 19336 to 19338, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad, Abu Marzuq, Juwaybir, al-Muharibi, al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: false. On "we are not learned in the interpretation of dreams" he says: we do not know what false dreams lead to. The first ba in "in the interpretation" belongs to "learned", and the one in "learned" is the ba that enters a predicate after a negative "ma"; "confused dreams" is in the nominative since the sense is: this vision is nothing, it is only confused dreams. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "interpretation". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 118}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
