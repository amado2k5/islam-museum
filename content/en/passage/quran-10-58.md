**Commentary on verse 58 (pp. 105-110)**

On [[passage/quran-10-58|10:58]] he says to His Prophet: say, Muhammad, to those who deny you and what was sent down to you, "by God's favour" people, which He bestowed on you and which is Islam, explained to you and which He called you to, "and His mercy", with which He had mercy on you and sent down to you, teaching you of His Book what you did not know and showing you by it the landmarks of your religion, and that is the Qur'an; "in that let them rejoice; it is better than what they gather", for the Islam He called them to and the Qur'an He sent down are better than the goods of this world, its wealth and treasures that they gather. The editor notes that both the printed text and the manuscript have a very faulty wording here and that he chose what seemed right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 105}

A group of interpreters said as we have said. Report 17668, from Abu Sa'id al-Khudri: God's favour is the Qur'an and His mercy is that He made you among its people; a note gives what is known of al-Tabari's teacher Ali b. al-Hasan al-Azdi. Reports 17669 to 17674 and 17681, from Hilal b. Yasaf: God's favour is Islam and His mercy the Qur'an, or Islam by which He guided you and the Qur'an by which He taught you; one adds that "better than what they gather" means gold and silver. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 106}

Reports 17675 from Qatada, 17676 from al-Hasan and 17680 from Ibn Abbas: His favour is Islam and His mercy the Qur'an. Reports 17677 and 17678, from Mujahid: the Qur'an. Report 17679, from Ibn Abbas on "better than what they gather": wealth and the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 107}

Others said that the favour is the Qur'an and the mercy is Islam. Report 17682, from Ibn Abbas: by God's favour is the Qur'an, and by His mercy when He made them people of the Qur'an; report 17683, from Zayd b. Aslam; report 17684, from al-Dahhak; and report 17685, from Ibn Zayd's father: His favour is the Qur'an and His mercy is Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 108}

The readers differed on "in that let them rejoice". Most readers of the main cities read both the verb of rejoicing and "better than what they gather" with ya, as a report about the polytheists: let these polytheists rejoice in Islam and the Qur'an to which He called them, not in the wealth they gather. Report 17686, from Abu al-Tayyah: it means the disbelievers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 108}

Ubayy b. Ka'b is reported in reports 17687 and 17688 to have read both with ta. Al-Hasan al-Basri held the same, though it is related of him that he read "better than what they gather" with ya, the first as address and the second as a report about the absent; and Abu Ja'far, the reader, read like Ubayy, with ta in both. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 109}

Al-Tabari says the correct reading is that of the readers of the main cities, both words with ya, for two reasons: the agreement of the authoritative readers on it, and its soundness in Arabic, since the Arabs hardly command the addressee with the lam and ta but say the plain imperative; he knows of no Arabic scholar who does not find the lam command of the addressee poor and a disfavoured usage, except al-Farra, who claimed the lam in the command is the basic form, whether addressing or not, but dropped for the addressee because commands are so frequent, as they drop the ta from the verb; once the ta is dropped the lam goes, and a light alif is brought in at the start of "strike" and "rejoice" because the first radical is silent and one cannot begin with a silent letter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 109}

He then answers that al-Farra's reason goes against him: if the Arabs dropped the lam in addressing and left it, no one else speaking their language may insert into it what is not theirs, and God's Book, sent down on Muhammad in their tongue, may be recited only in the most eloquent of their speech; how then with what is not known in the language of any clan or tribe of theirs, being only a claim that establishes neither proof nor soundness. In the notes the editor says the bracketed words are his addition, inferred from al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an because the copyist omitted them, and that he preferred the manuscript to the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 110}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
