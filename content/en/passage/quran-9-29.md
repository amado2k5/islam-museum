**Commentary on verse 29 (pp. 198-201)**

Al-Tabari says God addresses the believers in Him among the Messenger's Companions: fight, believers, the people who do not believe in God or the last day, that is do not affirm a Garden or a Fire, who "do not forbid what God and His messenger have forbidden" and "do not follow the religion of truth", that is do not obey God with the obedience of truth, meaning they do not obey as the people of Islam do, "of those given the Book", the Jews and Christians. Everyone who obeys a king or one in authority is "religious" toward him, as one says so-and-so obeyed so-and-so, and he cites a line of Zuhayr to the effect that if you settle at Jaww among Banu Asad, in the obedience of Amr, with Fadak lying between us; the editor tells the story behind the poem, that Zuhayr composed it about al-Harith ibn Warqa' al-Saydawi of Banu Asad, who raided Banu Abdullah ibn Ghatafan and drove off Zuhayr's camels and his herdsman Yasar, and explains that Jaww is the name of many places, Amr is Amr ibn Hind and Fadak a well-known village of the Hijaz. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 198}

The words "of those given the Book" mean those given God's book, the people of the Torah and the Gospel, "until they give the poll tax". The word for poll tax is a verbal noun of the form of "sitting", from "so-and-so repaid so-and-so what he owed him", and the sense is until they give the tribute for their necks which they hand over to the Muslims to ward off harm from them. "From the hand" means from his hand into the hand of the one he pays, and the Arabs say this of everyone who gives something to one who has power over him, willingly or unwillingly, giving it "from his hand"; it is like their sayings "I spoke to him mouth to mouth" and "I met him face to face", which the editor explains. As for "and they are humbled", it means abased and subdued, the lowly and contemptible being called so. It is reported that this verse came down on the Messenger when he was ordered to fight the Romans, and after it came down he went on the Tabuk campaign. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 200}

Report 16616, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: it came down when Muhammad and his Companions were ordered on the Tabuk expedition; report 16617 is similar. The interpreters differed over the humiliation God meant here. Some said it is that he gives it standing while the taker sits. Report 16618, from Ikrima, through Abd al-Rahman ibn Bishr al-Naysaburi, Sufyan and Abu Sa'd: you take it while you sit and he stands. Others said "from the hand" means from themselves, walking with it in their hands, unwilling, which is said to be from Ibn Abbas by a route needing scrutiny. Others said their giving of it is itself the humiliation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 201}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
