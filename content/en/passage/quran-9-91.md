**Commentary on verse 91 (pp. 419-420)**

Al-Tabari says: there is no blame on the people of disability and incapacity for travel and campaigning, nor on the sick, nor on whoever finds no provision with which to reach where he would fight, "any harm", which is sin, meaning no sin is on them if they are sincere to God and His Messenger in their absence from striving with the Messenger of God; "there is no way against those who do good" means no road lies open for whoever does good and is sincere to God and His Messenger in his staying behind from striving with the Messenger of God, for an excuse by which he is excused, by which he may be punished on that account; "and God is forgiving, merciful" means God covers the sins of the doers of good, shrouding them in His pardon of them, and is merciful to them in not punishing them for it. The editor refers to earlier explanations of the weak, the harm, the doer of good, the way, forgiveness and mercy. It is mentioned that this verse came down about Aidh ibn Amr al-Muzani, and some said about Abdallah ibn Mughaffal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 419}

Report 17078, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it came down about Aidh ibn Amr. Report 17079, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: the Messenger of God ordered the people to set out to fight with him, and a band of his Companions came to him, among them Abdallah ibn Mughaffal al-Muzani, and they said "Messenger of God, give us mounts", and the Messenger of God said to them "by God, I find nothing on which to carry you", and they turned away weeping, it being hard on them to sit out of the striving with no provision and no mount; and when God saw their eagerness for love of Him and love of His Messenger, He sent down their excuse in His book, saying "there is no blame on the weak or the sick or those who find nothing to spend", to the words "so they do not know". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 420}
