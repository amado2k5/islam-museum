## Summary
Verse 106 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 106 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "O you who believe, witness between you, when death comes to one of you, at the time of the will, two just men from among you": let two men of sound judgement and reason among the Muslims bear witness between you at the time of the will. Report 12882, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab on the verse of Surat al-Talaq, takes "just men" to mean men of reason. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 154}

The editor identifies Ubayd Allah ibn Yusuf al-Jubayri, a teacher of al-Tabari, corrects his name as written in the manuscript, and notes that al-Tabari did not give the Surat al-Talaq report where that verse is explained, one of the ways he abridged his commentary.

The interpreters differed on "just men from among you". Some said: from your own community. Report 12883, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: two witnesses from the Muslims. Report 12884, from Yahya ibn Ya'mar: from the Muslims. Report 12885, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: two of your religion. Reports 12886 to 12890, from Ubayda by chains through Ibn Sirin: from the community, or from the people of the community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 155}

Report 12891, from Mujahid, is the same. Report 12892, from Ibn Abbas: just men from the people of Islam. Report 12893, from Ibn Zayd: from the Muslims. Report 12894, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: from the people of Islam. Others said: just men from the tribe of the one making the will, a view reported from Ikrima, Ubayda and others. Al-Tabari then records the difference about the "two": some said they are witnesses to the will, others that they are executors; for the first group "witness between you" means that two witnesses bear witness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 156}

For the second group it means being present at what the sick man entrusts to them, as in "I witnessed so-and-so's will", meaning I was present. Al-Tabari chooses "from among you" as meaning from the community, not from the tribe of the testator, because God addressed the believers generally and a general statement is not narrowed to a particular except by a proof that must be accepted. He holds that the better meaning of "witness" here is the oath, not the testimony one gives for another before judges, since he knows of no ruling in which the witness must swear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 157}

The editor notes that the printed text has "from among them" where the manuscript is plainly right, and that the manuscript dropped the word "oath" in one sentence; the printed wording agrees with what al-Qurtubi reports from al-Tabari.

Al-Tabari adds that the oath required of the just men and those who take their place, in "you detain them after the prayer and they swear by God", is the clearest proof that "witness" here means oaths. He raises an objection: have you found in God's law an oath that falls on the claimant? He answers that this is found in most matters, such as a man who claims money from another who admits it but says he repaid it, where the word of the creditor stands, or a man who recognises goods in another's hand who says he bought them from the claimant or was given them; so God made the oath fall on the two claimants who found the two guilty of their breach of trust. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 158}

The editor corrects several printed readings from the manuscript, among them "the man recognises goods in another's hand" and "the two who betrayed, in what they betrayed".

Al-Tabari then records the grammarians' disagreement over what governs "witness between you" and "two just men". Some Basran grammarians said the sense is the witness of two just men, with "witness" dropped and the two put in its place, like the dropped "people" in the verse about asking the town in Surat Yusuf, and "two others" is then joined to "the two". Some Kufan grammarians said the two are governed by "witness", meaning that two Muslims or two others from outside you bear witness to you. Another of them said "witness" is governed by "when it comes", a fresh witness occurring only in this situation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 159}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that "witness" is governed by "when it comes", meaning at the coming of death, and the two are governed by the implied sense that two bear witness; he reasons that "witness" is a verbal noun and "the two" a noun, that the Arabs sometimes put nouns in the place of verbs, and that taking the speech in its soundest sense is better. He then begins "or two others from other than you": let two just Muslims or two non-Muslims bear witness between you. Some said it means two others not of your community, and report 12895, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, says: from the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 160}

The editor says the last report's number is printed here as 12890 and the sequence shows it is 12895 (inferred), and corrects the name Bishr ibn Mu'adh in the chain.

We stop here at the foot of p. 160, in the middle of the commentary on verse 106, after report 12895 from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab on "or two others from other than you".

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
