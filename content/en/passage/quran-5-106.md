## Summary
Verse 106 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 106 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Al-Tabari explains "O you who believe, witness between you, when death comes to one of you, at the time of the will, two just men from among you": let two men of sound judgement and reason among the Muslims bear witness between you at the time of the will. Report 12882, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab on the verse of Surat al-Talaq, takes "just men" to mean men of reason. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 154}

The editor identifies Ubayd Allah ibn Yusuf al-Jubayri, a teacher of al-Tabari, corrects his name as written in the manuscript, and notes that al-Tabari did not give the Surat al-Talaq report where that verse is explained, one of the ways he abridged his commentary.

The interpreters differed on "just men from among you". Some said: from your own community. Report 12883, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: two witnesses from the Muslims. Report 12884, from Yahya ibn Ya'mar: from the Muslims. Report 12885, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: two of your religion. Reports 12886 to 12890, from Ubayda by chains through Ibn Sirin: from the community, or from the people of the community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 155}

Report 12891, from Mujahid, is the same. Report 12892, from Ibn Abbas: just men from the people of Islam. Report 12893, from Ibn Zayd: from the Muslims. Report 12894, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: from the people of Islam. Others said: just men from the tribe of the one making the will, a view reported from Ikrima, Ubayda and others. Al-Tabari then records the difference about the "two": some said they are witnesses to the will, others that they are executors; for the first group "witness between you" means that two witnesses bear witness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 156}

For the second group it means being present at what the sick man entrusts to them, as in "I witnessed so-and-so's will", meaning I was present. Al-Tabari chooses "from among you" as meaning from the community, not from the tribe of the testator, because God addressed the believers generally and a general statement is not narrowed to a particular except by a proof that must be accepted. He holds that the better meaning of "witness" here is the oath, not the testimony one gives for another before judges, since he knows of no ruling in which the witness must swear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 157}

The editor notes that the printed text has "from among them" where the manuscript is plainly right, and that the manuscript dropped the word "oath" in one sentence; the printed wording agrees with what al-Qurtubi reports from al-Tabari.

Al-Tabari adds that the oath required of the just men and those who take their place, in "you detain them after the prayer and they swear by God", is the clearest proof that "witness" here means oaths. He raises an objection: have you found in God's law an oath that falls on the claimant? He answers that this is found in most matters, such as a man who claims money from another who admits it but says he repaid it, where the word of the creditor stands, or a man who recognises goods in another's hand who says he bought them from the claimant or was given them; so God made the oath fall on the two claimants who found the two guilty of their breach of trust. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 158}

The editor corrects several printed readings from the manuscript, among them "the man recognises goods in another's hand" and "the two who betrayed, in what they betrayed".

Al-Tabari then records the grammarians' disagreement over what governs "witness between you" and "two just men". Some Basran grammarians said the sense is the witness of two just men, with "witness" dropped and the two put in its place, like the dropped "people" in the verse about asking the town in Surat Yusuf, and "two others" is then joined to "the two". Some Kufan grammarians said the two are governed by "witness", meaning that two Muslims or two others from outside you bear witness to you. Another of them said "witness" is governed by "when it comes", a fresh witness occurring only in this situation. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 159}

Al-Tabari prefers the view that "witness" is governed by "when it comes", meaning at the coming of death, and the two are governed by the implied sense that two bear witness; he reasons that "witness" is a verbal noun and "the two" a noun, that the Arabs sometimes put nouns in the place of verbs, and that taking the speech in its soundest sense is better. He then begins "or two others from other than you": let two just Muslims or two non-Muslims bear witness between you. Some said it means two others not of your community, and report 12895, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, says: from the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 160}

The editor says the last report's number is printed here as 12890 and the sequence shows it is 12895 (inferred), and corrects the name Bishr ibn Mu'adh in the chain.

Reports 12896, 12897 and 12898, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: "or two others from other than you" means from the People of the Book. Report 12899, from Ibrahim and Sulayman al-Taymi, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: from other than your community. Report 12900, from someone who heard Sa'id ibn Jubayr, is the same; 12901, from Abu Mijlaz: from other than your community; 12902, from Ibrahim, the same. Report 12903, from Ibrahim: if there is a Muslim nearby, call him to witness; if not, call two of the polytheists. Report 12904, from Ibrahim and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: from other than your community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 161}

The editor notes that Abu Hafs al-Jubayri is Ubayd Allah ibn Yusuf, met in report 12882.

Reports 12905 to 12907, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: from the People of the Book. Report 12908, from Yahya ibn Ya'mar: "two just men from among you" means from the Muslims, and if you find none among the Muslims, then from the non-Muslims. Report 12909, from Shurayh: when a man is in a foreign land and finds no Muslim to witness his will, and he calls a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian, their testimony is valid; but if two Muslims come and testify to the contrary, the Muslims' testimony is accepted and the other pair's is voided. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 162}

The editor identifies Abu Qutayba Salm ibn Qutayba al-Sha'iri and corrects his by-name from the manuscript, identifies Muhammad ibn Sawa al-Sadusi, a truthful man whose name the publisher misread, keeps the manuscript's "their testimony" and corrects the name of al-Tabari's teacher in the chain of 12909.

Report 12910, from Shurayh: he did not accept the testimony of Jews and Christians against a Muslim except in a will, and not in a will except on a journey. Reports 12911 and 12912, from him, are similar. Report 12913, from Ibrahim: Hisham ibn Hubayra wrote to Maslama about the testimony of polytheists against Muslims, and he wrote back: it is not allowed except in a will, and not in a will unless the man is travelling. Reports 12914 to 12916, from Ubayda: from other than the community. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 163}

Report 12917, from Ubayda: from other than the people of the prayer; 12918 to 12920: from other than your religion or community. Report 12921, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: from other than your community. Reports 12922 and 12923, from Mujahid, are the same. Report 12924, from Ibn Abbas: from other than the people of Islam. Report 12925, from Abu Ishaq: from the Jews and Christians; and Shurayh said: the testimony of a Jew or Christian is not allowed except in a will, and not in a will except on a journey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 164}

The editor says that report 12921 breaks off in the manuscript at the word "from", the copyist marking doubt in the margin, and that the printed text supplied the bracketed words, which are right in sense.

Report 12926, from al-Sha'bi: a Muslim man was dying at Daquqa and found no Muslim to witness his will, so he called two men of the People of the Book; they came to Kufa and told Abu Musa al-Ash'ari of his estate and his will, and he said: this is a matter that has not occurred since the time of the messenger of God! He made them swear and carried out their testimony. Report 12927, from al-Sha'bi: Abu Musa judged accordingly at Daquqa. Report 12928, from Muhammad ibn Sirin: two witnesses, of the Muslims and of the non-Muslims. Report 12929, from Ibn Zayd: from other than the people of Islam. Report 12930, from Mujahid, is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 165}

The editor identifies Daquqa as a town between Irbil and Baghdad, where a battle of the Kharijites took place, keeps the manuscript's mention of it, and notes that Abu Dawud recorded the report in his Sunan.

Report 12931, from Zayd ibn Aslam on the whole verse: it concerned a man who died with no Muslim present, in the early days of Islam when the land was at war and the people were unbelievers except the messenger and his companions in Medina; people then inherited by will, and the will was abrogated, the fixed shares were imposed and the Muslims acted by them. Al-Tabari then gives another view: "or two others" means from outside your tribe and clan. Report 12932, from al-Hasan: two witnesses of your people and of other than your people. Report 12933, from al-Zuhri: the sunna is that the testimony of an unbeliever is not allowed at home or on a journey; it is among Muslims only. Report 12934, from al-Hasan: "two just men from among you" means from his clan and "two others" from outside his clan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 166}

The editor corrects the name Abd Allah ibn Ayyash al-Qitbani of Egypt, and identifies the trustworthy Uthman ibn al-Haytham al-Ashajj and Salih ibn Abi al-Akhdar al-Yamami, al-Zuhri's servant.

Reports 12935 to 12937, from Ikrima: from other than your tribe, meaning from the Muslims. Report 12938, from al-Hasan: from other than your clan and your people, all of them Muslims. Report 12939, from Ubayda: Muslims from outside your tribe. Report 12940, from Aqil: I asked Ibn Shihab about the verse up to its words on the transgressors, saying: do you think the two whom God mentioned, from outside the testator's people, are Muslims or from the People of the Book? And the other two who take their place, are they from outside the testator's people or from... {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 167}

The editor says the bracketed word in report 12940 is necessary and that the copyist marked doubt in the margin.

The rest of 12940: Ibn Shihab said: we have heard no sunna from the messenger of God or from the imams of the community on this verse that I can cite; we discussed it with our scholars and they did not mention a known sunna or a ruling of a just imam, but their opinions differ. The view we liked best was theirs who said it concerns the heirs among the Muslims: some of them are present with the dying man whom they inherit from and some are absent, and those present witness what he bequeathed to relatives and tell the absent what they heard; if the absent accept, the will stands, and if they suspect that the others changed the dead man's words and favoured whom they wished, the two witnesses swear after the Muslims' prayer; once they swear, their testimony and oaths stand unless it is found that they were guilty of a sin, in which case two others of the heirs, who deny what the first two testified, take their place and swear that their testimony is truer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 168}

Al-Tabari then states his choice: the sounder of the two interpretations is that of those who said "or two others" means from outside the people of Islam, because God taught his believing servants at the time of a will the testimony of two just believers or of two non-believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 169}

On p. 168 the editor notes that the sentence in brackets is not in the manuscript and that he supplied it from al-Tabari's context and the verse, and that the added phrase "truer than your testimony" is necessary; on p. 169 he corrects "meaning" where the printed text has another word, and says the bracketed "other than" is necessary or the sentence is spoiled.

His reasoning is that it makes no sense in speech to describe the testimony as of believers from among you or of two men from outside your clan; one says rather two men of your clan or outside your clan, or two of the believers or of the non-believers; so the words are taken only in their best sense. He has already shown that "just men from among you" means men of your religion and community, so "two others from other than you" means from outside your religion, whether they are Jews, Christians, Zoroastrians, idol worshippers or of any religion, because God did not single out one community once they are not of the people of Islam. He then begins on "if you are travelling in the land and the calamity of death strikes you": if you travel, going and returning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 169}

Al-Tabari explains "the calamity of death strikes you" as death coming upon you. Most interpreters took this passage as a sequence, not a choice: two just men from among you if they can be found, and if not, two others from outside, because they understood "witness" as the testimony a witness gives before the judge. Report 12941, from Yahya ibn Ya'mar: from the Muslims, and if you find none, from the non-Muslims. Report 12942, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: two of your religion, and the two others from the People of the Book when he is in a land where he finds none other. Report 12943, from Shurayh: whoever calls a Jew, Christian or Zoroastrian when abroad and finds no Muslim, their testimony is valid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 170}

Report 12944, from al-Suddi: "two just men from among you" is at home and "two others" on a journey; a man whom death overtakes on a journey with no Muslim present calls two Jews, Christians or Zoroastrians and leaves his will with them. Report 12945, from Ibrahim and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when death comes to a man on a journey he calls two Muslims, and if he finds none, two of the People of the Book. Report 12946, from Ibn Abbas: the first is for one who dies with Muslims by him, whom God ordered to call two just Muslims; the second for one who dies with no Muslim near, whom God ordered to call two non-Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 171}

On 12944 the editor keeps the manuscript reading, without the extra word in the printed text.

Others took it as a choice, and said "witness" here means oaths on the will and the dead man's trusting the two with money to deliver to his heirs, if they are suspected; a man may trust with his property one he thinks fit for trust, believer or unbeliever, at home or abroad. Al-Tabari then begins "you detain them after the prayer and they swear by God, if you doubt, we will not sell it for a price, even if he were a relative": if two just men testify or you leave your will, on a journey, with two others and give them your goods, and death strikes you and the heirs claim they betrayed the trust, the ruling is that you detain them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 172}

The editor corrects the printed reading of the verb for entrusting, preferring the manuscript, and keeps the forms he gives.

Al-Tabari supplies an omitted clause: the calamity of death strikes you after you entrusted your will and goods to them, so you detain them after the prayer, and "they swear by God, if you doubt" means if you suspect them of betrayal in changing or altering a will, doubt being suspicion; "we will not sell it for a price" means we will not swear falsely for a gain, a sum we would take or a right we would deny these people; the "it" refers to God in the sense of the oath. "Even if he were a relative": we seek no gain by our oaths so as to lie for anyone, even a relative. This is reported from Ibn Abbas. Report 12947, from him: this is for one who died with no Muslim, whom God ordered to call two non-Muslims; if their testimony is doubted they are made to swear after the prayer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 173}

The editor keeps the manuscript wording in two places where the printed text altered it.

They differed on the prayer meant: some said the afternoon prayer. Report 12948, from al-Sha'bi, in the story of the man who died at Daquqa and Abu Musa's ruling: he made them swear after the afternoon prayer that they had not betrayed, lied, changed, concealed or altered, and that it was the man's will and estate, and he carried out their testimony. Reports 12949 and 12950, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and Ibrahim: if a man is in a land of polytheism and leaves his will to two of the People of the Book, they swear after the afternoon prayer. Report 12951, from Qatada, makes the same point, and its text runs on to p. 175. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 174}

The rest of 12951, from Qatada: this is a man who died in a foreign land, left his estate and made his will, with two witnesses to it; if their testimony is doubted they swear after the afternoon prayer, and it was said that oaths become binding at that time. Report 12952, from Ibrahim and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: when death comes to a man on a journey he should call two Muslims as witnesses, and if he finds none, two of the People of the Book; when they bring his estate, if the heirs believe them their word is accepted, and if they suspect them they swear after the afternoon prayer by God that they did not lie, conceal, betray or change. Report 12953, from Amir al-Sha'bi: a man died at Daquqa and found no one to witness his will but two Christians of the town; Abu Musa made them swear right after the afternoon prayer in the mosque of Kufa, by God, that they had neither concealed nor changed anything and that this was the will, and he allowed it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 175}

On 12953 the editor refers back to his note at report 12948.

Others said: they swear after the prayer of their own religion and community. Report 12954, from al-Suddi, on "two just men from among you": this concerns the will at the approach of death; he makes his will and has two Muslims witness what is his and what he owes, and this is at home, while "two others from other than you" is on a journey. A man whom death overtakes on a journey with no Muslim by him calls two Jews, Christians or Zoroastrians, leaves his will with them and hands over his estate, and they take it; if the dead man's family accept the will and recognise their kinsman's property they leave the two alone, and if they doubt they take them to the ruler. That is the meaning of "you detain them after the prayer, if you doubt". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 176}

The same report has Ibn Abbas say that he seemed to see the two foreigners as they were brought to Abu Musa al-Ash'ari in his house; he opened the document, the dead man's family denied it and called them traitors, and Abu Musa wished to make them swear after the afternoon prayer. Ibn Abbas told him that the two do not care for the afternoon prayer and that he should make them swear after their own prayer in their own religion. So the two are stood after praying in their religion and swear by God that they will not sell it for a small price even for a relative, nor conceal God's testimony, and that their companion made this very will and this is his estate. Before the oath the imam tells them that if they have concealed or betrayed he will shame them before their people, their testimony will not pass and he will punish them, which makes it likelier that they give the testimony properly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 176}

The editor explains "ilj" as a man among the non-Arab unbelievers.

Al-Tabari then chooses: the sounder of the two views is that of those who said "you detain them after the afternoon prayer". God made "the prayer" definite here with the article, and the Arabs use it only for what is known, either a kind or one particular thing known to the speakers. It is agreed that all the prayers are not meant, and it cannot mean the prayer of the Jew or Christian being sworn, because they have several prayers and no one of them would be known as the one meant; so it is a particular prayer of the Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 176}

Since it is sound that the Prophet held the mutual oath between the two Ajlanis after the afternoon prayer and no other, the prayer meant is the one he chose for swearing someone whose oath he wished to make weightier; and the unbelievers also honour that hour because it is near sunset. Report 12955, from Ibn Zayd, on "we will not sell it for a price": we will not take a bribe for it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 177}

For the story of the two Ajlanis the editor refers to al-Bayhaqi's al-Sunan al-Kubra, vol. 7, p. 398 and after.

Al-Tabari next takes up "nor will we conceal the testimony of God; then we would be among the sinners". The readers differed. Most readers of the cities read "testimony" in annexation to the name of God, with God in the genitive, meaning we will not conceal a testimony that we owe to God. Report 12956, from al-Sha'bi, as Ibn Waki' related it, has him read it with the alif of God's name cut, the name still in the genitive. Al-Sha'bi seems to have taken it as: the two swear by God that they will not sell it for a price nor conceal testimony they hold, and then begin a new oath in question form, "by God, if the two sold it for a price or concealed his testimony, are they among the sinners?" {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 177}

A different report is given from al-Sha'bi on the reading: 12957, from Abbad ibn Abbad through Ibn Awn, has "testimony" with tanwin and God in the genitive, joined; Abu Ubayd said that some related it with the alif cut as a question. Al-Tabari says that what he himself remembers of al-Sha'bi's reading is without the question. Some read it with tanwin on "testimony" and God in the accusative, meaning we will not conceal from God a testimony we hold. Al-Tabari prefers the reading with "testimony" annexed to God and God in the genitive, because it is the widespread reading of the readers of the cities, whose soundness the community does not dispute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 178}

On p. 178 the editor notes that the printed text has a wrong form for al-Sha'bi's reading, the right one being in the manuscript, and that al-Sha'bi's readings are cited in Abu Hayyan's commentary and Ibn Jinni's al-Muhtasib. He corrects the name Ahmad ibn Yusuf al-Taghlibi, printed "al-Tha'labi", identifies Abbad ibn Abbad al-Arsufi, Abu Utba al-Khawwas, among Ibn Awn's transmitters, and corrects a muddled sentence of the printed text from the manuscript.

Ibn Zayd used to say the meaning is: we will not conceal God's testimony even if it is distant; this is 12958. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 179}

The editor keeps the manuscript reading, says he is in doubt about Ibn Zayd's wording in any case and fears that something fell out of it, and could not find his statement in the books before him.

Verse 106 closes here at p. 179, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-5-107|5:107]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
