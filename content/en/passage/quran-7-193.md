**Commentary on verse 193 (pp. 320-321)**

Al-Tabari says, in describing and faulting what these polytheists associate in their worship of their Lord: it is of their description that, people, if you call them to the straight road and the sound, right course they will not follow you, because they have no reason, to leave the ways that swerve and stray from the middle course and take what is straight and right. God described their gods so only to warn them of the greatness of their error and the ugliness of their choice, saying: how will one guide you to right guidance who, if called to it and told of it, does not know it and does not tell guidance from error, so that for him the call of one calling him to guidance and his silence are the same, since he does not understand his call, hear his voice or grasp what is said to him? How is one of that description worshipped, and how can the greatness of the ignorance of one who takes such a thing as a god be obscure? The Lord who is worshipped is the one who benefits whoever worships Him, harms whoever disobeys Him, helps His friend, abandons His enemy, guides to right guidance whoever obeys Him and hears the call of whoever calls Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 320}

It is said "it is the same for you whether you call them or are silent", where "silent", a noun, is joined to "you called them", a past verb, and it did not say "or you were silent", as the poet said in a line saying it is the same to him whether he leaves Mina or spends a night among the people of the domed tents of Numayr ibn Amir. The editor notes that the line is in al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an, that the manuscript and printed edition read "poverty" in error and the right word is "departure", meaning the departure from Mina in the days of the pilgrimage, that he did not know the poet, and he refers to Sibawayh; the line is sometimes recited with "or you are one who spends the night". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 321}
