## Summary
Verse 33 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 33 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 33: "the recompense of those who wage war on God and His messenger and strive to spread corruption", pp. 243-247**

Al-Tabari says this explains the ruling on corruption in the land mentioned in the previous verse; God informs His servants of the penalty the corrupter deserves: nothing for him in this world but being killed, crucified, having hand and foot cut on opposite sides, or banishment from the land, as disgrace for them, and in the hereafter, if he does not repent in this world, a great punishment. The interpreters differed over whom it was revealed about. Some said it was about people of the earlier scripture who were under a truce with the Prophet, broke the covenant and spread corruption, so God told His prophet the ruling about them.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 243}

Reports 11803, 11804 and 11805, from Ibn 'Abbas and al-Dahhak, say a people had a covenant with the Prophet, broke it, cut the roads and spread corruption, and God gave His prophet the choice: if he wished he could kill, crucify, or cut their hands and feet on opposite sides.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 244}

Others said it was revealed about polytheists. Report 11806, from 'Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, says that whoever of them repented before being overcome had no case against him, and that this verse does not protect a Muslim from the prescribed penalty: if he killed, corrupted or fought and then joined the unbelievers before being caught, that does not prevent the penalty for what he did. Report 11807, from al-Hasan, says it was revealed about polytheists. The editor traces report 11806 to the Sunan of al-Nasa'i and Abu Dawud.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 244}

Others said it was revealed about men of 'Urayna and 'Ukl who left Islam and fought God and His messenger. Reports 11808 and 11809, from Anas ibn Malik, say a group of 'Ukl and 'Urayna came to the Prophet saying they were herders, not farmers, and found Medina unhealthy; he ordered them a herd and a herdsman and that they go out and drink its milk and urine, but they killed the Prophet's herdsman, drove off the herd and disbelieved after having accepted Islam. They were brought, and he cut their hands and feet, blinded their eyes and left them in the lava field until they died, and it was said this verse was revealed about them. The editor explains that this is a sound report in Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i, and explains its words.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 245}

Report 11810, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, says some people came to the Prophet and pledged to him on Islam, lying, then said they found Medina unwholesome; he said these milch camels go out to you morning and evening, drink their milk and urine. While so, a cry for help came that they had killed the herdsman and driven off the camels; the call went out "riders of God, mount", and they rode, the Prophet after them, pursuing them until they brought them into their safe place, and the Companions returned with some captured. God revealed the verse. Their banishment was driving them into their own land, out of the Muslims' territory; the Prophet killed some, crucified, cut and blinded, and never mutilated before or after, forbidding mutilation, though Anas said he burned them after killing them. The editor notes a narrator's weakness.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 246}

Some say they were people of Banu Sulaym, 'Urayna and Bajila. Report 11811, from Jarir, says men of 'Urayna came to the Prophet barefoot and ill and he had them cared for; when they recovered and were strong they killed the herders of the camels and left with them for their people's land. The Prophet sent Jarir with a group who caught up with them as they neared their lands, brought them back, and he cut their hands and feet on opposite sides and blinded them; they kept asking for water and he said fire, until they died. God disliked the blinding and revealed this verse.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 247}

In his notes on the narrators of report 11811 the editor says the report from Jarir is very weak and cannot be right, since Jarir came to the Prophet in the year he died while the 'Urayna affair was in the year six, with Kurz ibn Jabir al-Fihri as commander according to al-Waqidi; he quotes Ibn Kathir that it is an odd report with the weak al-Rabadhi in its chain, and that the statement that God disliked blinding is rejected, since Muslim's Sahih has them blinding the herdsmen's eyes, so what was done to them was retaliation.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 248}

Report 11812, from 'Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, says people of 'Urayna raided the Prophet's milch camels, drove them off and killed a boy of his among them; he sent in pursuit of them and they were caught and he cut their hands and feet and blinded them. The editor says the report is sound apart from the weak Ibn Sam'an, and that al-Nasa'i gave it without him. Report 11813, from 'Abdallah ibn 'Umar, from the Prophet, gives the same and that the verse on warring was revealed about them. Report 11814, from Anas, says eight men of 'Ukl came to the Prophet, accepted Islam and then found Medina unhealthy, so he told them to go to the charity camels and drink their milk and urine; they did, killed the herdsmen and drove off the camels, and he sent trackers after them, they were brought, he cut their hands and feet and left them unsealed until they died.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 249}

Report 11815, from Anas, says there were four of 'Urayna and three of 'Ukl, whose hands and feet he cut and eyes he blinded, not sealing them, leaving them to put stones in their mouths from thirst in the lava field; God revealed the verse. Report 11816, from Yazid ibn Abi Habib, says 'Abd al-Malik ibn Marwan wrote to Anas asking about the verse, and Anas replied that it was revealed about those men of 'Urayna, of Bajila, who left Islam, killed the herdsman, drove off the camels, terrorised the road and committed forbidden sexual acts. The editor traces the report in Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim, Abu Dawud and al-Nasa'i.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 250}

Report 11817, from al-Suddi, says it was revealed about the black men of 'Urayna who came to the Prophet with yellow water disease and complained, and he told them to go to the charity camels and drink their milk and urine; when they recovered they killed the herdsmen and drove off the camels.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 251}

Al-Tabari then gives his choice: the soundest view is that God sent down this verse to His prophet to make known the ruling on one who wages war on God and His messenger and strives to spread corruption, after what the Prophet did to the 'Urayna men; for the stories God told before and after this verse are those of the Children of Israel, so it is more fitting that this be set among them as teaching the ruling for them and their likes; and he says it came after the 'Urayna affair because the reports from the Companions agree. So the sense is: for that reason We decreed for the Children of Israel that whoever kills a soul or strives for corruption in the land is as if he had killed all people; and whoever of them does so, Muhammad, his recompense is that they be killed, crucified, have hand and foot cut on opposite sides, or be banished from the land.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 251}

He raises an objection: how can the verse have been sent down about a disbelieving Israelite breaking his covenant while its ruling is for Muslims and not for polytheists at war? He answers that the ruling for one who wages war on God and His messenger and spreads corruption is one for people of our protection and of our community, that those meant were people of covenant and protection though every protected person and Muslim enters its ruling, and that others' entering does not invalidate its coming down about those it came down for.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 252}

Scholars differed over whether the Prophet's ruling on the 'Urayna men was abrogated. Some said it was abrogated by his prohibition of mutilation, through this verse, and that it came as a rebuke for what he did to them. Others said what he did is a standing ruling for their like that was never abrogated, the verse being God's ruling on whoever wages war by banditry, and that the 'Urayna men left Islam, killed, stole and fought God and His messenger, so their ruling differs from the Muslim or protected-person bandit. The editor explains the word for banditry.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 252}

Others said the Prophet did not blind the 'Urayna men but intended to, and God sent down the verse teaching him the ruling and forbidding it. Report 11818 has al-Walid ibn Muslim discussing with al-Layth ibn Sa'd the blinding and leaving them unsealed, and al-Layth saying he heard Muhammad ibn 'Ajlan say the verse came as a rebuke for that and taught him the penalty for the like, cutting, killing and banishing, and that he blinded none after them; al-Awza'i denied it came as a rebuke, saying that was the penalty of those men in particular and the verse then gave the penalty for others who fought after, lifting blinding from them. Report 11819, from al-Suddi, says he wished to blind them and God forbade it and commanded him to apply the prescribed penalties as revealed. The editor points to al-Nahhas's book on abrogation for the dispute.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 253}

Scholars also differed over who deserves the name of one who wages war on God and His messenger and comes under this ruling. Some said it is the robber who cuts the road, as report 11820 gives from Qatada and 'Ata' al-Khurasani. Others said it is the open robber who overpowers in the city and elsewhere, among them al-Awza'i, and report 11821 gives it from him and from Malik, al-Layth ibn Sa'd and Ibn Lahi'a.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 254}

Report 11822 has al-Walid ibn Muslim asking Malik ibn Anas whether warring can occur within a town, and he said yes: for us the one who wages war is whoever bears arms against Muslims in a town or the open country without a prior feud, cutting the road and homes and terrifying them with his weapon; if he kills one, the ruler kills him as a warrior, with no pardon or retaliation for the slain person's guardian. Report 11823 has al-Layth ibn Sa'd and Ibn Lahi'a saying warring occurs in the houses of a town, city or village when they enter on people with swords openly or at night with fires; if they kill they are killed, and if they took money without killing they are cut on opposite sides, since one who fights Muslims in the open is no worse than one who fights them in their homes. Report 11824, from al-Awza'i, says warring can occur in a town by drawing arms on its people by night or day, and that Malik counts killing by deception as warring: a man deceives another or a boy, takes him into a house and kills him for his property, so the ruler is the one to execute and the guardian has no retaliation; this is al-Shafi'i's view too, as report 11825 relays.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 255}

Others said the warrior is the highway robber, while the open robber in towns is not the warrior who bears the ruling on warriors; among them Abu Hanifa and his companions. Report 11826, from Dawud ibn Abi Hind, says they discussed the warrior at Ibn Hubayra's with people of Basra and agreed that the warrior is one outside the town. Reports 11827 and 11828, from Mujahid, say corruption is adultery, theft, killing people and destroying crops and offspring.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 256}

Al-Tabari chooses that one who wages war on God and His messenger is whoever fights on the roads of the Muslims and their protected people, or raids them in their towns and villages by banditry, since there is no dispute that whoever sets up unjust war on Muslims is a warrior against them, and it is the same whether he does it in their town or on their roads: he wages war on God and His messenger by fighting those they forbade him to fight. He explains "strive to spread corruption" as working disobedience in God's land by terrorising the roads of His believing servants or of their protected people, cutting their roads, taking their property unjustly and aggressively, and violating their sanctities wickedly.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 256}

**Commentary on verse 33, continued: "that they be killed or crucified or have their hands and feet cut on opposite sides or be banished from the land", pp. 257-261**

Al-Tabari says that for one of the people of Islam or its protected who wages war on God and His messenger and spreads corruption, there is only some of these measures. The interpreters then differed over whether all of them apply to him by deserving the name or whether what applies depends on his offence, one group saying the penalty is according to the offence, differing with offences. The editor says the heading of this view dropped out here and he reconstructed it from the context.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 257}

Report 11829, from Ibn 'Abbas, says that if he fought and killed, the penalty is death if caught before repenting; if he fought, took property and killed, crucifixion; if he fought and took without killing, cutting hand and foot on opposite sides; if he fought and terrorised the road, banishment. Reports 11830, 11831 and 11832, from Ibrahim and Abu Mijlaz, say similar: terrorising the road and taking property brings cutting on opposite sides, killing brings crucifixion or death, and one who only corrupts is banished. Report 11833, from al-Hasan, says terrorising the road without killing or taking property brings banishment, and report 11834, from Husayn, that taking property without killing brings cutting and killing with taking brings crucifixion.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 258}

Report 11835, from Qatada, says these are four prescribed penalties God sent down: whoever took life and property is crucified, whoever took life and left property is killed, whoever took property and left life is cut, and whoever took nothing is banished. Report 11836, from al-Suddi, says God forbade His prophet to blind the 'Urayna men who raided his camels and told him to apply the penalties as sent down: he cut hand and foot on opposite sides, right hand and left foot, of one who took property without killing, killed one who killed without taking, and crucified one who took property and killed; likewise for anyone who terrorises the Muslims' road. Report 11837, from 'Atiyya al-'Awfi, says banishment by the sword means pursuing him until he leaves the land, and if he took property his hand is for the property and his foot for terrorising the Muslims.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 259}

Reports 11838 to 11842, from 'Ata' al-Khurasani, Qatada, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Rabi', Muwarriq al-'Ijli and Ibn 'Abbas, say much the same: killing with taking property is crucifixion, killing without property is death, taking property without killing is cutting on opposite sides, and terrorising without any of these is banishment. Report 11840, from al-Rabi', adds others who fought and withdrew without more and were expelled from the land. Report 11842, from Ibn 'Abbas, has the one who killed and took property cut on opposite sides and then crucified.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 260}

Report 11843, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, says that one who terrorised Muslims and seized property without shedding blood is cut; if he shed blood, he is killed and crucified; if he did both, cut, then killed, then crucified, as if crucifixion were a deterrent and cutting like the verse on the thief and killing like the verse on a life for a life; if he refuses, it is the ruler's and the Muslims' duty to seek him until they apply God's book, banishment being from Islamic land to the land of unbelief. Al-Tabari notes that those who said this argued that God made retaliation due on the killer and cutting on the thief, and by the Prophet's saying that a Muslim's blood is lawful only for one of three: one who killed and is killed, one who committed adultery after marriage and is stoned, and one who left Islam; so killing him for terrorising the road without having killed or taken property would be going ahead of God and His messenger in contradicting them in ruling.  {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 261}

We stop here at the foot of p. 261, in the argument of those who hold that the penalty follows the offence; the rest of verse 33 follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
