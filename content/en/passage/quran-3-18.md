## Verse 18 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 267-268)

Al-Tabari ([[passage/quran-3-18|3:18]]) says the sense is that God bore witness to His oneness, and the angels and the people of knowledge also bore witness; the word for the angels is joined to the name of God, and the first particle takes the opening vowel because the witnessing governs it. He then notes that some Basrans took the verb to mean that God decreed, and read the angels as raised, in the sense that the angels and the people of knowledge are witnesses. The editor says this is the view of Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 89), whom al-Tabari does not name, saying only 'some of the Basrans', and refers to al-Tabari's reply on p. 272. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 267}

He says the reciters of the Muslims read the first particle with the opening vowel, as governed by the witnessing, and the second, which opens the next verse, with the other vowel as a fresh start. One later grammarian (al-Kisa'i, the editor says, referring to al-Farra, 1: 200, and al-Qurtubi, 4: 42-43) read both with the opening vowel, in the sense that God witnessed His oneness and that the religion with Him is Islam, joining the second to the first and dropping the joining letter, which is understood. He argued that Ibn Abbas read the first with the other vowel and the second with the opening one, making the first a parenthesis that the witnessing does not govern, and that Ibn Mas'ud read the first with the opening vowel and the second with the other one, and said he read both with the opening vowel to combine the readings of the two. Al-Tabari says he thereby differed from all the reciters of Islam, early and late, claiming an interpretation from Ibn Abbas and Ibn Mas'ud that they said and read, which is not known by any sound or weak report, and it is enough to show the error of his reading that it departs from the reading of the people of Islam. He then begins to state that the right reading is the opening vowel on the first and the other on the second. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 268}

Al-Tabari then completes his argument: the right reading is the opening vowel on the first particle and the other on the second, as a fresh start, and this is better than giving both the opening vowel. He gives report 6760 from al-Suddi (Musa, Amr, Asbat): God bears witness, He and the angels and the scholars among people, that the religion with Him is Islam. Al-Tabari says this interpretation shows that the witnessing governs the second particle, so on it two readings of the first are possible: one, that it takes the opening vowel as giving a reason, so that the sense is that God witnessed that the religion with Him is Islam because He is One (grammarians count that vowel as the genitive in some views and the accusative in others); the other, that it takes the other vowel as a parenthesis, the witnessing falling on the second, so that the sense is that God witnessed, for He is One, and the angels, that the religion with Him is Islam, as someone says 'I testify, for I am right, that you are clear of what you are blamed for'. The editor notes that the printed text and the manuscript differ in a few words of report 6760 and of al-Tabari's sentence, that he kept what he judged right and dropped a superfluous letter, and refers to al-Farra (1: 200). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 268-270}

Al-Tabari then speaks of the word by which God describes Himself in the verse as upholding justice between His creatures. It means that He is the one who takes charge of justice among them; the word for justice is from the saying 'he acted justly'; and its accusative is 'by cutting off', that is, as a circumstantial. Some grammarians of Basra claimed it is a circumstantial of the pronoun in the clause of oneness; some of Kufa claimed it is a circumstantial of the name of God with the first verb, so the sense is that God, who upholds justice, witnessed to His oneness, and it is said that it is so in Ibn Mas'ud's reading, which has the word after the people of knowledge in the nominative, the definite article then being dropped so that it became indefinite, an attribute of a definite and so accusative. Al-Tabari's own choice is the view that makes it a 'cutting off' as an attribute of God, since the angels and the people of knowledge are joined to Him, so it is right that the word be a circumstantial of Him. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on the word for justice (p. 77), explains the term for 'cutting off' as the circumstantial, quoting al-Farra (1: 200) that it is an indefinite used as an attribute of a definite and cut off from the case of the attribute, and says he found no explanation of the term in any book at hand and thinks it a Kufan term. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 270-271}

He then explains the verse's close. The first clause denies that anything deserves worship other than the One who has no partner in His kingdom (the editor notes that the word for worship is a verbal noun of the verb on the pattern 'sharufa', meaning submission and abasement, and refers to Ibn al-Qatta' in Kitab al-af'al, 2: 337). The Mighty is the one from whom nothing He wills is withheld and who is not defeated by anyone He punishes or takes vengeance on; the Wise is wise in His ordering, so that no flaw enters it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 271}

Al-Tabari says God meant by this verse to deny what the Christians who disputed with the Messenger about Jesus ascribed to Him, namely sonship, and what the other polytheists ascribed, that He has a partner, and their taking lords besides Him. He told them of Himself that He is the creator of everything besides Him and the Lord of everything that any unbeliever or polytheist took as a lord beside Him, and that He, His angels and the people of knowledge among His creation bear witness to it. He began with Himself to magnify and exalt Himself above what the polytheists ascribed to Him, as He set His servants the custom of beginning their affairs with His mention before anyone else's, teaching them manners. The aim is to report the testimony of those He approved among His creation, who sanctified Him, His angels and the scholars among His servants; He informed them that His angels, whom those who worship others besides Him revere and many of them worship, and the people of knowledge among them, reject what they persist in of their unbelief and their words about Jesus and the words of whoever took a lord besides Him among all creatures. So the angels and the people of knowledge testified to His oneness and that whoever takes a lord besides God is lying, as His argument to His prophet against the Najran delegation who disputed with him about Jesus. The mention of God and His attribute is thrown in, as in al-Anfal ([[passage/quran-8-41|8:41]]), as an opening of the speech by His name; so here too the testimony opens with His name and His praise of Himself, with the denial of divinity to anyone else and the belying of the polytheists. The editor notes that the manuscript and print have a word which he suspects a copyist's slip for 'they sanctified Him', given the sense of exalting; that in al-Anfal the mention of God's name opens the speech, and that al-Tabari preferred there that the sense is that a fifth is for the Messenger; and that in the sentence about the angels and the people of knowledge, 'the saying of whoever took a lord' is the object of 'rejecting'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 271-272}

As for what the one whose view was described said, that the first verb means 'decreed', al-Tabari says this is not known in the language of the Arabs or the non-Arabs, since witnessing is one meaning and decreeing another. The editor says this is a reply to Abu Ubayda. Al-Tabari then gives what was reported from some of the early people in the same sense. In 6761, Muhammad ibn Ja'far ibn al-Zubayr (Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq): contrary to what they said, meaning the Christian delegation of Najran; and the word for justice means equity. In 6762, Mujahid (al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl, Ibn Abi Najih): with justice. The editor says Ibn Ishaq's report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 227) and belongs to the remaining reports that ended at 6649. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 272-273}
