**Commentary on verse 10 (pp. 366-368)**

On "equal among you, one who conceals speech and one who proclaims it, one who hides by night and one who goes forth by day", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: equal with God, among you, people, are the one who concealed speech and the one who proclaimed it, and the one hidden by night in its darkness in disobedience to God, "and one who goes forth by day", that is, visible in the daylight; nothing of that is hidden from Him, the secret and the open of His creation being the same to Him, for nothing is concealed from Him or hidden. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 366}

The verb is said "sariba yasrubu surub" when someone appears. Al-Tabari cites a verse of Qays ibn al-Khatim addressed to a woman who travelled by night, though she was not used to going out and appearing, and explains it as: how did you travel by night over this long road when you did not go out and show yourself? Some said it is the one who walks in his "sarb", that is, his way and place; the editor thinks this refers to Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an, whose wording is "one who walks in his sarb, that is, his ways and aspects". The scholars of Arab speech differed over "sarb": some said "he is safe in his sarb" with a fatha on the sin, others "in his sirb" with a kasra. The interpreters said the same. Report 20202, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: he is a man of suspicion who hides by night, and when he goes out by day he shows people that he is free of sin. Report 20203, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "one who goes forth by day" means visible. Report 20204, from Abu Raja', through Ibn Bashshar, Ibn Abi Adi and Awf: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 367}

God knows them best; equal are the one who conceals speech and the one who proclaims it, the one who hides by night and the one who goes forth by day. Report 20205, from Abu Raja', through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ali ibn Asim and Awf: the one who hides is in his house, and "one who goes forth by day" is one who goes off on his way; His knowledge of them is one. Report 20206, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa, Shibl and Ibn Abi Najih: secret and open are equal with Him; the one who hides is in his house and the one who goes forth is out by day wherever he is, and the one hidden in his place of absence and the one who is out are alike with Him. Report 20207, from Khusayf, through al-Himmani and Sharik: "hiding by night" is plunging headlong into disobedience, "going forth by day" is being visible by day. Report 20208, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: all of that is equal with Him, the secret being open to Him; "hiding by night" is in the darkness of the night and "going forth" is being visible by day. Report 20209, from Mujahid and Ikrima, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sharik and Khusayf: "going forth by day" means visible by day. Al-Tabari then explains the parsing of "man" in the verse: the first is in the nominative through "sawa'" (equal), the second is joined to the first and the third to the second. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 368}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
