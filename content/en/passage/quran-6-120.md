## Summary
Verse 120 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 120 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On verse 120, "leave the outward sin and the inward": al-Tabari says, leave, people, the open side of sin, which is its outward part, and its secret side, which is its inward part. He relays from Qatada (report 13794) "its little and its much, its secret and its open", and from him again (13795) "its secret and its open"; from al-Rabi' ibn Anas (13796) the same, who cited "what is outward of them and what is inward" (7:33). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 72}

He transmits from al-Rabi' ibn Anas by another chain (13797) that God forbade the outward and inward sin, whether done in secret or in the open, and from Mujahid (13798) that it is disobedience to God in secret and in public, and from Mujahid through Ibn Jurayj (13799) that it is what a person intends of what he is going to do. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 73}

The commentators then differed on what is meant by outward and inward here. Some said the outward is what God forbade in "do not marry what your fathers married of women" (4:22) and "forbidden to you are your mothers" (4:23), and the inward is fornication; this is reported (13800) from Sa'id ibn Jubayr. Others said the outward means women of the banners among the fornicators, and the inward means women who keep secret lovers; the editor explains that "women of the banners" were the prostitutes of pre-Islamic times who set up flags at their tents or houses by which they were known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 73}

For the second saying he reports from al-Suddi (13801) that the outward are the women in the taverns' shops and the inward is the woman friend a man takes and visits in secret. From al-Dahhak on "do not approach indecencies, outward or inward" (6:151) he reports (13802) that the people of the pre-Islamic era concealed fornication and thought it lawful if secret, so God forbade it both secret and open. From Mujahid (13803): the outward is marrying two sisters together and a man marrying his father's wife after him, and the inward is fornication. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 74}

Others said the outward is nakedness, going unclothed, in circling the House, and the inward is fornication; this is reported (13804) from Ibn Zayd, and the editor's note explains "al-'urya" as the noun for being stripped of one's garment. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 74}

Al-Tabari's choice: God told His creation to leave the outward and inward sin, its secret and its open, and "sin" is whatever of His prohibitions is disobeyed. It includes secret and open fornication, dealings with the women of the banners and those with lovers, marrying fathers' wives and mothers and daughters, circling the House naked, and every act of disobedience, seen or unseen. Since God made the wording cover everything outward and inward, no one may single out one part except by a decisive proof. Were narrowing without proof allowed, the better narrowing would be to the forbidden foods, carrion and blood, since the preceding verses begin with their prohibition and this verse follows in that context; yet it is not objectionable that these were meant and the order to avoid all similar disobedience was added, so the command is general. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v12 p. 75}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
