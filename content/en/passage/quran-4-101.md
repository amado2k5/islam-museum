## Summary
Verse 101 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 101 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 101: travelling, shortening and the trial by unbelievers (pp. 123-124, notes 1-5 on p. 123).** The commentary on verse 101 of sura 4 begins on p. 123. Al-Tabari says that "striking in the land" means travelling in it and that "no blame" means no hardship or sin. Shortening the prayer means shortening its number, so that the traveller prays two where he would pray four at home, according to some; others held that it means shortening to the least of its number, indicating one; and others said it means shortening from the limits of the prayer. He explains the condition of fear as the believers dreading that the unbelievers will put them to trial in prayer, their trial being that they attack them while they are prostrating until they kill or capture them, preventing them from performing it and standing between them and the worship of God and the pure affirmation of His oneness. Then God tells them that the unbelievers who deny His oneness are open enemies, who have made their enmity plain by making war on them over their faith in God and His Messenger, their abandoning the worship of idols and their opposing the error of the unbelievers (p. 124). The notes refer to the earlier explanations of striking in the land, blame, fear and trial; the editor says (note 5) that the first printing puts the word in the nominative and the right reading is the accusative of the manuscript, which is al-Tabari's usage in such places.
**The disagreement over shortening, and the report of Ya'la ibn Umayya: reports 10310-10312 (pp. 124-125, notes 1-2 on p. 124 and note 1 on p. 125).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the shortening for which God removed blame. Some said it is in travel, from the prayer whose completion in residence was obligatory at four rak'as, with permission to shorten it in travel to two. Report 10310 (Ubayd ibn Isma'il al-Habbari, from Abd Allah ibn Idris, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ibn Abi Ammar, from Abd Allah ibn Babayh, from Ya'la ibn Munya): I asked Umar ibn al-Khattab why the prayer is shortened when people are safe, and he said that he had wondered as I wondered until he asked the Prophet, who said it is a charity God has given, so accept His charity. Report 10311 (Abu Kurayb, from Ibn Idris with the same chain, from Ya'la ibn Umayya, from Umar, from the Prophet) is the same, and report 10312 (Sa'id ibn Yahya al-Umawi, from Muhammad ibn Abi Adi, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Ammar, from Abd Allah ibn Babayh, from Ya'la ibn Umayya) gives the same meaning. The editor says Ubayd ibn Isma'il is Ubayd Allah ibn Isma'il al-Habbari; that Ibn Abi Ammar is Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi Ammar al-Qurashi, known as al-Qass, associate of Salama, and reliable; that Abd Allah ibn Babayh is reliable (the spelling of his name varies); that Ya'la ibn Munya is Ya'la ibn Umayya al-Makki, named after his grandmother Munya, a companion; that the first printing has "ibn Umayya" in both reports and the manuscript has Munya in this one alone; that Ahmad gave the report in the Musnad, Muslim in his Sahih by two chains, and al-Bayhaqi, Abu Dawud and Ibn Majah also, with Ibn Kathir tracing it; that the hadith is sound, and Ali ibn al-Madini called it good and sound from Umar's report, known only by this route, its men known; and that the chains of 10311 and 10312 are sound and repeat the one before. These reports are given as transmitted and the museum gives no verdict on them.
**Report 10313 (pp. 125-126, notes 1-3 on p. 125 and note 1 on p. 126).** Report 10313 (Ibn Bashshar, from Hisham ibn Abd al-Malik, from Abu Awana, from Qatada, from Abu al-Aliya): I travelled to Mecca praying two rak'as, and readers of that region met me and asked how I prayed. I said two, and they asked whether this was sunna or Qur'an. I said both, since the Messenger of God prayed two; they said he was at war. Abu al-Aliya answered by pointing to the verse on the vision at the start of the conquest ([[passage/quran-48-27|48:27]]) and to the verse on shortening here, which he read to the point of being secure. The editor says he keeps the manuscript reading and that of the Durr al-Manthur against the first printing's addition, which he takes to be the copyist's or publisher's; that the word "I said" is not in the Durr al-Manthur, so he proposes a reading in brackets; and that al-Suyuti gave the report in the Durr al-Manthur and ascribed it to Ibn Jarir alone.
**Report 10314 and its notes (p. 126, notes 1-2).** Report 10314 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abd Allah ibn Hashim, from Sayf, from Abu Ruq, from Abu Ayyub, from Ali): a group of merchants asked the Prophet how they should pray when they travel in the land, and God sent down the ruling on shortening, and then the revelation stopped. A year later the Prophet went on a campaign and prayed the noon prayer; the polytheists said that Muhammad and his companions had given them an opening from behind, and asked why they had not pressed harder, and one said they would soon have another prayer like it. God then sent down between the two prayers the condition of fear of the unbelievers' trial and the verses through to the humiliating punishment ([[passage/quran-4-102|4:102]]), and the prayer of fear was revealed. The editor says al-Suyuti in the Durr al-Manthur and Ibn Kathir gave the report and ascribed it to Ibn Jarir alone; that Ibn Kathir has "Ibn al-Muthanna" and the editor thinks the printed reading is right, since al-Muthanna narrates from Ishaq ibn al-Hajjaj al-Tahuni hundreds of times; that the manuscript and printed text have "Yusuf" where "Sayf" is right, as in Ibn Kathir and in al-Tabari's comment that follows, being Sayf ibn Umar al-Tamimi, whose hadith is abandoned; that Ibn Kathir said part of it has a witness from the report of Abu Ayyash al-Zuraqi and then gave the report that comes below as 10323 (part of the note is garbled in the OCR and is not reproduced further); and that al-Tabari's reply below shows that he judged the report weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 124}

**Al-Tabari's comment on report 10314 and Ubayy's reading: reports 10315-10316 (pp. 127-128, notes 1-4 on p. 127 and note 1 on p. 128).** Al-Tabari says this interpretation would be good if the following clause did not contain the word "when", which signals that what follows is cut off from what precedes; without it the sense, on the interpretation Sayf narrated from Abu Ruq, would be: if you fear, believers, the unbelievers' trial in your prayer, and you, Muhammad, are among them and lead them in prayer, let a group of them stand with you. He then says that this is also reported in the reading of Ubayy ibn Ka'b. Report 10315 (al-Harith, from Abd al-Aziz, from al-Thawri, from Wasil ibn Hayyan, from Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abza, from his father, from Ubayy ibn Ka'b): he used to read the shortening clause joined directly to the clause on the trial, and not to read the condition of fear. Report 10316 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Bakr ibn Shurud, from al-Thawri, from Wasil al-Ahdab, from Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman, from his father, from Ubayy) is like it, and Bakr said the condition is present in "the Imam", the codex of Uthman. The editor says al-Harith is Ibn Abi Usama and Abd al-Aziz is Ibn Aban, who came earlier under 10295, that Wasil ibn Hayyan al-Ahdab and Bakr ibn Shurud came earlier, and that the manuscript has an odd slip of the copyist in writing "and furthermore". Al-Tabari then says (p. 128) that this reading shows that the fear clause is joined to the shortening clause, so that the sense is: when you travel in the land and fear the unbelievers' trial, there is no blame in shortening, and that the clause "when you are among them" ([[passage/quran-4-102|4:102]]) begins a separate story from the story of this verse. This is because the sense of Ubayy's reading is that the "not" has been dropped because the sentence shows it, as in [[passage/quran-4-176|4:176]], where the sense is "lest you go astray". In his view this is clear evidence that the interpretation Sayf narrated from Abu Ruq is wrong. The editor says the passage in the manuscript is disordered and faulty, as if someone added to it until it ran straight, so he leaves the printed text but corrects "from the source of" to "joined to".
**The view that shortening is for travel and for fear of an enemy: reports 10317-10320 (pp. 128-129, notes 1-3 on p. 129).** Al-Tabari gives the view of others: it is shortening in travel, but God permitted it to the traveller only when he fears an enemy who may put him to trial in his prayer. Report 10317 (Abu Asim Imran ibn Muhammad al-Ansari, from Abd al-Kabir ibn Abd al-Majid, from Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq, from his father): I heard Aisha say about travel, "Complete your prayer"; they said the Messenger of God prayed two rak'as in travel, and she said he was at war and feared; do you fear? Report 10318 (Muhammad ibn Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Hakam, from Ibn Abi Fudayk, from Ibn Abi Dhi'b, from Ibn Shihab, from Umayya ibn Abd Allah ibn Khalid ibn Asid): he told Abd Allah ibn Umar that they find in the Book of God the shortening of the prayer of fear but not of the traveller's prayer, and Abd Allah said that they found their Prophet doing something and they did what he did. Report 10319 (Ali ibn Sahl al-Ramli, from Mu'ammal, from Sufyan, from Hisham ibn Urwa, from his father): Aisha used to pray two rak'as in travel. Report 10320 (Sa'id ibn Yahya, from his father, from Ibn Jurayj): he asked Ata which companions of the Messenger used to complete the prayer in travel, and he said Aisha and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas. The editor says Abu Asim Imran ibn Muhammad was a teacher of al-Tabari and he found no biography of him; that Abd al-Kabir ibn Abd al-Majid, Abu Bakr al-Hanafi, came earlier; that Muhammad ibn Abd Allah is reliable and that "Umar" in the printed text, manuscript and Durr al-Manthur is an error for "Muhammad" which the copyist often writes as "Umar"; that his father Abd Allah ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr, known as Ibn Abi Atiq, narrated from his father's aunt Aisha; that he found 10317 in none of the collections of sunna in his hands, and al-Suyuti gave it in the Durr al-Manthur ascribed to Ibn Jarir alone; and that Ibn Kathir and the Durr al-Manthur gave 10318, again ascribed only to Ibn Jarir, with al-Bayhaqi giving it through Ibn Wahb from Yunus from Ibn Shihab and remarking that al-Layth narrated it from Ibn Shihab from Abd Allah ibn Abi Bakr and that a group traced it to Ibn Shihab without making the chain firm.
**The view that the verse concerns the prayer of fear: reports 10321-10324 (pp. 130-131, notes 1-2 on p. 130 and 1-3 on p. 131).** Al-Tabari gives the view of others: the verse means the shortening of the prayer of fear when swords are not yet drawn, and was revealed about it. Report 10321 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid): the day the Prophet and his companions were at Usfan and the polytheists at Dajnan, the two sides stood facing each other; the Prophet led the noon prayer in two rak'as (or four, Abu Asim was unsure) with all bowing, prostrating and standing together, and the polytheists meant to raid their baggage, so God sent down what He sent down on a group standing with him ([[passage/quran-4-102|4:102]]). He then prayed the afternoon prayer, set his companions in two rows and said the takbir for all; the first row prostrated while the others stood, the others prostrated when the Prophet rose, then he said the takbir and all bowed, the rear row came forward and the first fell back, and they alternated in prostration as before, and he shortened the afternoon prayer to two rak'as. Report 10322 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is much the same. Report 10323 (Ibn Humayd, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Mujahid, from Abu Ayyash al-Zuraqi): we were with the Messenger of God at Usfan with Khalid ibn al-Walid in command of the polytheists; we prayed the noon prayer and the polytheists said they had been in a state in which, had they wished, they could have caught them off guard; then the verse on shortening came down between noon and afternoon, people took their weapons and lined up behind the Messenger facing the qibla with the polytheists facing them; he said the takbir and all said it, he bowed and all bowed, he rose and all rose, he prostrated and the row next to him prostrated while the others stood guarding them; when these finished prostrating the others prostrated, then the row next to him drew back and the others advanced and stood in their place, and they repeated it; then he gave the salam to all. He prayed it at Usfan and on the day of Banu Sulaym. Report 10324 (Abu Kurayb, from Ubayd Allah ibn Musa, from Shayban al-Nahwi, from Mansur, from Mujahid, from Abu Ayyash al-Zuraqi, and from Isra'il, from Mansur by the same chain) is like it. The editor says "tawaqafa" means the two sides held back from fighting for an hour and the printed "tawafaqu" is a mistake; that the manuscript reads "for his prostration"; that he keeps the word "indeed" which the printed text dropped; that "mustaqbilahum" means in front of them since the polytheists were between them and the qibla; and that al-Tabari gives 10323-10324 by three chains and the report comes again by another chain as 10387, a sound hadith, given by Ahmad in the Musnad by two chains (through Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Mansur, and through Ghundar from Shu'ba from Mansur), by al-Tayalisi, by al-Nasa'i by two chains, by Abu Dawud (no. 1236) through Jarir like al-Tabari's first chain, by al-Hakim in the Mustadrak who calls it sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs with al-Dhahabi agreeing, and by al-Bayhaqi in two places, who says its chain is sound and that Qutayba ibn Sa'id narrated it from Jarir stating that Mujahid heard from Abu Ayyash Zayd ibn al-Samit al-Zuraqi; Ibn Kathir says its chain is sound with many witnesses.
**Report 10325 (pp. 132-133, note 1 on p. 132 running on to the next page).** Report 10325 (Ibn Bashshar, from Mu'adh ibn Hisham, from his father, from Qatada, from Sulayman al-Yashkuri): he asked Jabir ibn Abd Allah on which day the shortening came down, and Jabir said: we went out to meet a Quraysh caravan coming from Syria, and when we were at Nakhl a man of the party came to the Messenger of God and said "Muhammad!", and he said "Yes"; he asked "Do you fear me?" and he said "No"; he asked "Who will protect you from me?" and he said "God will protect me from you". The man sheathed his sword (the reading of the verb is uncertain in the extracted text) and threatened him, and the Prophet called for departure and for arms, and the call to prayer was made; he led one group of the party while another guarded them, led those next to him in two rak'as, they drew back to the places of their companions, the others came and he led them in two rak'as while the first group guarded, and he gave the salam. So the Prophet had four rak'as and the people two each; on that day God sent down the revelation on shortening and told the believers to take their arms. The editor says Sulayman al-Yashkuri is Sulayman ibn Qays, who narrated from Jabir and Abu Sa'id al-Khudri and from whom Qatada, Amr ibn Dinar and Abu Bishr Ja'far narrated; that al-Bukhari said he is said to have died in Jabir's lifetime and that Qatada and Abu Bishr did not hear from him; that Abu Hatim said he sat with Jabir and wrote a sheet from him that stayed with his wife, so that Abu al-Zubayr, Abu Sufyan, al-Sha'bi and Qatada narrated from Jabir mostly from that sheet; that Ahmad gave it in the Musnad through Abu Awana from Abu Bishr from Sulayman ibn Qays in other words with the same meaning, that Abu Dawud, al-Bayhaqi and al-Tahawi allude to it; and Ibn Kathir said Ahmad alone gave it by this route.
**The view that the shortening is from the traveller's prayer, not the resident's: reports 10326-10330 (pp. 133-135, notes 1-2 on p. 133, notes 1-3 on p. 134 and the rest of note 3 on p. 135).** Al-Tabari gives the view of others: the verse means shortening the prayer of fear when fear is not extreme, but it is shortening from the traveller's prayer, not from the resident's, because the traveller's prayer without fear is two rak'as, complete and not shortened, as the resident's is four, and in fear while travelling it was shortened again to half, a single rak'a. Report 10326 (Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi): when the prayer is prayed in two rak'as in travel it is complete; shortening is not allowed unless you fear the unbelievers' trial in prayer, and the shortening is one rak'a: the imam and his troops stand in two groups, one behind him and one facing the enemy; he prays one rak'a with those with him and they walk backward to the place of their companions; the other group comes and prays another rak'a with the imam, who sits and gives the salam, and they stand and each prays one for himself, return to their row, while the others add a rak'a to theirs. People say no, it is a single rak'a, none adds anything to it, and the imam's rak'a suffices them, so the imam has two and they have one. This is, on this view, what the verse on standing with the Prophet in prayer and taking precaution says ([[passage/quran-4-102|4:102]]). Report 10327 (Ahmad ibn al-Walid al-Qurashi, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from Simak al-Hanafi): I asked Ibn Umar about the traveller's prayer, and he said two rak'as are complete, not shortened; shortening is the prayer of danger, in which the imam prays one rak'a with one group, they are replaced by another and he prays a rak'a with them, so the imam has two and each group one. Report 10328 (Ibn Bashshar, from Yahya, from Sufyan, from Salim al-Aftas, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): how can it be shortening when they pray two? It is one rak'a. Report 10329 (Sa'id ibn Amr al-Sakuni, from Baqiyya, from al-Mas'udi, from Yazid al-Faqir, from Jabir ibn Abd Allah): the prayer of fear is one rak'a. Report 10330 (Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman, from his uncle Abd Allah ibn Wahb, from Amr ibn al-Harith, from Bakr ibn Sawada, from Ziyad ibn Nafi', from Ka'b, a companion whose hand was cut off at al-Yamama): the prayer of fear is for each group one rak'a and two prostrations. The editor says the printed text has a different wording where the manuscript reads "the shortening is in the traveller's prayer, not the resident's", which he keeps; that al-Bayhaqi gave 10327 and al-Suyuti added Abd ibn Humayd; that Yazid al-Faqir is Yazid ibn Suhayb; that Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, Abd Allah ibn Wahb and Amr ibn al-Harith ibn Ya'qub al-Ansari have come earlier; that Bakr ibn Sawada al-Judhami is a reliable follower, Ziyad ibn Nafi' al-Tujibi is listed by Ibn Hibban among the reliable, and Ka'b al-Aqta' has an entry in al-Isaba, where Ibn Hajar says he thinks the chain has a break since al-Bukhari cites it by Ziyad ibn Nafi' from Abu Musa al-Ghafiqi from Jabir, and al-Bukhari in the Tarikh says Ka'b had his hand cut off at al-Yamama, had companionship, and Ziyad narrated from him. These views and reports are given as transmitted, the tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict on the legal questions.
**The argument of those who hold this view: report 10331 (p. 135).** Al-Tabari says those who hold this view argued from reports including 10331 (Muhammad ibn Bashshar, from Yahya ibn Sa'id, from Sufyan, from Ash'ath ibn Abi al-Sha'tha', from al-Aswad ibn Hilal, from Tha'laba ibn Zahdam al-Yarbu'i): we were with Sa'id ibn al-As at Tabaristan and he asked which of us remembered the Prophet's prayer in fear; Hudhayfa said he did; he stood us behind him in one row, with another row facing the enemy, prayed one rak'a with those next to him, then they went to the places of the others and those came and he prayed one rak'a with them. The editor says this report comes with another chain as 10333; that Ash'ath ibn Abi al-Sha'tha' is Ash'ath ibn Sulaym ibn Aswad al-Muharibi, one of the reliable Kufan teachers; that al-Aswad ibn Hilal al-Muharibi lived in the pre-Islamic period and met Islam, narrating from Mu'adh ibn Jabal, Umar and Ibn Mas'ud; that Tha'laba ibn Zahdam al-Hanzali is disputed as a companion, narrated from Hudhayfa and Abu Mas'ud and mostly from the companions; that Ahmad, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i, al-Bayhaqi, al-Hakim (who says the chain is sound, with al-Dhahabi agreeing) and al-Tahawi gave it, and al-Suyuti added Ibn Abi Shayba, Abd ibn Humayd and Ibn Hibban; and that the manuscript reads the wording of the second row differently from the printed text. Report 10332 begins at the foot of the page and is not yet recorded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 128}

**Reports 10332-10339 (pp. 135-137, notes 1-3 on pp. 136-137).** The proof-texts continue for those who say the shortening in the prayer of fear is to one rak'a. Report 10332 (Ibn Bashshar, from Yahya and Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-Rukayn ibn al-Rabi', from al-Qasim ibn Hassan): he asked Zayd ibn Thabit, who narrated something similar to Hudhayfa's account in 10331. Report 10333 (Ibn Bashshar, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from al-Ash'ath, from al-Aswad ibn Hilal, from Tha'laba ibn Zahdam al-Yarbu'i, from Hudhayfa) is similar. Report 10334 (Ibn Bashshar, from Yahya, from Sufyan, from Abu Bakr ibn Abi al-Jahm, from Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah, from Ibn Abbas): the Prophet prayed at Dhu Qarad and arranged the people in two rows, one behind him and one facing the enemy; he prayed a rak'a with those behind him, then they went to the others' place and the others came and he prayed a rak'a with them, and they did not make it up. Report 10335 (Tamim ibn al-Muntasir, from Ishaq al-Azraq, from Sharik, from Abu Bakr, from Ubayd Allah, from Ibn Abbas) is the same. Reports 10336-10339 are from Ibn Abbas by way of Mujahid and Bukayr ibn al-Akhnas: God made the prayer obligatory on the tongue of your Prophet as four at home, two on a journey and one in fear. They come from Bishr ibn Mu'adh via Abu Awana (10336), Ibn Bashshar via Abd al-Rahman via Abu Awana (10337), Nasr ibn Abd al-Rahman via al-Muharibi via Ayyub ibn A'idh al-Ta'i (10338) and Ya'qub ibn Mahan via al-Qasim ibn Malik via Ayyub (10339). In the notes the editor Shakir says that al-Rukayn ibn al-Rabi' ibn Umayla al-Fazari is a reliable Kufan; that Ibn Hibban lists al-Qasim ibn Hassan al-Amiri among the reliable, that Ibn Hajar first put him among the followers of the followers, which would mean he did not hear from Zayd, and then found Ibn Hibban also listing him among the followers, and that al-Bayhaqi gives the report with an explicit statement that he heard from Zayd; that Abu Bakr ibn Abi al-Jahm is Abu Bakr ibn Abd Allah ibn Abi al-Jahm al-Adawi, named after his grandfather, a reliable jurist of whom Ibn Sa'd says he narrated little (the printed text and manuscript misspell "Ibn Sukhayr" with an undotted ha); that Ubayd Allah ibn Abd Allah is the son of Utba ibn Mas'ud al-Hudhali, a reliable follower, learned, pious, a fine poet, one of the jurists of Medina and the teacher of Umar ibn Abd al-Aziz; that the chain of 10334 is sound in Ahmad's Musnad and that al-Tahawi also gave it; that Abu Awana is al-Wadih ibn Abd Allah al-Yashkuri and Bukayr ibn al-Akhnas is a reliable Kufan, with Ahmad and Muslim giving the report; that he doubts the attribution of Nasr ibn Abd al-Rahman as "al-Azdi" (his brother Ahmad Shakir held it is al-Azdi, not "al-Awdi" as in the printed text) but could not check it in the references at hand and so followed his brother; and that al-Muharibi is Abd al-Rahman ibn Muhammad, Ayyub ibn A'idh al-Ta'i is reliable, and al-Qasim ibn Malik al-Muzani, one of Ahmad's teachers, is reliable. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 137}

**Reports 10340-10342 (pp. 138-139, notes 1-2 on p. 138 and note 1 on p. 139).** Report 10340 (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, from Muhammad ibn Ja'far, from Shu'ba, from al-Hakam, from Yazid al-Faqir, from Jabir): the Prophet led them in the prayer of fear, with one row before him and one behind; he prayed a rak'a of two prostrations with those behind him, then these moved forward to their companions' place and the others came to theirs and he prayed a rak'a of two prostrations with them and gave the salam, so it was two rak'as for the Prophet and one for them. Report 10341 (Ahmad ibn Abd al-Rahman ibn Wahb, from his uncle Abd Allah ibn Wahb, from Amr ibn al-Harith, from Bakr ibn Sawada, from Ziyad ibn Nafi', from Abu Musa, from Jabir): he led them in the prayer of fear on the day of Muharib and Tha'laba, each group one rak'a of two prostrations. Report 10342 (Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Tusi, from Abd al-Samad, from Sa'id ibn Ubayd al-Huna'i, from Abd Allah ibn Shaqiq, from Abu Hurayra): the Prophet halted between Dajnan and Usfan and the polytheists said these people have a prayer dearer to them than their children and firstborn, the afternoon prayer, so unite your plan and attack them in one rush; Gabriel came to the Prophet and told him to divide his companions into two halves, pray with one while another group stood behind them with their caution and weapons, then pray with the other while the first took their caution and weapons, so each group had one rak'a with him and he had two. The editor notes that Yazid al-Faqir is Yazid ibn Suhayb, mentioned before at 10329; that 10340 is in al-Nasa'i and al-Bayhaqi (who points to the route through al-Hakam ibn Utayba) and in Ibn Kathir's commentary; that Abu Musa in 10341 is Ali ibn Rabah, a known follower whom Muslim cites, and that Ibn Hajar in Fath al-Bari adds that this Abu Musa is said to be al-Ghafiqi, Malik ibn Ubada, also a known companion, with al-Bukhari giving the report; and that Sa'id ibn Ubayd al-Huna'i is "a shaykh" in Abu Hatim's words and listed by Ibn Hibban among the reliable, that Abd Allah ibn Shaqiq al-Uqayli came before, and that 10342 is in al-Nasa'i and al-Tirmidhi (who calls it sound and strange from this chain) while al-Suyuti in al-Durr al-Manthur attributes it only to Ibn Jarir and al-Tirmidhi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 139}

**The view of shortening in the heat of battle, and al-Tabari's choice (pp. 139-141, notes 1-2 on p. 140).** Al-Tabari gives another view: the shortening meant is the shortening of the travellers' prayer, but in intense fighting, when swords meet, so that at the clash the person praying may pray one rak'a by nodding the head wherever he faces. Report 10343 (Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, his uncle, his father, his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas): the shortening is, if you meet the enemy when the prayer's time has come, to say the takbir and lower the head by gesture, riding or on foot. Al-Tabari then states his choice: the most fitting interpretation is that the shortening is of its limits, that is, leaving the full bowing and prostration and permitting it to be performed however possible, facing the qibla or away from it, mounted or walking, in the state of drawn swords, hand-to-hand fighting and advancing lines, the very state mentioned in [[passage/quran-2-239|2:239]], where the obligatory prayer is permitted mounted with gestures for bowing and prostration, as reported from Ibn Abbas. His argument rests on [[passage/quran-4-103|the next verse]], which orders the prayer to be established once secure: establishing it is completing its limits of bowing, prostration and the other obligations, not adding to its number, which was not obligatory in fear. He answers anyone who takes this as an order to complete the number required in safety once fear has gone: the traveller who shortens would then not be establishing his prayer by going from four to two, which goes against the consensus of the community that one who performs the prayer with all its limits and fewer rak'as is not described that way. So, since God told the person allowed to shorten out of fear to establish the prayer once reassured, what was lifted in fear is exactly what is required in safety, the establishing of its limits. This is al-Tabari's own choice and argument. The editor notes that the printed text has a wrong word for "drawn swords", corrected from the manuscript to the word meaning the drawing of swords as fighters close, and that "so that is a saying" in the printed text was corrected from the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 141}
