**Commentary on verse 18: the Jews' and Christians' claim to be God's sons and beloved (pp. 150-152)**

On 'the Jews and the Christians say: we are the sons of God and His beloved. Say: why then does He punish you for your sins?', al-Tabari says this is a report from God about some of the Jews and Christians who said this, and a report from Ibn Abbas names the Jews who said it. Report 11613, from Ibn Abbas, says that Nu'man ibn Ada, Bahri ibn Amr and Shas ibn Adi came to the Messenger of God and spoke with him; he called them to God and warned them of His vengeance, and they said: you do not frighten us, Muhammad, by God we are God's sons and His beloved, as the Christians say, so God sent down this verse about them. The editor corrects their names from Ibn Hisham's biography. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 150}

Report 11614, from al-Suddi, says that the Jews' 'sons of God' meant that God had revealed to Israel that some of his children would enter the Fire and remain forty days until it purified them and consumed their sins, then a caller would say to bring out every circumcised child of Israel; that is their saying the Fire would touch them only a few days. As for the Christians, one party among them called the Messiah son of God. Al-Tabari explains that when the Arabs boast they put the report in the form of a report about the group even if the boast concerns one of them, as in 'we are the generous and noble' when only one is generous, and he cites a line of Jarir in which a deed done by one man of his people is told as a deed of the group; so God reports the Christians saying this. The editor explains that the line comes from Jarir's satire on al-Farazdaq and concerns the killing of one of al-Farazdaq's ancestors, attributed to the whole tribe. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 151}

'His beloved' is the plural of 'beloved'. God tells His Prophet to say to these lying slanderers of their Lord: why does your Lord punish you for your sins if it is as you say, that you are His sons and beloved, since a beloved does not punish his beloved and you admit He will punish you? The Jews had said that God would punish them forty days, the number of days they worshipped the calf, and then bring them all out. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 152}

Then 'rather you are human beings among those He created; He forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills': say to them that it is not as you claim; you are human beings of those He created from the children of Adam, created like all of Adam's children, and if you do good you will be repaid for your good; the sentence runs on to the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 152}

He continues: if you do good you are repaid for your good as the rest of Adam's children are, and if you do wrong you are repaid for your wrong; you have with God only what others of His creation have, for He forgives the sins of whom He wills among those who believe in Him, overlooking them by His favour and covering them by His mercy, so He does not punish them. He explained the meaning of forgiveness elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 153}

'And punishes whom He wills' means He deals justly with whom He wills of His creation, punishing them for their sins and exposing them before all, not covering for them. This is a warning from God to those Jews and Christians who rely on the standing with God of their righteous forebears, whom He preferred for their obedience and chose for their haste to His approval and patience in what befell them. He says to them: do not be deceived by their place with Me, for they gained what they gained by obedience and by putting My approval before their desires, not by wishful hopes; I forgive the sins of whom I will among those who obey Me and punish whom I will among those who disobey Me, and not someone whose fathers were close to Me while he is My enemy and opposes My command and prohibition. The editor corrects a reading in the printed text and manuscript and glosses the verb for choosing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 153}

Report 11615, from al-Suddi, says that 'forgives whom He wills and punishes whom He wills' means He guides whom He wills of you in this world and forgives him, and lets whom He wills die in disbelief and punishes him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 154}

Then 'to God belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what is between them, and to Him is the return': God directs all that is in the heavens, the earth and between them, His is the command and the dominion, He turns it as He wills and arranges it as He likes, with no partner and no one holding any dominion with Him. So know, you who say 'we are God's sons and His beloved', that if He punishes you for your sins nothing prevents Him and nothing repels Him, since no one is related to Him so that He would favour him, and no one has any dominion apart from Him to stand between Him and punishing; to Him is the return of everything. Fear His punishment, you slanderers, and do not be deceived by wishes and the merits of fathers and forebears. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 154}

We stop here at the end of the commentary on verse 18 on p. 154; verse 19 begins on the next page.
