## Summary
Verse 21 of [[passage/quran-12|Surat Yusuf]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 21 of 111 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 21 (pp. 17-20)**

Al-Tabari says: the one who bought Joseph said, of his seller in Egypt; it is mentioned that his name was "Qitfir". Report 18941, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: the name of the one who bought him was Qitfir. It is also said that his name was Itfir ibn Ruhayb, that he was the "Aziz", in charge of the treasuries of Egypt, and that the king then was al-Rayyan ibn al-Walid, a man of the Amalekites; report 18942, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama. The editor notes that al-Tabari gave this report in his History and that the name is vowelled differently in the manuscript in the two places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 17}

It is also said that the one who sold him in Egypt was Malik ibn Dhu'r ibn Buwayb ibn Ufqan ibn Madyan ibn Ibrahim; report 18943, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, Muhammad ibn al-Sa'ib and Abu Salih. On "to his wife", her name, as Ibn Ishaq mentioned, was Ra'il bint Ra'a'il; report 18944, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama. On "honour his lodging" he says: honour the place of his stay, as one says "so-and-so stayed in such a place" when he lodged there; reports 18945, from Qatada, and 18946, from Ibn Jurayj: his rank, and she was the wife of the Aziz. The page of the scan carrying this passage is garbled, so only what is stated here could be established; the editor's footnote records the variant vowelling of the name of Malik's forebear in the manuscript, the printed text and the History, and refers to the explanation of "lodging". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 18}

Report 18947, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: the king bought him, and the king was a Muslim. On "perhaps he will be of use to us, or we may adopt him as a son" it is mentioned that the buyer of Joseph said this to his wife when he handed him to her, because he had no child and did not go in to women; he said to her, honour him, perhaps he will spare us some of our burdens once he understands the matters he is charged with; "or adopt him as a son" means to adopt him. Report 18948, from Ibn Ishaq: Itfir, as he was told, was a man who did not go in to women, and his wife Ra'il was a beautiful, delicate, well-fed woman in kingship and worldly ease. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 19}

Reports 18949 and 18951, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Ibn Waki', his father, Sufyan, Abu Ishaq and Abu al-Ahwas, and through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il and Abu Ubayda: the most perceptive of people are three: the Aziz when he saw Joseph's worth and said to his wife "honour his lodging" while his own people held him cheap; Abu Bakr when he saw Umar's worth and appointed him successor; and the woman who said to her father about Moses that the best one to hire is the strong and trustworthy [[passage/quran-28-26|28:26]]. Report 18950, from al-Suddi: Joseph was taken to Egypt, the Aziz, king of Egypt, bought him and took him to his house and said to his wife what he said. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 19}

On "thus We established Joseph in the land" he says: as We rescued Joseph from his brothers' hands when they had resolved to kill him, and brought him out of the well after he was cast in it, and brought him to honour and a high station with the Aziz of Egypt, so We established him in the land, putting him over its treasuries. On "and to teach him the interpretation of events" he says: that We might teach Joseph the interpretation of dreams We established him in the land; reports 18952, 18953 and 18955, from Mujahid, and 18954, from al-Suddi: the interpretation of dreams. The editor refers to the explanations of "established" and "interpretation". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 20}

On "and God prevails in His affair" he says: God is in charge of Joseph's affair, managing, ordering and guarding it, and the pronoun in "His affair" returns to Joseph. Report 18956 on the meaning of "prevailing" is from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Isra'il and Abu Husayn: "doer of what He wills"; the editor discusses how to read the wording and prefers a reading of it as "fa''al". On "but most people do not know" he says: most of the people who held Joseph cheap and sold him for a paltry price, and those of Egypt among whom he came when he was sold, do not know what God is doing with Joseph and where his affair is headed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 21}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
