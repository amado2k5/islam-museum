## Summary
Verse 161 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 161 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 161: the readings of the verb of 'ghulul' (pp. 348-349).** The reading of the first verb in the verse differed. A group of Hijazi and Iraqi readers read it with fatha on the ya and damma on the ghayn, meaning that the Prophet would betray his companions in what God gave them as spoil from their enemies' property. Some of them argued that the verse came down about a red velvet cloak missing from the booty at Badr, when some said perhaps the Messenger of God had taken it. Ibn 'Abbas (8136), through Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Malik ibn Abi al-Shawarib, from 'Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad, from Khusayf, from Miqsam: it was revealed about a red cloak lost at Badr; some people said that he took it and said much about it, so the verse came down. (The number of this report is read from the sequence and the footnote, because the scan garbles it.) In a footnote (pp. 348-349) the editor gives notes on Ibn Abi al-Shawarib al-Qurashi al-Umawi, saying that Muslim, al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Maja reported from him and that al-Nasa'i said of him 'no harm in him', a reliable, eminent man and truthful; on 'Abd al-Wahid ibn Ziyad al-'Abdi, whose life was given earlier at 2616; and on Khusayf ibn 'Abd al-Rahman al-Jazari, who saw Anas and reported from 'Ata', 'Ikrima, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Mujahid and Miqsam, citing Ahmad ('weak in hadith', 'very unsettled in the musnad'), Ibn 'Adi (his hadith is unobjectionable when a reliable man narrates from him), and Ibn Hibban (a group of our imams left him and others used him as proof; he was a pious jurist and worshipper who erred often in what he reported and stood alone among the well-known in what was not corroborated; the fair course is to accept what agrees with the reliable and leave what is not corroborated). He adds that al-Tirmidhi recorded it in his chapter on Qur'an commentary through Qutayba from 'Abd al-Wahid and called it 'hasan gharib'; that 'Abd al-Salam ibn Harb reported something similar from Khusayf; that some reported it from Khusayf from Miqsam without Ibn 'Abbas, that is, as a mursal report; that Ibn Kathir (2: 279) also attributes it to Abu Dawud and al-Suyuti (Durr 2: 91) to Abu Dawud, 'Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn Abi Hatim, al-Tirmidhi and Ibn Jarir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 348} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 349}

**The cloak reports and Sa'id ibn Jubayr's reading (8137-8142, pp. 349-350).** Khusayf (8137): I asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr how he reads the verse, in the active or the passive; he said, rather with fatha on the ya and damma on the ghayn, for a prophet, by God, could be betrayed and killed. Ishaq ibn Ibrahim ibn Habib ibn al-Shahid, from 'Attab ibn Bashir, from Khusayf, from Miqsam, from Ibn 'Abbas (8138): it was about a red cloak missing in the battle of Badr, when some of the Prophet's companions said perhaps the Prophet took it, and the verse came down. The editor notes that the publisher put a sentence attributed to Sa'id, that prophets are betrayed and killed, in brackets and judges it more likely part of the completion of 8140, unless the copyist dropped another report from Sa'id; he corrects 'bal wallahi' to 'bala wallahi' from the manuscript, and gives notes on 'Attab ibn Bashir al-Jazari, quoting Ahmad: 'I hope there is no harm in him; late in life he narrated objectionable reports, which I think come from Khusayf.' Abu Kurayb, from Khallad, from Zuhayr, from Khusayf, from 'Ikrima, from Ibn 'Abbas (8139): a cloak was lost at Badr and they said the Messenger of God took it, so the verse came down. Abu Kurayb, from Malik ibn Isma'il, from Zuhayr, from Khusayf, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr and 'Ikrima (8140): both read it with fatha on the ya and damma on the ghayn; then the cloak account is given from 'Ikrima or another from Ibn 'Abbas. Mujahid ibn Musa, from Yazid, from Qaz'a ibn Suwayd al-Bahili, from Humayd al-A'raj, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8141): it came down about a red cloak lost at Badr from the booty; the editor quotes on Qaz'a Ahmad ('unsettled in hadith, close to being abandoned'), Abu Hatim ('not that strong'), Ibn Hibban (many errors and gross delusion until arguing from his reports lapsed) and al-Bazzar ('not strong; people of knowledge narrated from him'). Nasr ibn 'Ali al-Jahdami, from Mu'tamir, from his father, from al-A'mash (8142): Ibn Mas'ud read it with damma on the ya and fatha on the ghayn, and Ibn 'Abbas said: rather 'and be killed'; Ibn 'Abbas recalled that it was about a cloak, of which they said the Messenger of God had taken it at Badr, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 349} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 350}

**The second view among the active readers: the share of the scouts (8143-8147, pp. 350-352).** Others of those who read it with fatha on the ya and damma on the ghayn said it came down about scouts the Messenger of God had sent on an expedition; he then took booty and did not allot a share to the scouts, so the verse came to teach him that what he had done was a mistake, that the rule required allotting to the scouts the same as to others, and made known to him what is due regarding the spoil God gave him: he may not give anyone a special part of it, whether of those present at the fight or those who were support for them in their raid (the editor glosses 'rid'' as helper and supporter). Muhammad ibn Sa'd, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his father, from Ibn 'Abbas (8143): a prophet may not allot to one party of the Muslims and leave another and be unjust in the division, but allots with justice, takes God's command and rules by what God sent down; God would not make a prophet who betrays his companions, and if the Prophet did so they would follow him in it (the editor says this chain recurs in the commentary and refers to the discussion at 305). Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak (8144): he read it with fatha on the ya and said: to give some and leave some when he takes booty. Ibn Waki', from his father, from Salama ibn Nubayt, from al-Dahhak (8145): the Messenger of God sent scouts, took booty and did not allot to the scouts, so the verse came down. Al-Husayn, from Abu Mu'adh, from 'Ubayd ibn Sulayman, from al-Dahhak (8146): not allot to one party of his companions and leave another, but be just, take God's command and rule by what God sent down. Yahya ibn Abi Talib, from Yazid, from Juwaybir, from al-Dahhak (8147): when he took booty he could not allot to some of his companions and leave others, but divided it among them equally. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 350} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 351} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 352}

**The third view: concealing revelation (8148, p. 352).** Others of those who read it with fatha on the ya and damma on the ghayn said it was sent to let people know that the Prophet conceals nothing of God's revelation. Ibn Ishaq (8148): a prophet may not conceal from the people what God sent him with out of fear or desire of the people, and whoever does so will bring it on the Day of Resurrection (from Ibn Hisham 3: 124, the continuation of reports ending at 8135, with slight differences of wording). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 352}

**Al-Tabari's account of the first reading, and the language (8149-8150, pp. 352-353).** Abu Ja'far: the sense of this reading is that it is not fitting for a prophet to be a 'ghall', that is, that betraying communities is not among the acts of prophets. One says 'ghalla al-rajul yaghullu ghululan' when he betrays, and also 'aghalla al-rajul yughillu ighlalan', as in Shurayh's saying that the borrower who is not a 'mughill' bears no liability, meaning one who is not a betrayer; and one says 'aghalla al-jazir' when a butcher steals some meat along with the hide (the editor explains that in flaying he leaves meat clinging to the skin). He says the interpretation of the people of tafsir came as he said. Al-Suddi (8149), through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn, from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal, from Asbat: it was not fitting for him to betray, and as it was not fitting for him, do not betray. Mujahid (8150), through Muhammad ibn 'Amr, from Abu 'Asim, from 'Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih: to betray. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 352} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 353}

**The second reading, with damma on the ya and fatha on the ghayn (8151-8154 and after, pp. 353-354).** Others read it with damma on the ya and fatha on the ghayn, the reading of the great majority of the readers of Medina and Kufa, and they differed in interpreting it. Some said it means that his companions betray him, the companions being dropped so that the verb stays passive; its sense is then 'to be betrayed'. Al-Hasan (8151), through Ya'qub ibn Ibrahim, from Hushaym, from 'Awf: he read it in the passive and said: to be betrayed. Qatada (8152), through Bishr, from Yazid, from Sa'id: that his believing companions who are with him betray him; we were told the verse came down on the Prophet at Badr when groups of his companions had defrauded. Qatada (8153), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, from 'Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar: that his companions betray him. Al-Rabi' ibn Anas (8154), through 'Ammar, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father: that his companions who are with him betray him; we were told, and God knows best, that the verse came down on the Prophet at Badr when groups of his companions had defrauded. Others of them said it means that a prophet is accused of ghulul, called a traitor and a thief; they took the verb to derive from a form meaning 'to be dealt with as a betrayer', with the doubled letter lightened, as some read with lightening in the verse of Surat al-An'am on denial [[passage/quran-6-33|6:33]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 353} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 354}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 354-355).** Abu Ja'far holds the better of the two readings to be that with fatha on the ya and damma on the ghayn, meaning that ghulul is not among the attributes of prophets and cannot be a thing done by a prophet. His ground is that after this clause God threatens those who commit ghulul with its punishment on the Day of Resurrection, which is clear proof that the prohibition concerns ghulul and that He is telling His servants it is not among the attributes of His prophets; had He meant to forbid the companions to accuse the Messenger of ghulul, He would have followed with a threat for accusation and ill opinion, not for ghulul. He then raises an objection from those of the other reading: would it not be better to say that his companions do not betray him, if God followed only with a threat on ghulul, the passive reading being right because the sense is that his companions betray him in the spoil? His reply: were they allowed to defraud others than the Prophet, so that they were singled out for the prohibition on betraying him? If they say yes, they leave the position of the people of Islam, since God never permitted betraying anyone according to any of them; if they say it was not allowed in a prophet or anyone else, what is the point of singling them out, when his ghulul and the ghulul of some Jews are alike in what God forbade of their property to the one who defrauds and in what the trustee owes in returning the trust to both? So it is clear that the meaning is as he says: God denied that ghulul and betrayal are among the attributes of His prophets, forbidding His servants ghulul and telling them to follow their prophet's way, as Ibn 'Abbas said in the report from 'Atiyya (8143), and then followed their prohibition with a threat in the two verses together. In the footnotes on p. 355 the editor explains that 'fa-awla minhu' is the objector's phrase, that the copyist dropped 'kana' which he restored, and that 'Atiyya is 'Atiyya ibn Sa'd ibn Junada al-'Awfi, who reported from Ibn 'Abbas and is named in the earlier chain 'from his father'; some who annotated the commentary found this puzzling and said no mention of him had gone before, but he is mentioned, as one sees. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 354} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 355}
