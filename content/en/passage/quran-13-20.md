**Commentary on verses 20-21 (pp. 419-421)**

On "those who fulfil God's covenant and do not break the pledge, and those who join what God has commanded to be joined, fear their Lord and fear an evil reckoning", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: only those with understanding take admonition and learn from God's signs, those who fulfil the charge God charged them with, "and do not break the pledge", and do not turn from the covenant they made with God to its opposite, so doing other than what He commanded them and going against what He forbade. We explained the meaning of "covenant" and "pledge" earlier with evidence, which spares repeating it here. The interpreters said the same: report 20330, from Qatada, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Hisham, Amr and Sa'id: "only people of understanding remember", then He made clear who they are and said "those who fulfil God's covenant and do not break the pledge"; so hold to fulfilling the covenant and do not break this pledge, for God, exalted, has forbidden it and put forward in it the strictest warning, mentioning it in some twenty-odd places as counsel to you, a warning to you and a proof against you; a matter is made great by what God has made it great with among people of understanding and sense, so make great what God has made great. Qatada said: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 419}

it was mentioned to us that the Messenger of God used to say in his sermon: no faith for the one who has no trustworthiness, and no religion for the one who has no covenant. On "and those who join what God has commanded to be joined" he says: those who join the kinship that God commanded them to join and do not cut it, "and fear their Lord", fear God about cutting it, lest they cut it and He punish them for cutting it and for going against His command about it. On "and fear an evil reckoning" he says: they dread God's rigorous reckoning of them, after which He does not pardon any sin, so out of their dread of that they are earnest in obeying Him and keep His limits. Report 20331, from Abu al-Jawza', through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Affan, Ja'far ibn Sulayman and Amr ibn Malik: "who fear their Lord and fear an evil reckoning", he said, being measured against deeds; the editor notes that the printed text has "being questioned closely", which he finds better. Report 20332, from Ibrahim, through Affan, Hammad and Farqad: an "evil reckoning" is that one is reckoned with and not forgiven. Report 20333, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 420}

on "and fear an evil reckoning", he said, and what is an evil reckoning? He said: the one in which there is no leniency; the editor explains "jawaz" as tolerance and indulgence. Report 20334, from Ibrahim, through Ibn Sinan al-Qazzaz, Abu Asim, al-Hajjaj and Farqad: do you know what an "evil reckoning" is? I said I do not know. He said: the servant is reckoned with for all his sin, none of it forgiven him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 421}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
