## Summary
Verse 69 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 69 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse: the promise to the obedient (p. 530).** Al-Tabari says God next mentions what he promised those who obey him and his Messenger, lasting honour with him and high stations. He explains that whoever obeys God and the Messenger by submitting to their command, sincerely accepting their judgement, stopping at their command and refraining from what they forbade of disobeying God, is with those he has graced with guidance and success in obedience: in this world with his prophets, and in the next when he enters paradise. He then takes the verse's words one by one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 530}

**The meaning of siddiqin: two views (pp. 530-531).** Al-Tabari says there was disagreement about the meaning of siddiqin, the plural of siddiq. Some said they are the followers of the prophets who believed them and followed their path after them until they joined them, so siddiq is of the intensive form from sidq, truthfulness, as one says of a habitual drinker or a habitual wine-lover; others said the form is from sadaqa, charity, and a report from the Prophet is given in line with their reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 531}

**Report 9923 (p. 531, notes 1-4).** Sufyan b. Waki (9923, from Khalid b. Makhlad, from Musa b. Ya'qub, from his aunt Qariba bint Abdullah b. Wahb b. Zam'a, from her mother Karima bint al-Miqdad, from Duba'a bint al-Zubayr, who was the wife of al-Miqdad) reports from al-Miqdad: I said to the Prophet that something I heard from him I had doubted; he said, if one of you doubts a matter let him ask me. I said, your saying about your wives that you hope for them after you the siddiqin; he said, whom do you count as siddiqin? I said, our children who die young. He said, no, but the siddiqin are the musaddaqun. Al-Tabari says that were the chain sound, we would not allow ourselves to turn from this report to another, even with some defect in the chain. The editor says the manuscript has "Karima bint al-Miqdam", an error with the print right; that it has "Mata'a bint al-Zubayr", an error with the print right; and that both manuscript and print have a meaningless wording for "whom do you count", which he corrected from the abridgement of this report in Muntakhab Kanz al-Ummal. In tracing the report he says Sufyan b. Waki b. al-Jarrah, al-Tabari's teacher, is weak, as detailed earlier; Musa b. Ya'qub al-Zam'i of Medina is reliable, trusted by Ibn Ma'in and Ibn al-Qattan and weakened by Ibn al-Madini and others, with al-Bukhari and Ibn Abi Hatim recording no impugning and Ibn Abi Hatim confining himself to Ibn Ma'in's trust; no impugning is recorded of Qariba, Musa's aunt; her mother Karima bint al-Miqdad is a reliable Successor named by Ibn Hibban in al-Thiqat; and Duba'a bint al-Zubayr b. Abd al-Muttalib, the Prophet's cousin, is a known woman Companion and the wife of al-Miqdad, with reports from the Prophet and her husband. He adds that al-Suyuti gives it abridged in al-Durr al-Manthur attributing it to Ibn Jarir only; that Muntakhab Kanz al-Ummal attributes it to al-Tabarani in al-Kabir; that he did not find it in Majma al-Zawa'id; and that he does not know whether al-Tabarani's route is through Sufyan b. Waki or another narrator, in which case the chain would be good, since the criticism of Sufyan b. Waki was not about his truthfulness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 531}

**Al-Tabari's choice on siddiq (p. 532, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the better meaning of siddiq is one whose word is confirmed by his deed (his choice), since the intensive form in Arab speech, when taken from a verb, expresses emphasis in praise or blame, and he cites God's description of Mary in [[passage/quran-5-75|5:75]]; and when that is the meaning, whoever fits what he said about the givers of charity and the confirmers falls within it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 532}

**The martyrs, the righteous and the sense of companion (pp. 532-533, notes 1-4).** Al-Tabari says shuhada is the plural of shahid, one killed in God's way, so named for standing for the witness of truth beside God until he was killed; salihin is the plural of salih, anyone whose inner and outer life are upright. The praise at the verse's close means that these described people are fine companions in paradise, and the word for companion comes in the singular with the sense of the plural; he cites a line of Jarir in which women are called by the singular "friend" for the plural. The editor refers to earlier interpretations of the martyrs (p. 368, note 3) and the righteous (p. 293, note 1) and to his note on the word for goodness in volume 4 (p. 458, note 2); says the print has "in the singular form" and he kept the manuscript; gives the sources for the line (Jarir's diwan, Tabaqat Fuhul al-Shu'ara and Lisan al-Arab) and says the print has another variant of one half-line unlike the manuscript and the diwan; and gives other lines of the poem. (The line order on the scan of p. 533 is scrambled.) {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 533}

**The grammar of the word for companion (p. 533).** Al-Tabari says the people of Arabic differed over why this word is in the accusative. Some Basran grammarians held it is accusative as a circumstantial state, like a man's saying that Zayd was generous as a man, turning it from the sense of "how excellent a man", and said "ni'ma" applies only to a noun with the definite article or to an indefinite. Some Kufan grammarians held it is accusative as a specifier and denied it is a state, citing that the Arabs say "Zayd was generous as a man" with the preposition "min" and similar forms, and that the preposition's entry shows the word is the specifier, and a phrase reported from the Arabs, "how excellent you were as men". Al-Tabari says the Kufan view is the more correct (his choice) for the reason its holders gave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 533}

**The occasion: reports 9924-9928 (pp. 534-535, notes 1-3).** Al-Tabari says it was mentioned that the verse came down because some people grieved at losing the Messenger of God, fearing not to see him in the next life. Ibn Humayd (9924, from Ya'qub al-Qummi, from Ja'far b. Abi al-Mughira) reports from Sa'id b. Jubayr that a man of the Ansar came to the Prophet grieving and, asked why, said that they go to him morning and evening, look at his face and sit with him, but tomorrow he will be raised with the prophets and they will not reach him; the Prophet answered nothing until Gabriel brought this verse, and he sent for the man and gave him the good news. Ibn Humayd (9925, from Jarir, from Mansur, from Abu al-Duha) reports from Masruq that the Companions said it is not fitting for them to part from him in this world, for if he died he would be raised above them and they would not see him; and God sent down the verse. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9926, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that it was mentioned to them that men said they see him in this world but in the next he is raised and they will not; and it came down. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9927, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that some of the Ansar said, when God admits you to paradise and you are in its highest part and we long for you, what do we do? and it came down. Al-Muthanna (9928, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father) reports from al-Rabi that the Companions said they knew the Prophet had a superiority in the degrees of paradise over those who believed him, so how would they see one another when gathered there? and it came down about this; it is said the higher ones come down to those below them and gather in its gardens, recall what God poured out on them and praise him, and the people of the levels come down and serve them what they desire, so that they delight and take their ease in a garden. The editor notes in the footnotes the differences between the manuscript and the print in these reports and says he kept the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 535}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 535}
