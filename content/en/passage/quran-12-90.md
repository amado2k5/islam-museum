**Commentary on verse 90 (pp. 244-245)**

On "they said, are you indeed Joseph? He said, I am Joseph, and this is my brother; God has been gracious to us; whoever is mindful of God and patient, God does not waste the reward of those who do good", al-Tabari says: Joseph's brothers said to him, when he said that to them, "are you indeed Joseph?" and he said: yes, I am Joseph, "and this is my brother; God has been gracious to us", by bringing us together after you separated us; "whoever is mindful of God and patient" means whoever fears God, keeping watch over himself by performing His obligations and avoiding disobedience to Him, and is patient, that is, restrains himself and holds it back from what God has forbidden him of word or deed at a misfortune that comes to him from God; "God does not waste the reward of those who do good" means God does not void the reward of his good deed and the recompense of his obedience in what he was commanded and forbidden. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "taqwa" and "sabr". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 244}

The readers differed in reading "inna-ka la-anta Yusuf". Most readers of the great cities read "a-inna-ka", as a question. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 244}

It is reported that in Ubayy ibn Ka'b's reading it is "a-wa-anta Yusuf". It is related from Ibn Muhaysin that he read "inna-ka la-anta Yusuf" as a statement and not a question. Al-Tabari says: the correct reading in our view is that of those who read it as a question, because the authoritative body of readers agree on it. Report 19791, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when he said that to them, meaning "do you know what you did to Joseph and his brother when you were ignorant?", the veil was lifted and they knew him, and said "are you indeed Joseph?", and the rest. Report 19792, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, one who heard Abd Allah ibn Idris, and Layth: "whoever is mindful of God and patient", he says, whoever fears disobeying God and is patient in prison. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 245}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
