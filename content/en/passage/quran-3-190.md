## Summary
Verse 190 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 190 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 190 (pp. 473-474).** Al-Tabari says this is God's argument against the speaker and all His creatures, that He is the one who directs and turns things about, and that enriching and impoverishing are His and in His hand; as if He says: consider, people, and take a lesson from what He made of the heavens and earth for your livelihood, food and provision, and from the succession of night and day over you, in one of which you work for your living and in the other you rest, a repose for your bodies, so in that are lessons, reminders, signs and admonitions. Whoever has a mind knows that one who ascribes poverty to Him while He is rich is a liar and fabricator, for all of that is in His hand, which He turns and directs, and had He cancelled it the people would perish; how can poverty be ascribed to one in whose hand and to whom belongs all by which those in the heavens and earth live? Or how can he be rich whose provision is in another's hand, who gives it if he wishes and withholds it if he wishes? The editor's note on p. 473 says the doubled verb al-Tabari uses means to alternate between two things, as in "da'afa" and "da'‘afa", and that "night and day followed each other" means one came after the other. His notes on p. 474 say the manuscript's wording is garbled and the printed text is closer to right, and that the printed text moved a preposition which should come first, as in the manuscript. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 474}
