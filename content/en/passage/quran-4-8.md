## Summary
Verse 8 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 8 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Volume 8 begins with verse 8: is it binding or abrogated (pp. 7-10, reports 8658-8680).** Volume 8 of the Shakir edition opens at p. 7 with the commentary on verse 8 of sura 4 (the volume covers verses 8 to 87, reports 8658 to 10048, as the title page states), and Abu Jafar says the interpreters differed over whether its ruling is binding or abrogated. Some said it is binding: Ibn Abbas (8658, through Abu Kurayb from Ibn Yaman from Sufyan from al-Shaybani from Ikrima; and 8659 through al-Ashja'i, where the editor says the printing dropped this report and merged it with the next); Ibrahim and al-Sha'bi (8660, where the editor says the printing put "al-Ashja'i" in place of "Ibn Yaman" from the dropped chain and he restored it from the manuscript); Mujahid (8661, 8662, 8664): it is obligatory as far as the heirs are willing; Ibrahim and al-Sha'bi (8663): binding, not abrogated; Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8665, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Hushaym from Abu Bishr), asked about it, said people treat it lightly, and that there are two guardians, one who inherits and one who does not, the heir being commanded to give them and the non-heir to speak kindly, and that it is binding and not abrogated; Ibrahim to the same effect (8666); al-Hasan (8667, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid from Sa'id from Mutarrif): it stands, but people became stingy and miserly; Mansur and al-Hasan (8668): binding; Ibn Abbas (8669, through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Abbad ibn al-Awwam from al-Hajjaj from al-Hakam from Miqsam): standing and acted upon; Mujahid (8670): as far as hearts are willing, a due that is obligatory; al-Hasan and al-Zuhri (8671): binding; Yahya ibn Ya'mar (8672, through Hushaym from Mansur from Qatada): three binding Medinan verses that people abandoned: this verse, the verse on asking permission ([[passage/quran-24-58|24:58]]) and the verse on creating people from male and female ([[passage/quran-49-13|49:13]]); and Qatada (8673): al-Hasan used to say it is firm. Others said it is abrogated: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab (8674, through Ibn Bashshar and Ibn al-Muthanna from Ibn Abi Adi from Sa'id from Qatada): this verse was a division before the shares of inheritance, and when God sent down the shares to their holders the bequest was made for kinsfolk who grieve but do not inherit; asked about it (8675, through Qurra ibn Khalid from Qatada) he said it is abrogated; the same (8676); Abu Malik (8677, 8678): the verse of inheritance abrogated it; Ibn Abbas (8679, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, uncle, father and grandfather): that was before the shares were sent down, and afterwards God sent down the shares and gave each right-holder his right, and charity was placed in what the deceased named; al-Dahhak (8680): the inheritance rules abrogated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 7} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 10}

**The view that it is binding and the division is the testator's division by bequest (pp. 10-12, reports 8681-8686).** Others said it is binding and not abrogated, but the division meant is the dead man's division of his wealth by bequest to those he wished to bequeath to. Al-Qasim ibn Muhammad (8681, through Sa'id ibn Yahya al-Umawi from Ibn al-Mubarak from Ibn Jurayj from Ibn Abi Mulayka): Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman divided his father's inheritance while Aisha was alive and left no one in the house without giving him something, and recited this verse; al-Qasim said: I told Ibn Abbas, who said he was not right, this is the bequest, in which the dead man intends to bequeath to his kin. In the editor's note, Sa'id ibn Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Umawi has an entry at report 2255 and elsewhere and the printing swapped the order in his name, the manuscript being right; Abd Allah ibn Abd al-Rahman is the son of Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Bakr al-Siddiq and the son of the sister of Umm Salama, wife of the Messenger of God. The same (8682, through Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn Jurayj); Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab (8683, through Imran ibn Musa al-Saffar from Abd al-Warith ibn Sa'id from Dawud): he was commanded to bequeath a third to his kin (the editor says Imran ibn Musa al-Saffar has an entry at report 2154 but is described in al-Tahdhib and Ibn Abi Hatim as al-Qazzaz, a discrepancy that should be noted); Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab (8684): this is only at the bequest from his third; again (8685): it is the bequest of people; Ibn Zayd (8686, through Yunus from Ibn Wahb): the division is the bequest, for when a man made a bequest they said "so-and-so is dividing his wealth", so the command means bequeath to them, and to the one who bequeaths say kind words, and if you do not bequeath to them say something good to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 11}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 12-13).** Abu Jafar says the soundest view is that the verse is binding and not abrogated, and that what is meant is a bequest to the testator's kin and kind words said to the orphans and the poor. His argument is that he showed in several places in his book that no ruling of God set down in His book or given on the tongue of His Messenger may be called abrogating or abrogated unless the two rulings contradict each other so that they cannot both hold at one time in any way, while it is impermissible to turn it into something other than abrogation, or unless a proof that one abrogates and the other is abrogated is established; and the verse can mean that when kin, orphans and poor are present at the division of the wealth of one who divides it by bequest, they should be provided for from it, that is, bequeath to those of your kin who do not inherit from you and say kind words to the orphans and the poor, as in another place of the Book, the verse on bequests to parents and near kin ([[passage/quran-2-180|2:180]]); so it is not abrogated by the inheritance verse and no one may turn it to abrogation, since there is no proof of it from the Book or an established sunna and the verse admits the interpretation given. So in his view the verse means the division by the testator of his wealth by bequest, with his kin present, and the orphans and poor, that is, bequeath to the kin out of your wealth, and a prayer for good is made for the other two, as Ibn Abbas and the others he mentioned said. In the editor's notes are references back to earlier places in the volumes, a wrong preposition in both witnesses in one place, and the observation that the long sentence has a connecting clause between its condition and result. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 12} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 13}

**Those who said abrogated, or binding with the heirs as the ones addressed (pp. 13-18, reports 8687-8706, with some lost to the scan).** Al-Tabari says that those who said it is abrogated by the verse of inheritance and those who said it is binding and the heirs of the dead man are the ones addressed took the sense as: give them from it and speak kindly to them. Ibn Abbas (8687, through al-Muthanna from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha): God commanded the believers at the division of their inheritances to join their kin and orphans from the bequest, if he bequeathed, and if there was no bequest, to give them something from their inheritances; Ibn Abbas (8688): at the division of the inheritance; Hisham ibn Urwa (8689): his father gave him from the inheritance of al-Mus'ab when he divided his wealth; Ibn Sirin (8690, through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hushaym from Awf): they used to give them small gifts at the division (the editor glosses this as a small gift); Abu Musa (8691, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh from Yazid from Sa'id from Matar from al-Hasan from Hittan): he commanded that at the division of an inheritance the kin, orphans, poor and poor neighbours be given something; the same (8692, 8693); al-Ala ibn Badr (8694, through Ibn Humayd from Jarir from Mughira): they gave from it the chest and things one is ashamed to divide (the editor says that the meaning of "chest" here puzzled him and he does not know what the speaker meant); al-Hasan and Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8695): that is at the division of inheritance; Abu al-Aliya and al-Hasan (8696): they give small gifts and speak kindly. Then those who said it is binding and that the division for kin, orphans and poor is obligatory on the heirs differed where one of the heirs is a minor and his guardian divides his inheritance: some said the guardian of the minor's wealth may not give anything from the minor's wealth, nor from his own bequest, since he owns none of it, but says kind words to them, the one commanded to speak kindly being the guardian of the orphan's wealth when he divides it between the orphan and his co-heirs, unless the guardian is himself one of the heirs, in which case he gives them from his own share and from the shares of those whose disposal is valid, but not from the minor's. Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8697, through Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from al-Suddi from Abu Sa'id): if the dead man bequeathed them something it is carried out; if the heirs are adults they give small gifts; if they are minors their guardian says: I do not own this wealth, it is for the minors; and that is the kind word; Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8698, through Ibn Bashshar from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba from Abu Bishr): two guardians, one who inherits and one who does not; the one who inherits gives and the one who does not is spoken to kindly; al-Hasan and Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8699, through Ibn al-Muthanna from Abd al-A'la from Dawud): that is at the division of the inheritance; if the inheritance belongs to one who has come of age he may clothe from it and feed the poor and needy; if it is... and the report is cut off at the end of p. 15. Printed pp. 16-17 are missing from the scan; they hold the rest of report 8699 and reports 8700-8705, and p. 18 begins in the middle of a report in which someone has food prepared and says that without this verse he would have preferred it to be from his own wealth, and then recites it (its chain and opening are on the missing pages). Al-Tabari says that those who followed what Ibn Abbas and Sa'id ibn Jubayr said, and those who said gifts are given at the division of the inheritance to kin, orphans and poor, understood the word for providing as giving, while those who followed what Ubayda and Ibn Sirin said understood it as feeding. They then differed over the kind speech. Some said it is a command from God to the guardians of orphans to say to their kin, the orphans and the poor, when they attend the division of the property of those under their guardianship between them and their co-heirs, that they apologise to them in the way described earlier; Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8706, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Hushaym from Abu Bishr): it is the non-heir who is commanded to speak kindly to them: this wealth belongs to absent people or to small orphans, and you have a right in it, but we cannot give you anything of it; that is the kind speech. Others said the one commanded is the man who bequeaths from his wealth, and the kind speech is praying for them to have provision and wealth and similar good words. In the editor's note, the printing adds after "earlier" a phrase "which spares repeating", as if imitating Abu Jafar's habit of repeating such a sentence; it is not in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 13} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 14} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 15} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 18} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 18}
