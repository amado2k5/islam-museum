## Summary
Verse 3 of [[passage/quran-1|Surat al-Fatiha]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

Its place: verse 3 of 7 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari says that the account of "the Compassionate, the Merciful" has already been given in the interpretation of the basmala and need not be repeated here. He also did not need to explain the repetition, since he does not hold the basmala to be a verse of the Opening of the Book, so no one can ask why the two names are repeated in neighbouring verses. Rather he takes the repetition as an argument against anyone who claims the basmala is a verse of the Fatiha: it would repeat a verse of one wording and one meaning twice with nothing between, and nowhere in the Book of God are there two adjacent verses repeated in one wording and one meaning with no separating speech of a different sense. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 146} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 147}
He then takes up the objection that [[passage/quran-1-2|"praise be to God, Lord of the worlds"]] stands between them. He reports that a group of interpreters denied this and said the order is one of postponement with the sense of precedence, so that the sense is "praise be to God, the Compassionate, the Merciful, Lord of the worlds, Master of the Day of Judgement", arguing that the description by kingship or ownership should sit beside its like, "Lord of the worlds", and that the description by greatness and godhead should sit beside its like in praise, "the Compassionate, the Merciful". They said that advancing and delaying words are too common in Arabic speech to count, and gave a line of Jarir, whose sense is "the phantom came by a brief visit, and where is it from you?", and [[passage/quran-18-1|18:1]], whose sense is that He sent down the Book to His servant, upright, and made no crookedness in it. Al-Tabari sees in this a witness for the view that the basmala is not a verse of the Fatiha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 147} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 148}
In a note the editor Ahmad Shakir says that al-Tabari went to the view that the basmala is not a verse of the Fatiha and that this is not the place to set out the disagreement; he adds that in his own commentary on the Sunan of al-Tirmidhi he has examined the question and set out what he regards as sound proofs that it is a verse of the Fatiha. The museum reports the two positions as given and holds no verdict between them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 148}
