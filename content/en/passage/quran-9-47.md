**Commentary on verse 47 (pp. 278-282)**

Al-Tabari says: had these hypocrites gone out among you, believers, "they would have added to you nothing but trouble", that is they would have added to you by going out among you only corruption and harm, and for that I held them back from going out with you; the meaning of the word for trouble has been explained earlier. "And they would have hurried about among you" means they would have urged their mounts quickly among you; the word comes from the urging of horses and riding beasts, which is speeding them on, and one says of a she-camel that went quickly that she "hurried", and of her rider that he "made her hurry" when he pressed her on, and the rajaz poet Duraid ibn al-Simma uses it in a line in which he wishes he were a young man riding fast in the fighting, a rajaz he said on the day of Hunayn; the editor tells the story, that Malik ibn Awf did not want Duraid to have a say in their war. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 278}

As for the "gaps", the word comes from the openings between people in rows and elsewhere, and from it the Prophet's saying "close up in the rows, so that the devils do not slip through the gaps among you like little black sheep"; the editor explains the black sheep as small black hornless sheep. As for "seeking to cause you sedition", it means they seek for you what will tempt you away from your going out on your campaign by holding you back from it; one says "I sought evil for him" and "I sought good for him" when you seek it for him, and likewise "I tied your loads" and "I milked for you", and when they mean "I helped you in seeking it" they use the other form, "I helped you seek such". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 279}

Report 16771, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "they would have hurried about among you" means among you, seeking sedition for you by that; report 16772 is similar through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id. Report 16773, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: they would hold you back, namely Rifa'a ibn al-Tabut, Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and Aws ibn Qayzi; report 16774, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: they would have hurried, meaning they would hold you back, Abdullah ibn Nabtal, Rifa'a ibn Tabut and Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul; report 16775, from Qatada, is similar. Report 16776, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "had they gone out among you they would have added only trouble", he said these are the hypocrites at the Tabuk campaign, God consoling His prophet and the believers, saying what grieves you? Had they gone out among you they would only have added trouble, saying that a force has been gathered against you and letting you down; "seeking sedition for you" meaning unbelief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 280}

As for "and among you are those who listen to them", the interpreters differed. Some said the sense is that among you are those who listen to your talk to carry it to them, a help to them against you. Report 16777, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: they relate your talk, spies, not hypocrites; report 16778 is similar; report 16779, from Ibn Zayd: they hear what they carry to your enemy. Others said the sense is that among you are those who hear their speech and obey them. Report 16780, from Qatada: among you are those who hear their speech. Report 16781, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: those who asked leave, as it reached me, were men of standing, Abdullah ibn Ubayy ibn Salul and al-Jadd ibn Qays, nobles among their people, and God held them back because He knew that if they went out with them they would spoil his army, in which there were people who loved and obeyed them in what they called them to, for their standing among them, and so He said "and among you are listeners for them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 281}

Al-Tabari says that on this explanation the sense is that among you are people who hear and obey you who, had these accompanied them, they would have corrupted for you by holding them back from marching with you; on the first explanation it means that among you are some of them who listen to your talk about them and carry it to them, spies for them against you. The better of the two explanations in his view is that of those who said the sense is that among you are listeners to your talk about them who convey it from you, spies for them, because the commoner usage of the Arabs in the word for "listener" describes one who listens to speech, as God says elsewhere, at [[passage/quran-5-41|5:41]], "listeners to falsehood", describing people who listen to false talk; when they describe a man as hearing another's speech, command and prohibition, accepting it and abiding by it, they say of him that he is "an obedient hearer" and hardly say "he is a listener, obedient". As for "God knows the wrongdoers", it means God knows who directs his acts to the wrong ends and puts them in the wrong places, who asks the Messenger's leave for an excuse and who asks it from doubt in Islam and hypocrisy, who listens to the believers' talk to tell the hypocrites and who listens to be glad of what gladdens the believers and sad at what saddens them; nothing of the secrets or open acts of His creatures is hidden from Him. The meaning of wrongdoing has been explained in other places in the book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 282}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
