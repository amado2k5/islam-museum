## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 25 to 29 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "He said, 'Indeed, your messenger who was sent to you is mad.'" This messenger of yours, who claims to have been sent to you, is overwhelmed because he speaks words we do not know nor understand. He said this and attributed Moses, the enemy of God, to Paradise, because he and his people believed that there was no god but Him, and that what Moses called him to was falsehood and had no truth. Moses then said in argument against them and their knowledge of their Lord by His attribute and evidence, for the people of Pharaoh believed that what they knew was a lord for them At that time, he was Pharaoh, and what they knew of their ancestors were lords of other kings who had passed before Pharaoh. They did not have Moses telling them something meaningful that they understood but could not understand. Therefore, Pharaoh told them that he was mad, because his words were uncomprehensible to them: 'The one I invite you and Pharaoh to worship, the Lord of the East and the West and everything between them,' meaning the King of the east and the west
