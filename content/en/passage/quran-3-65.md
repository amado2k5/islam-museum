## Summary
Verse 65 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 65 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 65: the dispute over Abraham.** Al-Tabari says the address is to the people of the Torah and the Gospel, asking why they argue and quarrel about Abraham, the Friend of the Merciful. Their dispute was that each group claimed he was one of them and followed their religion. God reproached them for the claim and pointed to its inconsistency: how can you say he followed your creed when your creed is Judaism or Christianity, the Jew holds that his religion is upholding the Torah and acting by it, the Christian that his is upholding the Gospel, and these two books were only sent down some time after Abraham's death? It is also said that the verse came down about the quarrel of Jews and Christians over Abraham. The editor notes that the manuscript has a lapse from the copyist's haste, that the printed text corrected it wrongly, and that the right reading is the one he adopted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 489} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 490}

**Report 7202 (Ibn Abbas).** Al-Tabari gives (Abu Kurayb, from Yunus ibn Bukayr, from Muhammad ibn Ishaq; and Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, client of Zayd ibn Thabit, from Said ibn Jubayr or Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) that the Christians of Najran and the Jewish rabbis gathered before the Prophet and fell into dispute; the rabbis said Abraham was only a Jew and the Christians that he was only a Christian, and the verse came down, God informing them that both scriptures came down only in times after him and that Judaism and Christianity came after him. The editor refers to Ibn Hisham (2: 201-202), abridged, and notes that the preceding report from Ibn Ishaq's biography in al-Tabari is 6782. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 490}

**Reports 7203-7207.** Report 7203 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) has it: on what ground do you contend over him, calling him a Jew or a Christian, when Judaism came after the Torah and Christianity after the Gospel. Others said the verse concerns the Jews' claim that Abraham was one of them. Report 7204 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says the Prophet called the Jews of Medina to the just word, the ones who disputed about Abraham and claimed he died a Jew, and God gave them the lie and removed him from them; report 7205 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdallah ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same. Report 7206 (Muhammad ibn Amr, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid): the Jews and Christians, God acquitting him of them when each community claimed him as its own and joining to him the believers who followed the hanif way; report 7207 (al-Muthanna, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) is the same. The editor notes that the form in report 7206, in both the manuscript and the print, is a slip of the copyist's pen. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 490} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 491}

**The close of verse 65.** Al-Tabari says the rhetorical question that closes the verse means: do you not grasp the error of saying Abraham was a Jew or a Christian when you know Judaism and Christianity arose some time after his death? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 492}
