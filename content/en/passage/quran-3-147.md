## Summary
Verse 147 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 147 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The saying and the word for excess (pp. 271-272).** Al-Tabari says "their saying" is the saying of the ribbiyyun, the pronoun returning to them: when their prophet was killed they had no other word, and held fast only through patience under what struck them, struggle against their enemy, and asking their Lord for forgiveness and victory. Excess, he says, is overstepping in a matter and going beyond its measure; here it means "forgive our small sins, and those in which we went on into the great ones", that is, minor and major sins. The editor notes in a footnote that the phrase is the verse's own wording, that the construction would have been clearer had "their saying" been the predicate of "was" and the clause "that they said" its subject, and refers to pp. 273-274. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 271} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 272}

**Reports on excess (p. 272).** Mujahid from Ibn 'Abbas (7987): our faults. Mujahid (7988): our faults and wronging ourselves. Al-Dahhak (7989, 7990): the great faults, the major sins. Ibn Jurayj from Ibn 'Abbas (7991): our faults. Ibn 'Abbas by the chain of al-'Awfi (7992): our faults. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 272}

**Firm feet and help against the deniers (pp. 272-273).** Al-Tabari: to ask that their feet be made firm is to ask to be among those who stand fast for war with God's enemy and the fight, not among those who are routed, flee and do not hold ground in one place; to ask for help against the deniers is help against those who denied God's oneness and the prophethood of His prophet. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on a like phrase. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 272} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 273}

**Reproach and discipline (p. 273).** Al-Tabari reads the verse as a reproach from God to those who fled the enemy at Uhud and left the fight, and as a discipline: why did you not do, when told your prophet was killed, what the ribbiyyun before you did, namely keep patient, not abase yourselves, and ask for victory, so that God would help you as He helped them; for He loves whoever is patient in His command and in struggle against his enemy. Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq (7993): say as they said, know that what befell you came through your sins, ask forgiveness as they did, keep to your religion and do not turn back on your heels, ask for firm feet and for help; all of this was their saying, though their prophet had been killed, and they did not act as you acted. The editor traces it to Ibn Hisham's Sira (3: 118-119), as a continuation of the reports ending at 7984, and refers to earlier uses of the two words for inheriting (vol. 6, p. 127, note 4). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 273}

**The accusative reading of "saying" (pp. 273-275).** Al-Tabari: the sound reading is the accusative, by the agreement of the readers of the great cities, transmitted widely from the authority. The accusative is preferred because "that" (an) is always definite and so is better as the subject than nouns that are sometimes definite and sometimes indefinite; for that reason the accusative is preferred for any noun that follows "was" when the light "that" comes after it, as in [[passage/quran-29-24|29:24]] and [[passage/quran-6-23|6:23]]. When the noun after "was" and the one after it are both definite, nominative and accusative are alike, as with the words for outcome and evil in [[passage/quran-30-10|30:10]], and he gives both analyses. He then cites a line by an unnamed poet about a routed squadron whose undoing lay in the cowardice of its leader, reported with either case, and says the same would be allowed with "that", though the most fluent speech is what he described. The editor says he does not know the poet; the line is in Sibawayh (1: 24) unattributed, and he quotes al-Shantamari on the equal standing of subject and predicate of "was" when both are definite, noting Thahlan is a mountain. His other footnotes say the printed text added a faulty word that the manuscript corrects, point to al-Farra''s Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 237), and observe that he kept the accusative reading as al-Tabari gives it for the other verse, while his own mushaf and recitation read it in the nominative. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 273} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 274} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 275}
