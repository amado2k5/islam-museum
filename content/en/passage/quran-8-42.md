## Summary
Verse 42 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 42 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 42 (pp. 563-569)**

Al-Tabari says on "when you were on the near side of the valley and they on the far side, and the caravan below you" [[passage/quran-8-42|8:42]]: be certain, believers, that the division of spoils is as your Lord has set out for you, if you believe in God and in what He sent down to His servant on the day of Badr, when He distinguished truth from falsehood by helping His Messenger; "when you" then were "on the near side", meaning on the edge of the valley nearer Medina, "and they on the far side", meaning your polytheist enemy was camped on the edge of the valley farther toward Mecca, "and the caravan below you", meaning the caravan, with Abu Sufyan and his men in it, was in a place below you toward the seashore. The editor explains the "edge of the valley" as its side from above, its limit and rim. The interpreters spoke likewise; report 16139, from Qatada: the edge of the nearer valley, and they on the edge of the farther valley, and the caravan below them, Abu Sufyan and his men. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 563}

Report 16140, from Qatada: both are the edge of the valley, God's Prophet being at the top of the valley and the polytheists at the bottom, and the caravan meaning Abu Sufyan went down with the caravan until he reached Mecca, the editor saying the words in brackets are the printed edition's publisher's and he does not know what they are, and that the manuscript gives no reading he can trust; report 16141, from Ibn Ishaq: "on the near side" of the valley toward Mecca, "the caravan below you" meaning Abu Sufyan's caravan which you went out to take and they went out to defend, with no appointment between you and them, as in the Sira; report 16142, from Mujahid: Abu Sufyan and his men were coming from Syria as traders, unaware of the Companions at Badr, and Muhammad was unaware of the unbelievers of Quraysh and Quraysh of Muhammad and his Companions, until they met at the water of Badr, where the one who waters for them all came, and they fought, and Muhammad's Companions overcame them and took them captive; reports 16143 and 16144, from Mujahid, are like it. The editor notes that the manuscript repeats "they fought" and that he is in doubt about the whole sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 563} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 564}

Report 16145, from al-Suddi: he mentioned the camps of the people and the caravan, "the caravan" being Abu Sufyan, "below you" on the seashore. The reciters differed in reading "on the side": most reciters of Medina and Kufa read it with a damma on the first letter, some of Mecca and Basra with a kasra. Abu Ja'far said: they are two well-known dialect forms of one sense, so whichever the reciter reads, he is right; he cites a line of al-Ra'i and a line of Aws ibn Hajar with the kasra, and the editor says he did not find the first line elsewhere and that Aws's line is from his elegy for Fudala ibn Kalada al-Asadi and has a better variant in the transmission. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 565}

Then he says on "had you made an appointment you would have differed over the appointment; but it was that God might accomplish a matter that was to be done": meaning, had your gathering in the place where you gathered, you believers and your polytheist enemy, been by appointment between you and them, you would have differed over the appointment because of your enemy's many numbers and your few, but God brought you together without an appointment between you and them "that God might accomplish a matter that was to be done", and that accomplishing was His giving victory to His friends of those who believe in God and His Messenger and the destruction of His enemies and theirs at Badr by killing and capture. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "appointment" and "accomplishing". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 565} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 566}

Report 16146, from Ibn Ishaq: had it been by appointment between you and them and the news of their many numbers and your few reached you, you would not have met them, but that God might accomplish what He wished by His power of making Islam and its people mighty and humbling polytheism and its people, without any consultation among you, so He did what He wished of that by His kindness; the editor corrects "without consultation" from the manuscript and the manuscripts of Ibn Hisham, explains it as without taking counsel and gathering, and calls the printed edition's changes tampering. Report 16147, from Ka'b ibn Malik on the campaign of Badr: the Messenger of God and the Muslims went out wanting the caravan of Quraysh until God brought them together with their enemy without an appointment; the editor says it is part of Ka'b's long report on the campaign of Tabuk and his staying behind until God accepted his repentance, given by Ahmad, al-Bukhari and Muslim. Report 16148, from Umayr ibn Ishaq: Abu Sufyan came with the caravan from Syria and Abu Jahl went out to keep him from the Messenger of God and his Companions, and they met at Badr, neither party aware of the other, until the water-carriers met, and the people rose against each other, meaning they rose to fight. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 566} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 567}

Then he says on "that he who perished might perish by clear proof, and he who lived might live by clear proof; God is hearing, knowing": but God brought them together there to accomplish a matter that was to be done, that whoever perished might perish by clear proof; the "that" in "might perish" repeats the "that" in "accomplish"; and by "that he who perished might perish by clear proof" He means that whoever of His creatures dies may die with a proof of God established against him that cut off his excuse and a lesson he saw with his eyes, and "he who lived might live by clear proof" means that whoever of them lives may live with a proof of God established for him and plain to his eyes, which he knew. Report 16149, from Ibn Ishaq: that whoever disbelieved might disbelieve after the proof, because of the sign and lesson he saw, and whoever believed might believe on the like, the addition in brackets being from Ibn Hisham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 568}

As for "God is hearing, knowing", its meaning is that God, believers, hears what you say and what others say, when He shows His prophet in his sleep, and shows you, your enemy as few in your eyes though they are many, and shows you to your enemy as few in theirs, and knows what your souls hide and your hearts hold then and at all times; so fear your Lord, people, in your speech lest you speak what is not true and in your hearts lest you believe in them other than right guidance, for nothing hidden, outward or inward, is concealed from God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 569}
