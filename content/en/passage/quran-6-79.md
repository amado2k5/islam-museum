## Summary
Verse 79 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 79 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "I have turned my face to him who originated the heavens and the earth, upright, and I am not of the polytheists" he says: this is God's report of his friend Abraham, that when the truth became plain to him and he knew it, he bore the witness of truth and made plain his difference from his people, the people of falsehood and of associating others with God, and the blame of no blamer held him back for God's sake, and he was not daunted by saying the truth and standing firm in it though all his people differed from his word and denied it to him; he said to them "my people, I am free of what you associate" with God, who created me and you, in worshipping him, of your gods and idols; I have turned my face in my worship to him who created the heavens and the earth, the lasting one, who remains and does not perish, gives life and death, not to one who perishes and does not remain, who passes and does not last and neither harms nor benefits. Then he told them that his turning his face to his worship, by making worship pure for him and being upright in that to his Lord as he loves of oneness, was not in the way that one turns his face who is not upright but an associator with him, since turning the face other than in uprightness does not profit the one who turns it but harms and destroys him; "and I am not of the polytheists" means I am not of you, that is, not of those who follow your religion and keep your creed, polytheists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 487}

The editor refers for "originated" and "upright" to earlier passages and says the printed text has a different wording for "since turning the face was not on uprightness" and the manuscript another, the right being the one he set.

Ibn Zayd said the like (13465 bis), on the saying of Abraham's people to him "have you left the worship of these?" and his saying "I have turned my face to him who originated the heavens and the earth"; they said you have brought nothing, we worship him and turn towards him, and he said no, "upright", he said sincerely, not associating him as you associate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 488}

Verse 79 closes here on p. 488, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-80|6:80]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
