**Commentary on verse 20 (pp. 8-15)**

Al-Tabari says: by "they sold him" is meant that Joseph's brothers sold him, whereas when speaking of buying one says "I bought it"; he cites a line of Ibn Mufarrigh al-Himyari in which he says he sold a slave of his called Burd. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "buying" and of the line. In a footnote he gives the colophon of the twelfth part of the manuscript, completed in Ramadan of the year 715. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 8}

Report 18899, from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, through Ya'qub, Ibrahim, Hushaym, Mughira and Abu Ma'shar: he disliked buying and selling for the bedouin, and said the Arabs say "ishri li kadha" meaning "sell me such a thing"; he recited the verse, meaning they sold him, and his sale was forbidden. Reports 18900 to 18904, from Mujahid: Joseph's brothers were eleven men, and they sold him when the bucket-lowerer drew him up, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; Ishaq and Abd Allah; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj. Report 18905, from Ibn Abbas: "he was sold among them"; reports 18906 and 18907, from al-Dahhak, through Hushaym and Juwaybir: they sold him; report 18908, from Ibn Abbas: his brothers sold him for a paltry price. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 9}

Others said the caravan is meant, that they sold Joseph for a paltry price; report 18909, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: they are the caravan who sold him. Al-Tabari said: the sounder of the two views is that of the one who said the meaning is "Joseph's brothers sold Joseph for a paltry price". God reported of those who bought him that they hid his purchase from their companions for fear they would ask for a share, claiming he was merchandise; they said that only out of eagerness that he be theirs alone and thinking his price cheap, for they bought him, as He says, "for a paltry price". Had his buyers held him of little account, their telling their companions "he is merchandise" would have no meaning, nor would their buying him while holding him of little account, unless their minds were overpowered; for a sane person cannot buy what he holds of little account without being forced, and then lie about it to people. Rather that is the speech of one who is stingy with his goods because they are precious to him and because of the fine price and profit he hopes for. The editor notes that the printed text mis-vowelled the wording and the manuscript has the right one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 10}

As for "bakhs", it means a shortfall, a verbal noun from "I wronged so-and-so of his due", when one wrongs him and diminishes what is owed him, and from it comes "do not diminish people's things" [[passage/quran-11-85|11:85]]; what is meant is "for a diminished, reduced price", the verbal noun standing in the place of the passive participle, as "false blood" stands for "blood lied about". Then the interpreters differed on its meaning. Some said it was called "paltry" because it was forbidden to them; reports 18910 to 18914, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', al-Muharibi and Juwaybir; al-Hasan ibn Muhammad and Ali ibn Asim; al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman; al-Muthanna, Amr ibn Awn and Hushaym; and al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hushaym: "bakhs" is the forbidden, his price was forbidden and not lawful for them to eat, his sale was forbidden and his purchase forbidden. The editor refers to the explanation of "bakhs", and notes that the printed text dropped a whole line of the manuscript and ran reports 18911 and 18912 into one chain, which he restored to its origin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 11}

Report 18915, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: it was not lawful for them to eat his price. Others said that "bakhs" here means wrong; reports 18916 and 18917, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, and Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "bakhs" is wrong, and Joseph's sale and price were forbidden to them. Others said "bakhs" means little; reports 18918 and 18919, from Ikrima, through Ibn Waki', Yahya ibn Adam, Qays and Jabir, and through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays and Jabir. Al-Tabari said: we have already explained the sound view on this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 12}

As for "a few counted dirhams" he means they sold him for dirhams not weighed, short and incomplete, because of their disregard for him. It is also said it is called "counted" to show it was less than forty, because at that time they did not weigh anything under forty dirhams, their smallest weight being the ounce, which weighed forty dirhams, so counting indicates the smallness of the sum. Some said it was twenty dirhams; reports 18920 to 18924, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and then Nawf al-Bikali, through Ibn Waki', Humayd ibn Abd al-Rahman, Zuhayr, Abu Ishaq and Abu Ubayda; al-Muthanna, al-Himmani and Sharik; Ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman and Sufyan; and Abu Kurayb and Waki': what Joseph was bought for was twenty dirhams. The editor notes that "Nawf al-Shami" is Nawf ibn Fadala al-Bikali himself, which the printed text altered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 13}

Report 18925, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: twenty dirhams; report 18926, from al-Suddi: it was twenty dirhams; reports 18927 and 18928, from Qatada: we were told he was sold for twenty dirhams; report 18929, from Atiyya, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad and Abu Idris: the dirhams were twenty, which they divided two each. Others said it was twenty-two dirhams, each of the eleven brothers taking two; report 18930, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Asbat, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih, and report 18931 from him through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 14}

Reports 18932, 18933 and 18934, from Mujahid, are similar, and the editor notes that the addition "and the brothers were" in report 18931 is necessary and dropped out through the copyist. Others said it was forty dirhams; report 18935, from Ikrima, through al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz, Qays and Jabir, and report 18936, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: they sold him and the price they sold him for did not reach an ounce; people used to trade in ounces then, and anything short of an ounce is a count. Al-Tabari said: the right view is that God reported they sold him for counted dirhams, not weighed, and did not fix the sum by weight or number, nor put any indication of it in a book or a report from the Messenger; it may have been twenty, twenty-two, forty, less or more, and whichever it was, it was counted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 15}

Al-Tabari said: knowing the weight of that sum brings no benefit in religion, and ignorance of it brings no harm; belief in the plain sense of the revelation is required, and anything beyond it we are relieved of the burden of knowing. The editor notes that this is one of "Abu Ja'far's scales", which he stated in his book without repeating them at every place: his rule between himself and those who claim that he leaned in his commentary toward believing much of what he cited, for which there was no sound proof from a report of the Messenger or a proof of reason that must be accepted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 16}

On "and they held him of little account" he says: Joseph's brothers held him of little account, not knowing his honour with God nor his rank with Him, and still wished to come between him and his father, so that his face would be free for them and they could cut him off from nearness to him, and the benefits that went to Joseph rather than to them would go to them. Report 18937, from al-Dahhak, through Ibn Waki', Amr ibn Muhammad, Abu Marzuq and Juwaybir: they did not know of his prophethood and his rank with God. Report 18938, from him on the verse: the caravan halted at the well, they sent their waterdrawer, he drew water and brought Joseph out, and they rejoiced that they had found a boy, not knowing his knowledge or his rank with his Lord, so they held him cheap and sold him; his sale was forbidden, and they sold him for a few counted dirhams. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 16}

Reports 18939, from al-Dahhak, and 18940, from Ibn Jurayj: it was his brothers who held him cheap and did not know his rank with God, his prophethood and his standing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 17}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
