## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari explains "the fools among the people will say" as the ignorant, namely the Jews and the hypocrites, and holds that God called them fools because they were foolish toward the truth: the Jewish rabbis feigned ignorance and their ignorant ones grew arrogant about following Muhammad because he was an Arab and not of the Children of Israel, while the hypocrites were bewildered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 129} He then reports from Mujahid, al-Bara' ibn 'Azib and Ibn 'Abbas that they were the Jews, from al-Bara' in another report that they were the People of the Book, and from al-Suddi that the verse was revealed about the hypocrites. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 130}

On "what has turned them from their qibla which they were on" he explains that "ma wallahum" means what thing turned them away, and that the qibla of anything is what faces it. The sense, for him, is that the fools will say to the believers, when they turn their faces from the direction that was theirs before the command to face the Sacred Mosque: what turned their faces? God informed His Prophet of what they would say and taught him the answer: "to God belong the east and the west; He guides whom He wills to a straight path". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 131}

He then presents the disagreement of the scholars over how long the Prophet prayed toward Jerusalem after the migration. From Ibn 'Abbas, the qibla was turned in Rajab, seventeen months after his arrival in Medina, and a group of Jews, named in the report, came to him and said: what turned you from your qibla while you claim to be on the creed of Abraham? Go back to it and we will follow and believe you; they wanted to tempt him away from his religion, and the verse was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 132} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 133} From al-Bara' come several reports: seventeen months; sixteen or seventeen (the narrator unsure); and the story of a man who passed people at prayer and told them the qibla had changed, so they turned while still praying, and that the Jews had liked his praying toward Jerusalem and objected when he turned to the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 133} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 134} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, sixteen months, then he was directed to the Ka'ba two months before Badr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 134} The editor comments on the chains of some of these reports and notes that Ibn Majah has it as eighteen months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 133}

On the duration of prayer toward Jerusalem al-Tabari gives further reports: from Anas ibn Malik, nine or ten months, with the Prophet turning to the Ka'ba while leading the noon prayer in Medina after two units, whereupon the fools said "what has turned them from their qibla"; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 135} from Mu'adh ibn Jabal, thirteen months; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 136} and from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab, that the Ansar prayed toward the first qibla three years before his arrival and that he prayed toward it sixteen months after arriving. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 137} The editor comments on the chains of these reports and their differing wording, and cites Ibn Kathir that the Jerusalem direction lasted some months over a year and that the Prophet often prayed to be directed to the Ka'ba, the qibla of Abraham. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 135} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 136} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 137}

He then presents the disagreement over why the Prophet prayed toward Jerusalem before facing the Ka'ba was made obligatory. Some said it was his own choice: from 'Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri, the qibla was the first thing of the Qur'an to be abrogated, and he faced the rock of Jerusalem for seventeen months so that the Jews might believe in him and follow him and so that he would call the unlettered Arabs by it; from Abu al-'Aliya, he was given the choice to face wherever he wished and chose Jerusalem to win over the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 138} Others said it was by God's obligation: from Ibn 'Abbas, when he migrated and most of Medina were Jews, God commanded him to face Jerusalem and the Jews were glad; he loved the qibla of Abraham and would pray looking at the sky, and "We see the turning of your face toward the sky" was revealed, at which the Jews doubted and said "what has turned them from their qibla". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 138} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 139} From Ibn Jurayj, he first prayed toward the Ka'ba, then was turned to Jerusalem, then God turned him back to the Ka'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 139}

On why they said "what has turned them", he reports two views from Ibn 'Abbas: that a group of Jews said to the Prophet to go back to his qibla and they would follow and believe him, wanting to tempt him from his religion; and the other is the earlier report of 'Ali ibn Abi Talha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 139}

From Qatada, the Ansar prayed toward Jerusalem two years before the Prophet's arrival, and he prayed toward it sixteen months after arriving before God turned him to the Ka'ba; some people then said "what has turned them from their qibla", the man longs for his birthplace, and "say: to God belong the east and the west" was revealed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 139} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 140} It is also said the speakers were the hypocrites, mocking Islam; from al-Suddi, when the Prophet was directed to the Sacred Mosque people split into groups, and the hypocrites asked why they had been on one qibla for a time and then abandoned it, so the verse was revealed about them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 140}

On "say: to God belong the east and the west; He guides whom He wills to a straight path", al-Tabari says: say to those who asked you and your companions what turned you from your qibla, that to God belongs what lies between the two ends of the sun's rising and setting and all between; He guides whom He wills of His creation, directing and enabling him to the sound road, which for him is the qibla of Abraham, whom He made a leader for people, and abandons whom He wills so that he strays from the way of truth. The sense is that God guided us by facing the Sacred Mosque, the qibla of Abraham, and left you, Jews, hypocrites and the company of polytheists, to be abandoned from what He guided us to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 140} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 141}
