## Summary
Verse 48 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 48 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Forgiveness and what lies short of associating partners (pp. 448-449, notes 1-2 on p. 448 and note 1 on p. 449).** Al-Tabari says God does not forgive the association of partners with Him, nor disbelief, and forgives what is lesser than that to those on whom His will falls among the people of sins and wrongdoing, and that this is linked to the call to the People of the Book to believe. He then treats the grammar of the clause about associating others with God: it is in the accusative as the object of the verb of forgiving, or by the dropping of the governing preposition, on the sense that God forgives no sin that goes with shirk or comes from shirk; some grammarians put it in the genitive. In his note the editor says al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1:272) has a different wording, that the right reading is the one in the commentary, and that this is al-Farra's statement. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 449}

**The reported occasion of revelation (pp. 449-450, reports 9730-9731, notes 3-4).** Al-Tabari mentions that the verse came down about people who were uneasy about the fate of the polytheists when the verse addressed to God's servants who had transgressed against themselves came down ([[passage/quran-39-53|39:53]]). Then al-Muthanna (9730, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Ja'far, from his father, from al-Rabi', from Mujabbir) and Ammar (9731, by a close chain) report from Abdullah b. Umar that when that verse came down a man stood and said: and shirk, Prophet of God? The Prophet disliked that and recited what is in this verse ([[passage/quran-4-48|4:48]]). The editor notes that Ibn Abi Ja'far is Abdullah b. Abi Ja'far al-Razi and al-Rabi' is Ibn Anas al-Bakri; that Mujabbir is a nickname, the man being Abd al-Rahman b. Abd al-Rahman al-Asghar b. Umar b. al-Khattab, with sources for his life (Mus'ab's Nasab Quraysh p. 356, Ibn Hazm's Jamhara p. 146, al-Dhahabi's al-Mushtabih p. 462, al-Ta'jil pp. 392-393); that he has a report in the Musnad (1402) from Uthman and Talha which the editor thinks broken because his generation was too young to have met them; and that he is mentioned in the Muwatta' (p. 397) in a case of a man of Ibn Umar's family called al-Jabr who had left Mina without shaving or trimming out of ignorance, whom Abdullah b. Umar told to go back and shave or trim. The editor adds that he found no other biography, that this is a tabi'i whose person is known and not criticised, so his report is at least hasan; that Ibn Kathir (2:481) copied the report from this place and said Ibn Mardawayh narrated it by several routes from Ibn Umar; that al-Suyuti (1:169) also credits Ibn Abi Hatim; and that the next report is weak because al-Tabari's informant is unnamed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 450}

**Ibn Umar on the people of major sins (pp. 450-451, report 9732, note 1).** Muhammad b. Khalaf al-Asqalani (from Adam, from al-Haytham b. Jammaz, from Bakr b. Abdullah al-Muzani) reports from Ibn Umar that they, the Companions, had no doubt about the killer, the devourer of an orphan's property, the false witness and the cutter of kinship until this verse came down, and they held back from testifying against them. The editor notes that Adam is Ibn Abi Iyas al-Asqalani; that al-Haytham b. Jammaz al-Bakka' al-Hanafi al-Basri, the judge, is weak, weakened by Ahmad, Ibn Ma'in, al-Nasa'i and others, and that the manuscript and print read "Hammad" for Jammaz, a corruption; that Bakr b. Abdullah al-Muzani is a trustworthy tabi'i used by the compilers of the standard books; that al-Suyuti also credits the report to Ibn Abi Hatim and al-Bazzar; and that its sense is confirmed from Ibn Umar by other reports. In al-Durr al-Manthur (2:169) he cites Ibn al-Duraysh, Abu Ya'la, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Adi, by a chain the editor calls sound, that they used to refrain from asking forgiveness for people of major sins until they heard this verse from their Prophet, who said he had kept his supplication in reserve as intercession for the people of major sins of his community, and they then held back from much of what was in their minds and afterwards spoke and hoped. He adds that al-Haythami (Majma' al-Zawa'id) records it, saying Abu Ya'la narrated it and its men are the men of the Sahih save Harb b. Surayj, who is trustworthy, and gives another similar report (10:210-211) from al-Bazzar with a good chain, with further reports of this sense. He also says the manuscript has a gap between the words for the killing and for devouring an orphan's property, with the letter ta above it showing the copy used was unclear, so he adopted what other reports give. After these reports al-Tabari says the verse made clear that everyone guilty of a major sin is subject to God's will, who may pardon him or punish him, provided his major sin is not association of partners with God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 451}

**The meaning of fabricating (p. 451, note 1).** Al-Tabari says whoever associates with God in worship anything of His creation has fabricated a grave offence; he is called a fabricator because he speaks falsehood and lies by denying God's oneness and affirming that God has a partner among His creatures, or a consort or a child; so every liar is a fabricator of his lie. The note refers to the earlier explanation of the verb. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 451}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 451}
