## Summary
Verse 4 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 4 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

Verse 4 of sura al-Ma'ida opens with the companions' question about what has been made lawful for them. Al-Tabari says it means: your companions ask you, Muhammad, what has been made lawful for them to eat of foods and meals; say to them: the good things are lawful to you, the lawful that your Lord permitted you to eat of slaughtered animals, and also lawful to you is the game of what you have trained of the jawarih, the earners among the predators of beasts and birds. They were called jawarih for wounding game on behalf of their owners and earning them their food from the hunt; one says 'so-and-so earned (jarah) good for his family' when he earned it for them, 'so-and-so is his family's earner (jarihah)', and 'she has no earner' when no one provides for her. Al-Tabari cites a line of al-A'sha that uses 'ajtarah' in the sense of earning, and the editor explains the lines in a footnote: a satire likened to a brand that cooks the skin and leaves a lasting mark, reminding the offender of what he did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 543}

In 'what you have trained' the word 'game of' is left out, since what is said shows what is omitted. As for the occasion, the people, as we are told, asked the Messenger of God, when he ordered the killing of dogs, which of them they might keep and hunt with, and God sent down this verse, making an exception to what had been forbidden to keep, and ordering the keeping of hunting dogs, herding dogs and farm dogs and permitting them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 545}

Report 11134, from Abu Rafi': Gabriel came to the Prophet asking leave to enter, and he was given it; Gabriel said: we have given you leave, Messenger of God. He said: yes, but we do not enter a house with a dog in it. Abu Rafi' said: he ordered me to kill every dog in Medina, and I killed them until I reached a woman with a dog that barked for her; out of pity I left it, then went to the Messenger and told him, and he told me to go back and kill it, which I did. They came and said: Messenger of God, what is lawful for us of this community you ordered killed? He was silent, and God sent down the verse. The editor notes the chain is weak because of Musa ibn Ubayda al-Rabadhi, and that al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi recorded it briefly by a sounder route. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 545}

Report 11135, from Ikrima: the Prophet sent Abu Rafi' to kill dogs, and he killed until he reached al-Awali; then Asim ibn Adi, Sa'd ibn Khaythama and Uwaym ibn Sa'ida came in and asked: what is lawful for us, Messenger of God? and the verse came down. Report 11136, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: when the Prophet ordered the killing of dogs they asked what was lawful for them of this community, and the verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 546}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over the jawarih God meant. Some said it is every animal or bird taught to hunt and that learned it. Report 11137, from al-Hasan: everything taught that hunts, dog, falcon, cheetah or other; report 11138, from him, is similar. Report 11139, from Mujahid on hunting with a cheetah: it is among the jawarih. Reports 11140 to 11144, from Mujahid: birds and dogs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 547}

Reports 11145 and 11146, from Khaythama ibn Abd al-Rahman: the falcon and the hawk, and in the second the dog also, are among the jawarih. Report 11147, from Ali ibn Husayn: the hawk and the falcon are of the jawarih; report 11148, from Abu Ja'far, is the same. Report 11149, from Ibn Abbas: the jawarih are the trained hunting dogs, cheetahs, falcons and the like. Report 11150, from Tawus: from dogs and others such as falcons and hawks and the like that can be taught. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 548}

Report 11151, from Ibn Abbas: the jawarih are trained dogs and falcons; report 11152, from Ubayd ibn Umayr: dogs and birds. Others said God meant by jawarih the dogs alone among predators; report 11153, from al-Dahhak: they are dogs; report 11154, from al-Suddi: lawful to you is the game of the dogs you have trained; report 11155, from Ibn Umar: as for what hunts among birds, and the hawks among birds, what you catch alive is yours, and otherwise do not eat it. Al-Tabari then weighs the two views: the sounder reading of the verse is that everything that hunts, bird or beast of prey, is among the jawarih, and the game of all of them is lawful if they hunt after training. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 549}

His reason is that God made the term general, covering every jarih without singling any out, so whatever jarih fits the description God gave, whether bird or beast of prey, its game may be eaten. A report from the Prophet agrees with this: report 11156, from Adi ibn Hatim: I asked the Messenger of God about the game of the hawk and he said: eat what it holds for you. In a footnote the editor says that Isa ibn Yunus's report from Mujalid belongs to the early narrations before Mujalid's memory changed, so al-Tabari seems to have judged it sound and relied on it; that Muslim and al-Bukhari report it for the dog without mention of the hawk; and that al-Bayhaqi observed that only Mujalid brought the hawk into it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 550}

So the Prophet allowed the hawk's game and counted it among the jawarih, which clearly refutes those who say God meant dogs only. If someone thinks 'mukallibin' (as dog-keepers) shows the jawarih are dogs alone, he thinks wrongly, because the verse means: say, the good things are lawful to you, people, while you are dog-keepers, and the game of what you have trained of the earners among beasts and birds; 'mukallibin' describes the hunter, even if at times he hunts without dogs. It is like someone telling a group that the good things and what they trained of the jawarih are lawful to them as dog-keepers and believers: he plainly means they are lawful while they are believers, not that game is lawful only when taken by dogs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 551}

Then 'you teach them of what God has taught you'. Al-Tabari says it means: you discipline the jawarih and teach them to seek game for you, from the discipline with which God disciplined you and the knowledge He taught you. Some interpreters said 'of what God taught you' means 'as God taught you'; report 11157, from al-Suddi: you teach them hunting as God taught you. Al-Tabari answers that we know no use in Arabic of 'min' for the comparative 'ka', since 'min' is partitive and 'ka' is for likeness, and one particle stands for another only when their senses are close; God's Book is the language most to be kept from what departs from the understood and the eloquent. The editor judges that some of al-Tabari's words have dropped out just before this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 552}

Report 11158, from Adi ibn Hatim al-Ta'i: a man came to the Messenger of God asking about game taken by dogs, and he did not know what to say until the verse 'you teach them' was revealed; the editor says it is weak because of Abu Hani'. Al-Tabari then notes that the interpreters differed over what training means. Some said it is that the animal is urged on to seek game when its owner sends it, holds the catch for him without eating from it, answers when called and does not run from him; when this has happened repeatedly it is trained. This is the view of a group of Hijaz scholars and some Iraqis. In a footnote the editor examines how the word for urging a dog on is used by the lexicographers and by al-Shafi'i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 553}

Report 11159, from Ata: anything your hunter kills before it is trained, holds game and hunts is carrion, and its killing is not slaughter until it is trained and holds and hunts; if it is so and then kills, that is its slaughter. Report 11160, from Ibn Abbas: a trained dog is one that holds its catch and does not eat from it until the owner comes; if it eats before he comes and he can still slaughter, do not eat its catch. Report 11161, from Ibn Abbas: if the dog eats, do not eat, for it held the game for itself. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 554}

Report 11162, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr on Ibn Abbas: if a man sends his dog and it eats from the catch, it has spoiled it, even if he named God when he sent it, for it held it for itself; God says 'you teach them of what God taught you', so if it eats before its owner comes it is not trained and should be beaten and taught until it leaves that habit. Report 11163, from Ibn Abbas: if the dog seizes, kills and eats, it is a beast of prey. Report 11164, from him: do not eat of it, for had it been trained it would not eat; it held for itself, not for you; report 11165 is similar; report 11166, from Ibrahim on Ibn Abbas: if the dog eats, do not eat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 555}

We stop here at the foot of p. 555, after report 11166, in the commentary on verse 4 of sura al-Ma'ida, at the sign of a trained dog when it eats from its catch, and will continue from p. 556. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 555}
