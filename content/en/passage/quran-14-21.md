**Commentary on verse 21 (pp. 557-559)**

while most of the readers of Kufa read it "khaliqu", in the active participle form. They are two widespread readings, each read by leading readers, close in meaning, so whichever the reader reads he is correct. Al-Tabari says on "they will all come forth before God, and the weak will say to those who were arrogant: we were your followers; can you avail us anything against God's punishment? They will say: had God guided us we would have guided you; it is the same for us whether we are impatient or patient, we have no way of escape": by "they will all come forth before God" He means that these who disbelieved in Him appeared on the Day of Resurrection from their graves and came to open ground, "all", meaning all of them; "and the weak will say to those who were arrogant", he says, the followers among them will say to those they followed, who were arrogant in the world about devoting worship to God and following the messengers sent to them, "we were your followers" in the world. "Tabi'" is the plural of "tabi'", as "ghayb" is the plural of "ghayib". By their saying "we were your followers" they meant only that they were their followers in the world, obeying what they ordered them to do of worshipping idols and disbelieving in God {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 557}

and desisting from what they forbade them of following God's messengers; "can you avail us anything against God's punishment?", they mean, can you today repel from us anything of God's punishment? Ibn Jurayj used to say much the same: report 20638, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "the weak said", he said, the followers, "to those who were arrogant", he said, to the leaders. On "had God guided us we would have guided you" He says, mighty be His mention: the leaders in disbelief in God said to their followers, "had God guided us", they mean had God made clear to us something by which to repel His punishment here today, "we would have guided you", we would have made it clear to you so that you could repel the punishment from yourselves; but we were impatient at the punishment, and our impatience at it did not help us nor did our patience with it; "it is the same for us whether we are impatient or patient, we have no way of escape", they mean they have no place of flight to flee to. One says "hasa 'an kadha" when one shrinks from it, "yahisu hayṣan wa-huyusan wa-hayasanan". Report 20639, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, through al-Muthanna, Suwayd ibn Nasr, Ibn al-Mubarak, al-Hakam and Umar ibn Abi Layla: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 558}

I heard Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi say, it has reached me, or it was mentioned to me, that the people of the Fire said to one another, people, such torment and trial has come upon you as you see, so come, let us be patient, perhaps patience will help us as the people of the world were patient in obeying God and patience helped them when they were patient. He said, they agreed on patience, and were patient and their patience grew long, then they were impatient and cried out "it is the same for us whether we are impatient or patient, we have no way of escape", that is, no deliverance; in a long note the editor says that al-Hakam al-Makki is unknown and that this is a ruined report. Report 20640, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: "it is the same for us whether we are impatient or patient, we have no way of escape", he said, the people of the Fire said to one another, come, the people of Paradise reached Paradise only by their weeping and supplicating God, so come, let us weep and supplicate God; he said, they wept, and when they saw that it did not help them they said: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 559}
