**Commentary on verse 67 (pp. 144-145)**

On [[passage/quran-10-67|10:67]] al-Tabari says: your Lord, people, to whom worship is owed, is the Lord who made the night for you and set it apart from the day, so that you rest in it from the toil and fatigue of your day and are still from moving about for a living. His words 'and the day for seeing' mean that He made the day seeing; He attributes seeing to the day although one sees in it, but because the meaning is understood in Arab speech He addresses them in their own language. He cites a line of Jarir in which sleeping is attributed to the night, meaning that the poet and his camel were not asleep in it. The editor gives the line's place in Jarir's diwan and in Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an, and says it comes from the long poem with which Jarir answered al-Farazdaq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 144}

Al-Tabari says that the one who does this is your Lord, who created you and what you worship, and not what neither benefits nor harms nor does anything. His words 'in that are signs for a people who hear' mean that in the difference between night and day and between the state of their people in them are signs and proofs that the one owed worship sincerely, without partner, is the one who created night and day and made them differ, making one a rest for creation and the other their livelihood, unlike what creates nothing and does nothing. He says 'a people who hear' because those meant are the ones who hear these proofs, reflect on them, take a lesson and are admonished, and not those who hear with their ears and then turn away from the lessons and admonitions. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 145}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
