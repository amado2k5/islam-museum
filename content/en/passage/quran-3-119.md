## Summary
Verse 119 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 119 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**You love those who do not love you, and you believe in all the books (pp. 148-149).** Al-Tabari says the believers love these disbelievers whom they were forbidden to take as intimates, befriending and keeping ties with them, while they do not love the believers but hide enmity and deceit. The editor notes that print has a word meaning "awaiting", the manuscript an unpointed shape, and the right reading is the one he kept, as the Amiriyya press also judged. Al-Tabari says the word for the Book here has a collective sense, as one says the dirham became plentiful in people's hands, meaning dirhams; so it means all the books: the believers' book, theirs, and the other books God sent down. Since the believers believe in all the books and know that those they were forbidden to take as intimates are disbelievers in all of it, through denying God's covenants with them and altering what is in them of command and prohibition, the believers have more right to enmity, hatred and suspicion toward them than they have toward the believers, even as they reject some books and deny others. The editor sets out the sentence structure in a note on p. 149. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 148} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 149}

- From Ibn Humayd, Salama, Ibn Ishaq, Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad, Ikrima or Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Ibn Abbas (7695): it means your book, theirs and the books that came before, and they disbelieve in your book, so you have more right to hate them than they to hate you. The editor notes the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2:207) and completes the earlier reports, the last of which is 7680. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 149}

**The construction and the Kufan term for "bringing near" (pp. 149-150).** Al-Tabari notes that God said the phrase with the attention particle first, then the pronoun of the addressees, then the plural demonstrative, and did not put the demonstrative first and the pronoun after. The Arabs separate the attention particle and the demonstrative with the pronoun when they mean "bringing near" and the incomplete sense that needs the predicate completed; for example, asked "where are you?" one answers with the particle, the pronoun and then the demonstrative, and they hardly say the demonstrative before the pronoun; this extends to dual and plural, and they sometimes repeat the particle with the demonstrative. They do this only for bringing near; when not, they say other forms, such as "this is he" or "this is you". With plain nouns they likewise say "this is so-and-so, standing" when it is bringing near, the standing in the accusative. They did this with the pronoun to distinguish the incomplete demonstrative that needs completing from the same word as a full noun. He says the verb of loving is the predicate of the bringing near. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 149} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 150}

In the notes to these pages the editor says that the term for bringing near is the Kufans' technical term, which al-Suyuti explained in Hamʿ al-Hawamiʿ. His summary of al-Suyuti: the Kufans held that a demonstrative used for bringing near needs a nominative noun and an accusative predicate, and give examples of an arriving caliph and a rising sun, analysing the demonstrative as bringing near, the nominative as its noun and the accusative as its predicate, since the sense is a report of arriving and rising, and the demonstrative is brought in only to bring them near; dropping it does not disturb the meaning, as dropping the verb "to be" would not. That note, begun on p. 149, ends on p. 150. He further says print has an imperfect with the wrong person-letter, the right form being the one for the Arabs, that in one place the manuscript differs from print and print is better and runs better with the context, and that the error is old in al-Tabari's copies and perhaps is al-Tabari's own, who seems to have shortened the opening of al-Farra's discussion and kept one of the demonstratives al-Farra named while his mind was on the verse's text. He says the conditional particle with a conjunction in both print and manuscript spoils the sentence badly, and that someone added a word in print thinking it clearer, which in his judgment does great harm. His gloss on "predicate of bringing near" is that it stands in the accusative as al-Suyuti showed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 149} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 150}

**The mercy of the believers and the harshness of the disbelievers (7696-7698, pp. 150-151).** Al-Tabari says this verse makes clear the state of the two parties: the mercy and tenderness of the people of faith toward those who differ with them, and the hardness of heart and roughness of the disbelievers toward the people of faith. From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7696): by God, the believer loves the hypocrite, shelters him and has mercy on him, and if the hypocrite could do to the believer what the believer can do to him, he would wipe out their mass. From al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Ibn Jurayj (7697): the believer is better to the hypocrite than the hypocrite to the believer, and he has mercy on him; if the hypocrite had the power over the believer that the believer has over him, he would wipe out their mass. From Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, Mujahid (7698): the verse was revealed about the hypocrites. In a note on p. 151 the editor glosses the first verb as pitying, being concerned for and showing mercy to, and the last phrase as wiping out their mass and bulk, because a great gathering looks dark from afar and the Arabs call the green dark. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 150} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 151}

**Biting the fingertips in rage (7699-7701, pp. 151-152).** Al-Tabari says these people, whom the believers were forbidden to take as intimates, when they meet the believers among the Companions of the Messenger give them with their tongues a show of acceptance, from caution about themselves, and tell them they believed and affirmed what Muhammad brought; and when alone, out of the believers' sight, they bite their fingertips at what they see of the believers' harmony, unity of word and good relations, in rage at their own resentment toward them and grief over having no support to lean on for open enmity and fighting. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of the word for being alone in volume 1 (pp. 298-299) and glosses the word for back as helpers and supporters. He then relates the reports of the interpreters: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 151} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 152}

- From Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id, Qatada (7699): when they meet the believers they say they believed, and all that is in them is fear for their lives and property, so they placate them; when alone they bite out of the rage and dislike they carry in their hearts for what the believers are on; had they found strength they would have been against the believers, and so they are as God described. The editor glosses the word for wind here as strength and victory, and cites a line by Ta'abbata Sharran or al-Sulayk ibn al-Sulaka, which is not reproduced. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 152}
- From Ammar, Ibn Abi Ja'far, his father, al-Rabi (7700): the same, except that he said the rage was from their dislike of what the believers are on, and did not give the part about finding strength and what follows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 152}
- From Abbas ibn Muhammad, Muslim, Yahya ibn Amr ibn Malik al-Nukri, his father (7701): when Abu al-Jawza' recited this verse he said, "They are the Ibadiyya". This saying is given as transmitted, and the museum gives no verdict on it. The editor's note on this report begins on p. 152 with the entry for Abbas ibn Muhammad ibn Hatim al-Dawri, whom the four compilers narrated from, and continues on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 152}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 152 in the commentary on verse 119 and continue from p. 153. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 152}
