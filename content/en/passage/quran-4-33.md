## Summary
Verse 33 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 33 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The meaning of 'mawali' (pp. 269-272, reports 9258-9265).** Al-Tabari says on [[passage/quran-4-33|4:33]] that the meaning is: for all of you, people, We made heirs from cousins, brothers and the rest of the agnates. The Arabs call a cousin a mawla, and he cites a line about a cousin around whom they contended, whom the poet describes as a mischief-maker among people, and a line of al-Fadl b. al-Abbas addressing his cousins, the mawali of his people, not to bring out what lay buried. The editor says he did not know the speaker of the first line, that he did not find it elsewhere though it is in the manuscript, and that he is unsure of some of its words so left it as it stands; he explains the mischief-maker as a man of malice among people, the shameful deeds as disgraces, and 'we contended around him' as defending him against those who contend for him. For al-Fadl's line he refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Quran, al-Kamil, al-Mu'talif wa'l-mukhtalif, Mu'jam al-shu'ara, the Hamasa, al-Sadaqa wa'l-sadiq and the Lisan, and prefers the version that tells them not to dig up what is buried between them, as the better and the closer to the sense of the poem. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 272}

Al-Tabari then gives the interpreters. Abu Kurayb (9258, from Abu Usama, from Idris, from Talha b. Musarrif, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says the mawali are heirs. Al-Muthanna (9259, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya b. Salih, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says they are the agnates, that is the heirs. Ibn Bashshar (9260, from Mu'ammal, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Mujahid) says they are the agnates. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9261, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from Mansur, from Mujahid) says they are the guardians. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9262, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says agnates. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9263, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) says the guardians of the father, brother or brother's son or other agnates; the editor notes the print dropped the 'or' between father and brother, and the manuscript is right. Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9264, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says they are the people of inheritance. Yunus (9265, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says they are the agnates, so called in the Jahiliyya; when the non-Arabs came among the Arabs they found no name for them, and God in [[passage/quran-33-5|33:5]] said that those whose fathers are not known are brothers in religion and mawali, so they were called mawali. He said that today there are two kinds of mawla: one who inherits and is inherited from, the blood relatives, and one who is inherited from but does not inherit, the freedmen. He argued from the mawali Zakariyya feared in [[passage/quran-19-5|19:5]], who are the heirs there; and that what the parents and relatives left means the estate left by his parents and kin. The editor explains that a mawla by emancipation is one freed from slavery, that the noun 'ataqa is a verbal noun like freeing, and that the phrase means the mawali of emancipation unless the copyist dropped a word.

Al-Tabari says (p. 272) the meaning is: for all of you, people, We made agnates who inherit from the estate of his parents and kin.

**The reading of the word for binding (pp. 272-273).** Al-Tabari says the readers differed over reading the next clause of the verse. Some read the simple verb, meaning that the oaths bound the alliance between you and them, the reading of most Kufan readers; others read the form of mutual action, meaning that your oaths and theirs bound each other in alliance. He says both readings are known and widespread among the readers of the Muslim cities, with one meaning. He argues that the word 'oaths' itself shows they are the oaths of both contracting sides, so no indication is needed from the mutual form; the supporters of the mutual form said a contract of alliance exists only between two parties and the wording needs an indication, and overlooked that the word 'oaths' is that indication; some even claimed the first reading needs an implied preposition to make sense, which al-Tabari takes as moving away from what he said. He holds the two close in meaning, though the simple-verb reading is sounder in sense for the reason he gave. He explains binding as joining, tightening and confirming, and the oaths as the covenants which some of them made with others. These are al-Tabari's choices and arguments. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 273}

The editor's notes (pp. 272-273) say the command to give the portion is not in the manuscript or print at this point, so he put it where al-Tabari explains it afterwards; that the print has a word meaning 'relative clause' that is meaningless and he kept the manuscript's word for 'preposition', in the early grammarians' sense; that al-Tabari's phrase about protecting the sentence is obscure to him, perhaps meaning that the preposition guards the sentence from a fault of meaning, adding that above every knower there is a more knowing; and that a muddled phrase was corrected by dropping an extra word and reading 'sufficing', while he smoothed the sentence because the references of the prepositions are tangled.

**The meaning of the allies' portion (pp. 274-275, reports 9266-9269).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the portion God ordered allies to give each other in Islam. Some said it is his portion of the inheritance, since they inherited from each other in the Jahiliyya and God made it binding in Islam, then abrogated it by the fixed shares for blood relatives. Ibn Humayd (9266, from Yahya b. Wadih, from al-Husayn b. Waqid, from Yazid al-Nahwi, from Ikrima and al-Hasan al-Basri) reports that a man would ally himself with a man with no kinship and one inherited from the other, and God abrogated this in the sura of al-Anfal, referring to [[passage/quran-8-75|8:75]]. Ibn Bashshar (9267, from Muhammad b. Jafar, from Shu'ba, from Abu Bishr, from Sa'id b. Jubayr) reports that a man would contract with a man and inherit from him, and that Abu Bakr contracted with a mawla and inherited from him. Al-Muthanna (9268, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from Mu'awiya, from Ali b. Abi Talha, from Ibn Abbas) says whichever of two allies died, the other inherited, so [[passage/quran-33-6|33:6]] came down, which put blood relatives first unless they bequeath something to the allies who contracted, valid out of a third of the dead man's property; that is the recognised thing. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9269, from Yazid, from Sa'id, from Qatada) says a man in the Jahiliyya would ally himself in a formula making his blood and his friend's one, each inheriting and seeking vengeance for the other; Islam gave the ally a sixth of all the property, the heirs then dividing the rest, and this was later abrogated in al-Anfal, [[passage/quran-8-75|8:75]]. The editor explains the formula: if someone kills me, you will seek my blood-price as you would for your kin; one reading of one of its terms is that whoever wastes my honour or blood has wasted yours, or that what I pardon of blood you must pardon, while with another vowel it means what is torn down and is said to be the grave, that I be buried where you are buried, meaning they would never part in life or death; the 'for' in 'seek for me' marks cause. He says all these words confirm the pact, a binding contract obliging both to help in good and ill. He also notes that the two readings of the binding word will appear in the following reports, one way or the other, and that he leaves them as in the manuscript, taking the manuscript where print and manuscript differ, and that he took the full verse from the manuscript; and that the original reads 'some of them' where the context needs 'some of you'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 275}

**Report 9270 (p. 275).** Al-Tabari begins report 9270 with the chain of al-Hasan b. Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, and the chain is cut off at the foot of the page.

**End of report 9270 and reports 9271-9274 (pp. 276-277).** The chain of report 9270 (al-Hasan b. Yahya, from Abd al-Razzaq, from Ma'mar, from Qatada) is completed: a man in the Jahiliyya would ally himself with a man in a formula making their blood one, each inheriting from and avenging the other; when Islam came some of them remained, and they were told to hand the allies a portion of the inheritance, the sixth, which was then abrogated by the inheritance rule of [[passage/quran-8-75|8:75]]. Al-Muthanna (9271, from al-Hajjaj b. al-Minhal, from Hammam b. Yahya, from Qatada) gives the same, with the ally receiving a sixth of all the property and the heirs dividing the rest, then abrogation in al-Anfal so that inheritance went to blood relatives. Ibn Waki (9272, from his father, from Isra'il, from Jabir, from Ikrima) says this was an alliance of the Jahiliyya, in which the man said: you inherit from me and I from you, you help me and I you, you pay blood-money for me and I for you. A report that al-Tabari introduces as 'I was told' (9273, from al-Husayn b. al-Faraj, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman, from al-Dahhak) says a man would attach himself to a man and contract with him, saying that if he died the other would have the like of what one of his sons inherits, and that this is abrogated. In his notes the editor says the 'aql is the blood-money, and 'to pay for him' is to pay what his offence owed; and that the print has 'Ubayd b. Salman', an error frequent in it, the manuscript being right, which he will correct from now on without remark. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9274, from his father, from his uncle, from his father, from his grandfather, from Ibn Abbas) says that in the Jahiliyya a man was attached to a man as his follower; when the man died the inheritance went to his family and kin and the follower was left with nothing, so the command came to give him from the inheritance, and afterwards the abrogating text came. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 277}

**The second view: the pairing in brotherhood (pp. 277-278, reports 9275-9276).** Others said the verse came down about those the Prophet paired in brotherhood among the Emigrants and the Helpers, who inherited from each other by that pairing, which God then abrogated by the fixed shares. Abu Kurayb (9275, from Abu Usama, from Idris b. Yazid, from Talha b. Musarrif, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says that when the Emigrants came to Medina the emigrant inherited from the Helper rather than the Helper's kin, by the brotherhood the Prophet made between them, and when the verse on heirs came down it was abrogated. The editor says al-Bukhari gave it at length in his Sahih (Fath al-Bari 8:186) and al-Tabari split it, part here and the rest at 9277; he quotes Ibn Hajar that Idris is Ibn Yazid al-Awdi, father of the Kufan jurist Abd Allah b. Idris, trustworthy to them, with only this report in al-Bukhari, and that al-Bukhari's wording has an omission, the command to give the portion, which the Hafiz supplied from this report of al-Tabari. Yunus (9276, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) says those with whom the Prophet made a pact were to be given their portion when no kinship stood between them; that this does not happen today, since it was only for a group he paired, and it ended and belongs to no one but the Prophet, who paired Emigrants and Helpers, whereas today no one is paired. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 278}

**The third view: help and counsel, not inheritance (pp. 278-280, reports 9277-9287).** Others said the verse came down about the parties to the alliance, who were told to give each other their portions of help and counsel and the like, without inheritance. Abu Kurayb (9277, from Abu Usama, from Idris al-Awdi, from Talha b. Musarrif, from Sa'id b. Jubayr, from Ibn Abbas) says the portion is of help, counsel and rifada, with bequests for them, inheritance having gone. Ibn Bashshar (9278, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from Mansur, from Mujahid) says it was an alliance in the Jahiliyya and in Islam they were told to hand the allies a portion of blood-money, counsel and help, with no inheritance. Ibn al-Muthanna (9279, from Muhammad b. Jafar, from Shu'ba, from Mansur, from Mujahid) says help, aid and the alliance. Al-Hasan b. Yahya (9280, from Abd al-Razzaq, from al-Thawri, from Mansur, from Mujahid) says help, loyalty and counsel, with no inheritance. Zakariyya b. Yahya b. Abi Za'ida (9281, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Abd Allah b. Kathir, from Mujahid) says it is the alliance, and the portion is help. Zakariyya b. Yahya (9282, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, from Ata) says the alliance, and the portion is blood-money and help. Muhammad b. Amr (9283, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih, from Mujahid) says help, rifada and blood-money. Al-Muthanna gives the same from Mujahid (9284, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl), and says they are the allies (9285, from al-Himmani, from Sharik, from Salim, from Sa'id; 9286, from al-Himmani, from Abbad b. al-Awwam, from Khusayf, from Ikrima). Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9287, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says that in the Jahiliyya a man would settle among a people who allied with him that he was one of them and shared with him; when they had a right or a fight he was as one of them, but when he had a right or needed help they forsook him; when Islam came they asked about him, and God refused except to strengthen it, and the Prophet said Islam added only strength to allies. In his notes the editor says 9277 completes 9275; that the manuscript had a blank which the print filled from al-Bukhari's wording; that rifada is aid by gift and kindness, as in the Quraysh rifada, where each gave money according to his means to buy camels, food and raisins for the pilgrims and to feed people until the pilgrimage days ended, the rifada and the watering of the pilgrims belonging to the Banu Hashim; that 'it was' in 9278 is a complete verb with no subject or predicate; that the print and manuscript order 'blood-money, help, counsel' but the manuscript marks a swap of the last two, which he followed; that 'Muhammad b. Muhammad b. Amr' in the print at 9283 is a plain error, the manuscript being right, in a chain that recurs, the nearest being 9239; and that sharing in livelihood is what the verb of consoling means. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 280}

**The fourth view: adoption (pp. 280-281, report 9288).** Others said the verse came down about those who adopted other people's sons in the Jahiliyya, who were told in Islam to leave them a bequest at death. Al-Muthanna (9288, from Abd Allah b. Salih, from al-Layth, from Uqayl, from Ibn Shihab, from Sa'id b. al-Musayyab) says it came down about those who adopted men not their sons and made them heirs; God gave them a share in the bequest and returned the inheritance to the mawali of kin and agnates, and God refused that claimants should inherit from those they claimed and adopted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 281}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 281-282).** Al-Tabari says the soundest view on the clause about the pact is that it means the alliance, and they are the allies. His reason is that it is known to the scholars of Arab days and reports that an alliance was bound by oaths, covenants and pledges; that God described those whose oaths bound what they bound among themselves, not those between whom oaths made no contract; and that the Prophet's pairing of Emigrants and Helpers was not by their oaths, nor was adoption; so the alliance alone is meant. On the command to give the portion he holds the better of the two readings is the one on which all agree as the established ruling: that the people of the pre-Islamic alliance give each other portions of help, counsel and opinion, not inheritance, because of the sound report that the Prophet said there is no alliance in Islam, and what alliance there was in the Jahiliyya Islam only strengthened. The editor notes (pp. 280-281) that the print's 'in the kin' reading is also right and he kept the manuscript's; that al-Tabari's sentence is corrected as printed, with the later clause joined to 'since God described', and 'it was known' being the answer to that condition. Al-Tabari gives the report: Abu Kurayb (9289, from Waki, from Sharik, from Simak, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas, from the Prophet). The editor says its chain is sound; Ahmad gave it in the Musnad via Sharik in abridged form without the sentence 'no alliance in Islam', which is present in Abu Ya'la's version as al-Haythami records in Majma al-zawa'id (8:173), calling its men the men of the Sahih; Ibn Kathir cites it from this place in al-Tabari, and al-Suyuti gave it abridged and ascribed it only to Abd b. Humayd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 282}

Abu Kurayb (9290, from Mus'ab b. al-Miqdam, from Isra'il b. Yunus, from Muhammad b. Abd al-Rahman, mawla of the family of Talha, from Ikrima, from Ibn Abbas) reports that the Prophet said no alliance in Islam, that every alliance of the Jahiliyya Islam only strengthened, and that it would not please him to have red camels and to have broken the alliance made at Dar al-Nadwa. The editor says this is another chain with longer wording and sound; that Muhammad b. Abd al-Rahman b. Ubayd, mawla of the family of Talha, is trustworthy per Ibn Ma'in and others; that al-Haythami recorded the red-camels addition as a separate report (8:172), saying al-Tabarani narrated it and its chain has Marzuq b. al-Marzuban, whom he did not know, the rest being men of the Sahih; that al-Tabari's chain lacks that man so the report is sound by this route; and he refers to the explanation of 'red camels' at report 9185.

Ibn Humayd (9291, from Jarir, from Mughira, from his father, from Shu'ba b. al-Taw'am al-Dabbi) reports that Qays b. Asim asked the Prophet about alliance and he said no alliance in Islam, but hold to the alliance of the Jahiliyya. Yaqub b. Ibrahim (9292, from Hushaym, from Mughira, from his father, from Shu'ba b. al-Taw'am, from Qays b. Asim) says he asked about alliance and was told to hold to whatever alliance was made in the Jahiliyya, there being none in Islam. The editor says Mughira is Ibn Miqsam al-Dabbi (treated at 2349), his father Miqsam al-Dabbi is a Follower who narrated from al-Nu'man b. Bashir and was listed by Ibn Hibban among the trustworthy, with no criticism recorded by al-Bukhari or Ibn Abi Hatim; Shu'ba b. al-Taw'am al-Dabbi, also called al-Tamimi, is a trustworthy Follower; al-Tayalisi narrated it from Jarir b. Abd al-Hamid from Mughira (the first chain) and Ahmad in the Musnad from Hushaym from Mughira (the second); Ibn Kathir and al-Haythami cite it, and Ibn Abi Hatim points to it in the entry on Shu'ba b. al-Taw'am. Abu Kurayb (9293, from Waki, from Dawud b. Abi Abd Allah, from Ibn Jud'an, from his grandmother, from Umm Salama) reports that the Prophet said no alliance in Islam, and what alliance there was in the Jahiliyya Islam only strengthened. In his note on 9293 the editor says Dawud b. Abi Abd Allah, mawla of the Banu Hashim, is trustworthy and listed by Ibn Hibban; that the Ibn Jud'an best known among the scholars is Ali b. Zayd b. Jud'an; that al-Tirmidhi narrated by this chain the report that the adviser is trusted; that Ibn Asakir in al-Atraf thought it was Ali b. Zayd and al-Mizzi corrected him in Tahdhib al-Kamal, saying it was an error and the right person is his grandfather Abd al-Rahman b. Muhammad b. Zayd b. Jud'an, because of the chain's 'from Ibn Jud'an, from his grandmother'; the note continues on the next page.

**End of the editor's note on report 9293 and report 9294 (pp. 284-285).** The editor goes on: Tahdhib al-Kamal and Tahdhib al-Tahdhib say, under Dawud and under Abd al-Rahman, that al-Bukhari narrated in al-Adab al-Mufrad the report that the adviser is trusted by Dawud's route from Abd al-Rahman b. Muhammad, and that this shows who is meant by Ibn Jud'an. He says what he found in al-Adab al-Mufrad (p. 29) by this chain is a long hadith without that sentence, so they must mean the hadith's root, yet al-Bukhari's narration is what revealed the right name. The grandmother's name is unknown; he thinks this does no harm, since she was most probably a Companion or at least one who lived in both eras, because Abd al-Rahman was a Follower who narrated from A'isha and Ibn Umar, and he cites al-Dhahabi in al-Mizan, in his section on unknown women, that he knew of no woman accused or abandoned. He says the printed text reads 'from one who told him' instead of 'from his grandmother', a corruption, as does the printed Ibn Kathir, and that the right reading stands in the al-Azhar manuscript of Ibn Kathir. He quotes al-Haythami in Majma al-Zawa'id that Abu Ya'la and al-Tabarani narrated it and that it has the grandmother of Ibn Abi Mulayka, whom he did not know; the editor explains this is the grandmother of this Ibn Jud'an, who is the nephew of Ali b. Zayd, both being named after their remote grandfather, and that the one famed among hadith scholars as Ibn Abi Mulayka is Abd Allah b. Ubayd Allah, a cousin of Ali b. Zayd. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 285}

**Report 9294 (pp. 284-285).** Al-Tabari narrates from Humayd b. Mas'ada, from Husayn al-Mu'allim; from Mujahid b. Musa, from Yazid b. Harun, from Husayn; and from Hatim b. Bakr al-Dabbi, from Abd al-A'la, from Husayn. Husayn says: my father told us, from Amr b. Shu'ayb, from his father, from his grandfather, that the Prophet said in his sermon on the day Mecca was taken: keep faith with an alliance, for Islam only increases its strength, and make no new alliance in Islam. In his note the editor says the father's name of al-Tabari's teacher Hatim stands here as Bakr, earlier (3222) as Bukayr, that al-Taqrib and al-Tahdhib have Bakr and al-Khulasa Bukayr; he went back to the photographed manuscript of Tahdhib al-Kamal and found the copyist dropped 'Bakr' and wrote the name after the grandfather, which he holds certainly a slip, so the difficulty in the name remains (the end of the passage is garbled in the scan). He then names two problems in these chains. First, Humayd b. Mas'ada died in 244 and Husayn al-Mu'allim in 145, ninety-nine years apart, so Humayd cannot say 'Husayn told us'; he thinks copyists dropped a teacher between them. Second, Husayn is the son of Dhakwan and narrates from Amr b. Shu'ayb directly, while Dhakwan has no recorded narrations in the books on men and nobody says his son narrated from him, so he thinks 'my father told us' is a copyists' addition, supported by Ibn Kathir quoting the chain without the father. He refers to reports 9297-9299 for the sense of the hadith and its sources. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 285}

**Reports 9295 and 9296 (pp. 285-287).** Abu Kurayb and Abda b. Abd Allah al-Saffar (9295, from Muhammad b. Bishr, from Zakariyya b. Abi Za'ida, from Sa'd b. Ibrahim, from his father, from Jubayr b. Mut'im) report that the Prophet said there is no alliance in Islam, and any alliance there was in the Jahiliyya Islam only increased in strength. The editor says Zakariyya b. Abi Za'ida al-Hamdani al-Wadi'i is a well-known trustworthy teacher of Shu'ba and al-Thawri, narrated from by the six collections; Sa'd b. Ibrahim b. Abd al-Rahman b. Awf, judge of Medina, is trustworthy and firm; his father Ibrahim is a trustworthy senior Follower; Jubayr b. Mut'im is a Companion of Quraysh from the Banu Nawfal who came to Medina to ransom the captives of Badr and became Muslim afterwards; Ahmad (in the Musnad), Muslim and al-Bayhaqi narrated it by Zakariyya, and Ibn Kathir cited it from the Musnad. Humayd b. Mas'ada and Muhammad b. Abd al-A'la (9296, from Bishr b. al-Mufaddal, from Abd al-Rahman b. Ishaq), and Ya'qub b. Ibrahim (from Ibn Ulayya, from Abd al-Rahman b. Ishaq), from al-Zuhri, from Muhammad b. Jubayr b. Mut'im, from his father, from Abd al-Rahman b. Awf, report that the Prophet said: I witnessed the alliance of the Mutayyabin as a boy with my uncles, and I would not love red camels in exchange for breaking it. Ya'qub adds from Ibn Ulayya the saying of al-Zuhri that Islam met no alliance but increased its strength, that there is no alliance in Islam, and that the Prophet brought Quraysh and the Ansar together. The editor says Bishr b. al-Mufaddal is trustworthy, a teacher of Ahmad, Ishaq and Ibn al-Madini; Abd al-Rahman b. Ishaq is al-Amiri, trustworthy, rated so by Ibn Ma'in and narrated from by Muslim; Muhammad b. Jubayr is a trustworthy Follower, of whom Ibn Ishaq said he was among the most knowledgeable of Quraysh in their hadith; Ahmad narrated it (1655) by Bishr and its beginning (1676) by Ibn Ulayya; al-Bukhari gave it in al-Adab al-Mufrad (p. 83), where a printing error is corrected from this place. He says the hadith is really two: a connected hadith from Abd al-Rahman b. Awf and a mursal one, al-Zuhri's saying, as he explained in the Musnad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 287}

**Reports 9297-9299 (p. 287).** Tamim b. al-Muntasir (9297, from Yazid, from Muhammad b. Ishaq), Abu Kurayb (9298, from Yunus b. Bukayr, from Ibn Ishaq) and Abu Kurayb (9299, from Khalid b. Makhlad, from Sulayman b. Bilal, from Abd al-Rahman b. al-Harith), all from Amr b. Shu'ayb, from his father, from his grandfather, report that the Prophet stood preaching in the year of the conquest when he entered Mecca and said that whatever alliance there was in the Jahiliyya Islam only increased in strength, and there is no alliance in Islam (the other two in similar words). The editor says these are three chains of one hadith, which passed in similar form at 9294; Yazid is Ibn Harun, and Abd al-Rahman in the third chain is b. al-Harith b. Abd Allah b. Ayyash b. Abi Rabi'a; Ahmad narrated it within a long hadith (6692) from Yazid, from Ibn Ishaq; al-Bukhari gave it abridged in al-Adab al-Mufrad (pp. 83-84) from Khalid b. Makhlad by the last chain; and Ibn Kathir cited it from report 9298, then pointed to 9294 and 9299. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 287}

**Al-Tabari's choice (pp. 288-289, with the editor's notes).** Al-Tabari says that if what he related from the Prophet is sound, and a verse whose ruling is disputed (abrogated or not) may not be judged abrogated, when the view of non-abrogation has a sound ground, except by a proof that must be accepted, as he showed elsewhere in his books, then the sound view on this clause is that the binding of oaths is the alliance, and that giving them their portion is help, aid, counsel and opinion, as the Prophet ordered in the reports he cited, and not the view that the portion was from inheritance and was abrogated by the verse cited at [[passage/quran-8-75|8:75]], nor any other view. And if this is right, the verse is firm and not abrogated. The editor's notes correct copyists' slips in the wording, explain the sentence structure and refer to al-Tabari's statement on abrogation earlier (p. 131). He also gives Ibn Kathir's objection to this passage, in substance that some alliances were for mutual help and some for inheritance, as more than one of the early authorities related, and that Ibn Abbas said the emigrant inherited from the Ansari rather than his own kin until that was abrogated, so how can the verse be called firm. The editor replies that al-Tabari explained this and built his whole approach to abrogation on it, repeating it often; that the proof one must accept is, for him, the apparent sense of the Qur'an and the sound report from the Prophet; that the interpretation of Ibn Abbas or another leading figure is not a proof of abrogation where reading the verse as firm has a sound ground; that he is surprised at Ibn Kathir, who should have refuted al-Tabari's argument and not object with a proof al-Tabari rejects; and he closes with a prayer for al-Tabari and for Ibn Kathir. This is the editor's argument; the choice is al-Tabari's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 289}

**The close of the verse (p. 289).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: give those whom your oaths bound their portion of help, counsel and opinion, for God is witness over what you do in this and in your other acts, watching and keeping all of it, until He repays you all for it: the doer of good who follows His command and obedience with the best, and the wrongdoer who opposes His command and prohibition with the worse. The word 'witness' he takes as one who bears witness to that. The note refers to the earlier explanation of this word (volume and page numbers are garbled in the scan and not given). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 289}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 289}
