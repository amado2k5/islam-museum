## Summary
Verse 93 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 93 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "who does greater wrong than one who invents a lie against God, or says: it was revealed to me, when nothing was revealed to him, or says: I shall send down the like of what God sent down" al-Tabari says: by "who does greater wrong than one who invents a lie against God" he means who is more mistaken in speech and more ignorant in deed than one who invented a lie against God, that is, one who fabricated a lie against God, claiming he sent him as a prophet and a warner, and he is false in his claim and a liar in his saying. This is God's declaring foolish and ignorant of the polytheists of the Arabs in their opposition, through Abdullah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh and the Hanafi Musaylima, to God's prophet, one claiming prophethood and the other claiming he had brought the like of what God's messenger brought; and his denial for his prophet Muhammad of inventing a lie against him and claiming falsehood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 533}

The editor refers for "invention" to an earlier passage.

The interpreters differed on it. Some said of it the like of what we said, and 13555, from Ikrima: "who does greater wrong than one who invents a lie against God, or says it was revealed to me when nothing was revealed to him" came down about Musaylima, brother of Banu Adi ibn Hanifa, in his rhymed prose and soothsaying, and "or says I shall send down the like of what God sent down" came down about Abdullah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh, brother of Banu Amir ibn Lu'ayy, who wrote for the Prophet; when the Prophet dictated "mighty, wise" he would write "forgiving, merciful" and so change it, then read to him such and such as he had changed, and the Prophet would say yes, the same. He left Islam and joined Quraysh and said to them: "mighty, wise" was sent down on him and I would change it, then read him what I wrote, and he would say yes, the same! Then he returned to Islam before the conquest of Mecca, when the Prophet halted at Marr. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 534}

The editor says the printed text has "used to write for the Prophet", the manuscript's good reading being right; that in print "then I say what I write" and in the manuscript "then I say write" with the copyist's letter marking an old error in the copy he took from, so he preferred the reading as he set; and that "Marr" is Marr al-Zahran.

Others said it came down about Abdullah ibn Sa'd in particular. 13556, from al-Suddi: "who does greater wrong than one who invents a lie against God, or says it was revealed to me when nothing was revealed to him" to "you will be repaid with the punishment of humiliation", he said: it came down about Abdullah ibn Sa'd ibn Abi Sarh, who became Muslim and wrote for the Prophet; when the Prophet dictated to him "hearing, knowing" he would write "knowing, wise", and when he said "knowing, wise" he wrote "hearing, knowing"; he doubted and disbelieved and said: if Muhammad is inspired, then I have been inspired, and if God sends it down I have sent down the like of what God sent down! Muhammad said "hearing, knowing" and I said "knowing, wise"! He joined the polytheists and informed against Ammar and Jubayr to Ibn al-Hadrami or to Banu Abd al-Dar; they seized them and tortured them until they disbelieved, and Ammar's ear was cut off that day; Ammar went to the Prophet and told him what he met and the unbelief he had given them, and the Prophet refused to side with him; so God sent down, about Ibn Abi Sarh, Ammar and his companions, [[passage/quran-16-106|16:106]], "whoever disbelieves in God after his belief, except one forced while his heart is at rest in belief, but one whose breast opens to unbelief"; the one forced is Ammar and his companions, and the one whose breast opened to unbelief is Ibn Abi Sarh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 535}

The editor says "his ear was cut" means cut off, and he was called al-Ajda', that print and manuscript have "his ear was cut" with the ear masculine when it should be feminine; that the report is strange, since Ibn Sa'd related from Ibn Umar that he saw Ammar at Yamama on a rock with his ear cut dangling, fighting fiercely, though Shu'ba said we did not know it was hurt at Yamama and it is commonly said to have been hurt with the Prophet in some campaign; and that this report should have been mentioned in the commentary on the verse of al-Nahl but Abu Jafar did not, another sure sign of his abridgement.

Others said the one who said "it was revealed to me when nothing was revealed to him" is Musaylima the liar. 13557, from Qatada: we were told this verse came down about Musaylima, and we were told that God's prophet said: I saw in a dream as though two gold bracelets were in my hands, and they weighed on me and troubled me, and it was revealed to me to blow them, and I blew them and they flew off; I interpreted them in my dream as the two liars I am between, the liar of Yamama, Musaylima, and the liar of San'a, al-Ansi, who was called al-Aswad. 13558, from Qatada: it came down about Musaylima. 13559, from al-Zuhri, from the Prophet, the like: while I slept I saw two gold bracelets in my hands, which weighed on me, and it was revealed to me to blow them and I blew them and they flew off, and I interpreted that as the liar of Yamama and the liar of San'a, al-Ansi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 535}

The editor says the manuscript has "troubled me" in another form marked as an error, the printed text being right and agreeing with the report of al-Bukhari and Muslim; and that the dream report is related by al-Bukhari in the Fath and Muslim in his Sahih.

Abu Jafar said: the best of the views on this for being right is to say that God said "who does greater wrong than one who invents a lie against God, or says it was revealed to me when nothing was revealed to him", with no disagreement among the scholars of the community that Ibn Abi Sarh was one who said "I have said the like of what Muhammad said" and apostatised and joined the polytheists, so by that saying he was surely an inventor of a lie. Likewise there is no dispute among all that Musaylima and al-Ansi, the two liars, claimed falsely against God that he had sent them as prophets, and each said God inspired him, and he is a liar in his saying. This being so, every one who fabricated a lie against God and said, in that time or another, "God inspired me" while a liar, to whom God inspired nothing, comes within this verse. As for the sending down, it may have come down because of some of them, or because of all of them, or he may have meant all the polytheists of the Arabs since those who said it were among them and they did not change it; God rebuked them for that and threatened them with punishment for leaving off denouncing it, while in leaving off denouncing it they were deniers of his prophet Muhammad, rejecters of his prophethood and repellers of the verses of God's book and its revelation. So he said to them: who does greater wrong than one who claims falsely on prophethood and says "it was revealed to me" when nothing was revealed to him, and yet says "God has sent down nothing on a human being", contradicting his saying with his saying, denying what he affirmed and denying what he asserts? When the wise and acute consider that, they know its doer lacks intelligence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 536}

It is also reported from Ibn Abbas that he said of "or says I shall send down the like of what God sent down" (13560): he claimed that had he willed he would have said the like, meaning poetry. Ibn Abbas seems to direct the sense of the saying "I shall send down the like of what God sent down" to "I shall send down the like of what God said of poetry"; so al-Suddi also took it, and we have mentioned the report from him before. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 537}

The editor says "poetry" is not mentioned in al-Suddi's earlier report 13556, and perhaps Abu Jafar forgot to write it, or meant it is related in that report though he did not mention it there.

On "if you could see when the wrongdoers are in the agonies of death and the angels stretch out their hands: give up your souls" he says to his prophet Muhammad: if you could see, Muhammad, when death overwhelms with its pangs these wrongdoers who hold gods and rivals equal to their Lord, who say "God has sent down nothing on a human being", the inventors of a lie against God who claim God inspired them when nothing was inspired to them and who say "I shall send down the like of what God sent down", and you saw them as the pangs of death cover them and God's command descends on them and the end of their terms arrives, and the angels stretch out their hands striking their faces and backs, as God said, [[passage/quran-47-27|47:27]], "how will it be when the angels take them, striking their faces and their backs"; they say to them: give up your souls. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 537}

The editor says the plural form of "the inventors... the claimants... the sayers" is so in the manuscript too, though the context calls for the singular, and he left it as its meaning is plain, thinking the singular probably right.

"Ghamarat" is the plural of "ghamra", and the "ghamra" of anything is its mass and most of it, the root being what overwhelms things and covers them; and he cites a line of Bishr ibn Abi Khazim that nothing saves from the agonies but standing firm in the field of battle and striving in the fight, or holding ground. 13561, from Ibn Abbas: the agonies of death are the pangs of death; 13562, from al-Dahhak: the pangs of death. As for the angels' stretching out their hands it is extending them. The interpreters then differed over the reason for their stretching out their hands then. Some said as we said, and 13563, from Ibn Abbas: this is at death, and the stretching is striking, they strike their faces and backs; 13564, from him: the wrongdoers are in the agonies of death and the Angel of Death takes them; 13565, from al-Suddi: they strike them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 538}

The editor says the line is in the commentary on al-Mufaddaliyyat, al-Naqa'id, al-Aghani, al-Khansa's diwan and Lisan al-Arab, the last line of a poem in the Mufaddaliyyat, that "baraka'" is standing firm in the field of war and striving in the fight, from "buruk", the fighter kneeling in his place, that is, holding firm, and that print has "tarak" which is a plain error, as is the manuscript's; and that "their hands" in print is another form from the manuscript's right one.

Others said rather their stretching out their hands is with punishment. 13566, from al-Dahhak: with punishment; 13567, from Abu Salih: with punishment. Some Kufan grammarians took it as stretching out their hands to take out their souls. If someone says: what is the point of "give up your souls" when souls of the children of Adam are taken from the bodies of their people only by the Lord of the worlds? how were these unbelievers addressed and commanded at the time of death to bring out their souls? If so, the children of Adam would be the ones taking the souls of their bodies! It is said: the sense of that is contrary to what you took; it is a command from God on the tongues of his messengers who take these people's souls from their bodies, to deliver what their Lord lodged in them of spirits to him and hand them to his messengers who take them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 540}

The editor says the addition in brackets is required by the context.

On "today you will be repaid with the punishment of humiliation for what you said against God other than the truth and for being too proud to follow his signs" he says: this is God's report of what the messengers of God who take the souls of these unbelievers say to them; he tells of them that they say to their bodies and their owners "give up your souls" to God's anger and curse, for today you are repaid for your unbelief in God, your false saying against him, your claim that God inspired you when nothing was inspired to you, your denial that God sent down anything on a human being, and your pride against yielding to God's command and his messenger's and submitting to obey him, "the punishment of humiliation", the punishment of Hell that disgraces them and abases them until they know the smallness of their selves and their lowliness. 13568, from al-Suddi: as for the punishment of humiliation, it is what disgraces them; 13569, from Ibn Jurayj: the punishment of humiliation in the next world "for what you used to do". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 541}

The editor refers for "repayment" to earlier passages and says the printed text and manuscript have "and your warning that God send down anything on a human being", which has no sense and is the copyist's corruption, the right being as he set.

The Arabs, when they mean by "hun" the sense of "humiliation", put a damma on the ha, and when they mean ease, rest and lightness of burden, they put a fatha on it, saying "he is light in the hawn of his burden", and from it is [[passage/quran-25-63|25:63]], "those who walk on the earth gently", meaning with ease, calm and dignity. He cites a rajaz line of Jandal ibn al-Muthanna al-Tuhawi in which he speaks of days that wore down his strength gently, and the old man casting off his pride in weakness with age, and a line of Dhu Jadan al-Himyari bidding his two companions gentleness, saying time does not return what has passed, so let them not destroy themselves with grief for one who has died. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 541}

The editor says print and manuscript have "al-Muthanna ibn Jandal al-Tuhawi", a plain error for Jandal ibn al-Muthanna al-Tuhawi, an Islamic rajaz poet who traded satire with al-Ra'i; that he did not find the rajaz, "asr" being strength and "casts off his pride" a figure for the old man's weakness at age; that the second is by Dhu Jadan al-Himyari, or Alqama ibn Sharahil ibn Marthad al-Himyari, found in Ibn Hisham's Sira, al-Tabari's history, al-Aghani, Mu'jam ma Ista'jam, Mu'jam al-Buldan and Lisan al-Arab, and that later verses of the poem mourn the forts of Yemen, Baynun, Salhun and Ghumdan, which Aryat the Abyssinian destroyed, grieving at the humiliation and abasement that came on Himyar; and refers to Abu Ubayda's Majaz al-Qur'an.

The fatha of the ha in the sense of humiliation has also been reported, with a line of Amir ibn Juwayn cited for it, in which he says that the lowering of souls in the hard day is dearer to them. But the known thing of their speech is the damma on the ha when it means humiliation and abasement, as Dhu al-Isba' al-Adwani said, that he goes his way from the one he addresses, for his mother is not a herder pasturing pregnant camels and he does not close his eyes to "al-hun", that is humiliation; and when it means gentleness it is a fatha. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 542}

The editor says print has "rawdan", an error, the right being "arwida" from the manuscript, "irwad" being giving time, gentleness and unhurriedness; that Abu Jafar ascribed the line to Amir ibn Juwayn, while it is known to be al-Khansa's in her diwan, some verses of her poem being related to Amir ibn Juwayn al-Ta'i; that the sources are al-Khansa's diwan, al-Aghani, al-Naqa'id and Lisan al-Arab, which read "on the day of hardship dearer to them", print having "highest" and the right being from the manuscript; and that Dhu al-Isba's line is in the commentary on al-Mufaddaliyyat, al-Amali and Lisan, the well-known version being "turn away from me, for my mother is not a herder pasturing pregnant camels, nor is my judgement cheated", containing verses in which he boasts of being proud and protective, not softening for one who does not seek his softness, and the evidence is in the last line.

The editor refers for "stretching out hands" to an earlier passage and says the Kufan referred to is al-Farra in Ma'ani al-Qur'an.

Verse 93 closes here on p. 542, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-94|6:94]].
