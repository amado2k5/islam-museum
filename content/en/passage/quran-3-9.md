## Verse 9 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 221-222)

For [[passage/quran-3-9|3:9]] al-Tabari says that the rooted in knowledge also say, along with their saying that they believe in the resembling and in all of the firm and the resembling as from their Lord, that their Lord is the gatherer of people for a day about which there is no doubt, and that God does not break His promise. He regards this as speech in which what is mentioned stands for what is left out, and its sense, for him, is: Our Lord, You will gather people for the Day of Resurrection, so forgive us and pardon us on that day, for You do not break Your promise that whoever believes in You, follows Your messenger and does what You commanded in Your book, You will forgive on that day. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 221}

He says this is from the people a request to their Lord that He keep them firm in the sound insight they hold in belief in God and His messenger and in what came to them of His revelation, until He takes them at the best of their deeds and their faith; if He does that, Paradise becomes due to them, since He promised to admit to it whoever He does that for among His servants. So although the verse is in the form of a report, its interpretation for him is a request, a prayer and a longing addressed to their Lord. The absence of doubt about that day he explains as the absence of uncertainty, and he says he has shown the evidence for this earlier in his commentary. The editor says the print has a phrase with no meaning in the passage, that the manuscript has a word without dots, that he adopted what he judged the right reading, and that his note refers to earlier places in the commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 221}

On p. 222 al-Tabari explains the preposition in the phrase about 'the day' as meaning 'on a day', the day on which God gathers His creation for the decision between them at the place of presentation and reckoning, and he notes that the word for 'the appointment' is of the pattern mif'al from 'the promise'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 222}
