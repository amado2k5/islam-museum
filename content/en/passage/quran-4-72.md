## Summary
Verse 72 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 72 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on the verse (p. 538, note 1).** Al-Tabari says this is God's description of the hypocrites, which he gave to his Prophet and companions: among you, believers, meaning of your number and people, one who resembles you and shows himself of your call and creed while a hypocrite, holds back from fighting your enemy those who obey him among you when you go out against them. If a defeat, killing or wounds from your enemy strike you, he says God showed him kindness by not having him present among them to suffer the same, and he is glad to have stayed behind, gloating over you, since he is of those in doubt about God's promise to the believers of reward for what befalls them in his way, and about his threat; he neither hopes for reward nor fears punishment. The editor refers to the earlier interpretation of calamity striking (page number garbled). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 538}

**Reports 9935-9939 (pp. 538-539).** Muhammad b. Amr (9935, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that all between this verse and the closing clause of 4:74 on reward is about the hypocrites; al-Muthanna (9936, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) from Mujahid, likewise. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9937, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that they hang back from jihad and raiding in God's way, and that the speech of the one left behind at the calamity is the speech of a denier. Al-Qasim (9938, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) reports from Ibn Jurayj that the hypocrite holds Muslims back from jihad, that the calamity is the enemy killing Muslims, and that his words are those of a gloater. Yunus (9939, from Ibn Wahb) reports from Ibn Zayd that the calamity is a defeat. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 539}

**The two lams (p. 539, note 1).** Al-Tabari says the first lam, on the opened word, is the one entering the predicate for emphasis after "inna", as one says, there is in the house one who honours you, and the second lam entered for the answer to an oath, so that the sense is: among you, people, there are those who, by God, will hang back. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 539}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 539}
