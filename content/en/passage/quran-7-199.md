**Commentary on verse 199 (pp. 326-329)**

On "take what is easy, enjoin the customary good, and turn away from the ignorant", al-Tabari says: the interpreters differed over its sense. Some said it means take what is easy of the character of people, which is the surplus and what does not strain them. Report 15535, from Mujahid: of people's characters and deeds without prying; report 15536, from Mujahid: the ease of people's characters and the ease of their affairs; report 15537, from Urwa: God commanded His messenger to take what is easy of people's characters, the editor noting that al-Bukhari gave it in his Sahih by the route of Abd Allah ibn Barrad from Abu Usama from Hisham from his father from Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr; report 15538, from Urwa from Ibn al-Zubayr: God revealed this verse only about people's characters; report 15539, from Mujahid like 15535; report 15540, from Ibn al-Zubayr: of people's characters, by God I shall take it from them as long as I keep their company; report 15541, from Ibn al-Zubayr: God revealed "take what is easy" only of people's characters; report 15542, from Mujahid: of people's characters and deeds without prying or spying, Abu Asim being unsure which. The editor explains "tahassus" as probing and seeking, as though pressing the search, notes the manuscript has a different form, that Ibn al-Zubayr is Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr and "Abu al-Zubayr" in the manuscript and printed edition is an error, and that Hisham ibn Urwa is trustworthy and well known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 326} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 327} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 328}

Others said it rather means take the surplus from people's property, which they said was commanded before the zakat was revealed, and abrogated when the zakat came down. Report 15543, from Ibn Abbas: take what is left over to you of their property, and whatever they bring you take it; this was before Bara'a came down with the obligations of the alms and their detail; report 15544, from al-Suddi: "the surplus" is the surplus of property, abrogated by zakat; report 15545, from al-Dahhak: take what is left of their property, and this was before the obligatory alms came down. Others said it is rather a command from God to His prophet to pardon the polytheists and leave off harshness with them before fighting them was made obligatory for him. Report 15546, from Ibn Zayd: He commanded him and he turned away from them for ten years in Mecca; then He commanded harshness toward them, to sit in wait for them at every lookout and to besiege them, then said "if they repent and keep up prayer", [[passage/quran-9-5|9:5]], and he recited "Prophet, strive against the unbelievers and hypocrites and be harsh with them", [[passage/quran-9-73|9:73]], and commanded the believers to harshness toward them, saying "fight those of the unbelievers who are near you and let them find harshness in you", [[passage/quran-9-123|9:123]], after having commanded them to pardon, and he recited "say to those who believe, let them forgive those who do not hope for the days of God", [[passage/quran-45-14|45:14]]; then after that nothing was accepted from them but Islam or death, so this verse abrogated pardon. The editor refers to another report, no. 4175, with this verse and its explanation so from Ibn Abbas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 328} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 329}

Al-Tabari chooses: the best of these views is that of those who said its sense is take what is easy of people's characters and leave harshness with them, and said that the Prophet of God was commanded this regarding the polytheists. He says it is best because God followed it with His teaching His prophet how to argue with the polytheists in speech, in His words "say: call your partners, then plot against me and give me no respite", and after it came His words "their brothers draw them on into error and then do not stop; and when you bring them no sign they say, why have you not chosen one", [[passage/quran-7-202|7:202]] and [[passage/quran-7-203|7:203]]; so what lies between is more fitting and better as part of the teaching of His prophet in how to deal with them than as an interruption by a command to take alms from Muslims. If someone says: is it abrogated? He says: we have no indication that it is abrogated, since it could be that, though God sent it down to His prophet in teaching him how to deal with those of the polytheists whom he was not commanded to fight, it was meant as teaching for the Prophet of God and the Muslims all in how to deal with people, and their being commanded to take the easy of… The editor explains that the pronoun in "in dealing with them" refers to "his teaching". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 329}

We stop here at the foot of p. 329, in the commentary on 7:199 in the middle of al-Tabari's sentence at "and their being commanded to take the easy of"; the next batch begins at p. 330. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 329}
