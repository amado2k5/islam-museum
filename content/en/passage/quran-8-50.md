## Summary
Verse 50 of [[passage/quran-8|Surat al-Anfal]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 50 of 75 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 50 (pp. 15-17)**

Al-Tabari says: God says to His Prophet Muhammad, if you could see, Muhammad, when the angels take the souls of the unbelievers, drawing them out of their bodies, striking their faces and backsides, and saying to them, taste the punishment of the fire that burns you on the day you come to Gehenna. The editor refers to the earlier explanations of taking in death, "backs", tasting and burning. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 15}

Report 16200, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa and Ibn Abi Najih: that was on the day of Badr. Report 16201, from Mujahid: "their backs" means their rears, but God is generous and speaks by allusion; report 16202 is the same from him, through Ibn Waki, his father, Sufyan and Abu Hashim. Report 16203, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Wahb ibn Jarir, Shu'ba and Ya'la ibn Muslim: God spoke by allusion, and had He wished He would have said "their rears"; by "backs" He meant the rears. The editor gives biographies of Yahya ibn Sulaym al-Ta'ifi and Isma'il ibn Kathir al-Hijazi and notes a word the publisher changed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 16}

Report 16204, from Mujahid and Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: when the idolaters came on facing the Muslims, the angels struck their faces with swords, and when they turned away the angels overtook them and struck their backs. Report 16205, from al-Hasan, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman and Abbad ibn Rashid: a man said, Messenger of God, I saw on the back of Abu Jahl something like a sandal strap; he asked what it was, and the man said it was the angels' blows. Report 16206, from Mujahid, through Muhammad, Abd al-Rahman, Isra'il and Mansur: a man told the Prophet he charged a man among the idolaters and, as he went to strike him, the man's head fell off, and he said, the angel got to him before you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 17}

Report 16207, from Umar, freedman of Ghufra, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb and Harmala: when you hear God say "they strike their faces and backs", He means their rears. The editor gives biographies of Harmala ibn Imran al-Tujibi and Umar the freedman of Ghufra, and explains "sandal strap" as the thong across a sandal and "fell" as dropped. Al-Tabari explains that something is omitted in the sentence, left out because the wording points to it, namely "and they say" before "taste the punishment of the burning", as in the sura of al-Sajda the words "our Lord, we have seen and heard" are preceded by an omitted "they say"; the editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 17}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
