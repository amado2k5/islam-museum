## Summary
Verse 91 of [[passage/quran-6|Surat al-An'am]], a Meccan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 91 of 165 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

On "they did not esteem God with his due esteem when they said: God has not sent down anything on a human being" al-Tabari says: they did not magnify God with his due magnifying nor exalt him with his due exalting; "when they said God has not sent down anything on a human being" says when they said God sent down on no man a book or revelation. The interpreters differed over who is meant and its interpretation. Some said the speaker was a man of the Jews, and then differed over his name; some said his name was Malik ibn al-Sayf, and some said Finhas; they also differed over the reason for which he said it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 521}

13535, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr: a man of the Jews called Malik ibn al-Sayf came to dispute with the Prophet, and the Prophet said to him: I adjure you by him who sent down the Torah on Moses, do you not find in the Torah that God hates the fat rabbi? and he was a fat rabbi; he grew angry and said: by God, God has sent down nothing on a human being! His companions with him said: woe to you, not even Moses! and he said: by God, God has sent down nothing on a human being! So God sent down the verse "they did not esteem God with his due esteem... say: who sent down the book that Moses brought". 13536, from Ikrima: it came down about Malik ibn al-Sayf, who was of Qurayza, one of the Jewish rabbis. 13537, from al-Suddi: Finhas the Jew said God has sent down nothing on Muhammad. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 522}

The editor refers for "human being" to an earlier passage.

Others said rather a group of the Jews is meant, who asked the Prophet for signs like Moses's signs. 13538, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: some Jews came to the Prophet as he sat wrapped in a cloth, and said: Abu al-Qasim, will you not bring us a book from heaven as Moses brought tablets he carried from God? and God sent down [[passage/quran-4-153|4:153]], "the people of the book ask you to bring down on them a book from heaven"; a man of the Jews knelt and said: God has sent down nothing on you, nor on Moses, nor on Jesus, nor on anyone! and God sent down "they did not esteem God with his due esteem". Muhammad ibn Ka'b said: they did not know how God, "when they said God has not sent down anything on a human being; say: who sent down the book that Moses brought as light", and God's messenger loosed his wrap and kept saying: nor on anyone! {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 523}

The editor says the phrase "they did not know how God" is very strange, he almost rejects it and fears a corruption, it being an explanation of the verse, "they esteemed God"; and that this report was not mentioned in the commentary on the verse of al-Nisa, one of the ways Abu Jafar abridged his commentary.

13539, from Qatada: "they did not esteem God with his due esteem... playing in their plunging", they are the Jews and Christians, people to whom God gave knowledge and who did not follow it, did not take it up and did not act on it, so God blamed them for that deed. We were told that Abu al-Darda used to say: among the most that I shall be disputed over tomorrow is that it will be said: Abu al-Darda, you knew, so what did you do with what you knew? 13540, from Ibn Abbas: meaning from the Children of Israel; the Jews said: Muhammad, has God sent down a book on you? He said yes; they said by God God sent down no book from heaven! and God sent down "say, Muhammad, who sent down the book that Moses brought as light and guidance for people" to "nor your fathers"; he said God sent it down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 523}

Others said this is a report from God about the polytheists of Quraysh, that they said God has not sent down anything on a human being. 13541, from Mujahid: the polytheists of Quraysh said it; and his saying "say: who sent down the book that Moses brought... they make it into sheets, showing them and hiding much" they are the Jews who show them and hide much; and "you were taught what you did not know, you nor your fathers" is for the Muslims. 13542, from Ibn Abbas: they are the unbelievers who did not believe in God's power over them; whoever believed that God is able over everything esteemed God with his due esteem and whoever did not believe that did not esteem God with his due esteem. 13543, from Mujahid: the polytheists of Quraysh. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 524}

The editor says that this is one of the two readings in the verse, with ya in all of "they make", "they show" and "they hide", differing from the reading in his Qur'an copy, and that Abu Jafar will mention it later.

Abu Jafar said: the best of these views for being right is that of those who said the polytheists of Quraysh are meant by "they did not esteem God with his due esteem", because it is in the course of the report about them from the start, so that it too be a report about them is likelier than that it be about the Jews when no mention of them has run to which it could be joined, along with what is in the report about the one God reported in this verse, his denial that God sent down any of the books on a human being, which is not something the Jews hold: the known thing of the Jews' religion is the admission of the scrolls of Abraham, Moses and the Psalms of David. And since no sound report with a connected chain has come of what is related that the speaker was a Jew, nor is there agreement of the interpreters that it was so, and the report from the beginning of the sura to this place is about the polytheists who worship idols, and "they did not esteem God with his due esteem" is joined to that without separation, it is not allowed us to claim that it is turned from what it is joined to without proof that must be accepted, from a report or from reason. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 524}

But I think that those who interpreted it as a report about the Jews found the saying "say: who sent down the book that Moses brought as light and guidance for people; you make it into sheets, showing them and hiding much, and you were taught what you did not know, you nor your fathers", so they directed its interpretation to the people of the Torah and read it as address to them, and made the beginning of the verse a report about them since its end was in their view address to them. Another interpretation and reading is closer to the revelation, for what I described before, that "they did not esteem God with his due esteem" is in the course of the report about the Arab polytheists and idol worshippers and joined to it, so it is likelier that it be a report about them. The sounder reading of "they make it into sheets, showing them and hiding much" is with ya and not ta, meaning that the Jews make it into sheets, showing them and hiding much, with the address in "say: who sent down the book" to the polytheists of Quraysh. This is the sense Mujahid meant, God willing, in his interpretation of it, and so he read. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 525}

The editor says the printed text and manuscript have a wording that is not straight, the right being as he set, "since no sound report with a connected chain has come"; that the construction is of successive conjoined clauses and the answer to "since it did not come" is "it was not allowed"; and that this second reading of the verse is the reading in his Qur'an copy today.

13544, from Mujahid that he used to read this word "they make it into sheets, showing them and hiding much" with ya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 526}

On "say: who sent down the book that Moses brought as light and guidance for people, you make it into sheets, showing them and hiding much" he says to his prophet Muhammad: say, Muhammad, to the polytheists of your people who say to you "God has sent down nothing on a human being": say "who sent down the book that Moses brought as light", meaning clearing and brightness from the darkness of error, "and guidance for people" says clarity for people by which the truth is made clear to them from falsehood in what confused them of their religion, "you make it into sheets, showing them". Whoever reads "you make it" makes it address to the Jews as I explained of the interpretation of those who interpreted it so, and whoever reads it with ya "they make it" interprets it in his reading as its people make it into sheets, the speech about "they show them" running by mention of the sheets while what is meant is what is written in the sheets, so that they show much of what they write in the sheets, making it appear to people, and hide much of what they record in the sheets, keeping it secret and concealing it from people, and by what they concealed from them is what is in them of Muhammad's affair and his prophethood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 526}

The editor says he set the verse on the reading of his Qur'an copy though Abu Jafar's commentary after it is on the other reading, so he alerts the reader of the commentary to the place of difference as Abu Jafar worked it out at pp. 524 and 525, and refers for "light", "guidance" and "sheet" to earlier passages.

13545, from Mujahid: "sheets, showing them and hiding much" are the Jews. 13546, from Ikrima: "they make it into sheets, showing them" means the Jews, for what they showed of the Torah, "and hiding much" for what they hid of the mention of Muhammad and what was sent down on him; and from Mujahid: they are the Jews who show and hide much. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 527}

On "and you were taught what you did not know, you nor your fathers. Say: God; then leave them to play in their plunging" he says: God taught you through the book he sent down to you what you did not know of the reports of those before you and the tidings of those after you and what will be in your return on the day of resurrection, "nor your fathers" says your fathers, believers in God and his messenger among the Arabs, did not know it. 13547, from Mujahid: "you were taught", Arabs, what you did not know, you nor your fathers; 13548, from him: this is for the Muslims. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 527}

The editor says print and manuscript have "God taught you the book" without the particle "with", which must be restored, since the object of "taught you" is "what you did not know".

As for "say: God" it is a command from God to his prophet to answer the question of these polytheists about what he told him to ask them in "say: who sent down the book that Moses brought..." by God's word, like his command to him elsewhere in this sura, [[passage/quran-6-63|6:63]], "say: who delivers you from the darknesses of land and sea", telling him to question the polytheists about that as he told him to question them, when they said God has sent down nothing on a human being, about who sent down the book that Moses brought; then he commanded the answer there by his word [[passage/quran-6-64|6:64]], "say: God delivers you from it", as he commands the answer here to it by his word: God sent it down on Moses. 13549, from Ibn Abbas: "say: who sent down the book that Moses brought as light and guidance for people", he said God sent it down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 528}

The editor says this is the other reading Abu Jafar chose, so he left his commentary as it stood lest the speech confuse its reader; that he left verse 63 of al-An'am on Abu Jafar's reading "anjaytana" and not his Qur'an copy's "anjana"; and that report 13549 is an abridgement of the earlier 13540.

Were it said that the sense is "say: he is God", as a command from God to him to report that and not by way of answer, since "say: who sent down the book" was not a question from the polytheists to Muhammad that "say: God" would answer, but a command from God to Muhammad to ask the people "who sent down the book?" so that their answer must be other than what Ibn Abbas said in his interpretation, it would be allowable because it is a question and a question has no answer, and that is the view we chose for what we explained. As for "then leave them to play in their plunging" he says to his prophet Muhammad: then leave these polytheists who set idols and images equal to their Lord, after your argument against them over their saying "God has sent down nothing on a human being" by your saying "who sent down the book that Moses brought as light and guidance for people" and your answering that the one who sent it down is God, who sent down his book on you; "in their plunging" means in what they plunge into of their falsehood and their unbelief in God and his signs, "to play" says to mock and scoff. This is God's threat to these polytheists and warning to them; God says: then leave them playing, Muhammad, for I lie in wait behind their mocking my signs, and I shall make them taste my might and bring my wrath on them if they persist in their error. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v11 p. 529}

The editor says "it would be allowable" answers the earlier "were it said that its sense..." with a gap between; refers for "leave", "plunging" and "play" to earlier passages; and notes that the printed text has "and warning to them" with another noun, the manuscript's "tahaddud" being entirely right and the publisher having changed it in all the earlier places.

Verse 91 closes here on p. 529, and al-Tabari goes on to [[passage/quran-6-92|6:92]].

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
