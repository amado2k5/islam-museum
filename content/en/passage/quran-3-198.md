## Summary
Verse 198 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 198 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 198: the God-fearing and what God has prepared (pp. 494-495).** Al-Tabari says the God-fearing are those who feared God by obeying Him and following what pleases Him, doing what He commanded and avoiding what He forbade; that they have orchards with rivers running beneath them, and abide in them for ever; and that "nuzul" means a lodging God gives them in them, which they alight in. It is in the accusative as an explanation (tafsir) of the sentence before it, as one says "you have gardens with God as a reward", and "it is yours as alms", "it is yours as a gift". In the editor's note, "tafsir" with the Kufans is "tamyiz" (specification) with the Basrans, and he refers to an earlier place and to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an (1: 251). The notes say the printed edition and the manuscript read "he means by that" and the context requires what he set; and for the senses of "garden", "abiding", "with" and "the abrar" they refer to earlier places.

He then says that the lodging being from God means it is from His honour to them and His gifts; that what God has prepared of life, honour and a good return is better for the abrar than what the disbelievers move about in, because that is passing and perishing, a small and base enjoyment, while what God has of His honour for the abrar, who are the people of obedience to Him, is lasting, neither perishing nor passing. In the editor's note, the sentence in the printed edition has a meaning that is spoiled, and the manuscript has the same, except that in it the word "better" is struck through with a deletion mark, which the publisher did not understand and so left in; the editor corrected the sentence.

**Reports 8373-8375 (pp. 495-496).** Yunus (8373), from Ibn Wahb from Ibn Zayd: better for whoever obeys God. Al-Hasan ibn Yahya (8374), from 'Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from al-A'mash from Khaythama from al-Aswad from 'Abd Allah (Ibn Mas'ud in the known chain): there is no soul, righteous or wicked, for which death is not better; then he recited this verse and the verse of Al 'Imran [[passage/quran-3-178|3:178]]. In the editor's note this report came earlier as 8267, from Muhammad ibn Bashshar from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi from Sufyan, and Ibn Abi Hatim gave it through Abu Mu'awiya from al-A'mash, as Ibn Kathir (2: 328) reports. Al-Muthanna (8375), from Ishaq from Ibn Abi Ja'far from Faraj ibn Fadala from Luqman from Abu al-Darda, who used to say: there is no believer for whom death is not better, and no disbeliever for whom death is not better, and whoever does not believe me, God says in the two verses ([[passage/quran-3-198|3:198]] and [[passage/quran-3-178|3:178]]). In the editor's note, Faraj ibn Fadala is weak, as he explained earlier; Luqman is the son of 'Amir al-Wusabi al-Himsi, reliable, whom Ibn Hibban listed among the reliable, with entries in al-Tahdhib, al-Bukhari's al-Kabir and Ibn Abi Hatim, neither of whom mentions any criticism; "al-Wusabi" has a fatha on the waw and a doubled sad, as Ibn al-Athir in al-Lubab and al-Dhahabi in al-Mushtabih fix it, and Ibn Hajar was mistaken in giving it with the light form in al-Taqrib; Ibn Kathir (2: 328-329) gave the report from this place in al-Tabari, and his edition has "Nuh ibn Fadala" for "Faraj ibn Fadala", which the editor calls a dreadful printer's error; and al-Suyuti mentions it (2: 104) at verse 178 and also attributes it to Sa'id ibn Mansur, 'Abd ibn Humayd and Ibn al-Mundhir. These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 496}
