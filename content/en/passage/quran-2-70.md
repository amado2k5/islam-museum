## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari says "make clear to us what it is" reports a third instance of their ignorance: had they slaughtered any cow to hand it would have sufficed, and once the age was given any cow of that age would have done; since they insisted on having it defined by its marks, God made it hard for them because of their many questions and their disputing with their prophet. He cites a saying he gives from the Prophet, "Leave me as I leave you; those before you were destroyed only by their many questions and their disagreeing with their prophets", and the editor notes it is originally from Abu Hurayra and that the wording here is faulty. He then reports from Ibn Abbas, 'Ubayda and Ikrima that had they taken any cow it would have sufficed, but they pressed and God pressed them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 203} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 204}

Reports follow from Mujahid, 'Ata, Abu al-'Aliya, Qatada, al-Suddi, Ibn Zayd and Ibn Abbas that had the Israelites taken any cow it would have sufficed, but they pressed and God pressed them, and that without their "if God wills" they would never have been guided to it; Ibn Abbas adds that they bought it for as many gold coins as filled its hide. A saying attributed to the Prophet is reported to this effect through Ibn Jurayj and Qatada; the editor notes it is a mursal report (the chain skips the Companion) on which no argument stands, that Ibn Kathir gives its sense through al-Hasan from Abu Rafi' from Abu Hurayra and calls it gharib by that route, at best the words of Abu Hurayra, and that Abu Bakr ibn 'Ayyash's report from Ibn Abbas has a broken chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 204} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 205} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 206}
Al-Tabari infers, by his own reasoning, that these authorities held God's rulings in the Book and on the Messenger's tongue to be general in their plain sense unless something in the Book or from the Messenger specifies them, and that the Israelites erred in all three questions; he refers to his own work "al-Risala" on general and specific. He rejects the claim that they asked because they thought a particular cow was meant, like Moses' staff, saying it charges them with something worse than what it denies. He then explains "the cows resemble one another" and mentions the variant reading "al-baqir". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 207} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 208} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 209}
