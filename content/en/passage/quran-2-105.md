## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "neither those who disbelieve among the People of the Book nor the polytheists wish that any good be sent down to you from your Lord", al-Tabari says "yawaddu" means "loves", that "al-mushrikin" is in the genitive by coordination with "People of the Book", and that "an yunazzala" is in the accusative after "yawaddu"; so the sense is that the unbelievers among the People of the Book and the idol-worshipping polytheists do not love that any of the good with God be sent down to the believers, and they wished God would not send down the Furqan and what He revealed to Muhammad, out of envy and transgression against the believers, as he puts it. He finds in the verse a sign that God forbade the believers to lean on them, to listen to their words and accept what they bring as advice, since God informed them of the rancour and envy these hold within, even if their tongues show otherwise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 470}

On "and God singles out for His mercy whom He wills", he says God singles out whom He wills for His prophethood and message, sending him to whom He wills of His creation, and favours with belief whom He loves, guiding him; "singling out" is setting them apart for it from others of His creation, and all of that is mercy from Him. On "and God is the Possessor of great bounty" he says it reports that every good His servants obtain in religion and worldly life is from Him as a first gift and bounty, not from any entitlement on their part. He sees in the verse an allusion to the People of the Book, that the guidance given to His Prophet and the believers is a bounty from Him, and that His favours are not gained by wishes but are gifts He gives to whom He wills. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 471}
