## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 104 (pp. 458-462)**

Al-Tabari says: this is a report from God, mighty is His mention, by which He informed the believers that accepting the repentance of whoever of the hypocrites repents, and taking the charity from their wealth when they give it, are not up to the Prophet of God; and that when the Prophet of God refused to free those who had tied themselves to the pillars of the stay-behinds from the campaign with him, and when he left accepting their charity until God freed them, after He gave him leave in that, he did so only because that was not his to do and was for God, mighty is His mention, and not for Muhammad, Muhammad doing what he does of leaving, freeing, taking charity and other acts by God's command. So the Almighty said: do these who stayed behind from striving with the believers, who tied themselves to the pillars, who said "we shall not free ourselves until the Messenger of God is the one who frees us", and who asked the Messenger of God to take the charity of their wealth, not know that that is not up to Muhammad but to God, and that God is the one who accepts the repentance of whoever of His servants repents, or returns it, and takes the charity of whoever gives it, or returns it to him, and not Muhammad, so that they direct their repentance and charity to God and aim by it at His face and not Muhammad or anyone else, make their repentance sincere to Him and intend Him by their charity, and know that God is the Accepting, the Merciful, meaning the One who returns to His servants with pardon when they return to obedience to Him, and merciful to them when they turn back to His pleasure from His punishment. The editor refers to earlier explanations of repentance, the Accepting and the Merciful. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 459}

Ibn Zayd used to say of this, report 17162, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: the others, meaning those of the stay-behinds who did not repent, said "these, meaning those who repented, were with us yesterday, not spoken to and not sat with; what has become of them?", and God said "God is the one who accepts repentance from His servants and takes the alms, and God is the Accepting, the Merciful". Report 17163, from Abdallah ibn Mas'ud, through Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Muhammad ibn Ja'far, Shu'ba and Abdallah ibn al-Sa'ib: there is no servant who gives alms but they fall into God's hand, and He is the one who places them in the hand of the beggar; and he recited this verse, "He is the one who accepts repentance from His servants and takes the alms". The editor notes a difficulty in the chain of this report, in its mention of Qatada or the son of Qatada of Muharib, and says he fears the copyist spoiled the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 459}

Report 17164, from Ibn Mas'ud, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, al-Thawri, Abdallah ibn al-Sa'ib and Abdallah ibn Abi Qatada al-Muharibi: no man gives alms but they fall into God's hand before they fall into the hand of the beggar, and He places them in the hand of the beggar; then he recited "do they not know that God is the one who accepts repentance from His servants and takes the alms"; reports 17165 and 17166, from him, are similar, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad and Sufyan, and through Ibn Humayd, Jarir and al-A'mash. The editor notes that Abdallah ibn Qatada al-Muharibi related from Ibn Mas'ud and Abdallah ibn al-Sa'ib from him, that Ibn Hibban counted him reliable, and that al-Haythami says al-Tabarani related the report in al-Kabir and no one weakened him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 460}

Report 17168, from Abu Hurayra, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Abbad ibn Mansur and al-Qasim: the Messenger of God said "God accepts alms and takes them with His right hand and rears them for one of you as one of you rears his foal, until a morsel becomes like Uhud"; and its confirmation is in God's book, "God is the one who accepts repentance from His servants and takes the alms" and "God destroys usury and nurtures alms", which is [[passage/quran-2-276|2:276]]. Report 17169, from Abu Hurayra, is similar, through Sulayman ibn Umar al-Raqqi, Ibn al-Mubarak, Sufyan, Abbad ibn Mansur and al-Qasim, who said "I think he raised it to the Prophet". The editor notes that the verse comes in the manuscript in a form with an old error, which he kept so as to draw attention to it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 461}

Report 17170, from Abu Hurayra, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr, Ma'mar, Ayyub and al-Qasim ibn Muhammad: God accepts alms when they are from what is good, and takes them with His right hand, and a man gives alms the size of a morsel and God rears it for him as one of you rears his young camel or foal, so that it grows in God's palm, or he said in God's hand, until it is like a mountain. Report 17171, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: it was mentioned to us that the Prophet of God used to say "by Him in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, no man gives alms and it falls into the hand of the beggar until it has fallen into God's hand". Report 17172, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "and that God is the Accepting, the Merciful", meaning if they are upright. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 462}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
