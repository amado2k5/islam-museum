**Commentary on verse 94 (pp. 248-255)**

On "and when the caravan set out, their father said, I find the scent of Joseph, if you did not think me feeble-minded", al-Tabari says: when the caravan of Ya'qub's sons set out from Joseph, heading to Ya'qub, their father Ya'qub said "I find the scent of Joseph". The editor refers to the earlier discussion of "fasl". The rest of the speech is on the next page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 248}

It is reported that the wind asked its Lord's leave to bring Ya'qub the scent of Joseph before the bearer of good news reached him; He permitted it and it brought it. Report 19801, from Abu Ayyub al-Hawzani, through Yunus, Ibn Wahb and Abu Shurayh: the wind asked leave to bring Ya'qub Joseph's scent when the shirt was sent to his father, before the herald came, and did so; Ya'qub said "I find the scent of Joseph, if you did not think me feeble-minded". The editor says Abu Shurayh is Abd al-Rahman ibn Shurayh al-Ma'afiri, who is reliable, that he could not identify Abu Ayyub al-Hawzani, and that al-Tabari gives the report in the same words in his History. Report 19802, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Isra'il, Abu Sinan and Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl: a wind rose and brought Joseph's scent from eight nights' journey, and he said "I find the scent of Joseph". The editor says Abu Sinan is Dirar ibn Murra al-Shaybani the elder and Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl is Abd Allah ibn Abi al-Hudhayl al-Anazi, both reliable. Reports 19803 and 19804 are to the same sense: Ya'qub found Joseph's scent when he was eight nights' journey from him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 249}

Report 19805, from Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl, through Ibn Waki', al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Sufyan ibn Uyayna and Abu Sinan: I was beside Ibn Abbas when he was asked, from how far did Ya'qub find the scent of the shirt? He said: from seven or eight nights' journey. Report 19806, through Ibn Waki', Jarir and Abu Sinan: my companions said to me, you go to Ibn Abbas, so ask him for us; I said, I do not ask him anything, but I sit behind the couch and the Kufans come to him and ask about their needs and mine, and I heard him say Ya'qub found the scent of Joseph's shirt from eight nights' journey; Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl said, I said to myself, that is like the distance from Basra to Kufa. Reports 19807 to 19810 are similar from Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Ali ibn Asim and Dirar ibn Murra; Abu Kurayb, Waki' and Sufyan; al-Husayn ibn Muhammad, Asim and Shu'ba; and al-Muthanna, Adam al-Asqalani and Shu'ba: he found his scent from a distance such as lies between Basra and Kufa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 250}

Reports 19811 and 19812, from Ibn Abbas, through Abu Nu'aym and Sufyan, and al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq and Isra'il: when the caravan set out, a wind rose and brought Ya'qub the scent of Joseph's shirt and he said "I find the scent of Joseph", and he found his scent from eight nights' journey. Report 19813, from al-Hasan, through Bishr, Yazid, Sa'id and Qatada: we were told that there were then eighty farsakhs between them, Joseph in the land of Egypt and Ya'qub in the land of Canaan, and a long time had passed; the editor explains that he means the period of separation between Ya'qub and Joseph. Report 19814, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: we were told that there were then eighty farsakhs between them, and he had parted from him seventy-seven years before. Reports 19815 and 19816, from Ibn Abbas, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Sufyan and Isra'il: he found the scent of the shirt from eight days' journey. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 251}

Report 19817, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when the caravan left Egypt, Ya'qub caught Joseph's scent and said to those of his sons who were with him "I find the scent of Joseph, if you did not think me feeble-minded". As for "law-la an tufannidun", it means: were it not that you would rebuke me, call me incapable, blame me and call me a liar. Of this is a poet's verse in which he asks his two companions to stop blaming and faulting him, since what has passed of his affair cannot be returned; the editor says it is Hani ibn Shukaym al-Adawi, as Abu Ubayda ascribes it, and that he did not find the verse in his sources. One says "afnada fulanan al-dahr" when time has corrupted him, as in a verse of Ibn Muqbil telling the hearer to let time do what it wishes, for when it is charged with corrupting people it corrupts; the editor refers to Majaz al-Qur'an. The interpreters differed on the meaning. Some said it means: were it not that you would call me foolish: report 19818, from Ibn Abbas, through Ibn Waki', Ibn Uyayna, Abu Sinan and Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl: you call me foolish. Report 19819, through Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki' and Isra'il, is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 252}

Report 19820, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Sufyan and Khusayf: you call me foolish. Report 19821, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Ali ibn Dawud, Abd Allah, Mu'awiya and Ali: you call me ignorant. Report 19822, from Ibn Abbas: were it not that you call me foolish. Report 19823, from Mujahid, through Ahmad, Abu Ahmad, al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym, Sufyan and Khusayf: were it not that you call me foolish. Report 19824, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr from Ibn Abbas, and Salim from Sa'id, through al-Muthanna, al-Himmani, Sharik and Abu Sinan: one said you call me foolish and the other said you call me a liar. Report 19825, from Ata', through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Sulayman: were it not that you call me a liar, or call me foolish. Report 19826, from Ata': you call me foolish. Reports 19827 and 19828, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id, and Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Muhammad ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: were it not that you call me foolish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 253}

Report 19829, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya, Abd al-Razzaq, Isra'il, Abu Sinan and Ibn Abi al-Hudhayl: you call me foolish. Others said it means his mind has gone: reports 19830 to 19833, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa; Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa; al-Muthanna, Abu Hudhayfa and Shibl; and al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa, all from Ibn Abi Najih: his mind has gone. Report 19834, from Mujahid, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: were it not that you say, your mind has gone. Report 19835, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: were it not that you call me weak. Report 19836, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: one with no mind is the "mufannad", meaning one who does not reason; the editor notes that the printed text has "they say, he does not reason" and that the manuscript reading is correct. Others said it means were it not that you call me a liar: report 19837, from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, through Ibn Waki', Suwayd ibn Amr al-Kalbi, Sharik and Salim: you call me a liar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 254}

Report 19838, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: were it not that you call me senile and a liar. Report 19839, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Muhammad ibn Bakr and Ibn Jurayj: you call me a liar. Reports 19840 and 19841, from al-Dahhak, through Abda, Abu Khalid and Juwaybir, and al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: you call me a liar. Report 19842, from Ata', through al-Muthanna, Amr, Hushaym and Abd al-Malik: you call me foolish or a liar. Report 19843, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd, his father, his uncle and his father from his father: you call me a liar. Others said it means you call me senile: report 19844, from Mujahid, through Ahmad ibn Ishaq, Abu Ahmad, Isra'il and Ibn Abi Najih: were it not that you call me senile. Report 19845, from Mujahid, through Ibn Waki', Ubayd Allah, Isra'il and Abu Yahya, is the same. Report 19846, from Qatada from al-Hasan, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: you call me senile. Report 19847 begins at the end of the page, through Ya'qub, Hushaym and Abu al-Ash'ab. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 255}
