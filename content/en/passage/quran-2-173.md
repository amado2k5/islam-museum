## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "He has forbidden you only carrion, blood, the flesh of swine and what has been dedicated to other than God" al-Tabari says the sense is: do not forbid yourselves what I did not forbid, the bahira, sa'iba and the like; I forbade only carrion, blood, pork and what is dedicated to other than Me, and then He made clear and detailed what He forbade. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 317} "Innama" is a single particle meaning "He forbade you nothing but", which is why "carrion" is in the accusative; he notes some readers read it in the nominative, taking "inna" and "ma" as two particles, and that he does not allow reading so, though it has a sound Arabic basis, since the authoritative readers agree against it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 317} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 318}

Al-Tabari adds that were "harrama" read in the passive, "carrion" could take the nominative in two ways, and that he does not allow reading so, for the reason given. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 318} He reports that readers differ on "al-mayta" with a light or heavy ya, and holds these are two well-known usages in reading and in Arabic, with no difference in meaning, so whichever the reader uses is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 319} "What has been dedicated to other than God" is what is slaughtered for gods and idols with another name pronounced over it; "ihlal" is raising the voice, whence the pilgrim's talbiya and a newborn's first cry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 319} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 320} He gives two views: what was slaughtered for other than God, from Qatada, Mujahid, 'Ata', Ibn 'Abbas and al-Dahhak, with Ibn 'Abbas also saying what was dedicated to the idols by the unbelievers other than Jews and Christians; and what had a name other than God's pronounced over it, from al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd, with a report from 'Uqba ibn Muslim and Qays ibn Rafi' that what is slaughtered for the churches' feast is the food of the People of the Book and the verse concerns the Magians, idol-worshippers and polytheists. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 321}

On "but whoever is compelled, neither desiring nor transgressing, there is no sin on him" he says "compelled" is one afflicted by the necessity of hunger toward what is forbidden of these, who is not sinful in eating it; "neither desiring" is a circumstantial of "whoever"; and it is said the sense is one forced to eat it, from Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 321} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 322} He then reports the differences on "neither desiring nor transgressing". Some said not rebelling against the rulers, not a highway robber, not leaving the community and not going out in disobedience to God, who has the dispensation, while whoever goes out rebelling or transgressing has none even if compelled, from Mujahid and Sa'id; {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 322} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 323} others said not seeking the forbidden in eating it and not exceeding what is permitted, so he does not pass from lawful to forbidden when he has a way out, from Qatada, al-Hasan, Mujahid, 'Ikrima, al-Rabi' and Ibn Zayd, with al-Rabi' citing the verse of al-Mu'minun, "those are the transgressors". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 323} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 324} He notes that Ibn Zayd denies the two views differ and says they are one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 324}
