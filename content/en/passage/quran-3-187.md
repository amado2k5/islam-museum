## Summary
Verse 187 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 187 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 187 (pp. 458-459).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: remember too, Muhammad, of the affair of the Jews and other People of the Book, when God took their covenant to make clear to people your matter, which He took the covenant on being made clear in their book, the Torah and Gospel, that you are a messenger sent with the truth, and not to conceal it; they abandoned God's command and neglected it, broke His covenant, concealed your matter and belied you, and took in exchange for their concealing a base, small return from the goods of this world; God then blamed this purchase. The notes refer to earlier explanations of the words for casting aside, behind their backs, buying, favour and evil (page numbers in this scan are partly garbled), and say the bracketed addition is needed for the sentence to stand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 459}

**Who is meant (pp. 459-461, reports 8318-8326).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed. The first view is that the Jews alone are meant: Ibn 'Abbas (8318), through Abu Kurayb from Yunus ibn Bukayr from Ibn Ishaq from Muhammad ibn Abi Muhammad from 'Ikrima: Finhas, Ashya' and their like among the rabbis; Ibn 'Abbas (8319), through Ibn Humayd from Salama from Ibn Ishaq, likewise, and the note says both are in Ibn Hisham's Sira (2: 208) following reports 8300-8301. Ibn 'Abbas (8320), through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father from his uncle from his father from his grandfather: He had commanded them to follow the unlettered prophet, and when Muhammad was sent He renewed the covenant with them and said believe him and you will meet what you love with me, with reference to [[passage/quran-7-158|7:158]] and [[passage/quran-2-40|2:40]]. Al-Suddi (8321), through Muhammad from Ahmad from Asbat: God took the Jews' covenant to make Muhammad clear to people and not to conceal him. Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8322), through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from 'Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Abu al-Jahhaf from Muslim al-Batin: al-Hajjaj ibn Yusuf asked his companions about this verse, and a man rose to Sa'id ibn Jubayr and asked him; he said they are the Jews, and the one to be made clear to people is Muhammad. Ibn Jurayj (8323), through al-Qasim from al-Husayn from Hajjaj: in it was that Islam is God's religion which He made obligatory on His servants and that they find Muhammad written with them in the Torah and the Gospel. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 460}

Others said that everyone given knowledge of the religion is meant: Qatada (8324), through Bishr from Yazid from Sa'id: this is a covenant God took from the people of knowledge; whoever knows something should teach it, beware concealing knowledge, which is ruin, and let no man claim what he has no knowledge of and so leave God's religion; it used to be said that knowledge not spoken by is like a treasure not spent from, and wisdom not brought out is like something standing that neither eats nor drinks (the word for the thing compared is garbled in this scan); and it used to be said that blessed is the speaking scholar and the retentive listener, the one a man who learned, taught, gave and called to it, the other a man who heard good, kept it and benefited. Abu 'Ubayda (8325), through Yahya ibn Ibrahim al-Mas'udi from his father from his father from his grandfather from al-A'mash from 'Amr ibn Murra: a man came to some people in the mosque, among them 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, and said your brother Ka'b sends greetings and brings the glad news that this verse is not about you; Ibn Mas'ud said: greet him back and tell him it came down while he was a Jew. Abu 'Ubayda (8326), through Ibn Humayd from Jarir from al-A'mash, likewise, from 'Abd Allah and Ka'b. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 461}

Others said the covenant of the prophets with their peoples is meant: Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8327), through Ibn Bashshar from Yahya ibn Sa'id from Sufyan from Yahya ibn Abi Thabit: I told Ibn 'Abbas that 'Abd Allah's companions recite the verse in another form in which the Lord took the covenant from the People of the Book; he said: from the prophets on their peoples. Sa'id (8328), through Abu Kurayb from Qabisa from Sufyan from Habib, likewise: God took the covenant of the prophets on their peoples. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 461}

**The sense of making it clear, and the readings (p. 462).** Al-Hasan (8329), through 'Abd al-Warith ibn 'Abd al-Samad from his father from Muhammad ibn Dhakwan from Abu Na'ama al-Sa'di: he used to explain it as that they would speak the truth and confirm it by deed. The first note says the verse was in the printed edition with the ya' in all the reports, and the editor made it follow our copy's reading with ta'. Al-Tabari then says the reciters differed: some read with ta', the reading of most reciters of Medina and Kufa, in the address form, meaning God said it to them; others read with ya' in both verbs, as a report about someone absent, since at the time God told His Prophet they were not present, so the report about them became as one about an absent party. The second note says the printed edition has a slip in the phrase for the greatest of the reciters, the right reading from the manuscript being the word for most and the bulk of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 462}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 462 in the commentary on verse 187 and continue from p. 463. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 462}
