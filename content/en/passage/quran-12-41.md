**Commentary on verse 41 (pp. 107-108)**

Al-Tabari reports Joseph's words to the two who entered the prison with him: "as for one of you, he will give his lord wine" is the one who saw himself pressing wine and will serve his lord, meaning his master the king, that is, be his cupbearer; report 19294, from Ibn Zayd, through Yunus and Ibn Wahb: his master. "And as for the other", the one who saw bread on his head from which the birds ate, "he will be crucified and the birds will eat from his head". It is said that when he interpreted what they had told him they had seen in sleep, they said, we saw nothing, and he said, "the matter you ask about is decreed", meaning the matter on which they sought a ruling was finished and God's judgement on them was due as he had told them. The editor refers to the earlier commentary on "lord", "decreed" and "asking for a ruling". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 107}

Reports 19295 to 19298, from Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Abd al-Rahman, Sufyan, Umara, Ibrahim, Abu Kurayb, Waki', Ibn Waki', Muhammad ibn Fudayl, Alqama and Jarir: the two who entered the prison said to Joseph, we saw nothing, and he said, the matter is decreed; they had only feigned dreams to test him, and when he interpreted their dreams they said they were only playing, and he said the matter was decreed according to what Joseph had interpreted. Report 19299, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: he told Mujlith, as for you, you will be crucified and the birds will eat from your head, and told Nabu, as for you, you will be restored to your work and your master will be pleased with you, the matter you ask about is decreed. Report 19300, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj, ends the page; the editor notes that much has fallen out of this report, and put dots in place of the lost part. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 108}

Reports 19301 and 19302, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim, Isa, Ibn Abi Najih, al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa and Waraqa, on "the matter you ask about is decreed", said when they said we saw no dream, we were only playing: he said, the dream has come true as I interpreted it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 109}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
