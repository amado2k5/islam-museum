**Commentary on verse 153: the People of the Book ask for a book from heaven (p. 356)**

Al-Tabari says the People of the Book, meaning the Jews of the Torah, asked Muhammad to send down to them a written book from heaven, as Moses had brought the Torah written. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 356}

Report 10768, from al-Suddi: the Jews said, if you are truthful that you are God's messenger, bring us a written book from heaven as Moses brought. Report 10769, from Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi: some Jews came to the Prophet and said, Moses brought the tablets from God, so bring us tablets from God, and we will believe you! God then revealed the verse on their request for a book sent down from heaven, down to what is mentioned of their slander against Mary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 356}

Report 10770, from Qatada, says they asked for a book specific to themselves; they had asked Moses for something greater when they said, show us God openly. Report 10771, from Ibn Jurayj, says the Jews and Christians came to the Prophet and said they would not follow him until he brought them a book from God to such a man and such a man, saying that he is God's messenger. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 357}

Al-Tabari prefers that the People of the Torah asked the Prophet to ask his Lord to send down a written book from heaven, a sign that no creature could match, testifying to his truth and commanding them to follow him. It could be one book for their community or books to particular individuals; but the plain reading favours one book for the community, since the wording is in the singular. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 358}

Al-Tabari says that God's words 'they asked Moses for greater than that' are a rebuke and a reproof to those who asked for the book: God tells His Prophet not to be grieved by their request, for from their ignorance of God, their boldness towards Him, and their deception by His forbearance, had He sent down to them the book they asked, they would have opposed God's command as they opposed it after He revived their forefathers from their swoon, and worshipped the calf, taking it as a god besides their Creator. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 358}

Their forefathers asked Moses for something greater, saying show us God openly, that is, to see Him with our eyes and look at Him. Report 10772, through al-Harith, Abu Ubayd and Hajjaj, from Ibn Abbas, says the phrase 'openly' is put before and after, and that when they saw Him they had seen Him; that their request of Moses was openly. Al-Tabari notes that this view is not like anything given in the commentary on sura al-Baqara, which shows the abridgement of this work. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 359}

Al-Tabari says that 'the thunderbolt took them for their wrongdoing' means they were struck down for wronging themselves, and their wronging themselves was their asking Moses to show them their Lord openly, which was not theirs to ask. By 'then they took the calf' he means that those who asked Moses for the sight, after God revived them from their swoon, took the calf into which the Samiri cast the handful he had taken from the hoofprint of Gabriel's horse, as a god worshipped besides God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 359}

'After the clear signs came to them' means after the clear signs and evidence came to those who asked Moses for what they asked, that they would not see God openly in this world; those signs were the swoon God struck them with when they asked, then His reviving them after death, and the other signs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 360}

By this God rebukes their act and makes clear to His servants their ignorance and lack of reason: they acknowledged the calf as their god while seeing it with their eyes, after God had shown them the signs that they would not see Him openly in this life, and they clung to its worship, affirming its godhead. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 360}

'We pardoned that', that is, We pardoned the worshippers of the calf and those among them who affirmed it their god, through the repentance they made to their Lord by killing themselves, and their patience in God's command. 'We gave Moses clear authority' means a proof that shows his truthfulness and the reality of his prophethood, and this proof is the clear signs God gave him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 361}
