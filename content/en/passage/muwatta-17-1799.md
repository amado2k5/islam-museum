## Summary
Malik says of a man who buys a slave and hires him out for a large hire or a small return, and then finds a defect in him for which he may return him: he returns him for that defect, and the hire and the return are his. This is the agreed practice in our city. If a man buys a slave and builds a house for him, whose building is worth many times the price of the slave, and then finds a defect for which he may return him, he returns him, and the slave's labour for him is not counted against him. Likewise, the hire belongs to him when he hires him out to someone else, because he is the guarantor of him. {cite:muwatta-maruf-1997 p.137}
