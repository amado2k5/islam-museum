**Commentary on verse 69 (pp. 169-171)**

On "and when they entered upon Joseph he took his brother to himself; he said, I am your brother, so do not grieve at what they used to do", al-Tabari says: when Ya'qub's sons entered upon Joseph, "he took his brother to himself", that is, joined to himself his brother by his father and mother. Report 19503, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: he knew his brother, lodged them in a house and gave them food and drink; when night came he brought mattresses and said, let every two brothers among you sleep on one mattress; when the boy was left alone Joseph said, this one will sleep with me on my bed, and he spent the night with him, Joseph smelling his scent and holding him to himself until morning, while Rubil kept saying, we have never seen the like, relieve us of him! The editor explains that "mithal", with a kasra on the mim, is a bed or bedding, and that the printed text misread the manuscript at one point in the report and produced meaningless wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 169}

Report 19504, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: when Ya'qub's sons came in to Joseph they said, this is our brother you told us to bring to you, we have brought him. I was told that he said to them: you have done well and rightly, and you will find that with me; then he said, I see that you are men and I wish to honour you; he called the steward of his hospitality and said, lodge each two separately, then honour them and treat them well in hospitality. Then he said, I see that this man you brought has no second, so I will join him to me and his lodging will be with me. So he lodged them two by two in various dwellings and lodged his brother with him, taking him in; and when he was alone with him he said, I am your brother, I am Joseph, so do not grieve at anything they did to us in the past, for God has been good to us, and do not tell them anything of what I have told you. The editor points to a difference between the printed text and the manuscript in the word for steward and says he added a word so the sense holds. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 170}

Report 19505, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: he joined him to himself and lodged him, and he was Benjamin. Report 19506, from Wahb ibn Munabbih, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Isma'il ibn Abd al-Karim and Abd al-Samad ibn Ma'qil, who was asked about Joseph's words: how did it come out when he seized him by the goblet, when he had told him that he was his brother, while you claim he stayed disguised to them, plotting against them until they returned? He said: he did not acknowledge the kinship to him, but said "I am your brother" in place of your lost brother, "so do not grieve at what they used to do", meaning do not let his place grieve you. The editor notes differences among the copies in the wording of the question and keeps what he judged correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 170}

On "so do not grieve", he says: do not be humbled nor sad; the form is "tafta'il" from "bu's", and one says "ibta'asa yabta'isu ibti'asan". The interpreters said the same: report 19507, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: do not grieve or despair. Report 19508, from Wahb ibn Munabbih: do not let his place grieve you. Report 19509, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: do not grieve over what they used to do. Al-Tabari says the sense of the words is therefore: do not grieve or feel humbled in yourself over anything that has gone before from your brothers toward you, and toward your brother from your mother, and what they used to do to you before today. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 171}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
