## Summary
Verse 166 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 166 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Verse 166: what befell on the day of Uhud, by God's leave (8192, pp. 377-378).** Abu Ja'far: what is meant is what struck you on the day the two hosts, of the Muslims and of the idolaters, met, which is the day of Uhud, and what struck them was the killing of those killed and the wounding of those wounded; and it was by God's leave, that is by His decree and ordainment among you. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of "leave" (the reference numbers are garbled in the scanned text). Al-Tabari says the particle "fa" comes in the answer because "ma" is a conditional particle, and he had explained the like earlier (the editor refers to volume 5). He then explains that the next clause means that God willed by what befell them to know the believers and the hypocrites, that is, that the people of faith in God and His messenger might distinguish the believers among you from the hypocrites and recognise them, the affair of the two groups not being hidden from them; he had explained the meaning of this "knowing" earlier. Ibn Ishaq says the like (8192), through Ibn Humayd, from Salama: what struck you when you and your enemy met was by His leave when you did what you did, after His help had come to you and He had made good His promise to you, so that He might distinguish the hypocrites from the believers and bring out what was in those among you who dissembled. The editor notes that the printed text's "you believed" differs from the manuscript and Ibn Hisham's Sira, and that the report is in the Sira (3: 125), a continuation of reports ending with 8187. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 377} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 378}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 378}
