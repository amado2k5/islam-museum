## Summary
Verse 62 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## In al-Tabari's commentary
On "those who believed and those who were Jews" al-Tabari says "those who believed" are those who accepted the Messenger's truth, and "those who were Jews" are the Jews, "hadu" meaning they repented, though it is also said they were called "yahud" from their words "we have turned to You" (Ibn Jurayj). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 143}
"Nasara" is a plural of "nasran", like "sukara" and "sakran", though "nasrani" is the common form, and he cites verses; he says these show they were called Nasara for helping one another, while another view is that they settled a land called Nasira, from Ibn Jurayj and Qatada, and from Ibn Abbas, by a chain al-Tabari does not approve, that Jesus's village was called Nasira and his followers the Nasiriyyun. Qatada says it is a name they took and were not commanded to take, and al-Tabari records those who derived it from "who are my helpers to God". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 143} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 144} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 145}
"Sabi'un" is the plural of "sabi'", one who adopts a religion other than his own, like an apostate, the Arabs calling anyone who leaves one religion for another a "sabi'". The commentators differ on whom the name fits: some say anyone who leaves one religion for none, reporting from Mujahid that they are neither Jews nor Christians and have no religion; from him and al-Hasan and Ibn Abi Najih that they stand between the Magians and the Jews, their slaughtered meat not eaten nor their women married; and from Ata that the polytheists said of the Prophet that he had "sabi'a". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 145} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 146}

Al-Tabari completes the views on the Sabians: Ibn Zayd says they were people of a religion in the Mosul region who said "there is no god but God" and had no works, scripture or prophet, and did not believe in the Messenger, so the polytheists likened the Prophet and his Companions to them. Others say they worship angels and pray toward the qibla, from al-Hasan and Qatada, al-Hasan relating that Ziyad wanted to lift the poll tax from them because they prayed five times toward the qibla, then learned they worshipped angels; Qatada adds that they read the Psalms, Abu al-Aliya that they are a group of the People of the Book who read the Psalms, and al-Suddi that they are a group of the People of the Book. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 147}
On "whoever believes in God and the Last Day and does good, their reward is with their Lord" al-Tabari says the sense is: whoever accepts and affirms resurrection after death and does righteous deeds has the reward of his deeds with his Lord, and that this completes the first sentence, "whoever of them believes", the word "of them" being omitted because the wording indicates it. He explains that a believer's "believing" here means holding fast to his faith without changing it, while the belief of Jews, Christians and Sabians is acceptance of Muhammad and what he brought; whoever of them believes in Muhammad and the Last Day and does good, without altering until he dies, has the reward of his deeds. He also explains why the verb is singular and the pronoun plural in "so for them", since "man" is singular in form and plural in meaning, citing a verse of surah Yunus and two lines of poetry. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 148} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 149} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 150}
On "no fear shall be on them, nor shall they grieve" he says they will have no fear of the terrors of resurrection they come to, nor grief for what they left of this world on seeing what God has prepared for them. He gives al-Suddi's report that the verse came down about the companions of Salman al-Farisi, and tells Salman's story as al-Suddi relates it: from Jundishapur, a friend of the king's son, they met a monk reading the Gospel and became Muslims at his hands, after which Salman went to a church in Mosul with sixty men who worshipped God, then travelled with an old man to Bayt al-Maqdis, who told him that a prophet's time was near. The account continues in the following pages; it is al-Suddi's report and al-Tabari passes no verdict on it here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 150} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 151} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 152}

Al-Suddi's report continues: Salman's old teacher described the awaited prophet's sign as the seal of prophethood between his shoulders, one who eats a gift and not charity; Salman was then sold in Medina to a woman of Juhayna to tend sheep, and when the Prophet came to Medina Salman went to him, recognised the seal, brought him charity, which he did not eat but told the Muslims to eat, and then a gift, which he ate. Salman spoke of his monk companions, and the Prophet said they were people of the Fire, which grieved Salman, and the verse came down. Al-Suddi makes the "belief" of the Jews holding to the Torah and the way of Moses until Jesus came, and that of the Christians holding to the Gospel until Muhammad came. The editor says this report is broken, and that Salman's conversion story is narrated in many ways in the Musnad, the Mustadrak and elsewhere, with differences in its telling that are hard to reconcile. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 153} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 154}
From Mujahid: Salman asked the Prophet about those Christians and their deeds and was told they had not died as Muslims, so the earth darkened for him, and the verse came down; the Prophet then said that whoever died on the religion of Jesus and as a Muslim before hearing of him is in good standing, while whoever hears of him today and does not believe has perished, which the editor also calls broken. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 154} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 155}
Ibn Abbas is reported to have said that God sent down after this verse the verse of Al Imran "whoever seeks other than Islam as a religion", and al-Tabari takes this to mean Ibn Abbas thought God had promised paradise to those of the Jews, Christians and Sabians who did good, and then abrogated it. He then sets out the verse as Mujahid and al-Suddi read it, and holds that the first interpretation, which is his own choice, fits the apparent wording better, since God did not reserve the reward for righteous belief for some of His creatures over others, and "whoever believes" reports on all those named at the verse's start. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 155} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 156}
