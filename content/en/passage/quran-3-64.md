## Summary
Verse 64 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 64 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 64.** Al-Tabari says the meaning is: say, Muhammad, to the People of the Book, that is the people of the Torah and the Gospel, turn to a fair formula on which both sides can meet, and the formula is that we single out God for worship, disowning every object of worship besides Him and associating nothing with Him, and that none of us submit to another in obedience in what is disobedience to God or honour him with prostration as one prostrates to one's Lord. If they decline the invitation, the believers are to tell them that they affirm their own submission to God. After that the interpreters differ on whom the verse was revealed about. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 483}

**First view: the Jews of Medina (reports 7191-7193).** Al-Tabari gives (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) that it was mentioned to them that the Prophet called the Jews of Medina, those who had argued about Abraham, to the just word (7191); report 7192 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Abdallah ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is to the same effect; and the next report (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj, its number garbled in the printed text and inferred here as 7193) says it reached them that the Prophet called the Jews of Medina to this, they refused, and he fought them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 483} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 484}

**Second view: the Najran delegation (reports 7194-7196).** Report 7194 (Ibn Humayd, from Salama, from Ibn Ishaq, from Muhammad ibn Jafar ibn al-Zubayr) says the Prophet called the Najran delegation to fairness and cut off their argument; the editor refers to Ibn Hisham (2: 232) and to the report having come earlier as 7181. Report 7195 (Musa, from Amr, from Asbat, from al-Suddi) says he called the delegation of the Christians of Najran. In report 7196 (Yunus, from Ibn Wahb, from Ibn Zayd) what was explained about Jesus in the previous verse has already been covered (the editor points to report 7178), the delegation refused, and the Prophet was told to call them to something easier than that; so he called them to the just word and recited up to the clause about lords, but they refused both this and the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 484} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 485}

**Al-Tabari's choice: the verse concerns both peoples.** Al-Tabari says that "People of the Book" means the people of both books, since God did not single out one group, so directing it to the people of the Torah is no better than directing it to the people of the Gospel, nor the reverse, there being no indication of restriction and no sound report. So every holder of a book must be meant, because devoting worship to God alone and pure monotheism are obligatory for everyone under command and prohibition among His creatures, and the name People of the Book applies to both groups. This is al-Tabari's choice and argument. The editor notes that the printed text altered the manuscript because the editor could not read a corrupted word in it; the manuscript has "and you are the People of the Book" through the scribe's slip in place of "and the name", and the correct reading is the one he adopted. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 485}

**The verb of summoning.** Al-Tabari says it means come forward and hasten, and that the form is a reciprocal pattern from the root of height, so one who says it to a companion is asking him to come up to him, as one says "draw near me" from nearness and "come close to me" from closeness. The editor says Abu Jafar explained it at two earlier places (p. 474 and p. 483) and gives the full treatment here, and he does not know why he did not do so the first time; he also notes the printed text corrupted the manuscript and he corrected it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 485}

**The just word and its grammatical case.** Al-Tabari says the qualifier describing the word means justice. He then sets out the difference among the grammarians over why this word, in origin a noun and not an adjective, follows the word it describes in case. Some Basran grammarians said it is in the genitive because it is an attribute of the word, meaning level or equal, and had a verbal noun of levelness been meant it would be accusative; or the speaker may take it as levelness and still put it in the genitive as an attribute, as "creation" can be both a noun and a description, and they cite [[passage/quran-22-25|22:25]], where the word is a noun for the second party and not an adjective. The nominative, they said, is also good in this sense because it does not change, is not dual, plural or feminine, and so resembles nouns such as justice, content and one in a state of ritual impurity; and they point to [[passage/quran-45-21|45:21]], where equality belongs to life and death as the subject. He adds that you may also treat it as a fronted attribute following the first. The editor says he added a bracketed phrase to make the sense clear, fears there is a gap in the sentence he could not make out, and refers to Abu Jafar's explanation of this verse at an earlier place (Bulaq edition, number garbled in the scan). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 486} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 487}

**The Kufan grammarians.** Some Kufan grammarians said it is a verbal noun put in the place of the participle, that is in the place of a word such as "being equal", so sometimes it follows the participle and sometimes the verbal noun, and in the sense of justice it can have two other forms, as in [[passage/quran-20-58|20:58]], meaning a just and fair place between two parties. It is related from Ibn Masud that he read this place with the word for justice. The editor explains that "the verb" here means the derived adjective like the active and passive participles, and that this is the view of al-Farra in Maani al-Quran (1: 220). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 487}

**Reports 7197 and 7198.** Interpreters said the like of al-Tabari's account that the word means justice. Report 7197 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada) says it is a just word between us and you; report 7198 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same. The editor notes that the manuscript lacks the words "the same", that the publisher or an earlier copyist added them because the report seemed incomplete, and he thinks the rest of the commentary dropped out of the first scribe's copy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 487}

**Third view: the declaration of God's oneness (report 7199).** Others said the just word is the declaration of God's oneness itself. Al-Tabari gives (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) that Abu al-Aliya said this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 488}

**The grammar of the clause about worshipping God alone.** Al-Tabari says the particle introducing the clause stands in the genitive in the sense "come to the matter of not worshipping any but God", and the editor refers to al-Farra in Maani al-Quran (1: 220). Al-Tabari adds that he explained the sense of worship in Arabic speech earlier and showed the sound meaning of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 488}

**Lordship among people (reports 7200 and 7201).** Al-Tabari says that the mutual lordship the verse rules out means followers obeying leaders in what they order of disobedience to God and in abandoning what God forbade them, as in [[passage/quran-9-31|9:31]] concerning the rabbis, the monks and the Messiah. In report 7200 (al-Qasim, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) the sense is that none of us obeys another in disobeying God, and it is said that this lordship is people obeying their masters and leaders in matters other than worship, even if they do not pray to them. Others said it means prostrating to one another, which is report 7201 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Hafs ibn Umar, from al-Hakam ibn Aban, from Ikrima). The editor notes that the printed text has an added word with no basis in the manuscript. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 488} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 489}

**The call to bear witness to submission.** Al-Tabari says it means: if those you invite to the fair formula decline and disbelieve, then say to them, believers, be witnesses to our declaration that we acknowledge what you turned away from, the oneness of God, pure servitude to Him, and that He is the God who has no partner; and Muslims are those who submit to God in this, humbling themselves before Him by affirming it with hearts and tongues. He says he explained the sense of Islam earlier. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 489}
