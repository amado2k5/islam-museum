## Place in the museum

**Commentary on verse 101 (pp. 278-282)**

On "my Lord, You have given me of sovereignty and taught me of the interpretation of events; Creator of the heavens and the earth, You are my protector in this world and the Hereafter; take me as one submitting and join me with the righteous", al-Tabari says: Joseph said, after God had united his parents and brothers and spread over him of this world what He spread of honour and established him in the land, longing for the meeting of his righteous fathers: "my Lord, You have given me of sovereignty", meaning the sovereignty of Egypt, "and taught me of the interpretation of events", meaning the interpretation of dreams, counting God's favours to him and giving thanks for them, "Creator of the heavens and the earth", that is, O their Creator and Maker, "You are my protector in this world and the Hereafter", that is, You are my protector in my world against whoever was hostile to me and meant me harm, by Your help, and You nourish me in it with Your blessing, and in the Hereafter You will be my protector by Your grace and mercy, "take me as one submitting", that is, take me to You as one who has submitted, "and join me with the righteous", that is, join me with my righteous fathers Ibrahim and Ishaq and those before them of Your prophets and messengers. The editor refers to the earlier discussions of "ta'wil", "fatir", "wali" and "tawaffi". It is said that no prophet wished for death before Joseph. Report 19940, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: Ibn Abbas used to say: the first prophet to ask God for death was Joseph. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 278}

Report 19941, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "my Lord, You have given me of sovereignty", the verse; he said, he longed to meet his Lord and wished to join Him and his fathers, so he asked God to take him and join him to them, and no prophet ever asked for death except Joseph. Ibn Jurayj said: in some part of the Qur'an a prophet said "take me". The editor says he found no evidence in the Qur'an for what Ibn Jurayj said and thinks he erred, since the prohibition of wishing for death is explicit in the Sunna. Report 19942, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "take me as one submitting and join me with the righteous", when his family was gathered and his eyes comforted, he being then immersed in the growth of this world, its kingship and its freshness, he longed for the righteous before him. Ibn Abbas used to say that no prophet ever wished for death before Joseph. The editor explains "nabt" here as abundant wealth and growing blessing. Report 19943, from Qatada, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr, Sufyan and Ibn Abi Arouba: {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 279}

when Joseph's family was gathered and favours were complete, he asked to meet his Lord and said "my Lord, You have given me of sovereignty", the verse. Qatada said: no one ever wished for death, prophet or other, except Joseph. Report 19944, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Hisham, al-Walid ibn Muslim, more than one narrator and Ibn Abi Najih: when Joseph was united with his father and brothers, being then king of Egypt, he longed for God and for his righteous fathers Ibrahim and Ishaq, and said "my Lord, You have given me of sovereignty", the verse. Report 19945, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Hisham and Muslim ibn Khalid: "and taught me of the interpretation of events", he said, the interpretation of dreams. Report 19946, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: "take me as one submitting and join me with the righteous", he says, take me in obedience to You and forgive me when You take me. Report 19947, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: Joseph said, when he saw what he saw of God's honour and favour to him and his household, when God had united his family, returned him to his father and brought them together in the kingship and joy he was in, "O my father, this is the interpretation of my vision of before; my Lord has made it true", up to "He is the Knowing, the Wise"; then Joseph held back and remembered that what he was in of this world is passing away, and said "my Lord, You have given me of sovereignty", the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 280}

It is reported that Ya'qub's sons who did to Joseph what they did had their father ask forgiveness for them, and God turned to them, pardoned them and forgave their sin. Report 19948, from Anas ibn Malik, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj, Salih al-Murri and Yazid al-Raqashi: when God had united Ya'qub's family and comforted his eyes, his sons withdrew to confer and said to one another: do you not know what you did, and what the old man and Joseph suffered from you? They said: yes. He said: does their pardon beguile you? What of your Lord! They agreed to go to the old man and sat before him, with Joseph sitting beside his father, and said: O our father, we have come to you in a matter we never came to you in the like of, and a matter has befallen us the like of which never befell us! until they moved him, prophets being the most merciful of creation; he said: what is the matter, my sons? They said: do you not know what we did to you and to our brother Joseph? He said: yes. They said: have you two not pardoned? They said: yes. They said: your pardon avails us nothing if God has not pardoned us! He said: what do you want, my sons? They said: we want you to pray to God for us, and when revelation comes to you from God that He has pardoned what we did, our eyes will be cooled and our hearts at rest, and if not, there is no coolness of eye for us in this world ever. The old man stood facing the qibla, Joseph stood behind his father and they stood behind the two, humble and abased; he prayed and Joseph said amen, and there was no answer about them for twenty years; Salih al-Murri said, frightening them. Then at the head of twenty years Gabriel came down to Ya'qub and said: God, blessed and exalted, sent me to you to give you the good news that He has answered your prayer for your children, has pardoned what they did and has tied their covenants after you to prophethood. The editor says Salih al-Murri is Salih ibn Bashir ibn Wada', a rejected narrator, a storyteller abandoned in hadith, and Yazid al-Raqashi is Yazid ibn Aban, also an abandoned storyteller, and that this report is "ruined by these two abandoned storytellers". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 281}

Report 19949, from Abu Imran al-Jawni, through al-Muthanna, al-Harith, Abd al-Aziz and Ja'far ibn Sulayman: by God, had the killing of Joseph gone ahead, God would have put them all in the Fire, but God, mighty be His praise, held back Joseph's life so that his affair might reach its end, and as mercy to them. He then says: by God, God did not tell their story to taunt them with it, for they were prophets among the people of Paradise, but God told us their story so that His servant should not despair. It is reported that Ya'qub died before Joseph and bequeathed to Joseph, ordering him to bury him beside the grave of his father Ishaq. Report 19950, from al-Suddi, through Ibn Waki', Amr and Asbat: when death came to Ya'qub he bequeathed to Joseph that he be buried with Ibrahim and Ishaq. When he died he was embalmed with myrrh and carried to al-Sham. When they reached that place Esau, Ya'qub's brother, came and said: he beat me to the invocation, but by God he will not beat me to the grave! and he refused to let them bury him. When they were held up, Hisham ibn Dan ibn Ya'qub, who was deaf, said to some of his brothers: why is my grandfather not buried? They said: your uncle is preventing it! He said: show me where he is. When he saw him, Hisham raised his hand and struck Esau's head so hard that his eyes fell onto Ya'qub's thigh, and the two were buried in one grave. The editor gives the names in this report as they appear in the People of the Book's scripture and corrects the printed wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 282}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
