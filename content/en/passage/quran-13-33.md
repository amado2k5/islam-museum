**Commentary on verse 33 (pp. 462-468)**

On "is He who stands over every soul for what it has earned [like those who ascribe partners]? They made partners for God. Say: name them! Or are you informing Him of what He does not know on earth, or is it a manifest word? Rather their plotting was made attractive to those who disbelieved, and they were barred from the path; and whomever God leads astray has no guide", al-Tabari says, mighty be His praise: is the Lord who is everlasting, who does not perish or die, who stands over the guarding of the sustenance of all creation, who guarantees it, who knows them and what they earn of deeds, who watches over them so that nothing escapes Him wherever they are, like one who is perishing and gone, who hears nothing, sees nothing, understands nothing, and cannot ward off harm from himself or from whoever worships him or bring benefit to either, are the two alike? The answer is omitted, it not being said "like this and that", the hearer's knowledge of what was mentioned sufficing for what was left unmentioned; for when He, mighty be His praise, said "they made partners for God" it was known that the sense of the speech is "like their partners whom they took as gods", as al-Qattal al-Kilabi said in a rajaz addressing Umm Ali to choose between a short man and another with a torn shirt, then not stating the other alternative, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 462}

not saying "and between this and that", it being enough that he said "that one, or the one with the torn shirt?" and that the report about the torn-shirted man shows what he meant. In the footnotes the editor notes that the rajaz is in the Aghani, that Umm Ali is Aliya, a woman of Banu Nasr ibn Mu'awiya whom al-Qattal wrote love poetry about, that the "tinbal" is the short man, and that he judges the best sense to be that they praise the man as one who is always travelling and raiding, so his clothes keep wearing out. The interpreters said the same: report 20440, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: "is He who stands over every soul for what it has earned", that is, your Lord, blessed and exalted, who stands over the children of Adam by their provisions and their terms and preserves their deeds for them, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 463}

Report 20441, from Qatada, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la, Ibn Thawr and Ma'mar: "is He who stands over every soul for what it has earned", he said, God stands over every soul. Report 20442, from Ibn Abbas, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd: "is He who stands over every soul for what it has earned", he means Himself, saying He is with you wherever you are, so no doer does a deed but God is present; and it is said that they are the angels assigned to the children of Adam; the editor notes that this last sentence is without doubt not Ibn Abbas's but seems to be Muhammad ibn Sa'd's, the narrator of the report. Report 20443, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "is He who stands over every soul for what it has earned", over their provision and their food; I stand over that and they are My servants, and then they made partners for Me. Report 20444, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: it is God who stands over every righteous and wicked, providing for them and guarding them, and then some of them associate others with Him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 464}

On "they made partners for God. Say: name them! Or are you informing Him of what He does not know on earth, or is it a manifest word?" he says, mighty be His praise: I am the One who stands over the sustenance of these polytheists, directs their affairs and preserves their deeds, and they made partners for Me from My creation to worship besides Me; say to them, Muhammad, name these whom you associated in the worship of God; if they say "gods" they have lied, for there is no god but the One, the Subduer, who has no partner; "or are you informing Him of what He does not know on earth" means, are you telling Him there is a god on earth when there is no god but Him on earth or in heaven? The interpreters said the same: report 20445, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd: "they made partners for God. Say: name them", had they named them with a single word they would have lied and said what is not true, because God is one and has no partner; God said "or are you informing Him of what He does not know on earth, or is it a manifest word", he says, God does not know a god on earth other than Himself. Report 20446, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abd Allah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: "they made partners for God. Say: name them", and God created them. Report 20447, from Ibn Jurayj, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn and Hajjaj: "they made partners for God. Say: name them", had they named them they would have lied and said in that what God does not know, that there is a god other than God; that is His saying "or are you informing Him of what He does not know on earth". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 465}

"Or is it a manifest word?" is heard, but in truth it is false and baseless. The interpreters said much as we said, except that they said "a manifest" means "a falsehood", giving the meaning the word points to without stating the truth of its interpretation. Report 20448, from Mujahid, through al-Hasan ibn Muhammad, Shababa, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "by a manifest word", by supposition. Report 20449, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah and Waraqa: the same. Report 20450, from Qatada, through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Hajjaj and Ibn Jurayj: "or by a manifest word", and "the manifest of speech" is falsehood. Report 20451, from al-Dahhak, through al-Husayn ibn al-Faraj, Abu Mu'adh and Ubayd ibn Sulayman: "or by a manifest word", he says, or by false speech and lying, and were they to speak they would speak falsehood and lies. On "nay, their plotting was made attractive to those who disbelieved" he says, mighty be His praise: God has no partner in the heavens or on the earth, but their plotting was made attractive to the polytheists who call on a god besides Him, and that is their fabrication and lying against God. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 466}

Mujahid used to say that "plotting" here means speech, as if He meant their saying of associating partners with God. Report 20452, from Mujahid, through al-Muthanna, Ishaq, Abd Allah, Waraqa and Ibn Abi Najih: "nay, their plotting was made attractive to those who disbelieved", he said, their speech. Report 20453, from Mujahid, through Muhammad ibn Amr, Abu Asim and Isa: the same. As for "and they were barred from the path", the readers differed in reading it. Most of the Kufan readers read "wa suddu", with a damma on the sad, meaning God barred them from His path because of their disbelief in Him, the sad being given a damma because the agent is not named. Most of the readers of the Hijaz and Basra read it with a fatha on the sad, in the sense that the polytheists are the ones who barred people from God's path. Al-Tabari says: the correct view in my opinion is that they are two well-known readings, each read by leading readers, close in meaning, since the polytheists were barred from faith in Him and were also barring others, as God described them by saying "those who disbelieve spend their wealth to bar from God's path" [[passage/quran-8-36|8:36]]. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 467}

On "and whomever God leads astray has no guide" he says, mighty be His praise: whoever God has led astray from attaining the truth and guidance by forsaking him has no one to guide him to attain them, for that is attained only by God's enabling and help, and that is in God's hand and to Him it belongs, not to anyone else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v16 p. 468}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
