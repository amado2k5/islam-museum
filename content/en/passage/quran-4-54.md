## Summary
Verse 54 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 54 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Envy and the Jews (p. 476, reports 9812-9814).** Al-Tabari says the meaning is whether these Jews, to whom a part of the earlier scripture came, envy others. Muhammad b. Amr (9812, from Abu Asim, from Isa, from Ibn Abi Najih) reports from Mujahid that they are the Jews; al-Muthanna (9813, from Abu Hudhayfa, from Shibl) likewise; Bishr b. Mu'adh (9814, from Yazid, from Sa'id) from Qatada likewise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 476}

**The view that "people" means Muhammad alone (pp. 476-477, reports 9815-9819).** The interpreters differed over whom God meant by the people. Some said Muhammad alone. Al-Muthanna (9815, from Amr, from Asbat, from Hushaym, from Khalid) reports this from Ikrima; Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9816, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) from al-Suddi; Muhammad b. Sa'd (9817, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) from Ibn Abbas likewise; al-Qasim (9818, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj, from Ibn Jurayj) from Mujahid; al-Husayn b. al-Faraj (9819, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) from al-Dahhak similarly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 477}

**The view that it means the Arabs (p. 477, report 9820).** Others said God meant the Arabs. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9820, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that the Jews envied this tribe of the Arabs for what God gave them from His generosity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 477}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the meaning (pp. 477-478, note 1).** Abu Ja'far says the soundest view is that God rebuked the Jews described in these verses, saying to them, after their telling the idol-worshipping polytheists that they were better guided than Muhammad and his companions though they knew they were lying: do you envy Muhammad and his companions for what God gave them? His reason is that what precedes was a censure of the Jews who said that to the unbelievers, so joining this to that censure and to the praise of the believers is likelier and better unless a proof turns its sense away. The editor notes that the print has another interrogative form of the verb and that the right reading is the manuscript's. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 478}

**The favour is prophethood (p. 478, reports 9821-9822, note 1).** They also differed over the favour God said He gave those mentioned. Some said it is prophethood. Bishr b. Mu'adh (9821, from Yazid, from Sa'id) reports from Qatada that the Jews envied this tribe of the Arabs because God raised a prophet from among them; al-Qasim (9822, from al-Husayn, from Hajjaj) from Ibn Jurayj that it is prophethood. The editor refers to the language indexes for earlier explanations of the word for favour. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 478}

**The view that it is the permission to the Prophet to marry (pp. 478-479, reports 9823-9825, note 1).** Others said the favour is what God allowed His prophet Muhammad of women, to marry as many as he wished without limit, the people being Muhammad as above. Muhammad b. Sa'd (9823, from his father, his uncle, his father and his grandfather) reports from Ibn Abbas that the People of the Book said: Muhammad claims he was given what he was given in humility, and he has nine wives and cares only for marriage; what kingship is better than this? Muhammad b. al-Husayn (9824, from Ahmad b. Mufaddal, from Asbat) reports from al-Suddi that the meaning is Muhammad in marrying what women he wished. Al-Husayn b. al-Faraj (9825, from Abu Mu'adh, from Ubayd b. Sulayman) reports from al-Dahhak that the Jews said: what is Muhammad's case, given prophethood as he claims while hungry and unclothed, with no concern but marrying women? So they envied him for taking wives, and God allowed him to marry as many as he wished. The editor notes that print and manuscript dropped part of the chain, which he supplied; it is a recurring chain in the commentary, the nearest being report 9819; he then adds a remark about the present day that is partly unreadable in the scan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 479}

**Al-Tabari's choice on the favour (p. 479, note 2).** Abu Ja'far says the sounder of the two interpretations is that of Qatada and Ibn Jurayj: the favour here is prophethood, by which God preferred Muhammad and honoured the Arabs by giving it to a man from among them and not others, since the plain sense of the verse points to praise of the Prophet and his companions, as he explained earlier; marriage and taking wives, though of God's favour to His servants, is not praise or commendation of them. The editor notes that the print has a different invocation for the companions from the manuscript, that he restored the manuscript's, and that he has done so often without remarking on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 479}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 479 in the commentary on verse 54 of sura 4, within al-Tabari's choice between the two interpretations of the favour, and continue from p. 480. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v08 p. 479}
