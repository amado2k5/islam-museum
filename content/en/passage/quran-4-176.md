**Commentary on verse 176: the kalala and a sister's share (pp. 430-433)**

Al-Tabari says 'they ask you for a ruling' means they ask you, Muhammad, to rule for them about the kalala, and that in his view the kalala is what is other than child and parent. The ruling means: if a person dies, leaving no son or daughter, and has a sister of the full blood or of the father's side, she has half his estate as inheritance from him, apart from the rest of his male relatives; what is left goes to his male relatives. Report 10864, from al-Suddi: 'died' means died. Report 10865, from Qatada: the Companions of the Prophet were concerned about the kalala, so God revealed this verse about it; and it is reported that Abu Bakr said in his sermon that the verse at the start of sura al-Nisa on shares was revealed about child and parent, the second about spouses and maternal siblings, the one at the end of al-Nisa about full and half siblings, and the one at the end of al-Anfal about those of kin. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 431}

Report 10866, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: Umar ibn al-Khattab asked the Prophet about the kalala; he said: has God not made it clear? Then the verse came down. Report 10867, from Jabir ibn Abd Allah through Abu al-Zubayr, Ibn Ulayya and Hisham al-Dastuwa'i: Jabir was ill with nine sisters; the Prophet came to him and breathed on his face, and he recovered and asked whether he could bequeath two-thirds to his sisters; the Prophet said good, and when asked about half, said good; the Prophet left and returned and said God had revealed on what is due to his sisters, giving them two-thirds; Jabir used to say this verse was revealed about him. Report 10869, from Jabir through Ibn al-Munkadir and Sufyan ibn Uyayna: when he fell ill the Prophet and Abu Bakr walked to visit, he fainted, and the Prophet poured water of his ablution on him; he asked how to dispose of his wealth, with nine sisters and no father or child, and the Prophet did not answer until the inheritance verse was revealed. Report 10870, from al-Bara' ibn Azib: the last verse revealed was the kalala verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 433}

Report 10871, from al-Bara' ibn Azib through Abu Ishaq: the last verse revealed of the Qur'an was the kalala verse at the end of sura al-Nisa. Report 10872, in the same sense, through Malik ibn Mughawwal and Abu al-Safar; report 10873 gives: the last sura revealed complete was Bara'a, and the last verse to come down as the close of al-Nisa was the kalala verse. The editor notes the report is in Muslim, al-Bayhaqi and al-Tirmidhi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 434}

Al-Tabari notes disagreement on where the verse was revealed: Jabir ibn Abd Allah said in Medina; others said on a journey the Prophet and his Companions were making. Report 10874, from Ibn Sirin through Ma'mar and Ayyub: the Prophet was on a journey with Hudhayfa ibn al-Yaman beside him; the Prophet passed the verse to Hudhayfa, who passed it to Umar riding behind him. When Umar became caliph he asked Hudhayfa about it, and Hudhayfa said: you would be foolish to think your office obliges me to tell you what I did not tell you then; Umar said: that was not my intent. Report 10875 says the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 435}

Report 10876, from Ibn Sirin through Ibn Awn and Ibn Ulayya: Umar and Hudhayfa were on the journey; the Prophet passed the verse to Hudhayfa and Hudhayfa to Umar. Later Umar asked Hudhayfa, who said: you would be foolish if you thought the Prophet taught it to me and I taught it to you otherwise, and by God I will add nothing to it. Umar used to say: O God, if I explained it to him, then it was not explained to me. Al-Tabari notes that Umar's statements on the kalala differed: that it is one with no child and no father at his death, and before his death that it is what is other than the father. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 436}

Report 10877, from Ma'dan ibn Abi Talha: Umar ibn al-Khattab said: I never argued with the Messenger of God about anything as I argued about the kalala verse, until he struck my chest and said: the summer verse at the end of al-Nisa is enough for you, and I will judge about it in a way that readers and non-readers will know: it is what is other than the father. Al-Tabari adds that Umar said: I am ashamed to differ with Abu Bakr on it, and Abu Bakr used to say: it is what is other than child and parent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 437}

Report 10878, from Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: Umar wrote a letter on the grandfather and the kalala, then stayed praying God's guidance about it; when he was stabbed he called for the writing and erased it, and said: I had written a letter on the grandfather and kalala and had asked God's guidance; I decided to leave you as you were. Report 10879 is the same. Report 10880, from Murra al-Hamdani: Umar said: three things I would rather the Prophet had made clear to us than the world and all in it: the kalala, the caliphate, and the doors of usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 438}

Report 10881, from al-Amash: Umar said that he would rather know the kalala than have the tribute of Roman palaces. Report 10882, from Tariq ibn Shihab: Umar gathered the Companions with a writing tablet and said he would rule on the kalala in a way women would talk of in their chambers; a snake came out of the house and they scattered, and he said: if God had wanted this matter completed He would have completed it. Report 10883, from Ibn Umar: Umar on the pulpit of Medina said that he wished the Prophet had not left us before he gave a firm charge on three things: the hadd, the kalala, and the doors of usury. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 439}

Report 10884, from Ma'dan ibn Abi Talha through Qatada and Sa'id ibn Abi Arubah: Umar said he asked the Prophet about nothing more than the kalala, until he jabbed his finger in Umar's chest and said: the summer verse at the end of al-Nisa is enough for you. Report 10885 is the same. Report 10886: if I live I will judge in a way no reader of the Qur'an will disagree with. Report 10887 gives the same from Shu'ba. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 440}

Report 10888, from al-Hasan ibn Masruq through his father: Umar, asked about a kinsman who had inherited by kalala, said: kalala, kalala, kalala! and swore that knowing it was dearer to him than all on earth; he had asked the Prophet, who said: did you not hear the verse revealed in the summer? and repeated it three times. The editor notes that the chain of this report is weak and cannot be relied upon. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 441}

Report 10889, from Abu Salama: a man came to the Prophet and asked about the kalala; he said: did you not hear the verse revealed in the summer? Report 10890, from Abu al-Khayr: a man asked Uqba about the kalala, and he said: do you not wonder that he asks me about the kalala, when nothing confounded the Companions of the Prophet as the kalala did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 442}

Al-Tabari then asks how to understand the ruling on the sister, given the agreement of the people of the qibla, apart from Ibn Abbas and Ibn al-Zubayr, that a daughter takes half and the sister the rest. His answer: the sister takes half as a fixed share only when the dead man left no son or daughter and was inherited by kalala; when he left a daughter, she becomes a residuary heir and takes what the other residuaries would take. God set out her right when the man is inherited by kalala and set out what she has when there is a child, by revelation through His Messenger, making her a residuary with the daughters of the child. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 443}

On 'and he inherits her if she has no child': it means the brother of a woman inherits her if she dies before him, when she is inherited by kalala and has no child or father. On 'if they are two, they have two-thirds of what he left', it means: if the sisters left are two, they have two-thirds of what their brother left; if there are brothers and sisters, the male has the like of the share of two females; this is when he is inherited by kalala. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v09 p. 444}
