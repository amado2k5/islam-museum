**Commentary on verse 105 (p. 13 onward)**

Al-Tabari says that the readers differed in reading "worthy that I say nothing about God but the truth". A group of the readers of Mecca, Medina, Basra and Kufa read the word "on" with the letter ya left unstressed (and without doubling), giving the sense "I am worthy of not saying about God anything but the truth", taking "on" in the sense of "with", as one says "I shot with the bow" or "on the bow", and "I came in a good state" with either preposition. The editor refers to earlier passages and to al-Farra's "Ma'ani al-Qur'an". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 13}

The editor then notes that some who knew the speech of the Arabs said that, read this way, the sense is "eager that I say nothing" or "so it is right that I say nothing"; the editor explains that the wording is from Abu Ubayda's "Majaz al-Qur'an", and that both the printed text and the manuscript garble it. A group of the people of Medina read the word "on" with the doubled ya, in the sense "it is binding on me not to say" and "it is a duty on me not to say". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 14}

Al-Tabari's judgement is that these are two well-known readings with close meanings, each read by leading readers, so whichever a reader uses, he is right. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 14}

On "I have brought you a clear proof from your Lord" he says Moses told Pharaoh and his chiefs that he had brought them evidence from their Lord, bearing witness to the truth of what he said and to his being sent to them by God as a messenger; so let Pharaoh send the Children of Israel with him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v13 p. 14}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
