## Summary
Verse 6 of [[passage/quran-4|Surat an-Nisa]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 6 of 176 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of testing (p. 574, reports 8571-8575).** Abu Jafar says the first command of the verse means that guardians of orphans are to test the minds of their orphans in their understanding, their soundness in religion and their handling of their wealth. Qatada and al-Hasan (8571, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq from Mamar): examine the orphans; al-Suddi (8572): try their minds; Mujahid (8573), their minds, where the text of the printed page has an unclear spelling that we read from the context; Ibn Abbas (8574, through al-Muthanna from Abd Allah ibn Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha): examine them; Ibn Zayd (8575): test him in his judgment and his mind, how he is, and when it is known that sound judgment has been sensed in him his wealth is handed over, and that is after puberty. Al-Tabari says he showed earlier that the word means testing, which spares repeating it, and the editor's note refers to those places. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 574}

**The sense of reaching marriageable age (pp. 574-575, reports 8576-8578).** Al-Tabari says it means reaching puberty. Mujahid (8576, through Muhammad ibn Amr from Abu Asim from Isa from Ibn Abi Najih): until they reach puberty; Ibn Abbas (8577, through Ali ibn Dawud from Abd Allah ibn Salih): at puberty; Ibn Zayd (8578): puberty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 574} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 575}

**The sense of sensing soundness (p. 575).** Al-Tabari says it means you find in them and know. Ibn Abbas (8579, through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih): you know from them. In the language one says of someone that one perceived good in him with a long alif, meaning perception, and that one felt at ease with him with a short alif, when one has become familiar. He notes it is also reported that in Abd Allah's reading a different verb is used, meaning you perceived, you found. In the editor's notes, the printing misread a word in the manuscript and spoiled the sentence; and al-Farra' in Maani al-Quran (1: 257) gives the verb with a single silent letter and some copies of him agree with al-Tabari's text, while the manuscript wrote it with a doubled letter in both places, which is a mistake, the correct form being that of the printing and of al-Farra'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 575}

**The sense of sound judgment (pp. 576-578, reports 8580-8587).** They differed. The first view is mind and soundness in religion: al-Suddi (8580); Qatada (8581, through Bishr ibn Mu'adh): soundness of his mind and religion. The second is soundness in religion and good care of wealth: al-Hasan (8582, through Ibn Waki' from his father from Mubarak): right guidance in religion, soundness, and keeping the wealth; Ibn Abbas (8583, through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih): in their condition and in the good care of their wealth. The third is the mind only: Mujahid (8584, through Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan from Mansur): we do not hand the orphan his wealth even if he takes hold of his beard, that is, even if he is an old man, until sound judgment, meaning the mind, is sensed in him (the editor explains that white hair has taken hold of his beard); Mujahid (8585, through Ibn Bashshar from Yahya): the mind; al-Sha'bi (8586, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Hushaym from Abu Shubruma): a man may have his beard grasped and not yet have reached sound judgment. In the editor's note, Abu Shubruma is the kunya of Ibn Shubruma, the judge, jurist and mufti Abd Allah ibn Shubruma ibn Hassan al-Dabbi, described as chaste, resolute, intelligent, learned in law, close to the ascetics in manner, reliable in hadith, a poet, of good character and generous. The fourth is soundness and knowledge of what improves his wealth: Ibn Jurayj (8587). Al-Tabari holds that the soundest is the mind together with good care of wealth, because all agree that whoever is like that is not among those who deserve interdiction on his wealth and the taking of what is in his hand away from him, even if he is wicked in his religion; and since that is agreed, the same ruling applies when an orphan comes of age and has wealth in the hand of his father's executor or of a judge who managed it during his childhood: the wealth must be handed to him if he is sane, adult and a good manager who does not squander, because the reason for which one is put under another's management is the reason for which his hand is withheld from his own wealth, so there is no difference; and whoever distinguishes between the two is asked for the distinction from a principle or an analogy and whatever he says of one he will be bound to say of the other; so the sound judgment that entitles the orphan to his wealth is a sound mind and good care of wealth. In the editor's notes, an extra letter in one phrase in both witnesses is to be deleted, and in another place the printing has "if" where the manuscript has the correct "since". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 576} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 577} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 578}

**The command to hand over and the warning against excess (p. 578, reports 8588-8589).** Al-Tabari says the address is to the guardians of the orphans' wealth: when your orphans reach puberty and you find in them a sound mind and good care of their wealth, hand it over and do not hold it back from them; the warning against excess means not consuming it in a way other than what God has permitted them, where the editor says the printing has a plural pronoun and he adopted the manuscript's. Qatada and al-Hasan (8588, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq): do not be extravagant with it. Then report 8589 begins, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal; in the editor's note he is Muhammad ibn al-Husayn ibn Musa ibn Abi Hunayn al-Kufi (entry at report 7120), the manuscript and printing having "Muhammad ibn al-Hasan", a mistake, since this is a chain recurring in the commentary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 578}

**End of report 8589 and the sense of extravagance (p. 579).** Al-Suddi (8589, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Ahmad ibn al-Mufaddal from Asbat): he is extravagant in eating. Al-Tabari says the root sense of extravagance is passing the permitted limit into what is not permitted; it may be through excess or through falling short; for excess one says asrafa, yusrifu, israfan, and for falling short sarifa, yasrafu, sarafan, as in the expression "I passed by you and overlooked you", meaning I forgot you and erred about you. He cites a line by a poet describing men who give without reproach and without error, meaning they find the right places for giving and do not miss them. In the editor's note the poet is Jarir; he traces the line to Jarir's diwan, to Tabaqat fuhul al-shuara, to al-Ishtiqaq and to Lisan al-Arab, says it is from the poem praising Yazid ibn Abd al-Malik and satirising the Muhallabid family, gives lines that come before it, and explains the word for a gift of a hundred camels and that it gave its name to a woman's name because a hundred camels were driven as a bride's dowry; he also glosses the number in the line, and the note breaks off at the end of the page with no continuation on the next. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 579}

**Hasty consumption (pp. 580-581, reports 8590-8593).** Al-Tabari says the word means hurrying, a verbal noun from "I hurried the matter", and the address is to the guardians of the orphans' wealth: do not eat it extravagantly, meaning other than what God has made lawful to you, nor in haste before they come of age and show sound judgment, fearing that they will reach it and you will have to hand it over. Ibn Abbas (8590, through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih from Mu'awiya ibn Salih from Ali ibn Abi Talha): eating the orphan's wealth in haste before he comes of age and stands between him and it; Qatada and al-Hasan (8591, through al-Hasan ibn Yahya from Abd al-Razzaq): do not be extravagant with it nor hurry at it, where the editor says the printing has another form and the manuscript has what he adopted; al-Suddi (8592): hurrying before they grow up and take their wealth; Ibn Zayd (8593, through Yunus from Ibn Wahb): this is for the guardian of the orphan who eats from it, God having allowed him to eat with him if he finds nothing, putting his hand with his; then he goes and delays, saying "I will not hand him his wealth", and keeps eating it out of appetite, because if you do not hand it over you have a share in it and if you hand it over you have none. In the editor's note, this sentence is corrupt and undotted in the manuscript, the printing corrected it in places where he thinks it misread and miswrote, though it was right in adding one particle. Al-Tabari adds on grammar that the particle after the word is in the accusative, governed by the word for hastening, since the sense is: do not eat it hurrying their growing up; the editor refers to al-Farra's Maani al-Quran (1: 257). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 580} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 581}

**The rich and the poor: abstaining (pp. 581-582, reports 8594-8596).** Al-Tabari says a guardian who is rich should abstain, by means of his wealth, from eating the orphan's wealth, other than in the manner God allowed and without extravagance or haste. Ibn Abbas (8594, through Ibn Bashshar from Abu Ahmad from Sufyan from al-A'mash and Ibn Abi Layla from al-Hakam from Miqsam): by his own sufficiency, until he is independent of the orphan's wealth; Ibrahim (8595): by his sufficiency; Ibn Abbas (8596, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Ibn Ulayya from Layth from al-Hakam from Miqsam, finishing on p. 582): from his own wealth, and the guardian who is poor and in need of it may eat in the recognised manner. In the editor's note, in 8594 both witnesses have a wrong preposition and the correct one is the one that means "by". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 581} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 582}

**First view on eating in the recognised manner: a loan (pp. 582-586, reports 8597-8620).** Al-Tabari says the interpreters differed over the recognised manner in which God allowed guardians to eat if they are poor; some said it is a loan he takes from the wealth and later repays. Umar ibn al-Khattab (8597, through Abu Kurayb from Waki' from Sufyan and Israil from Abu Ishaq from Haritha ibn Mudarrib): I have put God's wealth, as it stands with me, in the position of an orphan's wealth: if I am rich I abstain, if I am poor I eat in the recognised manner, and when I am at ease I repay. In the editor's note, Haritha ibn Mudarrib al-Kufi narrated from Umar and Ali and Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i narrated from him, with entries in al-Tahdhib, in al-Bukhari's al-Kabir (1/2/87) and in Ibn Abi Hatim (1/2/255), and the printing and the manuscript have a wrong form of the name. Ibn Abbas (8598, through Abu Kurayb from Ibn Atiyya from Zuhayr from al-Ala ibn al-Musayyab from Hammad from Sa'id ibn Jubayr): it is the loan; Ubayda al-Salmani (8599, through Muhammad ibn Abd al-A'la from al-Mu'tamir from Yunus from Ibn Sirin): what he spends from the orphan's wealth is a debt on him; also from him (8600, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Ibn Ulayya from Salama ibn Alqama from Ibn Sirin) that it is only a loan, arguing from the later command to call witnesses at the handover, and Ibn Sirin said: I thought he said it from his own opinion; the same from him (8601, through Abd al-Razzaq from Hisham from Muhammad; 8602, through Hushaym from Salama ibn Alqama, whom the editor identifies as Salama ibn Alqama al-Tamimi, reliable, who narrated from Ibn Sirin, the two witnesses having "Salama from Alqama" by mistake; 8603, through Mamar from Ayyub). Ibn Abbas (8604, through al-Muthanna from Abu Salih): the loan; Ibn Abbas (8605, through Muhammad ibn Sa'd from his father, uncle, father and grandfather): if he is rich it is not lawful for him to eat anything of the orphan's wealth, and if poor he may borrow from it and, when he finds ease, give back what he borrowed, which is eating in the recognised manner. Sa'id ibn Jubayr: he eats as a loan in the recognised manner (8606, through Abu Kurayb from Ibn Idris from his father from Hammad; in the editor's note Ibn Idris is Abd Allah ibn Idris ibn Yazid ibn Abd al-Rahman al-Awdi, Abu Kurayb's teacher, the witnesses having "Abu Idris" by mistake, and his father Idris ibn Yazid al-Awdi narrated from his own father, Abu Ishaq al-Sabi'i, Simak ibn Harb and others, with an entry in al-Tahdhib; the manuscript has "I heard Abu Bakr" where the printing is correct); it is the loan, and what he takes he repays when he becomes well off (8607, through Hushaym from Hajjaj); if he took the amount of his sustenance as a loan he repays it if he later has means, and if death comes to him without ease he asks the orphan to release him, and if the orphan is young he asks his guardian (8608, through Ibn Ulayya from Hisham al-Dastuwa'i from Hammad, where the editor says the manuscript wording differs and the printing fits the context); he eats as a loan (8609, through Humayd ibn Mas'ada from Bishr ibn al-Mufaddal from Shu'ba from Hammad, where the manuscript has a wrong negation); the loan (8610, through Ibn al-Muthanna from Muhammad ibn Ja'far from Shu'ba). Al-Sha'bi (8611, through Ibn Humayd from Hakkam from Amr ibn Abi Qays from Ata ibn al-Sa'ib): he does not eat from it unless compelled, as one is compelled to eat carrion, and if he eats anything he repays it. Mujahid: as a loan (8612, 8613, through Shu'ba from Ibn Abi Najih); an advance from his orphan's wealth (8614); Mujahid and Sa'id ibn Jubayr together (8615, through Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri from Ibn Abi Najih): it is the loan, and al-Thawri said al-Hakam said it too, arguing from the command about witnesses; Mujahid (8616, through Hushaym from Hajjaj): what he takes he repays when he is at ease; Abu al-Aliya (8617, through Ibn Waki' from his father from Abu Jafar from al-Rabi'): the loan, with the same argument; Abu Wa'il (8618): as a loan; Sa'id ibn Jubayr (8619, through Ibn Humayd from Jarir from Mansur from al-Hakam): if the guardian is in need or poor and finds nothing, he eats from the orphan's wealth and writes it down, and repays it if he gets ease, and if he does not until death comes to him, he calls the orphan and asks his release from what he ate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 582} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 583} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 584} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 585} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 586}

**Second view: eating without repayment, and what "recognised manner" means (pp. 586-587, reports 8620-8630).** Mujahid (8620, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Ibn Ulayya from Ibn Abi Najih): from the orphan's wealth without extravagance and with no repayment due for what he ate. Al-Tabari says the holders of this view differed over the meaning of the recognised manner. Some said he eats from his food with his fingertips and does not wear anything of it: al-Suddi from one who heard Ibn Abbas (8621, through Ibn Bashshar from Abu Ahmad from Sufyan; 8622, through Abu Kurayb from Ubayd Allah al-Ashja'i; in the editor's note al-Ashja'i is Ubayd Allah ibn Ubayd al-Rahman al-Ashja'i, of whom Ibn Ma'in said no one in Kufa knew Sufyan al-Thawri better, reliable and trustworthy, with an entry in al-Tahdhib, the printing having "Abd Allah" by mistake); al-Suddi (8623, through Muhammad ibn al-Husayn from Asbat): a guardian who is rich should abstain from eating, a guardian who is poor eats with him with his fingers, not extravagant in eating and not wearing (the editor says the printing has "from his wealth" and the correct reading is the manuscript's); Ikrima (8624, through Ibn al-Muthanna from Harami ibn Umara from Shu'ba from Umara): your hand with their hands, and do not make a cap out of it; Ata and Ikrima (8625, through Abd al-Razzaq from Ibn Uyayna from Amr ibn Dinar): you put your hand with his hand. Others said the recognised manner is that he eats what satisfies his hunger and wears what covers his nakedness: Ibrahim (8626, through Yaqub ibn Ibrahim from Hushaym from Mughira): it does not mean wearing linen or fine robes but what stops hunger and covers nakedness; the same (8627, through Ibn Bashshar from Abd al-Rahman from Sufyan, where it is introduced with "it used to be said"; and 8628, through Abd al-Razzaq from al-Thawri). Makhul (8629, through Ali ibn Sahl from al-Walid ibn Muslim from Abu Ma'bad) was asked what eating in the recognised manner is for a poor guardian and said his hand with his hand; asked about clothing, he said he wears from his clothes, but he is not to make wealth for himself from his wealth. Ibrahim (8630, through Abu Kurayb from al-Ashja'i from Sufyan from Mughira): what stops hunger and covers... and the report breaks off at the end of the page. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 586} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 587} These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict. We stop at p. 587 in the commentary on verse 6 of sura 4, at report 8630, which is cut off at the end of the page, and continue from p. 588. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v07 p. 587}
