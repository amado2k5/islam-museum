## Summary
Verse 248 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 248 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) begins on p. 315. Al-Tabari holds that the verse shows the notables did not accept Talut's being sent when their prophet told them and made known his merit, but asked for proof of what he said, and he answered that the sign of his kingship was that the Ark would come to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 315} He says the story, though a report about the notables and their prophet, is also a lesson for the descendants of Israel living near the Prophet's place of emigration, the Jews of Qurayza and al-Nadir, warning them not to be, in denying Muhammad while knowing him truthful, like their forebears who denied their prophet Shamwil; an urging to the believers among Muhammad's companions to fight and a warning not to hold back when meeting the enemy; and an encouragement to advance against unbelievers without fearing their numbers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 316} This is al-Tabari's reading of the aim of the report and his choice, and the museum gives no judgement of anyone.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) from p. 317: al-Tabari says God told His believing servants that victory, triumph, good and evil are in His hand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317} He explains that the "them" refers to the notables of the Children of Israel who asked their prophet to send a king, and that the sign of Talut's kingship they had asked as proof of his truth is the coming of the Ark with calm from their Lord; it is the Ark that the Children of Israel put forward whenever they met an enemy, advancing with it, so that no enemy stood against them, until they wasted God's command and their quarrelling with their prophets grew, so God took it from them time after time and returned it, until the last time He took it and did not return it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317} The editor refers to the earlier meaning of the word "sign" and to his corrections of the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over the cause of the Ark's coming, and whether it had been taken from them before and God returned it when He made its coming a sign of Talut's kingship, or whether it had not been taken. Some said it was with them from the time of Moses and Aaron, passed on, until kings among the unbelievers took it from them, then God returned it as the sign of Talut's kingship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317} From Wahb b. Munabbih (5658): 'Ili, who raised Shamwil, had two young sons who introduced something into the offering that was not in it and who molested the women praying in the sanctuary; Shamwil heard a voice calling him three times and took it for 'Ili, who then told him to go and say to him that love of his sons kept him from restraining them from what they brought into God's sanctuary and offering, so He would remove the priesthood from him and his line and destroy him and them; 'Ili was greatly alarmed. An enemy marched on them, his sons brought out the Ark holding the two tablets and Moses' staff to be given victory, they were routed, the sons were killed and the Ark was taken, and 'Ili gasped and died. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 319} Those who seized the Ark put it in the house of their gods; the idol was found overturned, its hands and feet cut off, and the people were struck with a pain in their necks; a girl among the Israelite captives told them they would keep seeing what they disliked while the Ark was among them, and advised sending it on a cart drawn by two cows with calves that had never borne a yoke; when they reached the land of Israel they broke the yoke and laid it in a ruin, and whoever came near it died except two men whom the prophet let carry it to their mother's house, until Talut became king. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 319} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320} In the margin the editor explains the stirring-stick, the hooks and that this offering is the Jews' grain offering, with name corrections from the History and the People of the Book's scripture; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 319} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320}

Also from Wahb (5659): Shamwil told them the sign of his appointment by God is the Ark's coming, which would return to them the calm inside it and the remains of what the house of Moses and the house of Aaron left; the enemy were idol-worshippers, among them Goliath, a man given great stature and strength; the Ark was in a Philistine village called Azdud, and when the matter of the prophet came about, the idols were found overturned and God sent mice on the village people that ate a man's belly; so they said, send out the Ark, and they put it on a cart hung to two oxen and angels went driving them until it stopped at the Children of Israel, who said the takbir, pressed on with their fight and rallied to Talut. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 321} The editor discusses in the margin the pointing of the village's name and compares the People of the Book's scripture and Ibn Kathir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 321}

From Ibn 'Abbas (5660): when they refused to give him the leadership he told them of the Ark's coming carried by angels; when Moses cast down the tablets they broke and part was taken up and he gathered what remained in the Ark, of which only a sixth remained; the Amalekites, a group from 'Ad living at Jericho, had captured it, and the angels came with it, carrying it between heaven and earth until they placed it with Talut and they submitted. It is said Adam came down with the Ark and the Corner; Ibn Jurayj heard that it and Moses' staff are in the lake of Tiberias and will come out before the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 321} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 322} These are reports from Companions, Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) from p. 322: al-Tabari relates from Wahb b. Munabbih (5661) a broken report about Jeremiah when Jerusalem was laid waste, and the editor states in the margin that the text is cut off and that what follows the dots on p. 323 is a report about the village where the Ark was placed when taken, whose beginning is lost; he fears the copyist put one leaf before another or skipped a page of the book he copied, says the gap cannot be filled, and this is his estimate, which the museum reports and does not add to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 322} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 323} The report goes on that the Children of Israel were guided to two cows put under a yoke with the Ark bound on a cart, God set four angels over them to drive them, and when they reached the edge of Jerusalem they broke the yoke and left; David and those with him came down, and on seeing the Ark David hopped toward it in joy, which Wahb explained as like dancing; his wife said he had lightened himself until the people nearly hated him, and he said, would you slow me from obeying my Lord, and parted from her. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 323} The editor notes in the margin that a part of the old division from which the copy was taken ends here, that the manuscript has a closing note and a record of hearing the book read before the judge Abu al-Hasan al-Hasib, and that the writing is dated Sha'ban 408 in Egypt; this is the copyist's and the editor's wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 323} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 324}

Others said the Ark was in the wilderness, Moses having left it with his servant Joshua, and the angels carried it until they put it in Talut's house: Qatada (5662) and al-Rabi' (5663). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 323} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 324} Al-Tabari chooses, as his choice and argument, the view of Ibn 'Abbas and Wahb, that it was with an enemy of the Children of Israel who had taken it from them, because the prophet said "the Ark" with the definite article, which is used only of what is known to the speakers, so the sense is the Ark you know and used to seek help with; had it been an Ark whose worth they did not know, it would have been said as indefinite. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 324} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 325} He answers anyone thinking they knew it while it was with Moses and Joshua that it is not reported that Moses or Joshua met an enemy with it, and that those who said this claimed Joshua left it in the wilderness, so they could not have known it there. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 325} Wahb (5664): the Ark was about three cubits by two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 325} The editor gives the transmitters of the chain. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 325}

On the meaning of the calm (sakina) in the Ark the interpreters differed. Some said it was a gentle wind with a face like a human face, from 'Ali b. Abi Talib (5665 to 5668), and in another report from him a violent wind with two heads (5669 to 5671). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 326} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 327} Others said it had a head like a cat's and two wings, from Mujahid (5672 to 5675), in one report that it came with Gabriel and Abraham from Syria. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 327} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 328} Others said it was the head of a dead cat which, when it cried in the Ark, assured them of victory, from Wahb from a scholar of the Children of Israel (5677). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 328} Others said it was a golden basin from Paradise in which the hearts of the prophets were washed, from Ibn 'Abbas (5678), and from al-Suddi (5679), that God gave it to Moses and he put the tablets in it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 328} These are reports from Companions, Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) from p. 328: others said the sakina is a spirit from God that speaks, so that when they differed over something it spoke and told them what they wanted, from Wahb b. Munabbih (5680, 5681). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 328} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 329} Others said it is what they know of signs and find rest in, from 'Ata' b. Abi Rabah (5682); others that it is mercy, from al-Rabi' (5683); others that it is dignity, from Qatada (5684). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 329}

Al-Tabari chooses, as his choice and argument, 'Ata's view that it is what hearts find rest in of the signs they know, because sakina in Arabic is of the pattern fa'ila from "he found rest in a thing", when he is at ease with it and his soul is calm, citing a line of verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 329} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 330} He holds that if this is its meaning, what 'Ali b. Abi Talib said may be so, and so may what Mujahid, Wahb b. Munabbih and al-Suddi said, since all are sufficient signs that hearts rest in and breasts are cooled by, and that the sign in the Ark is called by the action's name while being other than it, as the speech shows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 330} The editor notes that Ibn Barri ascribes the verse to Abu 'Arif al-Kulaybi and that he doubts this, and explains the verbal noun after which the sign is named. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 330}

Al-Tabari explains the "remnant" as what remained of the legacy of the house of Moses and the house of Aaron. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 330} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 331} The interpreters differed over it. Some said Moses' staff and fragments of the tablets, from 'Ikrima and Ibn 'Abbas (5685 to 5687), from Qatada (5688, 5689), from al-Suddi (5690); from al-Rabi', Moses' staff and remains of the Torah (5691); from 'Ikrima, the Torah, fragments of the tablets and the staff (5692); fragments of the tablets (5693). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 331} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 332} Others said Moses' staff, Aaron's staff and some of the tablets: Abu Salih (5694), that it held Moses' staff, Aaron's staff, two tablets of the Torah and the manna; 'Atiyya b. Sa'd, the staffs of Moses and Aaron, the garments of Moses and Aaron and fragments of the tablets (5695). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 332} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 333} Others said the staff and the two sandals: al-Thawri, that some say a measure of manna and fragments of the tablets, others the staff and the two sandals (5696). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 333} Others said the staff alone, from Wahb (5697); others fragments of the tablets, from Ibn 'Abbas (5698). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 333} 'Ata' said knowledge and the Torah (5699); al-Dahhak, fighting in God's way, with which they fought alongside Talut (5700). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 334} The editor explains the words for fragments and the measure and corrects chains. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 331} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 332} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 333}

Al-Tabari chooses, as his choice and argument, that God reported of the Ark He made a sign of His prophet's truth that in it was calm from Him and a remnant of the legacy of the two houses, and that this remnant may be the staff, the tablet fragments, the Torah or part of it, the sandals, the garments or fighting in God's way, or some of these; knowledge of it cannot be reached by derivation or language but only by a report that necessitates knowledge, and Muslims have no such report, so no view may be declared right and another weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 334} These views are reported from Companions and Successors, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) from p. 335: the interpreters differed on how the angels bore the Ark. Some said they carried it between heaven and earth until they set it down among them, from Ibn 'Abbas (5701); Ibn Zayd, that when the prophet told them kingship is in God's hand they asked, who assures us that God gave him this, it is only your partiality; so he made the Ark's coming the sign of his kingship, and the angels came down with it by day as they watched, until they put it among them, and they acknowledged it unwillingly and left in anger (5702); al-Suddi, that the Ark and what was in it were found in Talut's house, so they believed in Sham'un's prophecy and accepted Talut's kingship (5703); Qatada, that they bear it until they put it in Talut's house (5704). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 335} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 336} Others said the sense is that the angels drove the animals that carried it, from al-Thawri from some of his teachers, that they carried it on a cart drawn by a cow (5705), and from Wahb, that four angels were set over the two cows (5706). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 336}

Al-Tabari chooses, as his choice and argument, the view that the angels bore the Ark until they set it in Talut's house among the Children of Israel, because God said they bear it and did not say they bring it, and what cows drew on a cart, though the angels drove them, is not bearing, for bearing as known is the carrier's own handling; and he holds that turning the interpretation to the better-known usage is better than the rarer. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 336} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 337} The editor notes in the margin where the printed and manuscript texts differ here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 336} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 337}

On the closing clause, al-Tabari says their prophet Shamwil told them that the Ark's coming in this manner is a sign and proof of his truth in what he told them of God's making Talut king, if they had denied and accused him, and if they are believers when the sign they asked comes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 337} His reason is that they had disbelieved in God by denying their prophet, answering him back and asking for a sign, so it cannot be said to them while unbelievers that they have a sign if they are people of faith; rather they asked for the sign of his truth in order to acknowledge it, and he told them that in its coming is a sign if they will then be affirming it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 337} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 338} This is al-Tabari's choice and argument.
