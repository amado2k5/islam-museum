## Summary
Verse 248 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 248 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) begins on p. 315. Al-Tabari holds that the verse shows the notables did not accept Talut's being sent when their prophet told them and made known his merit, but asked for proof of what he said, and he answered that the sign of his kingship was that the Ark would come to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 315} He says the story, though a report about the notables and their prophet, is also a lesson for the descendants of Israel living near the Prophet's place of emigration, the Jews of Qurayza and al-Nadir, warning them not to be, in denying Muhammad while knowing him truthful, like their forebears who denied their prophet Shamwil; an urging to the believers among Muhammad's companions to fight and a warning not to hold back when meeting the enemy; and an encouragement to advance against unbelievers without fearing their numbers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 315} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 316} This is al-Tabari's reading of the aim of the report and his choice, and the museum gives no judgement of anyone.

The rest of ([[passage/quran-2-248|2:248]]) from p. 317: al-Tabari says God told His believing servants that victory, triumph, good and evil are in His hand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 316} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317} He explains that the "them" refers to the notables of the Children of Israel who asked their prophet to send a king, and that the sign of Talut's kingship they had asked as proof of his truth is the coming of the Ark with calm from their Lord; it is the Ark that the Children of Israel put forward whenever they met an enemy, advancing with it, so that no enemy stood against them, until they wasted God's command and their quarrelling with their prophets grew, so God took it from them time after time and returned it, until the last time He took it and did not return it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317} The editor refers to the earlier meaning of the word "sign" and to his corrections of the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317}

Al-Tabari then says the interpreters differed over the cause of the Ark's coming, and whether it had been taken from them before and God returned it when He made its coming a sign of Talut's kingship, or whether it had not been taken. Some said it was with them from the time of Moses and Aaron, passed on, until kings among the unbelievers took it from them, then God returned it as the sign of Talut's kingship. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 317} From Wahb b. Munabbih (5658): 'Ili, who raised Shamwil, had two young sons who introduced something into the offering that was not in it and who molested the women praying in the sanctuary; Shamwil heard a voice calling him three times and took it for 'Ili, who then told him to go and say to him that love of his sons kept him from restraining them from what they brought into God's sanctuary and offering, so He would remove the priesthood from him and his line and destroy him and them; 'Ili was greatly alarmed. An enemy marched on them, his sons brought out the Ark holding the two tablets and Moses' staff to be given victory, they were routed, the sons were killed and the Ark was taken, and 'Ili gasped and died. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 319} Those who seized the Ark put it in the house of their gods; the idol was found overturned, its hands and feet cut off, and the people were struck with a pain in their necks; a girl among the Israelite captives told them they would keep seeing what they disliked while the Ark was among them, and advised sending it on a cart drawn by two cows with calves that had never borne a yoke; when they reached the land of Israel they broke the yoke and laid it in a ruin, and whoever came near it died except two men whom the prophet let carry it to their mother's house, until Talut became king. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 319} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320} In the margin the editor explains the stirring-stick, the hooks and that this offering is the Jews' grain offering, with name corrections from the History and the People of the Book's scripture; this is his estimate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 318} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 319} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320}

Also from Wahb (5659): Shamwil told them the sign of his appointment by God is the Ark's coming, which would return to them the calm inside it and the remains of what the house of Moses and the house of Aaron left; the enemy were idol-worshippers, among them Goliath, a man given great stature and strength; the Ark was in a Philistine village called Azdud, and when the matter of the prophet came about, the idols were found overturned and God sent mice on the village people that ate a man's belly; so they said, send out the Ark, and they put it on a cart hung to two oxen and angels went driving them until it stopped at the Children of Israel, who said the takbir, pressed on with their fight and rallied to Talut. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 321} The editor discusses in the margin the pointing of the village's name and compares the People of the Book's scripture and Ibn Kathir. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 320} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 321}

From Ibn 'Abbas (5660): when they refused to give him the leadership he told them of the Ark's coming carried by angels; when Moses cast down the tablets they broke and part was taken up and he gathered what remained in the Ark, of which only a sixth remained; the Amalekites, a group from 'Ad living at Jericho, had captured it, and the angels came with it, carrying it between heaven and earth until they placed it with Talut and they submitted. It is said Adam came down with the Ark and the Corner; Ibn Jurayj heard that it and Moses' staff are in the lake of Tiberias and will come out before the Day of Resurrection. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 321} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v05 p. 322} These are reports from Companions, Successors and the People of the Book, and the museum does not rule on their soundness.
