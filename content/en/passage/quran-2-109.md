## In al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari holds that the words "many of the People of the Book would love to turn you back into disbelievers after your belief" make plain that the address in all these verses, from "do not say ra'ina", though the speech is turned to the Prophet, is to the believers among his Companions, a reproach to them and a prohibition on taking the advice of the Jews and their like among the polytheists or accepting their opinions on any matter of their religion; he paraphrases: do not say to the Prophet, as the Jews do, "ra'ina", in imitation of them, for the Jews and polytheists do not wish that any good be sent down to you from your Lord, but many of them wish to turn you back after your belief into disbelievers, out of envy of their own, towards you and your Prophet, after the truth had become clear to them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 498} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 499}

Reports name who is meant by "many of the People of the Book": from al-Zuhri and Qatada, Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf; from Ibn Abbas (in Ibn Ishaq's report), Huyayy ibn Akhtab and Abu Yasir ibn Akhtab, who were among the Jews most envious of the Arabs and strove to turn people from Islam (the editor refers to the Sira of Ibn Hisham and notes what is said of Abu Sufyan al-Ma'mari). Al-Tabari rejects the view that it was Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, since Ka'b is one man and God described "many" of them in plural terms, which is not turned to one person without evidence, and the plain verse gives none. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 499} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 500}

On "out of envy from themselves" he says that many of the People of the Book wish apostasy on the believers out of envy and transgression; "hasadan" is in the accusative as a verbal noun from the sense of the speech, not as an adjective of "kuffar", meaning they envied you for the success and guidance to His religion and belief in His Messenger that God gave you, and that He made His Messenger to you a man from among you. "From themselves" means from their own side, from al-Rabi' ibn Anas (the editor completes the chain from Ibn Kathir and al-Durr al-Manthur), and God said so to inform that they were not commanded this in their Book and do what they do knowing God forbade it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 500} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 501}

On "after the truth had become clear to them" he says: after the truth became clear to these many of the People of the Book concerning Muhammad and what he brought from his Lord. Reports: Qatada, that it became clear to them that Muhammad is the Messenger of God and Islam the religion of God; Abu al-Aliya and al-Rabi', that they find him written with them in the Torah and the Gospel, so they disbelieved in him out of envy and transgression since he was from others; and al-Suddi and Ibn Zayd. Al-Tabari sees in this that their unbelief was obstinate and with knowledge, and reports from Ibn Abbas (via al-Dahhak) that they were ignorant of none of it, but envy carried them to denial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 501} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 502}
