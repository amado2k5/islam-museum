## Summary
Verse 11 of [[passage/quran-5|Surat al-Ma'ida]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 11 of 120 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**Commentary on verse 11: God restrains the hands of a people (pp. 100-103)**

Al-Tabari says: you who acknowledged God's oneness and the mission of his Messenger, remember the favour God gave you and thank him for it by keeping his covenant and the pledges you made to your Prophet. He then described the favour: it is that he held back from you the hands of the people who meant to strike you, turned them away and stood between them and what they wanted of you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 100}

Interpreters then differed on this favour. Some said it is God's rescue of his Prophet and Companions from what the Jews of Banu al-Nadir had planned on the day he went to ask their help with the blood-money for the two men of Amir killed by Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri. Report 11557, from Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada and Abdallah ibn Abi Bakr: the Messenger of God went to Banu al-Nadir to ask their help with the blood-money; when he came, they conferred and said: you will never find him nearer than now, so who will go up on this house and throw a rock on him and relieve us of him? Amr ibn Jihash ibn Ka'b said: I will. News came to the Messenger of God and he left them, and God revealed the verse about them. The editor notes that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 101}

Report 11558, from Mujahid, says the verse is about the Jews: the Prophet went into a garden of theirs with his Companions behind its wall, and asked their help with a fine he had taken on; he rose and left, and they plotted to kill him; he went out walking backwards watching them, then called his Companions one by one until they gathered to him. Report 11559, from Mujahid, is similar, saying he went out walking obliquely, watching them in fear. Report 11560, from Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad: the Messenger of God came to Banu al-Nadir to ask their help with a blood-money that had fallen on him, with Abu Bakr, Umar and Ali; they said: yes, Abu al-Qasim, sit until we feed you and give you what you ask; he sat and his Companions waited for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 102}

Huyayy ibn Akhtab, the head of the people, came and told his companions: you will not see him nearer than now, throw stones on him and kill him; they went to a large millstone to throw it on him, and God held their hands back, until Gabriel came and raised him from there; God informed his Prophet of what they intended. Report 11561, from Abdallah ibn Kathir, is like Mujahid's. Report 11562, from Ikrima: the Messenger of God sent al-Mundhir ibn Amr al-Ansari, of Banu al-Najjar and one of the leaders at the night of al-Aqaba, with thirty riders of the Emigrants and Helpers; they met Amir ibn al-Tufayl at the well of Ma'una and fought; al-Mundhir and his companions were killed except three who were looking for a lost animal; they saw birds circling in the sky with clots of blood falling from their beaks, and one said: by the Merciful, our companions are killed; he turned and ran until he met a man, and they exchanged two blows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 103}

Report 11562 is completed, from Ikrima: when the blow struck him he raised his head to the sky, opened his eyes and said: God is greatest, paradise, by the Lord of the worlds. He was called 'the one who raced to die'. His two companions returned and met two men of Banu Sulaym, with whom the Prophet had a truce; they claimed descent from Banu Amir for them, and killed them. Their people came to the Prophet asking for the blood-money, and he went out with Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali, Talha and Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, until they came to Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf and the Jews of al-Nadir and he asked their help with the blood-money of the two; the Jews gathered to kill the Messenger of God and his Companions and used a meal as pretext. Gabriel came to him with what the Jews had agreed on by treachery; he went out, called Ali and said: do not leave this place, and if any of my Companions come out and ask you about me, say that I headed to Medina and they should follow. They kept passing Ali and he told them so until the last of them passed, then he followed them; that is the verse about continual treachery from them, in [[passage/quran-5-13|5:13]]. The editor notes that 'the one who raced to die' was a nickname, said to be al-Mundhir ibn Amr or Haram ibn Milhan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 104}

Report 11563, from Abu Malik, says the verse came down about Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf and his companions when they wished to betray the Messenger of God. Others said the favour is that the Jews planned to kill the Prophet at a meal to which they invited him, and God informed his Prophet of what they planned, so he and his Companions refrained from accepting. Report 11564, from Ibn Abbas: some Jews prepared food for the Messenger of God and his Companions in order to kill him when it came; God revealed their affair to him, so the food did not come, and he told his Companions and they did not go. Others said the favour is God's letting his Prophet know what an enemy of his and theirs, among the polytheists, planned on the day of Batn Nakhl, to fall on the Muslims while they were busy praying, and his teaching him the prayer of fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 105}

Report 11565, from Qatada: the verse came down on the Messenger of God at Batn Nakhl in the seventh expedition, when Banu Tha'laba and Banu Muharib meant to attack him by surprise and God informed him. A man volunteered to kill him and came to the Prophet whose sword was laid down; he said: may I take it, Prophet of God? He said: take it. May I draw it? Yes. He drew it and said: who will protect you from me? He said: God will protect me from you. The Companions of the Messenger threatened him and spoke harshly, and he sheathed the sword; the Prophet ordered his Companions to move on, and the prayer of fear was revealed to him then. Report 11566, from Jabir: the Prophet stopped at a place and the people scattered among the thorny trees for shade; he hung his weapon on a tree and a bedouin came to his sword, drew it and turned to him saying: who will protect you from me? The Prophet said: God. The bedouin sheathed the sword, and the Prophet called his Companions and told them while the bedouin sat beside him; he did not punish him. Qatada mentioned something similar. The notes say Muslim, al-Bukhari and Ahmad report Jabir's account and that the bedouin is Ghawrath ibn al-Harith, as Ibn Kathir says. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 106}

Al-Tabari then says the soundest view is that the favour is God's favour to the believers in rescuing their Prophet from what the Jews of Banu al-Nadir planned, to kill him and those with him, on the day he went to them about the blood-money he had taken on for the two men killed by Amr ibn Umayya. He holds this sounder because God follows this mention by charging the Jews with their deeds and ugly acts and their betrayal of their Lord and prophets, then orders his Prophet to pardon them and overlook; so he was not ordered to pardon right after the verse except of those who had planned to stretch out their hands to him, for if it were others, the order to pardon would be about people who had not been mentioned, {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 107}

and the charge of betrayal would be laid on people whose betrayal had not been mentioned; this shows that the interpretations he judged sound are sound, and the others are not. He then explains 'fear God, and in God let the believers put their trust': beware God, believers, of opposing what he commanded and forbade or breaking the covenant he made with you, which would bring you his punishment; and let those who acknowledge his oneness and the mission of his Messenger entrust their affairs to God, submit to his decree and trust his help and support, for that is part of the perfection of their religion; if they do so, he will guard and protect them against anyone who means them harm, as he protected you from the Jews who planned to stretch out their hands to you, for no one else can ward off harm your Lord has willed or bring a benefit he has not decreed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 108}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
