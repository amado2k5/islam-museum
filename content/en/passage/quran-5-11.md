**Commentary on verse 11: God restrains the hands of a people (pp. 100-103)**

Al-Tabari says: you who acknowledged God's oneness and the mission of his Messenger, remember the favour God gave you and thank him for it by keeping his covenant and the pledges you made to your Prophet. He then described the favour: it is that he held back from you the hands of the people who meant to strike you, turned them away and stood between them and what they wanted of you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 100}

Interpreters then differed on this favour. Some said it is God's rescue of his Prophet and Companions from what the Jews of Banu al-Nadir had planned on the day he went to ask their help with the blood-money for the two men of Amir killed by Amr ibn Umayya al-Damri. Report 11557, from Asim ibn Umar ibn Qatada and Abdallah ibn Abi Bakr: the Messenger of God went to Banu al-Nadir to ask their help with the blood-money; when he came, they conferred and said: you will never find him nearer than now, so who will go up on this house and throw a rock on him and relieve us of him? Amr ibn Jihash ibn Ka'b said: I will. News came to the Messenger of God and he left them, and God revealed the verse about them. The editor notes that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 101}

Report 11558, from Mujahid, says the verse is about the Jews: the Prophet went into a garden of theirs with his Companions behind its wall, and asked their help with a fine he had taken on; he rose and left, and they plotted to kill him; he went out walking backwards watching them, then called his Companions one by one until they gathered to him. Report 11559, from Mujahid, is similar, saying he went out walking obliquely, watching them in fear. Report 11560, from Yazid ibn Abi Ziyad: the Messenger of God came to Banu al-Nadir to ask their help with a blood-money that had fallen on him, with Abu Bakr, Umar and Ali; they said: yes, Abu al-Qasim, sit until we feed you and give you what you ask; he sat and his Companions waited for him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 102}

Huyayy ibn Akhtab, the head of the people, came and told his companions: you will not see him nearer than now, throw stones on him and kill him; they went to a large millstone to throw it on him, and God held their hands back, until Gabriel came and raised him from there; God informed his Prophet of what they intended. Report 11561, from Abdallah ibn Kathir, is like Mujahid's. Report 11562, from Ikrima: the Messenger of God sent al-Mundhir ibn Amr al-Ansari, of Banu al-Najjar and one of the leaders at the night of al-Aqaba, with thirty riders of the Emigrants and Helpers; they met Amir ibn al-Tufayl at the well of Ma'una and fought; al-Mundhir and his companions were killed except three who were looking for a lost animal; they saw birds circling in the sky with clots of blood falling from their beaks, and one said: by the Merciful, our companions are killed; he turned and ran until he met a man, and they exchanged two blows. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v10 p. 103}

We stop here at the foot of p. 103, in report 11562 from Ikrima on the occasion of verse 11; the rest follows in the next batch.

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
