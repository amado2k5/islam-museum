## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari holds that whether their claim to reform meant making peace between the Jews and the Muslims or acting in their own religion, they thought themselves doers of good while God judged them wrongdoers, because God required them to affirm the Messenger and side with the believers; meeting the Jews in alliance and doubting the prophethood was the gravest corruption. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 291}
He reads "truly they are the corrupters, but they do not perceive" as God's denial of their claim to be reformers on the right path, and as the statement that they are the ones who oppose God's command and transgress His limits without knowing it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 291} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 292}
