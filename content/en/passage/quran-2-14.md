## In al-Tabari's commentary
Al-Tabari treats this verse as parallel to "and among the people are those who say: we believe": they say with their tongues to the believers "we believe and we affirm Muhammad", as a ruse to protect their lives, property and families, and when alone with their rebellious ones, the people of arrogance and evil among the polytheists, who are their "devils", they say "we are with you, on your religion and your allies against the Companions of Muhammad; we were only mocking". He gives Ibn Abbas that some Jewish men, meeting the Companions, said "we are on your religion", and to their own people, "we are with you". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 296}
On "their devils" he records Ibn Abbas that they are Jews who command denial, al-Suddi their leaders in disbelief, Qatada their leaders in evil and also the polytheists, Mujahid their companions among the unbelievers or among the hypocrites and polytheists, and al-Rabi' ibn Anas their brothers among the polytheists. From Ibn Jurayj he gives that when ease reached the believers they said "we are with you, your brothers". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 297} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 298}
He takes up the question of why the verse says "khalaw ila" and not "khalaw bi", citing a Basran grammarian's view that "khalawtu ila fulan" can mean only being alone with him on a particular need, whereas "khalawtu bihi" can also mean mocking him, so "ila" is the clearer wording. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 298}

On "khalaw ila shayatinihim" al-Tabari gives two further views: that prepositions can stand in for one another so "ila" means "with", as in a verse of al-Saff, or, the view of some Kufan grammarians, that "ila" is brought by the sense of their turning away from meeting the believers toward their devils, alone with them, rather than by "khalaw". He prefers this one, since each particle has the sense it is best suited to and should not be turned aside without proof. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 298} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 299}
He says the exegetes agreed without dispute that "we are only mockers" means "we are making fun", that is, of the Companions of Muhammad by saying "we believe" to them, and cites Ibn Abbas through al-Dahhak and through Ibn Ishaq, Qatada and al-Rabi' ibn Anas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v01 p. 300}
