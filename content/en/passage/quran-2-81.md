## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "Yes, whoever earns an evil", al-Tabari says it denies the Jewish speakers and reports that whoever associates partners or disbelieves and is enveloped by his sins God keeps in the Fire forever, since only the believers dwell in Paradise. "Bala" is "bal" with a "ya" added to allow a stop and to affirm after a negation. The "evil" here is associating partners, reported from Abu Wa'il, Mujahid, Qatada, 'Ata and al-Rabi', while al-Suddi says it is the sins for which the Fire is promised. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 280} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 281} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 282}
Al-Tabari holds that the "evil" meant is some evils and not others, though the wording is general, because permanent stay in the Fire belongs to those who disbelieve and not to the believers, as many reports from the Prophet say, and because God joins the verse with "those who believe and do good deeds are the people of Paradise, abiding in it". He argues that God promised to expiate the sins of those who avoid the major sins, and that, on conclusive reports he knows, the people of major sins are not in this verse; he says one who denies that must stop asserting their permanent stay in the Fire from this verse and its like. This is al-Tabari's position, in line with the Sunni method, and he does not set out other views in detail here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 282} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 283}
On "and his sin has encompassed him", it means his sins gathered upon him and he died in them before repenting; "ihata" is surrounding, like a wall around a house. Al-Dahhak, al-Rabi' ibn Khuthaym, al-A'mash and Abu Razin say he died in his sin; Ibn Abbas, that his disbelief envelops whatever good he has; Mujahid and Qatada, the major sins that make the Fire due; and al-Hasan, that every verse in which God promises the Fire is what "khati'a" means. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 284} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 285}

Al-Tabari reports from al-Suddi that the phrase means he died and did not repent, and from 'Ata that "khati'a" is associating partners, who then recited "whoever comes with the evil, their faces will be thrown into the Fire". On "those are the people of the Fire, abiding in it" he says it means those who earned evils and were encompassed by their sins are people of the Fire, remaining there forever, called its "companions" for preferring in their worldly life the causes that lead to it over the causes of Paradise; he reports from Ibn Abbas and al-Suddi that they do not leave it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 285} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 286} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v02 p. 287}
