**Commentary on verse 72 (pp. 398-399)**

On [[passage/quran-11-72|11:72]] al-Tabari says: Sarah said, when she was given the tidings that she would bear Isaac, in wonder at what was said to her, since she had reached the age at which neither man nor woman bears. It is said she was then ninety-nine and Abraham a hundred, and the report from Mujahid was given before; and of Ibn Ishaq, report 18330: Sarah, when given the tidings of Isaac, as some people of knowledge told me, was ninety and Abraham one hundred and twenty. 'Ya waylata' is a word the Arabs say at wonder at a thing and disapproval of a thing, saying at wonder, woe to his mother, what a man he is. The people of Arabic differed on this alif: a Basran said it is a true alif, and at the pause you say 'ya waylatah', like the alif of lamentation, softened from being a mere pause and given a ha' after it to make it clearer and carry further in sound; another said it is the alif of lamentation, and pausing on it is allowed and pausing on the ha' is allowed. Al-Tabari says the right view for him is that it is the alif of lamentation and pausing on it with or without the ha' is allowed in speech, as the Arabs use it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 399}

Her words 'shall I bear when I am an old woman' mean how can I have a child, 'and this my husband is an old man'. The 'ba'l' here is the husband, so called because he is the manager of her affairs, as they call the owner of a thing its 'ba'l' and call the palm that does without irrigation, living on the sky's water, 'ba'l', for the owner of a thing is the one who stands over it, and the 'ba'l' palm lives by it. 'That is a strange thing' means that a child should come from one like me and my husband at the age we are is strange. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 399}
