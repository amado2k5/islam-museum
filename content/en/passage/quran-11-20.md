**Commentary on verse 20 (pp. 285-287)**

On [[passage/quran-11-20|11:20]] al-Tabari says: 'those were not able to frustrate God on earth' means those described as barring from God's way were not ones to frustrate their Lord by fleeing from Him on earth when He wills to punish them and take vengeance; they are in His grasp and possession, cannot withhold themselves from Him when He wills them nor escape Him by flight when He seeks them. 'And they had no protectors besides God' means these polytheists, when He wills their punishment, have no helpers besides God to help them against God and come between them and Him when He punishes them, though in the world they had a defence by which they protected themselves from those who wished them harm; 'the punishment will be doubled for them' means their punishment will be increased, two in place of one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 286}

His words 'they could not hear and they did not see' were interpreted in differing ways. Some said God described these polytheists so: He sealed their hearing and sight, so they neither hear the truth nor see God's proofs with the hearing of one who benefits or the sight of one who is guided. Report 18092, from Qatada: deaf to the truth, so they do not hear it, dumb so they do not speak it, blind so they do not see it nor benefit from it; report 18093, from him: they could not hear good so as to benefit from it nor see good so as to take it; report 18094, from Ibn Abbas: God reported that He came between the people of polytheism and obedience to Him in this world and the next; in this world He said 'they could not hear', which is obeying Him, 'and they did not see', and in the next He said in [[passage/quran-68-42|68:42]] and [[passage/quran-68-43|68:43]] that they cannot, their eyes humbled. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 287}

Others said that by 'and they had no protectors besides God' the gods of those who bar from God's way are meant, and that the sense is: those and their gods were not able to frustrate God on earth, the punishment will be doubled for them, they could not hear and could not see, meaning the gods, which had neither hearing nor sight; al-Tabari says this saying is reported from Ibn Abbas by a route he disliked to mention for the weakness of its chain. Others said: the punishment is doubled for them because they could hear and did not hear, and because they could see and did not reflect on God's proofs with their eyes to take a lesson; they said the particle 'bi' should have entered, but its dropping is allowed in speech, which is the saying of some of the people of Arabic. Al-Tabari says that the right view for him is what Ibn Abbas and Qatada said, that God described them as unable to hear the truth as one who benefits or see it as one guided, because they were occupied with the disbelief they persisted in, rather than using their faculties in obeying God; they did have ears and eyes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 287}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
