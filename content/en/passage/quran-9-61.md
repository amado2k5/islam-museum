**Commentary on verse 61 (p. 324)**

Al-Tabari says: among these hypocrites is a group who hurt the Messenger of God and find fault with him, "and they say he is an ear", that is, a hearing one, who hears from everyone what he says, accepts it and believes it. It comes from the phrase "a man of ear" for one quick to listen and accept, as one says "he is all certainty" of one with certainty about everything he is told; its root is from "he listened to him", and from it comes the report of the Prophet that God does not listen to anything as He listens to a prophet chanting the Qur'an, and the line of Adi ibn Zayd, and the discussion continues on the next page. The editor refers to the earlier explanation of hurting in volume 8, says he could not determine the vowelling of the phrase "man of ear" on the pattern given and found nothing in the dictionaries to support it, the dictionaries giving only the forms with one or two vowels, that he is in great doubt of its soundness and fears something fell from the copyist, and states that the report is sound, related by Muslim from Abu Hurayra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 324}

We stop here at the foot of p. 324, in the middle of the commentary on verse 61 (at the citation of Adi ibn Zayd's line); the next batch begins at p. 325. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 324}
