**Commentary on verse 61 (pp. 324-328)**

Al-Tabari says: among these hypocrites is a group who hurt the Messenger of God and find fault with him, "and they say he is an ear", that is, a hearing one, who hears from everyone what he says, accepts it and believes it. It comes from the phrase "a man of ear" for one quick to listen and accept, as one says "he is all certainty" of one with certainty about everything he is told; its root is from "he listened to him", and from it comes the report of the Prophet that God does not listen to anything as He listens to a prophet chanting the Qur'an, and the line of Adi ibn Zayd, a verse in which he tells his heart to lighten itself of amusement, since his concern is with singing and hearing {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 325} The editor refers to the earlier explanation of hurting in volume 8, says he could not determine the vowelling of the phrase "man of ear" on the pattern given and found nothing in the dictionaries to support it, the dictionaries giving only the forms with one or two vowels, that he is in great doubt of its soundness and fears something fell from the copyist, and states that the report is sound, related by Muslim from Abu Hurayra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 324}

It is mentioned that this verse came down about Nabtal ibn al-Harith. Report 16899, from Ibn Ishaq, through Ibn Humayd and Salama: God mentioned their deceit, meaning the hypocrites, and their hurting of the Prophet, and said "among them are those who hurt the Prophet and say he is an ear"; the one who said it, as it reached me, was Nabtal ibn al-Harith, brother of Banu Amr ibn Awf, and about him this verse came down, for he said "Muhammad is only an ear, whatever anyone tells him he believes", and God says "say, an ear of good for you", meaning he hears good and believes in it. The editor notes that the line is in al-Sharif al-Murtada's Amali and the Lisan, that the words for amusement and hearing mean play and singing, that the printed text and the manuscript both give a name other than Nabtal, an outright error, and that the report is in Ibn Hisham's Sira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 325}

The reciters differed over the reading of "an ear of good for you". The general body of reciters in the great cities read it with the word for ear in construct with good, meaning say to them, Muhammad, he is an ear of good and not an ear of evil. It is reported that al-Hasan al-Basri read it with a nunation on ear, making "good" its predicate, meaning say: he who listens to you hypocrites and believes you, if Muhammad is as you describe, such that when you come to him and deny what was reported to him of your hurting him and finding fault with him he listens to you and believes you, is better for you than that he should call you liars and not accept what you say; then God called them liars and said rather he accepts only from the believers, "he believes in God and believes the believers". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 325}

Al-Tabari chooses the reading of those who read it with the ear in construct with good and good in the genitive, meaning say he is an ear of good for you and not an ear of evil. The editor refers to al-Farra's Ma'ani al-Qur'an. Report 16900, from Ibn Abbas, through al-Muthanna, Abdallah ibn Salih, Mu'awiya and Ali: he listens to everyone; report 16901, from Qatada, through Bishr, Yazid and Sa'id: they used to say Muhammad is only an ear, nothing is reported about us, he is only an ear that hears what is said to him; reports 16902, 16903 and 16904, from Mujahid: we say what we like and swear, and he believes us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 326}

As for "he believes in God", it means he affirms God alone, who has no partner, and "believes the believers" means he affirms the believers, not the unbelievers or the hypocrites. This is a denial by God of the hypocrites who said Muhammad is an ear; the Almighty says Muhammad is only a listener to good, who affirms God and what comes from Him and affirms the believers, not the people of hypocrisy and unbelief. It is also said that "believes the believers" means he trusts them, since the Arabs say "I believed him" in two constructions meaning I trusted him, as in the verse of al-Naml that has a verb meaning "is close behind you", [[passage/quran-27-72|27:72]], which has the same sense as a form without the preposition, and as in the verse of al-A'raf about those who fear their Lord, [[passage/quran-7-154|7:154]], with the same sense of the transitive form. Report 16905, from Ibn Abbas: he believes in God and affirms the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 327}

As for "and a mercy for those among you who believe", the reciters differed in reading it. The general body of reciters in the great cities read the word for mercy in the nominative, meaning say he is an ear of good for you and he is a mercy to those who believe among you, joining it to the ear; some of the Kufans read it in the genitive, joining it to good, meaning say an ear of good for you and an ear of mercy. Al-Tabari prefers the nominative, meaning he is a mercy to those of you who believe, and God made him a mercy to whoever follows him, is guided by his guidance and affirms what he brought from his Lord, because God rescued them from misguidance through him and gave them His gardens as an inheritance for following him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 328}

"And those who hurt the Messenger of God, for them is a painful punishment": the Almighty says to these hypocrites who find fault with the Messenger of God and say he is an ear, and their like among those who deny him and speak falsehood and nonsense about him, a painful punishment from God in the Fire of Hell. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v14 p. 328}
