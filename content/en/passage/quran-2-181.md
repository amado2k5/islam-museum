## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "whoever alters it after hearing it, the sin of it falls only on those who alter it" al-Tabari says: whoever changes what the testator bequeathed, a bequest according to what is customary, after hearing it, the sin of the change is on the one who changed; the pronoun in "alters it" returns to an omitted referent which the context indicates, the testator's command and bequest, not to God's command to make a bequest, since no one can alter that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 396} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 397} From Mujahid, al-Hasan, Qatada and al-Suddi, the bequest is meant; from Ibn 'Abbas, the testator's reward is secured with God and he is free of the sin, but if he bequeathed to cause harm his bequest is not allowed, as God said "without causing harm". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 397} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 398} From 'Ata', Salim ibn 'Abd Allah and Sulayman ibn Yasar, the bequest is carried out for the one it was made to; and from 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'mar, he would prefer him to bequeath to his kin and does not like removing it from the one bequeathed to. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 398} On "God is Hearing, Knowing" he says He hears your bequest as you make it, whether you are fair in it by what is customary or incline away from the truth, and knows what your hearts hide. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v03 p. 399}
