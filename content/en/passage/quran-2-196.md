## Summary
Verse 196 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 196 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "complete the hajj and the 'umra for God", al-Tabari says the commentators differed. One group said it means complete the hajj with its rites and sunan and the 'umra with its limits and sunan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 7} From 'Alqama, in the reading of 'Abd Allah it is "and establish the hajj and the 'umra to the House", meaning do not take the 'umra beyond the House; Ibrahim said he mentioned this to Sa'id ibn Jubayr who said "so Ibn 'Abbas said", and it is reported that Ibrahim read it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 7} From Ibn 'Abbas, whoever enters ihram for hajj or 'umra may not leave it until he completes it; the hajj is complete on the day of sacrifice when he has thrown the jamrat al-'aqaba and visited the House, for then he is out of all his ihram, and the 'umra is complete when he has circled the House and Safa and Marwa, for then he is out of ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 7} From Mujahid, whatever they were commanded in them. From al-Rabi', from Ibrahim, from 'Alqama ibn Qays, the hajj is the rites of the hajj and the 'umra is not taken beyond the House. From Ibrahim, one completes the rites of the hajj, 'Arafa and Muzdalifa and their stations, and the 'umra is for the House, that he circle the House and between Safa and Marwa and then leave ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 8}

Others said their completion is that you enter ihram for each separately from your family's dwelling. From 'Abd Allah ibn Salama, a man asked 'Ali about the verse and he said, "that you enter ihram from your family's house"; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, part of the completion of the 'umra is that you enter ihram from your family's house. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 8} From Tawus, their completion is to perform them separately, each begun from your family, and in another report, to perform them each timed from your family, and that is their completion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9} The editor explains duwayra, mu'tanafatayn and miqat, and notes that number 3192 is missing from the printed numbering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9}

Others said the completion of the 'umra is that it be done outside the hajj months, and the completion of the hajj is that it be performed with all its rites, so that no blood-offering falls due on the doer for qiran or tamattu'. From Qatada, the completion of the 'umra is what is outside the hajj months, and whoever is in the hajj months and then stays to perform the hajj, it is tamattu', with the hady owed if he finds one and otherwise fasting three days in the hajj and seven when he returns; and in another report, what is outside the hajj months is a complete 'umra and what is in the hajj months is tamattu' with the hady owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, an 'umra in the hajj months is not complete, and when asked about an 'umra in Muharram, he said they used to consider it complete. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10}

Others said their completion is that you set out from your family wanting nothing else. From Sufyan, that you set out from your family wanting only the hajj and 'umra and declare from the miqat, not that you go out for trade or a need and when near Mecca say "if only I performed hajj or 'umra", for that suffices, but the completion is that you go out for it and for nothing else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10}

Others said the meaning is: complete the hajj and the 'umra for God once you have entered them. From Ibn Zayd, the 'umra is not obligatory on anyone; he said it is not fitting for anyone, once he enters a matter, to do other than complete it, and once he has entered it, he should not declare for a day or two and return, as one who fasted a day should not break the fast at midday. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10}

Al-Sha'bi used to read "wa'l-'umratu" with the nominative. From Sa'id ibn Abi Burda, al-Sha'bi and Abu Burda discussed the 'umra; al-Sha'bi said it is voluntary and read it in the nominative, and Abu Burda said it is obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11} Al-Tabari says the opposite is also reported from al-Sha'bi, though this is the well-known view from him: that the 'umra is obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11} The reading of those who said the 'umra is obligatory is the accusative, meaning establish the obligation of the hajj and 'umra. From Masruq, "you were commanded in the Book of God four things: establishing the prayer, giving the zakat, the hajj and the 'umra", and then he recited "God has a claim on people to make the pilgrimage to the House" and "complete the hajj and 'umra for God to the House"; in another report from him, "we were commanded to establish four: prayer, zakat, 'umra and hajj, and the 'umra took from the hajj the place the zakat has to the prayer". From 'Ali ibn al-Husayn and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, both asked whether the 'umra is obligatory on people, "we know it only as obligatory, as God said 'complete the hajj and 'umra for God'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11}

From 'Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Sulayman, a man asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr whether the 'umra is obligatory or voluntary; he said "obligatory", and the man said "al-Sha'bi says it is voluntary", and he said "al-Sha'bi was wrong"; the editor says "kadhaba" here means he erred in his ijtihad, not lied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} From 'Ata', both are obligatory, the hajj and the 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} Al-Tabari says the interpretation of these is that both are obligatory duties that God commanded be established as He commanded the prayer to be established, and they made the 'umra obligatory like the hajj; they are many of the Companions, Successors and those after them, and he disliked lengthening the book by naming them; they said "complete" means "establish". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} From al-Suddi, establish the hajj and the 'umra. From 'Ali, through Thuwayr, "and establish the hajj and the 'umra for the House", and then it is obligatory like the hajj; the editor judges Thuwayr very weak and quotes what al-Thawri said of him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} From 'Abd Allah (ibn Mas'ud), "and establish the hajj and the 'umra to the House", then he said, "by God, were it not for scruple and that I have not heard anything from the Messenger of God about it, I would say the 'umra is obligatory like the hajj"; the editor says this is one of the irregular readings that differ from the written text of the codex and that both chains are weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 13} Al-Tabari says it is as if they meant by "establish the hajj and 'umra" bring both with their limits and rulings as they are obligatory on you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 13}

Others, who read 'umra in the accusative, said the 'umra is voluntary, and saw no indication of its being obligatory in the accusative, since some acts one must carry out and complete on entering them although entering them in the first place was not obligatory, like a voluntary hajj, about which there is no disagreement that once in ihram for it one must go on and complete it; so with the 'umra; they said the obligation of the hajj is established by "God has a claim on people to make the pilgrimage to the House". A number of Companions, Successors and later people said this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 13} From 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, the hajj is a duty and the 'umra voluntary; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the 'umra is not obligatory; from Ibrahim, a good sunna; from al-Sha'bi, the 'umra is voluntary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 14}

Those who read 'umra in the nominative said there is no ground for the accusative: the 'umra is a visit to the House, and no one deserves the name mu'tamir except as a visitor; when he reaches it and circles it and Safa and Marwa no work remains after it that he is commanded to complete, unlike the pilgrim who after reaching it is commanded to go to 'Arafa and Muzdalifa; so the right reading in their view is the nominative, as one of the works of devotion to God, with "lillah" as its predicate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 14} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 15}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the better of the two readings in his view is the accusative, joined to the hajj, meaning the command to complete both, and there is no weight in the argument of those who defend the nominative that the 'umra is a visit to the House, for when the mu'tamir reaches the House his visit has ended and what remains is the completion of the work God commanded him, namely circling the House, going between Safa and Marwa and avoiding what God commanded him to avoid, which is a work other than the visit; and the authorities agree on the accusative and all the readers of the regions oppose the nominative reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 15} As for the better of the two views on "and the 'umra for God" in the accusative reading, he holds it is the saying of 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and those who agree with him, that it means "complete the hajj and 'umra for God to the House after you have made them obligatory on yourselves", not that this verse is a command to begin them, enter them and perform them, since the verse bears both meanings: a command to establish them from the start, making them obligatory on the servants, or a command to complete them after entering them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 15}

Al-Tabari continues: since the verse bears both meanings, neither party has in it a proof against the other that the other does not have in the like; and since there is no report making the 'umra's obligation a decisive proof removing excuse, and the community differed over its obligation, a saying that "it is obligatory" without a proof that shows its correctness has no meaning, for duties bind the servants only by a clear indication. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 16} He then gives what someone might think shows it obligatory: from Abu al-Muntafiq, I came to the Prophet at 'Arafa and drew near until the neck of my mount and the neck of his were crossed, and said, "Messenger of God, tell me an act that will save me from God's punishment and enter me into His garden", and he said, "worship God and associate nothing with Him, establish the prescribed prayer, pay the obligatory zakat, and perform hajj and 'umra", with Ashhal's doubt whether he added "fast Ramadan", "and look at what you would like people to do to you and do it to them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 16} The editor judges the chain weak because some of its narrators are unnamed, and identifies Hatim ibn Bukayr, Ashhal ibn Hatim and Abu al-Muntafiq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 16} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 17} From Abu Razin al-'Uqayli, I said, "Messenger of God, my father is a very old man who cannot do the hajj or the 'umra or travel, and Islam has reached him; shall I perform the hajj for him?" He said, "perform hajj and 'umra for your father". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 17} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18} The editor says this report is sound, contrary to what al-Tabari says a few lines later in weakening all these reports, and traces it to al-Tayalisi, Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, who called it hasan sahih, and quotes Ahmad, "I know of no report on the obligation of the 'umra better or sounder than this". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18} From Abu Qilaba, the Prophet gave a sermon and said "worship God and associate nothing with Him, establish the prayer, pay the zakat, perform hajj and 'umra, and be upright and it will be upright for you"; the editor says it is mursal and no proof stands on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18}

Al-Tabari says, as his view, that these reports cannot establish a proof in religion because their chains are weak, and moreover, weak as they are, there are reports like them indicating that the 'umra is voluntary and not an obligatory duty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18} From Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, from the Prophet, asked whether the 'umra is obligatory, he said "no, but that you perform 'umra is better for you"; the editor traces it to Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi, who called it hasan sahih, and gives al-Bayhaqi's preference that its preserved form is Jabir's own saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 19} From Abu Salih al-Hanafi, the Messenger of God said "the hajj is jihad and the 'umra is voluntary"; the editor says it is mursal and gives what al-Shafi'i relates in al-Umm of a discussion of whether it is established, and his statement that what is closer to the apparent sense of the Qur'an and better for the people of knowledge, in his view, is that the 'umra be obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 19}

Al-Tabari answers someone who claimed the 'umra is obligatory because he found no voluntary act without a prescribed act as its foundation, so since it is shown to be voluntary it must have an obligation: the i'tikaf is made voluntary, so what is the obligation from which it comes, the foundation of its voluntary form? If he says it is obligatory he leaves the saying of the whole community, and if he says voluntary he is asked what made the i'tikaf voluntary and the 'umra obligatory; whatever he says of one binds him to the like of the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 20}

Al-Tabari then states his choices: the better reading of "al-'umra" is the accusative; the better interpretation of "complete the hajj and the 'umra for God" is that of Ibn 'Abbas, from the report of 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, that it is God's command to complete their acts after entering them and making them obligatory, according to the limits and sunan He commanded; and the better view on the 'umra is that of those who said it is voluntary and not an obligation, so the verse means: complete, believers, the hajj and 'umra for God after you enter them and make them obligatory on yourselves, according to the limits God commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 20} He says God revealed this verse to His Prophet at the 'umra of al-Hudaybiyya in which they were barred from the House, informing him and the believers what is on them in their ihram if they are allowed to reach the House, and explaining the way out of ihram if they entered it and were barred from the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 20}

Al-Tabari says that to mention what is required of them in their 'umra of the year of al-Hudaybiyya and what is required of them afterwards in their 'umra and hajj, God opened with "they ask you about the crescents; say, they are times for the people and for the hajj", and he refers to his earlier explanation of the meaning of hajj and 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 21}

On "and if you are prevented, then what is easy of the sacrificial animal", he says the commentators differed over the ihsar for which God made what is easy of the hady binding on one afflicted with it in his hajj or 'umra. One group said it is every obstacle or restraint that prevents the muhrim and holds him from the work God made obligatory on him in his ihram and from reaching the Sacred House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 21} From Mujahid, hasr is all detention: any man whose hajj or 'umra is obstructed sends his hady from where he is held; and on "if you are prevented" he said, a person falls ill, or breaks a limb, or something holds him and overcomes him, whatever it be, let him send what is easy of the hady and not shave his head nor leave ihram until the day of sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 21} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22} From 'Ata', ihsar is anything that holds him. From Qatada on the muhsar, it is fear, illness and being held up; when that befalls him he sends his hady and when the hady reaches its place he is free. From 'Urwa, anything that holds the muhrim is ihsar. From Ibrahim, illness, a break or fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22} From Ibn 'Abbas, whoever enters ihram for hajj or 'umra and is then kept from the House by an illness that exhausts him or an excuse that holds him, it is on him to make it up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22}

Al-Tabari gives the ground of those who said this: ihsar in Arab speech means prevention by an ailment such as illness and the like, not subjugation and overpowering by a conqueror, such as an ailment of illness, a sting, a wound, the loss of provisions or a broken mount; whereas prevention by an enemy, confinement by a jailer or the overpowering of a ruler or a coercive person the Arabs call hasr, not ihsar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} They cited "We made Gehenna a hasir for the unbelievers", meaning a restrainer, and that the Arabs say "the enemy was besieged (hawsira)" and "the enemy is besieged", not "ahsara al-'aduww", while "ahsara al-rajul" is said of an ailment from illness or fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} They said they made detention by an enemy like the hasr of illness by analogy, not by the apparent sense of "if you are prevented". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23}

Others said "if you are prevented" means if an enemy or a coercing human restrains you from reaching the House, and that bodily ailments such as illness and wounds are not included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} From Ibn 'Abbas, through Mujahid and 'Ata', hasr is the enemy's hasr, so the man sends his hady; if because of the enemy he cannot reach the House and finds someone to take it to Mecca for him, he sends it and enters ihram (Abu 'Asim said "we do not know whether he said 'enters ihram' or 'leaves it'") from the day he arranges with the bearer of the hady when he buys it, and when he is safe it is on him to perform hajj or 'umra; if an illness holds him and he has no hady he leaves ihram where he is held, and if he has a hady he does not leave until it reaches its place, and once he has sent it he need not perform hajj or 'umra the next year unless he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 24} From Ibn 'Abbas, through Tawus, there is no hasr except detention by an enemy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 24} Malik ibn Anas said: it reached me that the Messenger of God and his companions left ihram at al-Hudaybiyya, sacrificed the hady, shaved their heads and left ihram from everything before circling the House and before the hady reached it, and we do not know that the Messenger of God commanded any of his companions or any who were with him to make up anything or to return for anything; the editor says this is the text of Malik's words in the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 24}

From Malik ibn Anas, asked about one prevented by an enemy and kept from the House: he leaves ihram from everything, slaughters his hady and shaves his head where he is held, and owes no making up, unless he has never performed hajj, in which case he must perform the hajj of Islam. He said: and the rule with us for one prevented without an enemy, by illness or the like, is that he treats himself with what is necessary and pays a ransom, then makes it an 'umra, and performs hajj the next year and offers a hady. The editor says the text of the Muwatta ends at "the hajj of Islam" and what follows is an addition, and corrects the printed wording from the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 25} Al-Tabari gives the ground of those who said as Malik did: the verse was revealed about the polytheists' preventing the Messenger of God and his companions from the House, and God commanded His Prophet and those with him to slaughter their offerings and leave ihram, so its ruling may not be turned to a meaning other than that for which it was revealed; as for the sick person unable to travel who misses 'Arafa, he is a man who has missed the hajj and must leave ihram as one who missed the hajj does, and he is not within the meaning of the muhsar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 25}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the better of the two interpretations of "if you are prevented" is that of those who took it to mean: if fear of an enemy, or illness, or an ailment prevented you from reaching the House, that is, your fear or illness made you hold yourselves back from proceeding with the hajj and 'umra you made obligatory on yourselves; one says "fear of so-and-so kept me (ahsarani) from meeting you", and "my illness", meaning it made me hold myself back, whereas if the one who restrains is a man, one says "so-and-so restrained me (hasarani) from meeting you", meaning he held me; so if the verse meant what that interpreter thought it would have to read "husirtum". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 25} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 26} He cites "and when you are safe, whoever enjoys the 'umra up to the hajj", safety coming only by the removal of fear, so it is known that the ihsar God meant is a fear whose removal brings safety; hence confinement by one with no fear for one's life from the confinement, such as a ruler whose punishment is not feared, a father or a woman's husband, is not within the ruling of the verse by its apparent wording, though in his view its ruling is attached to it by analogy. He points to the report of Ibn 'Abbas, "hasr is the hasr of the enemy", and says that since this is a prevention from reaching the House, every obstacle that befalls the muhrim and bars him from reaching the House is its like in ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 26}

The scholars then differed on "what is easy of the hady". One group said it is a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 27} From Ibn 'Abbas by several routes, "what is easy of the hady" is a sheep; from al-Nu'man ibn Malik, I performed tamattu' and asked Ibn 'Abbas, and he said "what is easy of the hady"; I said "a sheep?" and he said "a sheep"; in another report from him, it is from the eight pairs, camels, cattle, goats and sheep; from al-Zuhri, Ibn 'Abbas used to say, from the flock. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 27} From al-Hasan, a sheep. From Qatada, its highest is a camel, its middle a cow and its lowest a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 28} From Ibn 'Abbas by other routes, a sheep; from 'Ata', a sheep; from al-Suddi, the muhsar sends a hady, a sheep or above; from 'Alqama, if a man declares ihram for hajj and is prevented, he sends what is easy of the hady, a sheep, and Ibrahim said he mentioned this to Sa'id ibn Jubayr who said "so Ibn 'Abbas said". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 28} From Ibn 'Abbas, a sheep or above, and in Abu Jamra's report from him, a camel, a cow, a sheep, or a share in a blood sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 28} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, Ibn 'Abbas held a sheep to be what is easy of the hady; from 'Ikrima, Ibrahim, 'Ata' and Abu Ja'far, a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29} From Ibn 'Abbas, he said the hady is a sheep, and when asked whether it can be less than a cow, he said, "I will read you from the Book of God what shows you the hady is a sheep: what is due for a gazelle? They said a sheep; He said 'a hady reaching the Ka'ba'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29}

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, from his father, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib used to say "what is easy of the hady" is a sheep; from Malik, it reached him that Ibn 'Abbas used to say a sheep, and Malik said "and that is most beloved to me"; the editor notes that these places are in the Muwatta and that Malik cited the verse of al-Ma'ida as Ibn 'Abbas did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30} From Ibn 'Abbas, the muhsar owes a hady: if he is well-off from the camels, otherwise from the cattle, otherwise from the flock. From him also, a sheep, and whatever you magnify of God's rites is better. From 'Ata', a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30}

Others said "what is easy of the hady" is from camels and cattle, one age below another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30} From Ibn 'Umar: a cow lower than a cow and a camel lower than a camel; when a man asked him about it, he said, "will you accept a sheep?", as if he did not approve it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 31} From him also, a she-camel or a cow, a she-camel lower than a she-camel and a cow lower than a cow; from him, a camel or a cow; and from him, camels and cattle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 31} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar and 'A'isha used to say what is easy of the hady is from camels and cattle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 31} From 'Abd Allah or 'Ubayd Allah ibn Jubayr, I asked Ibn 'Umar about the hady for tamattu' and he said "a she-camel"; I said "what do you say of a sheep?" and he said "do all of you want a sheep? all of you a sheep?"; the editor identifies the narrators of this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 32} From Mujahid and Tawus, a cow. From 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, in Ibn 'Umar's saying, a cow or above. From Ibn 'Umar, a camel or a cow, whereas a sheep is only a nusuk (sacrificial offering). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 32} From 'Urwa, a camel lower than a camel and a cow lower than a cow, and a sheep is only a nusuk. From Ibn 'Umar in two further reports, he used to say a cow, and the people of Yemen would come to him and ask and say "a sheep! a sheep!", and he would answer them "a sheep! a sheep!" to rebuke them, saying a camel lower than a camel and a cow lower than a cow, "but what is easy of the hady is a cow". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 32} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 33}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the better of the two views is that of those who said "what is easy of the hady" is a sheep, because God made binding only what is easy of the hady, and that applies to whatever is easy for the giver to give, whatever it may be, unless God singled out something from it, in which case what was singled out falls outside what the apparent text can bear, and everything else suffices when given, once it deserves the name hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 33} He takes up the objection of those who refused that a sheep is among what is easy, that it does not deserve the name hady, as if one offered a chicken or an egg, and answers that if the chicken and the egg were disputed as the sheep is they would be on one footing, but what is below the jadha' of sheep and the thaniy of goats, camels and cattle of age and upward is excluded from being the hady God made binding by the decisive proof transmitted from our Prophet by inheritance, and so falls outside "what is easy of the hady", although it is easy for us; and since there is disagreement about the jadha' of sheep and the thaniy of goats, it suffices by the apparent text because it is among what is easy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 33} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 34}

On the grammar of "ma", al-Tabari says its place is nominative, governed by an omitted "fa-'alayhi" (then upon him), so the meaning is: complete the hajj and 'umra for God, and if one prevents you from completing them by illness, a break or fear of an enemy, then upon you, for your leaving ihram if you wish to leave it, is what is easy of the hady; he chose the nominative because most of the Qur'an has the nominative in its parallels, as in "then a ransom of fasting" and "then fasting three days", and says that if one said its place is accusative, meaning "then offer what is easy of the hady", the speaker would not be wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 34} "Al-hady" is a plural whose singular is hadiyya, on the pattern of jadiyya of the saddle, plural jadi lightened; from Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala', "I know no word in speech that resembles it". The editor corrects Abu 'Ubayda's vowelling and explains the jadiyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 34}
