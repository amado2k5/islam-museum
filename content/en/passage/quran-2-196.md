## Summary
Verse 196 of [[passage/quran-2|Surat al-Baqara]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 196 of 286 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

## In al-Tabari's commentary

On "complete the hajj and the 'umra for God", al-Tabari says the commentators differed. One group said it means complete the hajj with its rites and sunan and the 'umra with its limits and sunan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 7} From 'Alqama, in the reading of 'Abd Allah it is "and establish the hajj and the 'umra to the House", meaning do not take the 'umra beyond the House; Ibrahim said he mentioned this to Sa'id ibn Jubayr who said "so Ibn 'Abbas said", and it is reported that Ibrahim read it so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 7} From Ibn 'Abbas, whoever enters ihram for hajj or 'umra may not leave it until he completes it; the hajj is complete on the day of sacrifice when he has thrown the jamrat al-'aqaba and visited the House, for then he is out of all his ihram, and the 'umra is complete when he has circled the House and Safa and Marwa, for then he is out of ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 7} From Mujahid, whatever they were commanded in them. From al-Rabi', from Ibrahim, from 'Alqama ibn Qays, the hajj is the rites of the hajj and the 'umra is not taken beyond the House. From Ibrahim, one completes the rites of the hajj, 'Arafa and Muzdalifa and their stations, and the 'umra is for the House, that he circle the House and between Safa and Marwa and then leave ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 8}

Others said their completion is that you enter ihram for each separately from your family's dwelling. From 'Abd Allah ibn Salama, a man asked 'Ali about the verse and he said, "that you enter ihram from your family's house"; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, part of the completion of the 'umra is that you enter ihram from your family's house. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 8} From Tawus, their completion is to perform them separately, each begun from your family, and in another report, to perform them each timed from your family, and that is their completion. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9} The editor explains duwayra, mu'tanafatayn and miqat, and notes that number 3192 is missing from the printed numbering. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 8} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9}

Others said the completion of the 'umra is that it be done outside the hajj months, and the completion of the hajj is that it be performed with all its rites, so that no blood-offering falls due on the doer for qiran or tamattu'. From Qatada, the completion of the 'umra is what is outside the hajj months, and whoever is in the hajj months and then stays to perform the hajj, it is tamattu', with the hady owed if he finds one and otherwise fasting three days in the hajj and seven when he returns; and in another report, what is outside the hajj months is a complete 'umra and what is in the hajj months is tamattu' with the hady owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, an 'umra in the hajj months is not complete, and when asked about an 'umra in Muharram, he said they used to consider it complete. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 9} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10}

Others said their completion is that you set out from your family wanting nothing else. From Sufyan, that you set out from your family wanting only the hajj and 'umra and declare from the miqat, not that you go out for trade or a need and when near Mecca say "if only I performed hajj or 'umra", for that suffices, but the completion is that you go out for it and for nothing else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10}

Others said the meaning is: complete the hajj and the 'umra for God once you have entered them. From Ibn Zayd, the 'umra is not obligatory on anyone; he said it is not fitting for anyone, once he enters a matter, to do other than complete it, and once he has entered it, he should not declare for a day or two and return, as one who fasted a day should not break the fast at midday. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10}

Al-Sha'bi used to read "wa'l-'umratu" with the nominative. From Sa'id ibn Abi Burda, al-Sha'bi and Abu Burda discussed the 'umra; al-Sha'bi said it is voluntary and read it in the nominative, and Abu Burda said it is obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 10} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11} Al-Tabari says the opposite is also reported from al-Sha'bi, though this is the well-known view from him: that the 'umra is obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11} The reading of those who said the 'umra is obligatory is the accusative, meaning establish the obligation of the hajj and 'umra. From Masruq, "you were commanded in the Book of God four things: establishing the prayer, giving the zakat, the hajj and the 'umra", and then he recited "God has a claim on people to make the pilgrimage to the House" and "complete the hajj and 'umra for God to the House"; in another report from him, "we were commanded to establish four: prayer, zakat, 'umra and hajj, and the 'umra took from the hajj the place the zakat has to the prayer". From 'Ali ibn al-Husayn and Sa'id ibn Jubayr, both asked whether the 'umra is obligatory on people, "we know it only as obligatory, as God said 'complete the hajj and 'umra for God'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11}

From 'Abd al-Malik ibn Abi Sulayman, a man asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr whether the 'umra is obligatory or voluntary; he said "obligatory", and the man said "al-Sha'bi says it is voluntary", and he said "al-Sha'bi was wrong"; the editor says "kadhaba" here means he erred in his ijtihad, not lied. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 11} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} From 'Ata', both are obligatory, the hajj and the 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} Al-Tabari says the interpretation of these is that both are obligatory duties that God commanded be established as He commanded the prayer to be established, and they made the 'umra obligatory like the hajj; they are many of the Companions, Successors and those after them, and he disliked lengthening the book by naming them; they said "complete" means "establish". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} From al-Suddi, establish the hajj and the 'umra. From 'Ali, through Thuwayr, "and establish the hajj and the 'umra for the House", and then it is obligatory like the hajj; the editor judges Thuwayr very weak and quotes what al-Thawri said of him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 12} From 'Abd Allah (ibn Mas'ud), "and establish the hajj and the 'umra to the House", then he said, "by God, were it not for scruple and that I have not heard anything from the Messenger of God about it, I would say the 'umra is obligatory like the hajj"; the editor says this is one of the irregular readings that differ from the written text of the codex and that both chains are weak. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 13} Al-Tabari says it is as if they meant by "establish the hajj and 'umra" bring both with their limits and rulings as they are obligatory on you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 13}

Others, who read 'umra in the accusative, said the 'umra is voluntary, and saw no indication of its being obligatory in the accusative, since some acts one must carry out and complete on entering them although entering them in the first place was not obligatory, like a voluntary hajj, about which there is no disagreement that once in ihram for it one must go on and complete it; so with the 'umra; they said the obligation of the hajj is established by "God has a claim on people to make the pilgrimage to the House". A number of Companions, Successors and later people said this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 13} From 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, the hajj is a duty and the 'umra voluntary; from Sa'id ibn Jubayr, the 'umra is not obligatory; from Ibrahim, a good sunna; from al-Sha'bi, the 'umra is voluntary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 14}

Those who read 'umra in the nominative said there is no ground for the accusative: the 'umra is a visit to the House, and no one deserves the name mu'tamir except as a visitor; when he reaches it and circles it and Safa and Marwa no work remains after it that he is commanded to complete, unlike the pilgrim who after reaching it is commanded to go to 'Arafa and Muzdalifa; so the right reading in their view is the nominative, as one of the works of devotion to God, with "lillah" as its predicate. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 14} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 15}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the better of the two readings in his view is the accusative, joined to the hajj, meaning the command to complete both, and there is no weight in the argument of those who defend the nominative that the 'umra is a visit to the House, for when the mu'tamir reaches the House his visit has ended and what remains is the completion of the work God commanded him, namely circling the House, going between Safa and Marwa and avoiding what God commanded him to avoid, which is a work other than the visit; and the authorities agree on the accusative and all the readers of the regions oppose the nominative reading. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 15} As for the better of the two views on "and the 'umra for God" in the accusative reading, he holds it is the saying of 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud and those who agree with him, that it means "complete the hajj and 'umra for God to the House after you have made them obligatory on yourselves", not that this verse is a command to begin them, enter them and perform them, since the verse bears both meanings: a command to establish them from the start, making them obligatory on the servants, or a command to complete them after entering them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 15}

Al-Tabari continues: since the verse bears both meanings, neither party has in it a proof against the other that the other does not have in the like; and since there is no report making the 'umra's obligation a decisive proof removing excuse, and the community differed over its obligation, a saying that "it is obligatory" without a proof that shows its correctness has no meaning, for duties bind the servants only by a clear indication. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 16} He then gives what someone might think shows it obligatory: from Abu al-Muntafiq, I came to the Prophet at 'Arafa and drew near until the neck of my mount and the neck of his were crossed, and said, "Messenger of God, tell me an act that will save me from God's punishment and enter me into His garden", and he said, "worship God and associate nothing with Him, establish the prescribed prayer, pay the obligatory zakat, and perform hajj and 'umra", with Ashhal's doubt whether he added "fast Ramadan", "and look at what you would like people to do to you and do it to them". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 16} The editor judges the chain weak because some of its narrators are unnamed, and identifies Hatim ibn Bukayr, Ashhal ibn Hatim and Abu al-Muntafiq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 16} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 17} From Abu Razin al-'Uqayli, I said, "Messenger of God, my father is a very old man who cannot do the hajj or the 'umra or travel, and Islam has reached him; shall I perform the hajj for him?" He said, "perform hajj and 'umra for your father". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 17} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18} The editor says this report is sound, contrary to what al-Tabari says a few lines later in weakening all these reports, and traces it to al-Tayalisi, Ahmad, Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi, who called it hasan sahih, and quotes Ahmad, "I know of no report on the obligation of the 'umra better or sounder than this". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18} From Abu Qilaba, the Prophet gave a sermon and said "worship God and associate nothing with Him, establish the prayer, pay the zakat, perform hajj and 'umra, and be upright and it will be upright for you"; the editor says it is mursal and no proof stands on it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18}

Al-Tabari says, as his view, that these reports cannot establish a proof in religion because their chains are weak, and moreover, weak as they are, there are reports like them indicating that the 'umra is voluntary and not an obligatory duty. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 18} From Jabir ibn 'Abd Allah, from the Prophet, asked whether the 'umra is obligatory, he said "no, but that you perform 'umra is better for you"; the editor traces it to Ahmad and al-Tirmidhi, who called it hasan sahih, and gives al-Bayhaqi's preference that its preserved form is Jabir's own saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 19} From Abu Salih al-Hanafi, the Messenger of God said "the hajj is jihad and the 'umra is voluntary"; the editor says it is mursal and gives what al-Shafi'i relates in al-Umm of a discussion of whether it is established, and his statement that what is closer to the apparent sense of the Qur'an and better for the people of knowledge, in his view, is that the 'umra be obligatory. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 19}

Al-Tabari answers someone who claimed the 'umra is obligatory because he found no voluntary act without a prescribed act as its foundation, so since it is shown to be voluntary it must have an obligation: the i'tikaf is made voluntary, so what is the obligation from which it comes, the foundation of its voluntary form? If he says it is obligatory he leaves the saying of the whole community, and if he says voluntary he is asked what made the i'tikaf voluntary and the 'umra obligatory; whatever he says of one binds him to the like of the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 20}

Al-Tabari then states his choices: the better reading of "al-'umra" is the accusative; the better interpretation of "complete the hajj and the 'umra for God" is that of Ibn 'Abbas, from the report of 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, that it is God's command to complete their acts after entering them and making them obligatory, according to the limits and sunan He commanded; and the better view on the 'umra is that of those who said it is voluntary and not an obligation, so the verse means: complete, believers, the hajj and 'umra for God after you enter them and make them obligatory on yourselves, according to the limits God commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 20} He says God revealed this verse to His Prophet at the 'umra of al-Hudaybiyya in which they were barred from the House, informing him and the believers what is on them in their ihram if they are allowed to reach the House, and explaining the way out of ihram if they entered it and were barred from the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 20}

Al-Tabari says that to mention what is required of them in their 'umra of the year of al-Hudaybiyya and what is required of them afterwards in their 'umra and hajj, God opened with "they ask you about the crescents; say, they are times for the people and for the hajj", and he refers to his earlier explanation of the meaning of hajj and 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 21}

On "and if you are prevented, then what is easy of the sacrificial animal", he says the commentators differed over the ihsar for which God made what is easy of the hady binding on one afflicted with it in his hajj or 'umra. One group said it is every obstacle or restraint that prevents the muhrim and holds him from the work God made obligatory on him in his ihram and from reaching the Sacred House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 21} From Mujahid, hasr is all detention: any man whose hajj or 'umra is obstructed sends his hady from where he is held; and on "if you are prevented" he said, a person falls ill, or breaks a limb, or something holds him and overcomes him, whatever it be, let him send what is easy of the hady and not shave his head nor leave ihram until the day of sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 21} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22} From 'Ata', ihsar is anything that holds him. From Qatada on the muhsar, it is fear, illness and being held up; when that befalls him he sends his hady and when the hady reaches its place he is free. From 'Urwa, anything that holds the muhrim is ihsar. From Ibrahim, illness, a break or fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22} From Ibn 'Abbas, whoever enters ihram for hajj or 'umra and is then kept from the House by an illness that exhausts him or an excuse that holds him, it is on him to make it up. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22}

Al-Tabari gives the ground of those who said this: ihsar in Arab speech means prevention by an ailment such as illness and the like, not subjugation and overpowering by a conqueror, such as an ailment of illness, a sting, a wound, the loss of provisions or a broken mount; whereas prevention by an enemy, confinement by a jailer or the overpowering of a ruler or a coercive person the Arabs call hasr, not ihsar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 22} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} They cited "We made Gehenna a hasir for the unbelievers", meaning a restrainer, and that the Arabs say "the enemy was besieged (hawsira)" and "the enemy is besieged", not "ahsara al-'aduww", while "ahsara al-rajul" is said of an ailment from illness or fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} They said they made detention by an enemy like the hasr of illness by analogy, not by the apparent sense of "if you are prevented". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23}

Others said "if you are prevented" means if an enemy or a coercing human restrains you from reaching the House, and that bodily ailments such as illness and wounds are not included. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} From Ibn 'Abbas, through Mujahid and 'Ata', hasr is the enemy's hasr, so the man sends his hady; if because of the enemy he cannot reach the House and finds someone to take it to Mecca for him, he sends it and enters ihram (Abu 'Asim said "we do not know whether he said 'enters ihram' or 'leaves it'") from the day he arranges with the bearer of the hady when he buys it, and when he is safe it is on him to perform hajj or 'umra; if an illness holds him and he has no hady he leaves ihram where he is held, and if he has a hady he does not leave until it reaches its place, and once he has sent it he need not perform hajj or 'umra the next year unless he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 23} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 24} From Ibn 'Abbas, through Tawus, there is no hasr except detention by an enemy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 24} Malik ibn Anas said: it reached me that the Messenger of God and his companions left ihram at al-Hudaybiyya, sacrificed the hady, shaved their heads and left ihram from everything before circling the House and before the hady reached it, and we do not know that the Messenger of God commanded any of his companions or any who were with him to make up anything or to return for anything; the editor says this is the text of Malik's words in the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 24}

From Malik ibn Anas, asked about one prevented by an enemy and kept from the House: he leaves ihram from everything, slaughters his hady and shaves his head where he is held, and owes no making up, unless he has never performed hajj, in which case he must perform the hajj of Islam. He said: and the rule with us for one prevented without an enemy, by illness or the like, is that he treats himself with what is necessary and pays a ransom, then makes it an 'umra, and performs hajj the next year and offers a hady. The editor says the text of the Muwatta ends at "the hajj of Islam" and what follows is an addition, and corrects the printed wording from the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 25} Al-Tabari gives the ground of those who said as Malik did: the verse was revealed about the polytheists' preventing the Messenger of God and his companions from the House, and God commanded His Prophet and those with him to slaughter their offerings and leave ihram, so its ruling may not be turned to a meaning other than that for which it was revealed; as for the sick person unable to travel who misses 'Arafa, he is a man who has missed the hajj and must leave ihram as one who missed the hajj does, and he is not within the meaning of the muhsar. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 25}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the better of the two interpretations of "if you are prevented" is that of those who took it to mean: if fear of an enemy, or illness, or an ailment prevented you from reaching the House, that is, your fear or illness made you hold yourselves back from proceeding with the hajj and 'umra you made obligatory on yourselves; one says "fear of so-and-so kept me (ahsarani) from meeting you", and "my illness", meaning it made me hold myself back, whereas if the one who restrains is a man, one says "so-and-so restrained me (hasarani) from meeting you", meaning he held me; so if the verse meant what that interpreter thought it would have to read "husirtum". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 25} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 26} He cites "and when you are safe, whoever enjoys the 'umra up to the hajj", safety coming only by the removal of fear, so it is known that the ihsar God meant is a fear whose removal brings safety; hence confinement by one with no fear for one's life from the confinement, such as a ruler whose punishment is not feared, a father or a woman's husband, is not within the ruling of the verse by its apparent wording, though in his view its ruling is attached to it by analogy. He points to the report of Ibn 'Abbas, "hasr is the hasr of the enemy", and says that since this is a prevention from reaching the House, every obstacle that befalls the muhrim and bars him from reaching the House is its like in ruling. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 26}

The scholars then differed on "what is easy of the hady". One group said it is a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 26} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 27} From Ibn 'Abbas by several routes, "what is easy of the hady" is a sheep; from al-Nu'man ibn Malik, I performed tamattu' and asked Ibn 'Abbas, and he said "what is easy of the hady"; I said "a sheep?" and he said "a sheep"; in another report from him, it is from the eight pairs, camels, cattle, goats and sheep; from al-Zuhri, Ibn 'Abbas used to say, from the flock. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 27} From al-Hasan, a sheep. From Qatada, its highest is a camel, its middle a cow and its lowest a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 28} From Ibn 'Abbas by other routes, a sheep; from 'Ata', a sheep; from al-Suddi, the muhsar sends a hady, a sheep or above; from 'Alqama, if a man declares ihram for hajj and is prevented, he sends what is easy of the hady, a sheep, and Ibrahim said he mentioned this to Sa'id ibn Jubayr who said "so Ibn 'Abbas said". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 28} From Ibn 'Abbas, a sheep or above, and in Abu Jamra's report from him, a camel, a cow, a sheep, or a share in a blood sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 28} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, Ibn 'Abbas held a sheep to be what is easy of the hady; from 'Ikrima, Ibrahim, 'Ata' and Abu Ja'far, a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29} From Ibn 'Abbas, he said the hady is a sheep, and when asked whether it can be less than a cow, he said, "I will read you from the Book of God what shows you the hady is a sheep: what is due for a gazelle? They said a sheep; He said 'a hady reaching the Ka'ba'". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29}

From Ja'far ibn Muhammad, from his father, 'Ali ibn Abi Talib used to say "what is easy of the hady" is a sheep; from Malik, it reached him that Ibn 'Abbas used to say a sheep, and Malik said "and that is most beloved to me"; the editor notes that these places are in the Muwatta and that Malik cited the verse of al-Ma'ida as Ibn 'Abbas did. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 29} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30} From Ibn 'Abbas, the muhsar owes a hady: if he is well-off from the camels, otherwise from the cattle, otherwise from the flock. From him also, a sheep, and whatever you magnify of God's rites is better. From 'Ata', a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30}

Others said "what is easy of the hady" is from camels and cattle, one age below another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30} From Ibn 'Umar: a cow lower than a cow and a camel lower than a camel; when a man asked him about it, he said, "will you accept a sheep?", as if he did not approve it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 30} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 31} From him also, a she-camel or a cow, a she-camel lower than a she-camel and a cow lower than a cow; from him, a camel or a cow; and from him, camels and cattle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 31} From al-Qasim ibn Muhammad, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar and 'A'isha used to say what is easy of the hady is from camels and cattle. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 31} From 'Abd Allah or 'Ubayd Allah ibn Jubayr, I asked Ibn 'Umar about the hady for tamattu' and he said "a she-camel"; I said "what do you say of a sheep?" and he said "do all of you want a sheep? all of you a sheep?"; the editor identifies the narrators of this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 32} From Mujahid and Tawus, a cow. From 'Ali ibn Abi Talha, in Ibn 'Umar's saying, a cow or above. From Ibn 'Umar, a camel or a cow, whereas a sheep is only a nusuk (sacrificial offering). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 32} From 'Urwa, a camel lower than a camel and a cow lower than a cow, and a sheep is only a nusuk. From Ibn 'Umar in two further reports, he used to say a cow, and the people of Yemen would come to him and ask and say "a sheep! a sheep!", and he would answer them "a sheep! a sheep!" to rebuke them, saying a camel lower than a camel and a cow lower than a cow, "but what is easy of the hady is a cow". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 32} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 33}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the better of the two views is that of those who said "what is easy of the hady" is a sheep, because God made binding only what is easy of the hady, and that applies to whatever is easy for the giver to give, whatever it may be, unless God singled out something from it, in which case what was singled out falls outside what the apparent text can bear, and everything else suffices when given, once it deserves the name hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 33} He takes up the objection of those who refused that a sheep is among what is easy, that it does not deserve the name hady, as if one offered a chicken or an egg, and answers that if the chicken and the egg were disputed as the sheep is they would be on one footing, but what is below the jadha' of sheep and the thaniy of goats, camels and cattle of age and upward is excluded from being the hady God made binding by the decisive proof transmitted from our Prophet by inheritance, and so falls outside "what is easy of the hady", although it is easy for us; and since there is disagreement about the jadha' of sheep and the thaniy of goats, it suffices by the apparent text because it is among what is easy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 33} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 34}

On the grammar of "ma", al-Tabari says its place is nominative, governed by an omitted "fa-'alayhi" (then upon him), so the meaning is: complete the hajj and 'umra for God, and if one prevents you from completing them by illness, a break or fear of an enemy, then upon you, for your leaving ihram if you wish to leave it, is what is easy of the hady; he chose the nominative because most of the Qur'an has the nominative in its parallels, as in "then a ransom of fasting" and "then fasting three days", and says that if one said its place is accusative, meaning "then offer what is easy of the hady", the speaker would not be wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 34} "Al-hady" is a plural whose singular is hadiyya, on the pattern of jadiyya of the saddle, plural jadi lightened; from Abu 'Amr ibn al-'Ala', "I know no word in speech that resembles it". The editor corrects Abu 'Ubayda's vowelling and explains the jadiyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 34}

Al-Tabari notes that the readers in every region read "al-hady" with a lightened ya' and a silent dal, except what is reported from al-A'raj, who read it with a kasra and doubled dal; the report from 'Asim differs, one version agreeing with al-A'raj and one with the other readers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 35} He says that in his view the hady is so named because one draws near to God by it, like the gift a man gives another; he cites a verse of Zuhayr ibn Abi Sulma, and the editor explains its occasion in Zuhayr's Diwan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 35}

He then explains "and do not shave your heads until the hady reaches its place": if you are prevented and want to leave ihram, then upon you is what is easy of the hady, and do not leave ihram until the hady reaches its place, since shaving the head is a leaving of ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 36} The editor quotes Ibn Kathir's commentary, which holds that this clause is joined to "complete the hajj and 'umra for God" and not to "if you are prevented" as Ibn Jarir held, arguing from the Prophet and his companions shaving and slaughtering outside the Haram in the year of al-Hudaybiyya and from the hadith of Hafsa in the two Sahihs; the editor adds that there is room for examination and detail in Ibn Kathir's faulting of Abu Ja'far which is not the place here. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 36}

Scholars then differed over the "place" of the hady that, once it reaches it, allows the prevented one to leave ihram. Some said: where the prevention is from fear of an enemy, its place is to slaughter or sacrifice it wherever he was held, in the lawful area or the Haram; where it is without an enemy, he does not leave ihram until he circumambulates the House and runs between al-Safa and al-Marwa. This is the view of those who said prevention means prevention by an enemy and nothing else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 36} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 37}

Among those who said it is Malik ibn Anas: it reached him that the Prophet and his companions left ihram at al-Hudaybiyya, slaughtered the hady, shaved and left everything before circumambulating the House and before the hady reached it, and he did not know that the Prophet ordered any of them to make up anything. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 37} From Nafi', 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar set out for Makka for 'umra during the civil strife and said, if I am kept from the House we will do as we did with the Messenger of God; he assumed ihram for 'umra, then reconsidered and said their affair is one, made the hajj binding with the 'umra, made one circumambulation, thought it sufficient and sent a hady. Malik said: on this is our practice for one prevented by an enemy; one prevented without an enemy does not leave ihram short of the House. Asked about one prevented by an enemy, Malik said he leaves everything, slaughters his hady and shaves wherever he was held, and owes no makeup, unless he has never performed hajj, in which case he owes the hajj of Islam. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 37} The editor points to the places of both reports in the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 37}

From Sulayman ibn Yasar, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar, Marwan ibn al-Hakam and 'Abd Allah ibn al-Zubayr gave a fatwa to Ibn Hizaba al-Makhzumi, who had been struck down on the road during hajj: to take the treatment he could not do without and pay the ransom, then make it an 'umra, perform hajj the following year and send a hady. Malik said that is our practice for one prevented without an enemy, and that anyone held back from hajj after entering ihram, by illness, a mistake in counting or the hidden crescent, is prevented and owes what the prevented owes, meaning to remain in ihram until he circumambulates and runs, then hajj the next year and a hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 38} The editor corrects the printed text's reading from the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 38} From Ayyub ibn Musa, Dawud ibn Abi 'Asim performed hajj once, fell ill, returned to al-Ta'if without running between al-Safa and al-Marwa, and wrote to 'Ata' ibn Abi Rabah, who wrote back: shed blood. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 38}

The argument of those who held Malik's view, that the place of the hady in prevention by an enemy is to slaughter it where its owner was held, is what Ibn 'Umar reported: when the hady was below the mountains that look down on the valley of al-Thaniyya, the polytheists confronted him and turned his face back, so the Prophet slaughtered the hady where they held him, which is al-Hudaybiyya, and shaved; some followed him and shaved, while others waited, so he said: God have mercy on those who shave; they said, and those who shorten; he said: God have mercy on those who shave; they said, and those who shorten; he said: and those who shorten. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 38} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 39} The editor states that the chain of this report is very weak because of Musa ibn 'Ubayda, that Abu Murra, freedman of Umm Hani', named Yazid, is a reliable Successor, that the meaning of the hadith is established from many routes, that something like it is established from Ibn 'Umar by a sound chain in the Musnad and al-Bukhari, and that the prayer for those who shave and those who shorten is established in the Musnad, the Muwatta and the two Sahihs. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 39}

From al-Miswar ibn Makhrama and Marwan ibn al-Hakam, when the Messenger of God wrote the agreement between himself and the polytheists of Quraysh at al-Hudaybiyya, he said to his companions, rise, slaughter and shave; by God no man rose, until he said it three times; so he went in to Umm Salama and told her, and Umm Salama said, go out, and do not speak a word to any of them until you slaughter your camels and call your barber to shave you. He did so, and when they saw it they rose and slaughtered, and some began shaving others until they almost killed each other with grief. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 39} The editor says it is part of a long, well-known hadith on the peace of al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 39}

They said: the Prophet slaughtered his hady when the polytheists barred him from the House at al-Hudaybiyya, and he and his companions left ihram; and al-Hudaybiyya is not part of the Haram. In that, they said, is a clear indication that "until the hady reaches its place" means that by slaughter or sacrifice it reaches the place where it is eaten and benefited from, at the spot of its slaughter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 40} They cited what is reported from the Prophet in a parallel case, when meat that Barira had received as charity was brought to him and he said: bring it near, it has reached its place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 40} Al-Tabari explains: it has reached the place of its being good and lawful for him by being a gift, after having been charity for Barira. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 40} The editor says this alludes to the well-known story of Barira in the two Sahihs, that the wording established there is that he said it is charity for her and a gift to us, or the like, and that he did not find the words "it has reached its place" in the Barira story, which may have reached al-Tabari by a transmission hidden from the editor; he adds that similar wording occurs in two other stories, one from Umm 'Atiyya al-Ansariyya (Musnad, al-Bukhari, Muslim) and one from Juwayriya bint al-Harith (Musnad, Muslim). {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 40}

Another group said the place of the prevented person's hady is the Haram, with no other place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 41} Among those who relate this is Ibn Mas'ud, in the report of 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid: a man of the Nakha' assumed ihram for 'umra and was stung at Dhat al-Shuquq; his companions went out to the road looking for travellers and met Ibn Mas'ud, told him, and he said: let him send a hady and set a day as a sign between you and him; when the hady is slaughtered he leaves ihram, and he owes the making up of his 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 41} In another version from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Yazid, we set out in ihram for 'umra, al-Aswad ibn Yazid among us, and a companion of ours was stung and we did not know what to do; we met a party that included 'Abd Allah ibn Mas'ud, who said: let the price of a hady be sent with you, and when the hady is slaughtered let him leave ihram, and he owes an 'umra the next year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 41} Al-Tabari repeats the report with several chains, and the editor notes that Dhat al-Shuquq is a stage on the road to Makka from al-Kufa and that "amar" and "amara" here mean a sign and a time. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 41} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 42} The later versions through al-A'mash, Shu'ba and al-Hajjaj agree, and say he owes the 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 42} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 43} The editor notes that al-Tahawi, in Sharh Ma'ani al-Athar, names the man "'Umayr ibn Sa'id al-Nakha'i", so that if this is right "'Amr ibn Sa'id" in these reports is a corruption of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 42} The editor also identifies the narrators of these chains, including al-Nakha'i, Ibn 'Utayba and al-Hajjaj ibn Arta'a. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 41} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 42} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 43}

From Ibn 'Abbas: whoever assumes ihram for hajj or 'umra and is then held back from the House by an illness that exhausts him or an excuse that detains him must slaughter what is easy of the hady, a sheep or above; if it was the hajj of Islam he must make it up, and if it was after the obligatory hajj, or an 'umra, he owes no makeup; then, on "until the hady reaches its place", he said that if he assumed ihram for hajj its place is the Day of Sacrifice, and if for 'umra its place is when it reaches the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 43} From him in another version: it is the man among the companions of Muhammad held from the House, who sends a hady to the House and stays in ihram until the hady reaches its place; when it does, he shaves and God completes his hajj for him. Prevention also means being barred from hajj: he owes a hady, from camels if well-off, otherwise cattle, otherwise sheep, makes his hajj an 'umra and sends his hady to the House; when it is slaughtered he has left ihram and owes the hajj next year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 43}

From 'Abd Allah ibn Salama, 'Ali was asked about the verse and said: when the pilgrim is prevented he sends the hady, and when it is slaughtered for him he leaves ihram, and he does not leave until his hady is slaughtered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 44} From 'Ata': one held back in his 'umra who sent his hady and it was intercepted gives something in charity or fasts; one whose hady was intercepted while on hajj, the place of the hady and of ihram is the Day of Sacrifice and nothing is owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 44} From al-Suddi: a man enters ihram, sets out and is prevented, by a sting or illness so that he cannot travel, or his mount breaks down; he stays and sends a hady, a sheep or above; if he recovers, travels and arrives in time he owes no hady; if the hajj passes him it becomes an 'umra and he owes a hajj the next year; and if he returned, he stays in ihram until it is slaughtered for him on the Day of Sacrifice, and if news reaches him that his companion did not slaughter for him he resumes ihram and sends another hady. Al-Suddi mentions that people differ over how many 'umras and hajjs are then owed, some saying two 'umras and some three. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 44}

From Ibn 'Abbas, through Mujahid and 'Ata': when a man is prevented he sends his hady, if he cannot reach the House; if he finds someone to convey it to Makka for him he sends it in his place and arranges a time with the bearer of the hady, and when he is safe he must perform hajj and 'umra; if an illness holds him and he has no hady he leaves ihram where he is held, and if he has a hady he does not leave until it reaches its place if he has sent it, and he need not perform hajj or 'umra the next year unless he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 44} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 45}

Al-Tabari gives the argument of those who held this: the place of the hadys and the camels is the Haram, since God mentioned the camels and hadys in Surat al-Hajj and made their place at the Ancient House, so the hady has no place short of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 45} As to those who cite the Prophet slaughtering his hadys at al-Hudaybiyya when he was barred from the House, they said that is not agreed, because of what is reported from Najiya ibn Jundab al-Aslami: I came to the Prophet when the hady was barred and said, Messenger of God, send the hady with me and I will slaughter it in the Haram; he said, how will you do it? I said, I will take it through valleys they cannot reach; and I went with it until I slaughtered it in the Haram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 45} They say this report shows that the Prophet slaughtered his hadys in the Haram, so there is no argument for those who cite his slaughtering at al-Hudaybiyya outside it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 46} The editor gives the standing of the men in this chain: al-Tabari's teacher al-Fadl ibn Sahl is a reliable memorizer whom both Sheikhs cite; of Muhawwal ibn Ibrahim, al-Dhahabi says in al-Mizan "a vehement Rafidi, truthful in himself", Abu Hatim says "truthful", and Ibn Hibban lists him among the reliable; Mujazza'a ibn Zahir is a reliable Successor cited by both Sheikhs; his father Zahir al-Aslami is a known Companion who gave the pledge under the tree. He adds that Najiya ibn Jundab al-Aslami is the keeper of the Messenger of God's hady, that some narrators confused him with another, and that al-Hafiz showed in al-Tahdhib and al-Isaba that they are two. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 45} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 46} He notes that al-Tahawi narrated it in Sharh Ma'ani al-Athar without "from his father", that Ibn Manda said Muhawwal ibn Ibrahim alone narrated it from Isra'il, and that al-Hafiz in al-Isaba attributes it to al-Nasa'i by another route, which the editor did not find in the lesser Sunan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 46}

Others said the verse means something other than these two views: if you, believers, are prevented from your hajj, kept by illness or fear of an enemy from going on with its ihram, until the standing at 'Arafa has passed you, then upon you is what is easy of the hady for the hajj you missed, together with making up the missed hajj. These people said the one prevented in hajj by illness and ailments has no way of leaving ihram except circumambulating the House and running between al-Safa and al-Marwa if the hajj passed him; whoever can attend the sites is not prevented; there is no prevention in 'umra because its time is always present, and the one on 'umra leaves only by completing his rites; the verse does not include the 'umra-performer and means only the pilgrim. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 46} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 47}

Those who hold this then differed. Some said there is no prevention by an enemy today, just as there is no prevention by illness that permits one whose hajj has passed to leave ihram before circumambulation and running. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 47} Among those reported: Ibn 'Abbas, there is no prevention today; 'A'isha, I do not know that a muhrim leaves ihram by anything short of the House; and from Ibn 'Abbas, there is no prevention except one held by an enemy, who then leaves by an 'umra and owes neither hajj nor 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 47}

Others of them said prevention by an enemy is established today and afterwards, and they explained the verse as: if you are prevented from hajj until it passed you, upon you is what is easy of the hady for its loss. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 47} From Salim, 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar used to reject making a stipulation in hajj and would say, is the sunna of the Messenger of God not enough for you? If one of you is held back from hajj he circumambulates the House, al-Safa and al-Marwa, then leaves everything until he performs hajj the next year, and sends a hady or fasts if he finds no hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 47} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 48} From Nafi', from Ibn 'Umar: the prevented one leaves nothing until he reaches the House and stays in his ihram as he is, except that if a wound strikes him he treats it with what remedies it and pays the ransom; when he reaches the House, if it was an 'umra he completes it, and if a hajj he converts it into an 'umra, and he owes the hajj the next year and the hady, and if he finds none, three days' fasting in the hajj and seven on return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 48} From Nafi', Ibn 'Umar passed Ibn Hizaba at al-Suqya, saw a fracture in him, and was asked for a ruling; he told him to stay as he was and not leave ihram until he came to the House, unless harm struck him, in which case he treats it, and he owes what is easy of the hady; he had assumed ihram for hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 48}

From Salim, from Ibn 'Umar: whoever is prevented after assuming ihram for hajj, held by fear, illness, a mount that knelt and would not rise, or anything of all such things, treats himself for his detention with whatever he needs, except that he does not leave off women and perfume, and pays the ransom God commanded, fasting, charity or a sacrifice; if the hajj passes him while detained, or he misses the standing at 'Arafa before dawn of the night of al-Muzdalifa, the hajj has passed him and his hajj becomes an 'umra: he comes to Makka, circumambulates the House, al-Safa and al-Marwa, slaughters in Makka near the Sacred Mosque any hady he has, shaves or shortens, then leaves the restriction on women, perfume and the rest, and then owes the hajj next year and a hady of what is easy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 48} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 49} The editor explains "khala'a" as said of a she-camel that kneels and refuses to rise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 48} From Malik, from Ibn 'Umar: the prevented one does not leave ihram until he circumambulates the House and runs between al-Safa and al-Marwa, and if he is compelled to wear clothes or take medicine he does so and pays the ransom; the editor refers to the Muwatta. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 49}

Al-Tabari says this is what is reported from Ibn 'Umar on prevention by illness and the like, while for one prevented by an enemy he held something like what was reported above from Malik ibn Anas. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 49} From Nafi', Ibn 'Umar wanted to make hajj when al-Hajjaj besieged Ibn al-Zubayr, and his sons Salim and 'Ubayd Allah told him it would do him no harm to skip hajj that year, as they feared fighting among the people and that he would be kept from the House; he said, if I am kept from the House I will do as we did with the Messenger of God when the disbelievers of Quraysh kept him from the House: he shaved and went back. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 49} On the saying that there is no prevention in 'umra, al-Tabari reports that Yazid ibn 'Abd Allah ibn al-Shikhkhir assumed ihram for 'umra and was prevented; he wrote to Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn 'Umar, who wrote back that he should send a hady and then stay until he left ihram from his 'umra; and he stayed six or seven months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 49}

From Abu al-'Ala' ibn al-Shikhkhir: I set out for 'umra, was thrown from my camel and broke my leg; we sent to Ibn 'Abbas and Ibn 'Umar to ask, and they said that 'umra has no time like the time of hajj, you do not leave ihram until you circumambulate the House; he said I stayed at al-Dathina or near it seven or eight months. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 50} The editor gives al-Dathina as a stage of Banu Sulaym on the road from al-Basra to Makka. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 50} From Malik, from Ayyub al-Sakhtiyani, from an old man of al-Basra: I set out for Makka and my thigh was broken on the way; I sent to Makka to 'Abd Allah ibn 'Abbas and 'Abd Allah ibn 'Umar and the people, and no one allowed me to leave ihram, so I stayed seven months until I left by an 'umra; the editor refers to the Muwatta, where the text has "at that water", which he thinks correct. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 50} From Ibn Shihab, on a man who suffered a fracture while on 'umra: he stays in ihram until he comes to the House, circumambulates it and al-Safa and al-Marwa, and shaves or shortens, and nothing is owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 50}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the best of these views is that of those who said God meant by "if you are prevented" every person prevented while in ihram, whether it was for 'umra or hajj; that the place of his hady is the spot where he was prevented; and that he leaves ihram when the hady reaches its place. He takes "place" to mean the spot of sacrifice or slaughter, once the slaughter is lawful, whether in the Haram or outside it, and he obliges him to make up what he left of his ihram before completing it, if he can find a way. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 51} He bases this on the many reports that the Messenger of God was barred from the House in the year of al-Hudaybiyya while he and his companions were in ihram for 'umra, that they slaughtered the hady at his command and left ihram before reaching the House, and then made up that ihram the following year; no scholar of the biographies or others claimed that he or any companion stayed in ihram awaiting arrival at the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 51} He says the act most worth following is that of the Messenger of God, since no report forbids it and no proof prevents it, and that scholars do not dispute that the verse was revealed then, concerning the ruling on the polytheists' barring him from the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 51} The editor corrects two readings in the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 51}

Al-Tabari reports in the same sense a hadith from al-Hajjaj ibn 'Amr al-Ansari, who heard the Messenger of God say: whoever is broken or lame has left ihram and owes another hajj; 'Ikrima said I told Ibn 'Abbas and Abu Hurayra and they said he spoke truly. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 52} The editor identifies al-Hajjaj al-Sawwaf as a reliable memorizer cited by the six books, says the hadith is in the Musnad and Abu Dawud no. 1862 and, as al-Mundhiri says, was also given by al-Tirmidhi, al-Nasa'i and Ibn Majah, and that al-Hakim judged it sound on al-Bukhari's condition, with al-Dhahabi agreeing. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 52} Al-Tabari says the meaning of the report is the order to make up the hajj he left, like the Prophet and his companions making up in the following year the 'umra from which they had left ihram in the year of al-Hudaybiyya, the year of the 'umra of the makeup. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 52}

Al-Tabari then argues against those who differ, in his own words. He says to those who claim that one whom an enemy prevented owes no makeup if he left ihram from a voluntary ihram, while one prevented by ailments does: what is the cause that required makeup of one and dropped it from the other, when each left an ihram he had to complete but for the obstacle? If they reply that the verse was revealed about one prevented by an enemy, he says: should the ruling of being barred by illness not be that of being barred by an enemy, since they agree in being kept from reaching the House even if their causes differ? {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 53} To those who said there is no prevention in 'umra he says: you know the Prophet was barred from the House while in ihram for 'umra and left ihram, so what is your proof that there is no prevention in it? And what of one who said there is no prevention in hajj, only missing it, a view held by a group of the leaders of the religion; they would say nothing of one case without being bound to the same of the other. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 53}

On the saying "whoever of you is ill or has harm to his head, then a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice", al-Tabari says the meaning is: if you are prevented, then what is easy of the hady, and do not shave your heads until the hady reaches its place, unless one of you is compelled to shave by illness or harm to the head from lice or the like, in which case he shaves for the necessity even though the hady has not reached its place, and owes for shaving his head a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 54} From Ibn Jurayj, I asked 'Ata' what "harm to his head" is, and he said lice and the like, and headache, and whatever is in the head. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 54}

Others said he does not shave, if he wants to ransom with a sacrifice or with feeding, until after the expiation, and if he wants to ransom by fasting he shaves and then fasts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 54} From al-Hasan: when a muhrim has harm to his head he shaves when he sends the sheep or feeds the poor, and if it is fasting he shaves and fasts afterwards. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 54} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 55} The editor notes that 'Ubayd Allah ibn Mu'adh al-'Anbari is reliable and died in 237, and that al-Tabari was born in 224, so hearing from him is not impossible although he had not yet travelled for hadith at that age; he doubts the printed text here, fearing that the name of a teacher between them dropped out, and has put dots before and after this report for a gap and confusion he could not resolve; he fears the text may be al-Tabari's own words and not al-Hasan's, and notes that al-Hasan's saying comes again as no. 3374 with Ibn Abi 'Imran in the chain, as Ibn Kathir also quotes it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 55}

From 'Alqama: when a man assumes ihram for hajj and is prevented, he sends what is easy of the hady, a sheep; if he hurries before the hady reaches its place and shaves his head, touches perfume or treats himself, he owes a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice; Ibrahim said I told this to Sa'id ibn Jubayr, who said Ibn 'Abbas said the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 55} From Mujahid: whoever is prevented by illness or a fracture should send what is easy of the hady, and not shave his head nor leave ihram until the Day of Sacrifice; whoever is ill, or applies kohl or oil, or treats himself, or has harm to his head and shaves, owes a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 55} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 56} From Qatada: this applies when he has sent his hady and then needs to shave his head from illness, or needs perfume or a garment to wear, a shirt or other; then the ransom is owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 56} From Ibn Shihab: whoever is prevented from hajj and in his detention suffers illness or harm to his head and shaves, owes a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 56} From Salim, from Ibn 'Umar, on the one prevented after assuming ihram for hajj: he treats himself in detention with whatever he needs, except that women and perfume are not lawful to him, and he pays the ransom God commanded. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 56} From 'Abd Allah ibn Salama, 'Ali was asked about the verse and said: this is before the hady is slaughtered; if something befalls him, the expiation is owed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 56}

Others said the meaning is: whoever of you is ill or has harm to his head owes a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice before the shaving, if he wishes to shave. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 56} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 57} From Ibn 'Abbas: whoever's illness becomes severe, or whose head troubles him while in ihram, owes fasting, feeding or a sacrifice, and does not shave his head until he has presented his ransom beforehand. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 57} The argument of those who say this is what Ya'qub related from 'Ata': Ka'b ibn 'Ujra passed by the Prophet with much lice and nits in his head, and the Prophet said, do you have a sheep? Ka'b said, I cannot find one; he said, if you wish feed six poor people, and if you wish fast three days, then shave your head. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 57} The editor explains that this report and those up to no. 3358, then 3364, are all routes of the hadith of Ka'b ibn 'Ujra, twenty-eight reports in all with differing wording and contexts; that this chain is weak because it is mursal, since 'Ata' relates an episode from the time of the Prophet which he did not witness without naming who told him, that this 'Ata' is apparently Ibn Abi Rabah though possibly al-Khurasani, and that the meaning is established as sound in the connected reports to come. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 57}

Al-Tabari then says of the illness for which treatment with perfume and shaving the head are permitted: it is every illness whose remedy is in shaving, such as birsam and the like, and wounds on a person's body that need treatment with a medicine containing perfume, and similar sores and ailments that affect bodies. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 58} As for the harm that affects the head specifically and permits shaving, it is such as headache, migraine, an abundance of nits in the head, and anything harmful to the head whose remedy and relief of the harm lies in shaving, all by the generality of God's words "or has harm to his head". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 58} The editor explains birsam, migraine and nits. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 58} Al-Tabari says the reports are many from the Messenger of God that this verse was revealed to him because of Ka'b ibn 'Ujra, when he complained of the abundance of harm to his head from nits, in the year of al-Hudaybiyya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 58}

From Ka'b ibn 'Ujra, through al-Sha'bi: the Messenger of God passed by me at al-Hudaybiyya; I had thick hair with vermin from the root of every hair to its tip, lice and nits. He said, this is harm indeed; I said, yes, Messenger of God, severe. He said, do you have blood (an offering)? I said no. He said, if you wish fast three days, and if you wish give in charity three sa' of dates to six poor, half a sa' for each poor person. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 58} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 59} The same is reported from Dawud, from 'Amir, from Ka'b. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 59} The editor says Dawud is Ibn Abi Hind, that the hadith is in the Musnad and Abu Dawud no. 1858, and explains the words for thick hair, vermin and sa', noting that jurists differ on the measure of the sa'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 59}

From 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'qil, from Ka'b: I set out with the Prophet at the time of al-Hudaybiyya with a thick head of hair that was full of lice, and nits were eating me; the Messenger of God saw me and said, shave; I did. He said, do you have a hady? I said I cannot find one. He said, it is what is easy of the hady; I said I cannot find it. He said, fast three days or feed six poor, half a sa' to each. He said: then this verse came down, "whoever of you is ill or has harm to his head, then a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 59} The editor notes that Asad ibn 'Amr al-Bajali, the judge, was a jurist among Abu Hanifa's companions from whom Ahmad narrated and said "he was truthful", whom Ibn Sa'd called reliable, and about whom opinion is greatly divided between trust and accusation of lying, the fair view being what Ahmad said; that Ash'ath is Ibn Sawwar al-Kindi, reliable, and that 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'qil ibn Muqarrin al-Muzani is a reliable Successor; the hadith is in the Musnad through Hushaym. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 59}

Al-Tabari comments, as his own inference: this report indicates that the sound view is that the ransom becomes due on the one who shaves after the shaving, and the unsoundness of the view of those who said he pays the ransom and then shaves, since Ka'b reports that the Prophet ordered him to shave and then ordered the ransom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 60}

From Ka'b: the Messenger of God ordered me to fast three days or a measure (faraq) of food among six poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 60} From 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'qil: I sat with Ka'b in the mosque and asked him about this verse, and Ka'b said it was revealed about me; I had harm in my head and was carried to the Messenger of God with lice scattering over my face; he said, I did not think the hardship had reached you as I see; do you find a sheep? I said no; then this verse came down. He said: it was revealed about me in particular and it is general for you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 60} The editor says the hadith was narrated by al-Tayalisi, Ahmad, al-Bukhari, Muslim and Ibn Majah (3079) through Shu'ba, that 'Abd al-Rahman al-Asbahani is reliable and cited by the six books, and explains the faraq as a Madinan measure of sixteen ratl, whose estimate is also disputed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 60}

From Ka'b: I performed hajj with the Prophet and my head, beard, moustache and eyebrows became lice-ridden; it was mentioned to the Prophet, who sent for me and said, I did not think this had struck you; then he said, call a barber for me, and he shaved me; then he said, do you have something to sacrifice for you? I said no. He said, fast three days or feed six poor, half a sa' of food to each. Ka'b said: this verse came down about me in particular, then was for people in general. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 61} From Ka'b: the Messenger of God passed by me while I was kindling a fire under a pot with lice scattering over my face, and said, do the vermin of your head trouble you? I said yes. He said, shave it, and fast three days, or feed six poor, or slaughter a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 61} The editor notes that "nasaka" means to slaughter, that the printed text has a misprint "al-Murri" for "al-Muzani", and that the second hadith is in the Musnad, al-Bukhari and Muslim through Ayyub. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 61}

In Ibn 'Ulayya's report from Ayyub it is similar, except that he said the lice were scattering over me, or he said over my eyebrows, and he said "or sacrifice an offering"; Ayyub said I do not know which of them he began with. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 62} From Ka'b: this verse was revealed about me; he said to me, come near; I came near and he said, do your vermin trouble you? He said (I think) yes; and he ordered me to fast, give charity or sacrifice, whatever was easy. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 62} From Ka'b, the Prophet came upon him at the time of al-Hudaybiyya while he was kindling a fire under his pot, the vermin of his head scattering over his face, and said, do your vermin trouble you? He said yes; he said, shave your head, and you owe a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice: slaughter an animal, or fast three days, or feed six poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 62} The editor says Muslim and Ahmad narrated the first two, that the printed text wrongly adds "ibn" to the name of Salih Abu al-Khalil, who is Salih ibn Abi Maryam, and that he did not find the two chains through him from Mujahid elsewhere. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 62}

From 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla: it was mentioned to us that the Prophet came upon Ka'b ibn 'Ujra at the time of al-Hudaybiyya, and then he gave the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 63} From Mujahid, from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla, from Ka'b: the Messenger of God passed by me while I was at al-Hudaybiyya with my head crawling with lice and said, do your vermin trouble you? I said yes; he said, shave; and about me this verse came down, "then a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 63} From Ka'b, in Ibn 'Uyayna's report: he passed by me on the day of al-Hudaybiyya while I was kindling a fire under a pot with lice falling on me and said, do your vermin trouble you? I said yes; he said, shave and sacrifice an offering, or fast three days, or feed a faraq among six poor; Ayyub said "sacrifice an offering", Ibn Abi Najih said "slaughter a sheep", and Sufyan said the faraq is three sa'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 63} The editor notes on the chains that Sayf ibn Sulayman is reliable, that the reports are in the Musnad, al-Bukhari and Muslim, and that al-Tirmidhi called the second "good and sound". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 63} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 64}

From Ka'b, the Messenger of God saw him with lice falling on his face and said, do your vermin trouble you? He said yes, and he ordered him to shave while at al-Hudaybiyya, though it had not become clear to them that they would leave ihram there, as they hoped to enter Makka; then God revealed the ransom, and the Messenger of God ordered him to feed a faraq among six poor, or send a sheep, or fast three days. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 64} From him also: we were with the Prophet at al-Hudaybiyya in ihram, the polytheists having besieged us; I had thick hair and the vermin began falling over my face; the Prophet passed by and said, do the vermin of your head trouble you? I said yes, and this verse came down. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 64} The editor identifies Abu 'Asim al-Nabil, 'Isa ibn Maymun, Abu Bishr Ja'far ibn Iyas, Ya'qub al-Dawraqi and Hushaym, and refers to al-Bukhari, the Musnad and al-Tayalisi. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 64}

From Mujahid, from Ka'b: it was revealed about me and meant me; the Prophet said, at al-Hudaybiyya by the tree while I was in ihram, do your vermin trouble you? I said yes, so God revealed the verse; and the "nusuk" is a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 65} From him also: by Him in whose hand is my soul, this verse was revealed about me and meant me; and he ordered him to shave his head. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 65} The editor notes he found these two chains nowhere else and that they have a break between Mujahid and Ka'b, since 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla stands between them as the other chains show. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 65} From Malik, from 'Abd al-Karim al-Jazari, from Mujahid, from 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla, from Ka'b, that the Prophet ordered him to shave his head and said, fast three days, or feed six poor two mudd each, or sacrifice a sheep; whichever you do will suffice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 65} The editor explains that this is in the Muwatta but with Mujahid omitted in most narrators' versions, that the versions of Ibn Wahb and Ibn al-Qasim that name Mujahid are the correct ones, and he quotes al-Daraqutni and the remark that al-Shafi'i said Malik erred in it, with references to the Musnad and al-Nasa'i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 65} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 66}

From Malik, from Humayd ibn Qays, from Mujahid [from Ibn Abi Layla], from Ka'b, that the Prophet said to him, perhaps your vermin, meaning lice, have troubled you? He said yes, so he said, shave your head and fast three days, or feed six poor, or sacrifice a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 66} The editor explains that "Ibn Abi Layla" dropped out of the chain here, due to some narrators of the Muwatta and not to the copyist or printer, that the Muwatta has it correctly in the versions of Yahya ibn Yahya and Suwayd ibn Sa'id, and he quotes Ibn 'Abd al-Barr that whoever omitted him spoiled the chain, that al-Qa'nabi, al-Shafi'i, Ibn 'Abd al-Hakam, Abu Mus'ab, Ibn Bukayr and al-Zubayri narrated it correctly and that Ibn al-Qasim, Ibn Wahb and Ibn 'Ufayr omitted him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 66}

From 'Ata' ibn 'Abd Allah al-Khurasani, who said an old man at the Suq al-Barm in al-Kufa told me, from Ka'b: the Messenger of God came to me while I was blowing on a fire under a pot for my companions, my head and beard full of lice, and he rebuked me and then said, shave this, or fast three days, or feed six poor; he knew that I had nothing to sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 66} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 67} The editor says 'Ata' is Ibn Abi Muslim, reliable, spoken against by some without proof, that the report is in the Muwatta, and quotes Ibn 'Abd al-Barr that the unnamed old man may be 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Abi Layla or 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'qil; the editor adds that he may be someone else, so the chain is broken until the unnamed man is known. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 67} From Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, from Ka'b: the Messenger of God ordered me, when the lice troubled me, to shave my head and then fast three days or feed six poor, and he knew I had nothing to sacrifice. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 67} In a shorter report from him: he ordered me to shave and ransom with a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 67} The editor identifies 'Abd Allah ibn Nafi' al-Sa'igh, Usama ibn Zayd al-Laythi and Muhammad ibn Ka'b al-Qurazi, and notes that Ibn Majah narrated it as no. 3080. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 67}

From Abu Wa'il Shaqiq ibn Salama: I met Ka'b ibn 'Ujra in this market and asked him about shaving his head; he said, I assumed ihram and the lice troubled me, and it reached the Prophet, who came to me while I was cooking a pot for my companions, scratched my head with his finger so that the lice fell off, and said, shave it and feed six poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 67} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 68} The editor identifies the narrators of this chain, Harun ibn al-Mughira, 'Anbasa and al-Zubayr ibn 'Adi, and refers to al-Nasa'i. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 68} From Ibn Jurayj, from 'Ata': the Prophet was at al-Hudaybiyya in the year they were held there, and the head of a man of his companions called Ka'b ibn 'Ujra was lousy; the Prophet said to him, do these vermin trouble you? He said yes; he said, shave and cut your hair, then fast three days or feed six poor two mudd each. Ibn Jurayj said: did the Prophet specify two mudd each? He said yes, so it reached us that he specified that to Ka'b and did not specify the sacrifice; and he told me that the Prophet told Ka'b this at al-Hudaybiyya before he and his companions were permitted to shave and slaughter, and 'Ata' did not know how long lay between the shaving and the slaughter. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 68}

From Ibn Shihab, from Fadala ibn Muhammad al-Ansari, who reported it from one of his people who is not accused, that Ka'b ibn 'Ujra suffered harm in his head and shaved before the hady reached its place, and the Prophet ordered him to fast three days. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 68} The editor notes that Ibn Musafir is 'Abd al-Rahman ibn Khalid, governor of Egypt in 118, reliable and firm, that Fadala ibn Muhammad is reliable and is listed by al-Bukhari in al-Kabir, and that he found the hadith nowhere else; he lists further chains of the hadith of Ka'b, among them Shu'ba from al-Hakam from Ibn Abi Layla in the Musnad, al-Layth from Nafi' in Abu Dawud 1859, Aban in Abu Dawud 1860 and Ibn Abi Za'ida in Muslim, and refers to al-Bayhaqi's Sunan and Majma' al-Zawa'id. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 68} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 69} From 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As: the Messenger of God said to Ka'b ibn 'Ujra, do the creatures of your head trouble you? He said yes; he said, shave it and ransom, either by fasting three days, or by feeding six poor, or a sheep's sacrifice; and he did so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 69} The editor says this chain is sound, and adds that there is a disagreement, reported by Ibn Abi Uways from Malik's books, over whether Makhrama ibn Bukayr heard from his father; he found the hadith nowhere else except al-Hafiz's indication in al-Fath that al-Tabari and al-Tabarani narrated it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 69}

Al-Tabari says he explained earlier the meaning of "fidya", that it means a recompense and substitute. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 70} Scholars then differed over the amount of fasting and food God made due from a muhrim who shaves his hair in illness or for harm to his head. Some said the fasting due is three days and the food three sa' among six poor, half a sa' each, counting on the reports mentioned above. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 70} Among those who said so is Abu Malik: the fasting is three days, the food is feeding six poor, and the sacrifice is a sheep; 'Ata' and Mujahid the same; Ibrahim and Mujahid: a sheep or above; and Ka'b ibn 'Ujra in 'Abd Allah ibn Ma'qil's report: three sa' of dates among six poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 70} From al-Suddi: if he does one he owes one ransom and if he does two he owes two, and he is free to do whichever of the three he wishes; the fasting is three days, the charity six poor at half a sa' each, and the sacrifice a sheep or above; and it was revealed about Ka'b ibn 'Ujra al-Ansari. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 71} From Mujahid: whoever is ill, or applies kohl or oil, or treats himself, or has harm to his head from lice and shaves, owes a ransom of three days' fasting, or charity of a faraq among six poor, or a sacrifice, which is a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 71} From al-Rabi': if he hurries before the hady reaches its place and shaves, a ransom: the fasting is three days, the charity is feeding six poor, a sa' between every two, and the sacrifice is a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 71} From Sa'id ibn Jubayr: the one owing the ransom fasts a day in place of every two mudd, one mudd for his food and one for his relish. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 71} From 'Ali: the fasting is three days, the charity three sa' for six poor, and the sacrifice a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 71} From Muhammad ibn Ka'b, about the man for whom the verse came down: the Messenger of God gave him a ruling; as for the fasting, three days; for the poor, six; and for the sacrifice, a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 72} From 'Alqama, and from Ibrahim and Mujahid, the same: fasting three days, charity three sa' for six poor, sacrifice a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 72}

Others said what is due on him, if he shaves his head because of harm, or uses perfume for an illness, or does what he was not permitted to do in health while in ihram, is ten days' fasting and, as charity, feeding ten poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 72}

From al-Hasan (in a chain that includes Ibn Abi 'Imran): when a muhrim has harm to his head he shaves and ransoms with any of these three; the fasting is ten days, the charity is on ten poor, two makkuk each, a makkuk of dates and a makkuk of wheat, and the sacrifice is a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 72} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 73} From al-Hasan and 'Ikrima: feeding ten poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 73} The editor refers to his note on report 3324 and says he could not identify Ibn Abi 'Imran after long search and that both chains need verification; he explains the makkuk as an Iraqi measure of a sa' and a half, and corrects the printed "Bishr ibn 'Amr" to Bishr ibn 'Umar al-Zahrani, of whom Abu Hatim said "truthful". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 73}

Al-Tabari reports that those who hold this view reasoned by analogy that every fast due on a muhrim, or charity as recompense for a deficiency entering his ihram or for doing what he was not permitted, in place of a blood sacrifice, follows what God made due on the mutamatti' who finds no hady, ten days of fasting; and that God set feeding one poor person in place of a day's fast for one unable to fast in Ramadan, so they made feeding ten poor due as the ransom for shaving. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 73}

Others said what is due on the shaver is the sacrifice, a sheep if he has one; if not, the sheep is valued in dirhams and the dirhams in food, which he gives as charity, and if not he fasts a day for every half sa'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 73} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 74} From al-A'mash, Ibrahim asked Sa'id ibn Jubayr about the verse and he answered: feeding is adjudged on him; if he has the means he buys a sheep; if not, the sheep is valued in dirhams and food put in its place and given as charity, and otherwise he fasts a day for every half sa'; Ibrahim said so I heard 'Alqama say; the report adds that when Ibrahim rose Sa'id said how elegant he is, and that he used to sit with us. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 74} From Mujahid: a man is judged in the case of game, and if he does not find its compensation it is valued in food, and if there is no food he fasts a day for every two mudd, and so for the ransom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 74}

Others said he is free to choose among the three options and may ransom with whichever he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 74} From Mujahid: everything in the Qur'an with "or ... or" is a matter of choice, like a bag with the white and the black thread, you take whichever comes out; in another report from him, what is "this or this" gives its owner the choice, while what is "whoever does not find, then this" goes in order. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 74} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 75} From 'Ata' and 'Amr ibn Dinar on the verse: he may take whichever of them he wishes, and everything in the Qur'an with "or ... or" allows its owner to choose whichever he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 75} From Ibn 'Abbas: everything in the Qur'an with "or ... or" is a choice, while where it is "whoever ... whoever" it goes in order; the editor explains "whoever" there as "whoever does not find". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 75} From 'Ikrima and 'Ata' the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 75} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 76}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: what is right in our view is what the report established from the Messenger of God and the narration widely transmitted, that he ordered Ka'b ibn 'Ujra to shave his head for the harm in it and to ransom, if he wished, by sacrificing a sheep, fasting three days, or feeding a faraq of food among six poor, half a sa' for each; the one ransoming has the choice among whichever he wishes, because God did not restrict him to one of them. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 76} He then argues against those who differ, in his own words. He asks what they say of one expiating his oath: is he free, if well-off, to expiate with any of the three expiations? If they say no, they leave the view of the whole community, and if they say yes they are asked the difference between him and the one who ransoms for shaving his head; he holds that what he says is agreed by the proof and needs no other witness to its soundness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 76} To those who claim the expiation for shaving comes before the shaving he puts the question whether the mutamatti's expiation comes before or after tamattu', and the expiation for an oath before the oath, and asks them for the difference. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 76} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 77} To those who say ten days and ten poor he says they contradict the text of the report established from the Messenger of God; he asks them about one who hit game and chose feeding or fasting, whether they equate small and large, and how they traced the shaver's obligation back to the mutamatti's when the mutamatti' is not free to choose among fasting, feeding and the hady and destroys nothing, unlike the shaver. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 77}

Al-Tabari completes his argument, in his own words: is there between you and one who made the shaver analogous to the one who hit game, joining their rulings for agreement in meanings and distinguishing him from the mutamatti' for the difference in their cases, a difference from principle or parallel? They will say nothing in one case without being bound to the same in the other; and he holds that the agreement of the authority in faulting the one who says this suffices without further witness to its unsoundness, and that besides it contradicts what came in the reports from the Messenger of God, with analogy attesting to its unsoundness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 78}

Scholars then differed over the place God commanded for sacrificing the shaving sacrifice and feeding the ransom. Some said the sacrifice and feeding are at Makka and are not sufficient in other towns. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 78} From al-Hasan: what is blood or charity is at Makka and anything else wherever he wishes. From Tawus: everything of hajj is at Makka except fasting. From 'Ata': the sacrifice must be at Makka, and the charity and sacrifice of the ransom at Makka and the fasting wherever you wish. From Tawus again: blood or feeding at Makka and fasting wherever he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 78} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 79} From Mujahid: the sacrifice at Makka or Mina, and the food at Makka. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 79}

Others said the shaving sacrifice, feeding and fasting are wherever the one paying the ransom wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 79} Al-Tabari reports from Abu Asma', freedman of 'Abd Allah ibn Ja'far: 'Uthman went on hajj with 'Ali and al-Husayn ibn 'Ali, and 'Uthman set out; I was with Ibn Ja'far, and we came upon a man asleep with his she-camel at his head; we said to him, sleeper, and he woke, and it was al-Husayn ibn 'Ali. Ibn Ja'far carried him to al-Suqya and sent for 'Ali, who came with Asma' bint 'Umays, and we nursed him about twenty nights. 'Ali said to al-Husayn, what do you feel? He pointed to his head, so 'Ali ordered his head shaved, then called for his camel and slaughtered it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 79} The editor identifies the narrators: Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari; Ya'qub ibn Khalid ibn al-Musayyab al-Makhzumi, reliable, against whom neither al-Bukhari nor Ibn Abi Hatim recorded a criticism; and Abu Asma', a reliable Successor; he notes that Ibn Kathir quotes this report. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 79} In another version from Abu Asma', when they were between al-Suqya and al-'Arj al-Husayn fell ill, and 'Ali nursed him nearly forty nights, then it was said to him, here is al-Husayn pointing to his head; he called for a camel and slaughtered it, then shaved his head. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 80} The editor corrects the printed "Mujahid ibn Yunus" to Mujahid ibn Musa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 80} From Yahya ibn Sa'id: al-Husayn ibn 'Ali came with 'Uthman in ihram and fell ill at al-Suqya; 'Ali and Asma' bint 'Umays came and they nursed him twenty nights; al-Husayn pointed to his head, so he shaved it and slaughtered a camel for him; Ibn Jurayj said I asked, did he take him back? He said I do not know. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 80}

Al-Tabari says this report can mean that 'Ali slaughtered the camel before shaving his head, if it was as Mujahid related from Yazid, as a leaving of ihram for al-Husayn on account of being prevented from hajj by the illness; and if it was as Ya'qub related from Hushaym, that 'Ali slaughtered it after the shaving, it would be as a ransom for shaving, and 'Ali would have held that the sacrifice of the ransom suffices if slaughtered short of Makka and the Haram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 80} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 81} From Mujahid: the ransom wherever you wish. From Ibrahim, in the ransom, in charity, fasting and blood: wherever he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 81}

Others said what is a blood sacrifice is at Makka, and what is feeding and fasting is wherever the one paying the ransom wishes. From 'Ata': blood at Makka and food and fasting wherever he wishes. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 81} Al-Tabari gives the reason of those who said blood and feeding are at Makka: it is analogy with the hady of compensation for game, since God conditioned its hady on reaching the Ka'ba; so every hady due as compensation or ransom in ihram follows the way of game compensation in having to reach the Ka'ba, and if that is the ruling of the hady, the ruling of charity is like it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 81}

Al-Tabari completes the reasoning of those who said Makka: if that is the ruling of the hady, the ruling of charity is like it, since it is due to those to whom the hady is due, because feeding is a ransom and compensation like blood and so their ruling is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 81} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 82} The reasoning of those who held that the one paying the ransom may sacrifice, give charity and fast wherever he wishes is that God did not stipulate a hady for the one who shaves his head for harm, but made due on him a sacrifice, feeding or fasting, and wherever he sacrifices, feeds or fasts he is one who sacrifices, feeds and fasts; had God wanted him bound to reach the Ka'ba he would have stipulated it as he did for compensation for game, and leaving out the stipulation is clear evidence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 82} The reasoning of those who said the sacrifice is at Makka while fasting and feeding are wherever he wishes is that the sacrifice is blood like the blood of the hady, so its way is that of the hady of the one who killed game; as for feeding, God did not stipulate that it be sent to the poor of any place, so no one may claim it is for the people of one place and not another. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 82}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the right view is that God made due on the muhrim who shaves his head for harm a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice and did not stipulate a place, but left it unspecified and unrestricted, so wherever he sacrifices, feeds or fasts it suffices for the one paying the ransom. He reasons that when God forbade the mothers of our women without restricting them to the mothers of women consummated with, their ruling is not to be returned to stepdaughters, who are restricted to those whose mother was consummated with; likewise every unspecified provision in the Qur'an may not have its ruling returned by analogy to the specified one, but each is judged by what the apparent text bears, unless a report from the Messenger comes shifting the apparent meaning to the inner one, in which case one must submit to the Messenger's ruling since he is the one who explains what God intends. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 82} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 83} They agreed that fasting suffices for the one who shaves his head for harm wherever in the lands he fasts. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 83}

They differed over what is done with the sacrifice of the ransom, and whether the one paying may eat from it. Some said he may not eat from it but must give all of it in charity. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 83} From 'Ata': three are not eaten from, compensation for game, compensation by sacrifice and a vow for the poor; in another report, do not eat from a ransom, compensation or vow, and eat from the tamattu' offering, the hady and the voluntary. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 83} From Mujahid: compensation for game, the ransom and the vow are not eaten from by their owner, while he eats from the voluntary and the tamattu' offerings. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 83} From 'Ata' also: he does not eat from his camel offered for having relations with his wife in ihram, and the expiations are the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 83} From 'Ata', Tawus and Mujahid: no eating from the ransom or compensation for game. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 84}

Some said he may eat from it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 84} From Ibn 'Umar: no eating from compensation for game and the vow, and eating from everything else. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 84} From Ibn Abi Layla that he eats from the ransom, compensation for game and the vow, and the editor explains the wording by al-Hasan's saying. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 84} From Hammad: the sheep is between six poor; he may eat from it if he wishes and give charity to six poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 84} From al-Hasan: eat of all of them, meaning compensation for game, the vow and the ransom, and he saw no harm in eating from compensation for game and the vow for the poor. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 84}

Al-Tabari gives the reasoning of those who barred the one paying from eating: God made due on the shaver a ransom of fasting, charity or sacrifice, and what He made due of feeding and sacrifice is either for himself, for others, or for himself and others; if for others he may not eat from it, since what is owed to another is discharged only by giving it to him; if for himself alone, it is not intelligible in language that something be owed by a person to himself; if for himself and others, his own share cannot be on him, so what is on him is part of the sacrifice and not all of it, and, they said, God's imposing the whole sacrifice shows this view to be wrong. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 85} The reasoning of those who said he may eat: God made a sacrifice due, and the nusuk in meaning is like the udhiya, slaughtering what suffices as an udhiya of the eight pairs of livestock, and God did not command giving it to the poor, so when he has slaughtered he has done what God commanded and may eat from it and give what he likes in charity, as with his own udhiya. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 85}

Al-Tabari then says: what we say is that God made a sacrifice due on the one paying the ransom, if he chooses to expiate by sacrifice; and what is due is either slaughtering it alone, or slaughtering it and giving it as charity. If slaughtering alone, then once he has slaughtered he has discharged what is on him even if he eats all of it and feeds no poor person from it, and he knows of no scholar who said that; and if slaughtering and charity, he may not eat what he must give as charity, as with zakat due on his property. Their consensus that what God imposed here He imposed for others is a clear indication of the ruling on what they differed over. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 85} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 86} The meaning of "nusuk" in Arab usage is slaughter for God; and from Ibn 'Abbas, the nusuk is to slaughter a sheep. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 86}

On God's words "when you are safe": the scholars of interpretation differed on their meaning. Some said it means when you have recovered from the illness that kept you from your hajj or 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 86} From 'Alqama: "when you are safe" means when you have recovered. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 86}

From 'Urwa on "when you are safe": when you are safe after being prevented, safe from your fracture and your pain, you must come to the House, and it becomes a tamattu' for you, and you do not leave ihram until you come to the House. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 87} Others said the meaning is when you are safe from your fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 87} From Qatada: "when you are safe", so that you know that the people were fearful then; from al-Rabi': when he is safe from his fear and has recovered from his illness. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 87} The editor notes the printed text has the word "pain" intruded into "from the pain of your fear", which he dropped because it spoils the sentence. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 87}

Al-Tabari gives his own choice: this view is closer to the verse's meaning, because safety is the opposite of fear, not of illness, unless it is an illness feared to be fatal, which is a remote sense; and we said the meaning is fear of an enemy because these verses came down on the Messenger of God in the days of al-Hudaybiyya when his companions feared the enemy, and God taught them by them what is upon them if fear of their enemy prevents them from hajj and what is upon them if they are safe and their fear is gone. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 87}

On God's words "whoever enjoys 'umra up to hajj, then what is easy of the hady", al-Tabari says the meaning is: if you are prevented, believers, then what is easy of the hady; and when you are safe, your fear of the enemy or the danger of your illness gone, and you enjoy your 'umra up to your hajj, then upon you is what is easy of the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 88} The interpreters then differed over the description of the tamattu' God meant here. Some said it is that fear of an enemy, or an illness or hindrance, prevents him while in ihram for hajj until the hajj is missed; he comes to Makka and leaves ihram by performing an 'umra, then enjoys being out of ihram until the coming year, then performs hajj and sends a hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 88} From Ibn al-Zubayr, in a sermon: people, by God tamattu' with 'umra to hajj is not what you do; tamattu' is that a man assumes ihram for hajj and an enemy, illness, fracture or some matter holds him until the days of hajj pass, so he comes, makes it an 'umra, enjoys being out of ihram until the next year, then performs hajj and sends a hady; this is tamattu' with 'umra to hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 88} From 'Ata': Ibn al-Zubayr used to say the mut'a is for the one prevented, and Ibn 'Abbas said it is for the one prevented and the one let go. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 88} In another report from 'Ata': Ibn al-Zubayr used to say the mut'a is only for the prevented, not for one let go. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 89}

Others said the meaning is: if you are prevented in your hajj, then what is easy of the hady, and when you are safe, having left ihram without making up an 'umra by which to leave your hajj ihram, but left with the hady when prevented and delayed the 'umra to the coming year, and then performed 'umra in the months of hajj, left ihram and enjoyed your release up to your hajj, then upon you is what is easy of the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 89} From 'Alqama: when a man assumes ihram for hajj and is prevented, he sends what is easy of the hady, a sheep ... "when you are safe": if he recovers and goes on from that route until he comes to the House, he leaves his hajj by an 'umra and owes the hajj next year; if he returns and does not reach the House on that journey, he owes a hajj, an 'umra and blood for delaying the 'umra; if he returns as one enjoying tamattu' in the months of hajj, he owes what is easy of the hady, a sheep, and whoever cannot find one fasts three days in the hajj and seven on return; Ibrahim said I mentioned it to Sa'id ibn Jubayr and he said Ibn 'Abbas said the same in all of it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 89} The editor corrects the printed "Ibrahim ibn 'Alqama" to "Ibrahim, from 'Alqama". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 89} From Qatada: this is a man struck by fear, illness or a detainer who holds him until he sends his hady; when it reaches its place he is out of ihram; if he is safe, or has recovered, or reaches the House, it is an 'umra for him and he leaves ihram and owes the hajj next year; and if he does not reach the House until he returns to his family, he owes an 'umra, a hajj and a hady; Qatada said this is the tamattu' about which people have no doubt that its origin was so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 89} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 90} The editor explains "yata'ajam" as that people neither doubt nor dispute it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 90} From Ibrahim: this is the prevented one when he is safe, he owes the mut'a in hajj and the hady of tamattu', and fasting if he finds none; if he hurries the 'umra before the months of hajj he owes a hady for it. From 'Ali: if he delays the 'umra until he joins it with the hajj, he owes the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 90}

Others said it means the prevented and the not prevented; from 'Ata', Ibn 'Abbas used to say the mut'a is for one prevented and one let go, and this verse covered the prevented and the one let go. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 90} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 91} Others said the meaning is: whoever annuls his hajj by an 'umra, making it an 'umra, and enjoys his 'umra up to his hajj, owes what is easy of the hady; from al-Suddi: the mut'a is a man who assumes ihram for a hajj and then demolishes it with an 'umra; the Messenger of God set out with the Muslims as a pilgrim and when they came to Makka he said to them, whoever of you would like to leave ihram, let him; they said, and you, Messenger of God? He said, I have my hady with me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 91}

Others said it is the man who comes for 'umra from some region in the months of hajj and, having completed his 'umra, stays out of ihram at Makka until he starts the hajj from there, performing the hajj that same year, enjoying his release up to his ihram for hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 91} From Mujahid: "whoever enjoys 'umra up to hajj", from the day of the breaking of the fast to the day of 'Arafa, owes what is easy of the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 91}

From Nafi': Ibn 'Umar once came in Shawwal and we stayed until we performed hajj, and he said, you have enjoyed an 'umra up to your hajj; whoever of you finds the means to send a hady, let him, and whoever does not, let him fast three days and seven when he returns to his family. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 91} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 92} From him also, that he set out with Ibn 'Umar for 'umra in Shawwal and the hajj overtook them in Makka, and Ibn 'Umar said: whoever performed 'umra with us in Shawwal and then hajj is a mutamatti', owing what is easy of the hady, and whoever does not find one fasts three days in the hajj and seven on return. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 92} From 'Ata', on a man who performed 'umra outside the months of hajj, drove a voluntary hady and came to Makka in the months of hajj: if he does not intend hajj he slaughters his hady and returns if he wishes; if he slaughtered and left ihram and then decided to stay for hajj, he slaughters another hady for his tamattu', and fasts if he does not find one; Ibn Abi Layla the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 92} From Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab: whoever performed 'umra in Shawwal or Dhu al-Qa'da and then stayed at Makka until he performed hajj is a mutamatti' who owes what the mutamatti' owes; 'Ata' the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 92} From Ibn 'Abbas: whoever assumes ihram for 'umra in the months of hajj owes what is easy of the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 92} From 'Ata': the mut'a is for all God's creation, man and woman, free and slave; it is for every person who performed 'umra in the months of hajj and then stayed without leaving until he performed hajj, whether he drove a garlanded hady or not; it was called mut'a because he performed 'umra in the months of hajj and enjoyed an 'umra up to the hajj, and not because he leaves ihram to enjoy women. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 92} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 93}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the best of these views for the verse is that of those who said it means: if you, believers, are prevented in your hajj, then what is easy of the hady; and when you are safe, whoever enjoys, of those who left their hajj ihram because of the prevention, by an 'umra performed the next year in the months of hajj because of missing the hajj, up to making up the hajj that passed him when he was prevented, and then entered his 'umra and enjoyed his release from the 'umra until he performs hajj, owes what is easy of the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 93} He allows that one who begins an 'umra in the months of hajj and completes it, then leaves ihram and stays out of ihram until hajj in the same year, can also be a mutamatti', but holds that the closer meaning of God's words "whoever enjoys 'umra up to hajj" is what he described, because God told what is upon the one prevented from hajj and 'umra in his prevention, and among what He told is that on him, when safe from the prevention and enjoying 'umra up to hajj, is what is easy of the hady, and if he does not find it, fasting three days; so it is known that what is meant is what is incumbent on him when safe from his prevention, not the mutamatti' whose 'umra and hajj were not preceded by prevention by illness or fear. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 93} The editor notes that a necessary "wa" is added to the printed text. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 93}

On "whoever does not find, then fasting three days in the hajj", al-Tabari says the meaning is: what is easy of the hady is his hady as recompense for enjoying his release from the ihram from which he left when he returned to make up his hajj in which he was prevented, and his 'umra made due by missing the hajj; if he does not find a hady, he owes fasting three days in the hajj, in his hajj, and seven when he returns to his family. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 94} The interpreters then differed over the three days God made due for fasting in the hajj, as to which days of hajj they are. Some said they are three days of his hajj, whichever he wishes, provided the last does not go beyond the day of 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 94}

From 'Ali: "fasting three days in the hajj" is the day before al-Tarwiya, the day of al-Tarwiya and the day of 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 94} From Ibn 'Abbas: the fasting for the mutamatti' is between his ihram and the day of 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 94} The editor identifies Ibrahim ibn Isma'il ibn Abi Habiba al-Ansari as reliable, spoken against by al-Bukhari and others and trusted by Ahmad and others, and says he held in his commentary on the Musnad that his hadith is at least good. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 94} From Ibn 'Umar: a day before al-Tarwiya, the day of al-Tarwiya and the day of 'Arafa, and if he misses them he fasts them in the days of Mina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 95} From 'Urwa, al-Hakam, Sa'id ibn Jubayr and 'Amir the same: a day before al-Tarwiya, the day of al-Tarwiya and the day of 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 95} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 96} From al-Hasan, Ibrahim and Mujahid: the last of them is the day of 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 95} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 96} From 'Ata': the mutamatti' fasts the three days for his tamattu' in the ten days up to the day of 'Arafa; he said I heard Mujahid and Tawus say that if he fasts them in the months of hajj it suffices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 95} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 96} From Mujahid: he fasts in the ten up to the day of 'Arafa and whenever he fasts it suffices; if he fasts in Shawwal or Dhu al-Qa'da it suffices. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 96} From 'Ata': whoever can fast them between the first day of Dhu al-Hijja and the day of 'Arafa should do so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 96} From Qatada: it used to be said, 'Arafa and the two days before it from the ten. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 96}

From al-Suddi, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, 'Ata', Tawus and Abu Ja'far: the last of the three days is the day of 'Arafa; from al-Rabi': 'Arafa and the days before it of the ten; from Mujahid and Ibrahim: in the ten, the last of them 'Arafa. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 97} From Ibn 'Abbas: this is on the mutamatti' with 'umra, if he finds no hady he owes fasting three days in the hajj before the day of 'Arafa; if the day of 'Arafa is the third, his fast is complete, and seven when he returns to his family. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 97}

Others said the last of them is the end of the days of Mina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 98} From 'Ali: whoever misses fasting the three days in the hajj fasts them in the days of al-Tashriq; from 'A'isha: the mutamatti' who misses the fast fasts the days of Mina; from Ibn 'Umar: whoever misses fasting the three days in the hajj should fast the days of al-Tashriq, for they are of the hajj; and from him, whoever performed 'umra in the months of hajj without a hady and did not fast the three days before the days of al-Tashriq should fast the days of Mina. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 98} From 'A'isha and Ibn 'Umar, both said that no concession was given to fast the days of al-Tashriq except for one who finds no hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 98} From 'Urwa on the verse: they are the days of al-Tashriq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 98} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 99} From Ibn 'Umar in another report: he fasts a day before al-Tarwiya, the day of al-Tarwiya and the day of 'Arafa; and 'Ubayd ibn 'Umayr said he fasts the days of al-Tashriq. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 99}

Al-Tabari gives the reasoning of those who said the last of the three is the day of 'Arafa: God made their fasting due in the hajj, and when the day of 'Arafa has ended the hajj has ended, since the Day of Sacrifice is a day of release from ihram, and all agree that fasting on the Day of Sacrifice is not permitted; if that is because it is not among the days of hajj, the days of al-Tashriq after it are more fitting not to be among them, and if it is because it is a feast day, the days of al-Tashriq are the same in meaning, and the Prophet forbade fasting them as he forbade fasting the Day of Sacrifice; and if their fasting is lost by the passing of 'Arafa, there is no way to fast them in the hajj, so nothing suffices for him but the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 99} The reasoning of those who said the last is the end of the days of Mina: God made due on the mutamatti' what is easy of the hady and then fasting if he does not find it; slaughtering the hady of tamattu' is due on the Day of Sacrifice even if he had found one earlier, so the concession to fast applies on the day he must slaughter the hady and cannot find one; so the fast is due beginning with the day after the Day of Sacrifice to the end of three days of al-Tashriq; and there is no sense in the saying that the days of Mina are not days of hajj, since rites are performed in them, stoning and staying for the work of the hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 99} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 100} They cite the report of Ibn 'Umar that the Messenger of God allowed the mutamatti' who finds no hady and has not fasted until the ten days passed him to fast the days of al-Tashriq in their place. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 100} The editor notes that Yahya ibn Salam al-Basri, who settled in Egypt, was called "truthful" by Abu Hatim and reliable by Abu al-'Arab, while al-Daraqutni weakened him as in al-Lisan, that Ibn Abi Layla is 'Abd Allah ibn 'Isa, reliable and firm, that al-Tahawi narrated it, that its root is established in al-Bukhari as a statement of a Companion, and that he holds the words of 'A'isha and Ibn 'Umar "no concession was given" to be in effect a statement from the Prophet, since a Companion means by it the one with the right to permit and forbid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 100} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 101} From al-Zuhri: the Messenger of God sent 'Abd Allah ibn Hudhafa, who called out during the days of al-Tashriq that these are days of eating, drinking and remembrance of God, except for one who owes a fast in place of a hady; the editor says this is mursal and that Sufyan ibn Husayn is reliable although his narration from al-Zuhri has been spoken against. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 101}

The scholars differed over the first time at which the mutamatti' must begin to fast the three days, and the time at which it is permitted for him even if not obligatory. Some said he may fast them from the beginning of the months of hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 101} From Mujahid and Tawus: if he fasts them in the months of hajj it suffices; from Mujahid: if he fasts a day in Shawwal, a day in Dhu al-Qa'da and a day in Dhu al-Hijja it suffices for the fast of tamattu'; he may, if he wishes, fast the first day of Shawwal; he may fast them in the ten, or in Dhu al-Qa'da, or in Shawwal. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 101} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 102} The editor says he could not identify Ahmad ibn al-Mughira, al-Tabari's teacher. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 102} Others said he fasts them in the ten of Dhu al-Hijja and not otherwise. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 102} From 'Ata': he fasts the three days for the tamattu' in the ten up to the day of 'Arafa, and there is no harm that the mutamatti' fast in the ten while out of ihram; from Abu Ja'far: it is fasted only in the ten. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 102}

From 'Ata': he fasts the three in the nine of Dhu al-Hijja, whichever he wishes; whoever fasted before that in Shawwal or Dhu al-Qa'da is as one who did not fast. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 103} Others said he may fast them before assuming ihram for hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 103} From 'Ikrima: if he fears he will not manage the fast at Makka he fasts on the road a day or two; from 'Ata': there is no harm in fasting the three days of tamattu' while out of ihram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 103} Others said he may not fast them except after assuming ihram for hajj. From Ibn 'Umar: he fasts them only while in ihram; from Ibn 'Abbas: the fast of the mutamatti' is between his ihram and the day of 'Arafa; from Ibn 'Umar: the fast of three days does not suffice while he is a mutamatti' unless he is in ihram, while Mujahid said it suffices if he fasted in Dhu al-Qa'da. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 103} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 104}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the right view in his opinion is that the mutamatti' may fast the three days God made due on him for his tamattu', if he does not find what is easy of the hady, from the start of his ihram for hajj, after completing his 'umra and enjoying his release until his hajj, to the end of the last work of his hajj, which is after the days of Mina except the Day of Sacrifice, on which he may not fast, whether he began fasting them before it or left it and delayed until the day of 'Arafa passed. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 104} If he fasts them before his ihram for hajj, it does not suffice, because God made fasting due on one who finds no hady among those who enjoyed their 'umra up to their hajj; the one on 'umra before leaving ihram from it and before entering the hajj does not deserve the name mutamatti', and is called only 'umra-performer until after his release he enters the hajj before leaving Makka; when he enters the hajj in ihram after completing his 'umra in the months of hajj and staying out of ihram at Makka until he performs hajj that year, he is called mutamatti', and once he deserves the name the hady is due, and then the fast is for him after lacking the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 104} If he fasted before entering the hajj, even intending hajj, he is a man who fasted intending a makeup for what may or may not become due on him, like a poor man who fasted three days intending expiation for an oath he wants to swear and break, which all agree does not suffice as expiation if he swears after the fast and breaks it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 104} To one who thinks it suffices by analogy with the oath-taker who expiates after swearing but before breaking, he answers that God made for an oath a release distinct from expiation, and the mutamatti' who fasts before his tamattu' fasts as expiation for what he thinks will be due and has not yet become due, like one who expiates for killing game he intends to kill in ihram before killing, or for perfume before using it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 105} He then argues with those who refuse, asking what of one who expiates, on the day of 'Arafa, for leaving the stoning of the pillars while intending to leave it, then leaves the stoning; what of one resolved to have intercourse in Ramadan, resident and healthy, who expiates before the month enters; and what of one who wants to pronounce zihar on his wife; if he keeps his saying consistent he leaves the view of the whole community, and if he refuses any he is asked for the difference. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 105} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 106} The editor explains "qada qawlahu" as keeping to his course without contradicting his own argument, corrects the printed "fada", and rejects a commentator's reading of it as "became confused". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 105}

On "and seven when you return", al-Tabari says: whoever does not find what is easy of the hady owes fasting three days in his hajj and fasting seven days when he returns to his family and town. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 106} To the question whether the seven are not due until after he returns, he answers that God made the ten days due on lacking the hady, but out of mercy to His servants granted a concession, as to the traveller and the sick in Ramadan; if the mutamatti' took it upon himself and fasted the seven on his journey before returning, or at Makka, he would be discharging what is on him, like one who fasts Ramadan while travelling or ill, choosing hardship over ease; and he says the scholars of the community said so. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 106} From Mujahid: it is a concession, he may fast them on the road or after he returns to his family. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 106} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 107} From 'Ata': fasting the seven when he has returned to his family is dearer to me. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 107} From Ibrahim: if you wish on the road, and if you wish after you reach your family. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 107} As proof that "when you return" means to your families and towns and not from Mina to Makka, he cites the consensus of all scholars, and gives 'Ata', Qatada, al-Rabi' and Sa'id ibn Jubayr: to your family and your towns. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 107} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 108}

On "that is ten complete", the interpreters differed over "complete". Some said the fast of the three days in the hajj and the seven after he returns to his family make ten complete in place of the hady; from al-Hasan: complete in place of the hady. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 108} Others said: it has been completed for you the reward of one who stayed in ihram and did not leave it or enjoy tamattu' as you did, with 'umra up to hajj. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 108} Others said the meaning is a command though phrased as a report: those are ten days, so complete their fast and do not fall short of it, for their fast is an obligation on you. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 108}

Others said "complete" is for emphasis, as one says I heard it with my ears and saw it with my eyes, and as in the verse of al-Nahl about the roof that fell on them from above, though falling is only from above. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 109} Others said it says "that is ten complete" though it mentioned seven and three, because it reports only that they suffice and does not report their count, "complete" meaning sufficient. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 109} Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the best of these views in his opinion is that of those who said the meaning is: those are ten complete, whose completion We have made obligatory upon you; for He said, whoever does not find the hady owes fasting three days in the hajj and seven when he returns, and then said those are ten days whose fast you must complete for your tamattu' with 'umra up to hajj; he gave it the form of a report but its meaning is a command. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 109}

On God's words "that is for one whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque", al-Tabari says "that" means tamattu' with 'umra up to hajj for one whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 109} From al-Rabi': it means the mut'a is for the people of the regions and is not suitable for the people of Makka. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 109} From al-Suddi: this is for the people of the towns, so that it is easier for them than for one to perform hajj one time and 'umra another, and to combine his hajj and 'umra in one year. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 110}

The interpreters then differed over whom "that is for one whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque" refers to, after all agreed that the people of the Haram are meant and have no tamattu'. Some said the people of the Haram in particular and not others are meant. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 110} From Ibn 'Abbas and Mujahid: the people of the Haram. From Sufyan: it reached us from Ibn 'Abbas that they are the people of the Haram, and the community holds that. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 110} From Qatada: it was mentioned to us that Ibn 'Abbas used to say: people of Makka, there is no mut'a for you; it was made lawful for the people of the regions and forbidden to you; one of you need only cross a valley, or put a valley between himself and the Haram, and then assume ihram for 'umra. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 110} From Yahya ibn Sa'id al-Ansari: the people of Makka used to go on expeditions and trade, and would come in the months of hajj and then perform hajj without owing a hady or fast; this was permitted to them by God's words "that is for one whose family is not present at the Sacred Mosque". {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 110} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 111} From Tawus: the mut'a is for people except the people of Makka, those whose family is of the Haram; and it reached him from Ibn 'Abbas the same as Tawus's saying; the editor notes that the wording in Ibn Kathir and al-Durr al-Manthur differs and that al-Tabari's text is the correct one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 111}

Others said it means the people of the Haram and whoever's home is within the miqats up to Makka. From Makhul: whoever is within the miqats; from 'Ata': whoever's family is within the miqats is like the people of Makka and does not enjoy tamattu'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 111} Some said it means the people of the Haram and those whose homes are near it. From 'Ata': 'Arafa, Marr, 'Uranah, Dajnan, al-Raji' and Nakhlatan. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 112} From al-Zuhri: a day or two; and from him, whoever's family is a day away or so may enjoy tamattu'. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 112} 'Ata' counted the people of 'Arafa as people of Makka. From Ibn Zayd: the people of Makka, Fajj and Dhu Tuwa, and what adjoins them is of Makka. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 112}

Al-Tabari then gives his own choice: the soundest of the views is that of those who said those present at the Sacred Mosque are those around it whose distance from it is not one for which prayers are shortened. He reasons that in Arab usage the one "present" at a thing is the one who is there himself, and one deserves to be called absent only if he is a traveller departed from his homeland; a traveller is a traveller only by departing to a distance for which prayer is shortened; so likewise one who is not at a distance from the Sacred Mosque for which prayer is shortened does not deserve to be called one who is not present. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 112} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 113} Tamattu' is not for those present at the Sacred Mosque because tamattu' is enjoying release from ihram for 'umra up to hajj, benefiting by not returning to home and homeland, by staying in the Haram until he begins the hajj ihram; when the 'umra-performer completes his 'umra in the months of hajj and then goes home, or leaves the Haram for a distance at which prayer is shortened, then performs hajj that year, he is no longer a mutamatti', because he did not take the benefit given to the mutamatti'; and the Makkan, being among those present at the Sacred Mosque, does not benefit in this way, since once he completes his 'umra he stays in his home in the Haram. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 113}

On God's words "and fear God and know that God is severe in punishment", al-Tabari says: "fear God" means by obeying Him in what He has made binding of His obligations and limits, and beware of transgressing in that and going beyond what He has explained to you of your rites, so as to treat as lawful what He forbade in them; "know" means be certain that His punishment is severe for whomever He punishes for violating His prohibitions and committing His disobediences. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v04 p. 114}
