## Verse 11 in al-Tabari's commentary (Shakir ed., volume 6, pp. 223-225)

For [[passage/quran-3-11|3:11]] al-Tabari says that the sense is that the wealth and children of those who disbelieved will avail them nothing against God when His punishment falls on them, like the custom and habit of the people of Pharaoh, and of those before them among the nations who denied God's signs, so that He seized them for their sins and destroyed them when they denied, and neither their wealth nor their children availed them when His might came upon them, like those who were hastened to punishment for denying their Lord before the house of Pharaoh: the people of Noah, the people of Hud, the people of Lot and the like. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 223} The editor says the manuscript has an undotted word in the phrase 'like their custom and habit', that the print's wording is right and the manuscript's a slip of the scribe, and that this wording is the text of Abu Ubayda in Majaz al-Qur'an (1: 87); he adds that in another place the print has a verb at odds with the context and the manuscript another through the scribe's oversight, and that he adopted the right one. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 223}

Al-Tabari then records the interpreters' difference over the word for 'custom' (da'b), in three views.

The first is that it means custom (sunna). In 6659, al-Rabi (through al-Muthanna, Ishaq ibn al-Hajjaj, Abd Allah ibn Abi Jafar and his father): like their custom. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 223}

The second is that it means deed. In 6660, al-Dahhak (through Muhammad ibn Bashshar, Mu'ammal and Sufyan; and al-Muthanna, Abu Nu'aym and Sufyan, both from Juwaybir): like the deed of Pharaoh's people. In 6661, al-Dahhak (through Yahya ibn Abi Talib, Yazid and Juwaybir): the same. In 6662, Ibn Zayd (through Yunus and Ibn Wahb): like their doing, like their denial when they denied the messengers; he cites a verse of Surat Ghafir ([[passage/quran-40-31|40:31]]) in the sense that the like of what befell those people of God's punishment may befall them, and says the da'b is the deed. In 6663, Ikrima and Mujahid (through al-Qasim, al-Husayn, Abu Tumayla Yahya ibn Wadih, Abu Hamza and Jabir): like the doing of Pharaoh's people, like their affair. In 6664, Ibn Abbas (reported to al-Tabari through al-Munjab, Bishr ibn Ammara, Abu Rawq and al-Dahhak): like the work of Pharaoh's people. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 224}

The third is that it means denial. In 6665, al-Suddi (through Musa ibn Harun, Amr ibn Hammad and Asbat): the verse mentions the disbelievers and their acts of denial as being like the denial of those before them in rejection and denial. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 224}

Al-Tabari then explains, in his own voice, that the root of da'b is the phrase 'I laboured at the matter', said when one persists in work and toil in it, and that the Arabs then carried its sense to the matter, the affair and the habit. He cites a line of Imru' al-Qays in his Mu'allaqa, in which the poet likens his state to that of women he names who came before his beloved, the word standing for their affair, their matter and their doing. He says they say 'this is my da'b and yours always', meaning my doing and yours, my affair and yours, my matter and yours, that the verb has the forms du'uban and da'ban, and that da'aban, with the glottal letter vowelled, is reported from the Arabs by hearsay, as 'sha'r' and 'nahr' are vowelled on the second letter because it is one of the six letters, so da'b was brought into line with them since its second letter is one of the six; he cites a line of Kuthayyir for this. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 225} The editor says Imru' al-Qays's line is in his Diwan (p. 125) and the second line comes later in the commentary, that the six letters are the throat letters, that the second poet is Kuthayyir Azza, whose line is in his Diwan (p. 112), in al-Hayawan (1: 266), al-Bayan and Lisan al-Arab, and that the Lisan's version differs from the Diwan's and has no bearing on the point. He says Kuthayyir spoke it on hearing of the death of Abd al-Aziz ibn Marwan in Egypt and mourned him, quotes lines from the poem, and explains that the man praised wore only tanned leather sandals so that the smell of hide was gone, that he is shown as a man of ease and luxury, and that his sandal took on his fragrance, so that if it were set down in a gathering of people they would turn to catch its scent. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 225}

Al-Tabari explains the verse's close by saying that God is severe in His punishment of whoever disbelieved in Him and denied His messengers after the proof was established against him. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 225}
