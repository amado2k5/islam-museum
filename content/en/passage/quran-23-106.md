## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 106 to 107 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "Were not My verses recited to you and you used to deny them": they are told: "Were not My verses recited to you?" meaning the verses of the Qur'an were recited to you in this world, "so you used to deny them." He omitted the mention of "it is said" to indicate the meaning of the speech on it. "They said, 'Our Lord, our misery has overwhelmed us.'" Reciters differed in their reading; the majority of the reciters of Medina and Basra, and some people of Kufa, read it as: "Our misery overcame us" with a kasra on the shin and without an alif. The general recitation of the people of Kufa read it as "shaqawatna" with a fatha on the shin and alif. The correct view is that they are two well-known readings, and scholars among the reciters read each with the same meaning, so whichever one the reader reads correctly. Interpretation of the words: They said: Our Lord, what has previously prevailed to us in Your knowledge and has been written for us in the Mother of the Book. In the passage, he quotes from Mujahid and Ibn Jurayj.
