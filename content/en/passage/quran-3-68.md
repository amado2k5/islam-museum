## Summary
Verse 68 of [[passage/quran-3|Surat Al 'Imran]], a Medinan surah. {cite:tanzil-quran}

## Place in the museum
Its place: verse 68 of 200 verses in the surah. The text is kept in the frozen Tanzil mushaf and is not retyped here.

**The sense of verse 68.** Al-Tabari says those with the strongest claim on Abraham, on his support and on allegiance to him, are those who took up his way and path, affirming God's oneness and devoting religion purely to Him, who took up his practice and observed his laws and were upright before God as Muslims, not associating anything with Him; and the Prophet of this time, meaning Muhammad; and those who believed him and what he brought from God; and that God stands as supporter of the believers who affirm Muhammad in his prophethood and in what he brought them, against those of other communities and creeds who oppose them. The editor refers to earlier places for the sense of the term for ally. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 497}

**Reports 7214 and 7215.** Report 7214 (Bishr, from Yazid, from Said, from Qatada): those who walked after him in his creed, practice, path and natural disposition, and the Prophet Muhammad of this time, and those who believed with him, who affirmed him and followed him; so Muhammad and the believers with him are the people with the closest claim to Abraham. Report 7215 (al-Muthanna, from Ishaq, from Ibn Abi Jafar, from his father, from al-Rabi) is the same. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 497} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 498}

**Report 7216 (Ibn Masud).** Al-Tabari gives (Muhammad ibn al-Muthanna, Jabir ibn al-Kurdi and al-Hasan ibn Abi Yahya al-Maqdisi, from Abu Ahmad, from Sufyan, from his father, from Abu al-Duha, from Masruq, from Abdallah ibn Masud) that the Prophet said every prophet has allies among the prophets, and that his ally among them is his father and his Lord's friend, meaning Abraham; then he recited the verse. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 498}

**The editor's study of the chain of report 7216.** He identifies the transmitters: Jabir ibn al-Kurdi al-Wasiti al-Bazzar, reliable and one of al-Nasai's teachers; al-Hasan ibn Abi Yahya al-Maqdisi, whom he could not identify; Abu Ahmad, who is al-Zubayri, Muhammad ibn Abdallah ibn al-Zubayr al-Asadi; Sufyan, who is al-Thawri; his father Said ibn Masruq al-Thawri al-Kufi, reliable and cited by the authors of the six books; Abu al-Duha, Muslim ibn Subayh; and Masruq ibn al-Ajda al-Hamdani. He judges the chain "sound and connected", and says the report is found in the collections in two forms, connected and disconnected with Masruq omitted, that Abu al-Duha did not meet Ibn Masud (Ibn Masud died in 33 and Abu al-Duha in 100 AH, as the editor states), and that the addition of a link by a reliable narrator is accepted. He traces it: al-Tirmidhi (4: 80-81) from Mahmud ibn Ghaylan from Abu Ahmad al-Zubayri, connected like al-Tabari's; al-Bazzar through Abu Ahmad as Ibn Kathir reports (2: 163); and two reliable narrators followed Abu Ahmad in connecting it, so al-Hakim has it in the Mustadrak (2: 292) through Muhammad ibn Ubayd al-Tanafisi from Sufyan, connected and raised to the Prophet, al-Hakim saying it is sound on the condition of the two Shaykhs though they did not extract it, with al-Dhahabi agreeing; and Ibn Kathir (2: 161-162) reports that Said ibn Mansur had it from Abu al-Ahwas Sallam ibn Sulaym, whom the editor calls reliable, precise and a memoriser, from Said ibn Masruq, Sufyan's father. He concludes that this favours the connected form, and that to him the difference is not really about Sufyan but that Sufyan himself would sometimes connect it and sometimes cut it, which is common in chains. These are the editor's judgements; the museum gives none. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 498} {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 499}

**Reports 7217 and 7218.** Report 7217 (Ibn al-Muthanna, from Abu Nuaym al-Fadl ibn Dukayn, from Sufyan, from his father, from Abu al-Duha, from Abdallah, the narrator saying he thinks he said it was from the Prophet) gives it similarly. The editor says this is the disconnected form of the report; that al-Tirmidhi (4: 81) has it from Mahmud from Abu Nuaym; that other reliable narrators agree with Abu Nuaym, among them Waki in what Ahmad reports in the Musnad (3800) and al-Tirmidhi from Abu Kurayb, and Yahya al-Qattan and Abd al-Rahman ibn Mahdi in what Ahmad reports (4088). He notes that Ibn Kathir, from Waki's commentary, has "from Abu Ishaq" in place of "from Abu al-Duha", which he judges a copyist's error. He says al-Tirmidhi preferred the disconnected report, which he regards as a preference without ground, that the narrator's "I think" shows doubt whether it was raised to the Prophet, probably from Ibn al-Muthanna, al-Tabari's teacher, or from al-Tabari himself, since al-Tirmidhi's report from Abu Nuaym has no such doubt, and that al-Suyuti (2: 42) mentions it without separating the connected from the disconnected and adds attributions to Abd ibn Humayd, Ibn al-Mundhir and Ibn Abi Hatim. Report 7218 (al-Muthanna, from Abdallah ibn Salih, from Muawiya ibn Salih, from Ali, from Ibn Abbas) says those who followed him are the believers. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v06 p. 499}
