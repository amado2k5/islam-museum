## Al-Tabari's commentary

Al-Tabari reads the verse within verses 32 to 33 in a single passage. He explains the phrase "And He was dutiful to my mother and did not make me a tyrant or miserable": informing about what Jesus said to the people: 'And He made me blessed and dutiful': meaning He made me dutiful to my mother. Al-barr is dutiful; it is said: He is dutiful to his father and dutiful to him, and with a fatha on the 'b', I read this letter as the reciters of the cities. It was narrated from Abu Nahik what is said: And from Abu Nuhayk that he recited: "And be dutiful to my mother" from the saying of Jesus. Abu Nuhayk said: He advised me to pray, give zakat, and be dutiful to my parents, just as he advised me to do so. It seems that Abu Nahik interpreted the phrase as 'and be dutiful to my mother' from Jesus' report of Allah's will to him through it, just as His saying: 'And He enjoined upon me prayer and zakat' is from his report on Allah's will to him in this regard. According to this view, the accusative of righteousness must mean the act of the will in it, because prayer and zakat, although weak in wording, are in the accusative sense because they are objects. In the passage, he quotes from Qatadah and Ibn Ishaq.
