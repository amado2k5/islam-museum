## Al-Tabari's commentary

He explains His saying, "He said that between me and you, for which term you have decided, so there is no aggression against me, and God is the trustee over what we say": "He said" and Moses said to the father of the two women, "That is between you and me," meaning this is what you said about marrying me to one of your daughters on the condition that I reward you for eight pilgrimages, which is obligatory between you and me. Each of us must fulfill to his companion what he has made obligatory upon himself. And His saying: "Whichever of the two terms have you decided," he means: which of the eight proofs and ten proofs have been fulfilled? He means: I have finished them, so your sheep and livestock will be grazed by them. "So there is no aggression against me," meaning: You have no right to attack me and demand more than that. And "what" in His saying: "Whichever of the two periods" is a connection that connects to it always, meaning always. The people of Arabic claimed that this is more in Arabic speech than 'any,' and he recited the poet's saying: And whichever one I follow, I am keen to follow what I follow. Abbas ibn Mirdas said: 'Whatever is with you is evil.' He also transmitted in the Maqta' statements from Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ibn Zayd, Ibn Jurayj, and Ibn Ishaq.
