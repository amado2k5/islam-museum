**Commentary on verse 86 (pp. 447-449)**

On [[passage/quran-11-86|11:86]] al-Tabari says: by 'what God leaves is better for you' He means what God leaves you, having made lawful to you after you give people their rights in measure and weight with justice, is better for you than what remains to you from cheating them of their rights; 'if you are believers' means if you believe God's promise and threat, His lawful and unlawful. He notes that this view is reported from Ibn Abbas by a chain not approved by the transmitters. The interpreters differ: some said it means obeying God is better for you, reports 18477 to 18482, from Mujahid. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 447}

Others said it means your portion from your Lord is better for you, reports 18483 and 18484, from Qatada. Others said it means God's provision is better for you, report 18485, from Ibn Abbas: 'what God leaves' is God's provision. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 448}

Ibn Zayd says in report 18486: 'destruction' is in the punishment and 'what is left' in mercy. Al-Tabari says: I chose the view I chose because God had first forbidden them to cheat people of their things in measure and weight, and Shu'ayb called them to leave short measure and defrauding, so to follow that with what they gain from fulfilling it, in this world and the next, is more fitting; besides, 'baqiyya' is a verbal noun from 'a remainder remained of such a thing', so its meaning can only be what God has left you of yours after you pay people their rights, which is better for you than what remains to you of the forbidden from your wronging people by cheating them in measure and weight. As for 'and I am not a guardian over you', it means I am not a watcher over you, people, at your measuring and weighing to see whether you pay people their rights or wrong them; I have only to convey my Lord's message, and I have conveyed it. {cite:tafsir-tabari-shakir-v15 p. 449}

These statements are given as transmitted; the choices and arguments are al-Tabari's, the notes and source tracings are the editor's, and the museum gives no verdict.
